# Sources, exact prior scope, and novelty

1. Yuyuan Zhang, Mingzhen Lv and Haitao Cao,
   [*On the possible cardinalities of quantum Latin squares*, arXiv:2607.19969v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19969v2#S5),
   Section 5, Table 6. The order-11 row lists 66, 106, 113 and 115
   within its uncertain interval 54–115, and lists 119 explicitly.
   None of these five appears in the table's attainable column. The
   interval in the uncertain column also contains several values the
   table separately marks attainable; we do not treat such overlap
   as an open question. In particular 105 is previously reported
   attainable and is not a novelty claim. Theorem 1.2 excludes order 11.
2. David J. Reutter and Jamie Vicary,
   [*Biunitary constructions in quantum information*, Higher Structures 3(1), 109–154 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.21136/HS.2019.04),
   Corollary 3.2.1, equation (41) and Figure 7(a) give the construction
   of a QLS from two Hadamards. Transpose the first matrix to use the
   column-column indexing of our formula. See also arXiv:1609.07775,
   Corollary 17.
3. Zhipeng Xu,
   [*New Cardinalities for Quantum Latin Squares of Order Six*, arXiv:2607.11800v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11800v3),
   Proposition 1 uses the column-column mixed Schur product directly.
   Section 7 applies it with a row permutation and Section 9 reaches
   one double ray at order six. The general Schur-product construction,
   row-permutation use, and exact signatures are not claimed new.
   Aishwarya P. Das and Durgesh Kumar,
   [*A Quantum Latin Square of Order Six with Cardinality 29*, arXiv:2608.12607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12607),
   settle the unrelated final order-six spectral gap, not these
   order-11 questions.
4. John J. Benedetto, Robert L. Benedetto and Joseph T. Woodworth,
   [*Optimal Ambiguity Functions and Weil's Exponential Sum Bound*, arXiv:1107.1887](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1887),
   Section 2, gives the two-value phase for primes \(p\equiv3\bmod4\),
   on nonresidues instead of residues. Argument reversal interchanges
   the two classes. Original attribution there: Göran Björck,
   *Functions of modulus one on \(Z_p\) whose Fourier transforms have constant
   modulus*, Alfred Haar Memorial Conference, Budapest 1985,
   North-Holland 1987, pp. 193–197. Orthogonality is established
   from scratch in this note. The related order-23 one-gap witness is
   recorded [separately](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/69a002a9a06523153067aa93d2f336f4867527b2/research/qls-order23-cardinality-528).
5. Yajuan Zang, Meihui Zheng, Zihong Tian and Xiuling Shan,
   [*On the cardinalities of quantum Latin squares*, arXiv:2508.01972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01972),
   Section 3 resolves maximal cardinality for all orders at least 4
   and is not the near-maximum question addressed here.

Bounded public search on 2026-11-27: checked the current versions above,
searched the exact title and identifier of Zhang–Lv–Cao with “order 11”
and individual cardinalities 66, 106, 113, 115 and 119, and searched the
same values with “quantum Latin square”, “Björck” and “Hadamard product”.
No earlier construction for these five order-11 values surfaced.
The result supplies five explicit independent attainability proofs
and exhaustively classifies only the cardinalities of the stated
row-permuted Björck family; other order-11 cases remain outside scope.
