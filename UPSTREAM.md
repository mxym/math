# Prior art and scope

- Z. A. Melzak, [*A Note on the Tarry–Escott Problem*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-mathematical-bulletin/article/note-on-the-tarryescott-problem/8F448D799B4688B77081C820811C861C), Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 4(3) (1961), 233–237, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1961-025-1), p. 233 eq. (3), records the classical quadratic-size counting bound. Melzak explicitly credits a simple combinatorial proof to his reference [1], Hardy–Wright, second edition; the original priority is not attributed to Melzak. Section 1 of this package gives the distinct-subset variant directly. Coppersmith, Mossinghoff, Scheinerman and VanderKam, [*Ideal solutions in the Prouhet–Tarry–Escott problem*](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3917), Math. Comp. 93 (2024), 2473–2501, discuss the classical moment formulation and ideal cases.
- Ajai Choudhry, *Equal sums of like powers and equal products of integers*, Rocky Mountain J. Math. 43(3) (2013), 763–792, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1216/RMJ-2013-43-3-763), is the direct equal-product antecedent. [The author bibliography](https://sites.google.com/view/dr-ajai-choudhry-maths/research-papers) and [the article abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258237048_Equal_sums_of_like_powers_and_equal_products_of_integers) identify its nonzero integer formulation and parametric or numerical families. We do not claim equal-product Prouhet–Tarry–Escott equations as new. The bounded source check did not independently audit that entire 30-page text.
- Ben Green and Terence Tao, [*The primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/167-2/p03), Annals of Mathematics 167(2) (2008), 481–547, [arXiv:math/0404188v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0404188v6), supply the prime specialization. Relative closeness comes only from using relevant offsets far down a much longer progression.
- In graph invariants, José Aliste-Prieto, Anna de Mier and José Zamora, [*On trees with the same restricted U-polynomial and the Prouhet–Tarry–Escott problem*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2016.09.019), Discrete Math. 340(6) (2017), 1435–1441, encode moment collisions in restricted tree invariants; their observable is not a pairing of a fixed set of prime vertices. Uwe Grimm and Michael Baake, [*Homometric point sets and inverse problems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.0094), define homometry by autocorrelation, not by the block statistics considered here.

The increment proved here is the four-vertex matching lift under simultaneous constraints: equal initial block moments, the same squarefree product and the same exact prime support, pairwise coprime squarefree semiprime blocks in each pairing, no common edges, and arbitrarily small relative range, for every moment order with quadratic block count. Targeted searches did not establish a priority claim for that combination. This dossier proves no hardness or security assertion.
