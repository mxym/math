# Full kernel and gap bounds for the deformed supersymmetric t–J chain

Use the two-site density of Eq. (9) and the \(t\)–\(J\) deformation of [Hagendorf §4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.0357#page=36). Here the **full** Hamiltonian is the sum of the \(N\) bond densities on the entire \(N\)-site ring, without projecting outside the signed momentum sector. We count both oriented bonds at \(N=2\). Its restriction to the eligible signed sector is the supersymmetric Hodge Hamiltonian of Eq. (8).

For \(N\ge2\) and real \(\zeta\ne0\) at twist \(0\) or \(\pi\), the full zero-energy kernel is exactly the two product states
\[
X^{\otimes N},\qquad Y^{\otimes N},\qquad
X=(|0\rangle+|2\rangle)/\sqrt2,\quad Y=(|0\rangle-|2\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]
The lower gap bound is \(4\min(\zeta^2,1)\), attained for twist zero at all lengths, and for twist \(\pi\) when \(|\zeta|\le1\) or \(N\) is even. For twist \(\pi\), odd \(N\), and \(|\zeta|>1\), we additionally bound the gap by
\[
4<\mathrm{gap}\ \le 2c-2ab\cos(\pi/N),\qquad
a=\zeta-1,\quad b=\zeta+1,\quad c=\zeta^2+1.
\]

## Local blocks and the gap

Let \(h=|1\rangle\) be a hole. The only nonzero local split is \(q(h)=r=aXY+bYX\) in this basis, as follows by expanding the displayed \(t\)–\(J\) split in §4.2. In the density formula \(q^\dagger q=2c|h\rangle\langle h|\). The \(qq^\dagger\) term is \(|r\rangle\langle r|\) on the no-hole block. A mixed contraction moves a hole past one particle with coefficient \(ab\); the two negative mixed contractions give the two swap directions. The one-hole block is \(cI-ab\,\mathrm{swap}\) on each two-dimensional space spanned by a particle–hole order and its reverse, and the two-hole block is \(2cI\). The \(\pi\) twist negates \(X,Y\) and fixes \(h\), so it flips only the seam hopping sign and leaves the no-hole block unchanged.

On the no-hole ring the directional diagonal of \(|r\rangle\langle r|\) can be symmetrized: each word has the same number of \(XY\) and \(YX\) bonds, so summing the density produces the same diagonal if both are set to \(c\). The off-diagonal still swaps a mixed bond with coefficient \(ab\). Thus on all mixed-content states the bondwise estimate is \((c-|ab|)\) times the mixed-wall count operator, which is at least \(2(c-|ab|)\). The only zero states in the whole no-hole space are the two constant words. In a sector of \(k\ge1\) holes the total diagonal from the hole terms is \(2ck\): each hole contributes two incidences. The absolute row sum of hole hopping is at most \(2k|ab|\), even including the twisted sign. The remaining no-hole terms on the other bonds are positive semidefinite. This proves the lower bound \(2(c-|ab|)=4\min(\zeta^2,1)\) and the exact kernel. Hagendorf already identifies the two periodic product states in the signed sectors where they occur; the bound here controls the full spectrum above the kernel.

The no-hole single flipped-particle waves have energy \(2c+2ab\cos(2\pi j/N)\), since the two disagreeing bonds support the flip hops. If \(ab\le0\) take \(j=0\); if \(ab>0\) and \(N\) is even take momentum \(\pi\). For untwisted \(ab>0\) and odd \(N\), the constant one-hole wave on an all-\(X\) background has energy \(2c-2ab\) and attains the gap. At twist \(\pi\) the one-hole plane waves in the same background have momenta \((2j+1)\pi/N\), giving the claimed upper bound when \(ab>0\).

To see strictness above 4 in the remaining regime, two or more holes have bound \(4k>4\). In the one-hole block, saturation at 4 would require zero energy for all particle–particle bonds, and identical amplitudes along a hole hop except for the minus sign at the seam, by equality in the positive hopping form of the antiperiodic graph. Fix a hole position and a particle content. The open particle segment's local rank-one constraints have a one-dimensional common kernel: each adjacent unlike swap imposes the nonzero ratio \(-a/b\) or its inverse. Connectivity by adjacent swaps and the inversion-number weighting solve all these equations. With \(|\zeta|>1\), \(|a/b|\ne1\) for finite real \(\zeta\). Moving the hole one full round cyclically rotates the \(N-1\) particles and imposes a negative sign. For a mixed content, moving the first particle to the last changes the inversion number by a nonzero amount, hence changes the modulus, contradicting that sign relation. For uniform content rotation is trivial, also impossible. In the no-hole block equality at 4 requires support only on the configurations with two walls and forces opposite amplitudes across every mixed-bond swap. For a mixed content with both counts at least two, a single unlike boundary swap turns a two-wall word into a four-wall word, forcing zero. The single flipped-spin sectors are odd cycles and cannot alternate signs around the ring. These arguments rule out equality at 4.

## The undeformed full and signed kernels

When \(\zeta=0\), use the magnetic basis \(0,2\) for particles and \(1\) as the hole. The density is \(I+\Pi\), with \(\Pi\) the graded swap, as in §4.2, and all seam conjugates are positive. A zero vector must be graded-antisymmetric across each ordinary neighboring bond. In particular two or more holes are impossible: move them together by swaps and use that adjacent identical holes are symmetric. With no holes, ordinary swaps of particles require all coefficients of a given content to agree, giving one line per content before the seam. With one hole, the particle coefficients are symmetric and there is an alternating factor indexed by the hole position. For \(\phi=0,\pi\), the seam leaves every no-hole content, and it leaves the one-hole space exactly if \(e^{i\phi}=(-1)^N\), giving counts \(N+1\) and respectively \(N\) extra. At other twists the no-hole mixed contents require \(e^{2i\phi}=1\), and the one-hole recurrence requires an eligible particle phase \(e^{\pm i\phi}=(-1)^N\), so only the two pure no-hole products survive. These constraints also apply to the doubled ring at \(N=2\).

For nonzero deformation, the two ground states have shift \(+1\) at twist zero and \(-1\) at twist \(\pi\), giving two singlets for odd \(N\) at zero twist and for even \(N\) at \(\pi\), zero otherwise. At zero deformation and zero twist the signed singlet counts are \(N+1\) for odd \(N\) and \(N\) for even \(N\); twist \(\pi\) interchanges the parities. Other twists have no singlets in the eligible signed momentum. The original periodic undeformed counts are credited to Hagendorf.
