Editors, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory

Please consider the manuscript “The finite-block cost of universality in
entanglement extraction” by Yongxian Zhang as a research article.

The paper studies one global unitary on an existing many-copy register followed
by deterministic local, LOCC, or completely PPT channels. The global unitary is
an explicit entangling resource; the manuscript distinguishes this task from
ordinary fixed-state LOCC distillation.

Its central contribution is a finite-dimensional support-versus-average method
that gives a sharp, target-uniform minimax fidelity profile for unknown
rank-bounded iid inputs. The common-support dimension and reciprocal purity of
the Haar-averaged input have the same leading asymptotic. This produces an
explicit logarithmic universality cost, including the local-cap transition,
which first-order capacity alone does not detect. The manuscript also develops
dimension-explicit finite-block control, exact universal fidelity and reliability
laws, and a matched original-register pure-input circuit application.

The mathematical proofs and prior-work attributions are included in the paper.
The finite matrix/channel core has separately scoped Lean proofs and reproducible
kernel audits. Representation theory, Haar averages, asymptotic probability and
circuit complexity remain ordinary mathematical proofs; the full manuscript is
not described as end-to-end machine verified.

This integrated manuscript consolidates two earlier repository extraction
preprints, cited and preserved in the source record. The cover package identifies
the additions and their relationship to those predecessors and to published and
recent quantum-information literature. It includes TeX sources, a theorem map,
source bindings, reproduction instructions, and internal review records. Internal
AI review is disclosed and is not represented as external human peer review.

The sole author received no external funding. AI assistance with research,
formalization, writing and internal review is disclosed in the manuscript.
