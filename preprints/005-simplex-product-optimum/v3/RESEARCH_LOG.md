## Selection and additional layers

The initial candidates were a sharp recursive spectral envelope, arbitrary-body equality for the lower cone invariant, and further transport/similarity extensions. Concurrent work already covered substantial transport/similarity continuations. The explicitly reserved nonpolytopal equality case in 005 had a concrete proof obstruction and a reusable probability formulation, so it was developed rather than adding more finite optimum tables.

The equality analysis first yielded a centered random-law theorem. It was then extended to finite-first-moment laws, with a strict sharp upper constant and a complete cancellation identity. This closed the convex-body equality problem by cone-volume measures and gave a dimension-uniform qualitative stability statement by maximum-simplex compactness.

Independent sign averaging then supplied the sharp symmetric upper bound and universal planar equality, while the octahedron and even interior-atom laws ruled out two overextensions. The spectral question was also advanced: an initial asymptotic nonattainment argument was strengthened before publication to a Cartesian-square construction with an explicit rationally checkable threshold. No numerical search is a proof dependency.

## Focused primary-literature comparison

The definition of cone-volume measure is classical. Boroczky--Henk, *Cone-volume measure and stability*, author-hosted manuscript dated 23 September 2016, equation (1.1), was read directly and its first page inspected. Its subspace-concentration and U-functional stability theorems are not the invariant $a$ or random first-absolute-determinant equality statement used here. A deeper relationship is not excluded.

Rademacher, *On the monotonicity of the expected volume of a random simplex*, Mathematika 58 (2012), 77--91, was read at the relevant definition and Lemma 4; the displayed second-moment covariance identity was inspected on the actual PDF page. It provides relevant random-simplex context, not a proof of the equality classification in this note.

Saroglou's 2011 paper on projection bodies of cones, double cones and zonoids has closely related geometric scope. Its primary publisher abstract and author listing were checked. Full-text retrieval from the author site failed in this pass. We therefore do not assert disjointness from its cone formulas or planar special cases. The full bibliographic references and available source links are in the manuscript.

The elementary Jensen and triangle inequalities are not advertised as new inequalities. The record is a proof disclosure with explicit refinements and limitations, not a first-priority claim. A complete literature comparison, including the full cone paper and related probability-law extremal results, remains necessary for an originality assessment.

## Highest-value remaining problem

For the full point-generated product/join class, determine the exact spectral supremum. The new theorem proves that no finite body attains it, with an explicit improving operation, but this does not show which infinite trajectory is optimal. The missing part remains an upper bound preserved by every product and join, matching an explicit sequence. Fixed finite hulls and the known $T_5$ orbit alone do not establish that upper bound.

A separate concrete problem is a quantitative version of simplex rigidity: convert the measure-weighted sign witness into an explicit affine-distance modulus. The current compactness proof gives no exponent. The symmetric equality problem in dimensions at least three also remains separate; products of planar bodies and intervals are examples, not a proved exhaustive class.
