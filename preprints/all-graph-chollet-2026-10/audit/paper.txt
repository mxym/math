                                 Strong Chollet inequalities
                               for all simple graph Laplacians
                                               Yongxian Zhang∗

                                               October 8, 2026



                                                    Abstract
            We prove the degree-product form of Chollet’s inequality for the Laplacian of every finite
        simple unweighted graph, including every principal submatrix with degrees retained from the
        original graph. The result also holds after arbitrary nonnegative diagonal additions. For a
        two-connected noncycle block, a feasible point of the full matching polytope supplies a permanent
        lower bound, while a cycle expansion and a trace estimate supply an upper bound for the
        entrywise square. The two logarithmic coefficients are separated by 1/60. Cycles are treated
        directly, and hereditary strength is preserved by diagonal additions and one-point sums. Block
        decomposition completes the proof for arbitrary graphs. This answers the two all-graph questions
        stated by Pant and Singh. A separate complete Lean proof establishes the same graph inequalities,
        including empty and singular cases; we explain its exact scope and its differences from the written
        proof.

Chollet’s conjecture asks whether per(A ◦ B) ≤ per(A) per(B) for Hermitian positive-semidefinite
matrices A, B, where ◦ is the entrywise product. The self case takes A = B. Pant and Singh [1]

self inequality holds for every graph Laplacian and, more strongly, whether its right-hand side can
be replaced by the permanent times the product of vertex degrees. We prove this stronger assertion
for all finite simple unweighted graphs and for every principal submatrix.

     There are no connectivity, minimum-degree, nonsingularity or nonemptiness assumptions. In
particular, taking a principal submatrix does not remove the contribution of edges leaving S from

matrices is made.
     The proof has three parts. On a two-connected noncycle block, diagonal normalization turns the
problem into a comparison between per(I + C) and per(I − B), where C = B ◦ B. A trace estimate
gives the upper coefficient 19/12 over unordered edges, and a fractional matching gives the lower
coefficient 8/5. Their difference is 1/60. Cycle and edge blocks require separate arguments. Finally,
the inequality for every principal submatrix is preserved by one-point sums, allowing the blocks of
an arbitrary graph to be assembled.
     We use Lieb’s established block permanent inequality [2] and Edmonds’ full matching-polytope
theorem [3], with all their required conditions stated below. The diagonal and coalescence closures
are already present in Pant–Singh [1, Theorem 4.3 and Appendix A]; we give the needed algebra in
full. The written proof was previously archived as a research note [5]. The present paper organizes
that proof and its verification scope; it does not present this editorial revision as a separate solution
of the underlying questions.

7    Assembly of all graphs
Proof of Theorem 1.1. The standard block decomposition of a connected graph expresses it as
successive one-point sums of its bridges and maximal two-connected blocks; see, for example, [4,
Chapter 3]. At each articulation vertex, its full degree is the sum of its degrees in those blocks.
Thus the same decomposition identifies the actual Laplacian, with its full diagonal, as their matrix
one-point sum. Each bridge is K2 . Each two-connected block is a cycle or a noncycle. Proposition 4.2
and Lemma 5.2 establish hereditary strength for all blocks, and Lemma 6.2 preserves it during
assembly. A one-vertex component has zero Laplacian: its nonempty permanent is zero, and its
empty permanent is one. Disconnected graphs are direct sums of their connected components. This
proves (1) for all G, S.
    Lemma 6.1 proves (2). Finally Theorem 2.1 gives per LG ≥ v dv ≥ 0. Multiplication by the
                                                                  Q

nonnegative per LG yields per(LG ◦ LG ) ≤ per(LG )2 .

Remark 7.1. Proposition 4.2 records the quantitative gap already implicit in the block comparison.
It classifies equality for those principal submatrices of a two-connected noncycle graph. It is not
a classification of equality for all assembled graphs, and the constant 1/60 is not claimed to be
optimal.

declaration is

in src/CholletAllGraphs.lean. It uses the actual Mathlib SimpleGraph.lapMatrix and
Matrix.permanent, and the shared StrongChollet definition quantifies over every principal vertex
subset, retaining the degrees of the original graph. The stronger declaration laplacianDiagonalS
trong_all permits arbitrary nonnegative diagonal additions to the whole graph Laplacian.

and Mathlib to d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612. The fixed records report
fresh compilation of all 83 source modules and empty-kernel, trust-level-zero replay of 985 owned

replaces explicit block-tree assembly. A semantic review checks that the unconditional endpoint has
exactly the graph quantifiers in Theorem 1.1. Neither the exponential refinement in Proposition 4.2

adaptations and licenses are documented in the package’s THIRD_PARTY.md.

9    Further questions
The graph theorem leaves the general positive-semidefinite Chollet conjecture untouched. An
extension to arbitrary positive edge weights would require additional control: the present fractional
matching uses unweighted vertex degrees and the fixed normalized edge size. The proof also does
not provide a full equality classification after block assembly. Those questions are distinct from the
two all-graph unweighted questions answered here.
