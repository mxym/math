                        Unbounded normalized cofactor spectra
                            for rank two Gram matrices
                                              Yongxian Zhang∗

                                               8 October 2026



                                                   Abstract
           For a positive-semidefinite Hermitian matrix A, let C(A)ij = aij per A(i | j), where the minor
        deletes row i and column j. We prove that λmax (C(A))/ per A has no dimension-independent
        upper bound, already for complex rank-two correlation matrices. The same construction makes
        λmax (Re C(A))/ per A arbitrarily large. The proof computes a spectral limit under repeated
        Gram vectors and combines it with projective equidistribution. It also gives positive-definite
        examples and unbounded local second-order relative coefficients for entrywise-positive real
        correlation perturbations.


1       Statement and scope
Our convention is that A(i | j) deletes row i and column j, without transposing this minor. All
Hermitian inner products below are linear in their first argument. The matrix C(A) is positive
semidefinite and C(A)1 = (per A)1. Drury disproved the assertion that this prescribed eigenvalue
is always the largest one [1]. Here we consider all constant multiples of the permanent.

Theorem 1. For every R > 0 there are an integer N and a complex Hermitian correlation matrix
A of rank exactly two such that

                          λmax (C(A)) > R per A,           λmax (Re C(A)) > R per A.

There are also positive-definite correlation matrices with both inequalities. The positive-definite
assertion does not retain rank two.

    The real part is entrywise, so its largest eigenvalue is the maximum Rayleigh quotient over
real unit vectors. The theorem is an unboundedness assertion as the order varies; no rate in
N is asserted. It is distinct from unbounded Hadamard-product bunching ratios [3], and from
bounds for the full Schur-power matrix. It does not resolve Lieb’s normalized character or subgroup
permanental-dominance conjecture [4]. A separate source audit records the finite literature search;
no claim of exhaustive priority verification is made.

3    Equidistribution and selection of one maximum
Choose collections of m unit rows whose empirical projective measures converge weakly to Haar
measure on CP1 . Such a sequence is obtained from midpoint grids in p = |a|2 ∈ (0, 1) and the
relative phase, with both grid sizes tending to infinity. Let um be any maximum of the corresponding
product, and rotate variables so that um = (1, 0).
    These moving rotations preserve weak convergence to Haar. Indeed, every subsequence of
rotations has a convergent subsubsequence by compactness of U (2). For each continuous test
function, the rotated functions then converge uniformly; weak convergence and Haar invariance give
the same limiting integral. This subsequence argument proves the claim for the full sequence.

    For a strict witness A, polynomial dependence of C(A) and per A, continuity of Hermitian
eigenvalues, and per A > 0 show that the strict inequalities persist for A + εI with sufficiently
small ε > 0. The matrix (A + εI)/(1 + ε) is a positive-definite correlation matrix. Both ratios are
unchanged by this final scalar rescaling, since C(cA) = cN C(A) and per(cA) = cN per A.
