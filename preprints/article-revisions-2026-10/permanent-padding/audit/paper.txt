       A padding proof of a conjectured permanent inequality
                                         Yongxian Zhang∗

                                          8 October 2026



                                              Abstract
         The unrestricted-cut permanent inequality stated as Conjecture 9.3 by Pan, Skandera,
      and Wang follows from their balanced inequality, Theorem 8.18, by adjoining an identity
      matrix. We give the complete reduction, including singular matrices and endpoint cuts.
      The only non-elementary input is the balanced theorem from their preprint.

For an integer n ≥ 0, write [n] = {1, . . . , n}, let On and En be its odd and even elements, and
write A[S] = AS,S for a principal submatrix in increasing index order. The empty permanent
is 1. A real matrix is totally nonnegative (TNN) if every square minor is nonnegative.
    We use the following even-order instance of Pan–Skandera–Wang’s Theorem 8.18 [1]: for
every m ≥ 2 and every TNN matrix B of order 2m,

                  per B[[m]] per B[{m + 1, . . . , 2m}] ≥ per B[O2m ] per B[E2m ].                 (1)

For m = 1 this is an equality.

Theorem 1. For every integer n ≥ 1, every TNN matrix A ∈ Rn×n , and every integer
0 ≤ h ≤ n,
               per A[[h]] per A[{h + 1, . . . , n}] ≥ per A[On ] per A[En ].  (2)
In particular, Conjecture 9.3 of [1] holds.

Proof. First recall two elementary facts about ordered direct sums. If C, D are TNN, then
C ⊕ D is TNN: a square minor selecting unequal numbers of first-block rows and first-block
columns is zero by rank deficiency, while a minor selecting equal numbers factors as the product
of a minor of C and a minor of D, without a sign change. Also,

                                  per(C ⊕ D) = per(C) per(D),                                      (3)

because only permutations preserving both blocks can contribute nonzero summands. These
assertions include empty blocks. Every principal submatrix of an identity matrix has permanent
1.
    Suppose 0 < h < n and 2h ≤ n. Put d = n − 2h, m = n − h, and

                                            B = Id ⊕ A.

This is a TNN matrix of order 2m, with balanced leading and trailing principal blocks Id ⊕A[[h]]
and A[{h + 1, . . . , n}]. Thus the left side of (1) equals the left side of (2). An index i of A
becomes d + i in B. Consequently the two parity principal submatrices of B are identity blocks

direct-summed with A[On ] and A[En ], in the same order if d is even and the opposite order if
d is odd. By (3),
                     per B[O2m ] per B[E2m ] = per A[On ] per A[En ].
Applying (1) proves the claim. The possible case m = 1 is n = 2, h = 1, where the claim is an
equality.
    If instead 0 < h < n and 2h ≥ n, put d = 2h − n, m = h, and B = A ⊕ Id . The two
balanced blocks are now A[[h]] and A[{h + 1, . . . , n}] ⊕ Id . The original indices of A do not
shift, and the parity principal submatrices simply acquire identity direct-summands. Thus
both product equalities again hold, and (1) applies. This also includes d = 0.
    Finally, if h = 0 or h = n, the left side is per A. Every entry of A is nonnegative. The
permutations preserving On and En contribute exactly the product on the right side, which is
therefore at most the sum over all permutations. This handles the endpoint cuts and n = 1.

Scope and attribution. The proof uses no computation and no strict-positivity, irreducibil-
ity, or nonvanishing assumption. It is a short consequence of the balanced theorem in [1], not
an independent proof of that theorem. The cited version explicitly states the balanced result
as Theorem 8.18 and the unrestricted-cut assertion as Conjecture 9.3. Since the balanced
case is included in the unrestricted assertion, identity padding shows that the two universal
statements are equivalent.
