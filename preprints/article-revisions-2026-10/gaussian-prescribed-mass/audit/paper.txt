A sharp Gaussian first-moment inequality for every prescribed mass
                             vector

Abstract
We establish a sharp first-moment inequality for every measurable simplex-valued function on
Gaussian space with prescribed positive mean. The label metric is the negative Hessian of the
Gaussian multi-bubble perimeter profile. The equality functions are exactly the indicators of the
translated regular simplex partition with those probabilities, extended cylindrically. The result holds
for every number of labels and every ambient dimension. Its proof derives a global tangent bound
for the squared optimal Gaussian assignment value from the established multi-bubble perimeter
theorem and a radial differential inequality. An exact nonnegative integral measures its deficit. At
equal masses this recovers the sharp ordinary squared-centroid sum; at unequal masses the metric is
generally anisotropic. We do not assert the false unequal-mass Euclidean simplex conjecture or the
full functional multi-bubble isoperimetric inequality. The profile Hessian identity is prior work; our
statement is a bound for all functions, not only perimeter minimizers.

The ordinary unweighted prescribed-mass squared-moment conjecture is false [EX]. The present
theorem uses the explicitly defined label metric Hp+ and makes no contrary Euclidean assertion. It
is a first-moment functional inequality, not the full simplex-valued Gaussian Bobkov or positive-
correlation noise-stability statement. The profile Hessian identity is prior work; its application here
bounds all functions, beyond variations of perimeter minimizers.


6. Proof and verification dependencies
The non-elementary geometric input is [MN22, Theorem 1.1], used only with k = n + 1, its positive-
mass hypotheses and its interface-perimeter normalization. The established profile Hessian identity is
stated with its tangent-space convention and independently normalized in Section 2. The remaining
tools are Gaussian integration by parts, dominated convergence, the implicit function theorem,
matrix square roots, pseudoinverses and one-variable calculus. There is no numerical or solver
premise, no differentiation at a singular endpoint, no assumption of covariance concavity and no
rank classification.
The package includes internal model reviews, finite exact diagnostics and partial Lean proofs with
explicit scope. They do not certify Gaussian measure, the imported geometric theorem or the
entire analytic endpoint. Internal model reviews are not external human peer review. The earlier
equal-mass result is preserved as its own immutable record; this theorem is a generalization of the
same proof mechanism, not an independent counting of the equal-mass case.
