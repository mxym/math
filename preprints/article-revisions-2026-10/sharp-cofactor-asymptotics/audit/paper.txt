         Sharp logarithmic growth of permanental cofactor spectra
                and an optimal rank-two endpoint constant
                                             Yongxian Zhang∗

                                              8 October 2026



                                                  Abstract
           For a Hermitian positive-semidefinite matrix A, let C(A)ij = aij per A(i | j), with row
        i and column j deleted. The largest possible ratio λmax (C(A))/ per A among matrices of
        order N is asymptotic to log N . The corresponding maximum over real Rayleigh directions,
        λmax (Re C(A))/ per A, is asymptotic to 12 log N . Both lower limits already hold within rank-
        two correlation matrices at every sufficiently large order. We give an explicit dyadic family,
        exact finite Rayleigh certificates, and a finite random-sign construction attaining the leading
        constants. We also determine the sharp asymptotic ramp Rayleigh constant 3π 2 /16 and its
        rank-two q-permanent endpoint counterpart π 2 /8. These are cofactor extremal results; Lieb’s
        character-dominance and Marcus’s permanent-of-permanents conjectures remain unresolved here.

7    What remains unresolved
Neither a large noncentral cofactor eigenvalue nor its real Rayleigh excess is automatically a normal-
ized character or subgroup projector. Ordinary rank-two immanants lie in two-row sectors where
classical dominance results already apply. Marcus’s rank-two large-order regime is independently
excluded by a factorial/Jensen comparison; the precise inequality and finite thresholds are recorded in
the stage summary. Higher-rank and genuine subgroup couplings have not been resolved. Numerical
searches are reported only as searches.
    An unrestricted higher-compound binary-vector formula was separately tested under an explicit
definition and has an exact internal counterexample. The full text and qualifications of the potentially
related statement in [3] were not obtained. We make no assertion that its theorem is false, and do
not use that unverified statement anywhere in this paper.
