              Uniform real-rootedness of the (132, 213)-avoiding
                        permutation-polytope family
                                             Yongxian Zhang∗

                                             8 October 2026



                                                 Abstract
          We prove that the Ehrhart h∗ -polynomial of the convex hull of the (132, 213)-avoiding
      permutations in Sd has only negative real zeros for every d ≥ 3. A uniform analytic argument
      proves positivity of the repaired Eulerian tails for every d ≥ 1001. Combining this with de
      Castro’s finite Theorem 1.3 for d ≤ 1000 resolves Conjecture 10.1 of arXiv:2609.06096v1. The
      new argument uses centered Bernoulli-sum comparison, finite centered moments of an infinite
      correction series, and degree smoothing for its large components. Exact rational certificates
      establish scalar envelopes on 33 closed intervals and the finite bases of infinite inductions. The
      original finite dataset is an explicitly cited prior input, not an independently reconstructed
      computation. A short corollary applies Zhang’s known Veronese preservation theorem to all
      positive integral dilates of this particular family.

Lemma 3.1. Every integer-valued distribution with finite variance V and largest atom M satisfies
M ≥ (12V + 1)−1/2 . If a Bernoulli sum has integer mean m, this bounds P(X = m) from below.
Proof. Adding an independent uniform variable on [−1/2, 1/2] gives a density bounded by M , with
variance V + 1/12. Among mass-one densities bounded by M , the density equal to M on the interval
of length 1/M centered at the mean minimizes the second moment. Indeed, subtract that density
and integrate against (t − µ)2 − 1/(4M 2 ): both the inside and outside products are nonnegative,
and the constant term integrates to zero. Its variance is 1/(12M 2 ). Darroch’s integer-mean mode
theorem gives the last assertion.

Proof of Theorem 1.1. For d ≥ 1001, Proposition 2.1 covers r ≤ ⌊d/4⌋. For the remaining needed
coordinates put m = d − 1 − j, so

                                      1 ≤ m ≤ d − 2 − ⌊d/4⌋ < 3d/4.

µd (15) ≥ dν(15) − 15/16 > 3d/4 for d > 1000 follows from log 8 < 209/100 and ν(15) = 15/14 −
(15/98) log 8 > 751/1000. Thus each required unique saddle lies below 15. Propositions 7.1
and 8.1 cover all these saddles, including their common endpoint 1/10. Hence ad,j > 0 for

endpoint facts and repair criterion give real zeros. Since the Ehrhart coefficients are nonnegative
and Hd (0) = 1, Hd (t) > 0 for t ≥ 0, so all its zeros are strictly negative.
    For d ≤ 1000, apply de Castro’s finite Theorem 1.3. Its base cases include H1 = H2 = 1 and
H3 = (1 + t)2 . This covers all dimensions.
