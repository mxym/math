# Semantic correspondence review

The unconditional endpoint is `Chollet.strongChollet_all_graphs` in `src/CholletAllGraphs.lean`. Its only parameters are a finite vertex type, decidable equality, a `SimpleGraph`, and decidable adjacency. The conclusion is the exact shared `StrongChollet` definition in `src/Target.lean`: every principal submatrix of the actual mathlib graph Laplacian, the actual matrix permanent, and the product of degrees in the original ambient graph. No connectivity, minimum degree, nonsingularity, nonempty index, numerical bound, or unproved matching/Fischer premise appears in the endpoint.

The stronger intermediate endpoint `laplacianDiagonalStrong_all` proves the whole-matrix bound after an arbitrary nonnegative real diagonal addition. The all-principal endpoint pulls back the graph through the subset embedding and adds the proved nonnegative ambient-minus-induced degree correction. This is why the final diagonal factors are the original degrees even when vertices are removed.

## Proof chain

1. Real PSD matrices are represented by an actual Gram system using the mathlib positive square root. Singleton and pair Fischer lower bounds are proved directly from polynomial identities and sums of squares. They imply permanent nonnegativity, the diagonal-product lower bound, and the matching lower bound even for empty or singular matrices. No general Lieb inequality is postulated.
2. The ordinary matching polytope and weighted matching selection are derived from the bundled proved perfect-matching polytope theorem by a doubled graph. The eight bundled upstream source modules, including their declaration dependencies, belong to this audit.
3. Graph edges use both orientations. Each orientation carries `(9/10)/(d_u*d_v)`; their combined unordered-edge weight is `(9/5)/(d_u*d_v)`. Vertex and all odd-set constraints are proved for every required noncycle block. Thus the logarithmic lower coefficient is `4/5` over oriented squared kernels, equal to `8/5` over unordered edges. Restriction preserves ambient degrees.
4. The actual permanent is expanded by permutation cycles. A rooted simple cycle is injected into a finite closed vertex walk. The cycle weight equals the walk product, and each unrooted cycle has exactly its support size many roots. This proves the cycle-weight sum is bounded by the actual finite trace series.
5. Weighted row Cauchy–Schwarz and a Frobenius contraction bound actual powers: `tr(C^(m+2)) <= (1/2)^m tr(C^2)`. The finite trace series has coefficient `19/24`. Applied to the normalized principal Laplacian square `I+C`, this compares with the matching lower coefficient `4/5`.
6. Degree-two vertex-robust blocks on at least four vertices are proved triangle-free directly from the proper-set boundary theorem. Therefore the third trace vanishes and the upper coefficient improves to `5/8`. For orders 4 through 10, scaling the actual feasible weights by `(10/9)*(n-1)/n` gives the missing full odd-set constraint. Its lower coefficient is at least `5/8` for every principal subset. Larger orders use the original feasible vector. Orders at most three use the bundled finite graph theorem.
7. Connectivity of every literal vertex deletion implies minimum degree two for orders at least four. A graph in this branch either has a degree at least three, or all degrees are two. The previous bounds cover both cases and all principal subsets.
8. Strong induction on the vertex count handles an arbitrary graph. A disconnected graph splits into a reachable component and its complement. A disconnected vertex deletion splits into two nonempty parts joined only through the deleted root. Explicit equivalences and exact degree sums identify their Laplacians with the actual direct-sum and one-point-sum matrices. Both parts are strictly smaller.
9. The one-point permanent identity and strong inequality are proved algebraically. PSD singleton pivots and nonnegative deleted permanents are supplied by item 1; the side whole/deleted strong bounds are supplied by the same vertex-count induction, with the nonnegative degree corrections of actual deleted graph matrices.
10. A finite induction over diagonal additions extends each whole-graph result to every nonnegative diagonal. Every deleted minor needed in this induction is a strictly smaller pulled-back graph Laplacian with a nonnegative degree correction. This closes the strengthened induction without assuming hereditary matrix closure.

## Scope

The theorem covers finite simple **unweighted** graphs over the real Laplacian. It does not assert the general PSD Chollet conjecture or an arbitrary weighted-graph theorem. It includes isolated vertices, disconnected graphs, the empty principal set, and singular Laplacians. Only the three pinned standard Lean axioms are permitted by the final audit; there are no additional mathematical axioms or omitted-proof premises.
