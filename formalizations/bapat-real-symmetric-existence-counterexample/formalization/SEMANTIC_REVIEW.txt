1. Quantified conclusion and actual q-permanent

BapatRealExistence.exists_integer_real_symmetric_counterexample asserts:
there exist a natural N>4 and B : Matrix (Fin N) (Fin N) Int such that
B.IsSymm, its entrywise real cast is Matrix.PosDef, and not B.IsDiag;
the real qPolynomial has strictly negative derivative evaluated at 1;
and there exist q0 q1 : Rat with 0<q0<q1<1 and
qPermanent(realOfIntMatrix B, q1) < qPermanent(realOfIntMatrix B, q0).
All these properties concern the same N and B.

BapatRankTwo.qPermanent sums over every permutation of Fin N. The weight
is q raised to the cardinality of pairs i<j for which sigma(j)<sigma(i),
multiplied by the full product of entries B(i,sigma(i)). There is no
restricted permutation class, surrogate coefficient list or free inversion
weight. BapatFiniteRank.qPolynomial_eval identifies the actual polynomial
with this q-permanent at every real q. Its polynomial derivative therefore
has the ordinary mathematical meaning required by the source.

The integer entries are embedded in R before positivity, differentiation
and evaluation. Matrix.PosDef is mathlib's positive-definiteness predicate;
over this finite real index set it includes symmetry and strict positivity
of the quadratic form on every nonzero vector. Integer symmetry is also
stated explicitly. q0 and q1 are positive rational interior points, not
merely real points or points in (-1,1).

2. Finite endpoint identity and row repetition

BapatMarkedInversions and BapatTwoRowCofactor use the actual ordered
inversion statistic. Finite color transport proves the multivariate Fischer
pairing identity, and BapatFiniteRankEndpoint combines it with the finite
wedge-coordinate Cauchy–Binet identity. BapatRealPolynomial supplies the

BapatOrderedRepetition enumerates contiguous blocks with their original

BapatRankFourSphereEndpoint and BapatPeakToEndpoint give the exact
rank-four integral endpoint formula and a conditional repetition reduction.
That reduction's existence hypotheses are all discharged in the final chain.

3. Real cloud, canonical coordinates and balanced maxima

BapatEmpiricalCloud constructs an actual deterministic equidistributed
real unit-sphere sequence using a countable dense family of continuous
tests and the strong law. BapatOrthogonalSphere proves orthogonal Haar
orbit invariance, transitivity and preservation of equidistribution under
moving rotations.

The logarithmic potential is integrable, including the endpoint t=1;
BapatGaussianBalance proves its strict maximum at t=1/2, and
BapatSphereBalance transfers the comparison to normalized sphere measure.
Canonical complex coordinates are constructed by an actual unit phase and
real isometries. Nonvanishing holds almost everywhere; logarithmic cutoffs
are continuous and their integrals converge by dominated convergence.
The Haar lower bound and compact positive maxima then prove maximizer
balance. BapatCanonicalCloud supplies a triangular cloud with genuine
positive canonical maxima and parameters tending to 1/2. No measurable
or continuous choice of maximizing points or canonical rotations is assumed.

4. Exact ratio gap and passage to finite clouds

The balanced ratio is the real part of (x2+i*x3)/(x0+i*x1), with Lean's
usual total division at zero. BapatRatioCoordinates proves its relation
to the actual row forms and its degree-zero homogeneity. Singular
coordinate hyperplanes have normalized sphere measure zero.

The formalization obtains the needed Gini value directly, without taking
the paper's full Cauchy density/CDF as an additional premise.
BapatGaussianLinear evaluates the absolute Gaussian covector integral.
BapatRatioDenominator proves integrability of its singular denominator
coefficient and computes its radial integral. BapatGaussianRatioIntegral
computes the full unnormalized eight-dimensional integral pi^5/2.
BapatRatioRegroup supplies measure-preserving block regrouping and
Gaussian-to-sphere normalization, yielding exactly E|X-Y| = pi/2.
BapatRatioIntegrability establishes genuine integrability for the sphere
pair, so this is not an artefact of a default nonintegrable Bochner integral.

BapatRatioLowerApprox uses continuous nonnegative lower approximations
whose denominator is cut off away from zero. Their integrals converge to
pi/2 by dominated convergence. BapatEmpiricalPairs proves uniformity over
compact continuous test families, pair-average convergence and moving
parameter convergence for triangular clouds. Consequently the actual
finite cloud's average ratio difference eventually exceeds 3/2. No
unbounded empirical-moment convergence or uniform integrability of
empirical ratio laws is assumed.

5. Selector, actual row ordering, and strict energy gap

BapatSelectorRows gives four actual real linear forms. The maximum/equality
analysis in BapatSelectorProfile, Quartic and Peaks proves exactly the two
conjugate phase orbits. BapatSelectedPeak proves equality of the real
homogeneous wedge observable at every selected maximum.

BapatOrderedRatioScore uses an actual permutation that sorts all augmented
rows by the real ratio. Pair score is permutation invariant and cannot
decrease upon appending rows. The product polynomial and its peak set are
preserved by permutation. No invariance of the inversion-weighted
q-permanent under arbitrary simultaneous row/column permutation is used.

BapatWedgeContraction proves the polynomial contraction identity by finite
expansion. BapatExteriorUnit proves that the two orthonormal directions
have unit exterior coefficient norm. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the squared
sorted ratio score times |F|^2 by the actual wedge energy.
BapatFiniteBase chooses one finite cloud and absorbs the four appended
rows with a strict numerical margin. BapatRealEndpoint then applies
contiguous repetition to that fixed ordered base. Its theorem
exists_real_gram_negative_endpoint is unconditional: a finite N>4 and
real four-coordinate rows with negative derivative actually exist.

6. Rational, positive-definite and integer conclusion

BapatRationalPerturbation proves continuity of the finite endpoint
polynomial in row entries and uses density of rational rows. The rational
Gram matrix is positive semidefinite. A sufficiently small positive
rational diagonal perturbation makes it positive definite and preserves
the strict negative derivative.

BapatRationalInterior constructs a strict decrease inside (0,1), then uses
density and continuity to obtain rational q0<q1 there. It also proves that
a diagonal matrix has zero endpoint derivative. Thus the negative
endpoint inequality itself establishes non-diagonality; preservation of
nonzero rows and a separate rank-versus-diagonal argument are unnecessary.

BapatIntegerCounterexample uses mathlib Matrix.num/Matrix.den to clear all
rational denominators with one positive natural multiplier. Positive
scaling preserves strict positive definiteness and the endpoint sign by
the proved qPolynomial scaling identity. The final rational interior
parameters are obtained for this same integer matrix.

7. Scope and trust boundary

This is a finite existence proof. No explicit upper bound on N, numerical
real matrix, or extraction of witnesses from classical choice is claimed.
The Gaussian Gini calculation and non-diagonality argument are the two
proof-route changes from the reference text; they preserve the conclusion.
The development does not assume the Cauchy law, the selector equality locus,
maximizer balance, or the existence of a finite strict-gap base as axioms.
