# Proof roadmap for literal truncation sharpness

This roadmap follows the proof of `Entry005.truncationSharpness`. Every claimed formal step below points to a source module. It explains how the actual geometric objects reach the exact target; it does not extend the Lean claim to all the analytic discussion in `project/SHARPNESS_PROOF.md` or the source paper.

Fix `d ≥ 3` and `0 < t < 1`. Write `e_1,…,e_d` for the standard Euclidean basis, `1` for the all-ones vector, and

\[
K_t=\{x:x_i\ge0,\ t\le\sum_i x_i\le1\},\qquad
S_{t,i}=\operatorname{conv}(t e_i,e_1,\ldots,e_d).
\]

There are three geometric ingredients: the actual defect of `K_t`, global maximality of `S_{t,i}`, and excess about that simplex's own centroid. A scalar real-power argument then supplies all final existential quantifiers at once.

## 1. Construct the actual body and compute its volume

[TruncationDefinitions.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationDefinitions.lean) defines the points `e_i`, `t e_i`, and the chosen simplex's ordered point tuple. [TruncationGeometry.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationGeometry.lean#L55) proves

\[
K_t=\operatorname{conv}\{e_j,t e_j:1\le j\le d\}.
\]

For a point `x ∈ K_t`, put `s = Σ x_j` and `p = x/s`. Since `s ≥ t > 0`, `p` is a convex combination of the standard basis. The identity

\[
x=\frac{s-t}{1-t}p+\frac{1-s}{1-t}\,tp
\]

has nonnegative coefficients summing to one and gives the required actual convex combination. Conversely, all displayed vertices satisfy the inequalities, and the carrier is convex. The finite convex hull is compact. `truncationBody` at line 109 wraps precisely this carrier as a `ConvexBody`; `truncationSet_interior_nonempty` at line 121 constructs a strict-inequality neighborhood about the point with all coordinates `(1+t)/(2d)`.

[TruncationVolume.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationVolume.lean#L62) decomposes the unit coordinate simplex into `K_t` and the removed simplex scaled by `t`. Their overlap is contained in `Σ x_j = t`, a measure-zero hyperplane. Actual simplex volumes and finiteness give

\[
V_t=|K_t|=\frac{1-t^d}{d!}.
\]

[TruncationSimplexVolume.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationSimplexVolume.lean) supplies eight selected OpenAI/math simplex-volume proof bodies as an exact 6,404-byte prefix, followed only by namespace closers. The source, pin, attribution, and prefix identity are detailed in [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md). These are actual volume proofs, not an assumed normalization.

## 2. Derive all intrinsic facet data

Set `c = 1/(d-1)!`, `q = t^(d-1)`, and `r = 1-q`. [TruncationFacetGeometry.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationFacetGeometry.lean) constructs outward unit normals and supports:

- coordinate facets: `n_j = -e_j`, `h_j = 0`;
- top facet: `n_+ = 1/√d`, `h_+ = 1/√d`;
- bottom facet: `n_- = -1/√d`, `h_- = -t/√d`.

The halfspace carrier equality is proved directly from the literal inequalities. The top facet's intrinsic area is derived from its actual radial cone, whose volume is the ambient simplex volume. The bottom facet is proved to be the top facet's homothet by `t`, so its intrinsic `(d-1)`-dimensional volume scales by `q`.

[TruncationCoordinateFacets.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationCoordinateFacets.lean#L13) constructs a genuine linear isometry inserting a zero coordinate, with coordinate deletion as inverse. It identifies each actual coordinate facet with a `(d-1)`-dimensional truncation. The resulting areas are `cr`, `c√d`, and `c√d q` for the coordinate, top, and bottom facets respectively.

Writing `a_j` for intrinsic area, [TruncationFacetVectors.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationFacetVectors.lean) proves that the area-normal vectors `v_j = a_j n_j` and area-support products `b_j = a_j h_j` are exactly

\[
(v_j,b_j)=(-cr e_j,0),\quad
(v_+,b_+)=(c\mathbf1,c),\quad
(v_-,b_-)=(-cq\mathbf1,-ct^d).
\]

These are equalities with chart-derived geometric data. The negative bottom support is retained with its correct sign; it is never used as a probability weight.

## 3. Use the genuine projection and pyramid bridge

[TruncationProjection.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationProjection.lean#L11) identifies the target's intersection-defined projection body with the facet zonotope generated by the vectors `v_j/2`. Its brightness theorem at line 29 proves, for every actual unit vector `u`,

\[
|\operatorname{proj}_{u^\perp}K_t|
=\frac c2\left(r\sum_j|u_j|+(1+q)\left|\sum_j u_j\right|\right).
\]

The inherited finite-halfspace cone-law/pyramid bridge requires strictly positive supports. [TruncationCenteredHalfspaces.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationCenteredHalfspaces.lean#L25) therefore translates by the proved interior point `z = ((1+t)/(2d))1`. It proves positivity of every translated support, compactness, injectivity of the unit-normal family, and equality of the translated carrier, facet areas, volume, and affine-invariant `entryA`/`entryDefect` with the relevant original data. [TruncationFacetTranslation.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationFacetTranslation.lean) proves facet-area invariance through translation of the intrinsic charts.

The lifted data change by `(b_j,v_j) ↦ (b_j-v_j·z,v_j)`. [TruncationActualDefect.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationActualDefect.lean#L19) proves that this is a determinant-one row shear, so the lifted determinant sums are unchanged. The positive-support bridge is thus applied legitimately, and its conclusion is transported back to the original `K_t`.

The inherited bridge concerns the literal convex-hull unit-height pyramid, actual Euclidean volumes and intrinsic facet charts, actual Cauchy projection geometry, and actual finite iid moments. ## 4. Evaluate ordered determinant sums and obtain the actual defect

Let `H_ord` be the sum of absolute horizontal `d × d` facet minors over all ordered `d`-tuples, and `L_ord` the corresponding lifted `(d+1) × (d+1)` sum over all ordered `(d+1)`-tuples. Repeated-index tuples are included and proved to have zero determinant. [TruncationFacetFormula.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationFacetFormula.lean#L24) derives from the actual finite cone law and pyramid bridge

\[
A(K_t)=\frac{L_{\mathrm{ord}}}{(d+1)dV_tH_{\mathrm{ord}}}.
\]

This formula uses ordered sums. The factor `d+1` must not be dropped while retaining that convention.

[TruncationFacetDeterminants.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationFacetDeterminants.lean#L326) reduces tuples to injections, partitions them according to use of the two special facets, and counts the orderings. Horizontal minors containing both top and bottom vanish because those horizontal vectors are parallel. Lifted minors containing both are computed explicitly. The theorems at lines 326 and 374 give

\[
H_{\mathrm{ord}}=d!\,c^d\left[r^d+d(1+q)r^{d-1}\right],
\]
\[
L_{\mathrm{ord}}=(d+1)!\,c^{d+1}
\left[r^d(1+t^d)+d r^{d-1}(q-t^d)\right].
\]

Equivalently, defining unordered distinct-facet sums `H = H_ord/d!` and `L = L_ord/(d+1)!` gives `A(K_t)=L/(dV_tH)`. This explains the differing-looking formula in the preserved analytic note without mixing normalization conventions.

After substitution of the actual volume and facet vectors, [TruncationScalarCancellation.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationScalarCancellation.lean) proves the rational identity, with positive factors justified before cancellation. The final theorem [truncation_entryDefect_exact](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationActualDefect.lean#L249) is

\[
e(K_t)=\frac{t^{d-1}\bigl[d(d-1)-(d+1)(d-2)t-2t^d\bigr]}
{(d+1)(1-t^d)\bigl[d+1+(d-1)t^{d-1}\bigr]}.
\]

Its hypotheses are only `d ≥ 2` and `0 < t < 1`. No facet-area, determinant-sum, volume, projection, pyramid, or defect identity remains a premise.

## 5. Prove global maximality against arbitrary inscribed simplices

[TruncationMaximum.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationMaximum.lean#L13) proves affine independence of `(t e_i,e_1,…,e_d)` for `t ≠ 1`, containment of its convex hull in `K_t`, and augmented determinant `1-t`.

For any tuple of `d+1` points chosen from the actual `2d` vertices, some coordinate ray is repeated. A repeated identical point gives determinant zero. A top/bottom pair on a repeated ray permits a determinant-preserving column operation extracting the factor `1-t`. The remaining horizontal determinant is bounded by the product of column `ℓ¹` norms, each at most one. Thus every such vertex tuple has absolute augmented determinant at most `1-t`, including degeneracies.

This finite-vertex bound is not itself enough for the target. [TruncationConvexDeterminant.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationConvexDeterminant.lean#L48) proves that the absolute augmented-determinant sublevel set is convex in each point separately, and then inducts over slots to extend the bound to arbitrary points in the convex hull. Accordingly, `truncation_tuple_determinant_bound` at [TruncationMaximum.lean line 198](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationMaximum.lean#L198) covers every tuple of inscribed points.

Finally, `truncationSimplex_maximumInscribed` takes any actual affine simplex `T` contained in `K_t`, applies the tuple bound to `T.points`, and uses the actual simplex-volume interface. Finiteness is established before converting between extended and real volumes. [TruncationSimplexActualVolume.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationSimplexActualVolume.lean) also proves `|S_{t,i}|=(1-t)/d!`.

The implementation uses the column-`ℓ¹` bound and separate convexity. It does not need the preserved analytic note's stronger classification of all vertex patterns or all maximizing simplices.

## 6. Compute the literal own-centroid infimum

For the chosen ordered simplex, [TruncationCentroid.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationCentroid.lean#L79) derives the barycentric coordinates from reconstruction and the sum-to-one property:

\[
\beta_0(x)=\frac{1-\sum_jx_j}{1-t},\qquad
\beta_j(x)=x_j-t\,\mathbf1_{j=i}\,\beta_0(x).
\]

Every barycentric coordinate of the actual `S.centroid` is `1/(d+1)`. For a nonnegative excess parameter `a`, membership in the image-defined centroid dilation is proved equivalent to

\[
\beta_j(x)\ge-\frac a{d+1}\quad\text{for every simplex coordinate }j.
\]

The backward implication explicitly inverts the positive homothety factor `1+a`. On `K_t`, all coordinates are at least `-t`; at the distinct-ray lower vertex `t e_j`, `j ≠ i`, the coordinate corresponding to `e_i` is exactly `-t`. Such a ray exists for `d ≥ 2`. Thus the containment theorem at line 162 is precisely

\[
K_t\subseteq D_a(S_{t,i})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(d+1)t\le a
\qquad(a\ge0).
\]

The theorem at line 201 proves that the admissible set has actual least element `(d+1)t` and applies `IsLeast.csInf_eq`. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{excess}(K_t,S_{t,i})=(d+1)t.
\]

There is no empty-infimum convention, unbounded-infimum shortcut, alternate centroid, or optimized center in this step.

## 7. Preserve the literal exponent and close the target

[TruncationRationalDefect.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationRationalDefect.lean) writes the scalar formula as `t^(d-1) k_d(t)`. The numerator bracket satisfies

\[
d(d-1)-(d+1)(d-2)t-2t^d
=(d+1)(d-2)(1-t)+2(1-t^d)>0.
\]

The coefficient is continuous at zero, with

\[
k_d(t)\longrightarrow\frac{d(d-1)}{(d+1)^2}>0.
\]

[TruncationPower.lean](project/formal/Entry005/TruncationPower.lean#L15) proves that `α > 1/(d-1)` implies both `α > 0` and `(d-1)α-1 > 0`. It rewrites

\[
\frac{C\,e(K_t)^\alpha}{(d+1)t}
=\frac{C}{d+1}\,k_d(t)^\alpha\,t^{(d-1)\alpha-1}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The scalar helper accepts an explicit equality identifying its supplied function with the rational formula.
