# Which even sizes admit non-antipodal spherical designs at all odd indices up to a cutoff?

For $m\ge1$, put $T_m=\{1,3,\ldots,2m-1\}$. A spherical $T_m$-design here is a finite **set** of unit vectors on which every homogeneous harmonic of each indicated degree sums to zero. We require $X\ne-X$; the set need not span its ambient space. We determine the answer for every dimension $d\ge2$ and every cardinality $N$.

| parity of $N$ | on the circle ($d=2$) | in $\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge3$ |
|---|---|---|
| odd | exists iff $N\ge2m+1$ | exists iff $N\ge2m+1$ |
| even | exists iff $N\ge4m+2$ | exists iff $N\ge4m$ |

This answers the even-size existence question on [Munemasa's slides 10–14](https://researchmap.jp/munemasa/presentations/51226116/attachment_file.pdf), including the interval $2m+2\le N\le4m$. In that interval the **only** yes is $N=4m$ in ambient dimension at least three. The $4m$-point twisted two-latitude construction is **Misawa's**, already on slide 14. The overall minimum $2m+1$, the regular-polygon construction, the circle example formed by two polygons, and the infeasible six-point circle case for $m=2$ were treated by [Misawa, Munemasa and Sawa](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09471v6) ([journal](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2026.114978)). Our contribution here is the **uniform nonexistence range**, not those constructions or the overall minimum.

There is also a single-index strengthening: if $d\ge6$, the same table for $d\ge3$ holds when one imposes just the top harmonic index $k=2m-1$, not the lower ones. In either setting the smallest possible number of **unpaired** points whose span is more than a plane is exactly $4m$ in the dimensions covered by that statement. For the single-index problem we make no claim here in dimensions three through five.

## 1. A short dependence is planar

Write $k=2m-1$. Delete antipodal pairs, which cancel on odd polynomials. The remaining $r$ vectors represent distinct real projective directions. Under the simultaneous harmonic hypotheses all homogeneous degree-$k$ polynomials sum to zero: by harmonic decomposition their restrictions to the unit sphere are sums of the indicated harmonics. We claim that if $0<r\le2k+1$, the residual vectors all lie in a real two-plane. In ordinary projective terminology this is the small Cayley–Bacharach line bound (see [Picoco, introduction and Proposition 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.01665)); here is an elementary proof covering the single-index case as well.

First suppose no two-plane contains more than $k+1$ of the directions. Fix a target $x$. Group the other directions according to the two-plane they span with $x$. Each group has size at most $k$. Write them consecutively, pad to length $2k$ with blanks and pair positions $i$ and $i+k$. Any two actual vectors in a pair belong to different groups. Therefore some real linear form vanishes on them but not on $x$; the singleton and blank cases are easier. The product of the $k$ forms isolates the target, a contradiction. There must be a two-plane with at least $k+2$ directions. If there is any outsider, isolate it instead: one linear form removes the whole plane, and at most $r-(k+2)-1$ more forms remove the other outsiders. Pad to degree $k$ with forms nonzero at the target. This too is impossible.

For **one** harmonic index in $d\ge6$, first project by three mutually orthogonal complex null forms, for example $L=(x_1+i x_4,x_2+i x_5,x_3+i x_6)$ after a generic real rotation. Holomorphic polynomials in these forms pull back to harmonic polynomials, by the chain rule. All real independent subsets of size at most three remain complex independent: for each subset place its span among the first three coordinates to see that the relevant minor is not identically zero, then avoid finitely many proper analytic zero sets. Both separating products in the preceding paragraph now use complex forms in $L$, preserving all the required incidences and nonincidences. This proves the claim without postulating lower-degree balance. Once the residual vectors are on a real two-plane, every degree-$k$ polynomial there is the restriction of an ambient harmonic: in orthonormal coordinates substitute $x_1+i x_3,x_2+i x_4$ for its two variables.

## 2. A parity obstruction on a circle

Let $z_1,\ldots,z_r$ be the residual unit complex numbers on the circle. The power sums $p_j=\sum_i z_i^j$ vanish for all positive odd $j\le k$. This holds directly in the simultaneous case, even when we start on the circle; under the conclusion of §1 it also follows from degree-$k$ polynomial balance by homogenizing with $|z|^2$. Newton's identities now give $e_j=0$ for odd $j\le\min(k,r)$, where $e_j$ are the elementary symmetric functions. Indeed in $j e_j=\sum_{h=1}^j(-1)^{h-1}e_{j-h}p_h$ either $h$ is odd or $j-h$ is a smaller odd number. For unit numbers $e_{r-j}=e_r\overline{e_j}$. If $r$ is even and $r\le2k+2$, every odd index or its complement is at most $k$ (the midpoint $k+1$ is even). Thus the monic root polynomial is even, forcing antipodal pairs among the residual points. A nonempty residual without antipodes is impossible.

In dimension two the circle argument applies through $r=2k+2$; hence the smallest possible even total size is at least $2k+4$. In higher dimensions §1 applies through $r=2k+1$, so an even non-antipodal design needs at least $2k+2$ points. The same argument in §1 shows that a nonempty residual of any parity has at least $k+2$ points (the target-isolating construction already works for at most $k+1$ directions using one factor per unwanted direction). It also excludes any nonplanar residual of size less than $2k+2$.

## 3. Attainment at both parities

* A regular $(k+2)$-gon is an odd example: the odd frequencies present have absolute value at most $k$ and none is zero modulo $k+2$. Two disjoint generic rotations give an even example of size $2k+4$ on the circle, still without antipodes.
* In dimension three, take two regular $(k+1)$-gons at opposite nonzero heights $\pm h$ on the unit sphere, choosing a generic relative phase to avoid antipodes. For $k=1$ the two-gons mean two diametrically opposite **horizontal displacements** at each of the two heights. Averaging an odd monomial of degree at most $k$ on a single latitude kills all nonzero angular frequencies because their absolute values are less than $k+1$. The remaining constant Fourier term, if any, has even horizontal degree and odd height degree, so cancels between the two levels. This is the $2k+2=4m$ construction credited above; its unpaired set spans three dimensions.
* Add any number of fresh disjoint antipodal pairs to reach each larger size of the same parity. Embeddings into higher dimensions preserve the vanishing of the odd homogeneous moments. No assertion about vanishing at *only* these indices is intended.

The proof is analytic. The companion [arithmetic checks](verify-odd-harmonic-parity.py) verify parity coverage, Fourier cancellation on the two constructions, and the separating-pair bookkeeping for bounded test ranges, not the universal quantifiers in the theorem.
