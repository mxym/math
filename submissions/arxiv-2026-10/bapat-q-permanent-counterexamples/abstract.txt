Bapat conjectured that the inversion-weighted q-permanent of every non-diagonal Hermitian positive-definite matrix is strictly increasing on [-1,1]. We give two counterexamples to this assertion. First, we specify a 200-by-200 complex Hermitian positive-definite matrix with rational real and imaginary parts, together with a rational q0 in (0,1) for which P_q0 > P_1. Its rank-two Gram precursor is checked by two independent integer-arithmetic certificates. Second, we prove the existence of a non-diagonal integer real symmetric positive-definite matrix and rational 0 < q0 < q1 < 1 for which P_q0 > P_q1. This second proof uses equidistributed real linear forms, a conjugate-pair selector, a Gaussian ratio ordering estimate, and concentration under contiguous repetition. It supplies no numerical real matrix or explicit dimension bound. Both counterexample conclusions have complete Lean formalizations for the actual inversion-weighted polynomial, with publicly reproducible proof-closure verification records.
