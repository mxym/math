# Primary sources, prior art, and exact scope

1. Neranga Fernando and Bhitali Kousik, [*A further study of polynomial \(g_{n,q}\) over finite fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01037), arXiv:2606.01037v1 (31 May 2026, CC BY 4.0), [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01037v1), Question 6.7 (PDF p. 18; HTML lines 727–732). Its base-4 exponent, condition \(e\geq4\), and target \(\mathbb F_{4^e}\) are the scope here. Its (1.1) defines the formal polynomial. Appendix Table 1 lists \((1469,4;4)\) with reference to Question 6.7, which is the positive member verified here. The right-hand side printed in the question represents the right function on the target field; the exact formal difference is given in the proof.
2. Neranga Fernando and Xiang-dong Hou, [*From \(r\)-Linearized Polynomial Equations to \(r^m\)-Linearized Polynomial Equations*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.03162), [*Finite Fields and Their Applications* 37 (2016), 14–27](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ffa.2015.08.003). The preprint Appendix, Table 1 (PDF p. 10), already reports \((1469,4;4)\); its table's explicit degree range is \(e\leq6\). The existence of that positive triple is not claimed to be new.
3. André Weil, [*On Some Exponential Sums*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.34.5.204), *PNAS* 34 (1948), 204–207. The standard additive character bound is applied in characteristic 2 to an odd-degree polynomial after absolute-trace reduction.
