# Unrestricted triple extraction reduces to one globally capped projection

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Scope: reduction toward the k=3 case of [Janzer, Jin, Sudakov and Wu, §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). This is not an unrestricted solution. Their published §6 asks for full internal witnessing at retention Ω(m^-3); their Theorem 1.7 allows witnesses in the ambient family and does not establish the internal conclusion.

## From unrestricted triples to one capped projection

Let H be a finite simple 3-uniform family with E edges. Randomly independently 3-color the vertices and keep a largest tripartite portion; there is an outcome keeping at least 2E/9 edges. In the tripartite family repeatedly select an occupied pair of degree less than m and delete all triples containing it, assigning those triples to that pair. A pair can be used at most once. The residue has all occupied pair codegrees at least m and is therefore valid for nonempty kernels. Any repeated pair is directly witnessed by its fiber; for a singleton vertex, its nonempty link is bipartite with min positive degree at least m on both sides, hence has a matching of size m. Indeed, a maximal matching of size less than m would leave an uncovered vertex on either side (each side has size at least m); all its neighbors would have to be covered on the other side, impossible.

For the deleted edges, choose whichever of the three assignment types (AB, AC, BC) is largest, or retain the residue if it is largest. Within a piece assigned to AB pairs, an AB pair occurs in at most m−1 triples because its contribution was made only when that pair was used. There is no bound on the other projections. Thus an Ω(m^-3) nonempty-kernel bound for the one-projection-capped class, with cap m−1, gives universal Ω(m^-3) nonempty retention. A stronger sufficient bound is Ω(m^-2) nonempty for the one-projection-injective class, since the at-most-m−1 triples at each capped pair can be placed in distinct classes and one class has at least the average size.

To restore the empty kernel for unrestricted full witnessing, reserve a matching of m triples if possible. If its endpoint set removes no more than half the edges, apply the global Ω(m^-3) nonempty bound to the disjoint residual and restore the matching. Otherwise ≥E/2 edges meet at most 3m endpoints. If no matching exists, take the endpoints of a maximal matching with fewer than m edges, covering all edges instead. In these concentrated cases some vertex has degree ≥ E/(6m); use the Ω(m^-2) full graph extraction on its simple link and lift at that vertex. This includes the link's empty kernel as the singleton consisting of that vertex, and the extracted triple set has no empty intersection. The graph bound follows from the weighted Ω(m^-1) nonempty graph lemma (pass first to a random bipartite cut if needed) via reserving an m-matching unless a vertex with degree at least E_link/(4m) is already available; take its whole star if it has size at least m, and a singleton otherwise.

## Secondary split within the AB-injective class

Represent AB-injective triples as a simple bipartite AB graph with edge colors C. Iteratively delete all incident edges at any currently occupied AC or BC pair of degree less than m, or any currently active A or B vertex that sees fewer than m distinct colors. Charge to the object chosen for deletion. In the residue all pair kernels are witnessed by their fibers (AB pairs cannot repeat). Every nonempty A- or B-link has an m-matching from m distinct colors, using AB injectivity to ensure distinct endpoints on the other side; every nonempty C-link is bipartite with min positive degree at least m by pair cutoffs. So the residue is nonempty-valid. Triples charged to AC or BC pairs have that corresponding projection capped at m−1 in addition to the AB injectivity. Triples charged to A vertices have A-side color diversity ≤m−1, with no B-side bound, and vice versa. Charge uniqueness makes these statements valid for the final pieces without recomputing deletion order.

This reduces the stronger Ω(m^-2) AB-injective target to two subclasses up to symmetry: AB injective plus one other projection capped at m−1 and remaining projection arbitrary; or AB injective and one-sided bounded color diversity. The former is not in general the existing two-injective theorem: replacing the capped projection by injective classes costs m, even before dealing with unbounded remaining codegree. The latter is not the published-in-workbench two-sided diversity bound. The primary source §6 and the related adjacent §4 color-certificate methods were checked; the precise degree-peeling split is a workbench reduction, without a first-priority claim.

## Both nonempty peeling residues also witness the empty intersection

For the all-pair-heavy tripartite residue, every part has at least m active vertices. Greedily construct m disjoint triples: after fewer than m choices pick an unused A vertex; its link has at least m active B vertices so pick an unused one; that AB pair has at least m completions in C so pick an unused completion. For the secondary functional residue, the color diversity hypothesis implies at least m active colors. For each next unused color c, its bipartite color graph has minimum positive degree at least m (from both pair-codegree thresholds); pick an unused A vertex in its support then one of its unused B neighbors. In both cases the resulting matching witnesses the empty intersection. The matching-reserve argument is still needed to deduce the unrestricted full statement from a nonempty theorem on the charged pieces.

## Sharp degeneracy range, up to order \(m^2\)

The full-kernel conclusion for a [bounded coordinate](InternalSunflowersLowDegeneracy.md) can be sharpened without modifying its nonempty-kernel proof. With \(M=\max(L,m)\) when one part has maximum degree \(L\), its three branches give a singleton for \(E\le8mM\), a concentrated link of size \(\Omega(E/m^3)\), or a disjoint reserve and nonempty extraction of size \(\Omega(E/(mM))\). The minimum is \(\Omega(E/\max(m^3,mM))\). Random tripartition and assigning each deleted triple to its first low-degree vertex extend this bound to vertex degeneracy at most \(L\), with constant losses. For \(L=O(m^2)\) this gives \(\Omega(E/m^3)\); the existing \(m\)-degenerate example shows the order cannot improve uniformly on any class with \(m\le L=O(m^2)\).

Equivalently one can start with a low-vertex-degree peeling at threshold \(C m^2\), with any fixed \(C\ge1\), before taking the primary pair-codegree split. Edges charged to low-degree vertices admit the sharp full bound immediately; the remainder has minimum positive vertex degree \(>C m^2\) *before* splitting by pair charges. A degree guarantee on that original residual is not necessarily inherited by each charged piece.
