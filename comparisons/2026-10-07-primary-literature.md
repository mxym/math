# Primary-literature comparison: transport stability and quadratic moats

Checked 7 October 2026. This note compares theorem statements and dependencies; it is not a priority certificate or an independent proof audit. The first public manuscript snapshot is [release commit 4d718fe](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/4d718fe55d8b53eb8dd8634508c3297f0a978149). The transport comparison below concerns the version-2 statement, including its all-finite-second-moment target extension. Equivalent prior results would change attribution, not whether a complete manuscript can be made public.

## 1. Transport: exact scope of the statements being compared

The transport manuscript asserts, for a full-dimensional source with density proportional to exp(−κ|x|²/2−W(x)), κ>0 and W extended-valued convex:

- Centered Brenier potentials satisfy ‖Uμ−Uν‖L²(ρ) ≤ √(2/κ) W₂(μ,ν), for every pair of targets in P₂. The constant is independent of target radius and source diameter.
- For all targets in one fixed ball, the maps satisfy a uniform W₂^(1/3) bound. The constant may depend on the source's coordinate-slice functional. It does not depend on atom masses, number, or separation.
- For one fixed standard Gaussian in dimension at least two, three-atom targets rule out every larger uniform W₂ exponent.
- For fixed sites and two positive-mass Laguerre partitions, conditional laws σᵢ⁻,σᵢ⁺ with masses αᵢ,βᵢ satisfy Σᵢ√(αᵢβᵢ) exp(κW₂(σᵢ⁻,σᵢ⁺)²/8) ≤ 1, including unbounded sources.

### Directly checked predecessors

**OpenAI, Sharp One-Third Stability of Brenier Maps (2026).** The source theorem already establishes uniform W₂^(1/3) map stability for the uniform probability measure on an arbitrary compact convex body and all targets in a fixed compact set. Its sharpness example uses three atoms and a fixed cube. Thus the uniform-source specialization of the compact companion theorem overlaps exactly at the exponent and target-uniformity level; the exponent, finite-cell machinery, and three-plane construction are inherited. The proposed extension is the weighted strongly log-concave source estimate, radius-free potential constant, Gaussian sharpness adaptation, and all-P₂ potential extension. The uniform-source theorem alone does not supply them. [Pinned primary manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Sharp-One-Third-Stability-of-Brenier-Maps-September-25-2026/article.pdf).

**Divol–Niles-Weed–Pooladian, Theorem 4.3.** W₂^(1/3) map stability predates that source in a nondegenerate semi-discrete regime. Assumption S1 requires a Hölder density bounded above and positively below on convex compact support. S2 requires finite targets with positively lower-bounded atom weights. Constants also depend on atom count, separation, and angular geometry. This is an exact exponent match, but not target-uniformity, Gaussian-source, or all-P₂ potential equivalence. The degenerating three-atom sharpness family is outside a common nondegeneracy bound. [Primary text, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02855#S4).

**Mischler–Trevisan, Theorems 1.1–1.2, quadratic case.** They obtain W₁^(1/2) potential stability for sources whose density relative to a compact log-concave measure is bounded above and below. Their W₁^(1/6) map theorem requires two-sided Lebesgue density bounds on a bounded convex support. These are partial overlaps. Their potential source class permits bounded non-log-concave perturbations, which the present strong-source theorem does not. The present compact log-concave map theorem permits vanishing boundary density. [Primary version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.19337v3#S1.SS1).

**Letrouit–Mérigot, Theorem 1.4.** For ρ=e^(−U−F)dx with D²U≥κI and bounded F, compact targets have potential bound W₁^(1/2)(1+|log W₁|)^(1/2). With D²U≤κ′I, the map exponent is κ/(2κ′+7κ) in W₁; exponents below κ/(2κ′+6κ) are also available. For a Gaussian these are 1/9 and exponents below 1/8. This does not give the radius-free all-P₂ Lipschitz potential theorem. Conversely, arbitrary bounded F can destroy log-concavity and lies outside that theorem. [Primary version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04908v3#S1.SS1).

**Mérigot, Sharp stability of Brenier maps via quantitative regularity of potentials (2026): partial, indirect theorem verification.** The HAL full text was inaccessible and has not been independently checked. However, Han–Zhu Proposition 2.7 explicitly reproduces an estimate attributed to its Theorem 2.2: for compact bounded-density sources, the fourth power of the L² map difference is bounded by a constant times the dual potential pairing. Their introduction reports the resulting sharp W₁^(1/4) estimate. This establishes a concrete overlapping result, rather than bibliographic existence alone. It does not establish the full contents of Mérigot's paper, or exclude a stronger W₂ or Gaussian theorem there. [Han–Zhu, §2.5, Proposition 2.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23037#S2.SS5); [Mérigot bibliographic record](https://hal.science/hal-05616391).

**Metric warning.** A larger exponent in a different metric is not automatically a stronger theorem. On a set of diameter D, W₁≤W₂≤√(DW₁). Consequently W₁^(1/4) gives W₂^(1/4), while W₂^(1/3) gives D^(1/6)W₁^(1/6). These estimates need not dominate one another. The sharp W₁ quarter exponent and sharp W₂ third exponent are compatible.

**Regular-data Lipschitz results.** Caja-Lopez–Delgadino–Kitagawa Theorem 1.1 gives Lipschitz map stability for nondegenerate Hölder densities on uniformly convex regular domains. Their potential estimates with less regular densities use density discrepancies. These do not cover arbitrary atomic targets or supply the stated universal all-P₂ potential bound. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24232).

**Dependency, rather than priority, conclusion.** Conditional covariance reduction, Prékopa-type midpoint arguments, and convex-gradient interpolation have classical antecedents. The cell-law inequality is a specific synthesis of strong log-concavity, coupled midpoint domination, and the common-site cell partition; it should not be described as a new general transport-entropy principle. No exact cell-law or radius-free all-P₂ theorem match was established in the inspected papers. This is a limited comparison result, not evidence of absence from the literature. The Hilbert-space curve-lift statement is a consequence of the potential Lipschitz theorem, rather than an independent stability mechanism.

## 2. Quadratic orders: exact matches and genuine changes of scope

**OpenAI, Bounded-Step Walks on Gaussian Primes, Theorems 1.1–1.2.** The Gaussian special case is an exact overlap: for every fixed step bound, all connected components of the graph on Gaussian irreducibles have uniformly bounded cardinality, including axis primes and all associates. The source also proves a finite periodic sieve obstruction. Its geometric sampling and entropy argument are the present manuscript's acknowledged proof source. The new statement is not a new proof method. [Pinned primary manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Bounded-Step-Walks-on-Gaussian-Primes-September-26-2026/paper.pdf).

**Li–Miller–Popescu–Sarnecki–Wattanawanichkul.** Theorem 3.3 in arXiv version 2 excludes bounded-step unbounded prime walks in the first quadrant of Z[√2] only when they stay within a fixed distance of y=x/√2. Conjecture 4.1 asks the unrestricted bounded-step question. Since this ring is a UFD and the same coefficient-plane metric is allowed, the current manuscript's corollary would settle that conjecture if its proof is correct. Uniform component cardinality is stronger than merely excluding infinite walks. [Primary version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.07386v2).

**Prasad.** The paper concerns imaginary quadratic integer rings with unique factorization, computes finite moats using minimum spanning trees and Delaunay triangulations, and studies prime densities. It does not establish the uniform component conclusion for every step bound and all quadratic orders. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.2310).

**Khale, Theorem 1.1.** Quantitative prime-free discs in general imaginary quadratic fields are a different conclusion. Isolated holes do not force a planar path to terminate. This theorem does not imply the all-order component bound. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14006).

The substantive transfer obligations are the five-hypothesis planar sieve interface; principal split-prime supply in nonmaximal orders using ray class theory; and restoration of exceptional irreducibles despite infinite unit-associate orbits in real quadratic fields. None follows merely by naming the Gaussian theorem. The metric must be a full planar coefficient/Minkowski metric, not a single real embedding. In non-UFD orders, the vertex set is irreducibles, not just prime elements.

The finite quotient's zero-voltage cycle criterion is an elementary covering-graph mechanism, not a separate novelty claim. Combined with the existence of a successful sieve and exhaustive arithmetic-witness enumeration, it yields a computable bound for an integral quadratic model and finite symmetric coefficient-step set. This does not quantify the analytic thresholds, give practical complexity, or allow arbitrary noncomputable real metric data.

No all-quadratic-order equivalent was established in this focused check. The comparison does not certify priority. Gaussian formal artifacts, if available, concern the Gaussian theorem and do not automatically verify the order-arithmetic transfer or real-unit restoration.
