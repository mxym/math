\section{Two exact results for periodic translation-invariant quantum
max-flow}\label{two-exact-results-for-periodic-translation-invariant-quantum-max-flow}

Yongxian Zhang · November 2026 · Version 1.0.0

This working paper gives matching lower bounds for Gesmundo, Landsberg
and Walter's binary even-cycle result and equal-dimension four-cycle
result. It is an AI-assisted, internally checked research note, not an
external peer-review claim. Both proofs are elementary tensor
calculations; they arose from the Euclidean tensor geometry research
program but do not invoke its pricing theorems.

Source commits: 572f27d8af713ec591750c0157af41581685ecf7 and
32e4511386fb6f44248be5e02ec67fa0270eccbe.

\subsection{Exact alternating quantum max-flow for the binary periodic
cycle}\label{exact-alternating-quantum-max-flow-for-the-binary-periodic-cycle}

\textbf{Result.} For every integer \(d\ge 2\), in the
translation-invariant complex matrix-product-state model with physical
and bond dimensions both two, \[
  \operatorname{QMF}'(C_{2d},(\mathrm{odd},\mathrm{even}),2)=3\cdot 2^{d-2}.
\] Thus the upper bound of Gesmundo, Landsberg and Walter,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.09106}{\emph{Matrix product states and
the quantum max-flow/min-cut conjectures}}, Theorem I.5, is attained for
all lengths. The authors reported numerical tightness through \(d=10\);
this note supplies a matching lower bound for arbitrary \(d\). The
min-cut is \(2^d\) by the same theorem. This is a special case of
periodic translation-invariant quantum max-flow, not a general
max-flow/min-cut theorem.

\subsubsection{Witness}\label{witness}

Use the same two \(2\times2\) slices at every vertex,
\(A_i=x_i y_i^{\mathsf T}\), with
\(x_0=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}, x_1=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}, y_0=(1,2)^{\mathsf T}, y_1=(2,1)^{\mathsf T}\).
Put \(K_{ij}=y_i^{\mathsf T}x_j\), so
\(K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For odd
and even binary words \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)\) and \(v=(v_1,\ldots,v_d)\),
the periodic tensor entry is \[
 \operatorname{tr}(A_{u_1}A_{v_1}\cdots A_{u_d}A_{v_d})
 =\prod_{i=1}^d K_{u_i v_i}K_{v_i u_{i+1}},
 \qquad u_{d+1}=u_1.
\] In the alternating flattening the row at \(u\) is therefore
\(\bigotimes_{i=1}^d f_{u_i u_{i+1}}\), where the coordinates of
\(f_{ab}\) are \(K_{a v}K_{v b}\) for \(v=0,1\). Explicitly \[
 f_{00}=(1,4),\quad f_{01}=f_{10}=(2,2),\quad f_{11}=(4,1).
\] In particular \(f_{a0},f_{a1}\) are independent for each start bit
\(a\), and \(f_{10}=\tfrac25(f_{00}+f_{11})\) with \(f_{00},f_{11}\)
independent.

\subsubsection{Independent rows}\label{independent-rows}

For any fixed start bit \(a\), the \(2^k\) vectors obtained by taking
products of the \(f\)'s along all \(k\)-edge paths on the two-bit
alphabet starting at \(a\) form a basis of
\((\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes k}\). This holds for \(k=0\). For \(k>0\),
divide the paths by their first next bit \(b\). By induction their
suffix vectors form a basis for each of the two choices of \(b\), and
the independent first factors \(f_{a0},f_{a1}\) put the two resulting
spaces in direct sum.

Select the rows with \((u_1,u_2)\) equal to \(00,10,11\), a total of
\(3\cdot2^{d-2}\). Let \(U_{ab}\) be the span of their tails after the
first edge factor, with \(u_1=a,u_2=b\). These tails are paths of
\(d-1\) edges starting at \(b\) and ending at \(a\). Each group's tails
are independent by the path-basis result. Moreover
\(U_{00}\cap U_{10}=0\): these two groups partition the same fixed-start
basis (start 0) by the distinct final bits.

A linear relation among the selected rows gives
\(f_{00}\otimes z_{00}+f_{10}\otimes z_{10}+f_{11}\otimes z_{11}=0\),
with \(z_{ab}\in U_{ab}\). Independence of \(f_{00},f_{11}\) and the
displayed expression for \(f_{10}\) imply \(z_{00}=-(2/5)z_{10}\) and
\(z_{11}=-(2/5)z_{10}\). The first equation and the disjointness of the
two fixed-start subspaces give \(z_{10}=0\), and then all three tails
vanish. Independence inside each group kills every coefficient. The
claimed lower bound follows; with Theorem I.5 this proves equality.

The broader exact periodic problem is identified by Gesmundo, Lysikov
and Steffan,
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2212.09794v2\#S1.SS3}{\emph{Quantum
max-flow in the bridge graph}}. This argument arose from testing path
and cycle factorization in the Euclidean tensor geometry research
program; the actual proof is elementary linear algebra and uses no
Euclidean-pricing theorem.

\subsection{Exact translation-invariant alternating quantum max-flow on
a
four-cycle}\label{exact-translation-invariant-alternating-quantum-max-flow-on-a-four-cycle}

For all integers \(q\ge1\), when both bond and physical dimensions equal
\(q\), the maximum alternating flattening rank on a periodic four-cycle
with the same complex slice tensor at every site is \[
 q^2-\bigl(\tbinom q2\bmod 2\bigr).
\] This matches
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09106}{Gesmundo--Landsberg--Walter,
Theorem I.2}; their discussion reports numerical evidence for tightness
in general and gives exact attainment in square dimensions. The deficit
pattern also appears numerically in
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03717}{Hastings}. The proof here treats
all dimensions simultaneously.

Let \(F_{ab,cd}=\operatorname{tr}(A_aA_cA_bA_d)\), and let \(P\)
interchange the two indices in an index pair. Cyclicity of trace gives
\(FP=PF=F^{\mathsf T}\). Accordingly \(F\) is block diagonal in the
symmetric and skew pair spaces, with symmetric and skew-symmetric blocks
respectively. Their dimensions are \(q(q+1)/2\) and \(q(q-1)/2\). The
latter block has even rank, proving the upper bound.

For the lower bound take rank-one slices \(A_a=e_a y_a^{\mathsf T}\),
the row \(y_a^{\mathsf T}\) being row \(a\) of \(K=I+tX\) for a matrix
\(X\) with zero diagonal. Then \[
 F_{ab,cd}=K_{ac}K_{cb}K_{bd}K_{da}.
\] In the symmetric block the \(aa\)-rows and columns give the identity
at \(t=0\); couplings between them and distinct-pair indices are
\(O(t^2)\). For distinct pairs \(a<b\), the leading \(t^2\) term of the
diagonal entry, in normalized symmetric pair bases, is
\(2X_{ab}X_{ba}\): both arrangements \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\) contribute.
Off-diagonal entries among distinct pairs are \(O(t^3)\), since only
equal unordered pairs can make two or more cyclic steps diagonal.
Eliminating the equal-index block changes this leading term only at
\(O(t^4)\). Thus whenever all \(X_{ab}\ne0\) for \(a\ne b\), the
symmetric block has full rank for sufficiently small nonzero \(t\).

In normalized skew pair bases, the leading term is \(t^3B(X)\). Index by
oriented edges, with reversing an edge negating its coordinate. Equal
and disjoint pairs give zero at leading order. For different edges
oriented out of \(a\), substitution gives \[
 B(X)_{ab,ac}=X_{ab}X_{bc}X_{ca}-X_{ac}X_{cb}X_{ba}.
\] The matrix is skew-symmetric. We show it can have maximal even rank
for every \(q\).

Use the fixed matrix \[
Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&2&1&1\\1&0&3&1\\3&1&0&2\\1&4&3&0\end{pmatrix}.
\] The Pfaffian of \(Y-Y^{\mathsf T}\) is \(-7\). The upper-triangular
entries of \(B(Y)\), in order \(01,02,03,12,13,23\), listed row by row,
are \[
 (17,-2,17,-2,0);\quad (-7,17,0,-7);\quad(0,-2,-7);\quad(21,21);\quad(21).
\] The Pfaffian is \(-3682\), obtained by expanding along the first row.
Leading submatrices of \(Y\) give starting cases \(q=1,2,3,4\) of ranks
\(0,0,2,6\) for \(B\) (the \(01,02\) entry for \(q=3\) is \(17\)).

Suppose \(B(X_q)\) has maximal possible even rank \(r\), and replace
\(X_q\) by a block matrix on \(q+4\) vertices with \(X_q\) on old
vertices, \(LY\) on new vertices, and all cross entries equal to
\(\varepsilon\). In the principal skew submatrix we keep \(r\) old
internal edges supporting a nonsingular principal minor, all six new
internal edges and all \(4q\) mixed edges. A principal minor of order
\(r\) exists in every skew-symmetric matrix of rank \(r\): the
\(r/2\)-th wedge power of its alternating form is nonzero, and its
coordinate coefficients are the principal Pfaffians.

The two internal blocks have no coupling (the relevant edges are
disjoint), and are nonsingular for \(L\ne0\). On the mixed block orient
every edge from old to new. Entries from edges sharing an old endpoint
give, after dividing by \(\varepsilon^2\), one copy of
\(L(Y-Y^{\mathsf T})\) per old endpoint. Edges sharing a new endpoint
give entries independent of \(L\) after this division, and disjoint
entries vanish. Thus the scaled mixed block is nonsingular for
sufficiently large fixed \(L\), by the nonsingular leading block
diagonal. Couplings of mixed with internal edges are
\(O_L(\varepsilon^2)\) because each contributing triangle uses two cross
entries. Eliminating the internal blocks gives an \(O_L(\varepsilon^4)\)
Schur correction, so the whole retained principal matrix is nonsingular
for sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\). Its dimension is
\(r+6+4q\), which is maximal even rank at size \(q+4\), since the
dimension of the edge space increases by the even number \(6+4q\).

The existence of this leading skew minor gives a nonempty Zariski open
condition on \(X\) (the complement of the zeros of its nonzero
polynomial). The condition \(X_{ab}\ne0\) for every off-diagonal entry
is also nonempty and open, so their intersection is nonempty in the
affine space of zero-diagonal matrices. Fix \(X\) in the intersection.
In the skew block of \(F\), the minor of order \(r\) has nonzero
coefficient at \(t^{3r}\), and the symmetric block has a nonzero
determinant polynomial by the computation above. A sufficiently small
nonzero \(t\) makes both minors nonzero. This proves the matching rank
lower bound.

This addresses the four-cycle with identical slices and equal
dimensions, not arbitrary periodic lengths or heterogeneous dimensions.
The calculation uses rank-one tensor slices and linear algebra, not the
Euclidean mean-width pricing theorem.
