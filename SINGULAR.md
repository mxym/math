# Singular couplings in the spin-one Fateev–Zamolodchikov dynamical complex

Let \(s\ge1\) be the number of sites, and restrict to the momentum eigenspace \(\mu_s=(-1)^{s+1}\) of the ordinary or twisted shift. At twist \(\pi\), restrict to total magnetic parity even so the shift has \(s\)-th power one. Use the notation \(x,y\) for the real couplings in [Hagendorf §4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.0357), not the tensor-network twist ratio. The harmonic/singlet dimension on the light-like lines \(x^2=y^2\ne0\) is exactly one at every length for either twist. At the origin it is \(s+1\) for odd \(s\), \(s\) for even \(s\) at twist 0, and \(s\) for odd \(s\), \(s+1\) for even \(s\) at twist \(\pi\). The untwisted origin counts were already obtained in Hagendorf §4.2; the argument here also works at twist \(\pi\). Hagendorf §4.1.4 exhibited at least one light-like singlet at twist \(\pi\); the proof below gives the exact multiplicity at both twists.

## Light-like lines: diagonal Hodge density

In the Cartesian basis from the non-light-like result the local map is the cross-product embedding followed on the input by \(\operatorname{diag}(x+y,x-y,1)\), up to global phase. Rename the zero-coordinate vector \(h\), and let \(U\) be the plane spanned by the other two. On \(U\), \(q(v)=h\wedge Bv\), where \(B\) has the matrix of opposite off-diagonal nonzero entries (with nonzero product) because one nonzero coordinate lies in the extreme-weight plane and one is the middle Cartesian axis. Thus \(B\) has opposite nonzero eigenvalues. The twist \(\theta\) is \(-I\) on the extreme-weight plane and \(+1\) on the middle axis. It has \(\theta h=-h\) and anticommutes with \(B\) on \(U\). Choose an eigenvector \(a\) of \(B\), let \(b=\theta a\), and drop the common nonzero scalar of \(q\). Then
\[
q(h)=0,\qquad q(a)=h\wedge a,\qquad q(b)=-h\wedge b,\qquad
\theta(h)=-h,\quad\theta(a)=b,\quad\theta(b)=a.
\]
Here \(v\wedge w=v\otimes w-w\otimes v\). These are changes of basis and an overall scaling of the complex, not unitary spectral equivalences.

Declare \(h,a,b\) orthonormal in these coordinates. This changes no dimension: the \(Q\)-complex is conjugate to the original one, and its cohomology dimension, equivalently the Hodge kernel dimension, does not depend on the positive inner product used to form adjoints. The shift remains unitary since \(\theta\) acts by a signed permutation. Put \(\kappa(a)=1,\ \kappa(b)=-1\). By the periodic density formula [Hagendorf, equations (8),(9)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.0357), the Hamiltonian on the signed sector is the sum of identical ordinary neighbor densities and the seam density transported by the twist. Substituting this \(q\) in (9) makes its two-site density diagonal:
\[
hh\longmapsto 0,\qquad h\alpha,\alpha h\longmapsto 2(h\alpha),2(\alpha h),\qquad
\alpha\beta\longmapsto (2+2\kappa(\alpha)\kappa(\beta))\,\alpha\beta
\quad(\alpha,\beta\in\{a,b\}).
\]
Indeed \(q^\dagger q\) has eigenvalues \(0,2,2\). In a mixed pair \(h\alpha\) or \(\alpha h\), the off-diagonal from \(qq^\dagger\) cancels the nonzero negative mixed contraction, leaving the displayed 2. On a pair \(\alpha\beta\), \(qq^\dagger=0\); each negative mixed contraction contributes \(\kappa(\alpha)\kappa(\beta)\). Thus every local term is positive semidefinite, and its zero pairs are exactly \(hh,ab,ba\).

The ordinary neighbor constraints consequently restrict support to the all-\(h\) word and the two alternating particle words. At twist 0 the seam admits alternating words only for even length. Their \(\mu_s=-1\) eigenspace is one dimensional because rotation exchanges the two. The all-\(h\) word has shift \(+1\) and supplies the single odd-length class. At twist \(\pi\), the seam exchanges \(a,b\) on one of its sites, so it admits the alternating particle words only for odd length. Both \(\theta^{\otimes s}\) and the twisted rotation exchange those words, so their common \(+1\) eigenspace is one dimensional. The all-\(h\) word is globally invariant only for even length and has twisted shift \(-1\), supplying the single even-length class. This proves the assertion for \(s\ge3\).

For \(s=1\) at twist 0 the two independent images \(h\wedge a,h\wedge b\) leave one class. At twist \(\pi\) the incoming domain is zero and the length-one sector consists of \(p=a+b\), whose split \(h\wedge(a-b)\) has twisted shift \(+1\) at length two and hence projects to zero. At \(s=2\) at twist 0 the signed sector is \(\bigwedge^2V\) and the incoming image has dimension two. The outgoing map vanishes on that image by nilpotency, and on \(a\wedge b\) by cyclic cancellation of \(hab-ahb+hba-bha\), giving one class. At twist \(\pi\), put \(d=a-b\). The signed even sector at length two is \(\mathrm{Sym}^2\langle h,d\rangle\), and the incoming image is zero. The first-split map kills \(hh\); on \(hd+dh\) it gives \(hph-phh\), which has nonzero projection because the two terms differ by minus a twisted rotation; on \(dd\) it gives \(hpd-phd\), whose two terms have distinct rotation orbits. The outgoing rank is two since the two nonzero projections have different letter multisets. This gives one class also in both low lengths.

## Origin: graded antisymmetry fixes the whole kernel

At \(x=y=0\) let \(h\) be the middle magnetic state \(|1\rangle\), and call the two extreme states \(a,b\). The twist is \(+1\) on the bosonic middle state \(h\) and \(-1\) on each fermionic state \(a,b\). Hagendorf §4.2 gives the positive semidefinite local density \(I+\Pi\), where \(\Pi\) is the graded swap, acting by ordinary exchange except on two fermions, where it is minus the exchange. The seam term is a unitary conjugate, also positive semidefinite. For lengths \(s\ge3\), a state in the selected shift sector is harmonic exactly if every ordinary neighbor graded swap acts by \(-1\): necessity is positivity of each summand, and sufficiency for the seam follows by conjugating one ordinary neighbor condition by the twisted shift.

The ordinary adjacency constraints impose total graded antisymmetry. Two \(h\)'s force zero, since moving them together is invertible and exchange of equal bosons has eigenvalue \(+1\). With no \(h\), coefficients are symmetric in the \(s\) fermionic factors, giving dimension \(s+1\). With one \(h\), coefficients are symmetric in the \(s-1\) fermionic factors and alternate sign as the distinguished \(h\) position advances; dimension \(s\). All these vectors satisfy the appropriate ordinary neighbor conditions. At twist 0 the ordinary shift is \(+1\) on the first space and \((-1)^{s-1}\) on the \(h\)-position wrapping term, with sign \(-1\) on all other position shifts; hence for even \(s\) the second space has eigenvalue \(-1\), and for odd \(s>1\) no nonzero vector of the second space has the required eigenvalue \(+1\) (a move not across the seam negates the coefficient). Thus the odd and even untwisted counts are \(s+1\) and \(s\).

At twist \(\pi\), even magnetic parity requires an even number of fermions: no \(h\) if \(s\) is even, one \(h\) if \(s\) is odd. On the no-\(h\) space the twisted shift is \(-1\). On the one-\(h\) space at odd \(s\), moving a fermion across the seam cancels the alternating sign from advancing the \(h\) position; moving the sole \(h\) across the seam also has sign \(+1\) because the wrapping positions differ by \(s-1\) even. Thus these spaces have exactly the desired eigenvalues \(\mu_s\). The twisted odd and even counts are \(s\) and \(s+1\).

At \(s=1\), the untwisted \(Q_1\) sends the middle state to a nonzero two-fermion antisymmetric vector and kills both extreme states, giving dimension 2, while the twisted eligible middle state has zero projected image, giving dimension 1. At \(s=2\) untwisted, the signed sector is \(\bigwedge^2 V\), the image from length one has dimension 1, and the outgoing map is zero: splittings of \(h\) have equal opposite cyclic terms in length-three momentum \(+1\). This gives dimension 2. At twist \(\pi\) the signed even sector at length two is the ordinary symmetric two-fermion space of dimension 3, with no images from length one and no outgoing splittings. All small lengths thus obey the same formulas.
