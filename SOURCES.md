# Sources and scope audit

1. **Marshall Ball, Peter Crawford-Kahrl, Vladimir Podolskii, Morgan Prior, [On the Complexity of Symmetric Boolean Functions in Multiparty Private Simultaneous Messages, arXiv:2610.21216v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21216v2); [ECCC TR26-325](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/325/).** Introduction defines the divisibility-is-one convention on n input bits. §2.1 gives one local message per party, hidden finite shared seed, correctness on every tape, and equality of whole transcript laws. §2.2 defines total communication C, not just the maximum message alphabet or sum of logarithms. Theorem 2 and Corollary 16 in §3 give the support-private Ω(n log q) lower bound for all integers 2≤q≤n, with no primality hypothesis. A uniform-constant reading follows directly from Corollary 15/Theorem 14 and Lemmas 6–7: for every live set with at least q−1 parties, further fixing to zero leaves NOR on q−1 live parties, so the individual-message lower bound is Ω(log q). If q−1 is close to n, instead keep j=min(q−1,⌊n/2⌋) live bits; j is Ω(q) for 2≤q≤n and n≥2, and Lemma 7 applies to all live sets of size at least j, yielding Ω((n−j+1)log q) with bounded q absorbed by C≥n. Theorem 1/Corollary 15 handles q>n by NOR↔OR. §3.1 Theorem 18 explicitly assumes q prime and gives n⌈log₂ q⌉ bits via an additive field mask, not a prime-power theorem. The complement of the known Ishai OR scheme (§2.3 Theorem 12) already covers q>n. This note fills the quantitative composite-modulus case left by that comparison; it does not resolve their OR constant question, unrestricted symmetric-function complexity, or any lower bound beyond what is attributed here.

2. **Classical masked branching-program products.** Ball et al. v2 §6.3 Lemma 39 and Appendix A.4 give in full the standard permutation masking/endpoint membership construction going back to [Joe Kilian, Founding cryptography on oblivious transfer, STOC 1988](https://doi.org/10.1145/62212.62215); see also [Yuval Ishai, Randomization Techniques for Secure Computation, §4.1.3 Claim 4.13](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~tal/6261/SP20/reading/rand-survey.pdf), cited in Ball et al. for arbitrary group products, and §4.1.1 Claim 4.3 for additive field zero tests. Their proof uses a bijective gauge change of variables and a simultaneous uniform relabeling of a point and accepting set in the full symmetric group. For the singleton acceptance here, the projective version is precisely a two-orbit subgroup specialization of that idea, via the PGL₂ action on a projective line; retaining actual row and column lifts gives the explicit scalar-orbit simulator. Likewise [Ishai and Kushilevitz, ISTCS 1997](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/private-simultaneous-messages-protocols-with-applications/) is an early multiparty PSM branching-program reference, and their [ICALP 2002 paper, §§2.2, 4](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~tal/6261/SP20/reading/IKicalp02.pdf) develops field/determinant randomizers for counting branching programs (quadratic output size in branching-program size). Those are predecessors for the technique, not a novel masking primitive here. The parameter choice and quantitative composite-MOD bound are the point.

3. **Justin Holmgren and Kewen Wu, [Improved Lower Bounds for Decomposable Randomized Encoding, arXiv:2609.18020v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18020v1), Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4.** They characterize constant-alphabet symmetric perfect DRE via bounded period, citing the general construction for any period a with alphabet at most a!. Thus their qualitative O(n) for fixed composite q already applies. The q-dependent upper here is O(log q) bits per party instead of O(log(q!)), and concerns divisibility and specified sparse tests, not every periodic predicate at that communication.

4. **Symmetric-function upper bounds checked.** [Shinagawa–Eriguchi–Satake–Nuida, Private simultaneous messages based on quadratic residues](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-023-01279-5), §§1.2–1.3, 3, discusses PSM for Boolean mod-p branching programs with O(log p · n · a²) communication for size a, and gives O(n²) for arbitrary symmetric Boolean functions via a different quadratic-residue construction. [Eriguchi–Shinagawa, Efficient Multiparty Private Simultaneous Messages for Symmetric Functions](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1815/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf), [Eriguchi, On the Communication Complexity of PSM and CDS for Symmetric Functions](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1959/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf), and [Nuida, Improved Private Simultaneous Messages Protocols for Symmetric Functions with Universal Reconstruction, §5](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/992.pdf) study broader symmetric/universal-reconstruction models. [Ideal Private Simultaneous Messages Schemes and Their Applications, ITCS 2026, §1.1.3 and Theorem 6](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol362-itcs2026/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.76/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.76.html) formalizes two-party ideal PSM using value-fibre orbits and includes iterated group products and generalized index/inner product. We found no same quantitative composite-divisibility statement in these sources. This is a checked literature comparison, not an assertion of priority against every equivalent unstated consequence of group-product garbling.

5. **Field size.** [Triantafyllos Xylouris, On the least prime in an arithmetic progression and estimates for the zeros of Dirichlet L-functions](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078), Acta Arithmetica 150.1 (2011), 65–91, introduction and Theorem 1.1, is a primary reference for the Linnik bound used with residue a=1 and modulus m (coprime automatically). No optimized exponent is needed. This is an unconditional prime-size result, not a bound on finding the prime in time polynomial in the bit length of m.
