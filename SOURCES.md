# Source map and prior-art scope

1. René Rodríguez-Aldama, Ajla Šehović, Enes Pasalic and Sadmir Kudin, [On different notions related to APN mappings, arXiv:2609.22394v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22394v3), version of 27 October 2026. [Definition 3.1 and §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22394v3) define breaking, strongly non-normal functions and sum-freedom; Open Problem 3 at the end of §3.2 asks the consecutive-order implication for \(k>3\). The preceding \(k=3\) statement for APN maps uses that APNness is order-two sum-freedom. The proof here resolves the asked implication and gives its stronger affine-span conclusion uniformly for \(k\ge3\) in arbitrary output dimensions. The local argument for a 2-flat speaks only of its hyperplanes, not an extension of the source's global \(r\ge2\) definition to order one.
2. Claude Carlet, [Two Generalizations of Almost Perfect Nonlinearity](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00145-025-09538-5), J. Cryptology 38, article 20 (2025), Definition 1 and §3, introduces the sum-freedom and strong non-normality terminology and examines sum-free restrictions. [On the vector subspaces ... over which the multiplicative inverse function sums to zero](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-024-01531-6), Des. Codes Cryptogr. 93 (2025), 1237–1254, concerns the inverse function and gives related consecutive-order observations about its zero-sum subspaces. Those do not give the present affine-image implication for arbitrary functions.
3. Xiang-dong Hou and Shujun Zhao, [Further Results on Sum-Freedom of Binary and \(q\)-ary Functions, arXiv:2609.31489v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31489v2), studies existence, the coding characterization, and Welch and other functions. [Counting zero-sum subspaces for the multiplicative inverse function, arXiv:2610.06445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06445) addresses zero-sum counts for a fixed function, rather than the one-flat arbitrary-function hyperplane rank and image condition.

No absolute priority claim is made.

Only the finite-geometric property is considered here: the result does not make claims about a real cryptosystem or about the paper's other open questions.
