# Sources and scope comparison

Search checked 2026-11-28 UTC. The underlying problem is from
Shu Liu, Jiang Li and Wenqi Huang, [Explicit Constructions of
MDS Cyclic Codes from Rational Function Fields, arXiv:2611.32165v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.32165v1#S1.ThmThm1),
§1.2, **Open Problem 1.1**, HTML lines 77–79. The question asks
for existence/nonexistence conditions over \(\mathbb F_q\) at
arbitrary simple-root length \(n\nmid q\pm1\), with “nontrivial”
meaning \(2\le k\le n-2\). The criterion here covers only \(k=3\)
and \(k=n-3\) at even length. Compare their §3 for \(n\mid q\pm1\)
and Theorem 4.1 for the separate repeated-root case. The public
version history showed only v1.

| M. Amin Shokrollahi, [On Cyclic MDS-Codes](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-59663-6_11), in *Coding Theory and Cryptography*, pp. 202–213 (2000). [Full chapter, scanned with publisher permission](https://yetanothermathblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/shokrollahi_mds-codes.pdf). | §1 states length \(q-1\); §2, p. 204, Proposition 1 explicitly restates the odd-characteristic RS conclusion at block length \(q-1\). The determinant normalization to the geometric-sum polynomial and injectivity are already there, over \(\mathbb F_q^\times\setminus\{1\}\). Our proof uses that same elementary calculation on a different root subgroup, without upgrading to a permutation of the whole base field. Neither \([8,3]_{11}\) nor \([16,3]_{23}\) has length \(q-1\), so they do not contradict Proposition 1. |
| Leo Storme and Hendrik Van Maldeghem, [Cyclic arcs in PG(2,q)](https://cage.ugent.be/~hvm/artikels/33.pdf), *J. Algebraic Combin.* 3, 113–128 (1994), DOI [10.1023/A:1022454221497](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022454221497). | §4, Theorems 4.4–4.7 and their proofs give the geometry of **complete** cyclic even arcs: two concentric ellipses, \(8\mid n,\ q=-1\bmod4\), and the representation as powers of an element of \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\) with norm \(-1\) in the two-ellipse case (Theorem 4.7, proof Part 3). Our even code is this same orbit, now including incomplete arcs without a completeness assumption. The paper's §2.3 (2), Remark 4.3, and the examples following it already give the length-8 arc over 11, length-16 arc over 23 and length-24 arc over 59. The exact arithmetic obstruction and point/minor counts are not claimed in those statements. |
| G. Korchmáros, L. Storme and T. Szőnyi, [Space-filling subsets of a normal rational curve](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-3758(96)00063-8), *J. Statist. Plann. Inference* 58, 93–110 (1997), Theorem 2.4; and G. Korchmáros and A. (8). | Ellipse-subgroup secant covering and the \(q^{3/4}\) scale are earlier. The latter paper explicitly recalls/refines the former in §4 before Theorem 4.15 and gives the chord-or-tangent criterion \(q+1-2(s-1)^2\sqrt q-2s>0\). Neither the order nor the use of a genus-\((s-1)^2\) curve is the new claim. Here the two opposite norms and the symmetry send every exceptional point to a genuine bad minor, allowing the version with no subtraction and a direct relation between the point count and the finite-field gcd degrees. We checked the 1997 result's public abstract and the 2009 primary-source description, not an independently accessible copy of the full 1997 text. |
| Tatsuya Maruta, “On the existence of pseudo-cyclic MDS codes of dimension three,” *Atti Sem. Mat. Fis. Univ. Modena* 41, 457–471 (1993); “Cyclic and pseudo-cyclic MDS codes of dimension three,” ibid. 43, 529–533 (1995), [author bibliography](https://mars39.lomo.jp/opu/papers.html), nos. 9 and 12; [Cyclic arcs and pseudo-cyclic MDS codes](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(96)00334-2), *Discrete Math.* 174, 199–205 (1997); [On the Existence of Cyclic and Pseudo-Cyclic MDS Codes](https://doi.org/10.1006/S0195-6698(97)90000-7), *Eur. J. Combin.* 19(2), 159–174 (1998). | Found and checked the bibliographic and accessible abstract records; the 1998 abstract announces finite existence tables for small alphabets and dimensions 3–5. The 1995 and 1998 full papers were not retrievable during this check. Consequently this note makes no absolute priority claim for any individual small parameter, nor asserts that the older complete-arc or pseudo-cyclic literature lacks other formulations of its necessary congruences. |
| K. Momihara and Q. Xiang, [Cyclic arcs of Singer type and strongly regular Cayley graphs over finite fields](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.10959v2), Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2, pp. 8–9. | Already gives the odd Singer-orbit large-characteristic result, citing Maruta and Szőnyi and making the sufficient cutoff explicit. The odd auxiliary branch in this note is not claimed as a new existence result. |
| Can Xiang and Chunming Tang, [Non-RS type cyclic MDS codes over finite fields via cyclotomic field reduction, arXiv:2604.06822v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06822v3), §§3.1–3.3, Theorems 4, 6, 9 and Tables 1, 4. | General complex Fourier-minor norms and exceptional characteristics are established. Their reduction construction works over a field containing the primitive root. The small support \(\{0,1,3\}\) of length 8 and the exclusions \(2,3\) occur already in Table 4. Our formula replaces the list of all minor norms by one pair of degree-\(n/4\) polynomials, and tests the descended cyclic code directly over any \(\mathbb F_q\) satisfying the residue condition. The small length-8 norm computation is a check, not priority. |
| Li Zhu and Hongfeng Wu, [Equal Hamming Weights at Fixed Root Order and Applications to Constacyclic MDS Codes, arXiv:2611.00637v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00637v1), Proposition 7.9, §§7.2–7.4. | The exact general Schur-minor norm iff in Proposition 7.9 applies to an irreducible generator; an odd trace-orbit cyclic code has such a generator in its dual. The even representative's dual instead has a **reducible** cubic generator (a fixed factor and a quadratic factor). We do not claim novelty for the odd exact test, nor for cyclotomic norm reductions. |
| Gaojun Luo, Xiwang Cao, Shitao Li and Yang Li, [On the maximum length of cyclic MDS codes, arXiv:2610.29810v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29810v1), Lemma 2.8, §3 and Theorem 1.1. Guanghui Zhang, [Length Bounds for Irreducible Cyclic MDS Codes, arXiv:2610.28047v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28047v1), §§2–3. | Fourier-support balance and the general length-bound methods, including (for Luo et al.) the \(n\mid q^2-1\) case of the \(n\le q+1\) bound. Zhang treats irreducible cyclic codes, not the even fixed-plus-quadratic orbit. They are not asserted to supply or to contradict the quarter-length exact polynomial test. |
