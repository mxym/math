# Sources and precise boundaries

* Rodrigo San-José, [*Common zeros of homogeneous polynomials and the weight hierarchy of projective Reed–Muller codes*, arXiv:2611.08203v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08203v2), revised 9 November 2026 (v1 was 5 November). Proposition 6.6 and Theorem 6.7 give the formula and constructive upper; Conjecture 6.9 asks for equality for all \(q,m,d,r\). Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 1.1 in v1) and Corollaries 4.2 and 5.2 give the low- and high-degree input ranges. Remark 6.13 explicitly confirms all the \(q=3,m=3\) layers, in particular using the GHWs algorithm for \(d_7(C_4(3))=16\); this work does not claim that value as new. The matching proofs treated in this note extend the verified parameter family to \(q=3,m=4\), not the general conjecture.
* The same author's v2 Remark 6.13 and reference [37] link his [published projective GHW tables](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24020768). The [pinned release v1.0.0, `docs/data/q3_m4.json`](https://github.com/RodrigoSanJose/ghw-tables/blob/ab10b60819c81517022ff8e96ebd6f0dd6614681/docs/data/q3_m4.json) (Git blob `607e1b851203a5ddc44984722044ca4000b60fbf`) covers \(q=3,m=4\) **with bounds**, not a fully exact hierarchy. The unequal intervals there are \(d=3:r=11\); \(d=4:r=5,11,12,15,21,32,38\); \(d=5:r=21\); and \(d=6:r=7,31,32\). In particular \(d_{31}(C_6(4))\) is listed as \([44,45]\), with the lower bound from SJ24, Theorem 7 (preprint Theorem 5.5), and the upper bound from SJ26, Theorem 6.7. Those tables already propagate bounds using augmented-dual inequalities. The proof here verifies the 12 remaining entries; the second-incidence-moment step is needed for just one of them after the least-intersection refinement and the first moment. Neither checker requires the external data.
* Rodrigo San-José, [*A recursive construction for projective Reed–Muller codes*](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05072), IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 70(12):8511–8523 (2024). Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.3 give the projection and dimensions; Theorem 5.5, notably the two separate bounds before Eq. (6), gives exactly the four support terms in (2). Tables 3 and 6 treat the \(q=3,m=3\) case. The note attributes the splitting inequality to that work; the added step is to apply its two bounds simultaneously over all hyperplanes and use (3), not to claim a new recurrence for one hyperplane.
* Petra Heijnen and Ruud Pellikaan, [*Generalized Hamming weights of \(q\)-ary Reed–Muller codes*](https://ruudp.win.tue.nl/paper/29.pdf), IEEE TIT 44(1):181–196 (1998), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.651015), Theorem 5.10. This is the exact **affine** formula used in both checkers, also restated as Theorem 5.4 of the 2024 projective paper. Anders Bjært Sørensen, *Projective Reed–Muller codes*, IEEE TIT 37(6):1567–1576 (1991), Theorem 2, supplies projective dual and full-code cases. Victor K. Wei, [*Generalized Hamming weights for linear codes*](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.133259), IEEE TIT 37(5):1412–1418 (1991), Theorem 3, supplies duality and establishes the wiretap-II interpretation.
* Close distinct results: [San-José's GHWs package](https://github.com/RodrigoSanJose/GHWs) computes weights for general matrices, rather than being a published assertion of the complete \(q=3,m=4\) table; our scripts do not depend on it. The AFTD entry [dimension reduction](https://rainwangphy.github.io/aftd-platform/d/prm_ghw_dimension_reduction_le/) verifies 2026 Lemma 6.10's one-sided inequality, not the equality cases proved here. [Nardi–San-José, *Structure of weighted projective Reed–Muller codes*, arXiv:2603.24397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24397) gives related weighted-code recursive bounds, not this complete hierarchy. [Ramkumar–Vajha–Kumar 1806.02028](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02028) is a binary exact-degree multilinear convention distinct from the standard projective Reed–Muller code (see [Beelen–Datta–Ghorpade, Remark 7.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01683)); it is not used here.

The comparison searched the paper identifier, its follow-ups, and the exact ternary length 121, degrees 3 and 6, rank 11 / rank 31, as well as additive-constant quadratic dual variants, through 28 November 2026. The degree-3 gap in the *direct* recurrence is an alternate proof, not a claim of a value inaccessible from previous inputs: combining the degree-5 outputs after the least-intersection mean refinement with Wei duality also suffices; the raw 2024/2026 comparison alone has a degree-5 gap at rank 21. The degree-6 dual is the degree-2 code augmented by the all-one vector, not the previously solved unaugmented quadratic code. We did not find the complete 121-point profile or the degree-6 incidence obstruction reported in the sources checked; this is bounded evidence, not an absolute priority claim.
