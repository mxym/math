A complete symbolic counterexample to the old isolated lemma:
Take s0=1, s1=2, G=1, k=0, p=1/24. The prescribed sampling construction
has m=5, B0=6, D=12, eta=1/24. Choose b=6913, so
p*eta*(b-1)=12 and lambda=6. Choose finite d and g satisfying the original
depth and unstable-density constraints, choose L>=24, and let T be the
resulting finite template span at U=4. All these choices are independent
of the initial occupied bin, since G and the gap bounds are fixed.
Choose an integer R>U+T and A={2^(-j):j>=R}. This configuration has
G=1, J=R. A permitted sample has j1=R+1, j_(n+1)=j_n+5 and
a_n=2^(-j_n), so z_n=j_n. Every s in [1,2] has
w_n(s)=s*z_n >= z_n > U+T. There are therefore no active inputs anywhere
in the template. A stable center exists because unstable density is <p<1.
On the positive-mass exposure atom C where every center selector is zero,
the center first defaults at the root. Every local test fails vacuously,
and P(C intersection miss)=P(C)>P(C)*exp(-6*L_d).
All old stipulated numerical restrictions hold apart from the missing
origin-versus-first-sample restriction, which was not yet stipulated.
