5. What is genuinely sharp, and what remains open
The accompanying complete notes isolate different hypotheses.
   • halfmass.md proves that the cone-specific power 2/3, its near-half-mass tail power two, and the
     contact-slab area power two are sharp for actual cone laws in every fixed d ≥ 3. The bodies are
     a doubly truncated three-cube times intervals, already in John position.
   • matching.md proves that the improved power 1/2 is sharp for bounded laws with determinant
     mass bounded below, even over every choice of basis and every line/plane decomposition. Its first
     obstruction is an arbitrary even law. The separate global-next.md strengthens the sharpness
     statement to actual cone laws and proves a geometric all-product distance obstruction for that
     family.

   • geometry-next.md proves that the cap exponent 1/d is sharp under volume-only information.
     With the stronger off-block normal measurement, truncated cubes instead exclude powers
     above 1/(d − 1). The general recovery theorem with power 1/(d − 1), now proved directly in
     geometry-rigidity.md, closes this intermediate geometric gap. It is sharp for the stronger normal
     input.
These sharpness examples do not establish optimality of (8): independent losses need not be simulta-
neously sharp on the same cone law. They rule out improving the first two isolated lemmas under
their present hypotheses, and explain why a further gain should retain more geometry or couple the
stages.

3. Robust balanced equality and the cone-specific half-mass gate
Lemma 3.1. For balanced normalized coefficients as above,

                                          D(t) ≥ min{t4 /2, g/4}.                                   (3.1)

Proof. On the capped simplex {si ≥ 0,            si = 1, si ≤ 1/2}, the convex function Eϵ | ϵi si | is

at most one half by the v3 inequality. Let y = (1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 0, . . .), whose value is 3/8. Set
α = min(4t4 , 2g). If positive, α < 1, and (t−αy)/(1−α) remains in the capped simplex: nonnegativity

construction when the minimum in (3.1) is g/4, and the second otherwise. This proves the distance

7. Comparison with primary cone-volume and stability literature
The class Ed is the familiar class of affine Cartesian products of centrally symmetric intervals and
planar bodies, called Weil bodies in Saroglou, On the shape of a convex body with respect to its
second projection body, pp. 2–4. That paper relates the class to Weil’s polytopal classification for
homothety under the second projection-body operator and gives a three-dimensional zonoid endpoint
with symmetric-cylinder equality (Theorem 1.2). These use a different functional from a(K); the
common extremal class alone does not make their statements equivalent to entry005 v4’s equality
theorem or Theorem A here.
The relevant antecedent is Böröczky–Lutwak–Yang–Zhang, The logarithmic Minkowski problem,
JAMS 26 (2013), 831–852. Its subspace concentration condition and complementary-subspace clause
characterize existence of symmetric cone-volume measures. They do not supply the near-short-circuit
estimate (3.4), a quantitative matching into dimensions at most two, or the present invariant’s
endpoint.
Böröczky–Henk, Cone-volume measure and stability, Adv. Math. 306 (2017), 24–50, Theorem 1.1
gives complementary-factor stability with containment exponent 1/(5d) from almost saturated cone
mass on an exact proper subspace; its constants and smallness threshold are stated as dimension-
dependent, rather than numerical formulas. Its U-functional stability targets parallelotopes. Our
equality class includes all symmetric planar factors, and our hypothesis is determinant magnitude, not
the U-functional’s independence indicator. Thus that theorem is not an equivalent endpoint result.
The exact-subspace premise cannot be inferred here: for even integers p → ∞, smooth strictly convex
ℓp balls converge to the cube and have a → 1/2, yet their cone-volume measures give zero mass to
every proper linear subspace. On each open coordinate octant the Gauss map is a diffeomorphism
with positive curvature; the omitted coordinate-zero parts have surface measure zero, proving absolute
continuity. Our method instead produces an expected distance to blocks and recovers an actual
product by Lemma 5.1.
Böröczky–De, Stable solution of the logarithmic Minkowski problem in the case of hyperplane
symmetries, J. Differential Equations 298 (2021), 298–322, obtains quantitative inverse cone-volume
estimates under Coxeter symmetry and a tube concentration margin. The reducible-group margin
degenerates at concentration on a proper group-invariant product block, and the symmetry hypothesis
does not cover arbitrary symmetric planar factors. Their anisotropic-squeeze example also explains

                                                       16
Stronger symmetric projection stability                                                         mxym


why normalization is necessary. Abdallah–Mérigot, On the reconstruction of convex sets from random
normal measurements, gives a surface-area inverse estimate of order 1/d with area and rotundity
control. Cone-volume data additionally contains the unknown support function; Lemma 5.1 avoids
solving that inverse problem.
Böröczky–De also discuss volume-normalized unconditional bodies obtained by truncating vertices of
a cube (published version, p. 302). This provides related geometric context. Theorem B independently
computes the upper-end scalar deficit 1/2 − a(Kt ) and proves a distance lower bound against every
affine product of symmetric one- and two-dimensional bodies. A comparison with one fixed cube
would not establish that full-class lower bound.
Saroglou’s Volumes of projection bodies of some classes of convex bodies is a primary projection-body
comparison. Only its publication record and abstract were available; a full-text comparison remains in-
complete. The recent Liu–Xiong–Yang mixed-volume stability paper, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2026.110886,
is relevant to broader mixed-volume stability questions. No theorem from either of these two papers
is imported as a dependency. The present literature comparison is confined to the primary statements
described above.

A sharp square-root matching theorem for bounded laws
This note strengthens the matching lemma in the completed upper-end stability supplement. It is

Statement and conventions
Let d ≥ 3, let ν be a Borel probability measure supported in the Euclidean unit ball, and let
X1 , . . . , Xd+1 be independent samples. Write

                                               A = E| det(X1 , . . . , Xd )|.

                           A ≥ a > 0,     Eϕ ≤ ε,      q = 4(d + 1)ε/a.

Theorem 1 (square-root matching). There are a basis matrix U whose columns lie in the support
of ν, and a partition of its coordinates into singletons and pairs, such that the union W of the resulting
coordinate blocks satisfies
                                                       √                   √
                           | det U | ≥ a/2,     ∥U ∥ ≤ d,      ∥U −1 ∥ ≤ 2 d/a,

                                                                      √
                                           E dist(U −1 X, W) ≤ 5d2 a−2 q.                                                      (1)
