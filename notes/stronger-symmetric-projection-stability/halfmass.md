# Sharpness of the cone-specific half-mass gate

7 October 2026. This note concerns the short-cofactor gate in Sections 2–3
of the completed upper-end stability manuscript. It does not modify that
manuscript or assert an improved stability exponent.

## Result

For every fixed dimension \(d\ge3\), the estimate
\[
 \mathbb E\phi\le F_d\delta^{2/3}
\]
has the best possible power under its stated hypotheses, even among
centrally symmetric polytopes already in symmetric John position. Here
\(\delta=1/2-a(K)\), and \(\phi\) is the fourth-largest absolute horizontal
cofactor of \(d+1\) independent cone-law samples. Neither the corner-slab
area power two nor the weighted near-half-mass tail power two can be improved
without adding hypotheses or changing the intermediate quantity.

The obstruction is an actual cone law. It does not use a freely prescribed
even law on a polar boundary.

## 1. A product of a truncated three-cube with intervals

For \(0<t\le1\), define
\[
 H_t=[-1,1]^3\cap\{|x_1+x_2+x_3|\le3-t\},\qquad
 K_{d,t}=H_t\times[-1,1]^{d-3}.
\]
The interpretation in dimension three is \(K_{3,t}=H_t\). The unit ball is
contained in \(K_{d,t}\): its first-three-coordinate sum has absolute value
at most \(\sqrt3<2\le3-t\). The body is contained in the cube and hence in
\(\sqrt d B_2^d\). Its coordinate-facet contacts \(\pm e_i\), with weights
\(1/2\) for each sign, give the symmetric John identity
\(\sum_i(e_ie_i^{\mathsf T})=I\). Thus the unit ball is its John ellipsoid;
there is no hidden change of normalization in the cofactor calculation.

Put
\[
 Z=24-t^3,\qquad p=\frac{8-t^2}{Z},\qquad
 r=\frac{(3-t)t^2}{Z},\qquad q=\frac1{3-t}.
\]
The cone law of \(H_t\) has three direction pairs \(\pm e_i\), each with
total probability \(p\), and one pair \(\pm q(e_1+e_2+e_3)\), with total
probability \(r\). Signs within each pair are uniform. In particular
\(3p+r=1\).

Indeed, \(|H_t|=8-t^3/3\). Each coordinate facet has area
\(4-t^2/2\) and support height one, giving pair cone mass \(8-t^2\).
Each new triangular facet has area \(\sqrt3t^2/2\), support height
\((3-t)/\sqrt3\), and scaled normal \(\pm q(e_1+e_2+e_3)\).
Its two-sign pair cone mass is \((3-t)t^2\). The normalization is
\(3|H_t|=Z\).

The product cone law is supported on the same four direction pairs in the
first three coordinates, together with \(\pm e_4,\ldots,\pm e_d\). Its
pair probabilities are
\[
 P=\frac3d p\quad\text{for each of the first three axes},\qquad
 R=\frac3d r\quad\text{for the diagonal pair},\qquad
 w=\frac1d\quad\text{for every remaining axis}.
\]
To verify the dimension factors directly, a facet lifted from the
three-dimensional factor has its area-times-height and total body volume
both multiplied by \(2^{d-3}\); division by \(d|K_{d,t}|\) instead of
\(3|H_t|\) multiplies the corresponding normalized mass by \(3/d\).
Every interval-axis pair has area-times-height \(|K_{d,t}|\), and therefore
normalized mass \(1/d\). This argument uses ordinary facet cone-volume
mass and does not assume that an arbitrary discrete measure is realizable.

## 2. Exact fourth-cofactor expectation

Call the unsigned direction pairs types. The \(d+1\) types span dimension
\(d\), and their unique linear dependence is supported on the first three
axes and the diagonal. A \(d+1\)-tuple with fewer than \(d\) distinct types
has all horizontal cofactors zero. A tuple with exactly \(d\) distinct types
has one repeated parallel direction. If its rank is \(d\), its kernel is
generated by the relation between those two columns and has just two
nonzero cofactors; if its rank is smaller, all cofactors vanish.

Consequently \(\phi>0\) occurs exactly when all \(d+1\) types occur. On
that event the absolute cofactors are
\[
 1,\ q,\ q,\ q,\ \underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{d-3},
\]
regardless of the independent signs. Since \(q\le1/2\), their fourth-largest
member is \(q\). The event probability is
\[
 \Pi=(d+1)!P^3Rw^{d-3}
      =\frac{81(d+1)!}{d^{d+1}}p^3r.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E\phi
  =\frac{81(d+1)!}{d^{d+1}}
    \frac{(8-t^2)^3t^2}{(24-t^3)^4}
  \sim\frac{(d+1)!}{8d^{d+1}}t^2.}                  \tag{2.1}
\]

## 3. Exact deficit

For completeness this deficit can be computed without invoking the
dimension-weighted product identity. The determinant expectation is
\[
 A=d!w^{d-3}P^2(P+3Rq)
   =\frac{27d!}{d^d}p^2(p+3rq).
\]
The first term uses all \(d\) axis types; the three remaining nonzero terms
replace one of the first three axes by the diagonal. Replacing any other
axis by the diagonal gives zero determinant.

Tuples with fewer than \(d\) types have zero cofactor mass. Tuples with
exactly \(d\) types have zero balanced Rademacher defect, because their two
nonzero cofactor magnitudes agree. On the all-distinct event, sign averaging
gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\epsilon|\epsilon_0+q(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2+\epsilon_3)|
   =1+\frac14(3q-1).
\]
Here only the two all-equal patterns in the last three signs have
\(|q(\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2+\epsilon_3)|>1\); all other patterns have value
\(q<1\). Its unnormalized cofactor defect is consequently
\[
 \frac{1+3q}{2}-\left(1+\frac14(3q-1)\right)
   =\frac{3q-1}{4}=\frac{t}{4(3-t)}.
\]
Using \(\Delta=(d+1)A\delta\), this proves
\[
 \boxed{\delta_{d,t}
    =\frac3d\frac{t^3(8-t^2)}{8(24-t^3)(4+t^2)}
    \sim\frac1{32d}t^3.}                                  \tag{3.1}
\]
In particular the normalization agrees with the completed manuscript's
dimension-three formula.

Combining (2.1) and (3.1), for every \(\beta>2/3\),
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\phi}{\delta_{d,t}^{\beta}}
 \sim\frac{(d+1)!}{8d^{d+1}}(32d)^\beta t^{2-3\beta}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
No dimension-dependent finite constant can repair an exponent
\(\beta>2/3\) in this intermediate estimate, in any fixed \(d\ge3\).

## 4. Sharpness of the weighted tail and contact-slab powers

On the all-distinct event,
\[
 M=1+3q=\frac{6-t}{3-t},\quad
 t_4=\frac1{6-t},\quad
 g=\frac12-\frac1M=\frac{t}{2(6-t)}.
\]
All other tuples have \(t_4=0\). Choose \(\rho=1/7\) and
\(\eta_t=t/[2(6-t)]\). Then \(t_4>\rho\) on every all-distinct tuple and
\(\eta_t\le1/10<1/4\). Thus
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl[M\mathbf1_{\{t_4>\rho,\ g\le\eta_t\}}\bigr]
  =M\Pi
  =\frac{81(d+1)!}{d^{d+1}}
    \frac{(8-t^2)^3t^2(6-t)}{(24-t^3)^4}
  \sim\frac{3(d+1)!}{4d^{d+1}}t^2.
\]
Since \(t=12\eta_t/(1+2\eta_t)\),
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}
 \frac{\mathbb E[M\mathbf1_{\{t_4>1/7,\ g\le\eta_t\}}]}
      {\eta_t^2}
  =\frac{108(d+1)!}{d^{d+1}}>0.                       \tag{4.1}
\]
Therefore the exponent two in the weighted near-half-mass bound cannot
be increased, even with \(\rho\) fixed and even for these actual cone laws.

The corner-slab area estimate is independently sharp. The two diagonal
facets of \(K_{d,t}\) lie in the respective three-coordinate corner slabs
of width \(t\): if \(x_1+x_2+x_3=3-t\), then each \(x_i\ge1-t\), with
the analogous negative assertion. Their total regular boundary area is
\[
 \sqrt3\,2^{d-3}t^2.
\]
Any area bound with a power strictly greater than two would fail as
\(t\downarrow0\). Flat facets, rather than exceptional nonregular contacts,
already realize this obstruction. The completed projection-area and
determinant-cancellation argument therefore cannot acquire a better power
merely by a stronger universal patch-area estimate.

## 5. A different geometric quantity does avoid this loss on the family

The preceding obstruction concerns an intermediate moment. It does not
exclude a stronger final stability exponent through another argument.
Let \(C_d=[-1,1]^d\) and consider the actual cone support surplus
\[
 v=\mathbb E_{\nu_{K_{d,t}}}[h_{C_d}(X)-1].
\]
Axis samples have zero surplus. At a diagonal sample the surplus is
\(3q-1=t/(3-t)\), and its probability is \(R\). Consequently
\[
 \boxed{v=\frac{3t^3}{d(24-t^3)},\qquad
   \frac v{\delta_{d,t}}=\frac{8(4+t^2)}{8-t^2}
     \le\frac{40}{7}.}                                  \tag{5.1}
\]
The actual mixed-volume error used in product recovery is therefore
linear in \(\delta\) on this sharp half-mass family. Bounding the support
surplus by a support-function Lipschitz constant times distance to the
coordinate line/plane union discards a factor of order \(t\) here: the
diagonal samples stay a positive distance from that union, while their
support surplus tends to zero. A proof of a global joint block-selection
and support-surplus estimate would be a different route; this note proves
no such estimate.

One loss-free weighted quantity is already available for arbitrary
balanced coefficients. Since \(t_4\le1/4\) and \(g\le1/2\),
\[
 t_4g\le\min(t_4/2,g/4)\le\mathscr D(t).
\]
Hence the exact deficit identity gives
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E[\phi g]\le\Delta=(d+1)A\delta.}        \tag{5.2}
\]
On the truncation family this weighted moment has order \(t^3\), rather
than the order \(t^2\) of \(\mathbb E\phi\). Equation (5.2) is not by
itself a geometric stability theorem: the missing step is to select one
global line/plane decomposition while retaining an actual support-surplus
weight instead of unweighted sparsity distance.

## Verification scope

The analytic argument above proves the all-dimensional statements. An
optional exact-rational regression in `check_halfmass_sharpness.py` checks
the formulas by direct determinant/type and sign enumeration in dimensions
three and four. It does not replace the proof and imposes no polytope
restriction on the completed upper stability theorem.
