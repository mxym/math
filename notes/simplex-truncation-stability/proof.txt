     An exact exponent obstruction for lower-end projection-cone
                         simplex stability
                 Research supplement to entry005; AI-assisted research note

                                            7 October 2026


Outcome. In every fixed dimension d ≥ 3, any power estimate for the lower-end defect of entry005,
whether in Banach–Mazur distance to any simplex or in centroid containment by every maximum-
volume inscribed simplex, must have exponent at most 1/(d − 1). The example also gives an exact
lower bound on the constant in the latter estimate at this exponent. This is a necessary limit, not
a claim that the endpoint exponent has been proved for arbitrary bodies. The existing explicit
power theorem in the quantitative supplement is not reproved here.

5    Literature check, limitations, and verification
Rogers–Shephard and Zhang. Böröczky’s 2005 paper proves linear Banach–Mazur stability for
the normalized Rogers–Shephard deficit, and a power 1/d estimate for the polar projection (Zhang)
deficit. Its Example 19 already uses truncation at all simplex vertices and gives an obstruction
beyond 1/(d − 1) for that different invariant. Truncation as a sharpness method is therefore prior
work. Equations (4)–(10) calculate the entry005 invariant and all its maximum-simplex centroid


                                                     6
distances specifically. Proposition 3 shows why the linear Rogers–Shephard result alone cannot
give a linear bound in e in dimension three.
Random simplices. Ambrus–Böröczky’s random-simplex stability paper studies uniform interior
sampling; its stability theorem for the simplex as a maximizer is planar. Entry005 instead uses
determinants for the centered cone-volume law on the polar boundary. No identity transporting
the relevant defects between these sampling models has been established here. The cited theorem
is therefore not a quantitative solution for this invariant in d ≥ 3.
Inverse Minkowski. Böröczky–Machado–Ramos, arXiv:2603.17726v3, prove the sharp Hausdorff
exponent 1/(d − 1) for inverse Minkowski stability in the dual-convex distance of suitably dispersed
normalized surface-area measures. This is a plausible conversion step once closeness to a simplex
measure has been proved. It does not itself supply a bound on that measure distance from the
scalar gap e. This missing implication remains a separate obligation, and no unconditional improved
exponent is asserted from it.
Minkowski asymmetry. Brandenberg, González Merino, and Grundbacher (arXiv:2607.27041v2)
prove dimension-independent linear-order simplex approximation when d−s(K) is small. Equation
(13) identifies that parameter exactly in our family; (6) shows that a linear control of it by e is
impossible in d ≥ 3. Thus this useful geometric theorem also requires a root-type bridge in the
present invariant.
What is and is not proved. The exponent ceiling, exact invariant, maximum-simplex classifi-
cation, centroid excesses, translated excesses for those simplices, asymmetry, and (31) are proved
above. The general upper bound with exponent 1/(d − 1), the exact unrestricted distance in (11),
and optimal universal constants are not proved. No first-discovery or best-known claim is made.
The exact obstruction is compatible with the quantitative supplement’s much smaller positive ex-
ponent.
Exact supporting computation. The accompanying standard-library script check exact.py
uses fractions.Fraction throughout. It checks facet determinants, all vertex-subset simplex
volumes, barycentric containment for several different maximum simplices, and the dimension-
three difference-body volume by independently enumerating supporting facets and triangulating
them with rational arithmetic. These are finite regressions; the all-dimensional statements and
asymptotic conclusions rest on the written proof, not on those computations. Errors are raised
explicitly, so checks remain active under python3 -O.
