6    A cap in one projection
Lemma 3 (Projection cap). If B2d ⊂ K ⊂ P ⊂ M B2d , d ≥ 2, and πP (u) − πK (u) ≤ ε in every
direction, then
                           dH (K, P ) ≤ (d − 1)(M + 1)ε1/(d−1) .
No simplex hypothesis is needed for this lemma.
Proof. Put s = dH (K, P ). If s = 0 there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose a farthest q ∈ P
and its metric projection k ∈ K, and set n = (q − k)/s. The projection optimality condition gives
n · k = hK (n), so n · q − hK (n) = s. Also s ≤ M , since 0 ∈ K. Choose a unit u ⊥ n and project
orthogonally onto u⊥ . Let P , K, q be the projected objects. The space has dimension m = d − 1,
n lies in it, P contains its unit ball and is contained in its ball of radius M , and n · q − hK (n) = s.
    With τ = s/[2(M + 1)] < 1, convexity places the ball (1 − τ )q + τ B2m inside P . Its lowest
n-coordinate is at least

7    Retaining every initially chosen maximum simplex
If 0 ≤ e ≤ e♯ , then QD ≤ Q(d + 1)e ≤ r♯ ≤ b and (19) gives s ≤ 1/(8M d). Because hP ≥ 1 and
hK ≥ hP − s, (1 − s)P ⊂ K. The initially selected ∆ is maximum, so
                                       |∆|
                                            ≥ (1 − s)d ≥ 1 − ds.
                                       |P |
Put δ = ds ≤ 1/8. The barycentric coordinates of the vertices of ∆ in P form a nonnegative
column-stochastic (d + 1) × (d + 1) matrix W with | det W | = |∆|/|P | ≥ 1 − δ. Each column has
norm at most one. Hadamard’s inequality therefore gives squared norm at least (1 − δ)2 in each
column, so each column has an entry at least 1 − 2δ.
    These dominant rows are distinct. Indeed, the permanent is the probability that independent
row selections from the columns are all distinct and dominates | det W |. A common dominant row
in two columns would give

                           | det W | ≤ per W ≤ 1 − (1 − 2δ)2 ≤ 4δ < 1 − δ,

a contradiction. Match the vertices by these rows. Each vertex of ∆ is within 2δ diam P ≤ 4M d s
of its matched vertex of P . Taking convex combinations and using B2d ⊂ ∆ gives

                             K ⊂ P ⊂ ∆ + 4M d s B2d ⊂ (1 + 4M d s)∆.

By (19), E(K, ∆) ≤ A♯d e1/m . This also covers e = 0: then h = 0, the projection deficits vanish,
s = 0, and the same matrix argument gives zero excess. At the threshold,
