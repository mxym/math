# Independent adversarial check of the two Shannon statements

Independent AI-assisted internal review. No external peer review or formalization. Both primary conclusions passed; no priority certified.

**Question and boundaries.** The [published 2015 paper, final section, printed p. 032109-10](https://chaos.if.uj.edu.pl/~karol/pdf/PRCPZ15.pdf#page=10) proposes in Conjecture 1 both maximizing the lower endpoint and minimizing the upper endpoint at a complete MUB; Conjecture 2 proposes minimizing the Haar standard deviation of the averaged entropy. Pp. 5–6 and Fig. 3 separate numerically optimized extrema (solid) and the analytic, potentially nonsharp bounds (dashed). Interpret the former explicitly as the sharp lower endpoint. This does not show a narrower interval, disprove the upper-endpoint assertion, determine the globally optimal bases, or say that every state's entropy increases **relative to that same state's former value**. The Haar mean is invariant, so the last assertion would be false. Every state does exceed the former global minimum.

**Qutrit and compactness.** The traceless diagonal decomposition gives summed collision probability 2, giving the stated nonnegative gap identity since the probabilities sum to 4. Equality in every term and both computational/Fourier zero equations give exactly the nine rays, without an assumed restriction on phases or extra dark states. A minimizer for a sequence t tending to zero must converge along a subsequence to this finite set in projective space by compactness and joint continuity. At each ray the nonzero probabilities are uniformly away from zero, and their first-order Taylor terms sum in each basis to minus its missing probability times the common derivative. Thus the smooth part only costs second order. Since the map on four zero amplitudes is injective in (z,t) over the reals, its smallest singular value bounds the sum of their probabilities from below by c times r squared near the origin. The bounded x log x estimate on the scaled probabilities is valid even at zeros. Comparing r² log(1/r²) with the test state at r=|t| rigorously forces r=O(|t|), and finite rays ensure uniformity. The least-squares lower bound and its bounded test vector then match with remainder O(t²).

**Independent spectral derivation of the constant.** Let w be the fourth deleted outcome for v, and let w_t=D_t w. Let S(t) be the sum of the four corresponding formerly-zero-outcome projectors. Since S(0)=2(I−vv*), the sum of the first three is G=2(I−vv*)−ww*, with spectrum 0,1,2 and adjugate 2vv*. Hence
det S(t)=det(G+w_t w_t*)=2|v* w_t|² exactly. The other two eigenvalues of S(t) are 2+O(|t|), so its smallest eigenvalue is (j−i)² t²/12+O(|t|³). Its eigenvector is v+O(|t|) after phase adjustment, by projecting its eigenvector equation onto the unperturbed spectral complement with gap 2. At any actual minimizing state in the local O(|t|) neighborhood, the sum of the four small entropies is the sum of their probabilities times log(1/t²)+O(t²), while the smooth part costs O(t²). The Rayleigh minimum of S(t) therefore gives the same asymptotic, divided by four. Across the nine rays the coefficients are six instances of 1/48 and three of 1/12.

**Variance.** The compensating tangent terms depend only on T after summing in a basis. In the subtracted two-basis calculation the remaining rank-two Laplace transform is exact even for coincidence and orthogonality. Only degrees ≥2 survive bistochasticity. Jensen makes every retained term nonnegative before truncation is removed, so Tonelli applies to the difference at that stage; untruncated products converge by Cauchy–Schwarz and L² domination. The separated integrals are 1/[k(k−1)]; k=2 gives the displayed stability constant. Positive weights give equality iff *all* pairs are unbiased; with zero weights this would apply only to pairs in the support. No assumption of existence in composite non-prime-power dimensions, or for more than d+1 bases. The nonnegative square root preserves precisely the variance minimizers.

**Second derivation.** Use Laguerre polynomials orthonormal for Exp(1). Their generating function, combined with the joint Laplace transform or a Gaussian conditional integral, gives E[L_k(X_u)L_m(X_v)]=δkm o^k: the double generating function is 1/(1−ozw). By the Rodrigues formula, the coefficient of x log x is 1/[k(k−1)] for k≥2 (after k integrations by parts the remaining integral is (k−2)!∫xe^(−x)dx/k!). L² approximation and Cauchy–Schwarz justify the full series even at o=1. The degree 0,1 contributions are basis-independent; dividing by the second radial moment d(d+1) and summing distinct pairs twice gives exactly (V1) independently. This is another check of the primary Shannon result, not a new conjecture.

**Literature.** The [original Appendix C](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevA.92.032109) proves Tsallis-2, not Shannon. [Brierley–Weigert–Bengtsson](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.4097) establish qutrit complete-MUB uniqueness. [Riccardi–Macchiavello–Maccone](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04304) and [Serino et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05025) study the fixed MUB extrema. [Dall’Arno's Cor. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0341#page=5) predates the stated qutrit MUB information value. [Huang et al., Fig. 3(d)](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.013127#page=5) compare analytic lower bounds in a qutrit four-basis family, not the actual minima off the MUB point. Earlier work, e.g. [Ballester–Wehner](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0606244), already distinguishes partial MUB families from random choices in information locking; the general notion of MUBs not necessarily maximizing every uncertainty measure was not new. Targeted searches for these exact two claims including newer publications, citations and related operational language found no prior proof of the precise Haar–Shannon variance comparison or explicit complete-qutrit-MUB sharp-lower-endpoint violation; absence is not a priority determination.
