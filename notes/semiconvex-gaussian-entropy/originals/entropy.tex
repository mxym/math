\title{Gaussian entropy loss under a lower curvature bound\\\large Complete finite-observation proof, sharp constants, and a horizon obstruction}
\author{Research working note prepared with AI assistance}

\begin{abstract}
For smooth positive densities $p,q$ with $D^2(-\log q)\succeq-\kappa\Id$, finite relative entropy and relative score energy, and an exponential-square moment under $p$, we prove a weighted upper bound for the entropy lost under common Gaussian convolution. It uses the conditional prediction of the initial relative score. The resulting uniform scalar factor is $(1-\kappa z)^{-1}$ for $z<1/\kappa$, and is optimal. Smooth quartic reference densities prove that no finite curvature-only factor exists at or beyond $1/\kappa$, and give an explicit finite counterexample to the unweighted inequality for a nonconvex reference. The argument adapts a public finite-observation lemma; every needed estimate and all limit orders are supplied. It uses no tensor hierarchy or covariance-freezing theorem, and proves no general non-log-concave logarithmic Sobolev inequality. Independent review and a priority assessment remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The precise theorem}
For probability densities $p,q$ on $\R^n$, let
\[
H(p\mid q)=\int p\log(p/q),\qquad
w=\nabla\log(p/q),\qquad I=\E_p|w(Y)|^2.
\]
Let $G$ be standard Gaussian independent of $Y\sim p$, let
$U_r=Y+\sqrt rG$, and write $p_r=p*N(0,r\Id)$, $q_r=q*N(0,r\Id)$.
Define
\[
H_r=H(p_r\mid q_r),\qquad
J(r)=\E_p\abs{\E_p[w(Y)\mid U_r]}^2 \quad(r>0).
\]
Expectations include the independent observation noise. Set $J(0)=I$.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted entropy-loss comparison]\label{thm:main}
Suppose $p,q$ are $C^\infty$ strictly positive probability densities on $\R^n$,
$\kappa\ge0$,
\[
D^2(-\log q)\succeq-\kappa\Id,\qquad
H_0<\infty,\qquad I<\infty,\qquad
\E_p e^{c|Y|^2}<\infty \text{ for some } c>0.
\]
For $0<z<1/\kappa$, with all $z>0$ allowed when $\kappa=0$,
\begin{align}
2(H_0-H_z)&\le\int_0^z\frac{J(r)}{(1-\kappa r)^2}\,dr,
\label{eq:weighted}\\
2(H_0-H_z)&\le\frac{1}{1-\kappa z}\int_0^z J(r)\,dr.
\label{eq:scalar}
\end{align}
The constants do not depend on dimension. The leading coefficient one in
\eqref{eq:weighted} and the scalar factor in \eqref{eq:scalar} are optimal
over this class. For each $\kappa>0$ and $z\ge1/\kappa$, no finite factor
$C(\kappa,z)$ can replace the scalar factor uniformly, already in dimension one.
\end{theorem}

The quantity $J(r)$ is not generally the relative Fisher information
$I(p_r\mid q_r)$. No identification of these two quantities is used.
Data processing gives $0\le H_z\le H_0$.
The theorem bounds an entropy difference, not $H_0$ itself; it does not establish
a logarithmic Sobolev inequality for $q$.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional version]\label{cor:conditional}
Let $L$ take values in a standard Borel space with law $\lambda$.
Let $p_\ell,q_\ell$ and their first derivatives be jointly measurable, with
$C^\infty$ strictly positive conditional densities for $\lambda$-almost every
$\ell$. Suppose $D^2(-\log q_\ell)\succeq-\kappa\Id$ with the same $\kappa$,
and
\[
\E_L H(p_L\mid q_L)<\infty,\qquad
\E|w_L(Y)|^2<\infty,\qquad \E e^{c|Y|^2}<\infty
\]
for some $c>0$, where $Y\mid L=\ell\sim p_\ell$.
Take $G$ independent of $(L,Y)$. Then both bounds hold after averaging the
entropies over $L$ and replacing $J(r)$ by
$\E|\E[w_L(Y)\mid U_r,L]|^2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All three finite nonnegative expectations give their one-label finiteness
almost everywhere. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} separately to each such label.
Then integrate the proved inequality and use Tonelli on its nonnegative
right side. In particular, no uniform mesh-error bound over labels is asserted
or needed: the three observation limits are completed at each label before
this integration.
\end{proof}

\section{Source audit and standard inputs}
The source is OpenAI's \emph{A dimension-free logarithmic Sobolev inequality
for subgaussian log-concave measures}, 23 September 2026, pinned at
\texttt{adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a}.
Its Lemma 4.2 (pp.\ 22--25) proves \eqref{eq:scalar} for $\kappa=0$ with the
same entropy, score, and exponential-square assumptions. Its proof was read
in full, including every finite-copy comparison and all three limits.
Lemma 2.1 and its complete curvature/approximation proof (pp.\ 6--7) were also
read. The following obligations were checked rather than inferred from the
main manuscript's abstract:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An adaptive projected observation has the same Gaussian likelihood
factor under both priors. The chosen direction is determined by the retained
past, so it supplies no additional likelihood ratio.
\item Coinciding projected means give an averaged $O(\delta^2)$ entropy cost,
with constants uniform in the finite mesh. Here the posterior curvature is
$a=b-\kappa$, not $b$; the explicit constant is proved below.
\item Independence is used for full observation arrays across copies.
Retained adaptive histories do not factor across copies. Future-dependent
residuals are nevertheless conditionally independent given the full past.
\item The comparison of the two quadratic minima uses a dual test and the
minimizer of the true minimum. It does not assume that random coordinate
projections commute with conditional expectations.
\item Limits are mesh refinement at fixed $N,u$, then $N\to\infty$ at fixed
$u$, then $u\to\infty$. No uniform entropy-cost constant in $N$ is required.
\end{enumerate}

Primary literature was checked at the relevant argument, not only its title.
Chen--Eldan \cite{CE}, Sections 2.4.2 and 3.2.3, gives the Gaussian-quadratic
form of localization and the mean-difference entropy drift, Equation (27).
That setup uses positive definite continuous-time controls; it does not
itself justify our singular adaptive finite observations.
Eldan--Koehler--Zeitouni \cite{EKZ}, Lemma 2 and its full proof in
Section 2.0.1, constructs smooth approximations to function-preserving
orthogonal controls for an SDE. Our finite observations require only
measurable controls, and no SDE with singular controls is invoked.
The positive-curvature criterion is the standard Bakry--\'Emery input
\cite{BE}; its precise anisotropic version and extension to finite-energy
densities are reproved in Lemma~\ref{lem:curvature}.
The Gaussian channel identity, conditional expectation as an orthogonal
projection, entropy chain rule, and the monotone-functions integral
inequality are standard.

No theorem about the source's critical sequence, symmetric tensor hierarchy,
matrix square roots, frozen covariance blocks, or scale-integrated information
contradiction is imported. In particular, a future use of the source's main
dimension-free LSI would separately require audits of Proposition 5.1,
Proposition 6.8, and Propositions 7.2 and 7.9 and their assumptions. They are
not dependencies of this note.

\section{Positive-curvature and one-step estimates}
\begin{lemma}[Anisotropic curvature inequality]\label{lem:curvature}
Let $\pi=Z^{-1}e^{-V}$ be a smooth strictly positive probability on $\R^D$
with $D^2V\succeq S$, where $S$ is a fixed positive definite matrix.
For any smooth positive probability density $\rho$ whose right side is finite,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lsi}
2H(\rho\mid\pi)\le
\E_\rho\langle\nabla\log(\rho/\pi),S^{-1}\nabla\log(\rho/\pi)\rangle.
\end{equation}
If $S\succeq a\Id$, $a>0$, and $X$ has law $\pi$ projected to a $k$-dimensional
subspace and centered there, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subgauss}
\E e^{v\cdot X}\le e^{|v|^2/(2a)},\qquad
\E|X|^2\le k/a,\qquad
\E e^{\theta|X|^2}\le(1-2\theta/a)^{-k/2}\quad(2\theta<a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The transformation $y=S^{1/2}x$ reduces the first assertion to $S=\Id$.
Write $V(y)=|y|^2/2+U(y)$, where $U$ is convex. First suppose that $U$
is smooth with bounded Hessian. The diffusion
$dX_t=\sqrt2\,dB_t-\nabla V(X_t)\,dt$ has globally Lipschitz drift, is
nonexplosive, and preserves $\pi$ by integration by parts.
Its derivative flow satisfies $\dot A_t=-D^2V(X_t)A_t$, so
$\norm{A_t}_{\rm op}\le e^{-t}$. For a bounded positive Lipschitz function
$g$ bounded away from zero, differentiation of the flow and weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\frac{|\nabla P_tg|^2}{P_tg}
\le e^{-2t}P_t\!\left(\frac{|\nabla g|^2}{g}\right).
\]
Integration by parts gives
$-\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Ent}_\pi(P_tg)
=\int|\nabla P_tg|^2/(P_tg)\,d\pi$.
Coupling a trajectory at $x$ with a stationary one using the same Brownian
motion bounds their separation by $e^{-t}$ times its initial value.
The affine lower bound on $U$ gives Gaussian tails of $\pi$; this coupling
therefore yields $P_tg\to\E_\pi g$. Boundedness and a positive lower bound
allow dominated convergence of the entropy to zero. Integration in time
gives
$\operatorname{Ent}_\pi(g)\le\frac12\int|\nabla g|^2/g\,d\pi$.
Use $g=f^2+\varepsilon$ and then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ to obtain
$\operatorname{Ent}_\pi(f^2)\le2\int|\nabla f|^2\,d\pi$ for compact smooth $f$.

For general convex $U$, take the Moreau envelopes
$U_j(x)=\inf_y\{U(y)+j|x-y|^2/2\}$.
They are convex with Lipschitz gradient, increase to $U$, and a fixed affine
minorant $U(y)\ge\ell\cdot y+c_0$ gives
$U_j(x)\ge\ell\cdot x+c_0-|\ell|^2/(2j)$.
Mollify each $U_j$ by a centered compact smooth kernel, choosing its radius
so the mollification differs by at most $1/j$ on the ball of radius $j$.
The resulting smooth convex functions have bounded Hessian and the same
uniform affine minorant. Their Gaussian-weighted normalized densities
converge pointwise and in $L^1$, dominated by a fixed integrable Gaussian
times a linear exponential. The compact-test inequality passes by dominated
convergence.

For $f=\sqrt{\rho/\pi}$ with finite energy, multiply by cutoffs with gradients
$O(1/R)$ and approximate locally by compact smooth functions. Their $L^2$
norms tend to one and their energies tend to the energy of $f$.
Lower semicontinuity of relative entropy follows from
$H(\rho\mid\pi)=\sup_h\{\E_\rho h-\log\E_\pi e^h\}$ over bounded $h$;
normalizing the approximating squared functions thus proves \eqref{eq:lsi}.
Undoing the linear transformation supplies $S^{-1}$.

For $S\succeq a\Id$, apply the inequality to exponential tests.
If $\psi(t)=\log\E_\pi e^{t v\cdot X}$, it gives
$t\psi'(t)-\psi(t)\le t^2|v|^2/(2a)$.
Integrating the derivative of $\psi(t)/t$, also for negative $t$, proves the
first bound in \eqref{eq:subgauss}. Bounded Lipschitz truncations justify the
tests; the Gaussian tails just established allow their passage to the limit.
Differentiation at zero gives the covariance bound. Finally average
$e^{\sqrt{2\theta}G\cdot X}$ over an independent $k$-dimensional Gaussian:
the first bound yields
$\E e^{\theta|X|^2}\le\E e^{(\theta/a)|G|^2}
=(1-2\theta/a)^{-k/2}$. This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform entropy cost for matching means]\label{lem:cost}
Fix $N$, put $D=Nn$, and let the signal $\mathbf Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_N)$ have
prior $p^{\otimes N}$. Suppose a retained history $\mathcal H$ gives the
reference posterior curvature at least $a\Id$, $a>0$. Let $Q$ be an
$\mathcal H$-measurable orthogonal projection for which the two projected
posterior means agree. At strength $\delta$, observe $Q(\sqrt\delta\mathbf Y+G)$.
Set
\[
\theta=\min\{c/16,a/4\},\quad
C_{N,a,p}=\frac{4e^{\theta D/(2a)}}{\theta^2}
\left[(\E_p e^{8\theta|Y|^2})^N+(1-2\theta/a)^{-D}\right].
\]
For $0<\delta\le\theta$, its conditional relative entropy cost, averaged
under the alternative history law, is at most $C_{N,a,p}\delta^2$.
The constant is finite and independent of the chosen history sigma-field,
mesh, and controls; the bound is averaged, not pointwise in the history.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Center the projected signal at its common conditional mean and call it $X$.
For the reference, \eqref{eq:subgauss} gives
$\E_q e^{\theta|X|^2}\le(1-2\theta/a)^{-D/2}$ and
$\E_q|X|^2\le D/a$.
For the alternative let $m=\E_p[\mathbf Y\mid\mathcal H]$.
Since $|Q(\mathbf Y-m)|^2\le2|\mathbf Y|^2+2|m|^2$, two conditional
Jensen inequalities give
\[
\E_p[e^{\theta|X|^2}\mid\mathcal H]
\le(\E_p[e^{2\theta|\mathbf Y|^2}\mid\mathcal H])^2,\qquad
\E(\E_p[e^{\theta|X|^2}\mid\mathcal H])^2
\le\E_p e^{8\theta|\mathbf Y|^2}.
\]
The last expectation is $(\E_p e^{8\theta|Y|^2})^N$, finite since
$8\theta\le c/2$. These bounds hold for every conditioning sigma-field.

In $\operatorname{ran}Q$, let $\phi$ be standard Gaussian density and $h$
the density of $\sqrt\delta X+G$. For a conditionally independent copy $X'$,
direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
\chi^2(h\mid\phi)=\E[e^{\delta X\cdot X'}\mid\mathcal H]-1.
\]
The linear term vanishes. Use
$|e^t-1-t|\le t^2e^{|t|}/2$,
$|X\cdot X'|^2\le|X|^2|X'|^2$, and
$|X\cdot X'|\le(|X|^2+|X'|^2)/2$.
For $\delta\le\theta$, $s e^{\delta s/2}\le(2/\theta)e^{\theta s}$,
so
\[
\chi^2(h\mid\phi)
\le\frac{2\delta^2}{\theta^2}
(\E[e^{\theta|X|^2}\mid\mathcal H])^2.
\]
Centering and Jensen also give
$h_q(x)/\phi(x)\ge e^{-\delta D/(2a)}$ pointwise.
Since $H(h_p\mid h_q)\le\chi^2(h_p\mid h_q)$,
\[
H(h_p\mid h_q)
\le2e^{\delta D/(2a)}
\bigl(\chi^2(h_p\mid\phi)+\chi^2(h_q\mid\phi)\bigr).
\]
Average and substitute the moment estimates to obtain the stated constant.
For a zero-dimensional observed subspace the cost is zero.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite observations and comparison of minima}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \eqref{eq:weighted}]
Fix $z$ in the stated range, set $b=1/z$, $a=b-\kappa>0$, and take $N$
independent copies. Write $\mathbf W=(w(Y_1),\ldots,w(Y_N))$.
All expectations and $L^2$ inner products in this proof use the alternative
$p^{\otimes N}$ together with independent full Gaussian noises.
In particular $\norm{\mathbf W}_2^2=NI$.

First observe $Z_0=\sqrt b\,\mathbf Y+G_0$.
The averaged remaining posterior entropy is $N(H_0-H_z)$ by the chain rule:
the channel is identical under the two priors and its scaled output
has law $p_z^{\otimes N}$ or $q_z^{\otimes N}$.
Fix $u>0$, $m\ge1$, and $\delta=u/m\le\theta$.
Let $\mathcal H_i$ be the retained history before step $i$, initially $Z_0$.
Given $\mathcal H_i$, let $P_i$ project onto the difference of posterior means,
with $P_i=0$ if that difference is zero, and put $Q_i=\Id-P_i$.
Retain $Q_iZ_{i+1}$, where $Z_{i+1}=\sqrt\delta\,\mathbf Y+G_{i+1}$.
Each $P_i$ is measurable in the retained past; positive posterior densities
give integral versions of the means. A measurable orthonormal frame in the
observed subspace, chosen for example by Gram--Schmidt with a deterministic
pivot rule, makes this a usual conditional Gaussian kernel.

For any realized retained history the likelihood as a function of $\mathbf y$
is a common Gaussian quadratic factor of precision
\[
B=b\Id+\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}Q_i.
\]
No likelihood factor is added by choosing $P_i$, since that choice is a
deterministic function of the retained past under both priors.
Thus the reference posterior curvature is at least
$B-\kappa\Id=a\Id+\delta\sum Q_i$, and its relative score is exactly
$\mathbf W$. Every reference posterior is proper with Gaussian tails
by the affine lower bound on its convex remainder. Alternative posterior
means are finite almost everywhere by the exponential-square hypothesis.
Lemma~\ref{lem:cost} applies at every step because $Q_i$ kills the
posterior-mean difference. The entropy chain rule and Lemma~\ref{lem:curvature}
for the final posterior imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:posterior}
2N(H_0-H_z)\le
\E\langle\mathbf W,(B-\kappa\Id)^{-1}\mathbf W\rangle
+2C_{N,a,p}u\delta.
\end{equation}
All chain-rule entropies are finite in expectation, bounded by $NH_0$;
the posterior Fisher right side is at most $NI/a$.

Work in the Hilbert space of square-integrable $D$-vector fields on the
signal and all full observations through $Z_m$. Completing the square,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:true-min}
aNI-a^2\E\langle\mathbf W,(B-\kappa\Id)^{-1}\mathbf W\rangle
=\mathcal M
:=\min_v\left\{a\norm{\mathbf W-v}_2^2+
\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\norm{Q_i v}_2^2\right\}.
\end{equation}
The minimizer is $v_*=a(B-\kappa\Id)^{-1}\mathbf W$, so
$\norm{v_*}_2\le\sqrt{NI}$.

Let $\mathcal F_i=\sigma(Z_0,\ldots,Z_i)$ and let $e_i$ be conditional
expectation onto $\mathcal F_i$. They are nested orthogonal projections,
and $P_i$ is $\mathcal F_i$-measurable. Define the comparison minimum by
replacing $Q_i$ in \eqref{eq:true-min} with $\Id-e_i$, and denote it
$\mathcal M_0$. Its minimizer is
\[
v_0=a\left(a\Id+\delta\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(\Id-e_i)\right)^{-1}\mathbf W.
\]
With $\alpha_k=a/(a+k\delta)$, the nested projection decomposition gives
\begin{align}
v_0&=\alpha_m\mathbf W+
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(\alpha_k-\alpha_{k+1})e_k\mathbf W,
\label{eq:v0}\\
g_i:=(\Id-e_i)v_0
&=\alpha_m(\mathbf W-e_i\mathbf W)+
\sum_{k=i+1}^{m-1}(\alpha_k-\alpha_{k+1})
(e_k\mathbf W-e_i\mathbf W).
\label{eq:gi}
\end{align}
Indeed a component in $\operatorname{ran}(e_j-e_{j-1})$ has precisely
$j$ penalties, hence multiplier $\alpha_j$; the final unpredicted
component has $m$ penalties. The nonnegative weights in \eqref{eq:gi}
sum to $\alpha_{i+1}\le1$.

Conditional on $\mathcal F_i$, the full arrays of signal and future noise
remain independent across copies: their original joint law is a product,
and $\mathcal F_i$ conditions each factor only on its own full past.
Moreover $e_k\mathbf W_j$ uses only copy $j$'s observations.
Thus the blocks $g_{i,j}$ are conditionally independent and centered.
The retained histories need not have this property.
Conditional Jensen, the tower property, and \eqref{eq:gi} give
\[
Z_{i,j}:=\tr\Cov(g_{i,j}\mid\mathcal F_i)
\le\E[|\mathbf W_j-e_i\mathbf W_j|^2\mid\mathcal F_i]
\le\E[|\mathbf W_j|^2\mid\mathcal F_i].
\]
For any integrable $Z\ge0$ and $M=\E[Z\mid\mathcal F]$,
\[
\E[M\ind_{\{M>K\}}]\le2\E[Z\ind_{\{Z>K/2\}}],
\]
because $M\le K/2+\E[Z\ind_{\{Z>K/2\}}\mid\mathcal F]$.
The conditional covariance of $g_i$ is block diagonal, and $P_i$ has
rank at most one. Therefore
\[
\E|P_i g_i|^2
\le\E\max_j Z_{i,j}
\le K+2N\E_p[|w|^2\ind_{\{|w|^2>K/2\}}].
\]
Put
\[
\tau_N=\inf_{K>0}\left\{K/N+
2\E_p[|w|^2\ind_{\{|w|^2>K/2\}}]\right\}.
\]
Then $\tau_N\to0$, by first fixing $K$ to control the integrable tail
and then taking $N\to\infty$. Uniformly in $i,m,\delta$,
$\norm{P_i g_i}_2^2\le N\tau_N$.

No commutation between $P_i$ and $e_i$ is required.
For every $v$,
$\norm{Q_i v}_2^2\ge2\langle v,Q_i g_i\rangle-\norm{g_i}_2^2$.
The normal equation for $v_0$ is
$a(\mathbf W-v_0)=\delta\sum_i g_i$.
Consequently the minimum of
$a\norm{\mathbf W-v}_2^2+2\delta\sum_i\langle v,g_i\rangle
-\delta\sum_i\norm{g_i}_2^2$ is attained at $v_0$ and equals $\mathcal M_0$,
using $\langle v_0,g_i\rangle=\norm{g_i}_2^2$.
Test the preceding linear bound at $v_*$ to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
\mathcal M\ge\mathcal M_0-
2\delta\sum_i\norm{v_*}_2\norm{P_i g_i}_2
\ge\mathcal M_0-2uN\sqrt{I\tau_N}.
\end{equation}

\section*{The three limits within the proof}
The same orthogonal decomposition gives
\[
\frac{\mathcal M_0}{N}
=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}
\frac{a^2\delta}{(a+i\delta)(a+(i+1)\delta)}
\left(I-\norm{e_i\mathbf W_1}_2^2\right).
\]
For a component first predicted at step $j$, the coefficients telescope to
$aj\delta/(a+j\delta)$, exactly its minimized quadratic penalty.
Full observations through $i$ have sufficient statistic
$\sqrt b Z_0+\sqrt\delta\sum_{k=1}^i Z_k$.
Completing the Gaussian likelihood square shows that all other observation
coordinates are independent Gaussian noise with no information about the
signal. The resulting precision is $b+i\delta$, not $a+i\delta$.
Thus $\norm{e_i\mathbf W_1}_2^2=J(1/(b+i\delta))$.
Combining \eqref{eq:posterior}--\eqref{eq:dual} and telescoping yields the
explicit prelimit bound
\begin{align}
2(H_0-H_z)\le {}&
\frac I{a+u}
+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}
\frac{\delta\,J(1/(b+i\delta))}
{(a+i\delta)(a+(i+1)\delta)}
\label{eq:prelimit}\\
&+\frac{2u}{a^2}\sqrt{I\tau_N}
+\frac{2C_{N,a,p}u\delta}{N}. \nonumber
\end{align}

The prediction $J(r)$ is nonincreasing in $r$ and is between zero and $I$.
Indeed a higher-noise Gaussian output is obtained from a lower-noise
output by adding independent Gaussian noise; the Markov property and
conditional Jensen contract the squared predicted norm.
The explicit Gaussian conditional-density formulas also make $J$ measurable.
The coefficient $\delta/((a+i\delta)(a+(i+1)\delta))$ is exactly the integral
of $(a+t)^{-2}$ over $[i\delta,(i+1)\delta]$.
Since $J(1/(b+t))$ is bounded and monotone, this weighted sum converges
to its integral as $m\to\infty$.

First take $m\to\infty$ at fixed $N,u$.
The explicit $C_{N,a,p}$ is finite at these fixed parameters, so its error
vanishes. Next take $N\to\infty$ at fixed $u$, using $\tau_N\to0$:
\[
2(H_0-H_z)\le\frac I{a+u}
+\int_0^u\frac{J(1/(b+t))}{(a+t)^2}\,dt.
\]
Finally take $u\to\infty$. The remainder tends to zero and the nonnegative
integral increases to a finite value bounded by $I/a$.
With $r=1/(b+t)$ and $a=b-\kappa$, its value is
$\int_0^z J(r)/(1-\kappa r)^2\,dr$.
This proves \eqref{eq:weighted} in the stated order, without exchanging
mesh and copy limits or requiring higher moments of the score.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \eqref{eq:scalar}]
$J$ decreases while $g(r)=(1-\kappa r)^{-2}$ increases.
Integrating
$(J(r)-J(s))(g(r)-g(s))\le0$ over $[0,z]^2$ gives
\[
\int_0^zJ(r)g(r)\,dr
\le\frac1z\left(\int_0^zJ(r)\,dr\right)
\left(\int_0^zg(r)\,dr\right).
\]
The last integral is $z/(1-\kappa z)$, including its limit at $\kappa=0$.
Combine with \eqref{eq:weighted}.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharpness and explicit obstructions}
All examples below are one-dimensional smooth positive densities and satisfy
the exact hypotheses, including finite entropy and score energy.
Fix $t>0$, $\kappa\ge0$, and $R>0$, and set
\[
p_t=N(0,t),\qquad
f_R(x)=e^{\kappa x^2/2-x^4/R^4},\qquad
q_R(x)=Z_R^{-1}f_R(x).
\]
Here $0<Z_R<\infty$ and
$(-\log q_R)''=-\kappa+12x^2/R^4\ge-\kappa$.
Write $A=\kappa+1/t$ and $d_R=4/R^4$. The relative score is
$w_R(Y)=-AY+d_RY^3$, and
\begin{align}
I_R&=A^2t-24At^2/R^4+240t^3/R^8,\label{eq:IR}\\
J_R(r)&=\frac{(A-3d_Rt)^2t^2}{t+r}
+\frac{6d_R^2t^6}{(t+r)^3}. \label{eq:JR}
\end{align}
To verify \eqref{eq:JR}, decompose
$w_R=(-A+3d_Rt)Y+d_R(Y^3-3tY)$ into orthogonal Gaussian Hermite terms.
For $U_r\sim N(0,t+r)$, its posterior mean and variance are
$tU_r/(t+r)$ and $tr/(t+r)$.
Their cubic moment gives the conditional Hermite term
$(t/(t+r))^3(U_r^3-3(t+r)U_r)$.
The two terms are orthogonal, with squared norms $t+r$ and $6(t+r)^3$.
This proves \eqref{eq:JR}; $r=0$ also gives \eqref{eq:IR}.

\subsection{Sharp weighted and scalar constants below the horizon}
For $z<1/\kappa$, define $h_R=f_R*N(0,z)$.
As $R\to\infty$,
\[
h_R(x)\longrightarrow h_\infty(x)
=(1-\kappa z)^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left(\frac{\kappa x^2}{2(1-\kappa z)}\right).
\]
Completing the square gives the stronger quantitative identity
\[
\frac{h_R(x)}{h_\infty(x)}
=\E_{\;T_x}e^{-T_x^4/R^4},\qquad
T_x\sim N\left(\frac{x}{1-\kappa z},\frac z{1-\kappa z}\right).
\]
Jensen therefore yields
$0\le\log h_\infty(x)-\log h_R(x)\le\E T_x^4/R^4$.
If $X\sim N(0,t+z)$, put
\[
T=\frac{t+z}{(1-\kappa z)^2}+\frac z{1-\kappa z}.
\]
Then $0\le\E\log h_\infty(X)-\E\log h_R(X)\le3T^2/R^4$.
No improper prior is used as a probability: $h_\infty$ only evaluates a
limit of unnormalized likelihoods, and $Z_R$ cancels from the entropy
difference for every finite $R$.

Since Gaussian differential entropy satisfies $h(p_t)=\frac12\log(2\pi et)$,
\begin{align*}
2(H(p_t\mid q_R)-H((p_t)_z\mid(q_R)_z))
&=\log(1+z/t)+2\E\log h_R(X)-\kappa t+6t^2/R^4\\
&\longrightarrow
\mathcal A_t(z)
:=\log(1+z/t)-\log(1-\kappa z)
+\frac{\kappa z(1+\kappa t)}{1-\kappa z}.
\end{align*}
Moreover the finite-$R$ expression is at least
$\mathcal A_t(z)-6T^2/R^4$.
From \eqref{eq:JR}, $J_R$ converges uniformly on $[0,z]$ to
$J_\infty(r)=(1+\kappa t)^2/(t+r)$.
Direct differentiation, with both sides zero at $z=0$, proves
\[
\mathcal A_t(z)=\int_0^z
\frac{(1+\kappa t)^2}{(t+r)(1-\kappa r)^2}\,dr.
\]
Thus the ratio in the weighted inequality tends to one for every fixed
$t,z$, establishing optimality of its leading coefficient.
For $\kappa>0$,
\[
\int_0^zJ_\infty(r)\,dr=(1+\kappa t)^2\log(1+z/t),\qquad
\frac{\mathcal A_t(z)}{(1+\kappa t)^2\log(1+z/t)}
\longrightarrow\frac1{1-\kappa z}\quad(t\to\infty).
\]
Indeed numerator and denominator have leading terms
$\kappa^2tz/(1-\kappa z)$ and $\kappa^2tz$.
For any smaller proposed factor, first choose finite $t$ large enough,
then finite $R$ large enough. This proves optimality with proper smooth
probability densities in the order $R\to\infty$ at fixed $t$, then $t\to\infty$.
When $\kappa=0$, numerator and denominator limits both equal
$\log(1+z/t)>0$, proving optimality of factor one as well.

\subsection{A finite counterexample to the unweighted extension}
Take $\kappa=t=1$, $z=1/2$, $R=4$.
Then $p=N(0,1)$, $q=Z^{-1}\exp(x^2/2-x^4/256)$, and $q$ is non-log-concave
because its potential has second derivative $-1$ at zero.
Here $T=7$ and the entropy bound just proved gives
\[
2(H_0-H_{1/2})\ge\log3+2-294/256
=\log3+109/128.
\]
The exact prediction integral is
\[
\int_0^{1/2}J_R(r)\,dr
=\frac{15625}{4096}\log(3/2)+\frac5{12288}.
\]
Strict convexity of $x\mapsto1/x$ gives
$\log3>1$ by its midpoint lower bound on $[1,3]$,
and $\log(3/2)<5/12$ by its trapezoid upper bound on $[1,3/2]$.
Consequently
\[
2(H_0-H_{1/2})-\int_0^{1/2}J_R(r)\,dr
>\frac{237}{128}-\frac{78145}{49152}
=\frac{12863}{49152}>0.
\]
This is an analytic finite counterexample; no numerical quadrature or
uncertified estimate of $Z$ is involved.

\subsection{No finite factor at or beyond the horizon}
Fix $\kappa>0$, $t>0$, and $z\ge1/\kappa$.
Using the same proper $q_R$, symmetry and $\cosh\ge1$ give
\begin{align*}
h_R(x)
&=\frac{e^{-x^2/(2z)}}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}
\int_\R e^{(\kappa-1/z)y^2/2-y^4/R^4}\cosh(xy/z)\,dy\\
&\ge\frac{2R}{e\sqrt{2\pi z}}e^{-x^2/(2z)},
\end{align*}
where the last bound integrates just $|y|\le R$ and uses $\kappa-1/z\ge0$.
Put $c_z=2/(e\sqrt{2\pi z})$. The exact entropy-difference identity above
now implies
\[
2(H_0-H_z)\ge
\log(1+z/t)+2\log R+2\log c_z-(t+z)/z-\kappa t+6t^2/R^4
\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
On the other hand, conditional Jensen and \eqref{eq:IR} give, for $R\ge1$,
\[
\int_0^zJ_R(r)\,dr\le zI_R
\le z\bigl(A^2t+240t^3\bigr)<\infty,
\]
uniformly in $R$. Every example has finite entropy and Fisher information;
the exponential-square moment belongs to the fixed Gaussian $p_t$.
Thus no finite curvature-and-time-only comparison factor exists in this range.
This proves the remaining sharpness assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Verification and proved boundaries}
The accompanying checker uses exact rational arithmetic for Gaussian
polynomial predictions, the finite explicit counterexample, precision-kernel
identities, and a finite-product test of genuinely adaptive rank-one
quadratic-minimum comparisons. Its finite-product noise is discrete: that
test probes the Hilbert-space comparison, not the Gaussian entropy-cost
lemma. Source hashes and a LaTeX build provide separate provenance and
readability checks. They do not substitute for the analytic proofs.

Proved under the displayed hypotheses: the weighted and scalar comparisons,
the conditional version, optimal constants, a finite failure of the
unweighted nonconvex extension, and impossibility at or beyond the horizon.
Not established: removal of the exponential-square moment, nonsmooth priors,
a general non-log-concave dimension-free LSI, a tensor/freezing extension,
priority or novelty, formal verification, or independent peer review.
The earlier Crouzeix files are frozen and were not edited.
