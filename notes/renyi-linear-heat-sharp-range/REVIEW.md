# Independent audit: dimension-uniform Rényi heat concavity
Internal mathematical review only. No publication, external peer review, or priority claim.

Reviewed private candidate PROOF.md SHA256 `83b6697fb8ded043a1291493b1a1a057d0faaf66f721d4cf2fa2e406b87bfbc4`; author check.py `96ee7909b62d1f1d9c9d96c8f3981fc4447d4c1aeff5cbb07efdcc3396f84bec`. **Verdict: PASS for the stated Euclidean all-dimensions, arbitrary-initial-density claim, on finite positive-time domains.** No mathematical defect found. The positive results are not original, nor are all the exterior obstructions. Keep the distinctions below.

1. For heat speed 1, put u=P_t f, w=u^(α/2), U=∫w², b=∫|∇w|²/U, A=Δw/w, B=|∇log w|², δ=2/α−1. Differentiation gives w_t=Δw+δwB, EA=−b and EB=b under normalized w². Thus U'/U=−2(1−δ)b, and h'=4b/α for α≠1; direct Shannon integration by parts gives the same at 1. Also b'=−2[E(A²+δAB)−(1−δ)b²]. This gives the stated T and N''. As a cross-check on prior sufficiency, the standard integrated Bochner identity gives E(A²+AB)=E||Hess log w||². The covariance combination equals (1−δ)Var(A)+δ E||Hess log w||². For 0≤δ≤1 it is ≥(δ/d)(E Δlog w)²=4δ b²/d. Thus T≥2(2−α); the limit in item 3 approaches this lower bound. This check concerns regular data; arbitrary normalized L¹ data at positive times are covered by the cited prior theorems on finite domains.
2. The radial Jacobian gives exactly z^(k−1)e^(−z) for z=r², k=m+d/2. The two negative Gamma moments exist for k>2. The identities for A and B follow from Δ log w=m(d−2)/z−d. One can verify the full answer *independently* without an escort A,B curvature formula: let P=Δu/u. Then ∂tP=ΔP+2∇log u·∇P, so h''=α/(1−α)[α Var(P)+E ∂tP] with expectation under u^α. Compute P and ∂tP as Laurent polynomials in z and integrate using moments of degrees −2 through 2. `independent.py` symbolically identifies these formulas in all parameters with the candidate T, checks h', takes the large-k limit, and at α=1 checks the removable singularity separately against the radial log-Hessian Bochner formula. Both this independent run and the author's 550 exact finite crosschecks pass.
3. For each fixed λ>0, k=λd/2, m=(λ−1)d/2; noninteger m is allowed since the final profile uses r²+ε². The scaled covariance C/k→(1−a₀)(1−c₀), b/k→λ^−2>0, giving the limiting T=2(2−α)+2(α−1)/λ. Signs are strict after choosing λ then a finite sufficiently large integer d; no single d or uniformity as λ→0 or ∞ is needed. α<1: λ↓0; α>2: λ→∞; 1<α≤2 with β above the line: λ→∞; α=1 with β>2: Gaussian. At α=2 plain entropy remains concave but no strictly positive exponent works in all dimensions. At α=1 the exponent 2 included and Gaussian attains T=2. At α=1/2 author's exact (d,λ)=(300,1/5) gives T=−94330/42237. β=0 makes the unscaled exponential identically 1, and is not h.
4. The singular helper profile need not define a probability density after taking its 2/α power, particularly for α<1; no mass convergence is required. For fixed d,λ the simultaneous regularization has weights with the required uniform integrable majorants (near 0 the exponents are d−5 or 2k−5, both >−1). The estimate for the logarithmic Laplacian and the weight covers also B² or AB, with an absolute value. The denominator b is positive in the limit and normalized escort moments are continuous. For each fixed j, positive, radial, normalized initial data have true exponential decay and relative spatial derivatives bounded through order four; the global log Lipschitz estimate gives two-sided Gaussian-convolution comparisons and allows differentiation twice with uniform majorants. No assumption of reverse heat flow is made. The interval of positive times follows by right continuity of h'' and h'. The normalized initial density need not have a Gaussian tail, and j need not be quantitatively bounded; neither feature was asserted. Avoid extending the positive statement at t=0 to all L¹ data without a separate interpretation.
5. [Wu–Yu 2611.13040v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13040v1#S6.SS3) Theorems 5 and 8 cover exactly the linear Euclidean positive-time case with arbitrary L¹ data and give the sufficiency directions. Remark 1 asks about the order interval of both Thms 3 and 5; it does **not** explicitly ask the entropy-power exponent question. Only the all-dimensions version of Thm 5's optimal interval is settled here. Theorem 3 concerns a fundamental solution under geometric assumptions; these smooth-data counterexamples do not test it. [Wu–Yu, §II-D Thm 6/Remark 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01837v2#S2.SS4) already rule out a universal β≥2 for α≠1, where their power convention doubles their exponent. [Wu–Yu–Guo's one-dimensional work](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2025.3561384) includes plain positivity for 0<α≤3 and stronger dimension-one power results; none are contradicted. [Sun, Prop 16, Remark 19](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12944v3#S4) gives the α>3 obstruction for Tsallis via sharp smooth testers; h''=S''/U+(α−1)(S')²/U² transfers this to Rényi for α>3, already in one dimension. Its Gaussian q<1 example has no such implication. [Bukal Thm 1, Eqs 12–13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12527#page=3) and [Savaré–Toscani](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.1035) evolve by a nonlinear p-diffusion, not this linear heat evolution. Targeted literature searches found no prior full classification, not an exhaustive novelty finding.

Editorial: write h=(1−α)^−1 log(∫u^α dx) explicitly. Keep “all dimensions simultaneously,” finite entropies, strict β>0, and source boundaries visible. In scripts, reported sample tests are of static moments; neither script by itself certifies smoothing. No fixed-dimensional threshold, unrelated geometric heat-kernel problem, or general Rényi complete-monotonicity conjecture is solved here.
