# Primary-source comparison checkpoint

8 October 2026. This is a focused literature check, not a priority certificate.
The frozen main mathematical draft has not been changed.

## Standard input actually used

F. Bihan and I. Soprunov, *Criteria for strict monotonicity of the mixed volume
of convex polytopes*, arXiv:1702.07676v2 (5 December 2017).
https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.07676
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.07676

The primary paper was opened and its Introduction and Section 2 inspected.
It explicitly records the classical monotonicity of mixed volume, the
Minkowski volume polynomial, and the essential-collection criterion for
positive mixed volume (Theorem 2.2). A collection with j copies of a body
of dimension less than j is not essential, agreeing with the vanishing
used in the apex-count cone proof. The paper's new strictness criteria are
not used in our proof. These standard facts must be credited as background,
not presented as new claims.

## Nearby stability results with different functionals

R. Schneider, *Stability for Some Extremal Properties of the Simplex*,
Journal of Geometry 96 (2009), 135--148, DOI 10.1007/s00022-010-0028-0.
Author-hosted primary text:
https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/rschnei/2184.pdf

Its introduction and abstract concern covering by homothetic copies,
Minkowski asymmetry, and Jung/Steinhagen-type quantities. This is relevant
simplex-stability background, but it is not the present actual pyramid/
projection-body defect, nor did the inspected material state the
vertex-excess exponent 1/min(r,d-1).

K. J. Böröczky and M. Henk, *Cone-volume measure and stability*, Advances in
Mathematics 306 (2017), 24--50, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2016.10.005.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7272
https://users.renyi.hu/~carlos/cone-volume-stability.pdf

The primary abstract states stability for cone-volume subspace concentration
and the U-functional/volume inequality. Those are distinct quantities and
equality problems. Their presence in the same cone-measure literature does
not by itself identify or refute overlap with the new apex-count argument.

## Projection-body context already relevant to the earlier checkpoint

C. Saroglou, *Volumes of projection bodies of some classes of convex bodies*,
Mathematika 57 (2011), 329--353, DOI 10.1112/S0025579311001860.
https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579311001860

The primary publisher abstract confirms sharp three-dimensional R results
for cones and double cones. This is a genuine prior-work overlap with
special cases of the earlier few-vertex R upper bound. It is not grounds
for claiming those special cases are new. The full article was not
obtained in this pass; its complete overlap is not certified.

N. S. Brannen, *Volumes of projection bodies*, Mathematika 43 (1996),
255--264, DOI 10.1112/S002557930001175X.
https://doi.org/10.1112/S002557930001175X

The primary abstract describes the conjectured simplex R maximum and
centrally symmetric counterexamples to a different proposed upper bound.
The full article was not accessible through the available web tool.

## Search boundary

Focused searches combined simplex stability, projection-volume deficit,
number of vertices, vertex excess, cone/pyramid apex points, mixed-volume
prismoids, and truncated simplices. They found standard mixed-volume facts
and nearby stability literature, but no exact prior statement of the
sharp exponent classification proved in the frozen draft.

This is not exhaustive: the argument should still be checked against the
full projection-body literature and specialized few-vertex polytope
stability work before making any novelty or priority assertion. The new
theorem is currently a mathematical research result pending independent
review, not a first-discovery certification.
