# Density-overlap and critical-boundary result: scope comparison

## Inherited project results

001 v3 supplies the all-finite-second-moment centered-potential Lipschitz estimate for strongly log-concave sources and the finite-moment tail framework. It already contains bounded-boundary examples and Gaussian/global-bounded-Hessian one-third estimates. 007 v2 reproduces a signed-square/minimum-weight argument under an untruncated translation-ratio hypothesis and adds superquadratic positive results plus a fixed smooth full-support steep-layer counterexample. These facts are cited, not counted as new in 008.

008 replaces the untruncated ratio by a bounded overlap loss, obtains a Sobolev-density-root and finite-Fisher-information criterion, and classifies an explicit boundary-vanishing source family. Its critical logarithm is proved necessary by a simultaneous multiscale construction with exact matching of every central and outer target mass. A single rare-atom example would not establish that endpoint. The source family has domain boundaries, so this is a different statement from 007's smooth full-support counterexample, not a purported replacement of it.

## Focused external comparison

Delalande and Merigot, Quantitative stability of optimal transport maps under variations of the target measure, Duke Mathematical Journal (2023), arXiv:2103.05934, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2022-0106, is an acknowledged predecessor for potential-to-gradient interpolation and unbounded-target stability. Its abstract describes a compact source density bounded above and below and finite moments p>d. The present upper bound uses the different explicit potential estimate in 001; no first-use claim is made for interpolation or linearized transport.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05934

Targeted searches were made using both available indexes for combinations of Brenier/optimal-transport stability, source Fisher information, density-root/Besov regularity, and critical logarithmic boundary behavior. No directly equivalent theorem was located in those limited results. This does not establish novelty: a systematic full-text review of all later transport and weighted interpolation literature has not been completed.

The phase diagram is for the precise displayed family, not all possible boundaries or all strongly log-concave sources. The critical lower bound allows the number of target atoms to increase. The threshold p=4 is sharp for the finite-Fisher class assertion, not necessary for one-third stability at each individual source.
