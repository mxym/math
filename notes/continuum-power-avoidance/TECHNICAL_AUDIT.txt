VERDICT: PASS AS A COMPLETE WRITTEN MATHEMATICAL PROOF.

No substantive gap or counterexample was found in the bounded-logarithmic-gap
statement in the received PROOF.txt. This verdict is specific to that statement,
its positive-power remainder bound, and the countable prescribed source family.
It is not a Lean result, novelty certification, external referee report, practical
construction claim, or proof of the larger positive-upper-Banach-density theorem.

Theorem actually audited
-----------------------
For a prescribed nonempty countable family A_l whose occupied dyadic logarithmic
bins have bounded gaps eventually, and each epsilon in (0,1), one closed,
nowhere-dense, one-periodic E has measure greater than 1-epsilon in every unit
interval and simultaneously has infinitely many distinct misses in every small
image tail satisfying f(a)=y+c a^s+O(a^(s+alpha)), with s,alpha>0, c nonzero,
y arbitrary, and finite error constant. The functions need no regularity or
measurability. In particular, for A={2^-n}, it excludes every affine null
geometric progression for every ratio in (0,1) simultaneously.

I. Infinite proof checks
------------------------

1. The selected subsequence is valid for arbitrary source sets.
   Occupied bins give z_n=-log_2(a_n) in [j_n,j_n+1). Taking a bin jump in
   [m,m+G-1], m=ceil(3/s0)+2, gives the strict lower gap 3/s0 and strict upper
   gap m+G. Thus every exponent in [s0,s1] gives output gaps in (3,D),
   D=s1(m+G). No cardinality, source measurability, or distribution assumption
   is being silently added.

2. The uniform count includes activation endpoints.
   Once u is after the first output point, an open interval (u,u+ell) contains
   at least ell/D-1 points: its length is covered by at most N+1 consecutive
   output gaps. For ell>=2D this is at least ell/(2D). Points equal to either
   endpoint are not counted. This is the correct count for the open activation
   later needed in the closed exceptional-center argument.

3. The preorder recurrence and local span are correct.
   A nonleaf edge has its own length ell_r=g+sigma_(r-1), followed by a gap
   and its child's subtree. Their span is exactly 2ell_r. All grids in that
   local test are refined by its final descendant grid. The whole tree's
   recurrence is sigma_r=2M sigma_(r-1)+(3M-1)g. With the finite tree fixed,
   its total span T is affine in the base length L.

4. Stable centers preserve every needed earlier decision.
   For a current window starting at u=b+g+1, every active positive offset is
   smaller than 2^(1-u)=2^(-b-g). The unstable measure per predecessor grid is
   at most 2^(b+3)2^(-b-g)=2^(3-g). At a stable center, nesting preserves all
   earlier keys, including ancestor decisions and preceding siblings. The
   endpoint convention (x,x+delta] is correct: a center may itself be a grid
   boundary, but reaching the next boundary is forbidden.

5. Selector and terminal address independence survives routing dependence.
   Current active offsets and the center have distinct own-grid addresses.
   Differences are bounded below by the logarithmic spacing; all offsets are
   in an interval of length at most 1/8, avoiding circular-distance problems.
   After center exposure, the tested own selector entries are independent fair
   bits. Conditional on all selectors, terminal table/address pairs are
   distinct: separate child subtrees use different tables; common leaves have
   finer grids, preserving the own-grid distinction. Therefore conditional
   failure is (1-p)^(sum of own selectors), and averaging is exactly
   (1-p/2)^(number of tests). Shared auxiliary selector entries would be
   harmless; shared terminal addresses would not be. The proof does not
   assume independent routing paths.

6. The continuum reduction is a finite exact stratification.
   The candidate-index count depends polynomially on absolute U+T+|k| and is
   not treated as an exponent-independent constant. An active pair has at
   most 1+2^(2ell+3) relevant finest-grid boundary curves.

   A particularly transparent justification is to change the second parameter
   to v=log_2(t), so v is in [0,1]. Every recorded crossing curve becomes the
   straight line v=log_2(beta)-k+z_n s. Activation boundaries are vertical
   lines. Thus the purported curve arrangement is an ordinary finite line
   arrangement in a rectangle. Coincident lines may be merged while retaining
   their labels. Its cells, open line pieces, and points have O(C^2) strata;
   the submitted bound 20(C+1)^2 is conservative. All activation boundaries,
   parameter edges, intersections, and degenerate point portions are retained.
   On every stratum the active flags and finest keys are constant for every
   realization of the remaining randomness. Representatives depend only on
   the fixed center, center-exposure atom, inputs, and grids. They are chosen
   before the remaining random entries. No rational approximation to s and
   no sampled exponent grid is used.

7. The terminal union bound and absolute-position cost are correct.
   Each stratum representative has the same uniform lower active count,
   including representatives on activation and parameter boundaries. A
   successful local test forces actual membership in B. A union bound gives
     20[P(3+2^(2ell+3))+5]^2 exp(-lambda ell)
     <=5120 M^2(U+T+|k|+2)^2 exp(-kappa ell),
   where lambda=p eta(M-1)/2 and kappa=lambda-4 log(2)>0.
   The choice p eta(M-1)>=12 gives lambda>=6. There is no union bound over
   uncountably many powers, over every center-exposure atom with an extra
   entropy factor, or over the whole tree's finest grid in place of the local
   final grid.

8. The window and buffer choices are noncircular.
   M,d,K,g are fixed before U. The submitted L(U)=O(log U) gives T(U)=O(log U).
   For all sufficiently large U one has T<=U and |k|<U, so the polynomial
   entropy factor is absorbed by the explicit four-log-U base window length.
   The local miss probability is then less than p at every local length.

   The error radius is
     r_U=(q 2^-k+1)2^-U 2^(-alpha0(U+k)/s1).
   The double-buffer cost on the overall finest grid is exactly
     4N r_U=2^(T(U)+4)(q 2^-k+1)2^(-alpha0(U+k)/s1).
   Its logarithm is O(log U)-alpha0 U log(2)/s1, so it tends to minus infinity.
   Both the entropy and buffer inequalities hold for every sufficiently large
   U. Increasing U and recomputing L,T does not invalidate an earlier choice.
   Positive alpha0 is essential to this argument.

9. The exceptional-center set is genuinely closed.
   There are finitely many potentially active indices. For each, 'inactive
   OR outside B1' is closed in the compact center/parameter product, because
   activation is open and B1 is open. The finite intersection is closed; its
   projection from a compact product is closed. This supplies measurable R
   for each outcome. There is no requirement to choose stratum representatives
   measurably as a function of the center in order to integrate the pointwise
   probability estimate.

10. The density and robust-boundary budgets close.
    Expected B2 density is at most p+4Nr_U<2p. Stable-center miss probability
    is less than 2p; unstable density is less than p. Thus expected R density
    is at most 3p, and a finite-table outcome has rho(B2)+rho(R)<=5p. Outer
    regularity gives V containing R at extra cost at most p, hence rho(H)<=6p.
    A point in the first open buffer B1 remains in the second open buffer B2
    under any allowed error, including equality in the error bound. At a center
    in R, openness of V and uniform convergence of the offsets and bounded
    errors supply late points in V. Those repair points need not be active in
    the finite template. This handles all error families, even ones chosen
    after H; no probabilistic independence of the errors is needed.

11. The theorem's quantifier order and distinctness conclusion hold.
    Summing over (l,N,j,k,q,h), with exponent compact ranges [1/N,N], alpha0=1/j,
    covers every real s,alpha>0. Coefficients use c=2^k t with t in [1,2], and
    negative coefficients use reflections. A total budget 12 sum p_tuple<epsilon
    gives the required strict density. Once h is large enough, f is defined and
    its error bound is valid on the entire tail. Hits at inputs below 2^-h tend
    to zero, and their values tend to y without equaling y, by the explicit
    lower bound |f(a)-y| >= |c|a^s/2. A finite set of non-y values cannot do this,
    so every sufficiently small image tail has infinitely many distinct misses.
    Affine maps with s=1 are included and rule out any interval in E, proving
    nowhere density. Periodicity gives the same measure in every unit interval.

II. The sparse-block obstruction is valid at precisely its stated scope
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Let R_m=2^(2^m), S=union_{m>=3}{R_m,...,R_m+m}, and A={2^-j:j in S}.
Every integer length L occurs in an occupied block, so F_S(L)=L and upper
Banach density is 1. For any fixed C and late U with 0<T<=C log U, an activating
j for some s in [1,2] must lie in [U/2,U+T]. At most one block intersects this
interval eventually, since intersecting centers have R_m>=U/3 and the next
center is R_m^2>5U/4. Its length parameter is O(log log U). All activating
exponents for the block are contained in
  (U/(R_m+m),(U+T)/R_m),
whose length is at most 3T/U+9m/U, eventually less than 1. Some s in [1,2]
is therefore completely inactive across the template.

This is a counterexample to using the present bounded-gap uniform-occupancy
step with every late logarithmic-span template. It is not a counterexample
to a continuum avoidance theorem for all positive-upper-Banach-density sources.
Longer spans or a different placement/routing principle remain unaddressed.
