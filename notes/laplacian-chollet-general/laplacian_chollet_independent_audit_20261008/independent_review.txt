2. IMPORTED THEOREMS AND THEIR ACTUAL HYPOTHESES

2.1 Edmonds
The original 1965 article, Section 2, Theorem (P), characterizes the convex hull
of matching incidence vectors by nonnegativity, vertex upper bounds of 1, and
internal-edge upper bounds r for subsets of 2r+1 vertices. It permits real edge
weights in its linear optimization consequence. Thus the candidate may apply
it to the real weights log(1+c_e). The singleton odd-set condition is vacuous.
There is no missing perfect-matching requirement or cardinality constraint.
  https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69B/jresv69Bn1-2p125_A1b.pdf

2.2 Lieb
The needed assertion is the non-strict two-principal-block inequality
  per([[B,C],[C*,D]]) >= per(B) per(D)
for Hermitian PSD matrices. It is not the unsolved permanental dominance
conjecture, nor the multi-block permanent-of-permanents conjecture. Repeated
application to complementary principal blocks gives the finite-partition
product bound used here.

The original publisher-hosted first page identifies the result and bibliographic
record. A separate primary proof by D. Z. Djokovic proves nonnegativity of the
coefficients of per([[lambda B,C],[C*,D]]). The top coefficient is per(B)per(D),
so evaluation at lambda=1 supplies the desired inequality. The stated no-zero-row
hypothesis in that presentation causes no restriction: with a zero row, per(A)
and at least one complementary-block permanent are zero. Iteration also proves
nonnegativity of all PSD principal permanents and their diagonal-product lower
bound. Singular PSD Laplacians are therefore fully included.

Djokovic notes a correction to an equality condition in Lieb's original paper;
that correction does not alter coefficient nonnegativity or the non-strict
inequality used by the candidate. No equality characterization is imported.

3. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE CANDIDATE'S UNIVERSAL LEMMAS

3.1 Cycle-gas upper bound: PASS
For symmetric entrywise-nonnegative zero-diagonal C with row sums <=1/2,
nontrivial cycles of a permutation form a vertex-disjoint family. Expanding
product_gamma(1+w_gamma) admits every such family and additional intersecting
families, all with nonnegative weights. Thus
  log per(I+C) <= sum_gamma w_gamma.
A directed 2-cycle is a single cyclic object; trace(C^2)/2 counts its two possible
starting vertices correctly. For k>=3, trace(C^k)/k counts each oriented simple
cycle once, together with additional nonnegative nonsimple closed walks.
No reversal division by 2 is permissible or used for longer directed cycles.

Symmetry gives real eigenvalues, and the row-sum bound gives |lambda|<=1/2.
For every k>=2, lambda^k <= (1/2)^(k-2) lambda^2, including negative lambda and
odd k. Hence trace(C^k) has the needed upper bound. It is also nonnegative
independently, from the closed-walk expansion. The infinite series converges,
although a finite graph has only finitely many simple cycles. The numerical
bound is exactly
  sum_{k>=2} 2^{-(k-2)}/k
  <= 1/2 + 1/6 + (1/4)(1/2) = 19/24.
Multiplication by trace(C^2)=2 sum_e c_e^2 gives 19/12. The empty matrix and
edgeless support give per=1 and logarithm 0; logarithms have no zero argument.

3.2 Matching feasibility: PASS
For a 2-vertex-connected noncycle simple graph, all degrees are >=2. Set
  c_uv=1/(d_u d_v), x_uv=(9/5)c_uv.
Vertex feasibility follows from sum_{u~v} 1/d_u <= d_v/2, so incident x-mass
is <=9/10.

For any U of size >=2, a vertex is exceptional if its full degree is >=3 or it
has a neighbor outside U. There are at least two exceptions:
* If U is proper and only v can be exceptional, deleting v separates the
  nonempty sets U\{v} and V\U. If no vertex is exceptional, connectivity itself
  is contradicted.
* If U=V, absence of exceptions makes the connected graph a cycle. If there is
  only one high-degree vertex v, deleting v gives a connected maximum-degree-2
  graph with at least three distinct degree-1 vertices, impossible. Simplicity
  ensures those neighbors are distinct and each loses exactly one incident edge.

The inequality 2/(d_u d_v)<=1/d_u^2+1/d_v^2 yields
  4 sum_{e in E(U)} c_e <= 2 sum_{v in U} d_U(v)/d_v^2.
Each ordinary vertex contributes at most 1 to the last sum; each exceptional
vertex lowers its contribution by >=1/3. For degree 2 with an external neighbor,
the drop is even >=1/2. Therefore the right side is <=|U|-2/3.
For odd |U|>=5, multiplying by 9/20 gives
  sum x_e <=(9|U|-6)/20 <=(|U|-1)/2,
since the last comparison is precisely |U|>=4.

For a three-vertex U with at most two edges, c-mass<=1/2 and x-mass<=9/10.
If it is a triangle, at least two of its vertices have degree >=3: otherwise
the possible unique attachment vertex is a cut vertex, or the whole graph is
the excluded triangle. Therefore c-mass<=1/9+1/6+1/6=4/9, so x-mass<=4/5.
This covers all odd subsets, with no omission of U=V or small U.

3.3 Application to every principal submatrix: PASS
For arbitrary S in the noncycle block, every d_v remains the degree in the
whole block, not in G[S]. The normalized principal Laplacian M=I-B is PSD by
principal restriction and positive diagonal congruence. Its squared off-diagonal
weights are c_e, with 0<c_e<=1/4; C=B o B has row sums <=1/2 even after restricting
to S. The cycle bound therefore gives
  log per(M o M) <=(19/12) sum_{e in E(G[S])} c_e^2.

The restricted x-vector lies in the matching polytope of G[S], either by its
constraints or by restricting every matching in a convex decomposition. There
is a matching F whose log(1+c)-weight is at least the fractional weight.
The 2-by-2 PSD blocks on matched edges have permanent 1+c_e; unmatched singleton
blocks have permanent 1. Lieb therefore gives the logarithmic lower bound.
The calculus step is exact:
  [log(1+t)-2t/(2+t)]' = t^2/((1+t)(2+t)^2) >=0.
On [0,1/4], 2/(2+t)>=8/9. Thus
  log per M >=(9/5)(8/9) sum c_e^2=(8/5) sum c_e^2.
The gap is 8/5-19/12=1/60, strictly positive if an internal edge exists. Lieb
also ensures per M>=1, so the lower logarithm is valid despite singularity.

For h=product_{v in S}d_v>0, the unnormalization identities are
  per M=per(L_G[S])/h,
  per(M o M)=per(L_G[S] o L_G[S])/h^2.
This gives precisely the desired strong inequality. If there is no internal
edge, M=I. No assumption that L_G[S] is the Laplacian of the induced graph is
made or needed.

3.4 Cycle and bridge blocks: PASS
For a real PSD matrix with nonpositive off-diagonal entries and bipartite
support, every nonzero permutation monomial has nonnegative sign because all
nontrivial cycles have even length. Every monomial has magnitude at most the
diagonal product by the PSD 2-by-2 minor inequality. Therefore its square is
bounded by that product times itself. Summation gives hereditary strength,
including zero diagonal entries (then the corresponding PSD row is zero).
This treats K_2, every even cycle, and every proper principal submatrix of any
cycle, whose off-diagonal support is a forest.

For the full odd n-cycle, M=L/2 has off-diagonal -1/2. A maximum matching gives
  log per M >=((n-1)/2)log(5/4)>=(n-1)/9.
For C=B o B, the cycle gas contains exactly n two-cycles of weight 1/16 and two
orientations of the full n-cycle, each of weight 4^(-n). This is also correct
for n=3. The upper bound is n/16+2/4^n. The difference is
  f(n)=(7n-16)/144-2/4^n,
with f(3)=1/288 and f(n+1)-f(n)=7/144+6/4^(n+1)>0.
The claimed positive gap therefore holds for all required odd n>=3.

3.5 Diagonal increase and vertex coalescence: PASS
All factors multiplied in the closure inequalities are nonnegative: diagonals
and principal permanents of PSD matrices are nonnegative. No division by them
is used, so zero or singular cases remain valid.

For increasing a distinguished diagonal a by alpha>=0, permanent multilinearity
gives P+alpha R and the entrywise-square permanent gives
Q+(2a alpha+alpha^2)T. The final comparison reduces exactly to
  alpha(P-aR)>=0,
which is Lieb's one-index case. This applies separately to every relevant
principal submatrix, including a singleton with empty complementary submatrix.

For a one-point sum, the new matrix is PSD because it is the sum of the two
zero-padded PSD embeddings. The common-vertex permutation cycle cannot move
between the two sides without repeating that vertex. Its fixed-point case
contributes (a_1+a_2)R_1R_2. This verifies, without double counting,
  per A=P_1R_2+R_1P_2,
  per(A o A)=Q_1T_2+T_1Q_2+2a_1a_2T_1T_2.
The difference after applying strength to both sides is
  h_1h_2[a_2R_2(P_1-a_1R_1)+a_1R_1(P_2-a_2R_2)]>=0.
The factors in square brackets are nonnegative by Lieb. The same reasoning
works if either side has only its distinguished vertex.

For a principal submatrix omitting the common vertex, it is a direct sum of
principal submatrices with their original diagonals retained. If it includes
the common vertex, restrict both operands while retaining that distinguished
index. In a one-sided restriction, the other operand contributes its original
nonnegative distinguished diagonal; the diagonal-increase lemma handles this
exactly. Thus excluded neighbors never cause an incorrect degree reduction.

Finally, finite connected graphs decompose along their block-cut tree into
bridges and 2-connected blocks. At each identified vertex, block degrees add
to the degree in the assembled graph. All block types have been covered.
A singleton isolated component has zero Laplacian; any nonempty principal
containing that zero row has both sides zero. Empty principal matrices have
permanent and empty diagonal product 1. Direct sums handle arbitrary disconnected
and empty graphs. The all-principal universal statement follows.

4. TWO DIFFERENT GRAPH LAPLACIANS

For two same-order real matrices A,B let t_sigma=product_i a_i,sigma(i) and
u_sigma=product_i b_i,sigma(i). Then ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz gives
  |per(A o B)| <= sqrt(per(A o A) per(B o B)).
Applying the established self-Chollet inequality separately to two simple-graph
Laplacians (or matching-size principal Laplacian submatrices) yields
  per(A o B) <= per(A) per(B),
because their permanents are nonnegative. The signed odd-cycle monomials do not
invalidate Cauchy--Schwarz. More strongly the proof supplies the symmetric bound
  |per(A o B)| <= sqrt(per(A) h(A) per(B) h(B)).
This is not the asymmetric mixed strong inequality per(A o B)<=per(A)h(B).
Neither the mixed argument nor the base proof proves the general Hermitian PSD
conjecture or an arbitrary weighted-graph version.
