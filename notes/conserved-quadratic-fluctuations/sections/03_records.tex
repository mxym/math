\section{Speed records and a uniform incidence law}\label{sec:records}
Give each particle a passive mark $y_j(t)$, initially zero. At every accepted
C event add its incoming normal speed $B$ to each participant. O and rejected
contacts add zero. A particle never copies its partner's previous mark.
Define the random cumulative speed sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:random-H}
 \cH_\eps^p(t)=\frac1{2\mu}\sum_j y_j(t)
             =\frac1\mu\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_t^p}B_c.
\end{equation}
Its expectation is denoted $m_\eps(t)$. This random variable must be
distinguished from V's deterministic notation for its expectation.

With root phase/record variables $\xi_j$, let
\[
 F_h^\eps=\mu^{-h}\E\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_h\ \text{distinct}}
                      \delta_{(\xi_{i_1},\ldots,\xi_{i_h})},
 \qquad c_1^\eps=F_1^\eps,\qquad
 c_2^\eps=F_2^\eps-F_1^\eps\otimes F_1^\eps.
\]
Records at different times can be included as a fixed finite root-output
list. No positive separation of those times is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted one- and two-root bounds]\label{lem:weighted}
Uniformly over observation times in $[0,T]$, for $h\le2$ and nonnegative
integers $b_j$ with $\sum b_j\le2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 \left|\int c_h^{\eps,p}\prod_{j=1}^h y_j^{b_j}\right|
                                      \le C\mu^{1-h}.
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hvar}
 \sup_{t\le T}\operatorname{Var}(\cH_\eps^p(t))\le C\mu^{-1}=C\eps^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first bound actual records, then estimate all signed-branch tests globally.
In slab $i$ a final accepted component has at most $\Lambda_i$ labels and no
accepted edge to its complement. Its energy is conserved throughout the slab,
including before its prefixes merge. If slab-start speeds are bounded by
$V_i$, every later speed in that component is at most $\sqrt{\Lambda_i}V_i$.
Across the fixed slab list this gives
\[
 V_\eps=R_v\prod_{i=1}^L\sqrt{\Lambda_i},\qquad
 \max_{j,t}|v_j^p(t)|\le V_\eps.
\]
Its accepted multigraph has at most $\Lambda_i-1+\Gamma$ edges, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:record-clip-scale}
 0\le y_j(t)\le D_\eps,
 \qquad D_\eps=2V_\eps\sum_i(\Lambda_i-1+\Gamma),
 \qquad P_\eps=1+D_\eps.
\end{equation}
For fixed $L$ these quantities are fixed powers of $1+|\log\eps|$.

Append the passive coordinate to the H2 root-output maps and use
$\ell_\eps(y)=\clip(y/P_\eps,-1,1)$ on \emph{every} signed branch.
Compatibility reproduces the same accepted history, hence the same mark.
Kernel weights, spectators, fresh-label coefficients, and one-root regrouping
are unchanged. Pushforward has variation norm at most one. Required recorded
lines continue to zero. Thus the finite output algebra and H3 unweighted
error mechanism remain valid, just as for the accepted-contact count record
in F. The mark normalization changes no trajectory.

On the actual process $P_\eps\ell_\eps(y)=y$, while on any artificial branch
$|P_\eps\ell_\eps(y)|\le|y|$. The bounded test must be global: actual speed
support cannot be assigned to artificial independent birth inputs.
Write $\delta_h=\|F_h-\widehat F_h\|_{\TV}$.
Actual factorial masses are at most one by H1 and conservation of $N$, so
$\|\widehat F_1\|_{\TV}\le1+\delta_1$. Finite inversion gives, directly,
\[
 \|c_2-\widehat c_2\|_{\TV}
             \le\delta_2+\delta_1(2+\delta_1).
\]
Restoring degree at most two costs at most $P_\eps^2$. It leaves H3's
exponential remainder smaller than every fixed power of $\eps$.
Choose complexity and nonphysical initial-tail accuracies after this degree;
for example an unweighted $O(\eps^6)$ tail, restored by a fixed polylogarithm,
is $o(\eps^2)=o(\mu^{-1})$. For a later field covariance, its additional
factor $\mu$ is included before choosing accuracy. No weighted microscopic
remainder is used.

At fixed complexity $K$, total slab size $n$, and total birth energy $E$,
there are at most $n+C_K$ prescribed contacts and $B\le\sqrt{2E}$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:record-energy}
 |y_j|\le C_K(1+n)\sqrt E,
 \qquad
 \left|\prod_jy_j^{b_j}\right|\le C_K(1+n)^2(1+E)
                             \quad\text{if }\sum b_j\le2.
\end{equation}
This uses active energy on the realizable branch, including determined
redundant updates; unrealizable branches remain zero. It also dominates the
restored clipped output. With $m$ births, $a=m-1-p$ mergers, and $p$ physical
initial links, the exact connected normalization at $h$ roots is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
 \mu^{m-h}\eps^{2a}\eps^{3p}=\mu^{1-h}\eps^p.
\end{equation}
Continuation contributes no extra birth or activity factor.

We detail why the energy insertion preserves the size base. On a dyadic
energy bin of upper scale $P$, summing the next merger pair in slab $i$
costs at most $n_i^{3/2}\sqrt P$. Normal integration adds a fixed exponential
factor, ordered merger times give $\tau^{a_i}/a_i!$, and fresh labels give
$1/n_i!$ up to fixed complexity polynomials. The birth product after one
translation integration is $C^m e^{-bE}$. The source's energy-bin calculation
therefore gives, before the final Stirling estimate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pre-stirling}
 C^{n+a}\tau^a(1+a)^{a/2}
              \prod_i\frac{n_i^{3a_i/2}}{a_i!n_i!},
\end{equation}
up to a fixed polynomial. Reserving a fixed Gaussian fraction absorbs
$(1+E)$ in \eqref{eq:record-energy}. Equivalently, a fixed integer shift
$r$ of the energy-bin power has gamma ratio
\[
 \frac{\Gamma(s+r+1)}{\Gamma(s+1)}
       =\prod_{k=1}^r(s+k)\le C_r(1+s)^r,
\]
and costs only a fixed power of size. Since $\delta_i=n_i-a_i\ge0$ and
$\sum_i\delta_i=O_K(1)$, Stirling and convexity yield
\[
 \frac{n_i^{3a_i/2}}{a_i!n_i!}
 \le e^{2n_i}(1+n_i)^{\delta_i}n_i^{-n_i/2},\qquad
 \prod_i n_i^{n_i}\ge(n/L)^n.
\]
Inserting these into \eqref{eq:pre-stirling} gives
$C_K\mu^{1-h}\eps^p(1+n)^{D_K}\theta^n$ with
$\theta=C\tau\sqrt L<1$. Choose $L$ once for the fixed smaller Gaussian
exponent. Later fixed degrees change front constants and powers of size,
not that choice. Summing $n$ and adding the restored small errors proves
\eqref{eq:weighted}. The reasoning is uniform on partial slabs and finite
record lists.

Finally, for $S_y=\sum_j y_j$, the identical-index and distinct-index terms
give the exact identity
\[
 \operatorname{Var}S_y
 =\mu\int F_1 y^2+\mu^2\int c_2y_1y_2.
\]
Dividing by $4\mu^2$ proves \eqref{eq:Hvar}. The second integral may be
signed; its absolute bound suffices. The $1/2$ in \eqref{eq:random-H}
is necessary because each contact is recorded by both participants.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Speed-incidence mean]\label{lem:incidence-mean}
The same pasted dynamics satisfies
$m_\eps(t)=\E\cH_\eps^p(t)\to H(t)$ uniformly on $[0,T]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the weighted incidence theorem of V; the short proof from the stated
package is included. Remove microscopic and complexity errors with the
globally clipped degree-one test as above. At fixed size and bounded birth
energy the raw finite speed sum converges by H5, and clipping is eventually
inactive. The bound \eqref{eq:record-energy}, its Gaussian domination, and
the size majorant remove the energy and size cutoffs. Physical initial links
retain $\eps$ and cyclic terms vanish after the same weighted truncation and
domination. Bol-norm convergence replaces interface births by $f_{i\tau}$.
No qualitative normalized-record convergence is multiplied by $P_\eps$.

In the surviving finite limiting tree class, inspect contacts in reverse
chronological order. Project unused future auxiliary records. The latest
contact that meets no tested root has equal incoming C/O flux, opposite
signs, and the same root output, including its mark, so its choices cancel.
Repeat until the latest remaining contact meets the root. Delete that
contact at time $s$ using H5. Its two incoming components factor with their
original fresh-label weights and the combined-size cutoff. For the terminal
test $y$, the C-minus-O increment is exactly $B$, independently of both
incoming historical marks. Both components can therefore be projected onto
their unmarked phase points, whose measures are $f_s(x,v)$ and $f_s(x,w)$.
The one-root moment is
\[
 M_y(t)=\int_0^t\int B^2 f_s(x,v)f_s(x,w)
                         \dd x\dd v\dd w\dd\omega\dd s.
\]
The flux supplies one $B$ and the mark supplies the second. Absolute
summability removes the combined-size cutoff before replacing the incoming
components by their unrestricted product. A one-root insertion has no factor
$1/2$; the relation $\sum_j y_j=2\sum_c B_c$ then gives $H=M_y/2$.
The unused partner history was projected out, not replaced by a fresh zero
mark. The argument holds at every fixed time, including a partial final slab.
Both $m_\eps$ and $H$ are nondecreasing and $H$ is continuous. A fixed finite
grid with small $H$ increments sandwiches intermediate values, giving uniform
convergence from the fixed-time convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform incidence LLN]\label{prop:LLN}
For the pasted flow,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L2-LLN}
 \E\sup_{t\le T}|\cH_\eps^p(t)-H(t)|^2\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
For the true speed sum $\cH_\eps(t)=\mu^{-1}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_t}B_c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:true-LLN}
 \sup_{t\le T}|\cH_\eps(t)-H(t)|\longrightarrow0
                                      \quad\text{in probability}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given $\eta>0$, take a fixed grid whose successive $H$ increments are at
most $\eta$. Uniform mean convergence makes successive $m_\eps$ grid
increments at most $3\eta$ for small $\eps$. Every sample and every mean
$\cH_\eps^p,m_\eps$ is nondecreasing. Thus
\[
 \sup_t|\cH_\eps^p(t)-m_\eps(t)|
 \le\max_i|\cH_\eps^p(t_i)-m_\eps(t_i)|+3\eta.
\]
The squared expectation of the maximum is bounded by the sum of its endpoint
variances, at most $C\#\{t_i\}\eps^2$. First let $\eps\to0$ for this
fixed grid, then $\eta\to0$, and add $\|m_\eps-H\|_\infty$.
This proves \eqref{eq:L2-LLN}. On the good event in H6 the true and pasted
events, hence their entire speed-sum paths, agree. Its exceptional probability
tends to zero, proving \eqref{eq:true-LLN}. No squared bad-event comparison
is used.
\end{proof}
