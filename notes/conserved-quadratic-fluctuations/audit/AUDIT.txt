Independent audit and conserved-moment extension
7 October 2026

Verdict
=======

The native draft's three-moment synthesis is valid relative to the stated
operational history package H1--H6 and the exactly centered functional theorem
of entry 009. The missing two-root speed-record estimate follows from that
package by the globally clipped record extension proved below. No unbounded
positive-time test CLT is imported. The correct limit is

  Y(t) = A(t)(Z+a) + (0, H(t), 2 integral_0^t H(s) ds),
  A(t) = [[1,0,0],[t,1,0],[t^2,2t,1]].

Both the initial vacancy bias a and the collision term are needed. The two-root
factorial normalization and the factor 1/2 in the total incidence are correct.
The pasted uniform L2 LLN and the true uniform probability LLN are justified.
The product path-space extension with the exactly centered Schwartz field is
also valid; a compact-localization proof is supplied below to cover its
nonmetrizable first factor explicitly.

The endpoint Gaussian cutoff gate is valid under the source's compact initial
anchor assumption, with the iterated order epsilon -> 0, then R -> infinity.
It is a uniform marginal covariance gate, not a general supremum-over-time
cutoff theorem. The native draft's conserved identities already resolve this
distinction for its three coordinates, so it is not a blocker for that theorem.

Pasted prelimit covariances converge. The stated coupling alone does not prove
true prelimit covariance convergence. This failure of a proof method supplies
no evidence of physical covariance divergence. The rare-event example in the
draft correctly illustrates the logical limitation, rather than a physical
counterexample.

The substantive generalization here is the maximal 13-dimensional quadratic
family closed under free transport and co-located collision invariance, and
its intensive, translation- and Galilean-invariant moment corollary. Neither
requires a new positive-time polynomial test-space CLT or a cubic activity
calculation.

Dependency boundary: this audit verifies the pinned source statements and
proves the extensions from them. It does not independently certify the entire
upstream kinetic construction. No remaining obstruction to the conditional
synthesis was found.

2. Complete weighted two-root proof
==================================

Give every particle a passive mark y_j(t), initially zero. At every accepted
pasted C event, add its incoming speed B to both participant marks. Add zero
at O events and rejected contacts. Do not copy a partner's prior mark. These
marks change no trajectory or acceptance decision.

For root variables xi containing their terminal phase points and passive
records, define the ordered normalized factorial measure

  F_h = mu^(-h) E sum_{i_1,...,i_h distinct} delta_(xi_i1,...,xi_ih).

Here c_1=F_1 and c_2=F_2-F_1 tensor F_1. A record can be taken at any fixed
observation time; using a finite list changes record count, not particle
normalization.

Claim. Uniformly over the observation times in [0,T], for h=1,2 and
nonnegative integers b_j with sum b_j<=2,

  | integral c_h product_j y_j^(b_j) | <= C mu^(1-h).       (W)

The convention is y^0=1. Constants can depend on the fixed record list,
source bounds, T, and the single chosen auxiliary dynamics, but not epsilon.

Step 1: an actual-process clipping scale.

In slab i a final accepted component has at most Lambda_i labels and no
accepted edge to its complement. Its total energy is conserved even before
its prefix components merge. If incoming speeds are bounded by V_i, all
its later speeds are bounded by sqrt(Lambda_i)*V_i. Across the fixed L slabs,

  V_epsilon = Rv product_i sqrt(Lambda_i)

bounds every actual pasted speed. A connected accepted multigraph with at
most Lambda_i vertices and excess at most Gamma has at most
Lambda_i-1+Gamma edges. Thus each actual record satisfies

  0<=y_j(t)<=D_epsilon,
  D_epsilon=2 V_epsilon sum_i (Lambda_i-1+Gamma).

The definitions A_L=|log epsilon|, Lambda_i=A_i^30, and
A_(i-1)=Lambda_i^30, with fixed L, make D_epsilon a fixed power of
1+|log epsilon|. Set P_epsilon=1+D_epsilon.

Step 2: use a bounded test on every branch, including artificial ones.

Extend each autonomous root output by the passive record and test it with
ell(y)=clip(y/P_epsilon,-1,1). H2 applies because compatible autonomous
components reproduce the same complete root histories, hence the same
accumulated records. Its weights, incompatibility factors, spectator
cancellation, and fresh-label coefficients are unchanged. Required recorded
lines continue to zero. The variation estimate uses bounded output kernels
and their finite record count; it needs no derivative of this output.

On actual trajectories, P_epsilon*ell(y)=y. On any signed artificial branch,

  |P_epsilon*ell(y)|<=|y|.

One must clip globally on those branches. An actual-process speed bound
cannot be asserted as support of their independent birth inputs.

Step 3: restore degree two only after estimating microscopic errors.

Write delta_h=||F_h-Fhat_h||_TV. H3 gives
delta_h<=C exp(-c |log epsilon|^30), uniformly in the finite observation list.
Actual factorial masses are at most one, so ||Fhat_1||_TV<=1+delta_1. Directly,

  ||c_2-chat_2||_TV
  <=delta_2+delta_1(2+delta_1).

This follows by expanding Fhat_1 tensor Fhat_1-F_1 tensor F_1 one factor
at a time. The h=1 error is delta_1. Restoring raw total degree <=2 costs
at most P_epsilon^2. It remains smaller than every prescribed power of
epsilon. The retained high-complexity and nonphysical-initial tails have
arbitrary-power unweighted bounds; choose their accuracy after the record
degree. For example, accuracy epsilon^6 absorbs any fixed polylogarithm
and leaves an error o(epsilon^2), which is o(mu^(-1)). More generally,
accuracy is chosen after every explicit power of mu appearing in the
statistic. No weighted microscopic remainder is used.

Step 4: retain the raw energy bound on finite-complexity histories.

At fixed complexity K, total slab size n, and total birth energy E, there
are at most n+C_K prescribed contacts. H4 gives active energy<=E, even
after redundant elastic updates. Hence B<=sqrt(2E) and

  |y_j| <= C_K(1+n)sqrt(E),
  |product_j y_j^(b_j)| <= C_K(1+n)^2(1+E)   if sum b_j<=2.   (R)

These statements concern prescribed realizable branches. Missing branches
have weight zero. Inequality (R) also majorizes restored clipped tests.

The exact connected activity/Jacobian count is

  mu^(m-h) epsilon^(2a) epsilon^(3p)
  =mu^(1-h) epsilon^p,
  a=m-1-p,

where p is the number of physical initial links. Continuing a particle
through an interface introduces no birth and no new activity factor.

After integrating the common spatial translation the birth envelope is
C^m exp(-bE). Reserve a fixed Gaussian fraction for (1+E), and keep the
remaining fraction for the same pair-choice, ordered-time, and fresh-label
summation. Alternatively, the dyadic energy-bin power E^(a/2) is shifted
to E^(a/2+1); its gamma integral differs by a fixed polynomial in 1+a.
This is the decisive distinction from a new exponential cost per contact.

Explicitly, on an energy bin of upper scale P, the sum over merger pair
choices in slab i is bounded at each step by n_i^(3/2)sqrt(P). Summing
normal choices and integrating ordered times gives

  C^(m+a) tau^a (1+a)^(a/2)
     product_i n_i^(3a_i/2)/(n_i! a_i!)

up to fixed polynomials. The record insertion contributes only another
fixed polynomial in n and E. Put delta_i=n_i-a_i; sum delta_i=O_K(1).
Stirling's inequality and

  product_i n_i^(n_i) >= (n/L)^n

give the summable majorant

  C_K mu^(1-h) epsilon^p
     (1+n)^(D_K) theta^n,   theta=C tau sqrt(L)<1.

A fixed smaller Gaussian exponent may increase C. Choose L once to make
the resulting base less than one, as allowed by the source; front constants
and polynomial degrees, rather than this base, depend on moment degree.
This choice is independent of epsilon and subsequent fixed record orders.
Summing n and adding Step 3's restored errors proves (W). This proof does
not use a new cyclic small-ball estimate or an unbounded-test CLT.

Step 5: variance normalization.

Every accepted event contributes to exactly two marks, so

  H_epsilon^p(t) = (1/(2mu)) sum_j y_j(t)
                 = (1/mu) sum_{accepted events c<=t} B_c.

Its expectation is the supplement's deterministic H_epsilon^p notation;
here the symbol denotes the random cumulative sum. Distinguishing the
random sum from its expectation prevents a centering error.

For S_y=sum_j y_j, separating identical and distinct indices yields

  Var S_y=mu integral F_1 y^2 + mu^2 integral c_2 y_1 y_2.

Consequently

  Var H_epsilon^p
  =(1/(4mu)) integral F_1 y^2+(1/4) integral c_2 y_1 y_2
  <=C/mu=C epsilon^2.                                    (V)

The second integral is signed; its absolute bound suffices. Omitting
either the diagonal term or the factor 1/2 in H would be incorrect.

3. Uniform LLN and exact true-flow transfer
=========================================

The supplement's speed-incidence mean theorem gives
m_epsilon(t)=E H_epsilon^p(t)->H(t) uniformly. H is continuous, nonnegative,
and nondecreasing. Each sample H_epsilon^p is also nondecreasing.

For eta>0 choose a fixed finite grid 0=t_0<...<t_k=T with all H increments
<=eta. Uniform convergence of m_epsilon makes its grid increments <=3eta
for sufficiently small epsilon. Monotonicity sandwiches the values between
grid endpoints and gives

  sup_t |H_epsilon^p(t)-m_epsilon(t)|
  <=max_i |H_epsilon^p(t_i)-m_epsilon(t_i)|+3eta.

The squared expectation of the maximum is at most the sum of its k+1
second moments. By (V) it is O((k+1)epsilon^2). Squaring the preceding
inequality, first letting epsilon->0 for this fixed grid, then eta->0,
and adding ||m_epsilon-H||_infinity proves

  E sup_t |H_epsilon^p(t)-H(t)|^2 ->0.                    (L2)

The order of these limits is essential. No grid whose size diverges with
epsilon is required.

H6 couples true and pasted trajectories on the same initial configuration
with probability of any full-time discrepancy <=C epsilon^(21/20).
On its complement all accepted events and incoming speed marks agree.
Therefore, for the true speed sum H_epsilon,

  sup_t |H_epsilon(t)-H(t)| ->0 in probability.            (P)

This is probability transfer, not covariance transfer.

The needed true mean convergence has a separate valid proof. Conservation
of energy and the initial support imply, for either flow,

  sum |v_j(t)|^2 <= Rv^2 N,
  (sum |x_j(t)|^2)^(1/2) <= (Rx+T Rv)sqrt(N),
  sum |x_j(t).v_j(t)| <= (Rx+T Rv)Rv N.

Thus the absolute particle sums of all three quadratics are <=C_T N.
Holder's inequality on the failure event E_epsilon gives, for p>21,

  epsilon^(-1) E[(N/mu) 1_Eepsilon]
  <=C_p epsilon^[-1+(21/20)(1-1/p)]
  =C_p epsilon^[(p-21)/(20p)] ->0.

For p=42 the exponent is 1/40. Applying the exact virial identity to
the two flows cancels their common initial and energy terms, so the same
bound controls |E H_epsilon-E H_epsilon^p| uniformly. This proves
E H_epsilon->H uniformly. Equivalently it is eq:true-incidence of the
supplement. Merely differentiating an asymptotic spread-mean formula would
not supply this statement; the source supplies the stronger virial identity.

4. Mechanical identities, initial centering, and the process limit
================================================================

At one contact the pair energy change is zero, and

  x.(v'-v)+(x+epsilon*omega).(w'-w)=epsilon*B.

Streaming gives d sum(x.v)/dt=sum |v|^2 and
d sum |x|^2/dt=2 sum(x.v). For either flow, pathwise,

  pi_t(q_E)=pi_0(q_E),
  pi_t(q_V)=pi_0(q_V)+t pi_0(q_E)+epsilon H_epsilon(t),
  pi_t(q_X)=pi_0(q_X)+2t pi_0(q_V)+t^2 pi_0(q_E)
             +2epsilon integral_0^t H_epsilon(s) ds.       (M)

Rejected events do not contribute. Restarts change no phase coordinates.
Overlaps of pasted paths do not invalidate these identities: accepted
contacts still have the stated separation and incoming orientation.

Boltzmann's co-located collision invariants and streaming give the same
free moment formulas without the finite-diameter H terms. The deterministic
moment integrations are justified by the source's Gaussian velocity and
propagated spatial second-moment bounds. This deterministic cutoff argument
is not a fluctuation theorem for unbounded tests.

Initial bias proof. The insertion identity for the excluded-product law is

  F_1,0(x,v)=f0(x,v) P{K_x=0},

where K_x counts centers of an independent configuration in the radius
epsilon ball about x. The initial factorial upper bound and one-root lower
bound yield, uniformly in x,

  E K_x=epsilon (4pi/3)rho0(x)+O(epsilon^2),
  E K_x(K_x-1)=O(epsilon^2).

The first formula uses the smoothness of rho0 and the factor
mu*epsilon^3=epsilon. The elementary Bonferroni bounds

  E K_x-(1/2)E K_x(K_x-1) <=P(K_x>=1)<=E K_x

give

  F_1,0=f0[1-epsilon(4pi/3)rho0+O(epsilon^2)].

For every fixed polynomial q, compact initial support now gives

  a_epsilon(q)=epsilon^(-1)[E pi_0(q)-f0(q)] ->
  a(q)=-(4pi/3) integral rho0 f0 q.                       (A0)

This also applies to the mass, momentum, position, and angular momentum
tests below. The negative mass correction alone shows why the activity
cannot silently be replaced by the expected particle count.

Choose eta in C_c^infinity equal to one near supp f0. Initially every
particle lies in supp f0, so pi_0(q_i)=pi_0(eta*q_i), almost surely, with
equality of expectations. Entry 009's compact/Schwartz initial theorem
therefore gives, exactly rather than by a positive-time approximation,

  epsilon^(-1)(pi_0(q_i)-E pi_0(q_i)) -> Z_i,
  Cov(Z_i,Z_j)=integral f0 q_i q_j,
  epsilon^(-1)(pi_0(q_i)-f0(q_i)) -> Z_i+a(q_i).

Write Y_epsilon for the Boltzmann-centered vector of the three moments.
Subtract the Boltzmann formulas from (M) to get the exact path identity

  Y_epsilon(t)=A(t)Y_epsilon(0)
       +(0,H_epsilon(t),2 integral_0^t H_epsilon(s) ds).    (Y)

For the pasted law use (L2), for the true law use (P). The second term
converges uniformly in probability to the continuous deterministic c(t).
The first term is a continuous map of the finite initial vector into a
continuous polynomial path. Slutsky and (Y) prove convergence in
D([0,T],R^3), J1, to A(t)(Z+a)+c(t). In fact the residual in (Y) tends to
zero in the uniform norm. No independence between the initial vector and
H_epsilon is required because H has a deterministic limit.

The mean limit is

  a(|v|^2),
  a(x.v+t|v|^2)+(1/2) integral_0^t J_f(s) ds,
  a(|x+tv|^2)+integral_0^t (t-s)J_f(s) ds.

The limiting covariance is

  A(t)Sigma A(s)^T,
  Sigma_ij=integral f0 q_i q_j,
  Cov(Y_i(t),Y_j(s))=integral f0 r_i(t)r_j(s),

with r_E=|v|^2, r_V=x.v+t|v|^2, and r_X=|x+tv|^2.
Since f0 is a nonzero smooth probability density, its local velocity
variance is positive on a set of positive measure. Hence J_f(0)>0, and
the continuous collisional drift is nonzero near the initial time.

Uniform convergence of the true means was proved separately above and
in (A0); subtracting them gives the exact-centered limit A(t)Z. For the
pasted law, the centered H remainder tends to zero in L2 of the time
supremum. Initial covariance convergence follows from the initial
factorial-cumulant bounds (the scaled pair term is O(epsilon)).
Cauchy--Schwarz now proves uniform pasted prelimit covariance convergence
to A(t)Sigma A(s)^T.

5. Joint convergence with the exactly centered Schwartz field
=============================================================

Let E denote entry 009's strong-dual generalized-J1 path space, and let
mathcal Z_epsilon be its exactly centered microscopic field. Its laws are
tight and converge to the Radon continuous Gaussian law G. Evaluation at
time zero against each fixed eta*q_i is continuous in this path topology:
every allowed time change fixes zero. Thus

  Y_epsilon(0)=
  (mathcal Z_epsilon(0)(eta*q_i))_i+a_epsilon

is an exact equality, not a statement that the positive-time polynomial
is a Schwartz test. Define the continuous map

  F(z)=(z, t -> A(t)[(z(0)(eta*q_i))_i+a]+c(t)).

Continuity follows from finite-dimensional evaluation at zero and the
uniform continuity of the resulting finite-coefficient paths. The
continuous mapping theorem gives F(mathcal Z_epsilon)->F(G).
By (Y), the actual second coordinate differs uniformly in probability
from F's second coordinate by a quantity tending to zero.

Here is an explicit justification of that last perturbation step despite
E being nonmetrizable. For a bounded continuous function g on E x D(R^3)
and a compact K carrying all but arbitrarily small probability of the
field laws, the graph {(z,F_2(z)):z in K} is compact. At each graph point,
continuity supplies a field neighborhood and a radius in the ordinary
metric J1 second factor on which g changes by less than a chosen eta.
Shrink the field neighborhoods using continuity of F_2, and choose a
finite subcover of K. The minimum of the finitely many radii is positive.
Whenever z lies in K and the second coordinate is within that radius of
F_2(z), its g value differs by at most 2eta. The exceptional probability
tends to zero; outside K use the bound 2||g||. Let the probability outside
K and eta tend to zero. This proves the desired convergence of integrals
of every bounded continuous g on the product, without assuming a general
nonmetrizable Slutsky theorem.

Consequently

  (mathcal Z_epsilon,Y_epsilon)
  -> (G,A(.)[Z+a]+c),   Z_i=G_0(eta*q_i).

Only the three moments are Boltzmann centered here. The phase-space field
remains exactly microscopically centered. Its cross covariance is

  Cov(G_s(phi),Y_i(t))
   =integral f0(z) [U(0,s)phi](z) r_i(t,z) dz.

The r_i factor can be replaced by eta*r_i on supp f0. Independence of
the source Gaussian noise from its initial field proves this formula.
No polynomial mapping property of the backward propagator is assumed.

6. Hostile audit of the endpoint cutoff gate
==========================================

Let chi_R(z)=chi(z/R), with 0<=chi<=1, chi=1 on |z|<=1 and chi=0 on
|z|>=2. Fix a polynomial q of degree d and q_R^tail=q(1-chi_R).

First, all actual pasted endpoints satisfy

  |v_j(t)|<=V_epsilon,
  |x_j(t)|<=Rx+T V_epsilon.

Choose a polylogarithmic Q_epsilon>=1 dominating |q| on this actual
endpoint region. It is independent of R. On all signed branches use
clip(q_R^tail/Q_epsilon,-1,1). Restoring total endpoint degree two costs
Q_epsilon^2. Exactly as in Section 2, choose error accuracy after this
degree, the requested M, and the explicit covariance factor mu. The
restored error contributes O(epsilon^M), uniformly in R. All tests used
on the microscopic error remain globally bounded.

On a retained branch the tested record-bearing particle line continues
to zero. Its initial input is supported in |x|<=Rx: either a one-root
initial density dominated by f0 or a physical initial cumulant dominated
by products of f0. Before replacing these inputs by spatial suprema,
retain this compact anchor. The continued line is position-continuous,
and H4 bounds every prescribed active speed by sqrt(E). Hence, at any
record time,

  |v_j(t)|<=sqrt(E),  |x_j(t)|<=Rx+T sqrt(E).

With Euclidean phase norm, |z_j(t)|<=Rx+(T+1)sqrt(E). If a tail factor
is nonzero and R>=max(1,2Rx), this implies

  E >= R^2/[4(T+1)^2].

The raw endpoint polynomial obeys |q(z_j(t))|<=C_q,T(1+E)^(d/2).
A product of two factors is therefore dominated by

  C_q,T(1+E)^d 1_{E>=c_T R^2}.

Use one fixed Gaussian fraction to extract exp(-c R^2), a second fixed
fraction to absorb the fixed polynomial, and the remaining fraction for
the H4 size sum. These reservations are made once; L is chosen once for
the resulting base. Constants may depend on d but the size base need
not. One can also check this with

  Gamma(s+r+1)/Gamma(s+1)
   =product_{k=1}^r(s+k)<=C_r(1+s)^r

for integer r, which shows the energy-bin power shift costs a fixed
size polynomial. This gives, at h=1,2, retained connected bounds
C mu^(1-h) exp(-c R^2), uniformly in observation times. The compact
anchor is discarded only after it has furnished the energy implication.
Later interface birth inputs need no compact spatial support.

The exact field variance identity is

  Var zeta_t^p(u)=integral F_1,t u^2+mu integral c_2,t (u tensor u).

Thus

  sup_t E|zeta_t^p(q_R^tail)|^2
    <=C_q exp(-c_q R^2)+C_q,M epsilon^M.

The constants and restored microscopic remainder are uniform in R.
For the iterated cutoff limit only finite constants at each fixed R
would be needed, but the global clipping here proves the stronger
uniform statement. One must take epsilon->0 first, then R->infinity;
letting R exceed the actual polylog endpoint bound at each fixed epsilon
would otherwise give a vacuous zero tail.

For the three quadratics the true law inherits the marginal probability
gate through the full-path coupling and the first-moment Holder bound
of Section 3, uniformly in R. It does not inherit the pasted covariance
gate from that argument.

Uniform marginal variance is not uniform path approximation in general.
For example, on the unit time circle let U be uniform and
X_m(t)=1 when the circular distance t-U lies in [0,1/m), zero otherwise.
For every t, E X_m(t)=1/m and Var X_m(t)<=1/m; but the supremum of its
centered absolute value is 1-1/m for m>=2. This is only an illustration
of the logical distinction. The native theorem uses the monotone event
sum and exact identities to prove full path convergence, so it does not
commit this error.

The cutoff estimates also define the Gaussian L2 endpoint limits of
G_t(q_i chi_R). To see this without importing an unbounded target theorem,
apply the pasted tail variance bound to differences of cutoffs, pass to
their fixed finite-dimensional Gaussian limits, and obtain an L2-Cauchy
family. Its joint probability comparison with the already established
moment limit identifies its limit as (A(t)Z)_i. This is a statement about
the three endpoint coordinates and finite-dimensional covariances, not
an all-polynomial nuclear path theorem.

7. Precise covariance limitation
===============================

On the bad coupling event a quadratic fluctuation difference can be
bounded by C epsilon^(-1)(N/mu), uniformly in time. The available moments
therefore give only

  epsilon^(-2) E[(N/mu)^2 1_Eepsilon]
  <=C_p epsilon^[-2+(21/20)(1-2/p)].

Even as p tends to infinity the exponent approaches -19/20, not a
positive number. This upper bound cannot establish L2 convergence.
It also cannot establish divergence: an upper bound that increases
provides no lower bound on the actual covariance.

For a purely logical example, take an independent event A of probability
epsilon^(21/20) and a bounded centered W. Let

  X_epsilon=W+epsilon^(-1)(1_A-epsilon^(21/20)).

Its difference from W tends to zero in probability, and its uncentered
expectation correction epsilon^(1/20) tends to zero. Yet

  Var X_epsilon-Var W
   =epsilon^(-19/20)(1-epsilon^(21/20)) ->infinity.

This satisfies the scales of the comparison bound but is not a hard-sphere
example. An additional weighted estimate could establish actual physical
covariance convergence; this audit neither rules it out nor claims it.

8. Generalization: the maximal quadratic conserved family
========================================================

Define P_2^inv to consist of all degree-at-most-two real polynomials q
such that q(x+t v,v) is a co-located elastic collision invariant for
every real t. In three dimensions it is the 13-dimensional space

  q(x,v)=a+b.x+c.v+k|v|^2+x^T A v+d(x.v)+e|x|^2,
  A^T=-A.                                                (Q)

A basis is mass (1), position (three coordinates), momentum (three),
energy (one), angular momentum x cross v (three), scalar virial (one),
and spatial spread (one).

Maximality proof. Write a general quadratic as

  a+b.x+c.v+x^T D x+x^T M v+v^T F v,

with D,F symmetric. In a collision at the same x, momentum is conserved.
Writing pair velocities as their center velocity plus/minus half their
relative velocity shows that pair invariance requires g^T F g unchanged
under every reflection of g. A single reflection can send g to any
other vector of the same length. Hence F=kI. The velocity-quadratic
coefficient of q(x+t v,v) is F+t sym(M)+t^2 D. Requiring it scalar for
every t forces sym(M)=dI and D=eI, while the skew part of M is arbitrary.
This proves (Q), and also proves maximality among degree-two families
with this transport/invariance property. Its dimension in d dimensions
is 2d+4+d(d-1)/2, hence 13 for d=3.

Mechanical proof. Mass and momentum are constant. The position sum is
X(t)=X(0)+tP. At a finite-diameter contact the angular-momentum jump is
epsilon*B omega cross omega=0; its streaming derivative is also zero.
The skew term in (Q) therefore has no impulse or streaming derivative.
Energy is constant. The virial and spread are given by (M). Consequently,
for r_q(t,z)=q(x+t v,v), either microscopic flow obeys

  pi_t(q)=pi_0(r_q(t))
       +epsilon[d H_epsilon(t)+2e integral_0^t H_epsilon(s) ds].

The Boltzmann moment is f_t(q)=f0(r_q(t)). The whole finite-dimensional
family therefore converges jointly in D([0,T],R^13), J1, with

  Y_q(t)=Z_0(r_q(t))+a(r_q(t))
            +d H(t)+2e integral_0^t H(s) ds,
  Cov(Z_0(q),Z_0(r))=integral f0 q r.                     (G13)

Here Z_0 is a single Gaussian linear map on P_2^inv. Only the compact
initial tests eta*q are used to obtain it. Exactly centered limits are
Z_0(r_q(t)). The proof is the same finite-coefficient path argument as
Section 4, now with the explicitly conserved moment vector. Its joint
extension with the Schwartz field follows from Section 5 verbatim.
All q in (Q) have absolute particle sum <=C_q,T N, so the source's
first-moment mean transfer continues to apply. Pasted prelimit covariance
convergence follows from the same L2 event-sum residual. The true
prelimit covariance limitation is unchanged.

An anisotropic spatial quadratic x^T D x with nonscalar D fails this
closure: two transport derivatives produce 2 v^T D v, which is not a
co-located collision invariant. The scalar energy/virial/spread reduction
therefore does not silently cover the full anisotropic stress tensor.

9. Intensive intrinsic-moment corollary
======================================

The conserved family gives a useful nonlinear consequence. Let

  M_epsilon=pi_0(1)=N/mu,
  p_epsilon=pi_t(v)/M_epsilon,
  x_epsilon(t)=pi_t(x)/M_epsilon.

On N>0 define the per-particle intrinsic moments

  U_epsilon=pi_t(|v|^2)/M_epsilon-|p_epsilon|^2,
  D_epsilon(t)=pi_t(x.v)/M_epsilon-x_epsilon(t).p_epsilon,
  S_epsilon(t)=pi_t(|x|^2)/M_epsilon-|x_epsilon(t)|^2.

Assign any fixed value on N=0, whose probability tends to zero because
M_epsilon->1 in probability. Let p0=f0(v), x0=f0(x) and

  u0=f0(|v-p0|^2),
  d0=f0((x-x0).(v-p0)),
  s0=f0(|x-x0|^2).

Their Boltzmann values are (u0,d0+t u0,s0+2t d0+t^2 u0). Define compactly
supported initial observations by cutting off the three effective tests

  phi_U=|v-p0|^2-u0,
  phi_D=(x-x0).(v-p0)-d0,
  phi_S=|x-x0|^2-s0

on supp f0. Let Z^int be centered Gaussian with covariance
integral f0 phi_i phi_j, and a^int_i=a(phi_i). Then the fluctuations of
(U_epsilon,D_epsilon,S_epsilon), scaled by epsilon^(-1) about these
Boltzmann values, converge jointly to

  A(t)(Z^int+a^int)+(0,H(t),2 integral_0^t H(s) ds).        (INT)

Proof. Momentum and mass are constant, and
x_epsilon(t)=x_epsilon(0)+t p_epsilon. Algebra in (M) gives the same
triangular identities for these intrinsic moments, with collision
term epsilon H_epsilon/M_epsilon. Since M_epsilon->1, its unscaled event
term has the same uniform deterministic limit. At time zero the
differential of E/M-|P/M|^2 at M=1 is represented by phi_U; those of
V/M-(X/M).(P/M) and Q/M-|X/M|^2 are phi_D and phi_S. Expanding the three
smooth rational maps proves these formulas explicitly. The initial
13-vector is epsilon-close in probability to its deterministic value,
and is O_P(epsilon) away from it. On M bounded away from zero, the
epsilon^(-1)-scaled Taylor remainder is O_P(epsilon)->0. Thus the
ordinary finite-dimensional delta method supplies Z^int+a^int. The
triangular path identity and uniform event LLN prove (INT).

This describes thermal velocity spread, intrinsic dilation, and spatial
spread about the center of mass; the result is invariant under constant
translations and Galilean boosts. The effective tests integrate to zero
under f0 because normalization by particle count removes the leading
mass direction. This is a weak-law Gaussian conclusion and a limiting
covariance formula, not an assertion of true prelimit covariance transfer.
