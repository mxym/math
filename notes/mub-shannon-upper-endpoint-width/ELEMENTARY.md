# Elementary cross-check of the asymptotic counterexample

This alternative argument proves the asymptotic separation and positive-probability existence without invoking any sphere concentration or published entropy tail. It does not supply the explicit coefficients at d=2⁴⁰; see the separate [finite proof](FINITE.md) for those. The [audit](REVIEW.md) covers this argument.

**One basis.** For a fixed state and one Haar basis let E₁,…,E_d be independent unit-mean exponentials. The probability vector has law Eᵢ/ΣEⱼ. Put S=d⁻¹ΣEᵢ, Y=d⁻¹ΣEᵢ ln Eᵢ and b=E[E₁ ln E₁], which is a finite constant since |x ln x|≤1+x². Then its entropy is exactly ln d+ln S−Y/S. Set c_d=ln d−b.

Fix ε>0; it suffices to consider ε<1. Pick η>0 so small that |S−1|≤η and |Y−b|≤η imply |ln S−Y/S+b|≤ε/4. This is just continuity at (1,b) of a fixed function, independent of d. Truncate Eᵢ and Eᵢ ln Eᵢ to zero when Eᵢ>T, with T=d^(1/8). Any truncation occurs with probability at most d exp(−T). The expectations of the truncated variables tend to 1 and b by dominated convergence and so have bias at most η/2 eventually. Both truncated variables take values in intervals of length at most 2T² for large d. For independent variables in an interval of length R, the probability of an absolute centered mean deviation greater than s is at most 2 exp(−2d s²/R²). For completeness, the logarithmic moment generating function of a centered bounded variable has value and slope zero at zero; its second derivative is a variance under an exponentially tilted law, at most R²/4 (bound the squared distance to the midpoint). Thus it is at most λ²R²/8. Apply Markov to the product of these moment generating functions with λ=4s/R² and apply it again to the negatives.

Using the two bounded means with s=η/2, we have for one basis, uniformly in the fixed input,

    P(|H−c_d|>ε/4) ≤ d exp(−T)+4 exp(−dη²/(8T⁴))
                    ≤ exp(−d^(1/9))                                 (*)

for all sufficiently large d (depending on ε). Also |H−c_d|≤ln d+|b|=:D_d for all outcomes, since 0≤H≤ln d. By taking the expectation in (*) and allowing arbitrary ε, μ_d−c_d→0.

**Many bases.** For L=d+1 independent choices and a fixed input, label a basis bad if |H−c_d|>ε/4. When the fraction of bad bases is at most q_d=ε/(4D_d), the average F satisfies |F−c_d|≤ε/2. If more than q_d L bases are bad, at least ceil(q_d L) are bad. A union bound over subsets and independence across bases give probability at most 2^L exp(−q_d L d^(1/9)) by (*). This remains valid when q_d L is integral (it only enlarges the event). A deterministic net of radius ε/(4√d) has at most (1+8√d/ε)^(2d) points, as in the main note. If |F−c_d|≤ε/2 on the net it is ≤ε everywhere. The positive terms in the log failure bound are O_ε(d ln d); the negative term grows as a positive constant times d^(1+1/9)/ln d in magnitude. Therefore the probability of uniform deviation larger than ε tends to zero. Since μ_d−c_d→0, this proves supₓ|F(x)−μ_d|→0 in probability. With the fixed MUB gap (2) it independently gives the asymptotic upper-endpoint and interval-width counterexample. It is qualitative and does not establish the sharper radius in (5).

**Existence of competitor MUB designs explained.** For completeness, here is the standard finite-field argument for existence of d+1 MUBs when d is a prime power [Wootters–Fields](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(89)90322-9). On an orthonormal basis labeled by the elements of the field F of size d, define X(a)|z⟩=|z+a⟩ and Z(b)|z⟩=χ(bz)|z⟩, with χ any nontrivial additive character of F. The d² Weyl matrices X(a)Z(b) are mutually orthogonal for trace inner product: a nontrivial shift has zero trace, and any nontrivial diagonal character has zero sum. Arrange the indices (a,b) on the d+1 one-dimensional F-linear subspaces of F². The d Weyl matrices for each line commute, by the relation Z(b)X(c)=χ(bc)X(c)Z(b) and vanishing of bc−ae for indices (a,b),(c,e) on one line. They are normal and thus simultaneously diagonalizable. Their span is the whole diagonal space in such an eigenbasis, since its dimension is d. Distinct line spaces share I, and their traceless parts are trace-orthogonal. For any rank-one projectors P,Q diagonal in the two respective eigenbases, P−I/d is orthogonal to Q−I/d, giving tr(PQ)=1/d. This constructs a complete family, including in even characteristic. A common unitary conjugation can set any tuple's first basis to the standard basis without changing either sharp endpoint.
