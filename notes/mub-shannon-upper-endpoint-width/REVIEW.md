**Literature:** [Singh–Zhang–Pati, §III Theorem 1, Eq. (10)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.08939#page=3) exactly states the two-sided, natural-log, mean-centered tail used. A minor potential issue in the cited proof, not in the finite result: its Eq. (11) intermediate upper bound Σ p_i ln² p_i≤ln² d fails on the *full ball* for some small d (e.g. d=3, all p_i=e^{-2}). A simple repair: throughout the ball Σp_i≤1, so the surprisal tail Σ_{p_i<e^{-s}}p_i≤min(1,d e^{-s}). Integrating against 2s gives Σp_i ln² p_i≤ln²d+2ln d+2, whence ||∇H||²≤4(ln²d+2ln d+3)≤8ln²d for d≥21. Apply the same explicit sphere lemma, Eq. (4); for d≤20 the tail (10) is vacuous for u≤ln d and impossible events have probability 0 for higher u. The separate elementary backup bypasses this entirely.
