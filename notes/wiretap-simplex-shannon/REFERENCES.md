# What is prior, and what is compared here?

## The erasure-wiretap question

- Willie K. Harrison and Matthieu R. Bloch, [*On Dual Relationships of Secrecy Codes*, Allerton 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/ALLERTON.2018.8635841) ([author's publication record and abstract](https://www.bloch.ece.gatech.edu/publications/)), explicitly conjectured binary simplex and Hamming optimality as base codes of linear coset coding over the binary erasure wiretap channel. They identified sufficient submatrix-rank conditions and proved optimality against a restricted repeated-column class. See also their [ISIT 2019 paper on generator attributes](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISIT.2019.8849625).
- J. Pfister, M. A. C. Gomes, J. P. Vilela and W. K. Harrison, [*Quantifying Equivocation for Finite Blocklength Wiretap Codes* (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.05484), and subsequent work develop the exact equivocation–rank calculation. M. Shoushtari and W. K. Harrison discuss and extend earlier [duality identities for equivocation matrices](https://www.itsoc.org/archive/itw2021.org/paper/new-dual-relationships-error-correcting-wiretap-codes). Neither rank reduction nor duality is new here.
- W. K. Harrison, T. Welling, A. Swain and M. Shoushtari, [*On Best Erasure Wiretap Codes: Equivocation Matrices and Design Principles*, Entropy 27 (2025), 1245](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/27/12/1245). Definition 1 and §2.1 identify the performance criterion and the linear coset setting; the message dimension written $k$ there is $n-d$ here. Equations (11)–(15) provide the rank formulation and a stronger sufficient comparison, conditioning separately on each number of distinct revealed coordinates. Theorem 3 covers competitors with exactly one generator-column type repeated. §§7–8 explicitly distinguish this restricted comparison from the desired binary all-competitor result. This note gives the latter directly for the i.i.d. channel; it does **not** prove (15) separately for all reveal counts.
- D. Hunn and W. K. Harrison, [*Subspace Decomposition of Coset Codes* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09673v1), Theorem 3: local optimality of uniform nonzero column frequencies for binary Shannon equivocation loss, including realizable repeated lengths. Their Theorem 7 gives global optimality for the chi-squared criterion and is not a global Shannon theorem.
- A. Swain and W. K. Harrison, [*Best Linear Wiretap Coset Codes Must Maximize Minimum Distance*, ISIT 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISIT63088.2025.11195618); see also [Swain's dissertation](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/10766/). These results include necessary minimum-distance conditions, discussed in §6.2 of the later paper of Harrison et al. M. Pathegama and A. Barg, [*Universally optimal (wiretap) codes* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09040v1), §1, make a broad retrospective statement about Shannon-optimal families and cite Swain–Harrison. Their own erasure-channel Corollary 4.2 concerns **order-two** information measures. We record that discrepancy rather than treating either its introductory wording or our search as a proof of priority.

## Mathematical tools

- S. Dughmi, T. Roughgarden and Q. Yan, [*From Convex Optimization to Randomized Mechanisms: Toward Optimal Combinatorial Auctions*, STOC 2011](https://arxiv.org/html/1103.0040v3), Lemma 4.2 (§4.3) and Claims 4.5–4.6: Poisson rounding of matroid rank, discrete Hessians and negative semidefiniteness. The [main proof](PROOF.md) specializes their argument and uses the empty projective sample to obtain a strict comparison.
- S. Dughmi, [*A Truthful Randomized Mechanism for Combinatorial Public Projects via Convex Optimization*, EC 2011](https://arxiv.org/html/1103.0041v2), Lemma 3.2 and §3.3: expected matroid-rank-sum welfare for the bounded lottery is concave in the fractional allocation. In this lottery a fixed number of proposals is drawn **with replacement**. The [second derivation](FIXED_DRAWS.md) uses the same discrete-Hessian mechanism, proved there in the relevant full-simplex parametrization. Neither concavity method is presented as new.

## Other optimization targets

- R. Gross, Y. Grunbaum, M. Bertuzzo, E. Yaakobi and A. Ravagnani, [*Optimal Codes for the Coverage Depth Problem and the Performance of Random Codes* (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07370v1), §§IV and VI, establish simplex/Hamming results for the expected number of draws to full span. Their projective multiplicities, symmetry averaging and fixed-size independent-set comparisons are relevant prior ideas; full-span stopping time is not Shannon equivocation. X. Kong, O. Elishco, C. Wang and T. M. Duman [also prove uniqueness for the single-container simplex coverage-time problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02931). The [truReturing coverage-depth report](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Library/SparseCoding/bertuzzo2026coveragedepth.html) likewise concerns that objective and full-span failure.
- Y.-S. Su, H.-Y. Lin and M.-C. Chiu ([ISITA 2026, authors' abstract](https://hsuanyin-lin.com/docs/abstracts/YSSu-HYLin-MCChiu_26isita.html)) use average total variation for short $q$-ary erasure wiretap codes of lengths at most $q$. [Zhang, Tomlinson, Ahmed, Ambroze and Rodrigues (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1049/iet-com.2013.0889) study binary syndrome design on a binary **symmetric** channel, not the erasure channel here.

The scope of this comparison is the cited papers and announcements found in the literature search, not a certification that a similar argument has never appeared elsewhere. The claimed step is a global Shannon comparison of the specified linear coset codes by application of known matroid tools. No priority is claimed for the codes, rank identity, duality, projective symmetry, or the Poisson- and fixed-arrival concavity mechanisms.
