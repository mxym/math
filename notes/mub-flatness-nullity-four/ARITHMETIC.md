# All-dimensional arithmetic obstructions for a single stabilizer MUB spread

This part concerns a pure state flat on its support in every basis of one complete stabilizer MUB in ambient D=2^n. A complementary-pair argument below uses an actual spread; it is not asserted for an arbitrary fractional Pauli cover.

**Theorem.** An odd support has size one, in which case the state is a stabilizer. Every support S of size h divisible by four satisfies XOR(S)=0. In particular a four-point support must be an affine plane. Moreover, if any support has size divisible by an odd prime congruent to three modulo four, there is no full support in any of the spread bases, and XOR(S)=0 in *every* basis. In combination with FOUR.md this excludes supports of size six (and hence every support smaller than eight) for any nonstabilizer solution.

**Coordinates and local rationality.** Use a Clifford change taking the seed context to the computational Z basis. Every Hermitian Pauli expectation r_P is a signed sum of flat probabilities in the unique basis containing its label, hence rational. Pauli expansion gives every matrix entry of the density matrix in Q(i). In the seed, choose x₀ in S, make z(x₀)=1 and write ψ_x=z(x)/sqrt(h) on S. Then z(x)=h ρ_(x,x₀) is in Q(i) and has norm one. More generally each relative phase is a rational Gaussian unit-norm number.

Write π=1+i and let R be the localization of Z[i] at its prime ideal (π); its residue field is F₂. Conjugation preserves this prime, so a rational norm-one phase z has vπ(z)=0. In R/(π²)=Z[i]/(2), the only units are the distinct residues 1 and i. Record c(x) in F₂ by z(x) ≡ i^c(x) (mod π²). Minus signs vanish mod π².

**Chirps for arbitrary spreads.** Every complementary context is a graph {(a,Ma)}, with M a symmetric binary n×n matrix. There are D matrices and M−N is invertible for distinct M,N. One eigenvector of M has computational amplitudes D^(−1/2) i^Q_M(x), where
   Q_M(x)= Σ_j M_jj x_j + 2 Σ_(j<k) M_jk x_j x_k (mod 4).
Indeed Q(x+a)−Q(x)≡Q(a)+2x·Ma mod 4, and Q(a) has parity a·Ma, giving an eigenvector of P_(a,Ma); multiplying by the D binary characters gives the full basis. Consequently Q_M(x) mod 2 = diag(M)·x, and probabilities in this context are |T_(M,b)|²/(Dh) with
   T_(M,b)=Σ_(x∈S) z(x) i^(−Q_M(x)) (−1)^(b·x).
For each nonzero x, M↦Mx is injective (hence bijective) from the D matrices to the D vectors. The character sum Σ_M (−1)^(diag(M)·x) equals Σ_M (−1)^(x·Mx)=0. Fourier inversion shows diag(M) ranges exactly once over all binary vectors. This verifies the only spread property needed below without assuming that the spread is Desarguesian or linear.

**Parity and first moment.** If h is odd, then every term of T is 1 modulo π, so T never vanishes, and every complementary basis is full. The spread identity Σ_L 1/h_L=2 for a pure flat state (THREE.md or complete-MUB 2-design) then reads 1/h+1=2. Thus h=1.

For even h, in R/(π²) we have i^k=1+(i−1)(k mod 2) and (i−1)²=0. Hence
   T_(M,b) ≡ (i−1)[Σ_(x∈S)c(x) + diag(M)·XOR(S)] (mod π²),
independent of b. If the bracket equals one, then vπ(T_(M,b))=1 for every b. Thus the entire M basis is full. Flatness implies |T|²=h for every b in that basis. Since π² is associated to 2 and norm doubles the π-adic valuation, the usual rational valuation v₂(|T|²)=vπ(T)=1. If 4 divides h this is impossible, so the bracket vanishes for each M. The diagonal bijection forces XOR(S)=0 (as well as Σ c(x)=0 in this gauge). Exactly the same conclusion holds for any even h if no basis is full. For four distinct points in F₂^n a vanishing sum means they are precisely an affine plane.

**Odd inert primes (strong version).** For an odd prime p=3 mod 4, (p) is prime in Z[i] and conjugation preserves its valuation v_p, normalized by v_p(p)=1. For any pair of seed and complementary measurements, the unnormalized chirp Fourier matrix U with entries in {±1,±i} has inverse U*/D, also integral at p. Thus min-coordinate v_p of a vector is unchanged by U: one inequality follows by integrality of U, the reverse by integrality of U*/D. The seed vector uses z(x) (zero outside S), whose nonzero entries have v_p=0 by norm one. Consequently at least one T in the complementary basis has v_p(T)=0. All occupied entries there have equal norm Dh/h_M, so that entry gives v_p(h_M)=v_p(h). This holds for every pair, irrespective of full support. In particular if p divides one support size, it divides all; none is full because D is a power of two. Singleton support is then impossible, so by the parity argument every support is even and has vanishing XOR.

If h=6, the inert prime 3 forces XOR(S)=0. Translate S to contain zero (as h is even, translation preserves its XOR). The five nonzero vectors have sum zero, so its affine span has dimension at most four, and FOUR.md supplies a stabilizer, which cannot have a six-point support. Together with the odd obstruction and nullity≤4 result, any nonstabilizer flat example has h≥8 in *each* basis. None of these implications establishes the full conjecture.
