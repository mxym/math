# The first dimension in which an optimal product–join projection body requires nesting

**Research manuscript — 8 October 2026.** Prepared with AI assistance for `mxym/math`. Extends the certified exact arbitrary-tree Pareto classification through dimension 48. The geometry is inherited from entry 005 v2; all new arithmetic claims have replayable exact certificates. This is not an external human-referee report, a proof-assistant formalization of the whole theorem, or a mathematical priority claim.

## Abstract

We determine the **first dimension** in which permitting Cartesian products above existing affine joins strictly improves the optimum of the normalized projection-body volume, relative to the class consisting of joins of two-simplex Cartesian-product blocks. The transition occurs at **dimension 55**. For dimensions 1–54 the full point-generated product–join class has an optimal body in the two-layer class. In dimension 55 we establish exact, different maxima in the two classes; their strict separation is achieved by a concrete heterogeneous product/join expression. The proof combines an all-dimensional Pareto-dominance principle with two *independently checked* exact finite-state certificates: a 49–55 continuation of the previously certified full-tree result through 48, and a separate two-layer certificate through 55. Their new verifiers replay 2,523,858 and 430,360 exact operation comparisons, respectively. Ordinary and optimized Python results are compared byte-for-byte; regenerated producer certificates are byte-identical. This first-crossover result is for the **specified point-generated grammar** and does not assert an unrestricted convex-body maximum.

## 1. Projection-body calculus and the two classes

For a full-dimensional convex polytope \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\), let \(\Pi K\) be its projection body, and define
\[
 R(K)=\frac{|\Pi K|}{|K|^{d-1}},\quad
 g(d)=\frac{d^d}{d!},\quad
 c_d=(d+1)g(d),\quad
 M_{\mathcal A}(d)=\max_{K\in\mathcal A_d}\frac{R(K)}{c_d}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]
The constant \(c_d\) is the value of \(R\) on any \(d\)-simplex.

The class \(\mathcal C\) consists of the convex polytopes obtained from a formal point by finitely many **affine joins** and **Cartesian products**, with invertible affine equivalences on affine hulls permitted. A product with a point is neutral. The class \(\mathcal B\subset\mathcal C\) consists of all affine joins of finitely many point factors and blocks
\[
 B_{p,q}=T_p\times T_q,\qquad p,q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},
 \tag{1.2}
\]
where \(T_p\) is a \(p\)-simplex. Equivalently, in the \(\mathcal B\) expression tree every nontrivial product has two simplex children and no product node lies above an already nontrivial product/join block. These two grammars allow arbitrarily heterogeneous factor dimensions.

Entry 005 v2 defines and proves the positive affine invariant \(a(K)\) and the **exact** projection-body product/join identities. We use its state variables
\[
 D(K)=d+1,\qquad H(K)=1/a(K),\qquad
 Q(K)=\frac{a(K)R(K)}{g(d)},
 \quad\frac{R(K)}{c_d}=\frac{H(K)Q(K)}{D(K)}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]
For the formal point, \((D,H,Q)=(1,1,1)\). For \(A\in\mathcal C_r,B\in\mathcal C_s\), the join is
\[
 (D,H,Q)(A*B)=(D_A+D_B,\ H_A+H_B,\ Q_AQ_B),
 \tag{1.4}
\]
including joins with points. For \(r,s\ge1\), \(n=r+s\), \((h,u)=(H,Q)(A)\) and \((j,v)=(H,Q)(B)\), their product satisfies
\[
 H(A\times B)=\frac{n}{r/h+s/j},\qquad
 Q(A\times B)=uv\,\frac{g(r)g(s)}{g(n)}\frac{sh+rj}{n}.
 \tag{1.5}
\]
All states generated by these operations are rational. In particular for a simplex \(T_p\), \(H=p+1\) and \(Q=1\), and for a block \(B_{p,q}\) with \(n=p+q\),
\[
 H_{p,q}=\frac{n}{p/(p+1)+q/(q+1)},\qquad
 Q_{p,q}=\binom np\frac{p^pq^q(n+2pq)}{n^{n+1}}.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

## 2. The first-crossover theorem

**Theorem 1 (first strict nesting dimension).** For the two classes defined above,
\[
 \boxed{M_{\mathcal C}(d)=M_{\mathcal B}(d)\quad(1\le d\le54).}
 \tag{2.1}
\]
In dimension \(55\), both maxima are attained and have the **different exact rational values**
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \boxed{M_{\mathcal C}(55)}
 &=\frac{333068659627091928809841719168016922609375}
 {83816757831946947640666468303298107539456},\\[2pt]
 \boxed{M_{\mathcal B}(55)}
 &=\frac{9444402294359878125}{2393367762580799488},
\end{aligned}
 \tag{2.2}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{M_{\mathcal C}(55)>M_{\mathcal B}(55).}
 \tag{2.3}
\]
Thus \(d=55\) is the least positive integer dimension for which *no* maximizer of \(R\) over \(\mathcal C_d\) belongs to the two-layer class \(\mathcal B_d\).

Define \(A=B_{4,4}=T_4\times T_4\) and \(B=B_{5,5}=T_5\times T_5\). An **explicit global maximizer within \(\mathcal C_{55}\)** is
\[
 \boxed{K_{55}= A*B*B*\bigl[T_7\times(A*A)\bigr].}
 \tag{2.4}
\]
An **explicit maximizer within \(\mathcal B_{55}\)** is
\[
 \boxed{L_{55}=B_{5,6}*B_{5,5}^{*4}.}
 \tag{2.5}
\]
We do not classify **every** equality case and do not claim \(d=55\) is the first dimension at which *any* nested body appears among maximizers (there could be tied nested and nonnested maximizers earlier). The theorem precisely identifies the first dimension where nesting is **necessary to attain the maximum**.

## 3. The exact all-tree Pareto recursion

For fixed \(d\), write \((h,u)\succeq(j,v)\) when \(h\ge j\) and \(u\ge v\). Both (1.4) and (1.5) are increasing in each positive \(H,Q\) input. Therefore replacing a subtree by another same-dimensional subtree with no smaller \(H,Q\) cannot lower \(H,Q\) at the root, nor \(R\) (by (1.3)).

For each finite subset \(S\subset\mathbb Q_{>0}^2\), let \(\operatorname{Max}(S)\) denote the coordinatewise nondominated states. Starting with \(F_0=\{(1,1)\}\), form
\[
 F_d=\operatorname{Max}\!\left(
  \bigcup_{r+s+1=d}J(F_r,F_s)
  \ \cup\ \bigcup_{\substack{r+s=d\\r,s\ge1}}P(F_r,F_s)
 \right),
 \tag{3.1}
\]
where \(J,P\) apply the exact state operations (1.4) and (1.5). Empty or redundant products with the point are suppressed. Every nontrivial operation has factors of dimension strictly smaller than the output. Thus the recursion is finite in every fixed dimension.

**Lemma 2 (all-dimensional closure and attainability).** Every state of \(\mathcal C_d\) is dominated by some attained state in \(F_d\). Every point of \(F_d\) is attained by a body of \(\mathcal C_d\). Hence
\[
 M_{\mathcal C}(d)=\max_{(h,q)\in F_d}\frac{hq}{d+1}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

**Proof.** Induct on \(d\). The assertion is immediate for the point. Every other construction ends with either a join of smaller-dimensional bodies or a nontrivial Cartesian product of two positive-dimensional smaller bodies, up to an affine equivalence. By induction substitute dominating attained frontier states for both children. The coordinatewise monotonicity established above implies the substituted output dominates the original output. The finite candidate union in (3.1) contains the substitute. Its nondominated front has an attained state dominating that candidate. Conversely, each member of the candidate union is produced by an allowed geometric operation on smaller attained bodies, so every surviving state is attained. Finally the objective \(hq/(d+1)\) increases in both coordinates. \(\square\)

This is the all-dimensional Pareto theorem proved in [the preceding 48-dimensional supplement](../exact-product-join-finite-optima/paper.md), recalled here so the present finite extension's logical completeness is explicit.

## 4. The independent two-layer dynamic program

Define \(D=d+1\). A two-layer body is a join of an unordered finite multiset of point factors (each contributes \(D=1,H=1,Q=1\)) and product blocks \(B_{p,q}\) (each contributes \(D=p+q+1\) and \((H_{p,q},Q_{p,q})\) from (1.6)). Let \(E_0=\{(0,1)\}\) be a **formal empty-join identity**; note that \(E_0\) is not the geometric zero-dimensional point, whose D-coordinate is one. Set recursively for \(D\ge1\):
\[
 E_D=\operatorname{Max}\!\left(
     \{(h+1,q):(h,q)\in E_{D-1}\}
    \cup \bigcup_{\substack{p,q\ge1\\p+q+1\le D}}
       \{(h+H_{p,q},vQ_{p,q}):(h,v)\in E_{D-p-q-1}\}
 \right).
 \tag{4.1}
\]
As with Lemma 2, the join law is coordinatewise increasing, and every two-layer construction removes a final point or block to give a smaller empty-or-nonempty join. Thus induction proves
\[
 \boxed{M_{\mathcal B}(d)=\max_{(h,q)\in E_{d+1}}\frac{hq}{d+1}.}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The second certificate [`two_layer56.json`](certificates/two_layer56.json) includes *all* levels \(E_0,\ldots,E_{56}\), independently produced by [`code/build_two_layer.py`](code/build_two_layer.py). Its separate checker [`code/check_two_layer.py`](code/check_two_layer.py) verifies **5,611** attained, strictly nondominated states and **430,360** candidate joins against actual certified frontiers. For each \(D\le56\), it recomputes the exact maximum (4.2). This checker uses no all-tree producer and no arbitrary-tree frontier computation.

The two certified maxima agree **exactly** for dimensions \(49\le d\le54\); the all-tree checker cross-checks these fractions with the independent two-layer checker and confirms that the selected full-tree winners have two-layer witnesses. Together with the established result through 48, equation (2.1) follows.

## 5. Separate exact constructions at dimension 55

First compute the two small product blocks directly from factorials using (1.6):
\[
 (D,H,Q)(B_{4,4})=(9,5,175/128),\qquad
 (D,H,Q)(B_{5,5})=(11,6,189/128).
 \tag{5.1}
\]
The join \(A*A\) has \((D,H,Q)=(18,10,(175/128)^2)\), so \(\dim(A*A)=17\). A 7-simplex has \((D,H,Q)=(8,8,1)\). Apply the exact product calculus (1.5) with \((r,s)=(7,17)\):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H\bigl(T_7\times(A*A)\bigr)
 &=\frac{24}{7/8+17/10}=\frac{960}{103},\\
 Q\bigl(T_7\times(A*A)\bigr)
 &=\left(\frac{175}{128}\right)^2
   \frac{g(7)g(17)}{g(24)}\,
   \frac{17\cdot8+7\cdot10}{24}\\
 &=\left(\frac{175}{128}\right)^2
   \frac{103}{12}\frac{g(7)g(17)}{g(24)}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5.2}
\]
Finally join this body to one \(A\) and two \(B\) blocks. Its dimension is
\[
 d=8+10+10+24+3=55
 \quad (\text{equivalently }D=9+11+11+25=56).
\]
The \(H\)-coordinate is \(5+6+6+960/103=2711/103\). Therefore (1.3) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac{R(K_{55})}{c_{55}}
 &=\frac{2711}{672}\left(\frac{175}{128}\right)^3
   \left(\frac{189}{128}\right)^2\frac{g(7)g(17)}{g(24)}\\
 &=\boxed{\frac{333068659627091928809841719168016922609375}
 {83816757831946947640666468303298107539456}}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{5.3}
\]
The separate all-tree verifier proves that this exact attained value equals the **largest** \(HQ/56\) over the fully covered 55-dimensional frontier, establishing the first equality of (2.2) and the asserted maximizing construction.

For the two-layer winner, apply (1.6) to \(B_{5,6}\):
\[
 H_{5,6}=\frac{462}{71},\qquad
 Q_{5,6}=\binom{11}{5}\frac{5^5 6^6\cdot71}{11^{12}}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]
For \(L_{55}=B_{5,6}*B_{5,5}^{*4}\), one has \(D=12+4\cdot11=56\) and
\[
 \frac{R(L_{55})}{c_{55}}
 =\frac{24+462/71}{56}
  \left(\frac{189}{128}\right)^4Q_{5,6}
 =\boxed{\frac{9444402294359878125}{2393367762580799488}}.
 \tag{5.5}
\]
The *different* two-layer verifier proves that no other choice or number of point and two-simplex-product blocks can improve (5.5), establishing the second equality of (2.2).

The strict inequality between (5.3) and (5.5) is rational arithmetic; in reduced form the positive difference is
\[
 \boxed{\frac{662399852708916040442830158726516595207603266978125}
 {23913899202230402018080694993063261703236925958127616}>0.}
 \tag{5.6}
\]
This completes Theorem 1. \(\square\)

## 6. Why the finite proof is rigorous

The core inductive theorems (3.2) and (4.2) establish that a Pareto frontier is a **complete certificate for an entire recursive grammar**, not an empirical sample. The independent full-tree checker validates each *retained* state by a construction pointer and each possible binary operation on all lower retained states by an exact dominance comparison; it cannot justify its conclusion merely by reporting that the producer returned `true`. The separate two-layer checker verifies every possible block addition and point addition in exactly the same adversarial fashion. Their frontiers are finite in each dimension because no nontrivial product/join can have a parent dimension as large as its own output.

There are no numerical solvers or floating comparisons. The only mathematical inputs outside this note are the explicitly cited geometry identities for \(D,H,Q\) and routine exact rational arithmetic. The proof checks **attainability** and **upper closure** independently, as well as both explicit dimension-55 constructions. `sha256sum` pins the full certificate bytes, and normal/optimized Python outputs are byte-compared. The producer regenerates the exact all-tree extension and the entire two-layer certificate without third-party packages, and the independent checkers do not import either producer.

From repository root, the principal commands are

```sh
python3 notes/exact-product-join-finite-optima/code/check.py
python3 notes/projection-first-nesting-d55/code/check_all_tree.py
python3 -O notes/projection-first-nesting-d55/code/check_all_tree.py

No proof here classifies all maximizing expression trees, establishes a formula for all larger dimensions or identifies the unrestricted convex-body extremum.
