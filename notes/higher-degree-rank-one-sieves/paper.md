This supplement proves an all-degree restoration interface and a conditional
rank-one finite-state completion theorem, then gives exact cubic examples in
Z[theta], theta^3=2. Their periodic avoiding-component maxima are 6 and 56 for
two explicitly stated step sets, at supplied period-ideal indices 30 and 330.
The proofs and accompanying certificates are self-contained apart from clearly
identified standard arithmetic inputs: Dirichlet/S-unit ranks, ideal norms,
Beukers-Schlickewei, ray class fields, and Chebotarev.

No arbitrary-step, 26-neighbor, universal higher-degree moat, or globally
minimal-period theorem is asserted. Exact-integer computational checks and an
independent mathematical audit accompany the proof. They are not a formal
proof verification or an assessment of mathematical priority.

## 1. The useful new conditional engine

Let O be an order in a number field K of degree n, with its additive group
identified with Z^n. Let F be a finite symmetric set of nonzero coefficient
steps. Suppose a finite list of nonzero nonunit principal generators gives a
periodic avoiding set A_0. Choose any supplied finite-index additive period
lattice L, so A_0+L=A_0. The period need not be scalar.

Construct the **complete** finite quotient graph on A_0/L, retaining directed
step labels, loops and parallel edges. For each connected quotient component
C choose a spanning tree, quotient representatives r_v, and tree potentials
t_v in L. Put y_v=r_v+t_v, with signs chosen so every tree edge lifts exactly.
For every directed edge e:v -> u with step f, put

    h_e = y_v + f - y_u in L.

Let H_C be the subgroup generated by all h_e. This is exactly the subgroup
of closed-walk voltages: each non-tree edge closes with tree paths, whose
voltages are zero, while every closed walk has voltage equal to the sum of
its directed-edge voltages. Symmetry of F permits reversed walks, so subgroup
generation and realizable closed-walk translations coincide. All these objects
can be computed using exact integer linear algebra.

### Theorem 1. Rank-one finite-state completion

Assume rank(H_C)<=1 for every quotient component C. Assume also the arithmetic
supply of infinitely many distinct rational primes p admitting a principal
ideal (alpha_p) of index p in O. Then finitely many additional such principal
ideals make the F-step avoiding graph have only finite components.

Here are verifiable requirements and an explicit bound.

- If H_C=0, put V_C=|C|. Its lifted components already have V_C vertices.
- If rank(H_C)=1, compute a nonzero generator w_C of H_C and exact integers
  k_e with h_e=k_e w_C. Put K_C=max_e |k_e|, which is at least 1.
- For each pair (v,j) with v in C and 0<=j<K_C, select a different rational
  prime p_{C,v,j}>K_C and a principal generator alpha_{C,v,j} of norm +/-p.
  Require p_{C,v,j} not to divide N(w_C). Use distinct rational primes within
  this component; choosing all primes distinct across components is allowed.
- Put P_C=product_{v,j} p_{C,v,j}.

For the enlarged sieve one may take

    B = max( {1} union {V_C : H_C=0}
                   union {V_C(P_C-K_C) : rank(H_C)=1} ).

All the finite-state hypotheses, arithmetic witnesses, norms, exclusions and
bounds are exact checks. The prime-supply input guarantees these finite choices
exist. It does **not** guarantee existence of a rank-reducing initial gate sieve.

### Proof

For H_C=0, integrating all voltages gives finite lifted components, each with
one point y_v+lambda per quotient vertex v.

For rank one, every lifted component above C consists exactly of

    { y_v + lambda + m w_C : v in C, m in Z },

for a suitable translate lambda in L. This follows by lifting tree paths and
then closed walks: the attainable period translations are precisely H_C.
The representations are unique: different phases have different residues
modulo L, and w_C is nonzero. Each edge changes the level m by one of the
integers k_e, hence by at most K_C.

The ring O/(alpha_{C,v,j}) has p elements and is therefore F_p. Write its
surjective residue map as chi_{C,v,j}. The exclusion p not dividing N(w_C)
ensures chi_{C,v,j}(w_C) is nonzero: otherwise w_C=alpha z would imply
p divides N(w_C), by multiplicativity of the field norm.

For this particular lifted component, require the entire K_C-level slab

    y_v + lambda + (m_0+j) w_C,
    v in C, 0<=j<K_C,

to be deleted. Assign its (v,j) point to the corresponding principal ideal.
Each membership condition is the single solvable linear congruence

    m_0 chi(w_C) = -chi(y_v+lambda+j w_C) mod p_{C,v,j}.

The coefficient of m_0 is invertible. Distinct rational primes make the
Chinese remainder theorem applicable, giving one m_0 modulo P_C satisfying
all these conditions simultaneously. The chosen global sieve therefore deletes
that full slab and every translate of it by P_C w_C. The starting congruence
can depend on lambda; the list of excluded principal ideals does not.

A deleted slab of K_C consecutive levels blocks every edge from the levels
below it to the levels above it: such an edge would have level change at least
K_C+1. Between consecutive slabs there are P_C-K_C levels and V_C vertices per
level. Every surviving connected component has at most V_C(P_C-K_C) vertices.
Other deleted points only reduce components. This proves the theorem.

### Arithmetic supply in every order

Here is the precise class-field input and its application. Choose a positive
integer f with f O_K contained in O. Let M be the ray class field of K for the
finite modulus f O_K, and let E/Q be a finite normal closure containing M.
The class-field existence and Artin reciprocity theorems identify the ray class
group with Gal(M/K). If an unramified rational prime p splits completely in E,
each degree-one prime ideal p_K of O_K above p has trivial Artin symbol in M/K.
Its ray class is therefore trivial, so p_K=(alpha) for an integral alpha with
alpha congruent to 1 modulo f O_K. Consequently alpha belongs to O and
|N_{K/Q}(alpha)|=p.

The multiplication lattice alpha O has index p: its determinant in any
Z-basis of O is the field norm. Its inclusion in the kernel of the residue
map O -> O_K/p_K is automatic. The latter map is onto F_p because it contains
the residues of rational integers, so its kernel also has index p. The two
lattices are equal. Thus alpha O is a principal index-p ideal in O, even when
O is nonmaximal. Chebotarev applied to E/Q supplies infinitely many distinct
such rational primes. Removing the finitely many ramified primes and primes
dividing f does not change this conclusion.

The deep imported inputs are ray-class-field existence/Artin reciprocity and
Chebotarev, rather than an effective search bound. See J. S. Milne,
[Class Field Theory, V.3.6 and V.3.23](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf).
No class-number-one assumption is being made.

### Exact algorithmic scope

The ray-class/Chebotarev principal split-prime supply proved from the standard imported arithmetic inputs below provides the
required infinite set for every number-field order, including nonmaximal orders. Avoiding finitely many
primes dividing N(w_C), and choosing distinct primes larger than K_C, preserves
that supply. The present graph proof uses no further analytic input.

Equivalently, Theorem 1 can be used without any existence theorem by supplying
and checking the displayed finite generators directly. Given a certified
rank-one gate, enumeration of integral coefficient vectors, exact norm-prime
tests and the finite exclusions gives a terminating completion search under
that imported supply. This is not a terminating algorithm for arbitrary F:
no theorem here constructs the initial rank-at-most-one gate for arbitrary F.

The rank test uses closed-walk voltages, not the rank of the quotient vertex
set, apparent narrowness of a picture, or finiteness of the quotient. The
spanning-tree shifts and the gcd needed to obtain a generator of H_C must be
included; a merely primitive vector on the same rational line can be too small.

## 2. Explicit cubic arithmetic

Let

    O = Z[theta], theta^3=2,

with coefficient basis (1,theta,theta^2). The polynomial is irreducible by
Eisenstein at 2, so this is a degree-three field order. Multiplication by
alpha=a+b theta+c theta^2 has integer matrix

    [ a   2c  2b ]
    [ b    a  2c ]
    [ c    b   a ],

and determinant

    N(alpha)=a^3+2b^3+4c^3-6abc.

Take

    alpha_2=theta,           N=2,
    alpha_3=1+theta,         N=3,
    alpha_5=1+theta^2,       N=5.

Their ideals have the respective exact kernels

    a=0 mod2,
    a-b+c=0 mod3,
    a+3b+4c=0 mod5.

To prove each equality, multiplication of every matrix column gives residue
zero, the residue map is surjective (the coefficient of a is 1), and the
matrix determinant has absolute value p. Thus image and kernel have the same
index p. No ideal factorization or class-number assertion is needed.

Their product generator is

    gamma_30=theta(1+theta)(1+theta^2)=2+3theta+theta^2,
    N(gamma_30)=30.

The ring map

    phi_30(a,b,c)=a+8b+4c mod30

is well-defined since 8^3=2 mod30 and 8^2=4 mod30. It is surjective. Its kernel
is gamma_30 O: inclusion follows by multiplying matrix columns, and both
lattices have index30. Reduction modulo2,3,5 gives exactly the three kernels
above. The avoiding quotient therefore consists of the eight units modulo30.
The supplied period ideal has index 30 and contains 30 O, so 30 is a common
scalar period for this fixed sieve. No period optimality across other sieves
or choices of generators is asserted.

## 3. Six-axis-step cubic certificate: exact B=6

Define the meaningful full-rank coefficient step set

    F6={+/-1,+/-theta,+/-theta^2}.

The complete avoiding quotient modulo gamma_30 O has precisely two path
components:

    13 -- 17,
    29 -- 7 -- 11 -- 19 -- 23 -- 1.

All steps +/-1 are forbidden by parity. The other step residues are +/-8 and
+/-4. Exact lift sections for the eight states are

    1:  (1, 0, 0)
    7:  (1,-2,-2)
    11: (1,-2,-1)
    13: (1, 1, 1)
    17: (1, 1, 2)
    19: (1,-1,-1)
    23: (1,-1, 0)
    29: (1,-3,-2).

Every allowed directed edge r -> s with step f satisfies y_r+f=y_s as an
identity in Z^3. Thus all cycle voltages vanish, and every lifted avoiding
component is a translate by gamma_30 O of one of the two finite displayed
sections. The exact maximum component cardinality is B=6.

### The dimension-reduction proof, without a search

Before excluding alpha_5, parity fixes the odd constant coefficient a. Put
T=a+3k+1. Avoiding alpha_3 restricts b-c to the two adjacent values T,T+1 for
some integer k. Since an axis step changes b-c by +/-1 and cannot cross the
forbidden congruence class, every gate component is a doubly infinite zigzag:

    L_m=(a,m+T,m),
    U_m=(a,m+T+1,m),
    ... L_m -- U_m -- L_{m+1} ... .

It has translation direction w=theta+theta^2, of norm6. Modulo alpha_5,

    chi_5(L_m)=a+3T+2m,
    chi_5(U_m)=a+3T+2m+3.

If L_l is forbidden, then U_{l+1} is forbidden. All forbidden positions in
the path index j=2m for L_m and j=2m+1 for U_m are

    j=2l or 2l+3 mod10.

The cyclic gaps are3 and7, so the surviving path runs have sizes2 and6.
This proves the certificate structurally and explains why no scalar-grid
period tuning was needed.

## 4. A sharp obstruction to one-generator completion

Enlarge the steps to

    F8chain=F6 union {+/-(theta+theta^2)}.

This is an explicitly defined eight-step full-rank coefficient graph. It is
not the 26-neighbor coefficient graph.

Under the first two gate ideals (theta) and (theta+1), each zigzag becomes the
square of a bi-infinite path: every path-distance1 edge is an axis step, and
every path-distance2 edge is +/-w. The following is stronger than failure of
one tested period.

### Proposition 2. One additional principal ideal never suffices

For this fixed gate and F8chain, adjoining any single nonzero nonunit
principal ideal (alpha) leaves an infinite avoiding component.

Proof. Consecutive zigzag vertices differ by theta or theta^2. If both belong
to (alpha), then alpha divides theta or theta^2. The element theta is prime
since O/(theta)=F2. A nonunit divisor of theta^2 must be divisible by theta:
if alpha beta=theta^2 and theta does not divide alpha, primality forces
beta=theta beta_1, then alpha beta_1=theta, then beta_1=theta beta_2, making
alpha a unit. The same reasoning handles a divisor of theta. Hence (alpha)
is contained in (theta), contradicting the fact that both gate vertices have
odd constant coefficient.

Thus the vertices removed by one additional ideal are never consecutive on
the underlying path. Each missing vertex is bypassed by the path-distance2
edge, so all remaining path vertices are connected and infinitely many remain.
This proves the claim.

In particular the norm5 ideal alone cannot finish F8chain. An explicit
8-step nonzero-period witness for the norm2,3,5 sieve is

    (1,-1,0), (1,0,0), (1,1,1), (1,1,2), (1,2,3),
    (1,3,3), (1,3,4), (1,4,4), (1,4,5).

Its displacement is 5w=(0,5,5), which is in gamma_30 O. Every point avoids all
three ideals. Periodic concatenation gives an infinite avoiding walk. This
says nothing about existence of an infinite walk on irreducible elements.

## 5. Two-prime CRT slabs finish the eight-step cubic graph

Add

    alpha_11=-1+theta+theta^2, N(alpha_11)=11.

Its kernel is theta ->7 mod11, namely a+7b+5c=0 mod11. This follows by the same
matrix-index proof; 7^3=2 mod11 and 7^2=5 mod11.

The gate components above have two phases L_m,U_m and level jumps of size at
most1. To delete an entire level, require

    L_m in (alpha_5),    U_m in (alpha_11).

The conditions are

    m=-3(a+3T) mod5,
    m=-a-7T-7 mod11.

CRT gives one such completely deleted level modulo55 for every a,T. These
levels block all F8chain edges. The structural bound is at most2(55-1)=108.
This is an explicit instance of Theorem1 with V=2 and K=1.

The independent exact quotient certificate sharpens108 to56. The four-ideal
product is

    gamma_330=gamma_30 alpha_11=6+theta+4theta^2,
    N(gamma_330)=330.

Use the surjective ring map

    phi_330(a,b,c)=a+128b+214c mod330,

where 128^3=2 and 128^2=214 modulo330. Its kernel is gamma_330 O by the exact
matrix-index proof. The complete allowed quotient is the80 units modulo330.
The eight-step residues are +/-1,+/-128,+/-214,+/-12. There are216 allowed
directed edges and four quotient components, of sizes

    6, 9, 9, 56.

Unlike the F6 quotient, this graph has32 independent undirected cycles.
Consequently merely listing finite quotient components is insufficient.
The certificate supplies an actual integer lift section for every one of
its80 states. Every one of its216 directed edges satisfies y_r+f=y_s exactly.
All cycle voltages therefore vanish and the exact avoiding component bound
is B=56. The section identities are independently checked, not inferred from
a finite exploration cutoff.

## 6. A stronger associate-class restoration interface

A norm-cutoff restoration theorem is given below. The stronger interface in
this section uses the fact that only finitely many associate classes are
exceptional, together with the exact Beukers-Schlickewei theorem. It avoids the S-unit rank contributed by primes in
the generator norms.

### Theorem 3. Uniform intersections of finitely many associate classes

Let U=O^* have multiplicative rank r, let q_1,...,q_k be nonzero elements of O,
and put E=union_i q_i U. For every nonzero v in K,

    |{x in E : x+v in E}| <= k^2 * 2^{16(r+1)}.

The bound is independent of the sizes, norms and prime divisors of v and q_i.
It remains an upper bound for every subset of E. Overlap between associate
classes only overcounts the total.

For every order of signature (r_1,r_2), r=r_1+r_2-1. To justify this beyond
maximal orders, choose f with f O_K contained in O. The kernel of the finite
reduction map O_K^* -> (O_K/f O_K)^* has finite index. A unit in this kernel
and its inverse are both congruent to 1 modulo f O_K and belong to O, so this
kernel lies in O^*. Thus O^* has the same rank as O_K^*. The latter rank is
Dirichlet's unit theorem. This argument does not assume that elements outside
O with norm +/-1 are units of O.

Proof. Fix an embedding of K in C. For each ordered pair i,j, a solution
x=q_i u and x+v=q_j z, with u,z in U, gives

    (X,Y)=(-q_i u/v, q_j z/v),    X+Y=1.

These pairs lie in the subgroup of (C^*)^2 generated by U x U and the single
pair (-q_i/v,q_j/v). Its rank is at most2r+1. The exact Theorem1.1 of
Beukers-Schlickewei bounds solutions in its Q-closure by2^{8(2r+2)}, and the
subgroup itself is contained in that closure. The map from x to (X,Y) is
injective. Sum the bound over the k^2 ordered pairs. Torsion contributes no
rank; the normalization coefficients do not require additional places.

The imported primary theorem has the hypothesis of the Q-closure
of a finitely generated rank-R subgroup of (C^*)^2, and its numerical bound is
2^{8R+8}. [Primary paper, Theorem1.1](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/F-Beukers/publication/27708784_The_equation_xy1_in_finitely_generated_groups/links/53e731ea0cf25d674ea587e4/The-equation-x-y1-in-finitely-generated-groups.pdf).
The deep theorem is imported, not reproved.

### Corollary 4. Norm-independent restoration from k generators

Suppose a finite list of k nonzero nonunit principal generators gives an
F-step avoiding graph whose components have at most B vertices. Put

    D=max_{f in F} ||f||_infinity,  Delta=|F|,
    L_n(R)=(2R+1)^n-1,
    C=k^2 * 2^{16(r+1)}.

Then every irreducible F-step component has at most

    [1+L_n(D(B+1))*C] [1+Delta B]

vertices. The empty-generator and empty-step cases retain their obvious
stronger bounds B and1 respectively.

Proof. An irreducible divisible by a supplied nonunit q_i is q_i times a unit,
so the exception set lies in E. For each of the L_n(R) nonzero coefficient
differences with sup-norm at most R, Theorem3 bounds its ordered exception
edges by C. Therefore at most L_n(R)C exception vertices are nonisolated; each
exception component has at most1+L_n(R)C vertices. Take R=D(B+1). Two exception
vertices touching the same ordinary component are within R: that component
has at most B vertices, so a shortest connecting ordinary path has at most
B-1 edges, plus the two exception edges. Every exception vertex in one full
component is therefore in the same enlarged exception component. Each such
vertex touches at most Delta ordinary components, each of cardinality at most
B. This gives the displayed bound, exactly as in the preceding restoration
proof.

For this cubic signature (1,1), r=1. Our three-generator F6 certificate gives

    37(1+30366*2^32) = 4,825,576,145,682,469.

Our four-generator F8chain certificate gives

    449(1+24333984*2^32) = 46,926,635,792,162,881,985.

These remain very conservative irreducible cardinality bounds. The smaller
numbers B=6 and B=56 apply to the periodic avoiding graphs. No practical moat
radius, optimal irreducible bound or all-finite-step conclusion is claimed.

The broader bounded-norm theorem remains useful separately: a bounded-norm
set need not be supplied as a small fixed list of associate classes. The new
corollary is an improved interface for the specific exception set arising
from a finite principal sieve.

## 7. Bounded-norm intersections and arbitrary-order restoration

This complementary result applies when an exceptional set is not presented
as a short fixed list of associate classes. Let K have degree n and signature
(r_1,r_2), and let O be any order. For an integer M>=1 put

    E_M = {x in O : 0 < |N_{K/Q}(x)| <= M}.

Let S_M be the finite prime ideals of O_K above rational primes at most M,
and let U_M be the S_M-unit group. Its rank is

    r_M = r_1+r_2-1+|S_M|.

### Theorem 5. Translation-uniform bounded-norm intersections

For every nonzero v in K,

    |{x in E_M : x+v in E_M}| <= C_M = 2^{16(r_M+1)}.

One may use the coarser field-uniform bound

    C(n,M) = 2^{16 n (pi(M)+1)} >= C_M.

Here pi(M) counts rational primes at most M. The constants are uniform in v
and in the chosen order and coefficient basis for fixed K and M; the coarser
constant is uniform over fields of fixed degree n. They are not independent of
both degree and M. The condition v!=0 is essential.

Proof. For x in E_M the principal integral ideal x O_K has numerical norm
|N(x)|. Every finite prime ideal dividing it lies over a rational prime
at most M. Consequently x is an S_M-unit; the same holds for y=x+v in E_M.
This takes place in O_K and uses no ideal factorization in the possibly
nonmaximal order O.

For one embedding K -> C, the pair (-x/v,y/v) belongs to the subgroup generated
by U_M x U_M and (-1/v,1/v). The product group has rank 2r_M and the one added
pair raises rank by at most one. It satisfies X+Y=1 and determines x
injectively. Beukers-Schlickewei therefore gives
2^{8((2r_M+1)+1)}=2^{16(r_M+1)}. No places dividing v were added. Since at most
n prime ideals lie above each rational prime and r_1+r_2<=n, we have
r_M+1<=n(pi(M)+1), proving the coarser bound.

For the standard ideal-norm and unit/S-unit rank inputs see J. S. Milne,
[Algebraic Number Theory, Proposition 4.2, Theorems 5.1 and 5.11](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf).
The Beukers-Schlickewei theorem is imported with the exact scope stated in
Section 6; its proof is not claimed here.

### Lemma 6. Exceptional component counting

Suppose E is any set with at most C points x such that x,x+v belong to E,
for every nonzero coefficient difference v. Join points of E when their
coefficient sup-norm distance is at most an integer R>=0. There are
L_n(R)=(2R+1)^n-1 possible nonzero ordered differences. Thus the graph has
at most L_n(R)C ordered edges. Every nonisolated vertex is the initial endpoint
of such an edge, so at most L_n(R)C vertices are nonisolated. Each component
has at most 1+L_n(R)C vertices. This harmless extra one also covers isolated
vertices. The statement holds for every subset of E.

### Theorem 7. General restoration interface

Let F be a nonempty finite symmetric set of nonzero coefficient steps,
D=max_{f in F} ||f||_infinity and Delta=|F|. Let alpha_1,...,alpha_k be nonzero
nonunits of O. Suppose the F-step avoiding graph on
A=O\setminus union_i alpha_i O has components of cardinality at most B>=1.
An irreducible here means a nonzero nonunit x such that x=ab forces one of a,b
to be a unit in the domain O.

If the exceptional irreducibles are contained in a set E with the intersection
bound C of Lemma 6, every irreducible F-step component has at most

    max{B, [1+L_n(D(B+1))*C] [1+Delta B]}

vertices. For the principal-generator sieve one may take either

    C = k^2 * 2^{16(r+1)}

or

    C = C_M,  M=max_i |N(alpha_i)|.

An empty generator list gives the stronger bound B directly; an empty step
set gives cardinality 1 for every component.

Proof. If irreducible x=alpha_i u, nonunit alpha_i forces u to be a unit of O.
Thus exceptional irreducibles lie in union_i alpha_i O^* and in E_M, since
N(u)=+/-1. Every other irreducible lies in A; its induced graph has components
of at most B vertices and degree at most Delta.

Enlarge the exception graph using coefficient radius R=D(B+1). If two
exceptional vertices touch a common ordinary component, its two adjacent
ordinary vertices can be joined by a path of at most B-1 edges. Including the
two incident exception edges gives at most B+1 steps, hence distance at most R.
Consecutive exceptions along any finite original path are therefore adjacent
in this enlarged graph, either directly or through one ordinary component.
Consequently all exceptions in one original component lie in one enlarged
exceptional component and there are at most M_E=1+L_n(R)C of them.

Each exception touches at most Delta ordinary components, each with at most B
vertices. Counting these ordinary vertices and the exceptions gives
M_E(1+Delta B). A component without exceptions has at most B vertices. This
argument uses finite paths within a connected component and does not assume
in advance that the component is finite. It proves the stated cardinality
bound, including exclusion of infinite components.

### What a periodic certificate must prove

For nonzero alpha, multiplication on the coefficient lattice has determinant
N(alpha) != 0, so alpha O has finite index and contains some scalar sublattice
Q O. A finite family therefore has a common finite-index additive period.
This ensures a finite quotient is available, but its finiteness is not itself
a bound for the lifted graph. Every directed allowed quotient edge must carry
its step label and exact voltage. A section with zero voltage on every edge
proves finite lifted components of the exact quotient sizes. Conversely a
closed walk with nonzero voltage lifts to arbitrarily large translations and
an infinite component. This criterion holds in every lattice rank; it does
not assert existence of a successful principal sieve for every F.

## 8. What has and has not been established

Established:

1. A complete rank-at-most-one periodic voltage gate can be completed using
   finitely many norm-prime principal ideals, with an explicit CRT slab bound.
2. A full-rank degree-three six-axis-step principal sieve with exact B=6.
3. A full-rank degree-three eight-step principal sieve with exact B=56,
   obtained by a finite-state mechanism beyond degree-two gates.
4. An impossibility theorem for every single additional principal generator
   in the fixed two-ideal eight-step gate, explaining why multiple-prime CRT
   slabs are needed.
5. A norm-independent finite-associate-class restoration interface, with
   explicit improved irreducible bounds for both examples.

Not established:

- An initial rank-one gate for every finite F in a cubic field or order.
- The 26-neighbor graph, arbitrary bounded coefficient steps, a universal
  higher-degree moat theorem, or a general higher-arity entropy enrichment.
- Optimal scalar periods, independent mathematical priority, or formal proof
  verification. The examples are confined to the displayed coefficient basis.

## 9. Certificate contract and reproducibility

The package contains the unchanged complete JSON certificates
`cubic_f6_certificate.json` and `cubic_rank_one_f8_certificate.json`. Every
allowed state has one exact integer lift section; steps and quotient data are
explicit. The public author-derived checkers reconstruct all allowed directed
step-labelled edges and verify every section identity. Their metadata schema
now rejects booleans and floating-point replacements of exact integers. The
original verifier bytes are preserved separately, and exact unified diffs
document the changes.

`independent_check.py` imports no author verifier or search code. It uses
polynomial reduction for multiplication, the Leibniz determinant formula, and
Gaussian elimination over exact Fractions for ideal membership. It reconstructs
the quotient graphs and the complete two-ideal F8chain voltage gate. Its
15 targeted negative controls per certificate include wrong arithmetic,
omitted or duplicate states, changed steps, and a same-residue section change
with nonzero voltage. It also strictly rejects boolean/float metadata.

The 729 vectors in [-4,4]^3 provide 2,916 F6 and 3,645 F8 ideal-membership
cross-checks. These finite checks are auxiliary controls: global kernel
equalities follow from exact inclusion and lattice index, and lifted component
bounds follow from all-edge section identities. No finite exploration cutoff
is used as proof of global boundedness.

Run `python3 tools/reproduce.py` from the package directory. It verifies the
exact whitelist, hashes, derivative diffs, both checkers and their independent
counterpart normally and with `python3 -O`, and the saved result bytes. It can
