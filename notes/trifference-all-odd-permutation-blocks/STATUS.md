# Literature and novelty screen (November 2026)

The target is the exponential growth rate of unrestricted ternary trifferent codes. This note gives only improved lower bounds. All numerical comparisons below use bits per coordinate.

* C. Yuan and R. Zhu, [Improving the Trifference Lower Bound via Non-Perfect Hashing, arXiv:2611.11262v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11262v2), latest version located revised 12 November 2026. Theorem 3.2 gives `R(3)>0.2120639` (unrounded explicit bound approximately `0.2120639581`); Theorem 3.3 gives the absolute gap `1/250000` for all `q≥4` and supplies stronger per-alphabet examples in Table 1. They condition on one subset whose total count is odd, whereas the block in this note constrains the counts of *every* symbol. The length-14 formula gives a failure probability `Y=(25/81)^14 [1−4(7/9)^14+3(161/225)^14]/[1−(7/9)^14]^3`; `cert.py` checks `F^14<Y^11` exactly for our `9×11` construction. The sampling reduction and all claims that the old probabilistic bound can be beaten belong to earlier work; the proposed improvement is quantitative.
* J. Körner and K. Marton, [*New bounds for perfect hashing via information theory*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195669888800489), Eur. J. Combin. 9 (1988), 523–530. Their Tetra-code concatenation gives the earlier ternary bound `(log₂(9/5))/4 ≈ 0.21199923`. C. Xing and C. Yuan, [*Beating the probabilistic lower bound on q-perfect hashing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08792), Combinatorica (2023), give the inner-block reduction adapted in the above November paper. Both ingredients are explicitly attributed and proved in `PROOF.md`.
* [Han, Sun and Ge, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26376): polynomial prefactor improvement at the old Körner–Marton exponent; this is not a higher exponential rate. [Bhandari and Khetan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02390) improve the upper bound polynomially but have exponent `log₂(3/2)`, still well above this candidate lower bound. This note gives no new upper bound.
* [Li and Shi, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08230), concern `(q,q)` perfect hashing, not the all-`q` fixed-`t=3` result here. Their Fourier and spectral methods are prior work in hashing; Fourier inversion is not claimed new. [Brakensiek, Chen, Putterman and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19576), using output-list bound two, establish a leading normalized-rate tradeoff for large alphabets; the limit here instead concerns a constant additive gain over the explicit elementary bound in bits/symbol.
* [Bishnoi et al., The generalized trifference problem](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2026.3665439), [Della Fiore and Dalai, Bounds on k-hash distances and rates of linear codes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05239), especially the latter's Theorem 1 and discussion in section II.A, study positive relative hash distance as well. The Tetra-block positive-distance bound is already present in earlier work and is not a result of this note. [Bishnoi, D’haeseleer, Gijswijt and Potukuchi](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12938) already reach the classical zero-distance exponent within linear codes. The nine-letter, length-11 block here is not linear, so no improvement in those linear rates follows.
* Bounded searches by title/citations and keywords (all-symbol parity, all permutations, ternary hash rates, constant composition, generalized/perfect/non-perfect hashing and zero-error list recovery) found no matching finite count, ternary constant or explicit uniform gap. This is not a proof of priority. The permutation enumeration itself is an elementary labeled-count/derangements fact; no novelty claim for that combinatorial identity or the generating function in isolation. External subject-matter review still needed.
* [Wei and Ge, *Separating hash families with large universe II*, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41353v2) keep length fixed and send the alphabet size to infinity. That is different from the fixed-alphabet rate used here.
