# Primary sources and the exact question

1. Fionnuala Curran, *Quantum randomness beyond projective
   measurements*, [arXiv:2605.18291v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18291v1),
   also Quantum Science and Technology 11 (2026), 045095,
   [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1088/2058-9565/aeb3a9).
   Eq. (2) defines intrinsic guessing probability through
   decompositions of the input, Eq. (3) converts it to min-entropy,
   and Eq. (4) defines its optimization for a fixed measurement.
   §5.2 treats the qubit case. §7 of the publicly verified
   arXiv version asks whether higher-dimensional SIC POVMs have
   “the least maximal intrinsic randomness among all the unbiased
   MICs.” This is the question answered negatively in dimension
   three here. The printed journal body was not separately
   checked. This is an answer to an explicit question, not a
   correction of the positive qubit result.
2. Lane P. Hughston and Simon M. Salamon, *Surveying points in
   the complex projective plane*,
   [arXiv:1410.5862v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.5862v2),
   Advances in Mathematics 286 (2016), 1017–1052,
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2015.09.022).
   Definition 4.1 and Theorem 5.5 classify every qutrit SIC
   up to congruence to a member of the midpoint family.
   We use only that there is a linearly dependent triple of
   measurement vectors. We do not assert that all qutrit
   SICs lie in one unitary orbit.
3. Gabriele Cobucci, Raphael Brinster, Shishir Khandelwal,
   Hermann Kampermann, Dagmar Bruß, Nikolai Wyderka, and
   Armin Tavakoli, *Maximally non-projective measurements are
   not always symmetric informationally complete*,
   [arXiv:2508.03652v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03652v2),
   Physical Review Letters 136 (2026), 060201. This result
   concerns simulation by projective measurements with noise,
   a different objective from the intrinsic entropy maximized
   over inputs here.
4. Piotr Bereza, Wojciech Słomczyński, and Anna Szymusiak,
   *From minimal informationally complete measurements to
   orthocentric simplices and back again*,
   [arXiv:2608.00809v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00809v1),
   §7.4–7.5, is a recent reference citing Curran. Its
   correspondence and qubit remark concern the geometry of
   certain tight or skewed MICs, not the qutrit minimum of
   maximal intrinsic randomness.

5. Anna Szymusiak, *Maximally informative ensembles for
   SIC-POVMs in dimension 3*,
   [arXiv:1405.0052v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0052v2),
   Theorem 1 and §3, uses a dependent triple to obtain three
   zero and six \(1/6\) Born probabilities, minimizers of
   Shannon entropy for the stated SICs. The same section
   describes the qutrit Weyl--Heisenberg SIC family that
   includes (2) at \(p=1/2\). Blake C. Stacey,
   *SIC-POVMs and Compatibility among Quantum States*,
   [Mathematics 4 (2016), 36](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/4/2/36),
   §3, explicitly records the three-zero/six-\(1/6\) Hesse
   SIC probabilities. We do not claim those distributions
   are new. The intrinsic conditional min-entropy comparison
   between the SIC class and one non-SIC MIC is a different
   optimization from minimizing Shannon entropy for a
   fixed SIC.
6. Máté Farkas, Jurij Volčič, Sigurd A. L. Storgaard,
   Ranyiliu Chen, and Laura Mančinska,
   *Maximal device-independent randomness in every dimension*,
   [arXiv:2409.18916v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18916v2),
   Appendix B, gives the rank-one Weyl--Heisenberg
   construction of balanced informationally complete
   measurements. Rank-one informational completeness and
   extremality also appear in D'Ariano, Lo Presti and
   Perinotti, [arXiv:quant-ph/0408115](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0408115).
   Equation (2) is a standard orbit in a diagonal-unitary
   gauge. The contribution here is the parameter choice
   and the rigorous minimax separation, not the recipe
   for such orbits.

A bounded search on 27 November 2026 checked the exact
title/identifier and conclusion of Curran's manuscript,
articles citing it, and the phrases “qutrit SIC”, “covering
radius”, “least maximal intrinsic randomness”, “unbiased
MIC”, and “counterexample”. The primary references above
cover the known qutrit SIC classification and nearby but
different optimality criteria. No matching result for this
exact minimax objective was located; this is not a claim of
absolute priority. Nothing in the analysis depends on
computational optimization of a physical experiment.
