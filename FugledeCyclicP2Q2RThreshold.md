# Fuglede in odd order \(p^2q^2r\) when \(r>\min(p,q)\)

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal verification. This establishes the stated family, not all cyclic orders.

**Theorem.** Let \(p,q,r\) be distinct odd primes with \(r>\min(p,q)\). In the cyclic group of order \(p^2q^2r\), every nonempty set is spectral if and only if it tiles.

The previous preprint benchmark is [Fallon–Kiss–Mayeli–Somlai, arXiv:2308.16277v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.16277v4), Theorem 1.1, with \(r>p^2q^2\). Their proof already supplies key arithmetic and a reduction for the residual cardinality. Here [odd prime-power elimination](FugledeOddPrimePowerCoordinateElimination.md) handles all sets whose size omits a group prime, and a new simple counting observation gives the requisite mod-\(q\) cyclotomic zero when \(r>p\). This both lowers the threshold and avoids the preliminary large-prime arguments in that source. For example \(p=3,q=5,r=7\) gives the group of order \(1575\), outside their parameter range.

The tile-to-spectral direction is prior work: [Łaba–Londner, arXiv:2407.11374v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11374v1), Corollaries 1.4 (choose \(\alpha_3=1\) and no extra prime factors) and 1.6, in fact apply with no constraint comparing \(p,q,r\).

## Spectral-to-tiling direction

Relabel \(p,q\) so that \(p<r\). Let \((S,\Lambda)\) be an \(m\)-point spectral pair in \(G=\mathbb Z_{p^2q^2r}\). Write \(m_\Lambda(X)\) for the dual mask polynomial. We identify the dual with \(G\) using the standard cyclic pairing; the product character conventions only rescale coordinate labels by units, which do not change any difference orders or cyclotomic conditions. An order-\(d\) difference of points in \(S\) implies \(\Phi_d\mid m_\Lambda\), and conversely, if \(\Phi_d\) does not divide \(m_\Lambda\), there is no order-\(d\) difference in \(S\).

If a prime from \(p,q,r\) does not divide \(m\), delete its entire prime-power factor. The projected pair is spectral and has the same size; the remaining orders \(q^2r,p^2r,p^2q^2\) satisfy Fuglede by [Malikiosis–Kolountzakis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01328) and [Malikiosis](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2022.14). Lift the resulting tiling by taking the product of its complement with the deleted factor. If all of \(p,q,r\) divide \(m\), but \(p^2\) or \(q^2\) divides \(m\), then either \(m=|G|\), or \(|G|/\gcd(|G|,m)\) is a prime power. The latter is [Fallon et al., Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.16277v4#S4), which has no prime size constraint. It remains to handle \(\gcd(|G|,m)=pqr\).

For this remaining case, every \(p^2\)-fiber of \(S\) (fix the \(q^2,r\) coordinates) has at most \(p\) points. Otherwise it would contain differences of both orders \(p,p^2\), forcing both cyclotomic factors on the mask of \(\Lambda\), and then \(p^2\mid m\) on evaluating the product of those factors at 1. All \(q^2\)-fibers of \(S\) and \(\Lambda\) have at most \(q\) by the same reasoning. Moreover all saturated \(q^2\)-fibers in either set have a common tile complement within \(\mathbb Z_{q^2}\): a \(q\)-point set avoiding differences of order \(q\) is a transversal modulo \(q\), while one avoiding order \(q^2\) is an entire coset of \(q\mathbb Z_{q^2}\). One of the two orders must be absent across *all* fibers of that set.

**An \(r\)-zero modulo \(q\).** Suppose none of \(\Phi_r,\Phi_{qr},\Phi_{q^2r}\) divides \(m_\Lambda\). Inside each fixed \(p^2\)-coordinate fiber, points of \(S\) cannot have differing \(r\)-coordinates: the difference could have only one of those three orders. Each such fiber then has at most \(q\) elements. This gives \(m\le p^2q<pqr\), a contradiction. Thus at least one of these polynomials divides \(m_\Lambda\). Its reduction modulo \(q\) is a positive power of \(\Phi_r\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) by the elementary cyclotomic Frobenius identity (including the trivial exponent-one case when no \(q\) occurs in the order). Therefore \(\Phi_r\) divides \(m_\Lambda\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). In contrast with Lemma 3.3 of Fallon et al., no pure Fourier zero of order \(r\) in characteristic zero is needed.

For each \(d\in\{p,p^2,pr,p^2r\}\), consider the family of orders \(d,dq,dq^2\). We give separately the case that every family supplies a Fourier zero and the case that at least one family supplies none.

**All four families supply a zero.** The Frobenius identity shows that \(\Phi_d\) divides the projection mask of \(\Lambda\) onto \(M=p^2r\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) for all \(d\mid M\), \(d>1\); the \(r\) case was proved above. For clarity, reducing the mask modulo \(X^M-1\) to project preserves these divisibilities. Since \(q\nmid M\), the factors of \(X^M-1\) are pairwise coprime over \(\mathbb F_q\), so the projected multiplicities are all congruent modulo \(q\), by the factor \((X^M-1)/(X-1)\). Each multiplicity is at most \(q\). A nonzero common residue \(c\) would give every multiplicity exactly \(c\), contradicting \(q\mid m=cM\). Thus all nonempty \(q^2\)-fibers of \(\Lambda\) have size \(q\).

We use the finite common-complement consequence of [Pedersen–Wang's universal tiling theorem](https://journals.msp.org/mscand/article/view/880), in the form proved as [Fallon et al., Lemma 3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.16277v4#S3.SS3): sets with the same cyclic spectral partner admit a common complement if that partner tiles. This applies as well to a single set. If all nonempty fibers of \(\Lambda\) are subgroup cosets, the standard spectral subgroup reduction (Fallon et al., Lemma 2.2) shows \(\Lambda\) tiles because the quotient has order \(p^2 q r\), already known by [T. Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15174v2). The common-complement theorem then shows \(S\) tiles.

Otherwise all nonempty fibers of \(\Lambda\) are \(q\)-element transversals, so for some \(L\subseteq\mathbb Z_M\),
\[
\Lambda=\{(a,j+q f_a(j)):a\in L,\ j\in\mathbb Z_q\}.
\]
This digit decomposition is the one in Fallon et al., §3.3. Differences of order \(q^2\) within a fiber imply \(\Phi_{q^2}\mid m_S\), so the number of spatial points with \(q^2\)-coordinate \(t+qh\), for fixed \(t\bmod q\), does not depend on \(h\bmod q\). Write \(B_h=\{(x,t):(x,t+qh)\in S\}\) and \(C_j=\{(a,f_a(j)):a\in L\}\), as subsets of \(\mathbb Z_M\times\mathbb Z_q\). They both have size \(m/q\). For a difference of two points of \(B_h\) with equal \(t\), orthogonality in the original spectral pair gives orthogonality against every \(C_j\) directly. If the \(t\)'s differ by \(d\ne0\bmod q\), the zero inner product in the original pair takes the form
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\zeta_{q^2}^{dj}
   \left(\sum_{a\in L}\zeta_M^{(x-x')a}\zeta_q^{d f_a(j)}\right)=0.
\]
Coprimality gives relative cyclotomic degree \(q\) over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{Mq})\), so every parenthesized coefficient vanishes. Hence all \((B_h,C_j)\) are spectral pairs. The reduced group has order \(p^2 q r\), so \(B_0\) tiles. The sets \(C_j\) have common spectral partner \(B_0\), and therefore a common tiling complement. Transfer its \(\mathbb Z_q\)-coordinate by \(h\mapsto qh\); it tiles each corresponding translated layer of \(\Lambda\) within its residue class modulo \(q\), and consequently tiles \(\Lambda\) in \(G\). Finally \(S\) tiles by the same cyclic common-complement theorem applied to the pair \((S,\Lambda)\).

**One family supplies no zero.** Use CRT coordinates with \(P\in\mathbb Z_{p^2}\), \(Q\in\mathbb Z_{q^2}\), \(R\in\mathbb Z_r\), and write the first \(p\)-digit \(P\bmod p\) as the low digit. The following elementary observation is useful: a set of ordered pairs in which every two share a coordinate lies in a row or column. Indeed if two differ in the first coordinate, their second coordinate agrees, and every further pair must have that same second coordinate. In the four cases below, no restriction on the \(Q\) difference is necessary, because all three associated \(q\)-orders have been ruled out.

* If \(d=p\), at each fixed \(R\) and low digit, at most one full \(P\)-coordinate can carry points of \(S\). Each \(Q\)-fiber has at most \(q\) elements. There are \(pr\) cells, so \(m\le pqr\) and all cells saturate at \(q\). First tile each occupied \(Q\)-fiber with the common \(q\)-element complement. Then add the subgroup \(p\mathbb Z_{p^2}\) in the \(P\) direction, filling a full low-digit cell for each \(R\). The sums are unique.
* If \(d=p^2\), at each fixed \(R\) every occupied full \(P\)-coordinate shares one low digit. There are at most \(p\) such full coordinates, with at most \(q\) points each. Again \(m\le pqr\) and all bounds saturate. After tiling each occupied \(Q\)-fiber by its common complement, use a transversal to \(p\mathbb Z_{p^2}\) in the \(P\) direction, filling all low digits for each \(R\).
* If \(d=pr\), at each fixed low digit the set of occupied pairs \((P,R)\) has every two sharing a coordinate. Fixing \(R\) yields at most \(pq\) points in the cell; fixing \(P\) yields at most \(rq\). Since \(p<r\) and there are \(p\) cells, \(m\ge pqr\) forces each cell to be of the fixed-\(P\) type with all \(r\) choices of \(R\) and \(q\)-point \(Q\)-fibers. Tile the \(Q\) fibers and then add \(p\mathbb Z_{p^2}\) as in the first case.
* If \(d=p^2r\), in the projection to \((P\bmod p,R)\) every two occupied pairs share a coordinate. Therefore all points of \(S\) lie in a coset of a proper subgroup. After translation, restrict the original characters to that subgroup; the restrictions are still orthogonal and distinct, so the set is spectral there. The subgroup order divides \(p q^2r\) or \(p^2q^2\), each divisor of which is a Fuglede group by the quoted two-prime and \(p^a q r\) results. Lift the subgroup tiling by coset representatives.

This finishes all cases. The argument does not claim the case \(r<\min(p,q)\), nor cases with a factor 2 removed by the odd-prime-power theorem.
