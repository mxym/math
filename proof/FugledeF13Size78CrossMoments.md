# Size 78 in \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\): cross slices and moments through degree nine

Use the notation and conclusions of [the low-moment and grid stage](FugledeF13Size78GridMoments.md): line cap five, plane cap thirteen, and moments through degree seven for both members. Every projective line now meets each \(D(B)\) and \(U_B\) at least five times, because some affine slice contains at least six points, which cannot be collinear and must determine at least five directions by the planar census.

## 3. Moment-zero cross slices are impossible under the stated shapes

Suppose thirteen parallel affine slices of \(B\) have six points each and each is an affine copy of the cross \(H_0(1,0)\cup H_0(0,1)\) with its center deleted, where \(H_0=\{0,1,3,9\}\). Assume all slices have the same five directions. The unique-axis check of the [91-point geometric verifier](verify-fuglede-f13-size91-geometric.js) makes their two arm directions common. Choose fixed coordinates so they are the \(x,y\) axes and label slices by height \(z\in\mathbb F_{13}\). In a slice let \(c_x,c_y\) be its center and \(r_x,r_y\) its nonzero arm scales: each arm away from the center consists of \(r_i\{1,3,9\}\). The common five-direction mask fixes the multiplicative \(\{1,3,9\}\)-coset of \(r_y/r_x\); in particular \(r_y^3=\kappa r_x^3\) for a fixed nonzero cube \(\kappa\). Put \(w(z)=r_x^3\).

For a polynomial in planar coordinates of degree \(d\le7\), write \(P(z)\) for its sum on the slice at \(z\), viewed as a reduced polynomial over \(\mathbb F_{13}\). Moment vanishing through seven gives \(\sum_z z^jP(z)=0\) for \(0\le j\le 7-d\), so \(\deg P\le 4+d\) by the field power sums. Centered slice moments of degree 1,2,4,5 vanish, because the separate arms lie on the two axes and sums of powers on the order-three subgroup vanish unless the exponent is divisible by three. The first moments and pure second moments thus give \(\deg c_i\le5\), \(\deg c_i^2\le6\), and hence \(\deg c_i\le3\) without reduction wraparound. The moment of \(x^2y^2\) is \(6 c_x^2c_y^2\), of degree at most eight. If both center coordinates are nonzero polynomials this implies \(\deg c_x+\deg c_y\le4\), because the ordinary product has degree at most twelve.

Take \(Y=a x+b y\) with \(a,b\ne0\) and \(a^3+\kappa b^3=0\) (possible since \(\kappa\) is a cube). The centered cubic along \(Y\) vanishes, so its raw cubic is \(6 c_Y^3\) of degree at most seven, where \(c_Y=a c_x+b c_y\). This forces \(\deg c_Y\le2\). Neither \(c_x,c_y\) can therefore have degree three: cancellation would require both to have degree three, violating the degree-sum bound (if one is zero, cancellation is impossible). The pure fifth in \(Y\) is \(6 c_Y^5\), of degree at most nine, hence \(c_Y\) is affine. An affine shear makes \(c_Y=0\) and preserves moment constraints, centers and caps in the needed sense.

Take \(X=x\), write \(c=c_x\), and denote centered slice coordinates by \(\xi,\eta\) for \(X,Y\). The pure cubic in \(X\) is \(6c^3+3w\), hence \(\deg w\le7\) since \(\deg c\le2\). The raw mixed moment of \(X^4Y\) equals \(18 a c^2w\): expanding \((\xi+c)^4\eta\), only the centered degree-three term contributes, with \(\sum \xi^2\eta=3a w\). Thus \(\deg(c^2w)\le9\). If \(c\) had degree two this forces \(\deg w\le5\) (ordinary product degree at most eleven). Then the pure fifth in \(X\), \(6c^5+30c^2w\), has degree exactly ten, contradicting its degree-nine bound. Thus \(c\) is affine as well. Another affine shear removes it. Now pure sixth gives \(\deg(w^2)\le10\). The values \(w^2=r_x^6\) lie in \(\{1,-1\}\); any nonconstant function into \(\{u,-u\}\) for \(u\ne0\) has reduced degree 12 because its field sum is nonzero (the number of plus signs lies strictly between 0 and 13). Hence \(w^2\) is constant. The degree bound on \(w\) then makes \(w\) itself constant, by the same argument. Thus both centered arm sets are fixed, and the thirteen slices, after the affine shears, contain six full affine lines. This is impossible.

## 4. Use the cross obstruction to reach degree nine

For \(j=8\) or \(9\), suppose the form \(F=M_j^A\) is nonzero, so \(F\) vanishes on \(D(E)\). At most \(j-4\) rational line factors can be concurrent: if \(j-3\) are through a point, a nonfactor through it has at most \(1+j-(j-3)=4\) roots, contradicting the five-direction lower bound.

Suppose a tangent projective line has exactly five directions of \(D(E)\). Its thirteen affine slices each contain at most seven points by the planar census. Their occupancy profile is polynomial modulo thirteen of degree at most four, by the moments through seven. The univariate checker finds that constant six is the only profile of mass 78 and cap seven among all \(13^5\) quartics or lower. Thus each slice has six points. By the exact planar checker, all 36 normalized six-point completions with at most five directions are affine copies of the seven-point \(H_0\)-cross with one point deleted. Each determines all five directions, hence the common mask determines the two shared axes.

If one of these crosses has a full four-point arm in direction \(d\), compare it to the arm in direction \(d\) of each other slice (which always contains at least three points). The checker verifies that the difference of \(H_0\) with any scaled three-point subset of \(H_0\) has at least nine values. Hence those cross differences and direction \(d\) give at least ten projective points in each associated plane, forcing a line factor of \(F\) through \(d\). The affine plane through the chosen full arm of four points can contain at most three arms from other slices (each contributes at least three distinct points, and plane cap is thirteen). Thus there are at least four distinct factor lines through \(d\).

If their number reaches \(j-4\), each nonfactor line through \(d\) contains at most five zeros, and so its parallel \(E\)-slices have at most seven points, all uniformly six by the quartic check. There are at least nine such normal directions. The pencil through the four-point full arm then has occupancy at most \(9\cdot6+5\cdot13=119\), below \(78+4\cdot13=130\). This rules out four factors for \(j=8\) and five for \(j=9\). The only other case is \(j=9\) with four factors. Every nonfactor through \(d\) then meets \(D(E)\) in at most six points, so every corresponding slice has at most nine points (planar cap-five checker). At least five normals in the pencil are uniform. Even giving four disjoint factor normals the maximal cap, the total occupancy of the pencil is at most \(5\cdot6+4\cdot13+5\cdot9=127<130\). This rules out four factors for \(j=9\) as well. Therefore no six-point cross in the hypothesized five-direction pencil has its full four-point arm, so all are deleted-center crosses. Section 3 excludes that.

Hence under \(M_j^A\ne0\), \(D(E)\) meets every line at least six times. For \(j=8\), the projective degree-eight rigidity inequality with six-point lower bound from the geometric 91-point proof gives a contradiction (minimum 412 versus cap280). By symmetry both members then have moments through degree eight.

For \(j=9\), concurrence is now at most four by the six-point lower bound. The planar cap-five checker gives at most ten points with at most nine directions. Thus any affine line with at least four points would have pencil mass at least 130, but at least six uniform normals, at most four factor planes, and the cap of ten on all other planes give at most \(6\cdot6+4\cdot13+4\cdot10=128\). Hence \(E\) has line cap three. On nonfactor direction lines, the planar checker now gives at most nine points in any slice. The cubic occupancy profiles furnished by moments through degree eight have cap nine on these normals. Exact enumeration gives only constant six or a nonuniform profile of weight \(k=(\sum_t\binom{n_t}{2}-195)/13=3\) (104 profile polynomials). For any normal, trivially \(k\le21\) since each occupancy is at most thirteen and total mass is 78. Also \(|U_E|\ge\lceil 6\cdot183/14\rceil=79\), since it meets each projective line at least six times. There are at most nine factor normals. Thus the total weight is at most \(104\cdot3+9(21-3)=474\). But the projective incidence identity gives total weight exactly \(m(p^2-m)/2=489\). This contradiction proves \(M_9^A=0\), and likewise \(M_9^E=0\). Mixed moments of both vanish through degree nine.
