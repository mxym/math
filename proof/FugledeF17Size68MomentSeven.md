# Size 68 over F_17³: no full planes and moments through seven

Assume a spectral pair of size68=4·17 and use the notation U_B, D(B') and V_B as in the previous cardinality notes. Standard reductions give D(B')⊂U_B, every projective line meets U_B (pigeonhole on the partner), and no projective line is entirely in U_B. Each spatial line is contained in a uniform plane of its own member, so has cap4. A set of eighteen affine planar points determines all eighteen directions, so the affine plane cap is seventeen.

## No layers and no full planes

A layer means an occupancy profile consisting of four levels of size17. If two different normals were layers, project to the corresponding 4×4 grid. Its cells are spatial lines of cap4 and the entire member would have size≤64, impossible. Thus each member has at most one layer.

Any pencil with at least thirteen uniform normals has b≤5 nonuniforms and the reduced Radon congruence bounds every nonuniform profile degree by b−2≤3 (if b=1 it is constant directly). No nonconstant affine profile can have mass68. The exact centered quadratic and cubic census in [verify-fuglede-f17-size68-moment-seven.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size68-moment-seven.js) finds no such profile of mass68, including zero-residue lifts to17. Centering removes the linear coefficient of a quadratic and the quadratic coefficient of a cubic. Therefore the pencil's nonuniform profiles are only layers, and by the uniqueness and no-entire-uniform-pencil properties there is exactly one.

If either member has a full seventeen-point plane, the cap-five full graph lemma in [the full-plane bound](FugledeF17Size85DegreeEightGeometry.md) shows that this plane determines at least fourteen directions. This forces a layer in the other member, and repeating with a full plane of that layer forces a layer in the first. Each full plane's tangent contains the unique opposite layer normal, since the latter is the unique missing point on that plane's direction line. The opposite layer planes in turn give ≥14 U sites in the line dual to their normal, so the first member's full plane normals lie at the unique missing point of that pencil, i.e. its unique layer normal. In particular there is no lift to17 in an ordinary profile.

We now exclude the two simultaneous layers. With pair-count baseline 102 define the profile weight as \((\sum_t \binom{n_t}{2}-102)/17\). An unordered pair contributes in 18 perpendicular normals, so the total weight is \((18\binom{68}{2}-307\cdot102)/17=570\). This exceeds the sole layer's weight \((4\binom{17}{2}-102)/17=26\), hence each member has an ordinary nonuniform profile. Let R be the highest genuine nonconstant reduced profile degree in one member B and d=16−R. Then R≥4 by the preceding profile calculation, so d≤12. If R=16 then a degree-16 occupancy would have nonvanishing mass modulo17, impossible; hence d≥1. The projective moment form F_d is nonzero, and all prior moments vanish. Choose dual coordinates B=(x,f_s(x),s), A=(u,t,g_t(u)), for four supported levels each. To justify them, the opposite layer normal belongs to the tangent of each B layer and vice versa. In the B basis take the second axis as the opposite layer normal and the third dual normal as the B layer normal. Because each layer normal is nonuniform, the opposite set projects injectively along that axis; all layer slices have seventeen points, so each graph is over the full first-coordinate field.

Multiplying by a degree-three Lagrange indicator in s isolates each B layer and implies all its local planar moments through degree d−4 vanish (if d≤3 no such moments are needed). Hence F_d(u,v,w) has w-degree at most three: any larger w term would pair total horizontal degree at most d−4. Its nonzero dehomogenization at v=1 has a nonzero coefficient polynomial in u of degree≤d≤12. For at least five slopes a it is therefore nonzero as a polynomial in w, with at most three roots. For each such a and each x the A slice u−a t=x has four points (t,g_t(x+at)). Its differences, normalized by distinct t coordinates, have at most three slopes w/v because F_d vanishes on D(A). Four planar noncollinear points always determine at least four directions, so the four sites are collinear. Fix any three distinct levels t_i; their affine interpolation relation is Σ_i r_i g_{t_i}(x+at_i)=0 with all r_i≠0, for all x and at least five slopes a. For graph degree≥2, equate the coefficients of x^h,x^(h−1),x^(h−2) with h the maximal graph degree. The resulting polynomials in a of degrees at most 0,1,2 vanish identically, so their leading terms give a three-by-three Vandermonde system forcing all three degree-h coefficients zero. Thus all three graphs are affine, yielding a spatial affine line of seventeen points, contradiction. No layer or full plane exists. All affine plane occupancies are≤16 and all U pencils have at most twelve sites.

## Four-blocking under degree at most seven

Suppose a nonzero homogeneous form F of degree d≤8 vanishes on U_B and some projective line has at most three U_B directions. Its corresponding seventeen parallel affine slices in B' all have size at most four, since a noncollinear four-set needs at least four directions and line cap is four. By averaging they are all full collinear four-supports, so at least six have common axis e. Their quotient locations along e have line cap4 (five supports in a plane would give twenty sites). The cap4 six sites are noncollinear and determine at least five directions by the exact planar census. Cauchy–Davenport gives at least seven distinct transverse directions for each pair of parallel four-supports, so their radial projective line through e has at least eight D(B') sites. This forces it to be a factor if d≤7.

Assume first d≤7. The radial factor count k is at least five. Every radial nonfactor has at most 1+d−k≤3 U points, and any plane through the actual four-support having an outside point determines five directions, so every nonfactor plane in its affine pencil contains exactly that support. For k≤5, the total is at most 72+12k≤132, below the required 68+17·4=136. If k=d, every quotient direction of B' forces a radial factor (any cross direction lies on a radial line with only the axial root otherwise). The quotient has ≥17 sites; at >17 every direction occurs, while at =17 all fibers are full and its cap4 gives at least fourteen directions by the full graph lemma. Both contradict k≤7. The only remaining case is d=7,k=6. A radial nonfactor has ≤2 U points, so at least nine of its 17 slices use a common axis e'. Their quotient locations determine ≥5 directions (already true for six sites), hence force five radial factors through e'. Unless e'=e, the two radial factor families would give at least 6+5−1>7 lines. Thus e=e', and those nine quotient sites have at most six directions, contradicted by the exact cap4 census. This proves four-blocking for degree≤7.

For a nonzero degree-seven form F, put N=|U_B| and n_L=|L∩U_B|. Four-blocking and the twelve-site upper cap give n_L∈[4,12]. Only a line factor can make (n_L−4)(n_L−7) positive, so the sum is at most 7·8·5=280. Projective incidence makes the sum N²−181N+8596≥406, contradiction. Lower degrees lift to degree seven by multiplication; thus all moments through seven vanish because uniform directions annihilate the moments and no such form can remain. All reduced ordinary occupancy degrees are at most eight.

**Genuine degree-eight forms are not handled yet.** In particular, eight D sites on a radial line need not force an initial degree-eight factor: the transverse directions plus the axial e can exactly account for all eight roots. The geometric proof above asserts four-blocking only through degree seven.
