# Size 39 in F_13^3: moments through five and no layered direction

Suppose (E,A) is a spectral pair with 39 points. For each member B let D(B) denote its projective difference directions and U_B its normals with equal occupancies across the thirteen affine planes. We use the general preliminaries in [Ferguson–Iosevich, arXiv:2610.30712v1 §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1): D(B) lies in U of the partner, no U contains a full projective line, affine plane cap is 13, affine line cap is 3, and a pencil through an affine line containing a sites has total occupancy 39+13a. A layered profile has three entries 13 and ten entries zero.

## An almost-uniform projective pencil forces a layer

Suppose a projective line contains at least nine U_B normals, so there are 1≤b≤5 nonuniform profiles. Project B onto the plane along the common annihilator of the pencil. If f is the projected plane counting function and g_i(l_i) are the b nonuniform Radon profiles, the Radon identity is
```
Σ_i g_i(l_i(x,y)) = 3(b−1)+13 f(x,y).
```
Take each profile modulo 13 and represent it by its unique reduced polynomial of degree at most twelve. Homogeneous components in degree j≥b−1, j>0, have linearly independent columns l_i^j by the projective Vandermonde identity, and hence vanish. Thus all profiles have degree at most three. A nonconstant affine profile has mass at least 78, impossible. For genuine quadratics, after shifting the variable, enumerate a t²+c (a≠0): no choice has least-residue sum ≤39 with mass 39 obtainable by lifting zeros. For genuine cubics a t³+b t+c, the exact centered census gives only eight admissible triples, all with b=0 and c either 3 or 9, no lifts; they are (a,c)=(2,3),(3,3),(4,9),(6,9),(7,9),(9,9),(10,3),(11,3). The companion verifier checks both assertions by direct enumeration of at most 12·13² centered polynomials and the allowed integer zero-lift count (39−sum residues)/13.

If any cubic occurs, b=5 and all five profiles are genuine cubic, since any four distinct cubic leading columns are independent. Translate the projection coordinates to give the first two profiles center zero. The quadratic relation has only three surviving squared-form columns, which are independent, so the other three centers are also zero. The constant relation would require 15+6k≡12 mod13 for 0≤k≤5; the congruence gives k≡6 and is impossible. Thus all nonuniform profiles in the pencil are constant modulo 13 and necessarily layered. Now the constant relation forces 3(b−1)=0 mod13, so b=1. In particular a pencil through an actual affine line containing two or three B points cannot have nine uniform normals: its total would be at most 13·3+13=52, instead of at least 65.

## Degree four and degree five

Let F be homogeneous nonzero of degree d=4 or 5 and vanish on D(B). If a projective line meets D(B) in at most two points, its thirteen affine planes each have at most three B points, all collinear (a noncollinear triangle determines three directions). Hence all have exactly three, and at least seven supports are parallel to a common direction e. Their quotient locations cannot all be collinear, as five full triple supports would exceed the affine plane cap. Choose four noncollinear locations, determining at least four directions by the planar triangle lemma (any planar set with at most three directions is collinear or a triangle in odd characteristic). Each pair of parallel triple supports gives at least five cross directions by Cauchy–Davenport, plus e; thus at least four radial projective lines through e each have six D points and are rational factors of F. Any radial nonfactor has at most 1+d−4≤2 D points, hence uniform occupancy3. At least 14−d≥9 such normals lie in a pencil through an actual triple support, which is impossible by the previous paragraph. Consequently D(B) is three-blocking under F.

Write N=|D(B)| and n_L=|D(B)∩L|. Incidence gives
```
Σ_L (n_L−3)(n_L−d) = N² + (13−14(3+d))N + 183·3d.
```
For d=4 the right-hand side is at least 390 and the upper bound is 4·(13−3)(13−4)=360, because nonfactor summands are nonpositive and each factor contains at most 13 D. Therefore no F_4 exists, nor any smaller-degree nonzero form (multiply by a linear power). All moments through degree four vanish, since a homogeneous moment form of one member vanishes on the other member's D, and multinomial coefficients for these degrees are invertible.

Two distinct layers are impossible in one member: their joint affine projection lies on a 3×3 grid, and the product of the two degree-two level indicators isolates every cell by moments through four. Thus each cell count is 0 modulo 13, impossible since each cell has cap three.

For d=5 the incidence sum is at least 295. A factor with at most eight D points contributes at most (8−3)(8−5)=15, while every factor contributes at most (13−3)(13−5)=80. There must be at least four factors having nine or more D points, since 3·80+2·15=270. Each such factor lies in the partner's projective normal space with at least nine U points, so contains the sole possible layered normal, by the first section. Four or more concurrent degree-five factors leave at most 1+5−4=2 D on their nonfactor radial lines, contradicting three-blocking. Thus degree-five forms are excluded, and moments through degree five vanish.

## No layered member

Suppose E has a layer (three supported heights z, each with thirteen points). Its tangent projective line cannot be full in D(E), so choose a common missing direction as the y-axis, writing each layer as a full graph y=f_z(x). Multiplication by the degree-two height indicator shows that every graph has local moments through degree three. The exact cap-three graph census [verify-fuglede-f13-size39-layer-graphs.js](verify-fuglede-f13-size39-layer-graphs.js) normalizes the polynomial graph by scaling and affine shifts. It shows each admissible graph determines at least eleven directions in its tangent projective line (histogram {11:6,12:14,13:43}). The tangent pencil of A thus has at least eleven U points. By the first section it has one nonuniform normal, which is layered, and by D(E)⊂U_A it is a missing direction of D(E). Choose y instead along this specific missing direction (the graph lemma applies again if required). A projects injectively along the z-axis, since z is the layered normal of E and is not in U_E. Thus in mutually dual coordinates E consists of the three graphs (x,f_s(x),s), and A of the three graphs (u,t,g_t(u)), with s and t the respective supported levels.

The block-Fourier irreducibility argument of Ferguson–Iosevich §10 (Lemma 10.2 for p=11) applies without change at p=13. Here is the needed computation. The normalized Fourier matrix for (E,A) is unitary, and each 13×13 block indexed by one s and one t is a nonzero scalar times a 13×13 Fourier unitary multiplied by diagonal phase matrices, since
```
(x,f_s(x),s)·(u,t,g_t(u)) = xu + t f_s(x)+s g_t(u).
```
Thus write the normalized blocks as U_{s,t}/√3, where each U_{s,t} is unitary. Off-diagonal orthogonality of any two block rows gives three unitary matrices summing to zero, of form U_{s,t}U_{s',t}*. Their entries belong to Q(ζ_13) because the factors 1/√13 cancel in their products. Multiply the sum by the adjoint of its first unitary; one gets I+V+W=0 with V,W unitary over Q(ζ_13). Then W*W=I gives V+V*=−I, so V²+V+I=0. The polynomial T²+T+1 is irreducible over Q(ζ_13), since its primitive cube roots are not in that cyclotomic field (for example Q(ζ_3,ζ_13)=Q(ζ_39) has degree 24, whereas Q(ζ_13) has degree 12). This would make the dimension 13 over Q(ζ_13) divisible by two, a contradiction.

Thus moments through degree five vanish and neither member can be layered. In particular each U pencil contains at most eight uniform normals. The size-39 exclusion remains open at this stage.
