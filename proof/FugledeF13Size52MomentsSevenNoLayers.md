# Size 52 in F_13^3: moments through degree seven and no layers

Assume a spectral pair (E,A) with 52 points. Use the general preliminaries of [Ferguson–Iosevich, arXiv:2610.30712v1 §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1): for either member B, D(B) is contained in U of the partner, neither U contains a full projective line, affine planes have at most 13 points, affine lines at most 4, and a pencil through an affine line with a points has total occupancy 52+13a. At a uniform normal the occupancy is 4 in all planes.

## A three-direction pencil is incompatible with a degree-six or degree-seven form

Suppose D(B) lies in the zero set of a nonzero degree-six or degree-seven homogeneous form F; multiply by a power of a linear form to reach the stated degree if necessary. Assume a projective line L has at most three points of D(B). Among the thirteen parallel affine slices with direction L, each set of more than four sites would be noncollinear by the line cap, and any noncollinear planar set using at most three directions is a triangle (mark and normalize three noncollinear vertices; no fourth point has only their three directions). Therefore every slice contains exactly four sites, necessarily collinear. These thirteen support lines have at most three axial directions, so at least five are parallel to some d.

Project along d to an affine plane. The at least five resulting support locations cannot be collinear: any four collinear yield sixteen points in the spatial plane over that quotient line. They therefore determine at least four quotient directions by the same planar triangle lemma. Each pair of parallel four-point supports gives at least seven distinct cross directions in its corresponding spatial direction plane by Cauchy–Davenport (the axial-coordinate difference of two four-sets has size at least seven), plus the axial direction d. The corresponding at least four projective lines through d each contain at least eight points of D(B), so are rational linear factors of F.

If F has degree six, every nonfactor radial line through d has at most 1+6−4=3 points of D(B): away from d only the residual form can vanish. Its slices are uniform4 by the preceding triangle argument. The pencil of an actual four-point support line thus has at least ten uniform planes, bounding total occupancy by 10·4+4·13=92, less than 104.

If F has degree seven, each nonfactor radial line has at most 1+7−4=4 D points. We claim there are at least five radial factors. If all thirteen supports are parallel to d, their thirteen quotient locations include a noncollinear six-set. Such a set cannot determine at most four directions, by the size-65 standalone planar census (it only uses line cap five, whereas these quotient locations have cap three). Hence there are at least five factors. If some support line is not parallel d, its four points project to four distinct positions X in one level z0 of the quotient plane, with the slice level inherited from the initial plane slicing. Each parallel support location (xP,zP) lies at its own level distinct from z0. Each of the four quotient slopes (x−xP)/(z0−zP), x∈X, joins to one of the four projected positions. The associated radial projective line contains d and at least four cross directions (pair the corresponding nonparallel-support point with all four points of the parallel support); it is therefore a factor. Across the ≥5 distinct parallel support locations, these slope sets are images of X by ≥5 distinct affine transformations. At most four affine transformations can give the same image of X: any stabilizer of X fixes its barycenter and acts freely on the orbit of any member other than the barycenter, so the stabilizer has cardinality at most four. Thus two of these four-element slope sets differ and their union contains at least five factors. Finally, with five radial factors a nonfactor radial line has at most 1+7−5=3 D. Its planes are uniform4 as before; nine uniform planes in the support-line pencil give total occupancy at most 9·4+5·13=101<104.

Thus in either degree, D(B) is four-blocking.

## The remaining projective degree-six and degree-seven arguments

Now apply the degree-six factor and missing-point arguments from FugledeF13Size65MomentsSix.md, starting at the statement that all projective lines have between four and thirteen D sites. All the ensuing incidence identities, geometry and exact six-line checker concern only PG(2,13) and this range, not the spatial cardinality. They exclude a nonzero degree-six form vanishing on D(B). Every degree≤6 mixed moment vanishes: the corresponding homogeneous moment polynomial vanishes on the partner's D by uniformity, so is identically zero, and all multinomial coefficients are invertible because the degree is less than 13.

Two distinct layered directions in one member (profile 13^4,0^9) cannot coexist. Their normals are independent and project B onto a 4×4 grid. The product of the two per-axis degree-three Lagrange indicators gives a degree-six test isolating any one cell, so the cell count is a multiple of 13 by vanishing moments. Each spatial cell is a line and has count at most four. Hence all sixteen cells would be empty.

For degree seven, apply the projective incidence and seven-line arguments of FugledeF13Size65MomentsSeven.md, again starting after four-blocking. Their only other cardinality-dependent input is that a projective pencil of at least eleven U normal directions forces a layer. This remains true: its at most three nonuniform Radon profiles must be affine or constant modulo 13 by independence of powers of distinct forms in degree ≥ number of nonuniform profiles minus one. A nonconstant affine profile has mass at least 78, impossible. A nonuniform constant profile can only have zero residue and hence be layered. Reducing the Radon identity modulo 13 gives 4(b−1)=0, forcing exactly one nonuniform profile (b≥1 because U has no full line). Consequently all heavy factors pass through the sole possible layer, and the unchanged seven-line argument excludes a degree-seven form vanishing on D(B). This gives vanishing of all mixed moments through degree seven.

## Excluding the sole possible layer

Suppose B is layered over four z values, with thirteen points per z-slice. Since the directional projective line of the slices cannot be full in D(B), choose a common missing direction as the y-axis within all those slices. Each slice is then exactly a graph y=f_z(x) over F_13. Restricting global moments to a slice by the degree-three z-Lagrange indicator gives local moments through degree four. The exact normalized zero-quartic graph census in FugledeF13Size65ParabolaLayers.md applies (its cap is five, here four), so every f_z is a quadratic with nonzero leading coefficient. For clarity write f_z(x)=a_z x²+b_z x+c_z.

Let (u,v,w) be a spectral normal for B. If v=0 and u≠0, the graph Fourier sum vanishes slice by slice. If v=u=0 and w≠0, the sum is 13 times four thirteenth roots of unity and does not vanish (divisibility by the cyclotomic polynomial would require that coefficient sum is divisible by 13). If v≠0, completing the square in x gives, up to a nonzero common Gauss factor,
```
Σ_z χ(a_z) ζ^{wz+v c_z−(u+v b_z)²/(4v a_z)}.
```
Here χ is quadratic character and ζ a primitive thirteenth root. If this vanishes, the signed coefficient integer vector must be constant by the cyclotomic relation, and its total lies between −4 and 4, so all coefficients are zero. Thus there must be two positive and two negative signs and their phase multisets agree. For each of the two matchings between the positive and negative z indices, an equality in one pair determines w/v uniquely after u/v is given, because the z levels are distinct. In particular, for any fixed slope u/v, there are at most two choices of w/v giving a Fourier zero.

Let A be the partner. It projects injectively along the z-axis to the (x,y)-plane because the pure z normal is not uniform for B. For any of the thirteen quotient directions with nonzero y-component, each affine spatial plane over a line of that quotient direction uses at most two difference directions: any of its difference directions with nonzero projection has that quotient slope and therefore belongs to the at most two uniform lifts of B; a zero projection is forbidden by injectivity. A noncollinear triple determines three directions, so all thirteen slices are collinear, each of size at most four, hence each size exactly four. The image of A in the (x,y)-plane therefore has uniform occupancy four in each quotient direction except possibly the horizontal one. The planar Radon identity forces its indicator to be constant on horizontal lines (its only possibly nonuniform line sums are horizontal; summing the fourteen line sums through each point yields 52 plus 13 times the indicator). Thus the image consists of four full horizontal lines. Accordingly A itself is layered with those four y levels, each a graph z=g_y(x). The local-moment and exact graph lemma again gives g_y quadratic with nonzero leading coefficient.

For distinct supported y,y', and each horizontal displacement h, the differences of pairs on the two graphs are (h,y−y',g_y(x+h)−g_y'(x)) as x varies, so their z increments have at most two values by the fixed-slope bound above. Any nonconstant degree≤2 polynomial over F_13 has either 7 or 13 values. Hence all these differences are constant in x. At h=0 the two quadratics have the same quadratic and linear coefficients. At h=1 the difference then has nonzero linear coefficient, contradiction. Thus neither B nor its partner has a layered direction.

These conclusions give the inputs to the [weighted-support contradiction](FugledeF13Size52FinalWeightedSupport.md). They also imply every U pencil has at most ten uniform normals.
