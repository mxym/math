# Size 65: a five-layer member has a nonzero quartic slice moment

Consider a hypothetical size-65 spectral pair, with all mixed moments of degrees at most seven zero as in the [preceding note](FugledeF13Size65MomentsSeven.md). A layered member is contained in five parallel planes, one full 13-point planar slice on each. The plane of their common directions contains a missing direction (its full line cannot lie in D), so one choice of planar coordinates makes all five slices graphs of polynomials \(y=f_s(x)\) for \(x\in\mathbb F_{13}\). Write their levels as \(s\in S\), \(|S|=5\).

A degree-four Lagrange polynomial in level s isolates any one slice, so that each slice has all moments of degrees ≤3 zero. For the quartic slice forms write \(Q_s(u,v)=\sum_x(ux+vf_s(x))^4\). Four moment equations \(\sum_s s^j Q_s=0\), \(0\le j\le3\) show \(Q_s=\lambda_s Q\) where \(\lambda_s=(\prod_{t\ne s}(s-t))^{-1}\) and \(Q\) is common.

We rule out \(Q=0\). If it were zero, each graph would have all moments through degree four zero. Its reduced polynomial in x then has degree at most eight, since \(\sum_x x^j f(x)=0\) for j=0,1,2,3 kills degrees 12,11,10,9 successively. A degree-one graph violates the line cap five. Normalize each graph on its own by scaling y so the leading coefficient is one, translating x to make the next coefficient zero (d≠0 in the field), and an affine shear in y to kill constant and linear coefficients. This uses invertible affine maps, preserving local moments through four and line caps. The [accompanying exact checker](verify-fuglede-f13-size65-parabola-layers.js) tests all 402235 coefficient arrays of degrees 2..8, and finds that only \(f(x)=x^2\) meets the moment identities and line cap five. Thus each original slice is a nondegenerate parabola of the form \(a_s x^2+b_s x+c_s\) with \(a_s\ne0\), in the original common graph coordinates (the inverse normalizations did not mix x with y).

At a Fourier frequency \((u,v,w)\) with v≠0, each slice contributes a one-dimensional complete quadratic character sum. Completing the square shows that the contribution is a common nonzero Gauss sum times a signed thirteenth root of unity. The total for five slices therefore cannot vanish: an integer vector of coefficients of a zero linear combination of the 13th roots must be constant by primeness, hence its sum is divisible by 13, whereas five signs have odd nonzero sum of absolute value at most five. Thus every Fourier zero of the layered member has v=0. Its spectral partner would have all differences in one plane and would lie in an affine plane, contrary to the plane cap thirteen.

Thus \(Q\ne0\) for any hypothetical layered member. This is only one branch of the layered analysis, not a size-65 exclusion. The underlying parabola/Gauss step is the one used for p=11 by Ferguson–Iosevich [Lemma 12.6, arXiv:2610.30712v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1); the characteristic-13 exact normalization census supplies the bridge here.
