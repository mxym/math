# Size 65 in F_13^3: moments through degree six and one-layer limit

Let (E,A) be a hypothetical spectral pair of size 65. For either member B, write D(B) for its normalized difference directions and U_B for the normal directions with equal occupancies across thirteen perpendicular affine planes. As in Ferguson–Iosevich [arXiv:2610.30712v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), §8 (with general odd-prime preliminary arguments), spectrality gives D(E)⊂U_A and D(A)⊂U_E. No U_B contains a projective line, the affine plane cap is thirteen and the affine line cap is five. A pencil through an affine line with a points has total occupancy 65+13a. The uniform plane occupancy is five.

**Claims.** Every mixed moment of both members through total degree six vanishes. In either member there is at most one direction with plane-occupancy profile consisting of five 13's and eight zeros. This is not a nonexistence result.

## Projective degree-six rigidity

Suppose a nonzero homogeneous form of degree at most six vanishes on D(B). Multiply by a power of a linear form to set its degree to six. Any noncollinear planar set with at most three directions is a triangle: after normalizing three sites to 0,e_1,e_2, all three slopes are fixed, and there is no fourth compatible point in odd characteristic. The checker verifies the last short assertion over F_13.

If some projective line meets D(B) in at most three points, all thirteen parallel affine slices have at most five points, since otherwise a slice is noncollinear (by the line cap) and has at least four directions. The total forces thirteen collinear five-point slices. Their supporting lines have at most three directions, so at least five support lines are parallel to an axis d. Four of their quotient locations modulo d form a four-cap, because an affine plane cannot contain three of the five-point lines. The cap determines at least four quotient directions. Indeed if the six pairs were in three directions (each direction matching at most two pairs), all three perfect matchings would be parallel pairings; two force a parallelogram and the third is impossible in odd characteristic.

For each such direction two parallel five-point supports give at least nine cross differences along d by Cauchy–Davenport, defining nine distinct projective directions transverse to d in their direction plane. Hence that projective line has at least ten D(B) points, so divides the degree-six form. There are four distinct factor lines through d. Any nonfactor line through d has at most 1+(6−4)=3 zeros. Its parallel affine slices therefore each have at most five points and are uniformly five by total mass. At least eight normals on the pencil of an actual five-point support line are uniform; the total occupancy is at most 8·5+6·13=118, less than 65+13·5=130. Contradiction.

Thus D(B) has four or more points per projective line. It has at most thirteen since its partner U contains no full line. Put N=|D(B)| and n_L=|D(B)∩L|. Projective incidence identities give
sum_L (n_L−4)(n_L−6) = N²−127N+4392 ≥360.
On nonfactor lines the summands are nonpositive (degree six), and each rational line factor contributes at most (13−4)(13−6)=63. Five factors do not suffice, so the form is a product of six distinct rational linear forms. At most three can be concurrent: if four meet, a nonfactor through that point has at most 1+6−4=3 zeros. The zero set V is their union and is itself four-blocking.

Any point of V that is on two or more factors must be in D(B). For a triple point, deleting it leaves at most three points on any nonfactor line through it. For a double point P, consider the other four factors. If any intersection of two of them is off the two factors through P, the line joining that intersection to P loses P and sees at most three points from the other four. Therefore all intersections of the four must lie on the two factors through P. A triple intersection of the four on one such line would produce forbidden concurrence four. Otherwise all six pair intersections of the four are distinct. A line that is not among those four contains at most two of their six intersections, because any two pairs sharing an original factor have their intersections joined by that factor. The two lines through P cannot cover all six. This proves the assertion for double points too.

A missing simple point of V cannot lie on any four-secant of V, since deleting it would violate four-blocking. The accompanying exact checker enumerates all six-factor arrangements using three nonconcurrent factors, normalized to the three coordinate lines. It finds 17856 normalized arrangements whose union is four-blocking and concurrence at most three. Of these, 576 have sorted counts [0,0,0,0,0,0] of simple points per factor not on any four-secant of the union; the other 17280 have counts [0,0,1,1,1,2]. Thus at least one factor has no allowable missing point whatsoever. This forces a projective line in D(B), impossible. Hence no degree-six form vanishes on D(B).

The moment form M_j^B(v)=sum_{x in B}(v·x)^j for 1≤j≤6 vanishes on U_B by the field power sums, hence on D of the partner. It is identically zero by the rigidity just established. Mixed moments follow by comparing multinomial coefficients modulo 13, and the constant moment is zero because |B|=65.

## At most one layered normal

Two distinct layered normals would give an affine linear projection onto X×Y, |X|=|Y|=5, with multiplicity entries 0..5 by the line cap and all row/column masses thirteen. Put λ_X(x)=(prod_{x'≠x}(x−x'))^(-1), and likewise for Y. Through degree six the independent grid evaluations x^i y^j with i,j≤4 and i+j≤6 give twenty-two moment conditions. Their three-dimensional annihilator is exactly λ_X λ_Y Q with Q affine-linear: these candidates annihilate the moments because a test term times a linear monomial has total degree at most seven, hence at least one exponent at most three, and the corresponding weighted sum vanishes by Lagrange interpolation. The checker enumerates the ten affine orbits of five-element subsets of F_13, all 100 ordered orbit pairs and all 2197 Q for each pair. None gives a valid multiplicity matrix. This proves the claim.
