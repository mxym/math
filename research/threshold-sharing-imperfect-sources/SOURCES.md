# Primary sources, exact overlap, and scope

## Problem and prior resolution

- Carl Bosley and Yevgeniy Dodis, [*Does Privacy Require True Randomness?*, TCC 2007, proceedings PDF](https://iacr.org/archive/tcc2007/43920001/43920001.pdf), Sec. 5, pose the ordinary 2-out-of-2 secret-sharing question separately from their private-key encryption results. This note does not concern encryption or commitments.
- Divesh Aggarwal, Eldon Chung, Maciej Obremski and João Ribeiro, [*On Secret Sharing, Randomness, and Random-less Reductions for Secret Sharing*, TCC 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-22318-1_12); [primary expanded text](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/090/download/), also [IACR ePrint 2021/802](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/802). The expanded text poses the general threshold question in Sec. 1.4, states positive extraction results under stronger models such as leakage resilience or non-malleability, and gives the ordinary sharing reductions in Sec. 1.3.2 (Definition 5, Lemma 1) and Sec. 4. The proof of **Theorem 7**, printed pp. 17–18, explicitly uses the complement-incidence distribution design for a unanimous original scheme. This is not a new reduction proposed here.
- Divesh Aggarwal, [*Secret Sharing Despite Non-Extractable Randomness*, arXiv:2610.20173v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20173v2), also listed as [IACR ePrint 2026/2477](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2477). Definition 2.1 is the model used here; Definition 2.2 and the paragraph after it define deterministic extraction and reduce multi-bit lower bounds to one bit. Theorems 1.1, 4.1 and 5.1 already give the direct two-party, threshold-two, and unanimous schemes; their tag and source rules are Eqs. (5)–(8). The exact one-bit lower and upper bounds use the parity argument of Lemmas 4.4 and 5.4. Our endpoint relabelings are in Proof Sec. 6. The discussion at the end of Aggarwal Sec. 5 explains why one cannot obtain the larger endpoint schemes directly from the two-party scheme by an arbitrary distribution design; it does not preclude applying the old distribution design from a sufficiently large **unanimous** scheme.
- The continuation [Yanqing Yao and Zhoujun Li, JISA 89, 104008 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jisa.2025.104008) is discussed in Aggarwal v2 Appendix A (published Proposition 2 and its consequences for ordinary threshold sharing). The distinction there is between privacy of an unauthorized subset and reconstructability of the whole tuple. This note uses Aggarwal's ordinary subset-privacy definition throughout and does not examine the stronger models or evaluate any implementation.

## Exact comparison

Let $2\le t\le n$, $r=\binom n{t-1}$ and $q=2^b$. In the **known black-box route**, use Aggarwal Theorem 5.1 at the old threshold $(r,r)$ and ACOR Theorem 7 at the new threshold $(t,n)$. Old labels are indexed by $(t-1)$-subsets of $[n]$; each old share is sent to the new players outside that subset. The exact resulting costs compare as follows:

| Quantity | Aggarwal T5.1 + ACOR T7, this black-box route | Direct construction in this note |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sufficient field condition | $b\ge r$ | $2^b>2^{t-1}\binom{n-1}{t-1}$ |
| Number of $(b+1)$-bit pieces sent to each party | $\binom{n-1}{t-1}$ | $1$ |
| Dealer domain size | $(2^r-1)q^{r-1}$ | $Cq^{t-1}$ |
| Shortest fixed-width key encoding | $(r-1)b+r$ | $(t-1)b+\lceil\log_2 C\rceil$ |
| Minimum source min-entropy in bits | $(r-1)b+\log_2(2^{r-1}-1)$ | $(t-1)b+\log_2((C-1)/2)$ |
| Exact optimal deterministic one-bit error on its own source class | $1/[2(2^r-1)]$ | $1/(2C)$ |

Here $C=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom ni\binom{n-i-1}{t-1-i}$. The old encoding term $r$ is $\lceil\log_2(2^r-1)\rceil$, since $r\ge2$. The old reduction leaves its source class unchanged, so its exact error and entropy follow from Aggarwal Theorem 5.1 without a new analysis. The comparison is with that specific route, not a bound on every conceivable distribution design. For instance, at $t=3,n=4$ it compares $r=6$ to $C=17$: three pieces versus one, key label length $5b+6$ versus $2b+5$, and old source error $1/126$ versus new source error $1/34$. The qualitative separation for intermediate thresholds is therefore prior as a composition. The additional result here is a single directly encoded share at each intermediate threshold, together with its quantitative parameters and the precise restricted template optimum. At $t=2$ and $t=n$ the direct endpoints coincide with Aggarwal's constructions (including the sharper $q>n$ threshold at $t=2$).

## Standard ingredients and specificity

- Adi Shamir, [*How to Share a Secret*](https://doi.org/10.1145/359168.359176), [primary MIT preprint](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/91c51bfc-5678-409c-80c5-44ce8fcdbadf), supplies the standard polynomial-evaluation and interpolation mechanism. We use degree at most $t-2$ for the pads, with a separately chosen tagged shift for the secret.
- Aggarwal v2 Sec. 1.2 already observes the $b$-bit min-entropy lower bound on a single fixed-message share. Proof Sec. 5 records the per-party alphabet consequence using a balanced deterministic function, and the analogous dealer min-entropy floor by iterating the conditional bound. Standard randomness requirements for perfect threshold sharing are classical; see Carlo Blundo, Alfredo De Santis and Ugo Vaccaro, [*Randomness in Distribution Protocols*](https://doi.org/10.1006/INCO.1996.0095). No novelty is claimed for that asymptotic floor. The integer $b+1$ optimum is for standard fixed-width share encoding when exact deterministic one-bit extraction is impossible; it is not a minimum-alphabet assertion beyond the stated lower bound. The proof of a minimum $C$ labels applies solely to the local two-branch signed multiplicity template stated there.
- A bounded literature check searched both arXiv and ePrint versions, the ACOR title and its later exact-phrase citations, Yao–Li, and combinations of imperfect randomness, generalized thresholds, distribution designs, short shares, and signed multiplicities. The 2026 source and the established composition above are the closest identified precedents. The older weak-privacy short-share question in [Beimel–Ishai–Kushilevitz–Li, ePrint 2025/1978](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1978) concerns a different privacy condition and fair randomness. No exhaustive priority claim is made.
