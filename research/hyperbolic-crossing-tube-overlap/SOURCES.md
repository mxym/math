# Original question, prior tools, and scope

* [Stefan Steinerberger, *Well-Distributed Great Circles on S²*, §2, p. 5, open 2016 version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.03805#page=5) ([journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-018-9994-z)). The paper proves order bounds on the sphere and presents the corresponding intersecting-lines result in the Euclidean plane. Its final open question in §2 asks broadly what can be said in the hyperbolic plane; it does **not** conjecture this precise optimizer or uniqueness theorem. [The author's March 2026 open problems, §2](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf#page=5) again asks about negative curvature. Here the centers are distinct complete pairwise-intersecting geodesics in the curvature −1 plane, and the objective uses ordinary area, common half-width and ordered pairs. A spherical zone circles back on itself; its optimizer cannot be read off from this hyperbolic result.
* [Brauchart, Hardin and Saff, *Discrete Energy Asymptotics on a Riemannian circle*, Proposition 1(A)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.4720) establishes that equally spaced sites minimize a decreasing convex geodesic potential on a circle. Their proof is the classical winding-number/Jensen argument tracing to Fejes Tóth. This supplies precisely the optimization **after** reduction to a pencil; it is not new here. Steinerberger also uses cyclic gap sums in his Euclidean analysis. The kernel under consideration when the tube width is 1/n varies with n, and the microscopic profile affects the leading coefficient for powers greater than one.
* Polar hyperbolic area, the right-triangle sine rule, translations along a geodesic, and the angle-defect formula are classical; see [Cannon, Floyd, Kenyon and Parry, *Hyperbolic Geometry*](https://lelievre.perso.math.cnrs.fr/gg/hyperbolic_2up.pdf). We use them to obtain the two-tube integral and reduce a general family to a pencil via a three-line energy comparison.
* [Jaudon and Parlier, *On angles formed by N points of the Euclidean and Hyperbolic planes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0603156) optimize the smallest angle among angles spanned by **points**. [Dress, Koolen and Moulton, *On Line Arrangements in the Hyperbolic Plane*](https://doi.org/10.1006/eujc.2002.0582) study combinatorial counts for line arrangements. [Neumann-Coto and Scott, *A property of closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces*](https://doi.org/10.4171/GGD/699) study crossing angles and polygons formed by lifts of closed geodesics, with length information. None of those objectives is the sum of the powered pairwise intersection areas of this arbitrary finite crossing family.
