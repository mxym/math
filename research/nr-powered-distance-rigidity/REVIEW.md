## Analytic derivation

Write k=N−1, normalize the positive test radius to 1, and expand the product of (1−r exp(iθ))^k and its conjugate. The mth harmonic magnitude is A_m(r)=r^m ∑_l C(k,l)C(k,l+m) r^(2l); its sign is (−1)^m. The nonzero harmonics must vanish separately in the sum; a zero site contributes none. Distinctness makes the maximum radius R positive. Once all radii coincide, rotating an extreme site to angle 0 shows that its value N in the Fejér kernel is the entire sum N. Every other site is at one of the other Nth roots, and all roots are exhausted by distinctness. The conclusion is concentric, not necessarily the test circle.

For the disk, the real part P of the Möbius function (1+conj(ξ)z)/(1−conj(ξ)z) is positive and harmonic, with |∇P|=2/|ξ−z|². The chain rule gives Δ(P^(−k))=4k(k+1)(1−r²)^(−2) P^(−k). Its boundary-angle Fourier modes share the same differential equation on r<1. The Fejér combination T_S/(1−r²)^k is smooth at zero (the radial mode times its angle is a polynomial in z and its conjugate prior to division) and nonnegative on r=S<1. At a negative interior minimum its Laplacian would have the wrong sign. The S=1 result follows by taking S'↑1 at each r<1, then using Fejér separately on r=1; there is no use of a singular PDE at the boundary. The sum of T_R over the sites is ∑ A_0(r)/A_0(R)≤N with strict inequality for any r<R, while its extreme-site term alone is N and all terms are nonnegative. This gives precisely the disk theorem without the finite certificate.

For the shift c=1+h≥1, q/(1+q²)=r/(c+r²) has a unique q∈[0,1], increasing on r∈[0,√c], with q=0 iff r=0. The right side is ≤1/(2√c). The scale a=(c+r²)/(1+q²)>0 satisfies aq=r, giving the kernel identity without any arbitrary weights. The site with radius R is maximal also in q. The sum of the weighted Fejér tests after cancellation is bounded by N because a(r)^k A_0(q(r)) is strictly increasing: its angular integral has only even powers of cos, every one nonnegative and nondecreasing, including the strictly increasing term (c+r²)^k. Equal radii restore ordinary angular Fejér equality, as the weights then coincide. No arbitrary site radii are certified for h>0.

## The exhaustive unrestricted check, 2≤N≤18

Here B has constant term 1; summing over the sites gives exactly N, not the variable bound in the disk proof. Let t=r/R, s=R². At t=1 it is Fejér; at t<1 each scale-polynomial denominator d_m(s) has positive constant and nonnegative coefficients. In N D(s)[B−t^L F_N]/(1−t), the contribution of a t^j (j=m+2l) is ∑_(d=j)^(L−1)t^d, because D=d_m D_m and the value at t=1 multiplies the whole Fejér subtraction. The constant also contributes, with m=j=0. The maximum s-degree is k(k−1)/2, the sum of all degrees k−m. After t=u/(1+u) and multiplication by (1+u)^(L−1), the u^b multiplier is ∑_(d=j)^b C(L−1−d,b−d)=C(L−j,b−j). No sign changes are introduced. All u,s are nonnegative; (b,e)=(0,0) is positive even on the axes. Thus the coefficient tests imply strict B>0 for every t<1; the case t=1 is separate. This suffices to rule out any smaller-radius site.

Its power versus half-power notation is not consistent; credit both orders and do not claim a new unrestricted four-site result. [Their later explicit conjecture, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.2975), concerns a single exponent 2N−2 and one fixed circle, with unrestricted planar sites. Their all-even-powers theorem, common-radius case, triangle proof and varying-test-radius proposition are distinct.
* [Bosuwan–Ruengrot (2017)](https://www.scienceasia.org/2017.43.n4/scias43_267.pdf) give moment characterization (Thm 1), the common-radius case (Prop 1), N=4 with prescribed quarter-turn directions (Prop 2), and prime N with equally spaced directions and rational radii (Prop 3). [Bosuwan (2020), Conj. 2.2, Thm 2.3, Cors 2.4–2.5](https://thaijmath.com/index.php/thaijmath/article/download/1106/1114/1140) precisely formulate the shifted quantity, characterize its moments, and settle common radius and unrestricted N=3. An externally fixed circle of sites is not a closed disk of movable sites.
