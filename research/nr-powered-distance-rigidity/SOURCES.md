# Prior results and search limits (19 November 2026)

* **First published announcement and small orders.** Nikolai Nikolov and Rafael Rafailov, [Pacific Journal of Mathematics 253 (2011), 157–168, Theorem 2.2 and Remark 2.3, p. 161](https://msp.org/pjm/2011/253-1/pjm-v253-n1-p10-p.pdf). The theorem is the forward direction for an already regular polygon. The remark asks about a single power and reports that the conjectural converse has been verified for **both N=3 and N=4**. Its usage of k (whose double is the power one line earlier) is not consistent throughout the remark. We credit its announcement of N=4 and make no priority claim for N≤4; the present finite proof is an alternative for those sizes.
* **Original precise single-power question.** Nikolov and Rafailov, [On extremums of sums of powered distances to a finite set of points](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.2975), §2 Conjecture 1 in the authors' text, published in [Geometriae Dedicata 167 (2013), 69–89](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10711-012-9804-3). N distinct sites lie anywhere in the plane, and the sum of the 2N−2 powers is constant on one circle. The conclusion is a regular N-gon on a **concentric** circle, not necessarily the test circle. Their Theorem 2 treats constancy at **all** the even powers through 2N−2; Proposition 1 treats a common concentric site circle, Proposition 2 proves the N=3 case, and Proposition 3 allows general sites when constancy is assumed on arbitrary concentric test circles. N=2 is immediate. None of these latter predicates is equivalent to a single fixed circle and variable site radii.
* **Known Fourier criterion and special configurations.** Nattapong Bosuwan and Pornrat Ruengrot, [ScienceAsia 43 (2017), 267–274](https://www.scienceasia.org/2017.43.n4/scias43_267.pdf), Conjectures 1–2 and Theorem 1. Its vanishing Fourier equations for N-point multisets are a direct antecedent; no originality is asserted for the moment reduction. Its Propositions 1–3 give the common-norm case, four points already having equally spaced directions, and prime N with equally spaced directions and rational norms. The new finite-order test imposes neither equal directions nor rationality. Its separate Theorem 2 fixes the site circle in a polarization optimization problem and characterizes angular moment cancellations; it does not establish equal radii from a one-circle observation.
* **Shifted problem.** Nattapong Bosuwan, [Thai J. Math. 18 (2020), 1841–1855](https://thaijmath.com/index.php/thaijmath/article/download/1106/1114/1140). Conjecture 2.2 asks the same converse for a common additive h≥0 inside the squared distances. Theorem 2.1 assumes all even powers. Theorem 2.3 gives vanishing moments; Corollaries 2.4 and 2.5 settle the common-radius and N=3 cases, respectively. Our direct shifted disk consequence is for site radii ≤√(1+h) in the unit normalization, with no other radial or angular restriction. The arbitrary-plane certificate through N=18 here is for h=0, not for the general shifted question.
* **Other related questions.** The [2015 MathOverflow discussion](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/227009/regular-polygon-question) gives N=3 and the common-radius case. [Meskhishvili, cyclic averages](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10406) starts from a regular polygon; computing loci for given regular polygons does not prove this inverse assertion. The regular polygon extremal problem for nonexceptional powers (including the different Bosuwan–Ruengrot Conjecture 3) is not the inverse question at the exceptional power 2N−2.
