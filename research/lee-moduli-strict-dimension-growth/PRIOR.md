# Prior work and search coverage

- E.-Y. Lee's 2010 problem asks for the dimension-free sharp coefficient for two matrices. Her 2012 paper [How to compare the absolute values of operator sums and the sums of absolute values?](https://doi.org/10.7153/oam-06-42) develops right-modulus comparisons. For the m-summand version see Q. Tang and S. Zhang, [LAA 731 (2026), 196–204](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2025.11.015), [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16846). This note does not give the sharp dimension-free value or a new disproof of the historical formula. Lin and Y. Zhang settled the Frobenius two-matrix case; Tang and S. Zhang give the m-summand Frobenius case. Teng Zhang's [Operator symmetric moduli and sharp triangle inequalities](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70672) studied the dimension-sensitive constants. For a survey and exact fixed-dimension problem statement see Bourin and T. Zhang, [§5.3, Problem 5.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30538v1#S5.SS3). In particular the claimed strict increase is false outside our stated range in general: the p=1 constants are 1, and fixed-m Frobenius constants stabilize as soon as n≥m.
- Hongsen Qiu, [§5, Question 5.1 and text following](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17565v2#S5): proves the p≥2 dimension-free constants, disproves the proposed formula below 2 and gives numerical higher-rank evidence for p=6/5. He explicitly suggests the dimension-free constant might not be attained in any fixed dimension. This observation is not the strict adjacent-dimension theorem, and Qiu's exact-value question remains open here. [Zeng–Liu–Ratnavelu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15558) give rigorous rank-one counterexamples to the old formula at p=3/2 and positive results at other orders, and [Pang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18112) disproves that formula independently for every m≥2 and 1<p<2. None of these rank-one examples alone establishes a gap above *all* rank-one examples.
- Anonymous, [Sharp constants for sums of matrix moduli when 1<p<2](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24237738), v0.1.0-candidate, 15 Nov 2026 (explicitly unrefereed/AI-assisted); [original manuscript, especially open-problems section](https://github.com/ipitchford/schatten-sum-of-moduli-constants/blob/v0.1.0-candidate/manuscript.tex#L1083-L1103) and [release explanation](https://www.evidencepress.org/releases/schatten-sum-of-moduli-constants/). Gives the exact constants $D_{p,n}$ for $n\le m$ and sharp all-rank-one benchmark $D_{p,m}$ for $n\ge m$. The candidate already proves $D_{p,d}$ strictly increasing in d: **our strict dimension comparison when n+1≤m is already implied by its results**. It certifies a 3×3 gap past the rank-one constant at 9 particular p (hence, by continuity, in neighborhoods); no new priority is claimed for those nine strict rank gaps. It explicitly asks whether $c_p(m,n)>D_{p,m}$ for all $n>m$, all $1<p<2$, and whether $c_p(m,n)$ stabilizes in n. The contribution relative to this work is strict increase at each adjacent step **n≥m**, nonattainment, and the all-parameter rank gap in dimension m+1. Our proof is uniform, independent of the earlier rank-one formula and numerical comparison. We make no assertion of global priority, exact constants for n>m, explicit magnitudes of the gaps, or extremizers in operator ideals on an infinite-dimensional space.
- Li's [Sharp Concave-Function Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25989) compares sharp constants in a different nonlinear problem to the linear constants at fixed dimension. It does not determine their dimension growth. Left/right symmetrized moduli, noncommutative Hlawka inequalities, Schatten strong triangle inequalities and block norm compression are distinct problems. Searched exact title, author, arXiv identifiers, as well as dimension, nonattainment, higher rank, and sharp-constants terminology. No prior all-n result surfaced in this bounded external search; index or priority completeness is not guaranteed.
