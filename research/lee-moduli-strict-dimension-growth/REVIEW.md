# Internal adversarial review: strict growth of the right-modulus Schatten constants

**Disposition: pass as a finite-dimensional analytic theorem (internal AI-assisted mathematical review, not external peer review or a formal proof-assistant certificate).** The analytic proof was independently rederived; no missing hypothesis or failing case was found. A second route through the proof below does not assume equality of adjacent constants. All matrices are finite and complex. Here $H_i=|A_i|, B=\sum H_i, S=\sum A_i, E=\mathrm{ran}\,B$.

1. Existence is genuine: on the slice $\|B\|_p=1$ each $H_i\le B$ bounds $\|A_i\|_p$ by monotonicity of **eigenvalues**, not operator monotonicity of raising to $p>1$. Continuous modulus makes the slice closed in finite dimension. Its maximum $C=c_p(m,n)\ge1$ has $S\ne0$. The initial support of every $A_i$ is in $E$.
2. For rectangular maps $T_i$ project their common output onto the range of their sum and send that range isometrically into the input dimension. The sum's singular values survive and the denominator cannot grow, because $T_i^*PT_i\le T_i^*T_i$ and positive square root is operator monotone. The resulting denominator is not zero if the numerator is positive. This step does not require one projection to contain each individual range.
3. At every maximizing tuple $S$ is injective on $E$. Give summand $i$ both common output $\sqrt{1-s}A_i$ and orthogonal private output $\sqrt{s}A_i$. Its individual Gram matrix is unchanged. The sum's Gram matrix becomes $(1-s)S^*S+sD$ where $D=\sum H_i^2$ has exactly kernel $E^\perp$. If $S$ loses $k>0$ directions in $E$, the sum of the $p/2$ powers is at least $(1-s)^{p/2}\|S\|_p^p+k(sd)^{p/2}$, where $d>0$ is the minimum eigenvalue of $D|_E$. Since $p<2$, this improves the numerator for small $s>0$, contradicting compression in (2). No assumption $B>0$ on the ambient space.
4. **Direct proof of existence of a deficient summand.** Take a maximizer and a norming dual matrix $W=U_S|S|^{p-1}/\|S\|_p^{p-1}$. Factor $A_i=U_iH_i$ with full polar unitary extensions. Keep $B,U_i,W$ fixed and maximize the *real linear functional* $\mathrm{ReTr}(W^*\sum U_iK_i)$ over nonnegative $K_i$ supported in $E$ with sum $B$. It cannot exceed its value at $H$, by norm duality and fixed denominator. If some $H_i|_E$ is singular, use this tuple. If all are strictly positive on $E$, the opposite extension $H-\epsilon(K-H)$ is feasible at $K=(B,0,\ldots,0)$ for small $\epsilon>0$; linearity implies equality at $K$. This gives another maximizing tuple with a singular summand. **Recompute** $W,U_i,S$ for the new tuple when needed. Thus the selected maximizer has a nonzero $v\in E\cap\ker H_i$ for some $i$ (use a unit vector). The argument allows $H_i=0$.
5. For this maximizer the conjugate-norm-one $W$ vanishes on $E^\perp$ and is injective on $E$ by (3). Changing each $U_i$ alone is allowed at fixed moduli. The trace norm variational identity forces $M=U_i H_iW^*\ge0$: in a polar decomposition $M=V|M|$, real trace equality forces $\mathrm{Re}\langle x,Vx\rangle=1$ on each positive singular eigenvector, hence $Vx=x$. Nothing is required on the null space of $M$. Thus $W(\mathrm{ran}\,H_i)=\mathrm{ran}\,M^*=\mathrm{ran}\,M\subseteq\mathrm{ran}\,A_i$; dimension equality follows from injectivity of $W|_E$. Since $v\notin\mathrm{ran}\,H_i$, $Wv\notin\mathrm{ran}\,A_i$. Pick $y$ as the normalized nonzero projection of $Wv$ orthogonal to that range. Then $b=\mathrm{Re}\langle Wv,y\rangle>0$.
6. Pad by one zero direction, and let $w=av+\sqrt{1-a^2}e$ with $0<a<1$. Replacing only $A_i$ by $A_i+tyw^*$ gives *exactly* new modulus $H_i+tww^*$ for $t>0$: $A_i^*y=0$ kills the Gram cross terms and $H_iw=0$ kills the square-root cross terms. The old norming functional lower-bounds the new numerator by $C\|B\|_p+tab$. The new denominator equals $\|B\|_p+t a^2 h+o(t)$, $h=\langle v,B^{p-1}v\rangle/\|B\|_p^{p-1}$ for fixed $a$. The trace derivative applies at singular $B$ because the scalar $x\mapsto|x|^p$ is continuously differentiable there. Choose $a>0$ first such that $b>Ca h$, then choose $t>0$ sufficiently small. This constructs an improving competitor without a plateau hypothesis, giving strict inequality for every $n\ge1$. No shared $a,t$ or quantitative gap for all $p,n$ is asserted. The original plateau argument is equivalent and valid.
7. Rank-one comparison uses potentially different input and output isometries on the spans of the factors, each of dimension at most $m$. Singular values of the sum and eigenvalues of the sum of the right moduli are preserved up to zeros. Hence all rank-one tuples have quotient at most $c_p(m,m)$, and some tuple at size $m+1$ beats them all, for each fixed $p,m$. Nonattainment means **no finite maximizer for the supremum over sizes**; no statement about an infinite-dimensional extremizer, nor an exact value of the supremum. Endpoints excluded: $p=1$ gives 1 and $p=2$ stabilizes after $n=m$.
