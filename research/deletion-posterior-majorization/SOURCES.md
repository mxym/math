# Problem, prior results and search limits

* **Binary problem.** Arash Atashpendar, A. W. Roscoe and Peter Y. A. Ryan, [Information Leakage due to Revealing Randomly Selected Bits](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-26096-9_33) (2015; [authors’ text](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/21251/1/information_leakage_due_to_revealing_randomly_selected_bits_preprint.pdf)). Their hidden retained-position mask is uniformly distributed; their k is our m. Sections 3.3–3.4 and the discussion after Figure 3 identify constant observed strings as the conjectured binary Shannon and order-two Rényi minimizers. Although some summary wording is stronger, the text explicitly calls the claim a conjecture supported by experiments, as do the later papers below. Section 3.5 already gives the **binary min-entropy endpoint** for arbitrary lengths, and the constant-word weight formula is also prior. We make no novelty claim about these formulas or the fact that the compatible-supersequence set size is independent of the pattern.
* **Small deletion radii.** Atashpendar, Beunardeau, Connolly, Géraud, Mestel, Roscoe and Ryan, [From Clustering Supersequences to Entropy Minimizing Subsequences for Single and Double Deletions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00703), established the binary Shannon minimization for n−m=1,2, and included a one-deletion Rényi result. Their run transformations act on words; here the intermediate families of constraints need not come from any word.
* **Fixed-pattern asymptotic minimum.** Atashpendar, Mestel, Roscoe and Ryan, [A Proof of Entropy Minimization for Outputs in Deletion Channels via Hidden Word Statistics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.11609), Theorem IV.6: for each fixed binary m the minimization and uniqueness hold for all n above some N(m). That regime is not the unrestricted finite statement. See also [Atashpendar’s doctoral thesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11663), chapters 5–6.
* **Deletion versus insertion; minima versus maxima.** Singhvi, Sabary, Bar-Lev and Yaakobi, [Conditional Entropies of k-Deletion/Insertion Channels](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10026v2), §§III–VI, treat one-error extrema and the two-deletion and general **insertion** minima. With uniform input, the latter is not the arbitrary-radius *deletion input* posterior studied here. Ma and Zhang, [Maximal input entropy for binary two-deletion and two-insertion channels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01064), determine two-error **maximizers** and provide higher-radius histograms and bounded three-error enumerations. Separate public work reports a [counterexample to alternating minimization of positional autocorrelation](https://www.trureturing.org/research/atashpendar-mestel-roscoe-ryan-2018-kappa-alternating-minimizer/) and [failure of autocorrelation to rank finite posterior entropies](https://www.trureturing.org/research/atashpendar-mestel-roscoe-ryan-2018-kappa2-entropy-ordering/). Neither statement says that constant outputs do not minimize the posterior entropy. This note addresses only that minimum and the stronger order it obeys.
* **Method.** The local alignment inequality is elementary; viewed probabilistically, it is a special case of the classical ordering in which comonotone variables give the convex-largest sum for fixed marginals (e.g. Kaas et al., [ASTIN Bulletin 32, 71–80](https://doi.org/10.2143/AST.32.1.1015)). This fact applies conditionally in one coordinate. The final family need not be jointly comonotone across all coordinates; the proof iterates the local alignment, keeping the retained masks fixed. No new general majorization principle is asserted.

**Search bounds (19 November 2026).** External checks included the original and later deletion-channel papers and their titles, theses and recent preprints, searches for deletion-channel posterior or input entropy, constant words and arbitrary deletions, supersequence embedding counts, convex order and majorization, and coordinate alignment of cylinders/subcubes. No previous all-finite-length majorization result for these posteriors was located. This is a bounded bibliographic search, not a worldwide priority guarantee.
