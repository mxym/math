# Question, earlier results, and limits of the search (19 November 2026)

* [Stephan Wagner's original article](https://www.finanz.math.tugraz.at/~wagner/dominating.pdf) proves a *lower* bound $2^n$ for the sum of the numbers of dominating sets in a graph and its complement, and discusses the upper extremal problem.
* [Lauren Keough and David Shane, Involve **12** (2019), 1175–1181, Conj. 1.2](https://doi.org/10.2140/involve.2019.12.1175), [journal text](https://msp.org/involve/2019/12-7/involve-v12-n7-p07-p.pdf), first circulated [2018 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05576). They credit Wagner in the conjecture's formulation. They report a finite check through 10 vertices; Lemma 2.1 gives the single-vertex loss, and Thm. 3.1 the exact degree interval for any extremizer. Both are existing results; this manuscript uses them directly. Their §4 notes that the exponential order of the gap from the trivial $2^{n+1}$ is already determined, and Prop. 4.1 handles complete multipartite graphs. Our assertion is an exactly attained expression with equality classification *for every sufficiently large order without assuming any graph class*, not the first estimate of that exponential order.
* [BIRS workshop 24w5298, Movement and Symmetry in Graphs, report §3.4, pp. 7–8](https://stats.birs.ca/workshops/2024/24w5298/report24w5298.pdf). The working group led by JD Nir (Gabriel Verret, Andrii Arman, Candida Bowtell, Emily Heath, Jeanette Janssen, Karen Gunderson) restates the conjecture at Conj. 14. It identifies the family of nondominating subsets with sets having a common neighbor in the complement, including the empty set: our union of neighborhood subcubes is the same elementary characterization. It proposes a different inequality on first and second common neighborhoods (Conj. 17), proves it for complete bipartite graphs (Thm. 18), and derives a conditional degree-sum estimate below the requested bound. The section ends with Open Problem 19 about the second-common-neighborhood conjecture, not an unconditional determination of large-order extremizers. The present argument does not resolve Conj. 17.
* [T. N. Sanh, arXiv:2607.25188v1, posted 28 July 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25188v1), Thm. 1.3 and Cor. 2.2 already establish the claimed inequality for every bipartite graph at *all orders*, with the stronger expression for a specified bipartition; Thm. 3.1 gives exact equality including the one-vertex-side cases and $2K_2$ at sides $2,2$. The complement symmetry gives the corresponding co-bipartite cases. The result here does not replace Sanh's all-orders theorem for that class. We have not verified a journal version.
