# Independent internal audit of the order-160 domination argument

Reviewed frozen proof SHA-256 0c2cb55496706cd53eacce0f2df9a54eb65d8a80d55a1eb0238e6e6af250deb0. PASS for all n≥160 and for the exact equality classification on those orders. The public proof has the same mathematics; only its opening status label was changed. This is an independent AI-assisted check, not external peer review, and leaves the smaller general cases open.

* The failure-family identity and complete-bipartite deficiency were checked through the literal closed-neighborhood condition. The one-row bound restricts the two complementary open neighborhoods to size between m−2 and m+2. Hamming distance between the two corresponding rows changes by at most two when changing to complement neighborhoods.
* A pair of A-rows at distance 32 costs at most 3·2^(m−14) in the two overlap counts. Taking three such centers and one further row, fix the nearest center's vertex in every selected subset of the fourth row's matching cube. The index lies in the fourth row's generator but neither generator of its nearest center. The two farther centers are at distance at least 16, costing at most 6·2^(m−6), strictly less than this gain even after paying all three original overlaps. This works in the odd case where the three separate contributions alone have no extra exponential room. One-center covering is impossible by the degree lower bound applied columnwise.
* The majority group has a constant template per column, with at most n/2+5/2 actual instances of that template bit; its size is therefore at most n/2+33.5 from the total discrepancy. In an agreeing column of the two templates there are at least m−2 errors. This proves the 64-column maximum and the ensuing cross-group overlap cost. That cost is applied only to at most 11 chosen rows per group, never to a certificate with all n rows.
* An open-neighborhood duplicate in G and one in its complement cannot involve the same central vertex: they demand opposite adjacency to the two witness vertices (and those witnesses must be distinct), which forces contradictory adjacency between the witnesses by the duplicate equalities. Their nontrivial classes therefore have disjoint support, and advantage in one family is forced. The cube-union inequality follows by selecting one point of the new generator outside each earlier generator, ensuring a genuinely new subcube. Exact rational table confirms the lower budget (both parities) for six distinct same-degree generators. A second distinct generator in the other family supplies at least a half-size subcube, exceeding both baseline deficits and overlap.
* The ten identical open neighborhoods fill one cube completely and the complementary cube except for its small all-twin tail. Inserting any anomalous coordinate into its actual generator yields a half-cube wholly outside that family's template, exceeding the loss and overlap even against the other group's weaker baseline. No exceptions remain. Symmetry then gives constant within-group edge bits and a constant cross bit. No universal vertex or isolate is compatible with the degree bound, so the graph or its complement is complete bipartite; discrete convexity of the exact deficiency gives precisely the balanced split.
* The separate standard-library script directly compares domination to neighborhood unions for 33,866 labeled graphs and exactly replays the finite parity/certificate constants. These checks are auxiliary; the proof does not enumerate graphs of orders n≥160.

[Keough–Shane](https://msp.org/involve/2019/12-7/involve-v12-n7-p07-p.pdf) states the general conjecture. [BIRS 2024, §3.4](https://stats.birs.ca/workshops/2024/24w5298/report24w5298.pdf) already relates the deficiency to common-neighbor families; its Theorem 18 concerns a different common-neighborhood statement for complete bipartite graphs. [Sanh's 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25188) treats all bipartite graphs, not all graphs with at least 160 vertices.
