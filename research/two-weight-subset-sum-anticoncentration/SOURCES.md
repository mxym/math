# Earlier work, scope and search limits

1. [Nederlof, Pawlewicz, Swennenhuis and Węgrzycki](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1478112) first established a general quantitative trade-off between the number of distinct subset sums and the size of a large fiber in connection with bin packing (SODA 2021; SIAM J. Comput. 2023).
2. [Jain, Sah and Sawhney, *Anticoncentration versus the Number of Subset Sums*, Adv. Combin. (2021), p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07726v2#page=2), explicitly conjectured $\delta(\varepsilon)\le2\varepsilon$ where $\rho\ge2^{-\varepsilon n}$ implies $K\le2^{\delta(\varepsilon)n}$. Taking $\varepsilon=-\log_2(\rho)/n$ gives $K\rho^2\le1$; conversely that inequality implies their claim. The all-zero vector can be treated directly. The conjecture appears in the **sentence immediately before their Eq. (1.2)**; the equation itself is their proved $\delta(\varepsilon)=O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$. Their repeated, widely separated blocks already explain the asymptotically sharp factor 2. Rationally independent weight types and finite separated blocks both reduce to a product of binomial laws when the block-count map is injective. We do not claim the product example as new.
3. [Jiange Li, *Multiplicative comparisons of Rényi entropies for weighted Bernoulli sums*, arXiv:2609.01529v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01529v2), Conjecture 1.1 and Remark 1.2, state the general endpoint inequality as $H_0\le2H_\infty$, credit **Mokshay Madiman** with communicating the conjecture, and acknowledge the earlier equivalent formulation of Jain, Sah and Sawhney. Here $H_0=\log K$ and $H_\infty=-\log\rho$. Li's Theorem 1.4 gives constant-factor comparisons for strictly positive Rényi orders; Theorem 1.5 gives a general endpoint comparison with a logarithmic factor. Neither establishes the exact constant 2 in general.
4. A uniform independent subset is **not** a uniform subset of prescribed cardinality. In the latter model the two-type counts are coupled hypergeometric variables, not the independent binomials in this result. Nor are we counting the proportion of nonzero subset sums: $K$ here counts *different values* in the support.
