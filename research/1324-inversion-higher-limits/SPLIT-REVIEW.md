* Only two-expandable-family terms have nonzero affine slope. Their parents are on opposite sides of the other family because they are comparable to it and invert their own. Every ordinary point is in one of the three common gap intervals. A left-end ordinary point must invert the left parent or it and a right-family leaf would bracket a 1324; it is comparable to the right parent and all other ordinary parts. As it is not a regular leaf it also has an internal inversion neighbor. Thus the left decoration avoids 132 and has no singleton graph component. A decomposable 132-avoider must end in a singleton, so the decoration is empty or indecomposable. This is the essential correction to a bare two-star model. Its points form one contiguous subpermutation: if the left parent comes before its own increasing family in one coordinate order and after it in the other, the decoration is below the family in both and all its points invert the parent, forcing the parent outside the decoration in each order. The right case is symmetric. In the middle any inversion is bracketed by the two leaf families. A middle point cannot be another leaf or isolated, so it meets both parents. The positions and values of the parents are then precisely the axis insertion choices with those incidences, including both possible two-component decorations.

The only possible insertion gap is the between-components gap, whose saturated case is treated differently in the signed numerator. The formulas for h and k give j=t+e+u+v−g for the positive slope term and j−1 for the negative one when unsaturated. To test a forbidden pattern it collapses the consecutive position and value subinterval of every possible decoration use to a prescribed colored point.
