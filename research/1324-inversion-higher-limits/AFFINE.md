# Eventual affinity of moving first differences for finitely many forbidden patterns including 1324

Fix a finite basis B containing 1324; extra redundant restrictions are harmless. Put
a(n,k)=|Av_n^k(B)|, counting by exact number of inversions, and D(n,k)=a(n+1,k)−a(n,k).
For **each** integer j there are integers A_j,T_j such that for all sufficiently large n,
$$
             D(n,n+j-1)=A_j n+T_j .
$$
There is no nonnegativity assertion. In particular the difference of D between consecutive points of this diagonal is eventually constant, at A_j. A secondary sequence exists exactly when all the A_j are zero. This proves stabilization at latest at the tertiary difference in the sense of Claesson–Linusson–Ulfarsson–Verkama (CLÚV), arXiv:2604.01143v2 §5.2, Problem 8.4, for all pairs in that problem and many other finite bases. This theorem by itself does not designate which pairs stabilize at the earlier stage. Finite tests for bounded j are unnecessary to its proof.

**Cores and the first difference.** The inversion graph has one vertex per point and an edge for a pair in opposite orders of positions and values. Its connected components are the direct-sum components. Indeed, an entry between the positions of the endpoints of an inversion must be inverted with an endpoint, and the union of position intervals of the edges of any nontrivial component is an interval covering its full horizontal span (walks concatenate intervals). Thus no point of another component is interleaved, and the separated components are increasing relative to each other. Singletons are included. Delete all singleton components to obtain the unique core with c nonsingleton components, length h and k inversions. Always c≤2: when c≥3 one point, then an inversion, then one point in three distinct consecutive chosen components gives 1324. Write the permutation by inserting singleton runs of lengths z_0,...,z_c into the c+1 gaps. The no-core case is included.

Let M be the maximum of 4 and the basis lengths. For fixed core, whether all patterns of B are avoided depends only on g_s=min(M,z_s): any witness uses at most M points per run. At most one saturated gap g_s=M can occur in a legal tuple, since an inversion in an intervening component together with singleton points in the two gaps gives 1324. Write f for the number of saturated gaps and G for the sum of g_s. The contribution of the core and an allowed capped tuple to (1−x) times the length/inversion generating function is q^k x^(h+G) times 1−x if f=0 and times 1 if f=1. Thus D(n,k) sums contributions with
$$
                 n+1=h+G+o,\qquad o\in\{0,1\},
$$
of sign (−1)^o, with o=1 allowed only when f=0. On k=n+j−1, the inversion graph surplus (number of edges outside a spanning forest)
$$
                 s=k-h+c=j+G+o+c-2
$$
is nonnegative and bounded in terms of j and M, since G≤3M.

**Bounded because of 1324.** In each nonsingleton component take a shortest path v_0,...,v_L between the leftmost and rightmost points in its position order. Every outside vertex lies inside a position interval spanned by an edge of that path, so it has at least one neighbor on the path. A shortest path has no chords. If L≥5, the internal edge v_2 v_3 is not adjacent at either endpoint to v_0 or v_L; position extremality puts v_0 below and to the left of both internal points and v_L above and to their right. This gives 1324, impossible. Thus this dominating path has at most five vertices. Join all off-path vertices to the path by one chosen edge each. The resulting forest has all components of the core; any off-path vertex of degree ≥2 meets one of the s excess edges. Hence the union of the selected paths and the off-path vertices of degree ≥2 has size at most 5c+2s. In particular the path union includes every component of order two.

Call vertices of degree one in components of order at least three regular leaves. All regular leaves together are increasing: two adjacent degree-one vertices would be an entire component of order two. For any parent P of regular leaves (their unique neighbor), its neighbors in this increasing list form one interval, the elements between its insertion cuts in the two orders. All leaves of the list before this interval lie southwest of P, and all after it northeast of P. If both sides were nonempty, those two extremal witnesses enclose an inversion from P to a leaf of the interval, giving 1324. Thus every nonempty parent family is a prefix or suffix of the one common list. They partition that list, so there are at most two families.

Each such family is in fact a consecutive increasing interval of the core in **both** orders. For leaves a,b of the same family in increasing order, the parent P cannot lie horizontally between them (it inverts both). Any other point w horizontally between them is comparable to a,b and lies vertically between them too. Any inversion neighbor of w other than P must likewise be horizontally between a,b (or it would be comparable to w by its comparisons to a,b); it would again be vertically between them. This neighbor and w would give a 1324 bracketed by a,b. Thus w has no inversion neighbor except possibly P. Since the core has no isolated vertices, w too is a regular leaf of P. Interchanging axes proves the vertical assertion.

**Canonical contraction and enumeration.** Replace every regular-leaf family of length L by an increasing interval of length min(L,M), and standardize. Affected parents retain at least M neighbors, so they do not become leaves; all other surviving adjacencies, degrees outside those parents, and the components are preserved. The operation can be inverted by freely expanding precisely the regular-leaf families of length M in the resulting permutation (including when zero new points are added); the expanded points invert only their own parent. Hence both h and k increase by one per added point. This gives a bijection, not a cover by overlapping inflations: compression is unique and the left-to-right lengths recover each full core. Avoidance of B, even after inserting any fixed capped singleton-gap tuple, is unchanged by these expansions, since occurrences have length at most M. Conversely every compressed core appearing in this description is counted with exactly those expanded lengths and no others.

The contracted cores with given s,c have bounded size: aside from at most 5c+2s path or exceptional vertices, there are at most 2M retained regular leaves. Enumerate this finite list, capped gap tuples and signs. Retain only those consistent with the displayed value of s for a given j. For any such template let h_0 be the compressed length and d≤2 its number of expandable leaf families. The total expansion amount is m=n+1−G−o−h_0. If d=0 the count is zero for all large n, if d=1 it is one for m≥0, and if d=2 it is m+1 for m≥0. The surplus identity gives exactly the required inversion number automatically. Thus each signed contribution is eventually affine in n. Their finite sum proves the assertion. In particular coefficients can be found by exhaustive *finite* exact enumeration separately for each j.
