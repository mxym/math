# Sources and scope (19 November 2026)

**The precise question and predictions.** [A. Claesson, S. Linusson, H. Úlfarsson and E. Verkama, arXiv:2604.01143v2, November 6, §§5.2 and 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01143v2) define secondary stabilization of the first length difference at a fixed inversion number along each line j=k−n+1; tertiary stabilization takes one further difference along that line. Their Problem 8.4 asks why one of these occurs for every pair {1324,p} with p of length four. They state the six/five split as empirical, except for their Theorem 7.21: when k≤2n−7 the p=1342 first difference is already exactly the j-th coefficient of (2+2y) times their primary series. Their Theorem 5.10 already determines *all primary* series. Their Table 1 also records that {1324,2143} is not inversion monotone; the negative slope here is not a first disproof. Their eight half-monotone pairs and their separate Problem 8.1 concern signs, not stabilization.

The [original April version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01143v1) and the expanded Paper B in [Verkama's licentiate thesis, §§5.3–5.5, printed pp. 131–139](https://diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A2050045/FULLTEXT01.pdf) are particularly important. The latter reports the −2 followed by zeros for 2143, recognizes the factor three for 2413 and the shifted primary multiples for 2431 and 3412, and displays the initial 4231 coefficients (0,0,2,4,6,12,18,28) without an explanation. The proven identities here match those observations. The November supplementary Tables S3–S24 are identified in v2. Do not describe the observed sequences, or their previously suggested primary multiples, as new discoveries. The contribution is the finite structural proof, the classifications for all indices and the five exact identities, not new numerical evidence.

**Structural and enumerative inputs.**
* [Claesson–Jelínek–Steingrímsson, §4, Proposition 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5736) explicitly introduce cores and singleton padding, bound large gaps and derive polynomiality when the inversion count is fixed. Their Proposition 15 gives the stabilized primary 1324 count. Those tools do not bound the two expandable leaf families when k grows with n. [Homberger–Vatter](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4946) develop a general encoding and enumeration of polynomial permutation classes through monotone inflations; here the objects on a fixed-surplus diagonal are not themselves assumed to be a permutation class, and both the two-family bound and signed cancellation require proof.
* [Linusson–Verkama, *Enumerating 1324-Avoiders with Few Inversions*, EJC 32(3), P3.44 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.37236/13387) already obtain the principal 1324 secondary stabilization by exact enumeration in the wider wedge k≤2n−7. For the other pairs, CLÚV Proposition 5.6 and Corollary 5.8 already factor the primary count via indecomposable endpoint classes. SERIES.md explicitly *uses* this result, not a new partition factorization at the primary level.
* [Franklín, *Pattern Avoiding Permutations Enumerated by Inversions*, Theorem 18](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07467v4) and the previous [Franklín–Claesson–Bean–Úlfarsson–Pantone, GASCom 2024, Theorem 16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16403) identify indecomposable Av(132,231) by inversions with distinct-part partitions. This supplies the exact endpoint product in the 4231 tertiary identity. Earlier unweighted enumerations of particular two-pattern classes (e.g. [Albert–Atkinson–Vatter, 1324/4231](https://doi.org/10.37236/225)) and ordinary Wilf equivalences do not directly identify these inversion-diagonal differences.

**Recent parallel work.** [L. Meng, arXiv:2609.06717v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06717v2), contracts increasing runs and proves a fixed-defect polynomial (including its leading series) for non-almost-decomposable residuals at k=2n−7+δ. Its finite skeleton and occupancy analysis is closely related methodologically. This is not the present k=n−1+j signed difference or the pair classification. Meng's [companion, *The two ends of the inversion spectrum of 1324-avoiding permutations*](https://github.com/lsmeng/1324-inversion-monotonicity/blob/main/papers/two_ends.tex), gives an exact primary-class low-end identity using indecomposable counts and a polynomial top-end count at a fixed number of noninversions. [E. Verkama, arXiv:2611.17237, November 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.17237v1), proves lower bounds on inversions under full or weak graph connectivity and characterizes the excluded patterns giving stronger bounds. It does not derive CLÚV's secondary/tertiary split. The related results of the 2020 [Bevan–Brignall–Elvey Price–Pantone structural study of Av(1324)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2019.103115) concern staircase/grid decompositions and growth-rate bounds, not these inversion diagonals.
