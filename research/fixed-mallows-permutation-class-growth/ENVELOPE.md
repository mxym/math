# Square-root core entropy and a uniform weighted monotonicity window

Use the notation of the fixed-$q$ theorem. In its polynomial case the number of cores of inversion count $k$ is at most $\exp(C_B\sqrt{k+1})$. In particular, writing $a(n,k)$ for the 1324-avoiders, for some absolute $C,D$,
$$a(n,k)\le e^{C\sqrt{k+1}}\quad(n,k\ge0),\qquad
 Z_{n+1}(q)> Z_n(q)\quad (n\text{ sufficiently large},\ 0<q\le e^{-D/\sqrt n}).$$
The latter compares inversion-weighted sums at the **same** $q$, which may vary with $n$ within the indicated range. It does not give coefficientwise monotonicity.

**Positions and values near a cut.** Fix a nonempty core of size $h\le k+m-1$ and total inversion count $k\ge1$. By the closed-interval transversal argument in the main proof there is a set $S\subset[ h ]$ of at most $m-1$ *integer* cuts hitting all its inversion intervals (take right endpoints). Include its choice and $h$ at polynomial cost in $k+2$, with $m$ fixed. If $S$ is empty there is no inversion and nothing to count. Split $[h]\setminus S$ into open intervals. Mark as exceptional all entries whose position **or** value is on a cut, and then all entries whose position and value are in different open intervals. There are at most $2(m-1)$ entries of the first kind, and one may record both coordinates of all of them at another polynomial cost. Diagonal cell entries that remain form a single increasing subsequence: within each cell their inversion interval if inverted would miss $S$, and the diagonal cells are ordered in both coordinates.

Consider the second kind of exceptional entry $(x,y)$. There is a cut strictly between $x$ and $y$, so both $d(x,S)$ and $d(y,S)$ are at most $|x-y|$. The sum of position distances of this kind is at most $2k$ by the footrule inequality, as is the sum of its value distances. For each positive integer distance $w$ there are at most $2(m-1)$ possible positions at distance $w$. Therefore the number of possible position subsets of distance sum at most $2k$ is bounded by the cumulative coefficient sum through $2k$ of $\prod_{w\ge1}(1+z^w)^{2(m-1)}$, and likewise for values. Each such cumulative sum is $\exp[O_m(\sqrt{k+1})]$: take $z=e^{-1/\sqrt{k}}$, bound the cumulative sum by $z^{-2k}$ times the product, and use $\sum_{w\ge1}\log(1+z^w)\le z/(1-z)\le\sqrt{k}$.

There are $O_m(\sqrt{k+1})$ exceptional entries: each position within $\sqrt{k}$ of a cut contributes at most one, and those farther away number at most $2k/\sqrt{k}$. Given the two exceptional coordinate sets one need only specify the relative permutation of exceptional entries, because all other entries increase. This pattern is itself $L_m$-avoiding. The Marcus–Tardos theorem gives at most $C_m^e$ choices for an $e$-point exceptional pattern; if both coordinates of cut entries were already supplied this is still an upper bound. Combining all factors proves the asserted core estimate. The subsequent capped-gap proof gives $a_B(n,k)\le (1+n)^{r-1}\exp(C_B\sqrt{k+1})$ for the whole polynomial regime: each actual legal tuple with $f\ge1$ costs at most a constant times $(1+n)^{f-1}$ at fixed $n$, and a tuple with no free gap contributes at most one. In particular $r=1$ for 1324.

**Uniform positive difference.** By Linusson–Verkama, $a(n+1,k)-a(n,k)=4p_2(k-n+1)+2p_2(k-n)\ge0$ for every $k\le 2n-7$, using $p_2(i)=0$ for $i<0$. At $k=n-1$, for $n\ge6$, the difference is four. Above that line the total negative contribution in absolute value is bounded by $\sum_{k\ge K}a(n,k)q^k$ with $K=2n-6$. By the bound just proved and concavity of the square root, this tail is at most
$$\frac{q^K\exp(C\sqrt{K+1})}{1-q\exp(C/(2\sqrt{K+1}))}$$
whenever the denominator is positive. For all sufficiently large $n$, $K+1\ge n$, $K+1\le2n$, and $K-(n-1)=n-5\ge n/2$. Choose $D>4(C+1)$. Uniformly over $0<q\le e^{-D/\sqrt n}$, the denominator is at least $1-e^{-(D-C/2)/\sqrt n}\ge c_D/\sqrt n$ for large $n$. After division by $q^{n-1}$ the bound tends to zero, since its numerator is at most $\exp[(C\sqrt2-D/2)\sqrt n]$. The positive low term thus strictly dominates throughout the claimed window.
