The chosen endpoints are integer cuts and their positions cost only a fixed-degree polynomial based on the core-length bound. Allow arbitrary coordinates for the bounded entries whose position or value is a cut. Every remaining off-diagonal exceptional entry crosses a cut, so each axis contributes at most its displacement to the distance budget; each budget is at most the footrule, bounded by 2k. The set of choices at each distance has bounded multiplicity. The partition-product bound follows by evaluating its positive coefficients at e^(−1/sqrt(k)). Counting nearby and far positions yields O(sqrt(k)) exceptions, and their induced pattern still avoids the forbidden layered pattern, so the previously known Marcus–Tardos bound absorbs their assignment without a factorial loss. The remaining diagonal cells form a unique increasing matching. Capped gaps preserve this core coefficient bound times the stated power of n, and that power is zero for 1324.

Subtracting consecutive Linusson–Verkama formulas on their common range indeed gives 4p_2(k−n+1)+2p_2(k−n), including four at k=n−1 for n≥6. Above the common range the negative part is bounded by the single original-size weighted upper tail. Concavity of the square root bounds that tail by the displayed geometric series. The q-window yields an exponentially small ratio to q^(n−1) uniformly, despite the denominator's possible n^(−1/2) scale. This is not coefficientwise monotonicity, not a q=1 claim, and not a sharp √k constant or an optimized window edge.
