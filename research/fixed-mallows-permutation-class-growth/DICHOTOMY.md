# Fixed-q inversion partition weights of finitely based permutation classes

For a finite set of nonempty classical patterns $B$ write $C=\operatorname{Av}(B)$ and $Z_n(q)=\sum_{\pi\in C\cap S_n}q^{\operatorname{inv}\pi}$. Let $L$ be all direct sums of components of type $1$ or $21$. For positive integers $m$ let $L_m=(21)^{\oplus m}$. The following concerns the fixed parameter $0<q<1$, not the weak tilt $q=e^{\beta/n}$ or the unweighted Stanley–Wilf growth problem.

1. If $B\cap L=\varnothing$, then $L\subseteq C$ and $Z_n(q)\ge F_n(q)$ with $F_0=F_1=1$, $F_n=F_{n-1}+q F_{n-2}$. The exponential rate of $Z_n$ exists and is at least $(1+\sqrt{1+4q})/2>1$.
2. If $B$ contains an identity, $Z_n(q)=O_q(e^{-c_q n^2})$ for some $c_q>0$ (including possibly zero).
3. Otherwise, if $B\cap L\ne\varnothing$, put $r=\min\{\operatorname{inv}p:p\in B\cap L\}\ge1$. There is a polynomial $P(n,q)$ in $n$ of degree at most $r-1$, with coefficient functions holomorphic for $|q|<1$. Its degree at every real $0<q<1$ is exactly $r-1$, with positive leading coefficient. For every $0<t<u<1$,
$$\sup_{|q|\le t}|Z_n(q)-P(n,q)|=O_{B,t,u}(u^n).\tag{1}$$

In particular, classes excluding a one-inversion pattern but no identity have a nonzero finite limit at fixed $q<1$; the previously known stabilization of each individual inversion coefficient is not by itself a dominated convergence argument. In case 3, the fixed-$q$ inversion-weighted growth classification is insensitive to the other, non-$L$ forbidden patterns only at the level of polynomial degree, not the individual coefficient functions.

**The two easy regimes.** $L$ is a class, and every word in its two indecomposable blocks has unique decoding; this proves its displayed enumeration. For the existence of the exponential rate split a permutation after $n$ positions and keep the two standardized patterns. The choice of the values on the two sides contributes a Gaussian binomial weight, so
$$Z_{n+m}(q)\le {n+m\brack n}_q Z_n(q)Z_m(q)\le C_q Z_n(q)Z_m(q),\quad C_q=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^j)^{-1}.$$
Submultiplicativity and finiteness (e.g. $Z_n\le(1-q)^{-n}$) give the assertion in case 1. If an identity of length $h\ge2$ is forbidden, rank each entry by its longest increasing subsequence ending there. Equal-rank elements form a decreasing sequence, so there are at least $n^2/[2(h-1)]-n/2$ inversions. The crude factor $n!$ is absorbed by the quadratic penalty; $h=1$ is empty at all positive sizes.

**A summable bound independent of host length.** In case 3, fix $m$ such that $L_m\notin C$, possible because every member of $L$ is a pattern of some $L_m$. In any $\pi\in C$ there are at most $m-1$ non-singleton direct-sum components (one inversion can be chosen in each). Delete *all singleton components* to obtain a core $v$ with $j\le m-1$ non-singleton components, total size $h$ and $k$ inversions; at $k=0$ use the empty core. Any indecomposable of size $l$ has at least $l-1$ inversions: sorting by inversion-decreasing adjacent swaps, each of the $l-1$ index cuts must be crossed by some swap, since none was a separating cut in the original permutation. Thus $h\le k+j\le k+m-1$.

For each inversion of the core assign the closed real interval whose endpoints are the smallest and largest of its two positions and two values. Any $m$ pairwise disjoint such intervals would give an occurrence of $L_m$, since both positions and both values of one inversion precede those in the next. Hence $m-1$ points suffice to meet all inversion intervals: successively take the least remaining right endpoint and remove all intervals containing it, leaving a disjoint family of the selected witnessing intervals. For $k\ge1$ let $H=\lceil\sqrt{k}\rceil$. At most $2k/H$ entries have displacement $|v(i)-i|>H$, by the footrule inequality $\sum_i|v(i)-i|\le2k$ (alternatively, each adjacent swap changes the sum by at most two). Delete those entries and all remaining entries whose position lies within $3H$ of a chosen transversal point. There are $O_m(\sqrt{k}+1)$ deleted entries. Indeed in any inversion between two entries of displacement at most $H$, their positions differ by at most $2H$ and their inversion interval is entirely within $3H$ of either position. Thus all remaining entries are increasing. Given the exceptional positions, values and their relative order the permutation is determined. Summing also over $h\le k+m-1$ proves that the number of distinct possible cores at inversion count $k$ is
$$\le \exp\big[O_m(\sqrt{k+1}\log(k+2))\big].\tag{2}$$
This counts actual distinct cores by the existence of a small deletion set; uniqueness of its choice is unnecessary.

**Capped gaps and onset.** Put $M=\max_{p\in B}|p|$. The unique way to enlarge a core without changing its nontrivial direct-sum decomposition is to insert singleton sequences in the $j+1$ gaps around its components. Membership in $C$ depends only on each gap length truncated to $M$: any forbidden witness uses at most $M$ singleton positions in each gap, so can be copied in either direction. Partition the legal gap vectors by this truncated tuple $d\in\{0,\ldots,M\}^{j+1}$. Its entries less than $M$ are fixed, the $f$ entries equal to $M$ are free to grow. Write $a=h+\sum_i d_i\le k+C$, where one can take $C=m-1+Mm$. If $f=0$ its contribution at length $n$ is $\mathbf1_{n=a}$ and its approximating polynomial is zero. If $f\ge1$, its contribution is zero at $n<a$ and otherwise
$$B_{f,a}(n)=\binom{n-a+f-1}{f-1}$$
using the polynomial interpretation for all complex $n$. For $n>k+C$ the formula (including the identically zero one for $f=0$) is therefore exact, with **unit coefficient** of $k$ in the onset.

No legal tuple has $f>r$. Choose the $r+1$ distinct free gaps in order if possible, and an inversion from a component strictly between each consecutive pair. The identity blocks of lengths $M$ in those gaps plus these $r$ descents contain any fixed pattern $p\in B\cap L$ with $r$ double blocks. This is impossible by heredity. Conversely take the core $L_{r-1}$ with all its $r$ gaps free (empty core if $r=1$): all enlargements stay inside $L$ and have exactly $r-1$ inversions, so cannot contain any forbidden pattern. Thus the maximum $f$ is exactly $r$.

Let $P_k(n)$ be the sum of all the approximating polynomials over these tuples at $k$ inversions. Their number is at most (2) times $(M+1)^m$. All polynomial coefficients are bounded by that quantity times $O_B((1+k)^m)$, by the product formula for the binomial polynomial and $a\le k+C$. So $P(n,q)=\sum_{k\ge0}q^kP_k(n)$ has degree at most $r-1$ and analytic coefficients. For positive $q$ every tuple of maximal degree has a positive leading coefficient $1/(r-1)!$, one such tuple exists, and no cancellation in that coefficient is possible. For $k\ge n-C$ also $n=O_B(1+k)$, so both true and polynomial tuple counts are bounded in absolute value by $O_B((1+k)^m)$ per tuple, including negative polynomial arguments. All smaller $k$ agree exactly. Hence the error for $|q|\le t$ is bounded by $\sum_{k\ge n-C}t^k\exp[O_B(\sqrt{k+1}\log(k+2))]$, which is $O_{B,t,u}(u^n)$ for any $u>t$, as claimed.

**A different, weaker-than-coefficientwise consequence for 1324.** Let $p_2(k)=[x^k]P_2(x)$ with $P_2(x)=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-x^j)^{-2}$, and extend $p_2$ by zero below zero. Linusson–Verkama's exact low-inversion enumeration gives, for $k\le2n-7$,
$$a(n,k)=p_2(k)-4p_2(k-n+1)-6\sum_{i=0}^{k-n}p_2(i).$$
The coefficient of the right-hand side, with no truncation in $k$, is generated by $P_2(q)[1-(4+2q)q^{n-1}/(1-q)]$. For $k>2n-7$ the true count is bounded by (2) times $O_B((1+k)^m)$ since $n\le O(1+k)$. The other terms are subexponential as well, by convergence of $P_2$ on the unit disk and positivity (or the partition bound). For each $0<t<u<1$ this proves uniformly on $|q|\le t$ that
$$Z_n(q)=P_2(q)\left[1-\frac{4+2q}{1-q}q^{n-1}\right]+O_{t,u}(u^{2n}).\tag{3}$$
At each fixed real $q\in(0,1)$ choose $q<u<\sqrt{q}$; subtracting consecutive sizes gives $Z_{n+1}-Z_n\sim(4+2q)P_2(q)q^{n-1}>0$. This is eventual strict increase **after summing with weight $q^k$**, not the Claesson–Jelínek–Steingrímsson conjecture for each pair $(n,k)$. The explicit constants in the bracket are the earlier coefficient formula; the all-$q$ remainder is the analytic transfer.
