# Existing results, methods and scope as of 19 November 2026

**Core and padding are prior, not just terminology.** [Claesson, Jelínek and Steingrímsson, *Upper bounds for the Stanley–Wilf limit of 1324 and other layered patterns*, §4, Propositions 21, 23 and Corollary 24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5736), already identify the Fibonacci class of sums of 1 and 21, the nontrivial-component core, and its singleton padding. They prove eventual polynomiality at a fixed inversion count for a single forbidden pattern, with degree $r-1$ in the Fibonacci case for the indicated $k$ and degree $k$ in the non-Fibonacci case. Their proof uses downsets of integer compositions (Stanley's result) and counts large padding gaps. Their Proposition 15 already determines the stabilized 1324 coefficients. We use this framework; the uniform subexponential control across $k$, the finite-basis polynomial as a holomorphic sum, exponentially small errors and resulting weighted estimates are distinct. Their prospective precise upper bound by the two-partition coefficients is considerably sharper than $\exp(C\sqrt{k+1})$.

[Claesson, Linusson, Úlfarsson and Verkama, v2, November 6, 2026, Proposition 5.1 and (5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01143v2#S5.SS1) characterize the finitely based classes having a constant tail at *each fixed* $k$ by the presence of a forbidden pattern of at most one inversion. In the one-inversion case they even give onset $n\ge k+O_B(1)$; Proposition 5.6 gives an explicit stabilized pair description for classes forbidding 1324. This prior is credited for both the qualitative criterion and the unit-slope onset in that case. It does not on its own justify summing the tails at an arbitrary nonzero fixed $q$.

**1324 formula.** The entire low-$k$ coefficient correction, including the numerical coefficients used here, is due to Linusson and Verkama, [Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 32 (2025), P3.44, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.37236/13387); see also their [FPSAC 2025 paper](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~slc/wpapers/FPSAC2025/132.pdf). It applies for $k\le2n-7$. The bracket in (3) is exactly the generating function obtained by extending that prior formula formally. The new estimate bounds the tail outside its proved coefficient range; it does not rederive or replace their exact enumeration. [Meng, September v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06717v2), proves coefficientwise monotonicity through $2n+6$ and in regions with growing defect; its generating function for leading coefficients of fixed-defect *residual* counts is not the fixed-$q$ partition function. The [companion project](https://github.com/lsmeng/1324-inversion-monotonicity) treats both ends of the 1324 inversion spectrum and an exact low-end difference identity; these are related coefficient problems, not the general analytic classification. See also [Verkama, November 12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17237), on inversion lower bounds from higher connectivity of inversion graphs. No claim about the unresolved unrestricted coefficientwise conjecture, even for 1324.

**Fixed versus weak Mallows, classical versus consecutive patterns.** Ross Pinsky, [*Permutations avoiding a pattern of length three under Mallows distributions*, author full text](https://math-faculty.net.technion.ac.il/files/2021/05/Permutations-avoiding-a-certain-pattern-of-length-three-under-Mallows-distributions-pdf-to-appear-in-Random-Structures-and-Algorithms.pdf), published in [Random Structures & Algorithms 58 (2021), 676–690](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20988), treats the classical fixed-$q$ probability for length-three patterns. Theorem 1 gives quadratic probability exponent $q^{1/4}$ for 123, exactly the coefficient $\log(q)/4$ after taking logs; the unrestricted normalizer is only exponential in $n$. Proposition 3 and Theorem 2 cover the existence of the exponential probability rate in that framework and its value $1-q$ for 132 and 213. These width-three cases are not new. [Dubach](https://hal.science/hal-05536105v1/file/ejp.pdf) studies counts of classical patterns in unconditioned Mallows permutations; [Crane–DeSalvo–Elizalde](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01370) treat **consecutive** avoidance. [Budzinski et al., AofA 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.AofA.2026.18) treat $q=e^{\beta/n}$ for forbidden length three, not a fixed parameter; the [November permuton survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12104) was also checked.

**Classical tools and geometric scope.** The strong counting estimate invokes [Marcus–Tardos](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2004.04.002) for the number of patterns avoiding a fixed direct sum of descents, elementary interval stabbing, and the footrule bound [Diaconis–Graham](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1977.tb01624.x). The identity-forbidden branch applies the Erdős–Stone extremal density theorem and Erdős–Simonovits stability for a forbidden complete multipartite graph. For their precise form see [Füredi, §1, equations (1)–(2), including original references](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.03129) and [journal version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2015.05.001). Our use of stability is to identify the limiting ordered permutation plot from the balanced parts of the increasing-pair graph; this is not a new general graph stability theorem.

**Scope of the search.** Retrospective and July–November 19, 2026 checks covered the original and revised primary works above, classical and consecutive Mallows avoidance, inversion polynomials, fixed-$k$ padding, Fibonacci patterns, few inversions near the 1324 defect line, layered extremal density, comparability graph Turán/stability, and constrained permutons. No matching prior result on the combined finite-basis fixed-$q$ polynomial/shape classification or the stated uniform weighted 1324 window was located. The check is bounded, not an assertion of priority. Third-party materials retain their rights.
