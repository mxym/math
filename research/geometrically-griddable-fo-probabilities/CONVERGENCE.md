# A first-order corollary for geometrically griddable classes

**Claim.** Every infinite geometrically griddable permutation class satisfies a first-order TOTO logical convergence law, without a small-growth restriction. No 0–1 conclusion is asserted. This is distinct from permuton convergence. The negative answer for unrestricted proper classes in the companion oscillation note does not contradict it.

Take the unambiguous finite graph and its primitive cyclic components from SHAPES. For clarity replace the symbol of each edge by a new symbol unique to that edge, retaining the original cell as a function of the edge symbol. For each possible initial vertex separately this gives a partial **deterministic** finite automaton on the edge alphabet. Its accepted words are accepting paths. The disjoint family so obtained counts the intended permutations exactly once by global unambiguity; arbitrarily short or empty paths do not affect the conclusion. Trim to productive states and add no artificial productive sink.

For a given first-order TOTO sentence, the position and value comparisons of its point variables pull back to quantifier-free comparisons of cell-word positions: different columns compare by column index, same column by word order or its reversal depending on the column direction. Likewise for row, and equality is identity. Cell predicates are joins of edge predicates of the desired cell. The resulting word sentence is first order in the order and unary alphabet predicates. This quantifier-free interpretation is already in Braunfeld's *Decidability in geometric grid classes*, Lemma 3.3. By the classical characterization of first-order word languages (and Markström–Räty, arXiv:2610.22218v2, Lemma 7), choose a complete deterministic recognizer $B$ of this sentence with power-stabilizing transitions: for some $N$, all states $q$ and words $w$, $\delta(q,w^N)=\delta(q,w^{N+1})$. No periodic modular counting is thus introduced by $B$ on maximal components.

Specifically fix one base automaton $A$ and form the product $A\times B$ with usual predicate acceptance. For each productive cyclic product component $D$ let $C$ be the base component containing its projection. By Markström–Räty **Lemma 51**, the product radius is at most the base radius, and if they coincide and $C$ is primitive, then $D$ is primitive too. This applies to the edge alphabet, not by assuming the original lookahead automaton on cell letters is deterministic. If needed, complete $A$ by an unproductive dead state before constructing the product and then discard it; every relevant component still projects into an original productive component.

Let $r$ be the largest productive base radius over the finitely many initial choices and $d$ the maximum number of successive radius-$r$ components. The denominator is asymptotic to $c r^n n^{d-1}$ by SHAPES. A product graph having largest radius $<r$ contributes $o(r^n)$ (finite matrix powers obey polynomial times exponential bounds). Otherwise every contributing product component of radius $r$ projects to a base component of radius $r$, so is primitive. Use the same route count proof as in SHAPES, allowing subcritical components now to be periodic: their normalized counts remain summable and nonnegative. Thus the numerator has asymptotic $c' r^n n^{d'-1}$ for the largest such $d'$ if present. Since it is bounded by the denominator, $d'\le d$. It follows that the ratio has limit $0$ or $c'/c$. Positive examples for small and polynomial permutation classes and the FO spectral mechanism are due to Markström–Räty and Falgas-Ravry et al.; the input supplied here is the geometric unambiguous primitive presentation beyond those classes.
