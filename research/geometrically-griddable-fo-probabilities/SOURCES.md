# Source and scope comparisons

**Recent direct overlap.** Markström and Räty, [*Logical isomorphism and logical limit laws for small permutation classes and regular languages*](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22218v2) (v2, 19 October 2026), Theorem 1, already give logical convergence, rational counts for a sentence, and structured enumeration for all *small* classes. Their Lemmas 7 and 51 are direct proof ingredients here, not new automata theory. Falgas-Ravry, Markström, Räty and Skerman, [*On first-order limiting probabilities for small permutation classes*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34328) (v1, 25 October), Theorem 1.2 gives the exact **finite** spectrum for polynomial growth, Theorem 1.3 a zero–one law in the signed-permutation-matrix setting, and Theorem 1.4 density for all small exponential classes. Theorem 4.1 is the direct small-event covering precedent. These works are not confined to geometric grid subclasses, nor are geometric classes confined to their small-growth regime. The increasing-cell one-row matrix with $b$ cells has growth $b$ (DENSITY); thus an arbitrary fixed geometric presentation does not imply small growth. Ordinary first-order convergence and rational series already imply the single-field upper bound on their common range. The distinctive claim here is exact attainment of **all** that field interval for every exponentially growing geometric subclass, including outside the small regime.

Earlier special cases include Braunfeld–Kukla, [*Logical limit laws for layered permutations and related structures*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06307), and Albert–Bouvel–Féray–Noy, [*A logical limit law for 231-avoiding permutations*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/11751/download), which establishes convergence and density for 231-avoiders. We do not transfer our field formula to either class without the geometric hypothesis. The November [*Permutons* survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1), especially §5.2, distinguishes geometric from monotone gridding; a permuton scaling limit is not a first-order convergence or attainability theorem. A permuton's finite sampled pattern densities do not, by themselves, decide arbitrary two-order sentences.

**Geometric and word-side inputs.** Albert, Atkinson, Bouvel, Ruškuc and Vatter, [*Geometric grid classes of permutations*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.6319), Propositions 4.2, 7.1 and Theorem 8.1 supply the grid refinement, trace encoding and canonical ungridded section. Braunfeld, [*Decidability in geometric grid classes of permutations*](https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/17083) ([text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.04201)), Proposition 3.1 gives the strip-order acyclicity criterion, Lemma 3.3 provides the interpretation in cell words, and Theorem 5.2 gives effective rational enumeration. Acyclicity is not introduced here; a bounded shortest directed cycle makes the cut test first-order in this presentation. Thiagarajan–Walukiewicz, [*An expressively complete linear time temporal logic for Mazurkiewicz traces*](https://www.labri.fr/perso/igw/Papers/Tmp/igw-LTrL.pdf), §7, Definition 13 and Lemmas 14/16, already give a definable normal linearization on finite traces via comparisons of labels on exclusive upsets. EXACT §1 gives its own greedy greatest-letter decomposition for the *particular lexicographically least* representative needed by the scanner. We make no priority claim for definable trace linearizations. The repository's [geometric shape theorem](../geometrically-griddable-permutation-limit-shapes/SHAPES.md) supplies the unambiguous primitive presentation, route lengths and Parry local law; these are dependencies, not additional results of this note.

Berthé, Goulet-Ouellet and Perrin, [*Density of rational languages under shift invariant measures*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.143), Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 5.1 with §6, give a strong density result for aperiodic languages and field containment under their word-measure hypotheses, including Markov settings. [Lynch's convergence laws](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/7/ocr-ajc-v7-p145.pdf) already treat random independent words. A uniformly chosen ungridded permutation in the present setting corresponds instead to a uniform accepting path, potentially traversing several critical components and subject to endpoint conditions. These comparisons leave no general claim of novelty for word convergence or field containment.

**Probability and algebra used for attainment.** Guibas and Odlyzko, [*String overlaps, pattern matching, and nontransitive games*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(81)90005-4), study first-occurrence races by overlap analysis. Non-overlapping (cross-bifix-free) marker families are classical: see for example [Chee–Kiah–Purkayastha–Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.0236). We only need a simple equal-composition family; its role is to realize any prescribed finite uniform die by a first-order formula on a Perron bridge limit. The repository's [non-overlapping code comparisons](../non-overlapping-codes-oscillation-and-uniformity/README.md) address extremal dictionary **sizes**, not logical probabilities.

Mossel–Peres, [*New coins from old*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0304143), characterize univariate rational-function simulation by finite automata; the recent [Paes Leme–Schneider note](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29595) concerns a multivariate limitation, not used here. Goyal–Sigman, [*On simulating a class of Bernstein polynomials*](https://www.columbia.edu/~vg2277/simulate_bernstein.pdf), already give the fixed-budget Bernoulli trial followed by coefficient-dependent auxiliary randomization in EXACT §5. Bernstein degree elevation and positivity are classical. Akin, Dougherty, Mauldin and Yingst, [*Which Bernoulli measures are good measures?*](https://sites.math.unt.edu/~mauldin/papers/no142.pdf), especially Theorem 1.2, show a close antecedent for the significance of unique real conjugates to clopen value sets in a different, purely i.i.d. setting. Our die allows variable waiting described by first-order formulas, so it is not a mere clopen event of uniformly bounded observation length. The additional choices here are a common return-menu algebraic bias isolating the correct conjugate, its conversion to a rational positive polynomial for a chosen target, and the actual FO implementation on the *ungridded* sample; a general finite-state simulation need not be first-order.
