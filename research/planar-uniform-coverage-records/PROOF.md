# Records for planar uniform full coverage, including the interior–boundary competition

Let $A=\overline{A^\circ}\subset\mathbb R^2$ be compact, of positive area $a$, with $C^{1,1}$ boundary of total perimeter $P$. Sample iid uniformly in $A$. Fix an integer $k\ge1$ and let $R_{n,k}$ be the largest distance of a point of $A$ to its $k$th nearest sample point. Count its strict decreases after time $k$, with time $k$ counted as the first record: $N_{n,k}=1+\sum_{j=k+1}^n\mathbf1\{R_{j,k}<R_{j-1,k}\}$. Let $\nu_{j,k}$ be the index of the $j$th record. Define
$$
 b=P/\sqrt{\pi a},\quad p=\int_0^\infty e^{-z-b\sqrt z}\,dz,
 \qquad c_1=(1+p)/2,\quad c_k=1/2\quad(k\ge2).
$$
Then
$$
 \frac{2 N_{n,k}}{(\log n)^2}\to c_k\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad
 \frac{2\mathbb E N_{n,k}}{(\log n)^2}\to c_k,\qquad
 \nu_{j,k}^{1/\sqrt j}\to\exp\sqrt{2/c_k}\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$
This concerns precisely the uniform planar case $B=A$ conjectured after [Penrose (2026), Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19104v3#S2). The interior/boundary competition in the **one-time radius law** is already known. Here we identify the relative winning frequencies needed for the records; it would not be enough to apply an almost-sure compensator lemma requiring the individual normalized hole masses to converge to a constant.

## Which hole wins at one time?

Write $\lambda=n/a$, $s=(\log\lambda/(\pi\lambda))^{1/2}$, and $h(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial A)$. First use a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity $\lambda$ on $A$. Fix $k$. Parametrize a test radius $r=r(z)$ by
$$ \lambda\pi r^2=\log\lambda+(2k-1)\log\log\lambda+z,\qquad z\in\mathbb R.$$
Thus $r/s\to1$. Set the deep target $I=\{h\ge5s\}\cap A$ and the shallow target $H=\{h\le2s\}\cap A$. Their $k$-coverage events at any two fixed test levels $z_I,z_H$ are independent for large $n$: they inspect disjoint neighborhoods. Threshold equalities can be handled by squeezing fixed levels; all finite limiting CDFs below are continuous.

For any fixed $0<\epsilon<1$ and fixed $z$, with probability $1-o(1)$ every point with $\epsilon s\le h\le 5s$ is $k$-covered at $r(z)$. Indeed its $s/\log\lambda$-net, chosen in the strip, has $O(s^{-1}(\log\lambda)^2)$ points by the area of the thickened strip and packing. At each net point the disk of radius $r-s/\log\lambda$ intersects $A$ in area at least $(1/2+c_\epsilon)\pi r^2$ for some $c_\epsilon>0$. This follows by the uniform tangent half-plane estimate: over a ball of radius $O(r)$ the height error of a $C^{1,1}$ boundary relative to its tangent is $O(r^2)$, and the disk center is at depth at least $\epsilon s$. Centers deeper than $r$ have no truncation. The Poisson probability of fewer than $k$ points is $O((\log\lambda)^{C_k}\lambda^{-1/2-c_\epsilon})$ with an inconsequential smaller $c_\epsilon$ if needed; a union bound proves the claim. It holds also for binomial samples by the same bound or by the intensity sandwich below.

The deep-target probability is computed by extending the Poisson process to all of the plane. For $r<2s$, nothing added outside $A$ can affect the deep event. In that full process the events for $I$ and $A$ differ with probability $o(1)$: all strip centers of depth at most $5s$ now see a full disk, so their net failure probability is $O(s^{-1}(\log\lambda)^{C_k}/\lambda)=o(1)$. The classical interior $k$-coverage law, in [Penrose–Yang, Proposition 2.11, (2.16)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2.SS3), applies with a smooth sampling disk strictly containing the compact target $A$ and with $|\partial A|_2=0$. Its parameter is $\lambda\pi r^2-\log\lambda-k\log\log\lambda$, up to $o(1)$, since multiplying the intensity by a fixed domain volume changes the iterated logarithm by $o(1)$. As a result the deep probability tends to $\exp(-a e^{-z})$ when $k=1$, and to $1$ when $k\ge2$. For the latter use monotonicity because the interior parameter is $z+(k-1)\log\log\lambda\to\infty$.

For a disk $D$ of area $a_D$ and perimeter $P_D$, the known full-coverage limit at that radius is
$$
 \exp(-\mathbf1_{k=1}a_D e^{-z}-c_{2,k}P_D e^{-z/2}),
 \qquad c_{2,k}=\frac{2^{1-k}}{(k-1)!\sqrt\pi}.
$$
This is [Penrose–Yang, Theorem 2.1, (2.4)–(2.5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2.SS2), equivalently the smooth planar case of [Penrose's earlier Euclidean coverage theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-022-01182-5). The disk's deep and shallow events are independent at these levels, and the omitted middle is covered with high probability. Dividing the full law by the positive deep limit just obtained yields $\exp(-c_{2,k}P_D e^{-z/2})$ for the disk's $h\le2s$ target. The same proof gives this limit when intensity, radius or strip width change by relative $O(s)$: such perturbations change the radius parameter by $O(s\log\lambda)=o(1)$ and the fixed strips may be enlarged or reduced by, say, $s/4$ using the middle-strip bound.

Here the assumed boundary of $A$ need not itself be $C^2$. We transfer this disk calculation for each component of $\partial A$ by arclength. Its compact $C^{1,1}$ boundary has finitely many disjoint embedded closed curve components, each with an injective normal tube of fixed width (the uniform tubular neighborhood theorem for compact $C^{1,1}$ hypersurfaces; equivalently the positive rolling radius used in Penrose, Lemma 4.1). Parameterize the inward collar by arclength $u$ modulo the component length, depth $v$, via $\gamma(u)+v n(u)$ with the inward normal. The area element is $(1+O(s))du\,dv$ on $0\le v\le5s$, by the area formula and the a.e. bounded curvature; $v=h$. On corresponding collars of a disk with this circumference, map using the same $(u,v)$. For distances $O(s)$ between points this identifies the two Euclidean metrics with relative error $O(s)$. In detail, a small chord in a fixed tube has $|\Delta u|=O(s)$ for points of depths $O(s)$: the local arclength coordinate is Lipschitz there, distinct remote arcs being separated by compact embeddedness. With the short arclength difference, the chord equals $\gamma'(u)\Delta u+n(u)\Delta v+O((|\Delta u|+s)|\Delta u|)$ by Lipschitzness of the tangent and normal. Apply the same estimate on the circle; it works in either direction and for an inward normal on any component, including a component bounding a hole of $A$. Points outside the $5s$ tube cannot cover a center of depth $\le2s$ at $r<2s$. Thus a coupling between homogeneous comparison intensities $\lambda(1\pm Cs)$ on corresponding disk collars, and comparison radii $r(1\pm Cs)$, sandwiches the true shallow probability. Its limit on this component is the disk's. Different component collars are disjoint for large $n$, so multiplication proves $\exp(-c_{2,k}P e^{-z/2})$ for $H$.

Under fixed sample size $n$ the same joint limits follow from the Poisson counts of means $n\pm n^{2/3}$, using the *same* target strips and two test radii. Each joint coverage event is monotone in the point set; the geometric and parameter errors are $o(1)$ since $O(n^{-1/3}\log n)=o(1)$. If $k=1$ this proves joint convergence of the separately centered radius-squares on $I,H$ to independent $Z_I,Z_H$ with CDFs $\exp(-a e^{-z})$ and $\exp(-P e^{-z/2}/\sqrt\pi)$ respectively. Their difference has no atom at zero. The interior wins with probability tending to
$$
 \mathbb P(Z_I>Z_H)
 =\int e^{-P e^{-z/2}/\sqrt\pi}\,d\exp(-a e^{-z})
 =\int_0^\infty e^{-t-b\sqrt t}\,dt=p.
$$
For $k\ge2$ the deep centered maximum tends to $-\infty$ in probability while the shallow one has the displayed proper limit; the shallow region wins with probability tending to one. In both cases, choose a fixed low level $-T$ at which the probability the full maximum is smaller than $r(-T)$ is arbitrarily small; the same estimates exclude a winning center in $\epsilon s\le h\le5s$. Let $T\to\infty$ after $n\to\infty$. Thus the full radius has $R_{n,k}/s\to1$ in probability; asymptotically the winning ball is either contained in $A$ (deep winner) or centered at $h=o_{\mathbb P}(s)$ on the shallow-winning events. Write $W_n$ for the lexicographically first maximizing center and $Y_n=|B(W_n,R_{n,k})\cap A|/a$. The tangent half-plane approximation shows that the shallow winner has relative disk area tending in probability to $1/2$ on those events; e.g. bound the area discrepancy by $O(h/s+s)$ when $R/s$ lies in $[1/2,2]$. On the deep events the fraction tends to one. Consequently $nY_n/\log n$ converges in distribution to the variable taking value one with probability $p$ for $k=1$ (zero probability for $k\ge2$), and value $1/2$ otherwise. The full radius laws at successive multiplicities have centerings differing by $2\log\log\lambda$, so for $k\ge2$, $\mathbb P(R_{n,k-1}<R_{n,k})\to1$.

## From a one-time winner to records in a single nested sequence

All samples now come from a single iid sequence. Drop $k$ from $R_{j,k}$. Define $Y'_j=Y_j$ if the open maximizing disk contains exactly $k-1$ sample points, and zero otherwise. For $k=1$ these variables agree; for $k\ge2$ they agree whenever $R_{j,k-1}<R_{j,k}$. Let $I_{j+1}=\mathbf1\{R_{j+1}<R_j\}$, and $Q_j=\mathbb E[I_{j+1}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_j]$. At a unique maximizing center the new point must land inside its open disk and raise its interior count to $k$ to force a strict decrease of the global maximum. Indeed the updated continuous distance function is no larger anywhere and is then strictly smaller at the unique old maximizer, so its maximum on compact $A$ is smaller. The equality-sphere event has probability zero. Thus $Q_j=Y'_j$ at uniqueness, and always $0\le Q_j\le Y_j$.

We use two prior results of [Penrose, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19104v3): Proposition 5.2 gives uniqueness almost surely whenever $R_j$ is sufficiently small, and §4 (4.4), Lemma 4.10 gives $j\pi R_j^2/(a\log j)\to1$ almost surely in the uniform planar case and uniform integrability of that normalized area (indeed an exponential moment bound). These statements do not exclude the tied leading exponents in the present case. Hence $Q_j=Y'_j$ eventually almost surely. The distributional conclusion and uniform integrability imply $\mathbb E[jY'_j/\log j]\to c_k$, and the same holds with $Q_j$ by asymptotic uniqueness and domination by $jY_j/\log j$.

It remains to average a switching winner almost surely. Fix $j\ge\max(3,k)$, $a_j=\log j/j$, and define $H_j=\min(2,Y'_j/a_j)$. Eventually $H_j=Y'_j/a_j$ almost surely by the cited strong law. The bounded $H_j$ have expectations tending to $c_k$ by the one-time distributional result. For $i\le j/2$ large enough that $j-i\ge k$, form $H_j^{(i)}$ from $X_{i+1},\ldots,X_j$ only, using the **same divisor** $a_j$ and cutoff $2$. If no removed point falls in the chosen maximizing disk of the remaining sample, its radius and chosen center are unchanged on adding the first $i$ points: at the center its $k$th distance is unchanged and all other distances are nonincreasing, so even lexicographic tie-breaking still selects that center. The disk's interior count is also unchanged (conditionally null equality spheres). The remaining sample is independent of the deleted points and has law of a $(j-i)$-sample. Therefore
$$
 \mathbb P(H_j^{(i)}\ne H_j)\le i\,\mathbb E Y_{j-i}
 \le C i\log j/j.
$$
Since $H_i$ and $H_j^{(i)}$ are independent and all these variables lie in $[0,2]$,
$$|\operatorname{Cov}(H_i,H_j)|\le C\min(1,i\log j/j)\quad(i\le j/2);$$
use the trivial bound otherwise. This step controls dependence *across times*, not just independence of the two spatial competitors at one time.

For $S_n=\sum_{j\le n}a_jH_j$ (omitting the irrelevant first few indices) this yields $\operatorname{Var}S_n=O((\log n)^3\log\log n)$: for $i\le j/(\log j)^2$, bound the covariance by $C/\log j$ and use $\sum_{i\le j}a_i=O((\log j)^2)$; for larger $i<j$ the weight sum is $O(\log j\log\log j)$. Then sum in $j$ with weight $a_j$; the diagonal is bounded. Also $\mathbb E S_n\sim(c_k/2)(\log n)^2$. Chebyshev and Borel–Cantelli along $n_l=\lfloor e^{l^2}\rfloor$ give the same asymptotic almost surely, since $\sum_l\log l/l^2<\infty$. Interpolate by positivity of the summands and $\log n_{l+1}/\log n_l\to1$. Eventual unclipping and uniqueness imply
$$\sum_{j<n}Q_j\sim(c_k/2)(\log n)^2\qquad\text{almost surely}.$$
Finally $I_{j+1}-Q_j$ are martingale differences with expected conditional variance at most $\mathbb E Q_j=O(a_j)$. Since $\sum_j a_j/(\log j)^4<\infty$, the martingale series divided termwise by $(\log j)^2$ converges almost surely, and Kronecker's lemma makes the unweighted martingale sum $o((\log n)^2)$ almost surely. Taking expectations directly uses $\mathbb E Q_j\sim c_k a_j$ and $\sum_{j<n}a_j\sim(\log n)^2/2$. The record-time conclusion follows by monotone inversion of the almost sure count asymptotic. No conclusion here is asserted for nonuniform densities at tied exponents, polyhedral corners, or a target properly touching the support boundary.
