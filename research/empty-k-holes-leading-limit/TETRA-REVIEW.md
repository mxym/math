# Independent internal review: the tetrahedron evaluation

Pass on frozen TETRAHEDRON.md SHA-256 `820532191ae0b851d00233b58a624eaa4fe00a5c2d1005e5d8cb02fb2d56c205` (exact.py `68891861675c44ffa8bf3ed3f96b904541ffde875781185081961ee02f29cf25`). This evaluates an existing section-integral formula; the result is not a new proof of the simplex-size existence theorem.

Reitzner–Temesvari Theorem 1.4 and §3.1 use invariant hyperplane measure normalized to mass 2 on planes meeting the unit ball. Pushing forward (4π)^(−1)dσ dt on unit normals and signed heights has that normalization despite its two-to-one parameterization. For T of volume V=1/6 the section area equals V times the projected density; the resulting integral is V/4 times the sphere integral of f⁴ integrated in height. Since this functional has ray-degree −3, the ambient radial integral over 1≤height-range≤e matches the sphere integral. Affine vertex permutations preserve volume with determinant modulus one and permute the height orders up to a common translation; therefore all 24 chambers coincide, even for a nonregular reference tetrahedron. Setting w=(ra,rb,r) gives dr/r and a total outside factor 24V/4=1.

The first density spline piece follows from shrinking a simplex at the minimum-height vertex, and the last follows on reversing height. Each contributes 1/2 to the fourth-moment integral. The middle quadratic is indeed given by the two endpoint values and the left slope, giving coefficients x,1,y in the quadratic Bernstein basis. Direct differentiation of b=(1−y)/(1−xy), a=xb gives the Jacobian (1−x)(1−y)/(1−xy)^3, multiplied by b−a=(1−x)(1−y)/(1−xy). Tonelli applies to the multinomial and nonnegative (1−xy)^(-4) coefficients. The independent SymPy implementation in check_tetra_integral_independent.py computes all 15 kernels separately and sums harmonic contributions as exact rational numbers; it returns rational component 9/4 and zeta(2) coefficient 18/35. This agrees with the frozen standard-library verifier. Historical source scope: the earlier EuroCG 2021 Corollary 2.3 wording gave 3 as tight for a tetrahedron, superseded on this point by detailed full arXiv:2111.12533v2, pp. 4 and 6 (range 3.095–3.097 and explicit open Problem 1). The exact value lies in the later range. No formula for other bodies or independence from shape asserted.
