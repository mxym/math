- **LR0 Complete target.** There exist absolute constants C>=1 and k>=1 such that, for every pair of positive finite sizes m,n and every total Boolean matrix M in {0,1}^{m-by-n}, D(M)<=C[log_2(rank_R(M)+1)]^k. D is worst-case deterministic two-party interactive bit communication, with a publicly labeled output at each protocol leaf. All entries are defined, with no input promise.
- **LR1 Low-rank normalization.** The zero matrix has rank0 and D0. For rank1 the logarithm in LR0 equals1 and the absolute factor covers finite costs. This is the usual asymptotic conjecture made precise; refuting a literal unnormalized log(1)=0 formula is not a resolution. Sign matrices 2M-J have ranks differing by at most1, so the customary sign/Boolean formulations agree after this normalization.
- **LR2 Positive acceptance.** A complete ordinary proof must supply uniform C,k and a protocol or fully proved equivalent theorem for every finite total Boolean matrix. Randomized/quantum methods may be inputs only with a complete proved bridge back to LR0.
- **LR3 Negative acceptance.** A complete refutation needs a rigorously specified infinite family of actual total Boolean matrices and worst-case deterministic lower bounds growing faster than every fixed power of log(rank+1), with rank unbounded. One finite violation, a failed chosen exponent, arbitrary real matrices or an unproved rank/communication claim is inadmissible.
- **LR4 Rectangle formulation.** It is equivalent to uniformly finding, in every rank-r Boolean matrix, a monochromatic rectangle of relative area at least2^[-C log_2(r+1)^k]. A protocol gives a large leaf; the known Nisan-Wigderson whole-domain dyadic reduction gives the converse. Arbitrary monochromatic rectangle partitions are not automatically protocols of depth log(partition count); use the established simulation bridge.
- **LR5 Partition domain.** Let p(M) be the minimum number of disjoint all-one rectangles partitioning the ones; let chi_part(M) partition all entries monochromatically. Uniform quasipolynomial bounds on p for all Boolean matrices also cover J-M (rank<=r+1), hence chi_part<=p(M)+p(J-M), and the known Aho-Ullman-Yannakakis quadratic-log simulation yields LR0. These two partition numbers must not be identified.

Hambardzumyan-Lovett-Shirley2510.02583v3, Theorem1.2, proves q<=O(r log(r+1)), with finite ranks normalized. Their Corollary1.4 says quasipolynomial q-to-p conversion for all Boolean matrices is equivalent to log-rank. This theorem, and its proof read as a prior input, are not a new result here.
