# Uniform-in-precision finite-state bounds for the exact cubic distribution

Let C_n be the unconstrained optimum expected input-bit count for the first n binary output digits of a real with F(x)=x^3 on [0,1]. With M original states (output strings on edges need not have unit length), let e(G)=sup_n(E T_n-C_n) for an exact generator. Then
$$e(G)\ge 2^{-4M-5},\qquad \inf_{|G|\le M}e(G)=O(M^{-1/6}).$$
Equivalently, a uniform additive error epsilon requires Omega(log(1/epsilon)) states and O(epsilon^{-6}) states suffice. These are not matching bounds on the number of states. This is cumulative expected cost versus the optimum at each precision, not per-symbol loss, and not distributional approximation.

## Lower bound, with multi-letter edges counted in the original M

For any path of c coin edges printing n letters, its cylinder probability 2^{-c} cannot exceed 3·2^{-n}, so c-n>=-1. For each reachable original state v define d(v) as the minimum such difference; d(start)=0 since negative cycles are impossible, and -1<=d(v)<=M-1 by a simple access path. The nonnegative reweighted edge increments are 1 minus output length plus d(source)-d(target). A finite word seen at n outputs after n-1 coins must be printed exactly with no overshoot, d(end)=-1, and every edge tight (zero increment). Conversely any tight path to a vertex with d=-1 prints n letters after n-1 coins. It is a first hit there: hitting length n earlier would violate the same bound. Call these words early. Every early bin has average density at least 2; its upper endpoint is therefore at least alpha=sqrt(2/3).

Keep the tight subgraph reachable from the start and coaccessible to a state of d=-1. Every original graph cycle in this subgraph emits exactly as many letters as it uses coins; in particular there is no empty-output cycle. Every infinite walk in it prints infinitely many letters. Its infinite output real is >=alpha: each initial walk segment may be extended in the subgraph to end at d=-1, giving arbitrarily long early words sharing its prefixes.

Suppose that for a given n>=2M+1 the word of bin k=ceil(2^n alpha) is early (we will use n=4M+6, for which k<2^n). Its left endpoint is at most alpha+2^{-n} and its mean density is >=2. Its witnessing walk has n-1 edges. After its first n-1-M edges at least n-2M letters have been printed, using d<=M-1 and tightness. The last M edges visit some state twice; the cycle can be repeated forever. Thus the graph has an infinite walk sharing at least n-2M bits with the witnessing word. The least possible infinite output real beta lies in
$$\alpha\le\beta\le\alpha+2^{-n}+2^{2M-n}.$$
Moreover beta is rational of denominator at most 2^{M+1}. To see this without determinizing the graph, keep only vertices with infinite continuations and at each such vertex take the lexicographically least infinite output word. It exists by compactness of the space of edge walks and continuity of output (empty-edge runs bounded). Select one outgoing viable edge attaining this minimal continuation, breaking ties consistently. Repeatedly selecting these edges yields the minimal word since output is infinite and the same lexicographic concatenation rule holds after every edge. The chosen path from the start is a lasso with prefix and period together at most M original edges. By tightness and d>=-1 their total output length is at most M+1, and the cycle's output length is positive. The binary value of a lasso with these lengths has denominator at most 2^{M+1}. Writing beta=P/Q, the irrationality of alpha gives
$$\beta-\alpha=\frac{|3P^2-2Q^2|}{3Q^2(\beta+\alpha)}\ge\frac1{6\,2^{2M+2}}.$$
For n=4M+6 this contradicts the preceding upper bound (indeed 2^{-n}+2^{2M-n}<=2^{2M-n+1}). Thus this qualifying bin is not early. At input length n-1 its attainable-string count is 0 whereas the canonical count floor(2^{n-1} p_u) is at least 1. The tail-sum deficit gives E T_n-C_n>=2^{1-n}.

## Upper bound: locking stable prefix atoms

Fix an integer N>=2, h=2^{-N}, and a dyadic mesh g=2^{-floor(N/2)}, so sqrt(h)<=g<=2sqrt(h). Use an exact coin tree simultaneously optimal at all precisions up to N, with maximum depth at most 3N. For completeness this finite tree is obtained recursively: if a prefix mass has one atom per set bit, pool the canonical atoms of its two children. At each dyadic weight, working from smallest to largest, group tokens into pairs and carry each pair to the next weight, retaining one token when the count is odd. The unpaired tokens are the canonical atoms of the parent. Within each such parent atom the pairings are decoded by fair-coin bifurcations until a raw child token is reached and its label emitted. No fractional masses below 2^{-3N} occur in these finite refinements because the bin masses are differences of cubes of dyadic endpoints. No earlier hit can require more than 3N coins. Deterministic child labels requiring no coin are attached to the preceding edge (or initial forced prefix if present; for the cubic there is none). The finite stopping tree therefore uses O(2^{3N}) states.

A level-N bin [kh,(k+1)h] is good if its open interval of density values (3k²h², 3(k+1)²h²) contains no multiple of g. In a good bin choose a>=0 with [3k²h²,3(k+1)²h²] contained in [a,a+g], a a multiple of g. Otherwise put a=0 in that bin. In the first case the mean density is strictly between the mesh endpoints. Therefore the canonical input atoms of the bin of total mass h a are the high bits, and we declare these existing first-hit atoms locked; on each, copy uniform fair bits for the entire suffix. In a bad bin there are no locks. At every descendant of suffix length m the inherited locked atoms are canonical bits of its own probability: their sum is 2^{-m} h a (a multiple of 2^{-m}h g) and the remainder is less than 2^{-m}h g in a good bin. Write R_u=p_u-ha for the remaining mass of the level-N bin. In good bins R_u<hg. The number of bad bins is at most 3/g by strict monotonicity and disjoint interiors of their density ranges. Their individual mass is at most 3h. Hence
$$\sum_{|u|=N}R_u\le g+9h/g.$$

On each of the remaining canonical input leaves of bin u, generate the *same* normalized residual density on its suffix y in [0,1], proportional to
$$3(kh+hy)^2-a=3k^2h^2-a +6kh^2 y +3h^2 y^2.$$
This is a convex mixture of densities 1,2y,3y², since the coefficients are nonnegative and the residual is positive. Its normalized density is at most 3 and replacing the residual leaves in this way preserves the cubic law exactly.

Select the mixture using two rational cumulative thresholds on a fair uniform binary stream (continue until its dyadic cylinder lies in one of the mixture parts). At depth i the unresolved mass is at most min(1,2·2^{-i}); the expected number of coin bits is at most 3. The three unnormalized weights in units h² are 3k²-a/h², 3k and 1; these are integers of sum at most O(4^N). With a common denominator D=O(4^N) the rational interval test has O(D+1) states: before a decision retain the endpoint(s) in the cell and their transformed numerator(s) modulo D after successive doublings. For a single threshold there are at most D residues; for as long as both thresholds remain in the same cell, there are at most O(1+log D) levels when they are distinct, because their separation is at least 1/D. Endpoint zeros are assigned by half-open conventions; null infinite binary ties do not matter. A one-point interval or coincident thresholds can be omitted.

The elementary primitive for i=1,2,3 is the maximum of i independent uniform binary reals, compared one digit at a time retaining only contenders at the greatest prefix. This is finite-state: i<=3 and only the current contenders and the current digit comparisons are stored. To return m digits the baseline cost is m. All extra coin flips at digit j+1 are bounded by the number of equal pairs of length-j prefixes in the original sample (if k survive, k-1<=binom(k,2)). Their mean is at most 3·2^{-j}. Thus the entire residual continuation uses at most m+9 bits in expectation, uniformly in the bin and m.

For clarity compare to the discrete optimum, not merely n or entropy. For any scalar R>0 its canonical dyadic atoms of masses 2^{-j} have mean depth conditional on this scalar of at most log₂(1/R)+3: if J is the first set bit, then J<=log₂(1/R)+1 and the contribution beyond J is at most sum_{k>=1} k2^{-k}=2, since 2^{-J}<=R. At level N the unfinished atoms for each bin are exactly the canonical atoms of R_u. Consequently the expected cost from the respective residual events, *including* receipt of the N-prefix, is at most R_u(log₂(1/R_u)+m+12). At precision N+m the optimal tree has the already described locked bits for each descendant, with exactly their realized costs; its remaining bits for each descendant label are the canonical atoms of R_u p_v, where p_v denotes its residual conditional suffix-bin probability. Every such atom has depth at least log₂(1/(R_u p_v)). Averaging gives at least R_u(log₂(1/R_u)+H(p)). Since the residual density is <=3, H(p)>=m-log₂ 3. The difference between actual and optimum at any m>=0 is at most (12+log₂ 3) sum R_u; at earlier precisions it is zero. There are at most 2^N different residual mixture tests, each of O(4^N) states, sharing the finite primitive generators, in addition to the initial O(2^{3N}) tree. Hence O(2^{3N}) states attain O(2^{-N/2}) uniform additive error, as asserted.
