# Problem formulation and prior work

1. Thomas L. Draper and Feras A. Saad, [*Space-Entropy Lower Bounds for Random Sampling*, July 2026, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14503v1), [§9, “Continuous Distributions,” around (63)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14503v1#page=18). They distinguish three questions attributed to Knuth and Yao, p. 427: which laws have an optimal finite generator (iii); the state requirement to approximate the optimal cost for the cubic CDF (iv); and mere existence of a finite generator (v). They explicitly distinguish a bound on the additive excess at every output precision from a bound growing linearly in the number of outputs. Our benchmark is defined directly: $C_n$ is the fair-coin tree optimum for the discrete vector of $n$ emitted bits, and the same finite graph is tested against all $C_n$. We give a smooth-class characterization and nonmatching cubic bounds, not the unrestricted (iii) classification or an exact asymptotic for (iv).
2. Donald E. Knuth and Andrew C. Yao, *The Complexity of Nonuniform Random Number Generation*, Academic Press, 1976, pp. 357–428; [original volume catalogue](https://books.google.com/books/about/Algorithms_and_Complexity.html?id=SNEmAAAAMAAJ), [Knuth’s reprint](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/aa.html) and [author’s addendum](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/aaerr1.html). The addendum credits the solution of question (v). The bibliographic and archival records and later primary restatements were checked; a public full text of the original printed p. 427 was not independently verified. Our account of (iii)/(iv) is explicitly the July 2026 restatement, not a transcription of that original page. The optimal discrete distribution-generating tree, binary expansion leaf-depth characterization and use of order statistics are classical; see also [Saad et al., POPL 2020, Theorem 2.9](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rinard/paper/popl20.sampling.pdf) and [Devroye, Chapter XV](https://luc.devroye.org/chapter_fifteen_1.pdf).
3. Andrew C. Yao, [*Context-Free Grammars and Random Number Generation*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-82456-2_25) (1985); Guy Kindler and Dan Romik, [*On Distributions Computable by Random Walks on Graphs*, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 17 (2004), 624–633](https://doi.org/10.1137/S089548010343106X), [full manuscript, §1 and Theorems 1–2](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=bcf4ec7522b9dd21772ff826f528717a8becbed0&repid=rep1&type=pdf). Their model permits arbitrary finite strings on coin-selected edges. Yao obtained necessary conditions; Kindler and Romik proved sufficiency for polynomials and polynomiality under smoothness. In particular, for a normalized polynomial CDF, rational coefficients and absence of irrational derivative zeros in [0,1] characterize **ordinary** finite generation. Their result supplies a necessity here, not the precision-wise optimality obstruction. Exact generation of the cubic by order statistics of uniforms is not new.
4. B. B. Pokhodzeĭ, [Russian journal record and abstract, 1984](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=znsl&option_lang=rus&paperid=4790&wshow=paper); [English translation, “Complexity and asymptotically optimal algorithms for modeling of absolutely continuous distributions”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01095642), 1986. The journal abstracts identify an intermediate distribution-generating model for absolutely continuous laws. They do not state our classification or these bounds for the finite graph against every exact discrete optimum. We verified the Russian and English abstracts, not the full article, so cannot rule out partially overlapping results there. Vladimir Nekrutkin, [*Binary decompositions of probability densities and random-bit simulation*](https://doi.org/10.1515/mcma-2020-2063) (2020): its abstract explicitly describes selecting principal bits from a discrete distribution and continuing with unbiased bits. That broad construction is prior. Our cubic construction proves that selected dyadic masses retain their optimal bit positions in *every* refinement and bounds the cost and number of states for the remainder. We do not infer nonoverlap of every technical detail solely from an abstract.
5. Luc Devroye and Claude Gravel, [*Random variate generation using only finitely many unbiased, independently and identically distributed random bits*, v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02539v6), use a Wasserstein accuracy criterion for a finite-precision approximation of a continuous sample. That criterion is not a state-size bound for one machine emitting an infinite stream with the exact law. The [2026 SODA paper on online recycling](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611978971.89) and the main discrete results in the July paper concern sequences of target draws, also a different criterion.
