# Smooth distributions with prefix-optimal finite-state fair-bit generators

Use the Knuth--Yao finite directed generator convention: at each state a fair input bit selects one of two (possibly parallel) edges; an edge emits a finite, possibly empty, binary word. The start is fixed, and output is infinite almost surely. Let T_n be the number of fair bits used when the nth output bit is first available, including when one edge emits several bits. Prefix-optimal means E T_n is the unconstrained minimum for the discrete law of the n-bit prefix, for **every** n. This compares cumulative consumption to the true random-bit optimum, not to Shannon entropy alone and not just in rate.

**Theorem.** Among smooth distribution functions on [0,1] with F(0)=0,F(1)=1, the ones with a prefix-optimal finite generator have density
$$f(x)=1-r+2rx,\qquad -1\le r\le1,$$
where either r=0 or |r|=2^{-s} for some integer s>=0. No assertion about all nonsmooth distributions is made.

## 1. A finite-language obstruction

Write I_u for a binary cylinder, h=2^{-|u|}, p_u=P(I_u). In any coin tree stopped at output length n, the number N_k(u) of input strings of length k which have already returned u is at most floor(2^k p_u). Optimality is equality for every k,u: this is the Knuth--Yao criterion. Indeed summing deficits times 2^{-k} gives exactly the excess expected input length, by the tail-sum formula and the existence of the binary-expansion tree for the fixed distribution. Equivalently its first-hit leaves for u have one length for each set bit in the terminating-preferred binary expansion of p_u, no others.

**Lemma.** If the output density is bounded and the generator finite, then for fixed integers l and j>=1 the language of words u with |u|+l>=0 and N_{|u|+l}(u)>=j is regular.

Expand output words into individual output-letter edges at cost zero after charging the one input bit on the initial edge of the chain (an original empty-output edge costs one and emits nothing). Intermediate states are private to the original edge. This finite multigraph records distinct original input choices by edge multiplicity. For every path from the initial state up to any intermediate or ordinary vertex, with c coins consumed and n letters emitted, 2^{-c}<=p_u<=||f||_infinity 2^{-n}: completing the as-yet-unprinted suffix of an edge uses no further coin. Thus its integer weight c-n is bounded below by a fixed integer -D. Remove unreachable vertices. Each vertex v has a shortest path weight d(v), and d(start)=0 (no negative cycle can be reachable). Reweight an edge v->z of weight w as w+d(v)-d(z)>=0. For first-hit paths ending exactly on an output edge with last output the final letter of u and c<=|u|+l the total reweighted cost is c-|u|-d(z)<=l+D; retain a finite counter for these nonnegative slack costs. The requirement that the last edge is an output edge is a one-bit flag; in particular no arbitrary empty suffix after the first hit is counted. Each such accepting path of final slack a contributes exactly 2^{l-d(z)-a} possible length-(|u|+l) input strings (free unused padding), provided the exponent is nonnegative. Different first-hit paths correspond to disjoint input cylinders. Their weighted count is therefore precisely N_{|u|+l}(u). With the counter bounded, there is no cycle of empty labels: every cycle of only empty labels consumes positive bits. Thus epsilon-path sums are finite; the usual finite forward vector of path counts, capping additions and multiplications at j, including empty closures and the final bounded weights, recognizes the desired threshold. The empty word can be treated separately. This proves the lemma.

An endpoint consequence suffices. Suppose f is continuous and strictly increasing on a closed dyadic interval J, and f(x_0)=t=j/2^l in its interior. Intersect the lemma's language with words extending the prefix of J. For an optimal generator it accepts precisely the bins whose average density is >=t. Nested prefixes of arbitrarily long accepted words have infinite limit rays; their corresponding real numbers have infimum x_0. Indeed below x_0 bounded away from it the test eventually fails, and every interior point above x_0 is a limit. For a regular language the set of such rays is given by infinite paths in a finite deterministic automaton restricted to states with accepting completions of arbitrarily large length. On this restriction follow the lexicographically smaller viable outgoing letter at every step. The resulting least ray is eventually periodic. Its binary real value, including standard binary endpoint ambiguity, is rational. The same argument with a greatest ray treats strictly decreasing f. Consequently such a dyadic threshold cannot have an irrational regular crossing.

Kindler--Romik's two theorems (SIAM J. Discrete Math. 17 (2004), 624--633) imply that a smooth CDF with any finite generator is a polynomial with rational coefficients. If f had degree d>=2, with leading coefficient a_d, take a strictly monotone interval on which f is positive and has nonzero derivative, and within it a closed dyadic subinterval J. The interior of its image contains positive t=m/2^k with m odd and k arbitrarily large avoiding the residue -v_2(a_d) modulo d. For an x in the rationals with v_2(x) sufficiently negative, the uniquely smallest valuation term of f(x) is a_d x^d; hence v_2(f(x))=v_2(a_d)+d v_2(x). For all other rational x, the valuations of every nonzero term have a common lower bound, including x=0 separately. For the chosen sufficiently large k neither case permits f(x)=t. This contradicts the preceding crossing test. Thus f is affine, normalized as in the theorem with rational r and |r|<=1. Reflection interchanges r and -r while complementing output bits, so assume r>0.

## 2. Which affine slopes are possible?

Let t be an interior positive dyadic level in the range of f and x_0=(t-1+r)/(2r). If x_0 is rational but not dyadic, infinitely many n have 0<{2^n x_0}<1/2: after a finite initial segment its binary expansion is periodic, nonconstant, and has zeros. For the bin with k=floor(2^n x_0), its average density is t+eta_n, with eta_n>0 tending to zero, but its density near the left endpoint is strictly less than t.

Write t as a finite sum of distinct powers of two. For all sufficiently large n in this subsequence the binary expansion of h(t+eta_n) contains every shifted bit from h t; the remaining mass is h eta_n. At the first n-output hit, optimality gives one input leaf for each shifted bit. Conditional on each such leaf the subsequent binary real inside this bin has one of finitely many laws: its pending suffix in the final edge is determined, then the walk starts afresh at the edge's terminal vertex. Select a subsequence on which these finitely many chosen leaf types, indexed by the finitely many bits of t, are fixed. Divide their subprobabilities by h. Their mixture is a fixed measure of mass t. The remaining measure has mass eta_n, and the full rescaled density y -> f((k+y)h) converges uniformly to the constant t on [0,1]. Thus the fixed mixture is exactly t times uniform measure, which is impossible because the true rescaled density is strictly less than t on a nonempty interval adjoining 0. Therefore every interior dyadic value has a dyadic preimage. Taking two consecutive interior levels spaced by 2^{-s} for sufficiently large s yields that 1/(2r) is dyadic. There is a second, independent obstruction.


**Dyadic first-hit obstruction.** If a positive-probability word u has nondyadic mass, its optimal first-hit input tree has a leaf at each set bit of that mass and hence has arbitrarily deep leaves. There are only finitely many suffix laws conditional on such a leaf, indexed by state and pending output; choose a type occurring at unbounded depths. Its law is absolutely continuous relative to Lebesgue on the rescaled bin, since even a single occurrence of the type has positive probability and the overall restriction of the target is absolutely continuous. If the type gives a continuation cylinder v positive probability, then the first-hit input length for uv has unbounded support on outcomes emitting uv: for each of those leaves of length J there is positive probability of uv and T_{|uv|}>=T_{|u|}=J. This is impossible if p_{uv} is dyadic: its optimal tree gives that label only at its finite list of set bits. Thus there would have to be positive Lebesgue mass of continuations with no dyadic-mass extension at any positive depth (take the countable union of exceptions).

Write r=A/B in lowest terms, D the odd part of B. For an n-bit word of value k the cylinder mass is
$$p_{n,k}=\frac{(B-A)2^n+2Ak+A}{B4^n}.$$
If D>1, p_{1,0}=1/2-r/4 is nondyadic. But a depth-n bin has dyadic mass exactly when its numerator vanishes modulo D. Since 2A is invertible modulo D this holds in one residue class in k. Fix h with 2^h>=D. From every current prefix, at least one of its 2^h consecutive extensions by h bits has dyadic mass. Under uniform Lebesgue suffix bits, the probability of missing dyadic targets for J successive blocks is at most (1-2^{-h})^J. This contradicts the obstruction at u=0. Hence r is dyadic. Together with 1/(2r) dyadic, its positive reduced numerator and denominator are both powers of two, giving r=2^{-s}, s>=0.

## 3. Explicit optimal generators for the surviving laws

The uniform case copies the bits. Write r=2^{-s}>0. Below any n-bit prefix u with n>=1, let w=r4^{-n}. Its mass p_u is w times the odd positive integer (2^s-1)2^n+2k+1. Assign a uniform suffix to every set-bit atom of p_u *except* the smallest one, of mass w. Assign to the latter a triangular suffix of density 2y on [0,1]. These sublaws yield the correct restriction of f, because the unnormalized rescaled density is precisely (p_u-w)+2wy.

Implement the first output bit by any optimal finite coin tree for its dyadic two-point distribution. For each label, its unique smallest atom is of mass r/4, and is assigned triangular status; all others are assigned uniform status. For the triangular state, on coin 1 emit 1 and become uniform. On coin 0 read one additional coin i, emit i, and stay triangular. These rules give left child mass w/4, triangular, and right child masses w/2 (uniform) and w/4 (triangular). Each inherited uniform atom contributes its half to either child, of mass at least w, at distinct bit positions. There are no carries or duplicates; the unique smallest child atom has mass w/4. Induction proves both exactness and the optimal first-hit leaf criterion at every n. This is a finite graph; its fixed initial tree has depth at most s+2. Complement all output letters for negative r.

**Scope.** These are exact expected optimums at all precisions, not asymptotic entropy rates. The result does not classify nonsmooth optimal laws, and alone gives no matching state-size asymptotic for approximating the optimum at positive error.
