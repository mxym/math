# True-area sampling on ellipsoids: strict local Hall comparison in d≥3

Private analytic proof. For each d≥3 the sphere strictly locally maximizes the probability that three independent points from normalized hypersurface area on an ellipsoid form an acute Euclidean triangle, modulo translations and dilation. This does not assert global optimality, a claim for curves in d=2, or Hall's all-convex volume-sampling conjecture. In dimension three the exact quadratic deficit is (11/175) tr(H²)t² for shape stretch exp(tH/2), as independently derived in the earlier private note. The true area distribution differs from the affine pushforward of spherical area except for similarities.

Parametrize a near-sphere ellipsoid up to rotation, translation and scaling by exp(tH/2)S^(d−1), with H symmetric traceless and ||H||_F=1. The shape parameter t is real and small. Set M_t=exp(tH), h(x)=xᵀHx for unit x, and let F and f be the CDF and density of one projection of uniform spherical measure, so f(z)=C_d(1−z²)^((d−3)/2). Pulling normalized hypersurface area back to the sphere gives the exact weight proportional to ||exp(−tH/2)x||: the tangential Jacobian is det(A)||A^(−T)x|| for A=exp(tH/2). Consequently w_t(x)=1+t g(x)+t²k(x)+O(t³), uniformly, where g=−h/2, k is bounded, and both g and k have mean zero. All right or degenerate cases have probability zero by sections of spherical area.

For uniform X,Y write u=(X−Y)/||X−Y||, c=||X−Y||/2, s=√(1−c²), X=c u+s e and Y=−c u+s e, with e uniform on the transverse sphere. Rotational invariance implies c is independent of the uniform u and transverse e. The angle at X is nonobtuse exactly when Z·n_t≤z_t for n_t=M_tu/||M_tu|| and z_t=X·n_t. Acute probability equals three times this nonobtuse probability minus two, because the three obtuse events are disjoint. Let v=Hu−h(u)u. The direction derivative n'_0=v and u·n_t=1−||v||²t²/2+O(t³). Averaging the second-order expansion of F(z_t) over e leaves
  (||v||²/2)[−c f(c)+s² f′(c)/(d−1)] t².
This follows since transverse linear terms average zero and E(e·v)²=||v||²/(d−1). As (1−c²)f′(c)=−(d−3)c f(c), the metric-only quadratic contribution to the acute probability is
  − A (d−2) E[c f(c)],    A=3 E||v||²/(d−1)>0.
Spherical second/fourth moments give E||v||²=tr(H²)/(d+2).

For the weighting-only contribution freeze the Euclidean test. Any single g or k insertion vanishes by rotational invariance and its zero mean. For a=X·Y, the conditional second-degree harmonic identity gives
  E[g(X)g(Y)|a]=tr(H²) P_2(a)/(2d(d+2)),  P_2(a)=(d a²−1)/(d−1).
Indeed conditional mean of h(Y) given X,a is P_2(a)h(X), and E h(X)²=2 tr(H²)/(d(d+2)). The Euclidean conditional acute probability is 2F(c)+F(s)−2, from the two end-vertex caps at c and the remaining vertex criterion along X+Y at s. Since a has a symmetric law, only its symmetric part (3/2)(F(c)+F(s))−2 contributes. For a>0 the sum F(c)+F(s) is strictly decreasing in a: its derivative is (f(s)/s−f(c)/c)/4<0, because s>c and f(x)/x strictly decreases for x∈(0,1). P_2 is centered and increases in |a|. Their covariance is therefore nonpositive. Each of the three pair-weight terms has this sign. It is not necessary to evaluate their integral.

For the mixed metric-weighting term, a latitude at projection r in the direction n has conditional g average −P_2(r)h(n)/2; the first-order correction to the cap at z is therefore
  C_1(n,z)=h(n) z(1−z²) f(z)/(2(d−1)).
This follows by differentiating r(1−r²)^((d−1)/2). In the endpoint contribution (g(X)+g(Y)) f(c) z'_0, the first parenthesis is even in e and z'_0=s e·v is odd. The z′ derivative of C_1 is likewise odd. The n derivative of h(n_t) at zero equals 2||v||², so the net cross coefficient is
  + A E[c(1−c²) f(c)].
Since c∈(0,1) almost surely, metric plus cross is strictly negative for every d≥3; adding the weighting-only term cannot reverse that sign.

To justify uniform o(t²) in all unit traceless H, n_t and z_t are uniformly smooth. When d>3, the only potentially unbounded derivative factor in differentiating twice is f′(z_t)(z′_t)². Since n'_t⊥n_t and |z′_t|≤C√(1−z_t²), that term extends by zero continuously to |z_t|=1; f(z_t)z″_t does as well. At a contact point |z_t|=1 the increment of z_t is O(Δt²), and the increment of F is O(|Δt|^(d−1)), implying the same second derivative. The case d=3 has linear F on its entire support, directly. Thus metric Taylor remainder is uniform by compactness, including at extreme c or latitude. C_1 is continuously differentiable up to endpoints along the composed direction, as (1−z²) f(z) is a constant times (1−z²)^((d−1)/2). The cap integral from k is uniformly Lipschitz under changing n,z because cap symmetric difference lies in a strip whose spherical projection density is bounded for d≥3. The expansion of w_t has already a uniform third-order bound. Hence all second-order coefficient operations are legitimate. The strict negative gap in metric plus cross is independent of the normalized H by the stated moment identity, proving strict local maximum.
