# Sources, existing results and search limits

**What was observed, and what is proved here.** Berend, Ernst, Kontorovich and Kumar, [arXiv:2407.18688v1, §§5.2–5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18688v1#S5.SS3) and [*J. Theor. Probab.* 39, Article 58 (2026), §§5.2–5.3, Tables 2 and 3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10959-026-01487-4), record finite counts and suggest that exceptional sizes are rare. They list individual four-wise probabilities through $n=100$, and exactly nine six-wise sizes through $n=200$, all at $p=1/2$. They do not in that passage formulate a conjecture that these are the only possible six-wise probabilities in every size. Their numbered Conjecture 5.9 instead concerns rationality at each exceptional point. The present four-wise growth estimate makes the qualitative rarity observation precise for $k=4$ and allows every rational $p$ to recur. The six-wise theorem determines which *fixed* probabilities recur and gives the complete list of symmetric sizes.

**Closest recent paper.** [Hermann, arXiv:2609.25595v1, Theorem 1, Lemma 2 and Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25595v1), posted 22 September 2026, proves support reflection, uniqueness of the optimal count law and reflection of exceptional points about $1/2$. These are genuine inputs or neighboring results. Reflection at a fixed grid size does not determine which probabilities occur for infinitely many grid sizes.

**Classical inputs.** The extremal moment problem and paired supports appear in [Prékopa](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.36.1.145), [Peled–Yadin–Yehudayoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0059) and BEKK. [Infusino–Kuna–Lebowitz–Speer, §§6.1–6.2](https://cmsr.rutgers.edu/images/people/lebowitz_joel/publications/599.pdf) discuss low-order integer-lattice moment and Gaussian-node support tests. Integer zeros of Krawtchouk polynomials are an established subject: compare [Chihara–Stanton (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9045(90)90072-X) and [Habsieger–Stanton (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02988302). The quadratic Gaussian node formula in the four-wise test and the symmetric cubic nodes in the three-departure case are elementary classical polynomials, not new integer-zero results. Establishing a transition also requires actual neighboring phases; the two-departure six-wise case retains a nontrivial consecutive pair. The nonsymmetric finiteness proof includes such pairs and is not a claim about all zeros of arbitrary Krawtchouk polynomials.

**Earlier proofs in this repository.** [#220: four-wise phase classification](../k-wise-binomial-optimal-bases/FOUR.md), [rationality at $k=4,6$](../k-wise-binomial-optimal-bases/RATIONAL.md) and [binomial-to-Hermite localization](../k-wise-binomial-optimal-bases/SPREAD.md) supply the local test and bounded node offsets. In particular the four-wise iff test here is a reformulation and genuine-transition check, not the main new claim. [#223](../k-wise-binomial-exact-basis-moves/MOVES.md) identifies how many individual sites shift at a transition on a fixed grid. [#230](../k-wise-binomial-spread-monotonicity/) proves monotonicity of maximum spread with size. None of these gives the arithmetic recurrence contrast or the frequencies counted here.
