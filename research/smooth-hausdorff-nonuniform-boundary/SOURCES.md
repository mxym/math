# Primary sources and comparison (19 November 2026)

## Exact target and dependencies

* [Mathias Sonnleitner, arXiv:2508.16442v1, §1, Theorems 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16442v1#S1). The first theorem treats density proportional to the square root of Gaussian curvature. The next paragraph asks for a corresponding limit for other sampling densities. The second theorem gives the deterministic cubic remainder relating the Hausdorff error to half the squared covering radius in the second-fundamental-form metric; we use it directly. The [arXiv history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16442) lists the 22 August 2025 v1. [Glasauer–Schneider](https://doi.org/10.1515/form.1996.8.363) previously gave the first-order law for general positive smooth boundary densities (also reviewed in Sonnleitner §1); [Prochno–Schütt–Sonnleitner–Werner](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-025-03186-7) discuss expected errors and polygonal approximation. Neither a first-order rate nor the already optimal-density limit is a new conclusion here.
* [S. Janson, *Maximal spacings in several dimensions*, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176992269), gives the homogeneous iid empty-ball limit in a fixed Euclidean domain. [Janson, *Random coverings in several dimensions*, Theorem 1.2](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02399201), treats uniform centers for the manifold result. Both are prior. Our use of the former is at its ordinary critical scale, after repeating many independent cells and bounding holes crossing the artificial seams.
* [Aaron–Cholaquidis–Fraiman, Definition 1 and Theorems 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.2482): the nonuniform spacing multiplies the physical emptiness radius at a point by a local density factor. Theorem 2 proves a Gumbel limit for that **scaled** statistic under positive Hölder densities. Our global covering radius is in the original metric, not locally scaled; the distinction changes even the logarithmic centering. Their proof’s homogeneous block bounds and partitioning are relevant precedents. [Penrose, *Random Euclidean coverage from within*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-022-01182-5) and [Penrose–Yang, *Random coverage of a manifold with boundary*, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2) prove distributional results for uniform centers; the nonuniform generality in their statements is for strong laws. [Farhane](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASCFPA_1991__96_9_15_0/) studied almost-sure asymptotics for general multivariate spacings.
* [Hall (1984)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4149(84)90298-9) studied nonuniform random coverage on the circle. [Barbe (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-7152(92)90210-V) gives an explicit univariate maximum-spacing limit when the density has a positive minimum with a prescribed local power profile. No novelty is claimed for classical one-dimensional cases or the general Laplace method. [Hüsler–Glauser (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2818.1988.tb04686.x) discuss higher-dimensional nonuniform coverage on a cube with a unique interior zero of the density (publisher summary); our hypothesis explicitly excludes such zeros.
* [Bräker–Hsing–Bingham](https://doi.org/10.1239/aap/1035228070) studied planar **interior** random convex hulls. [Calka’s 2024 Bonn/HIM abstract and lecture](https://www.mathematics.uni-bonn.de/him/video-recordings/tp_2024_01_ws_1_video) announces higher-dimensional Hausdorff fluctuations under uniform **interior** sampling, joint with Yukich. Neither interior model should be conflated with this boundary problem.

## Current-work comparison

Historical and July–19 November 2026 searches covered the original author/title and citation, nonuniform geodesic coverage, unweighted maximum spacings, inhomogeneous empty balls, and Gumbel limits for inscribed random polytopes. The [recent Johnson–Mehl coverage paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10687-026-00529-8) studies variable random radii rather than this nonconstant sampling density problem. [Henze, 17 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23402) concerns the smallest ball **containing a spherical sample**, not the largest unoccupied geodesic ball or the Hausdorff distance to a smooth body's inscribed polytope. No exact positive-Hölder arbitrary-minimum-set boundary-hull theorem was located in this search. This does not exclude unindexed, inaccessible or unpublished work.
