# Hausdorff fluctuations for random boundary samples with a nonuniform density

Yongxian Zhang · AI-assisted research note · 19 November 2026

Choose independent points on a smooth, positively curved convex body’s boundary and take their convex hull. How much of the body does it miss at its worst-approximated point? The Hausdorff error has **Gumbel fluctuations for every strictly positive Hölder sampling density**, in every ambient dimension at least two. The centering is determined by a scalar integral of the density relative to the curvature metric. No assumption is needed about the number, shape or isolation of its minimum points.

This addresses the other-density question immediately after [Sonnleitner’s Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16442v1#S1), including the positive continuously differentiable densities discussed in his introduction. His optimal-density Gumbel theorem, his comparison to the geodesic covering radius, and the earlier first-order approximation law are prior results. Here the new step is a distributional limit for the **unweighted** covering radius with nonconstant density. It concerns boundary samples on a fixed body with $C^3$ boundary and positive Gaussian curvature, not volume samples, polytopes, vanishing densities or arbitrary continuous densities.

* [Proof and both normalizations](PROOF.md): a cell replication argument obtains the local rare-event precision from the classical homogeneous maximal-spacing theorem; a geometric net controls cells’ common faces.
* [Examples of the centering](CENTERING.md): a positive-volume set of minima and a smooth manifold of nondegenerate transverse minima. These are Laplace-method consequences, not separate novelty claims.
