# Original questions, existing results, and search limits

* Stefan Steinerberger, [*A sharp estimate for probability distributions*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spl.2019.108584), *Statistics & Probability Letters* **155** (2019), 108584; [open text, §1.3, pp. 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.00663). The two proposed inequalities are separate positive-threshold existence questions, one under median normalization and the other under mean normalization. A third sentence asks about unspecified replacement constants. The density-dependent estimate proved in §1.2 is a different inequality (and can be localized to thresholds at most the median). Our positive theorem allows thresholds up to twice the median and bounds a **ratio**, not the unconditional mixed event.
* The author's [*Some Open Problems*, §24, pp. 26–27, edition dated March 31, 2026](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf#page=26), explicitly numbers the median formulation as Question 1 and the mean formulation as Question 2. It separately discusses growth. Our lower bound uses the literal existential threshold quantifier of the displayed questions; it does not give a linear lower bound along unbounded thresholds.
* The *family* used for the counterexample is classical, with hazard $a+bt$. See, for example, Lee J. Bain, [*Analysis for the Linear Failure-Rate Life-Testing Distribution*](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1974.10489237), *Technometrics* **16** (1974), 551–559. The counterexample is the parameter choice $a=5,b=1$ and the resulting all-threshold comparison, not a new distribution.
* [N. D. Feldheim and O. N. Feldheim, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.03004), establishes an infinite limit superior for the equal-weight ratio without a specified rate. The repository's [weighted unequal-laws extension, #162](../feldheim-weighted-tail-ratio/README.md), proves their different weighted conjecture. It cites Steinerberger's questions but gives neither the present common-law witness nor a universal median-localized threshold.
* The distinction between finite-threshold existence and growth at infinity is real, even for finite means. For example, taking an ordinary Weibull survival $e^{-t^\beta}$ with $1<\beta<2$, [Asmussen, Hashorva, Laub and Taimre, Corollary 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.04070) gives $G(t)\sim c_\beta t^{\beta/2}$ with $c_\beta>0$: put $n=2$, $\alpha=0$, $c=1$, evaluate at $2t$ and divide by $e^{-2t^\beta}$. Thus $G(t)/t\to0$ for that law. This asymptotic follows from their earlier theorem; it is not claimed here as a new result. Their tail formula alone does not rule out a threshold at moderate $t$ reaching either of the proposed constants.
* [Barnard and Steinerberger's convolution inequalities](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.08731) concern convolution and autocorrelation norms and related combinatorial problems, not the two normalized existential comparisons.
