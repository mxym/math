# Primary-source scope and prior art

## Stated questions

- Vipul Goyal, Yuval Ishai and Yifan Song, [Tight Bounds on the Randomness Complexity of Secure Multiparty Computation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/799), ePrint 2022/799, latest posted revision 22 June 2022; [full PDF](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/799.pdf), [CRYPTO 2022 proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-15985-5_17). The full version §1.1, printed pp. 2–3, leaves the remaining polylogarithmic lower/upper gaps open and specifically identifies the explicit Kushilevitz–Mansour benchmark as open. §2.1 and §3.1 set the entire-joint-view, perfect semi-honest model and the worst-case fair-coin measure. Section 2.3, pp. 6–9, restates the Kushilevitz–Mansour serialized masked-prefix protocol and then the authors' explicit tree implementation; §5 Corollary 2 and §6 Theorem 4/Corollary 3 give the explicit O(t² log² n) binary (finite-field-element in general) upper bound by independently instantiating a generator at each tree depth. Appendix B.4, printed p. 42, separately asks about entropy with arbitrary sampling distributions. Section 3.2 Definitions 4–5 and §4.1 Lemma 2 give the one-bit coordinate-private encoding primitive and its exponential support bound; §4.2 Theorem 2 gives the worst-case fair-coin lower bound at least t²/2 for n≥3 and t≤n−2. The splice and intermediate-input structure of §4.2 Lemmas 3–4 are reused explicitly. Section 6 of this note differs by pairing all even inputs and bounding weighted conditional entropies, rather than counting distinct views on one fixed input.
- Eyal Kushilevitz and Yishay Mansour, [Randomness in Private Computations](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480196306130), SIAM J. Discrete Math. 10(4):647–661, 1997; [author preprint scan](https://www.academia.edu/80391056/Randomness_in_Private_Computations), §3.2 Theorems 1–2 and Appendix B. The serial schedule and zero-sum setup are prior. Their subset-family small-space construction achieves O(t log(n/t)) per trusted sampler and gives O(t² log(n/t)) total in the standard model by replicating the sampler among the first t parties. In the special case of corruption of the entire dealer prefix, no unmasked intermediate outsider cut is visible to that prefix. The fresh and differing matrices in this note are not identical copies of a single trusted-sampler distribution: the proof uses the actual count of honest dealers conditional on each dealer's separate local setup messages. The existing explicit construction given an arbitrary test list in the KM core combinatorial lemma is polynomial in the size of that list; the relevant lists here can be n^Θ(t), so that is not the same as a uniformly polynomial-time description algorithm in n,t. Both bounds are read as O(t²(1+log(n/t))) if stated up to t=n−1. Section 4 of this note also uses standard BCH columns; only their endpoint-mask composition is analyzed here, not a new code construction.
- The [AICR c/0087 problem index](https://aicr.info/c/0087/) helped locate the question but is not a primary result; use the preceding §1.1 for the exact published open scope. The nonexplicit bound of this note closes the order in the substantial sublinear range log n≤t=o(n) (more generally log n=O(t)), not all t=o(n). The explicit deterministic bound removes a log n relative to the GIS explicit bound, but does not establish the full KM explicit target, notably at intermediate t=n^(1−o(1)) with t/n→0.

## Neighboring lower bounds and other models

- Anna Gál and Adi Rosén, [Ω(log n) Lower Bounds on the Amount of Randomness in 2-Private Computation](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539703432785), SIAM J. Comput. 34(4), with [author PDF](https://www.irif.fr/~adiro/publ/GR2.pdf). Theorem 1.1 proves unrestricted Ω(log n) worst-case fair coins already for t=2, on every fixed input in their worst-over-runs sense. In particular the old bounds already imply Θ(log n) for any fixed t≥2 as n grows; also see the known one-coin result for t=1 discussed there. Their §4 / Theorem 4.2 proves Ω(t log(n/t)) for their masked-message (or restricted) class, including a model with one trusted dealer, not for arbitrary protocols. This note's template-specific t log n term uses a direct conditioning and orthogonality argument about two remaining independent original tapes in a fixed dealer-prefix chain. It is not a strengthening of their unrestricted lower bound. In fact their restricted-class fair-coin lower bound plus the GIS quadratic lower bound and our upper protocol already give the same order in the broader masked-message class.
- Carlo Blundo, Alfredo De Santis, Giuseppe Persiano and Ugo Vaccaro, [Randomness Complexity of Private Computation](https://doi.org/10.1007/s000370050025), Comput. Complexity 8(2):145–168, 1999; [author-text scan](https://www.academia.edu/33689890/Randomness_complexity_of_private_computation), §§5–6, especially Theorem 6.5 and Corollaries 6.6–6.7 in the scan. Under independent uniform inputs their entropy calculation for XOR gives H(all communications | inputs)≥t(t+1)/(2(n−t−1)) for 2≤t≤n−2; therefore any input-independent tape must have at least that much entropy. It is stronger than the present new entropy lower bound near unanimity, and is quadratic for n−t bounded. It is only O(t) for t a fixed fraction bounded away from 1, where the present weighted-encoding argument yields Ω(t log(t+1)). These are complementary entropic bounds. The quadratic GIS support argument and our Shannon lower bound should not be interchanged.
- Carlo Blundo, Clemente Galdi and Giuseppe Persiano, [Low-randomness constant-round private XOR computations](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10207-006-0007-5), Int. J. Information Security 6:15–26 (2007), gives an O(k t² log n) fair-bit result for k evaluations with a one-round initialization and two rounds per evaluation, under t≤c√(n/log n), c<1/2. This note only uses a single sequential evaluation. Kushilevitz, Ostrovsky and Rosén, [Amortizing Randomness in Private Multiparty Computations](https://doi.org/10.1137/S089548010135274X), SIAM J. Discrete Math. 16(4):533–544 (2003), treats repeated computations and randomness/round tradeoffs. Neither is a claim about arbitrary-tape entropy in the model above.
- Geoffroy Couteau and Adi Rosén, [Random Sources in Private Computation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/074), [author PDF](https://www.irif.fr/~adiro/publ/CR.pdf), §5.1, printed pp. 14–15, specifically notes the older KM bound can use exactly t sources. Its Conjecture 13 applies to functionalities that cannot be computed with perfect privacy for t≥n/2, not XOR. The main protocol in this note also uses t sources and optimizes total coin count, not the number of sources; no new source-count lower bound is claimed. A t+1-source variant is optional.
- Samuel Dittmer and Rafail Ostrovsky, [On Randomness Complexity of 1-Private Protocols](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.79), ICALP 2026, §1 and §§3–4, develops exact local sample spaces and studies private AND without helpers and arbitrary circuits with helpers at threshold one. The exact local generator there is a different object. The general independent-tape model here is not a common-randomness, private simultaneous message, conditional disclosure, preprocessing-distribution or side-channel implementation model.

## Sparse moment-matching construction

- Mark Bun and Justin Thaler, [Dual Lower Bounds for Approximate Degree and Markov–Bernstein Inequalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.6191), especially the OR discussion and §4.6 on square-spaced Hamming radii (the OR construction is attributed there to Špalek), and Alexander Sherstov, [Approximating the AND–OR Tree](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v009a020/), use related symmetrization and annihilation by sparse univariate signed weights. Such interpolation and the passage from moments to hypergeometric pattern probabilities are not claimed as new machinery. The statement here uses quartic spacing to ensure bounded total expected Hamming weight and combines it with an entropy lower bound for two disjoint distributions with matched coordinate marginals. It is not a lower bound on share size for a threshold secret-sharing scheme: no requirement here says every k+1 coordinates reconstruct.
- Targeted duplicate search checked the current GIS full version (including §6, §7, §9 and Appendix B), the KM original, Gál–Rosén, Blundo–De Santis–Persiano–Vaccaro, and nearby papers above; searches for the stated O(t²+t log n) single-XOR bound, dealer-specific separate conditional cut-mask construction, and the logarithmic-entropy one-bit encoder/H(t-private XOR) converse did not find the same statements. This is a scoped literature check, not a claim of exhaustive priority.
