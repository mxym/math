The upper estimate is uniform in the alphabet; this manuscript’s lower bound fixes the alphabet. ## Upper argument, line by line

* The sole imported nontrivial reduction really is [Stanovnik–Moškon–Mraz, Proposition 10](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-023-01344-z): positive integer first split summing to $q$, nonnegative subsequent splits, and the stated ordered convolution recursion and objective. This is the right prefix/suffix (including equal words) problem. The proof of the structural reduction is taken from that publication, not invented or fully re-proved here. Under division by $q^t$, every term of degree $t$ carries exactly this denominator. The only use of integrality afterwards is $\gamma=\max(a_1,b_1)\le1-1/q$.
* (1) The degree-one coefficient is $-1$, middle coefficients cancel, and every remaining coefficient is nonnegative. $s_2=a_1b_1>0$; for later $t$, an endpoint term involving the positive one-letter coefficient and one positive component at $t-1$ is positive. At the first real positive root the still nonzero factor is nonnegative, so the derivative of the product is nonpositive; coincident roots yield derivative zero. Thus $G(\sigma)=\sigma-1>0$, $h(\sigma-1)\le\sigma G'(\sigma)\le\sigma$. Since $s_h\sigma^h\le G(\sigma)$, maximizing $(x-1)x^{-h}$ gives the claimed $s_h$ bound. This works for each truncation without optimality. [Blackburn, (9)–(12), Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21913v1#S3.SS1) gives the binary precursor.
* (3) Evaluating the cutoff product at one is legitimate because its two first roots are greater than one. $a_h\le s_h\le(1-A_h(1))(1-B_h(1))\le a_1(1-A_h(1))$; symmetric for $b_h$. No division by $s_h$ here; it was separately shown positive. The Bellman bound for a positive lag uses $E_0=1$ for its last step rather than $\gamma$; that exceptional term is exactly the $a_h$ term controlled by (3).
* (4) For $t\le n<2N$ either both children are smaller than $N$ (an original seed), or there is a unique large child, in exactly one of the two orientations. The other child has length $t-u<N$. When a large index is split on the left its continuation multiplier is $\theta_u b_{t-u}$ and on the right $(1-\theta_u)a_{t-u}$, not vice versa. The directed chain consists of strictly increasing vertex sizes when read from seed to root, so it is finite even if several choices of intermediate sizes are possible. For each ordered binary tree there is a unique spine: descend to the only child of size at least $N$, stopping when neither is large. Distinct spines or distinct orientations partition the tree expansion; two labeled child subtrees may have the same shape but are still different locations. The resulting $K_n=1$ and $K_u$ really are the stated (unweighted) backward recursion. By induction the upper envelope $E_{n-u}$ bounds all $K_u$. The nontrivial bound is for $u<n$ only; the direct seed at $u=n$ cannot acquire a $\gamma$ factor. This proves (5) after using $\sigma^{n-u}\le\sigma^{n-N}$.
* (6) All direct predecessors are in $[k+1,N-1]$. At the cutoff root, $\sigma^i\le e$ for $i<N$, even if the arrays came from a larger problem. On $i\le n/2$ put $X_i=U_iV_{n-i}$. For $i>j$ the injective map $(i,j)\mapsto(i,n-j)$ charges the upper crossed term at degree $n+i-j>n$, within the *full tail* $G_N$, whose index cutoff is $N$, not $n$. The unused cross term $U_jV_{n-i}$ costs at most $2e^2/[n(k+1)]$ because $j>k$ and $n-i\ge n/2$. The same simple cap bounds $X_i$. Therefore $(\sum X_i)^2\le M\sum X_i+2MF\le3MF$. In the other half exchange the roles of $U,V$ and exclude the even midpoint. The two halves may reuse tail mass since each bound separately uses the entire $F$; the final factor two accounts for this. This is a uniform estimate even for arbitrary asymmetric or sparse feasible arrays, not a claim that all tail mass is negligible.
* (5)+(6) produce two distinct normalized errors $O(k/n)$ and $O(k^{-1/2})$. Both are $O(n^{-1/3})$ at the chosen $k$; the source of the constant $\gamma/e$ is consequently valid. For a concrete cross-check, if $n\ge512$ then $N-1\ge3n/4,\ k/(N-1)\le2n^{-1/3}\le1/4$. Hence $F\le2/n$ and $\sigma^k\le1+3n^{-1/3}$. The normalized direct-seed bound is at most $2\sqrt{12e^2}/\sqrt{k}<20n^{-1/3}$. Finally $(1-1/n)^{n-1}\le\exp(-1+1/n)\le 1/e+1/n$. These give $n s_n\le\gamma/e+25n^{-1/3}$ independently of both the arrays and $q$.

## Lower argument and the all-length quantifier

* Write $k=\lfloor\log_q n\rfloor-2$, $1\le t=\lfloor\sqrt k\rfloor<k$ eventually, $L=k+t+2$. A proper suffix of a proposed marker of length at most $k$ ends in the symbol 1, unlike a prefix; a longer matching suffix must contain the complete $0^k$ starting after position 1. This is impossible on the initial run, at either delimiter, or wholly in the $t$-letter payload. Thus the marker family remains mutually non-overlapping under every subset choice.
* $q^{t-1}<q^L/n\le q^t$, including at exact powers of $q$. Floors give a positive feasible cardinality eventually and $0\le1-np<n/q^L<q^{1-t}$, which tends to zero for fixed $q$ along all integers $n$. Initial marker and terminal nonzero are independent for $n>L$. Long, intermediate and short proper overlaps are all excluded as claimed, equally for two identical codewords.
* Under this conditioning, starts $2$ through $L$ are forbidden by marker non-overlap. The $m=n-2L$ starts $L+1,\dots,n-L$ use only free letters. Ordered increasing $j$-tuples of starts with gaps $\ge L$ reduce by subtracting $(h-1)(L-1)$ at position $h$ to ordinary increasing subsets; hence the exact binomial intersection sum. For every fixed $j$ it tends to $1/j!$ since $L=O_q(\log n)$ and $np\to1$. Applying the odd and even Bonferroni inequalities at fixed order, then taking limits in $n$ and then in order, squeezes the avoidance probability to $1/e$; no uniformity in the truncation order is assumed.
* The distinct final start $n-L+1$ uses the conditioned last symbol; because all markers end in 1 its conditional probability is $p/(1-1/q)=O_q(1/n)$. For each suffix length $k<d<L$, the $k$ checked symbols exclude the last letter and do not meet the first block for $n$ large. Their conditional all-zero probability is $q^{-k}\le q^3/n$. Summing over at most $L$ such tests is $o(1)$ regardless of the interior avoidance event. This is the required all-length lower bound, not a hidden sparse subsequence.

## Source comparison and editorial cautions

1. [Blackburn, arXiv:2611.21913v1 (submitted 16 November 2026), introduction, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21913v1). His best-binary-constant question is exactly the normalized limsup (the infimum of admissible $c$ in the upper asymptotic), and his bound is $5/(6e)$. His formula (9) and first-root Lemma 8 underlie the new argument; his own next step is an estimate involving tail rows at the final index, not the cutoff and positive-lag construction here. He explicitly separates the question of infinitely many finite optimal one-run codes, not answered by a leading constant.
2. [Blackburn–Nasr Esfahani–Kreher–Stinson, Thm 35](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.10309#page=15) proves a subsequential binary lower bound and attributes the historical constant to Levenshtein. [Blackburn 2015, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.1026#page=6) explicitly describes the classical fixed-$q$ subsequence $(q^i-1)/(q-1)$, gives a weaker all-length liminf in Lemma 3, and later uses multiple initial strings to manage rounding in Construction 2 and Thm 8.
3. [Chee–Kiah–Purkayastha–Wang, Thm 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.0236#page=5) indeed **states** a matching liminf for the size of *their particular construction*, but the displayed proof sets $n=\lceil c\alpha(k)^k\rceil$, then fixes the optimizing $c$. That passage controls a subsequence and does not by itself establish an all-length liminf. Do not adopt that claim as a black box, describe the current optimum proof as a proof for their one-run construction, or accuse the entire earlier paper of error. The rounded family above supplies the missing quantifier for the *optimum* by an independent construction.
4. [Guibas–Odlyzko](https://doi.org/10.1137/0135034) concern prefix-synchronized codes with a fixed prescribed marker, a distinct condition; their oscillations need not describe unrestricted non-overlapping codes. [Wang–Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06934) treat q-ary constructions and avoiding patterns from a non-overlapping forbidden family; neither scope by itself establishes this optimum upper bound. Bounded searches for non-overlapping, cross-bifix-free, mutually uncorrelated, prefix-synchronized, all-length/limsup constants and current announcements found no earlier exact fixed-$q$ upper bound. This is not a worldwide priority certification.
