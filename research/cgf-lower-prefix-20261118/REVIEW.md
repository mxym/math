# Scope, source screen and proof audit

## Which question is answered?

* **Original:** S. C. Billey and J. P. Swanson, [Cyclotomic generating functions, Conjecture 33](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v31i4p4/pdf/), *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* 31(4), P4.4 (2024), pp. 20–21. Their statement contains both the sum of the smallest parameters and the sum of the largest parameters. The statement only needs nonnegativity; our examples have strict positivity. Their earlier Remark 32 concerns coordinatewise comparison, not sums, and is not the question solved here.
* **Upper suffix:** An [anonymous 9 October 2026 candidate](https://www.evidencepress.org/releases/cgf-majorisation-counterexample/) claims a violation at a suffix of length 5,591 and explicitly states that its padded example satisfies every lower prefix. The [actual manuscript](https://github.com/ipitchford/cgf-majorisation-counterexample/blob/v1.1.0-candidate/paper.tex), especially its abstract and remark on the lower prefixes, and the [claim table](https://github.com/ipitchford/cgf-majorisation-counterexample/blob/v1.1.0-candidate/claims.md) were checked against that summary. The [27 October correction](https://github.com/ipitchford/cgf-majorisation-counterexample/blob/main/CORRECTIONS.md) repairs a factor in the proof of its Lemma 2 and reports no change to its statements. This packet does not depend on or certify that manuscript's proof.
* **Positive multipliers:** [Gatzweiler, Levicán-Santibáñez and Yoshida, Thm. 4.7 and Cor. 4.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22226v1) state an unrestricted multiplier of the form (1+q)^k, including for cyclotomic products with no factor q−1. That multiplier generally changes the low-degree coefficients. [Powers and Reznick, Thm. 1](https://reznick.web.illinois.edu/11200.pdf) give a quantitative form of Pólya's homogeneous positivity theorem. We invoke the classical theorem, not a claim of a new Pólya theorem.
* **Further antecedent:** [D. Handelman, *Deciding eventual positivity of polynomials*, especially Section V](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385700003291), develops general criteria for a fixed nonnegative multiplier to have a coefficient-nonnegative power multiple of another polynomial, including one-variable gap examples. We do not claim first discovery of eventual positivity with gaps. Our proof gives the explicit two-step residue-class argument for interpolation with cyclotomic factors and uses the controlled initial coefficients to force an arbitrarily small ratio of lower parameter sums.
* **Direct antecedent for prescribed initial sequences and Euler exponents:** S. T. Somu, [J. Number Theory 170 (2017), 3–9](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2016.06.004); [author manuscript, introduction and Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.03230). The paper recalls that an arbitrary finite integer sequence can be prescribed after the constant coefficient 1 in a divisor of some x^n−1 and studies the density of admissible n. Its Lemma 3.1 gives exactly the finite-jet uniqueness of the Euler exponents used here. It does not require all higher coefficients of the divisor to be strictly positive and does not address sorted q-integer parameter sums. We do not claim newness of unrestricted jet interpolation or of Euler-exponent uniqueness. Our coefficient-positive completion allows repeated cyclotomic factors, unlike a divisor of x^n−1 in characteristic zero.
* **Distinct from single-coefficient problems:** [Ji, Li and Moree, *Values of coefficients of cyclotomic polynomials II*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4898) realize individual integer values as coefficients. That is not simultaneous prescription of an initial sequence in a polynomial all of whose coefficients are positive.
* **Bounded search, 18 November 2026:** checked the original journal statement, the upper-suffix manuscript and its correction, the related positivity papers above, and web/arXiv-indexed queries for Billey–Swanson with *lower prefix*, *smallest parameters*, *majorisation/majorization*, *q-integer*, and for prescribed positive initial coefficients of cyclotomic products. A repository search on the default branch for the numbered conjecture, and a search for an existing CGF prefix PR, did not return this result. No earlier exact lower-prefix counterexample or zero-infimum claim was identified in the sources inspected. Indexes and title-word searches are incomplete; this is not proof of absolute priority. The repository's earlier result on prime cyclotomic divisors of **unimodal** polynomials concerns Conjecture 48, not 33.

## Proof and executable audit

**Finite witness.** Confirm the roots-of-unity factorization, the two reduced lists (9+12+17+17+5 = 4+17+22+17 = 60), the degree 228, and the inequality 18 < 19. Polynomial division in the second calculation is in truncated formal power series with invertible constant term; exact factorization and the known degree ensure that degrees 0 through 228 are the entire polynomial. Both integer implementations agree coefficient by coefficient. A separate internal reviewer used SymPy and obtained the same coefficient vector digest. Neither computation verifies or is needed for the qualitative theorem below.

**Jet completion.** A corrective factor has constant term 1 and first positive-degree term at exactly the targeted exponent with the prescribed sign, so no previous coefficient changes. The seed's degree is at least t−1 since that coefficient is positive. Every residue class occurs in the first and last blocks of t coefficients. In those classes the section polynomials thus have both constant and leading coefficients positive. All sections are simultaneously positive near 0 and infinity, and multiplication by a nonnegative-section polynomial with constant term 1 preserves that condition pointwise on those two intervals. In detail, writing X=q^t, the section of a product with residue j is a sum of terms X^floor((i+h)/t) F_i(X) B_h(X) over i+h congruent to j modulo t. It contains F_j B_0 and B_0(X)≥1.

On a compact intervening interval, the nontrivial rotations of 1+q^(t+1) have strictly smaller absolute value than its positive-real value, because t+1 is coprime to t. Finiteness of the rotations and compactness make this a uniform strict ratio. For one sufficiently high power, the positive term dominates all the error terms in each residue filter simultaneously. Pólya's theorem now applies to the homogeneous version of each section, including at both boundary points, and a common exponent exists since all sufficiently large exponents work. The leading coefficient in each residue class of the final polynomial is at its natural terminal index: reciprocity supplies the full positive upper-edge block just as at the lower edge. The multipliers are cyclotomic products and equal 1 modulo q^t throughout.

**An elementary check of the Pólya step.** If G(X)=sum_i g_i X^i has degree d, positive endpoint coefficients and G>0 on the positive axis, its homogenization has minimum c>0 on the unit simplex. For n≥d and 0≤k≤n, the coefficient of X^k in (1+X)^(n−d)G, divided by binom(n,k), equals
```
sum_(i=0)^d g_i (k)_i (n−k)_(d−i)/(n)_d ,
```
where the descending factorial (u)_h is zero for nonnegative integers u<h. For fixed d this differs uniformly in k from sum_i g_i (k/n)^i(1−k/n)^(d−i) by O_G(1/n), by expanding the finite falling-factorial polynomials. This last expression is at least c. Thus the coefficients are all strictly positive for sufficiently large n; the d=0 case is immediate.

**Zero infimum.** At fixed t one first chooses m large, since [q^j]H^m is a polynomial of degree j with leading coefficient 1/j! for each 1≤j<t. No simultaneous estimate over all t is assumed. The Euler product exponents below t are uniquely determined modulo q^t by the first differing exponent argument. The exponents of the completed P sum to zero because P(1)>0, so its two reduced parameter lists have the same length. Exactly m ones are in the denominator and 2m twos in the numerator; all remaining entries are ≥t. The choice k=2m is valid because both lists have at least 2m entries. The ratio is ≤4/(t+1) for each t, so the infimum is 0, not an attained zero.

**Assurance:** two exact coefficient algorithms in the packet, a separate symbolic computation and an internal reading of all three arguments. No external human review, public release, Lean certificate, certified exhaustive literature search or minimality claim.
