# Attribution and exact scope

## Problem being answered

- Sandro Coretti, Pooya Farshim, Patrick Harasser, Karl Southern, [Multi-Source Randomness Extraction and Generation in the Random-Oracle Model](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1258), ITC 2025, [primary proceedings PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol343-itc2025/LIPIcs.ITC.2025.10/LIPIcs.ITC.2025.10.pdf), DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.ITC.2025.10](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITC.2025.10). Section 1.4, pp. 10:6–7, motivates a decomposition technique for multi-source extraction, explains why applying a separate decomposition to each party can fail, and leaves suitable multi-source versions open. Section 3 defines sources and unpredictability levels for different oracle-access models. Section 6, especially Lemma 22 and Theorem 23 (pp. 10:16–18), gives a compression/next-bit-prediction extraction argument for the monolithic random oracle. It does not produce the consistent family required here. Its starting reduction to next-bit prediction pays for the symbol length; the proof here treats the full challenge alphabet directly. This note answers the precise monolithic, *jointly selected* two-source version below, not the original article's separate-decomposition or non-monolithic ambitions, and not its unbounded-distinguisher questions.
- [AICR c/0010 statement](https://aicr.info/c/0010/) and [full mathematical statement](https://aicr.info/c/0010/pdf/main.pdf), pp. 3–5, Definitions 1–3 and Conjecture 1. This is an AI-written formulation motivated by CFHS, not a numbered conjecture in the ITC paper. It fixes independent source coins conditional on the same table, unpredictable coordinates with both leakages and the full table in the predictor's information, the two experiments that retain the original coordinate pair, and challenge-oblivious (address-hidden) adaptive observers. In particular the family is indexed by the original table and leakage, is consistent with the original table, and is chosen before q and D. Those are exactly Theorem 2's experiments and order. The constants c=3,C=18 in the stated conjecture follow from the stronger displayed estimate, including for every γ. In the nontrivial N≥2 case the extra term γ is not needed.
- Exact public partial work in the associated [campaign ledger](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crypto-conjura/crypto-conjura.github.io/main/c/0010/campaign/PROGRESS.md), §§1–2, includes the query-free case, observers whose query positions ignore the challenge, and more general adaptive comparison but with restricted M and P, or with a coefficient depending on q. In particular [kappa-1-r3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crypto-conjura/crypto-conjura.github.io/main/c/0010/campaign/intermediates/split-decomp-kappa-1-r3.md) §§5–8 has the known hybrid and density family, and [kappa-3-r4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crypto-conjura/crypto-conjura.github.io/main/c/0010/campaign/intermediates/split-decomp-kappa-3-r4.md) and [kappa-4-r2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crypto-conjura/crypto-conjura.github.io/main/c/0010/campaign/intermediates/split-decomp-kappa-4-r2.md) refine the restricted P and M regions. The statement PDF Remark 6, pp. 9–10, already has a version of the κ(q)-to-decomposition reduction with a P₀ cap and a linear-in-P₀ hit estimate, and identifies its large-P/growing-q limitation. Here the q-dependent random mask is **only a proof device in κ(q)**, while the Cauchy–Schwarz posterior hit bound permits a larger q-independent cap and closes that limitation.

## Existing tools versus new estimates

- Sandro Coretti, Yevgeniy Dodis, Siyao Guo, John P. Steinberger, [Random Oracles and Non-Uniformity](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/937), EUROCRYPT 2018, Claim 2 and Appendix A (dense/bit-fixing partition). This is the density restoration in Section 4 of this note. The prior auxiliary-input presampling line includes Dominique Unruh, [Random Oracles and Auxiliary Input](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-74143-5_12), CRYPTO 2007. The exact output-conditioning hybrid and one-sided adaptive transcript ratio are not claimed as new.
- Yevgeniy Dodis, Siyao Guo and Jonathan Katz, [Fixing Cracks in the Concrete: Random Oracles with Auxiliary Input, Revisited](https://cs.nyu.edu/~dodis/ps/romai.pdf), EUROCRYPT 2017. The randomized encoding arguments (in particular the discussion beginning with the sampled set R and the untouched subfamily G on printed p. 19) already mask independent points and retain runs that avoid masked values. CFHS Lemma 22 uses that compression line. The new concentration step keeps *unbiased inverse-survival weights* for each deterministic truncated path and then integrates the exponential moment over the random mask. It is not a claim that the use of random masks itself is new. The concentration step controls all adaptive challenge inputs without a union over range symbols or an assumption that the query schedule ignores the challenge.
- The campaign's [kappa-1-r3, Lemma 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crypto-conjura/crypto-conjura.github.io/main/c/0010/campaign/intermediates/split-decomp-kappa-1-r3.md) already states the posterior-product factorization and notes that E[m₁m₂]≤δ² need not hold. The proof here instead applies min(1,s m₁m₂)≤sqrt(s m₁m₂) before taking the expectation, giving δ sqrt(s) by Cauchy–Schwarz without the false bound. That estimate, not independence of posterior maxima, is used for the cap in the actual consistent family.

The focused duplicate check covered the ITC publication/ePrint record, the named public campaign's latest ledger and refinements, the auxiliary-input compression lineage, and searches for subsequent split-source extraction and consistent decomposition applications. No same all-parameter statement or inverse-survival moment argument was located there. This is not an exhaustive priority claim. No result about a deployed oracle, quantum queries, or a non-monolithic construction is inferred.
