# Attribution and comparison

1. Divesh Aggarwal, [*Secret Sharing Despite Non-Extractable Randomness*, arXiv:2610.20173v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20173v2) (3 November 2026; also [ePrint 2026/2477](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2477)). Definition 2.1 gives exact reconstruction and pairwise statistical privacy for each allowed dealer distribution; Definition 2.2 gives worst-source deterministic extraction. Section 1.2 already observes the conditional min-entropy bound at perfect privacy, and states the need for near-ideal share or key sizes when pursuing a general extraction implication. That observation already implies a uniform extractor with an exactly equal share alphabet at \(\varepsilon=0\). The present claims concern the sharp positive-\(\varepsilon\) constant and the minimax witness, not the zero-error fact or a converse to their larger-alphabet constructions.
2. Divesh Aggarwal, Eldon Chung, Maciej Obremski, and João Ribeiro, [*On Secret Sharing, Randomness, and Random-less Reductions for Secret Sharing*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-22318-1_12), TCC 2022; [fixed expanded text](https://opencryptology.org/2021/802/versions/20211015T113637Z.pdf) §1.1 Definitions 1–2 and §1.4. Their ordinary statistical definition is compatible with (1), but their positive extraction theorems use additional conditions such as leakage resilience (see also §3) or non-malleability, with a different parameter tradeoff; (2) is for ordinary privacy and a single equal-size alphabet. Carl Bosley and Yevgeniy Dodis, [*Does Privacy Require True Randomness?*](https://iacr.org/archive/tcc2007/43920001/43920001.pdf), TCC 2007, §5, distinguish ordinary sharing from encryption. In particular the short-key restriction of their encryption result is not claimed for our witness.
3. [Prior quantitative threshold-sharing result in this repository](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/8b07ee5d6d29575c8d1f27cb76d1c0d7f078f5d9/research/threshold-sharing-imperfect-sources), PROOF §5, already records the exact \(\varepsilon=0\) min-entropy/ideal-alphabet implication, as well as larger-alphabet non-extractability and alphabet lower bounds. Our imperfect-privacy minimax result is separate from those constructions. It uses no multiplicity branches or Shamir interpolation.
4. Ignacio Cascudo, Jaron Skovsted Gundersen, and Diego Ruano, [*Improved Bounds on the Threshold Gap in Ramp Secret Sharing*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/099.pdf), IEEE TIT 65(7), 2019, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2019.2902151), Introduction, gives the standard secret/share size relationship and discusses the ideal/ramp scope; its bounds are not deterministic extraction error for a source class.  Aggarwal v2 Appendix A discusses the different ordinary-sharing extraction statement in [Yao–Li, JISA 89, 104008 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jisa.2025.104008). Our proof never applies privacy to the authorized complete tuple (or assumes equality of the two distributions compared there).

On 28 November 2026, version-identifier and recent arXiv/ePrint searches combined `ideal secret sharing`, `statistical privacy`, `same share alphabet`, `extractable randomness`, `fixed message extractor` and `exact total variation`. We found the perfect-privacy condition and stronger-model extraction theorems above, but not the positive-error constant with an all-extractor equality construction (5). This is a bounded duplicate search, not a claim of absolute priority.

Repository de-duplication also checked the research directory at pinned commit `6e05674d2c8b84aac7545ca287ec6e5370f493a2`. The most direct match is item 3; it gives the zero-error implication and a separate construction with a larger alphabet, rather than the positive-error minimax equality (5).
