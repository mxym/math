# Source map and claim boundaries

* Tim Alderson, [Length-Maximal Codes with Given Singleton Defect: Structure and Bounds, arXiv:2604.03784v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03784v2), 23 August 2026, [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03784v2). Theorem 3.16 and Corollary 3.17 supply symbol uniformity and orthogonal-array structure; Lemma 3.13 is defect preservation on shortening; Theorem 3.18 supplies the pairwise distance list. Lemma 3.20 gives \(s\le q-1\) and the weaker \((s+2)\mid q(q+1)\) for \(k\ge3\). §5 Problem 3 explicitly asks for the sharpened divisibility, and names \((13,125,10)_5\) as its smallest undecided instance. Its discussion of \(k\ge3\) is essential: the paper already gives dimension-two counterexamples to the condition when that dimension is included (Corollary 3.5).
* In the same paper, Theorem 3.32 and Corollary 3.33(3) give the dimension-four distance distribution at \(s=q-1\), including the absence of distance \(n-1\); Remark 3.37, Table 3 and §5 Problem 5 isolate the full-weight alphabet candidates \(2,4,7,10,16,34\). This note excludes precisely the five \(q>2\) candidates, leaving the known binary example. It does not settle unique extendability (Problem 2), the \(36\mid q\) MDS question, or the general nonlinear existence question.
* D. G. Larman, C. A. Rogers and J. J. Seidel, [On Two-Distance Sets in Euclidean Space](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/9.3.261), Bull. London Math. Soc. 9 (1977), 261–267, provides classical two-distance integrality context. P. Boyvalenkov, K. Delchev, D. V. Zinoviev and V. A. Zinoviev, [On Two-Weight Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13623), Discrete Math. 344 (2021), 112318, DOI 10.1016/j.disc.2021.112318, uses the standard coordinate-wise simplex embedding and studies bounds and two-weight codes; its separate linear-code divisibility theorem has additional hypotheses. We use neither a generic two-distance theorem nor any linear-code hypothesis. No novelty is claimed for the incidence embedding, the rational eigenvalue observation, or the example codes.
* For the linear and additive settings already covered separately, see Alderson v2 §3.4 and Remark 3.36, [Ball–Blokhuis–Mazzocca](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01196129), and [Alderson–Ball, arXiv:2603.27689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27689v2). The proof here applies to any finite alphabet, including non-prime-power sizes, and to arbitrary codes, including nonlinear ones.

Bounded comparison (2026-11-27 UTC): checked versions v1/v2, the author's 2026 CMS abstract, and searched the title and problem wording together with “sharpening,” “divisibility,” “length-maximal,” and the \((13,125,10)_5\) and \((22,256,16)_4\) parameters. The general two-distance spectral mechanism is classical; we did not find a matching application resolving either of these two stated cases in the checked sources. No absolute priority claim is made.
