# Sources and boundaries

- Anish Gupta, [*Balance Constants, Majority Cycles, and the Gold Partition Conjecture through Fourteen Elements*, arXiv:2607.23926v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23926v2). Section 4.1 defines the full/restricted majority relations and proves equality for triangles (Proposition 7); §4.5, Observation 11 reports equal spectra through 14 elements; §8, Question 14 asks for equality for all finite posets. We use exactly the strict threshold and directed **simple**, not necessarily induced, cycle lengths. Our counterexample is not a revision of the finite enumeration, nor an answer to the paper's questions about balance constants or Gold Partition.
- Peter C. Fishburn, [*On the family of linear extensions of a partial order*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(74)90030-6), JCTB 17 (1974), 240–243, disproved acyclicity of the linear-extension majority relation; his [1976 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(76)90028-9) further constructed majority graphs with no maximal element. The existence of majority cycles per se is classical. William V. Gehrlein and Peter C. Fishburn, [*Linear extension majority cycles for small (n≤9) partial orders*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0898-1221(90)90239-G), Comput. Math. Appl. 20 (1990), 41–44, found the five nine-element cycle examples, all with length three. Karel De Loof, Bernard De Baets, and Hans De Meyer [extended the census to 13](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.camwa.2009.12.021), Comput. Math. Appl. 59 (2010), 1541–1547. Their work on [cutting levels](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/144109/Kybernetika_50-2014-5_13.pdf) instead varies the probability threshold to prevent cycles; we keep the threshold fixed and delete only comparable-pair arcs. Earlier [Brightwell–Fishburn–Winkler](https://combinatorialpress.com/ars/vol36/) examples concern the occurrence of cycles in interval orders, not the claim here.
- Related but different: [Aires–Chan–Pak–Panova](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30888), [Peczarski](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2017.1368050), and [Sah](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01500) address balance constants or extremal posets, not this cycle-spectrum comparison. The [wustep/maths computational project](https://github.com/wustep/maths/tree/b98424fa6dfbf3a780ab7f609f7d335020aebb9f/problems/one-third-two-thirds) cites Gupta's census and investigates balance constants and ladders; its three-chain computations also include Cartesian products, which are not the disjoint unions used here.
- The candidate contribution is the explicit full-versus-incomparable cycle-length separation, including its unbounded form on a fixed three-chain architecture; the uniform-shuffle and elementary hypergeometric methods are standard. We do not claim a minimum witness order, an exact spectrum above length 66, or first discovery of nontransitivity for a class of posets. Primary-paper searches included stochastic precedence, mutual rank probabilities, and poset-majority cycles. No prior statement of this separation was located; this is not a guarantee of priority or an exhaustive literature search.
- Repository comparison on 19 November 2026 covered the main tree through [#150](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/150), tagged source not in that tree, formalizations, release indexes, and retrievable distinct release attachments; two older draft-release files identified in the repository inventory were not independently retrievable. The deltas through [#151](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/151) and [#152](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/152) concern a delta-interaction loop and bandit information, not poset majority. The repository's [Condorcet election paper](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/114) concerns independently sampled voter ballots rather than the uniform distribution on the extensions of one fixed poset.
