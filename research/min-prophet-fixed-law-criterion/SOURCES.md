# Sources, related results and scope

**Model and immediate predecessor.** Livanos and Mehta, [*Minimization is Harder in the Prophet World*, SODA 2024, Thms. 1.1/3.1](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10511091), formulate the fixed-law finite-mean guarantee in the forced one-choice model. [Repository PR #237](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/237) already gives explicit fixed bounded atomless counterexamples and a sharp distinction between pointwise and uniform subexponential growth. This paper does not claim those examples as its contribution: it supplies a lower-tail test covering every fixed nonnegative law of positive finite mean and an elementary support classification in the presence of a zero atom. The [2023 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07988v3) assumed an Entire cumulative hazard for its positive result. The [EC 2025 paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3736252.3742682) ([full text](https://vaslivanos.github.io/publications/Unified_IID_PI.pdf)) studies stopping asymptotics under extreme-value-domain hypotheses. It also notes the easy case of a positive left endpoint. Those sources, and the Bellman recurrence and order-statistic integral themselves, predate this note.

**Iteration theory.** The logarithmic differential is **not new**. For $f(x)=x-D(x)$ the clock's differential at continuity points of $F$ is exactly the Buff form $(f'-1)dx/[(f-x)\log f']$. Petersen and Zakeri, [*Buff forms and invariant curves of near-parabolic maps*, §1, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.17125), explicitly credit Xavier Buff and explain its relation to approximate translation in holomorphic dynamics. Classical iteration theory includes Szekeres, [*Regular iteration of real and complex functions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02559539), [original paper](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/5867-11511_2007_Article_BF02559539.pdf). Continuous and fractional iterates and Abel coordinates thus have a much older history. Here there is no assertion of a new differential or exact conjugacy. The pointwise cdf slope sandwich gives a summable error for this Bellman orbit even for laws with atoms; it yields a nonrecursive integral formula for the upper competitive ratio, complemented by a separate proof for a zero atom.

**Earlier optimal-stopping asymptotics.** Kennedy and Kertz, [*The Asymptotic Behavior of the Reward Sequence in the Optimal Stopping of I.I.D. Random Variables*](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176990547), established tail-index asymptotics in domains of attraction. Entwistle, Lustri and Sofronov, [*On Asymptotics of Optimal Stopping Times*, §3](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/10/2/194), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02875), use an asymptotic differential equation for threshold values in maximization and study stopping-time statistics for classes of densities. Approximating a stopping recursion by continuous dynamics is therefore also prior. The criterion here concerns the ratio of two expected *costs*, uses arbitrary cdfs rather than a differentiable tail, and controls an additive error in the time coordinate, not merely a relative one in time.

**Recent checks.** The [July 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05085) and [August ESA paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.55) by Azar, Biran and Fiat allow multiple selections and study how many are necessary for competitiveness; the one-choice worst-case lower bound recalled there permits changing the law with the horizon. The [2026 single-threshold paper on sharp prophet bounds](https://doi.org/10.1017/jpr.2026.10117) concerns maximization, not this minimum-cost tail test. Historical searches and a separate July–19 November 2026 sweep used minimum/cost prophet, fixed distribution, atom at zero, essential lower endpoint, regular variation, Bellman/Abel clock, corrected iterates and Buff forms. No source was located giving this all-law minimum ratio test or this zero-atom support dichotomy; this is a bounded comparison of retrievable work and not a global priority claim.
