# Primary sources and scope

Comparison date: 19 November 2026. This is a bounded search of publicly available material, not a guarantee of priority or a statement that no unindexed correction exists.

1. V. Livanos and R. Mehta, [*Minimization is Harder in the Prophet World*, SODA 2024](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611977912.17), [open proceedings PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10511091). Theorems 1.1 and 3.1 (the latter p. 434) make a claim for a fixed finite-mean cost law on a nonnegative interval, with a distribution-dependent finite competitive factor for all sufficiently large horizons. This is the statement tested here, not a guarantee uniform in the choice of law. The proof of Lemma 3.5 (p. 436) compares the expected second-smallest/expected minimum ratio to its two-draw counterpart. The main proof exhibits a law for which such a bound cannot hold. The infinite-mean statement and other, more restricted positive results are not being refuted.
2. The same authors' [*Prophet Inequalities for Cost Minimization*, arXiv:2209.07988v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07988v3) (23 February 2023) instead includes the Entire cumulative hazard assumption in its positive theorem (Theorem 1.1; see Definition 2.7). The logarithmic hazard in this note is not of that form. The negative example involving an infinite mean in the older manuscript also differs from the bounded fixed law here. Consequently the formal paper and its earlier online version cannot be used interchangeably for this question.
3. Livanos and Mehta, [*Minimization I.I.D. Prophet Inequality via Extreme Value Theory: A Unified Approach*, EC 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3736252.3742682), [author's full text](https://vaslivanos.github.io/publications/Unified_IID_PI.pdf), [November 2024 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19851). Compare also [Livanos's thesis](https://vaslivanos.github.io/theses/PhD_Thesis.pdf), Chapter 2, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.3. Their extreme-value comparison assumes a nondegenerate affine limit of normalized extrema. Three explicit minimum quantiles in PROOF.md rule out that hypothesis for the full-support example. Single-threshold, multi-choice and multi-unit guarantees are different statements.
4. H. Esfandiari, M. Hajiaghayi, V. Liaghat and M. Monemizadeh, [*Prophet Secretary*, journal publication](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1029394), [2015 preprint, Theorem 4 and its proof in §7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.01155). Their one-choice i.i.d. lower bound already grows exponentially in the horizon in the worst case: its three equiprobable costs are $0,1,2^n$. The law changes with $n$, even if rescaled to lie inside $[0,1]$. ENVELOPE.md uses this older low/middle/high mechanism at each stage, but puts every stage in a single atomless law depending only on the proposed envelope.
5. Y. Azar, I. Biran and A. Fiat, [*Multi Choice Min Prophet*, ESA 2026 (25 August)](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.55), [full text](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol388-esa2026/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.55/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.55.pdf). Their §1.4 recalls the horizon-dependent one-choice obstruction. Their own results bound the number of choices needed for competitiveness, with lower-bound instances allowed to depend on problem parameters. Neither scope yields a single bounded atomless law whose optimal one-choice ratio diverges, or the fixed-law no-envelope statement above.

The primary-source comparison included earlier and formal versions, subsequent citations, searches for corrections and counterexamples, and a separate July–19 November 2026 pass using author/title, minimum/cost prophet, fixed distribution, bounded/finite mean, slow variation, extreme-value domain, order-statistic and multi-choice terms. Other models that allow divisible procurement, multiple hires or cancellation do not give the asserted one-choice i.i.d. fixed-law bound. No matching prior resolution was located in this search.
