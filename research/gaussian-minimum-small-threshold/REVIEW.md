Otherwise the limiting two-planes formed by unordered pairs constitute a pair partition, and an active triple spanning three dimensions in the limit costs O(t^3) uniformly along the sequence. Away from these exceptional triples, nontrivial active indices belong to one flat. In a flat the cyclic sum of adjacent overlaps minus the full intersection is a valid lower bound on the count loss (include both directed cyclic edges and subtract one full intersection when m=2). In the planar case the active angular arc is consecutive, giving equality. Normalized adjacent masses have limits 2/(pi sin delta); conditioning on the transverse coordinates gives shifted strip intersections whose planar areas are bounded by the zero-offset area by the difference body and Brunn–Minkowski. Two independent projected strips bound all centered sections uniformly. The inradius-one polygon is of area 2 sum tan(delta/2); subtracting its contribution gives the cotangent formula. Convexity in the gaps and strict decline of r(m) force the regular full-flat limit. In detail the derivative inequality reduces to sin y>y(1-y/pi), from the cubic sine bound and pi^2<12. Compactness genuinely localizes every sequence of near optimizers; it is not a statement holding only for fixed nonsingular covariance.

In the resulting fixed neighborhood of the regular orbit, the top-two eigenspace has a spectral gap from the rest. For the weighted section mass, the support polygon formula follows by solving adjacent line intersections and summing support times edge length. The quadratic part on anti-periodic offsets diagonalizes into odd frequencies, with only the translation frequencies k=1 and 2n−1 not strictly negative.
