# Scope, prior work and overlap checks (19 November 2026)

**Named target.** Dmitriy Kunisky, [arXiv:2605.02023v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02023v1#S3), Conjecture 3.1, asks for a comparison of survival functions at every nonnegative threshold for every real correlation matrix of size n. Only v1 appeared in the [arXiv submission record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02023) when checked. The present result concerns one common interval of small positive thresholds for each fixed n, uniformly over **all** such matrices. It does not imply a global positive-moment inequality, a pointwise spherical comparison, or an effective or n-uniform cutoff.

**Explicit antecedents, including the planar theorem and transverse operator.**

* [A. E. Litvak (2018), Around the simplex mean width conjecture](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-71834-7_5), Conjecture 5.1, proposed a different moment minimizer. Kunisky, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2, gives the cosine law and uses n=4, p=2 to disprove that earlier proposal. Gaussian-minimum order estimates and Mallat–Zeitouni's nonlinear PCA question, discussed in Kunisky §5.1, are not the covariance extremum of Conjecture 3.1. The [Gao–Ji–Liu paper, arXiv:2607.15035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15035), for instance, concerns the retained energy of a best basis in nonlinear PCA.
* **Anonymous (Evidence Press), [Local stability of cosine configurations for Gaussian minima, v1.0.0](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23453252), 18 October 2026.** Its **Proposition 2.1 is precisely the all-threshold rank-two comparison, including its equal-gap equality statement**; Section 2 of our proof repeats this known lemma to be self-contained. Theorem 1.1 of that paper already gives strict local quadratic growth for positive moments of order p>1, including perturbations out of the plane. Its transverse form is the anti-periodic cycle matrix with only the two rotation/translation frequencies in the kernel. Neither this matrix, nor its Fourier spectrum, nor the general observation that the cosine frame is stable for a nearby optimization task is claimed here. It is the **same signed-cycle operator**, here arising from the area of a displaced strip intersection, not from a second derivative of an integrated moment. The truncation at scale |HZ|/t and control of all configurations, including nearly colliding or near-rank-deficient ones, are necessary for the common-threshold CDF inequality. A local moment bound is not that inequality and conversely this restricted CDF result does not prove the moment bound.
* [cyclemath, Convex Hulls of Great Subspheres and Gaussian Minima](https://github.com/cyclemath/great-subspheres-gaussian-minima), public v0.1, September 2026, [archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22783390), gives intrinsic-volume and second-moment identities, active-stratum corrections and a volume comparison conditional on a Gaussian-minimum conjecture. It explicitly does not assert either full conjecture. Its convex hull of entire great subspheres is not the offset-strip polygon used here.
* [Jiang and Polyanskii's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10550) addresses zones that **cover the whole sphere**, not their measure before complete coverage. [Fodor, Vígh and Zarnócz, §2, Lemma 1](https://publicatio.bibl.u-szeged.hu/8607/1/sphere_plank-2015-12-18.pdf) calculate explicit intersection areas for two spherical zones under stated angular restrictions and apply them to covering bounds. These facts do not by themselves optimize the Gaussian union of n strips for a fixed small threshold. Kunisky already distinguishes the spherical conjecture (4.1) and its one-way implication to (3.1) in Theorem 4.2. No such spherical theorem is being asserted here.
* Search terms covered the title/identifier, cosine covariance and rank-two regular frame, stochastic order for absolute Gaussian minima, small thresholds and thin central strips, spherical zones and union measure, pair intersections, competing transverse lifts, and current preprints and public archives. No result found gives this exact comparison for all correlations at a common positive interval, but a finite search does not establish novelty or priority.
