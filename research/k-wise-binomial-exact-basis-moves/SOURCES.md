# Prior results, conventions and coverage (19 November 2026)

* [BEKK, July 2024 preprint, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18688v1#S5) and [May 2026 journal version, §5.2–5.3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10959-026-01487-4). Both distinguish one-site and multiple-site changes and formally state the simultaneous +2 rule as Conjecture 5.5. The single-site clause and the hypothetical longer jump across packed neighboring pairs occur immediately before it. Conjecture 5.4's introductory qualifier about the number of breakpoints does not give a precise asymptotic frequency; we only assert the move at every nonexceptional transition when k≤n−2. Both versions open §5 with the even k≤n−1 convention, while their numerical ranges and Proposition 5.8 exclude k=n−1. The boundary calculation in the proof distinguishes these scopes. Their earlier endpoint phase and extremal-law results are prior, not new here.
* BEKK Proposition 5.8 expressly gives the same upper count and the equality formula conditional on the conjectured transitions (with no exceptional transition for equality). For the sum of *pair starts* a single unit pair shift increments the sum by one, not two; the factor two in their proof is appropriate instead to the sum of *all* nondistinguished sites. The published phase-count conclusion agrees. Here the transition theorem proves that conclusion without the move hypotheses, and yields the explicit simultaneous-move deficit.
* [Roy Hermann, September 2026, Theorem 1, Lemma 2 and Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25595v1) proves reflection of local support changes, including their multiplicities, and uniqueness of the optimizing count law. Reflection of an unknown transition does not determine its step size. [The owner's prior phase theorem, #220](../k-wise-binomial-optimal-bases/PROOF.md) proves ordered pair starts move by one or stay in place and explicitly leaves the actual replacement issue undecided. Its [source comparison](../k-wise-binomial-optimal-bases/SOURCES.md) covers the classical discrete moment LP (Prékopa, Boros–Prékopa and others), paired roots, and fixed-moment support algorithms such as Infusino–Kuna–Lebowitz–Speer (2017). None of these is being claimed as a new LP principle. The parity-weighted finite-difference annihilation and log-concavity of a real-rooted polynomial away from its roots are elementary ingredients.
