# Primary source map and scope

1. Yuval Ishai, Eyal Kushilevitz and Sahar Alon Yerushalmi, [On Perfect Additive Randomized Encodings](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/3070), ePrint 2026/3070, received November 26, approved November 27, 2026; [TCC chapter, pp. 84–113](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-43588-0_4). The authors' public abstracts state exact OR impossibility over every finite abelian group and exact XOR impossibility over prime Z_p, p>2, under rational encoding probabilities. They state stronger statistical OR restrictions under deterministic-zero encoding, and under deterministic-zero plus flat laws. These are different assumptions from the unrestricted finite-group size bound here; our inverse-square-root bound does not itself rule out negligible error in the group representation length. The public Springer Appendix A, Definition 15, Lemma 18, and Proposition 1 give perfect-correctness OR completeness for any fixed finite function, with the two-party quantitative reduction m*2^(k+1)*delta+2^(1-m), and reference the multiparty garbling extension. The ePrint PDF returned HTTP 403 during this check, and the public Springer preview exposes the abstract and Appendix A, not the main chapter. Thus absence of a quantitative auxiliary lemma in the inaccessible text is not a claim. The exact scope comparison is to the authors' public prime/rational statement, not a claim that no unpublished or inaccessible generalization exists. Exact OR impossibility is credited to IKY throughout.

2. Shai Halevi, Yuval Ishai, Eyal Kushilevitz and Tal Rabin, [Additive Randomized Encodings and Their Applications](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/870.pdf), CRYPTO 2023, §1.2, §§4.1–4.2 and §5.3. Their model and convolution/Fourier machinery appear in Theorems 4.5 and 4.14. Corollary 4.17 concerns two-party perfect privacy while permitting correctness error; Theorem 4.5's OR realizes this distinction directly with correctness error 1/|G|. Theorem 5.10 and Lemma 5.8 give the earlier completeness/garbling context. The present fixed-group classification instead requires exact correctness and exact privacy simultaneously.

3. Keitaro Hiwatashi, [Negative Results on Information-Theoretic Additive Randomized Encodings](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-95764-2_6), ACNS 2025, pp. 136–157. The abstract separates multiparty perfect-privacy classification with statistical correctness from an l2-statistical security model for two parties. Its l2 feasibility classification expressly permits the OR and XOR function classes. Neither statement is the fixed-G exact correctness plus exact privacy classification used here.

4. Nir Bitansky, Saroja Erabelli, Rachit Garg and Yuval Ishai, [Shuffling is Universal](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1442/versions/20261117T152235Z.pdf), ePrint 2025/1442, November 17, 2026 revision (STOC 2026 main paper). Section 1.4 and Appendix B, Theorems B.1/B.2, now establish perfect correctness with arbitrarily small statistical privacy error when the group is allowed to vary. In particular AICR [c/0073](https://aicr.info/c/0073/) is not treated here as an unresolved feasibility question. This package gives a converse and an exact fixed-group classification, not a new statistical construction.

Independent checks: searches for exact XOR for odd-order abelian groups, convolution squares with disjoint supports, and Fourier sign multiplier principal minors did not identify a more general ARE theorem in the accessible primary material. Group-algebra reduction, cyclotomic arithmetic, and finite Fourier diagonalization are standard. The contributions claimed here are the odd-order obstruction allowing arbitrary complex coefficients in the algebraic lemma, its fixed-G exact Boolean corollary, and the explicit unrestricted quantitative OR inequality. None is a conclusion about the security of a deployed protocol.
