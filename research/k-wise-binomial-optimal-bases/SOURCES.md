Sections 3 and 5 give the count LP, dual bases and question numbering used here. Read §5.1, §5.2 including Remark 5.7 and Prop. 5.8, §5.3 Conj. 5.9, §5.4 Conjs. 5.10–11, §5.5 Eq. (123) and following growth question. The present phase results intentionally restrict the printed feasibility definition to the interior before taking closures, and specify the nondistinguished reflection via s→n−1−s. The nominal hypotheses here are even 2≤k≤n−1. The four-wise Msp conjecture specifies n≥6. No general rationality conjecture, individual-site transition conjectures, general-n monotonicity for k≥6, or quantitative claims on all objective values at general k are asserted.
* [Roy Hermann, *Reflection of optimal supports for k-wise independent bits*, arXiv:2609.25595v1 (September 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25595v1): Theorem 1 establishes interior sign-reflection and Lemma 2 proves uniqueness of the optimal count distribution, including degeneracies, using the dual polynomial. Corollary 3 proves local reflection of transitions, in particular the exceptional-point symmetry 5.11. His Eqs. (5)–(9) supply the cardinal-polynomial/size-reduction identities used here. He explicitly distinguishes these results from connectedness, lexicographic ordering and fully indexed reflection.

[Courtois, Denuit and Van Bellegem, *Discrete s-convex extremal distributions*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aml.2006.02.006), [open author working paper §§2–4](https://sites.uclouvain.be/IAP-Stat-Phase-V-VI/ISBApub/Actu/2004-11-WP-Courtois_Denuit_VanBellegem.pdf): general discrete moment extrema, adjacent integer pairs, Vandermonde weights and fixed-moment nonnegativity tests. Our parameter-center test is for existence on a whole positive-length binomial parameter phase and is not a claim of new support characterization for arbitrary moment data.
* [Boros–Prékopa (1989), Mathematics of Operations Research](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.14.2.317) provide general two-sided binomial-moment formulas. [Prékopa et al., Rutgers report 4-2001, §2, printed pp. 4–5](https://rutcor.rutgers.edu/Prekopa/pdf/04.pdf) expressly attribute sharp four-moment bounds and mention the lower-union formula after Eq. (8) without reproducing it. The complementary-failures union lower bound is the relevant direction: its moment sums for the present model are binom(n,j)(1−p)^j, and exchangeability lifts a moment-optimal count law. BEKK §1 explicitly reports the earlier all-p closed-form special cases k=2,3,n−1, rather than identifying a four-wise formula there. We read the 1989 publisher abstract and 2001 authors' discussion, not the complete 1989 text: we do not equate a particular unverified earlier formula to our two-center selector, and make no first-all-p-formula claim. [Kwerel's 1975 work](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/abs/most-stringent-bounds-on-the-probability-of-the-union-and-intersection-of-m-events-for-systems-partially-specified-by-s-1-s-2-sk-2-k-m/FC1D79CF03535C4CF0769BEFA825D808) treats the broader sharp union/intersection problem from partial aggregated information.
* [Infusino, Kuna, Lebowitz and Speer, *The truncated moment problem on ℕ₀*, J. Math. Anal. Appl. 452 (2017), §§6.1–6.2](https://cmsr.rutgers.edu/images/people/lebowitz_joel/publications/599.pdf) are particularly close: general moment data yield an interleaving with Gaussian quadrature sites (Proposition 6.3), at least one site pair obtained by exact Gauss-site rounding (Corollary 6.4), and a degree-reducing recursion. For degree four, Theorem 6.8, Eqs. (6.10)–(6.13) and Remark 6.9 give an explicit fixed-moment support selector by the two Gauss sites and two tilted means (with the integer-Gauss-node case separated). It applies here: any feasible quadrature on J minimizes the fourth moment among all integer laws with its first three moments, since its monic quartic annihilator is nonnegative on the entire integer lattice. Thus even constant-size determination of the four-wise count support at a *fixed p* is available by combining earlier methods; the two-center rule is an alternative organized by the cross-parameter phase list. This is different from determining which basis appears for *some p*, its motion across p, and sharp maximal spread in n.
* [Peled–Yadin–Yehudayoff, Random Structures Algorithms (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20329) compare the optimum value with its continuous moment relaxation, using adjacent-pair coalescence and bounds on polynomial expectations (open text, §4). The classical binomial orthogonal polynomials and their limiting normal orthogonal polynomials are not new; see [Minabutdinov, expansion of the Krawtchouk polynomials](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.07421). Here the objects located to bounded additive lattice error are the actual integer starts of *all feasible paired bases*, uniformly in p for fixed even k, rather than just zeros of classical polynomials or objective-value estimates.
