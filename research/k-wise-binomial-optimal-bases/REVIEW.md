# Independent internal mathematical review

Mathematical pass on the three frozen core manuscripts: PROOF.md `2262be085cf27c0fa23d6952261d672b8ff5e6bd9a693c9cd99ac63d84a616a0`, FOUR.md `924214193fdc5dd94225da03dc093e7b46ac9ea4daf9d01e6d820e8367de41e9`, SPREAD.md `f8e888e2bb27af499938cc50e20a93de626a8ed352451ccd521af1909ff9ef73`. This review is about optimal count distributions, not uniqueness of nonsymmetric bit-vector laws, and is not external peer review.

For the even-order optimal phases, at interior p the positive binomial weights bound all nonnegative dual evaluations in a sublevel set. An optimal vertex necessarily has n active at value 1 and k independent other active integer sites; the polynomial with its k roots is negative between each odd/even consecutive pair, so those roots must be adjacent integers. Complementary slackness and interpolation give uniqueness of the optimizing count law, including at a breakpoint, as already in Hermann. The factor n(1−p)/(n−s) in the reduced binomial interpolation is positive at interior p. The two adjacent cardinal factors give the exact inequalities a≤u(p)≤a+1, and the derivative in the logit is a strictly positive tilted variance (both adjacent sites remain possible). Thus each basis has one interior interval, with vanishing sites only low on exit and high on entry, including simultaneous vanishings. A finite number of singleton-only bases cannot fill open gaps; the closed phase intervals consecutively cover the parameter interval with nonoverlapping interiors. The common positive support is tiled backward before and forward after: even positive runs use no outside partner, odd runs shift all their pair starts by one. This proves monotone chronological and lexicographic order and the finite phase-count bound. The reflected start formula n−a−2 is consistent with the prior site map N−s; endpoint-only feasibility at p=1 and the point-mass requirement at p=0 are correctly distinguished from phase closures.

For four-wise phases, the binomial third central moment gives the covariance identity for both quadratics with center c. At the designated p0 all prefactors of E Q in the outer pair, and of −E R in the inner pair, are positive. In each respective couple one weight switches only down and the other only up, so a wrong sign at the joint testing point cannot be corrected elsewhere. At equality the admissible interior set is exactly a singleton, and the other pair has strictly positive weights. The central choice of u,v for d=floor sqrt(N−1) gives the two strict inequalities and integer range check (N≥5), proving the exact spread formula. At a four-wise exceptional transition the reduced quadrature supported on the two positive sites matches degree 3; its monic annihilator consequently obeys both moment identities, which by the covariance formula force the claimed rational parameter. No general exceptional rationality or exact-k monotonicity beyond k=4 follows.

For the location theorem at fixed order, centered binomial standardized moments differ from Gaussian moments by O(1/σ) uniformly at large σ using binomial cumulants. Normalizing tilted polynomial coefficients, any limiting grid-nonnegative polynomial is globally nonnegative because the mesh tends to zero on expanding intervals; its coefficient norm remains one, so its normal mean is positive. This supplies the uniform coarse location rather than assuming a limiting weight lower bound. In the bounded-σ case the earliest nonzero tilted site is ≤k−2, and the ratio to each successive site's mass is dominated by the stated convergent factorial series. Exact quadrature of degree 2m−1 controls the degree-m root polynomial tested against lower powers; the normal Gram matrix is positive definite, so each coefficient is O(1/σ) from the Hermite coefficient. Simple real roots then give constant error in unscaled lattice coordinates. Limiting spread follows from σ≤sqrt(N)/2 and the feasible closure phase containing p=1/2. The integer support of the earlier general quartic-moment optimization is distinct from this sharp all-parameter lattice phase test and maximum spread.
