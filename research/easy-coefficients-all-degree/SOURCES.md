# Source record and scope of the ECC

1. T. W. Cusick, [Weight recursions for any rotation symmetric Boolean functions](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2017.2785773), IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 64 (2018), 2962–2968; [author preprint arXiv:1701.06648](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.06648), §4.1, pp. 13–16 (January 2017 revision of the Mathematica algorithm). This is the exact general matrix we analyze. The original recurrence theorem concerns a minimal polynomial after discarding zero factors. The claim here instead identifies the nonzero characteristic multiset and thus its multiplicities. The supplied Python check independently implements the relevant transition formulas; the original Mathematica program and its text are not distributed.
2. A. Chirvasitu and T. W. Cusick, [Symbolic dynamics and rotation symmetric Boolean functions](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-022-00563-w), Cryptogr. Commun. 14 (2022), 1091–1115; [author preprint arXiv:1910.01908](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.01908), Corollary 2.9 and Conjecture 4.18, especially the ensuing paragraph. Their Corollary 2.9 already gives easy coefficients from an auxiliary finite-type shift matrix in arbitrary degree. Their conjecture's second sentence expressly singles out the quadratic original-matrix identification, and the ensuing paragraph predicts the same identification in all degrees based on experiments. Cusick's 2026 note refers to the general formulation in the journal version as Conjecture 6.1. We do not attribute the all-degree variant solely to the wording of the preprint's second sentence. The similarly numbered Conjecture 6.1 in their separate 2024 paper on quadratic functions is a different question about multiplicative orders, not treated here.
3. T. W. Cusick, [Using easy coefficients conjecture for rotation symmetric Boolean functions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ins.2023.120075), Inform. Sci. 659 (2024), 120075, [author's university publication record reproducing the abstract](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/using-easy-coefficients-conjecture-for-rotation-symmetric-boolean/). The abstract explicitly describes the conjecture using the algebraic multiplicities in the original rules matrix regardless of degree. The special case then proved was monomial quadratic.
4. T. W. Cusick, [Recursions for quadratic rotation symmetric functions weights](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2025.06.059), Discrete Appl. Math. 378 (2026), 93–101, [arXiv:2502.10864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10864); and [The Quadratic Easy Coefficients Conjecture via Finite-Type Shifts and Zeta Functions, arXiv:2609.01399](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01399), Sept. 2026, Theorem 1.1 and §§1–2, 5–6. The latter completes the quadratic-only match and proves quadratic nonsingularity. We credit the signed de Bruijn and trace approach; the new step here is identifying the higher-degree *original* rules-state transitions and controlling them via bounded forgetting. We do not claim that easy coefficients detached from the original matrix, or signed de Bruijn path enumeration itself, is new.
5. [The weights of cubic 3-rotation symmetric Boolean functions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X26001962), 2026. This treats special 3-step cubic families, not a proof for arbitrary-degree fixed lists of ordinary rotation generators. It is noted to distinguish a relevant 2026 keyword hit rather than to claim its results.

Searches through 2026-11-27 combined the full ECC name, Cusick/Chirvasitu, rules matrix, cubic/higher degree/all degrees, and transfer/de Bruijn/synchronizing terminology. No prior source identifying the general original-matrix characteristic multiset was located. This is a scoped priority assessment, not proof of priority.
