# Sources and claim boundary

1. Maxime Bombar, Nicolas Resch and Emiel Wiedijk, [On the Independence Assumption in Quasi-Cyclic Code-Based Cryptography, extended version](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35231/35231.pdf), ePrint 2025/018, manuscript dated 2025-01-05. §1.2, printed p. 6, paragraph “Concentration of noise weight,” asks for further theoretical evidence for concentration of the weight of \(t_1R_1+t_2R_2\) beyond the cited Chebyshev argument. Lemmas 3.1–3.3 give the parity, marginal and single-product expectation in §3. The [ISIT 2025 version](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISIT63088.2025.11195347) (pp. 294–299) also gives the parity marginal for sums in §IV; the open-direction anchor here is the extended version. This note supplies an arbitrary-fixed-support weight-tail guarantee in the independent-Bernoulli model, not a result on distributional independence.
2. Akinori Kawachi, [Hamming Weight of Product of Random Sparse Polynomials](https://www.ieice.org/publications/proceedings/summary.php?expandable=13&iconf=ISITA&number=C01-7&session_num=C01&year=2020), ISITA 2020, DOI 10.34385/proc.65.C01-7. The extended ePrint paper lists this 2020 item under the key Kaw24 and describes the variance/Chebyshev approach in §1.2 and following Lemma 3.3. The bounds here use the conditional fixed supports as explicit inputs and do not assert a uniform improvement over every covariance-based bound.
3. Dmitry Gavinsky, Shachar Lovett, Michael E. Saks and Srikanth Srinivasan, [A Tail Bound for Read-\(k\) Families of Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.1478), Random Structures & Algorithms 47(1), 99–108 (2015), DOI 10.1002/rsa.20532. Theorem 1.1 already gives the general upper and lower KL tails, and its repeated-variable example already shows generic tightness. [Finner's generalized Hölder inequality](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176989534) is a more general antecedent for the elementary product argument in (3). Neither the general inequality nor the Chernoff optimization is claimed as new. Here the cyclic overlap and coset realization identify exactly how the theorem applies.
4. Laila El Aimani, [Weight of Polynomial Products Mod \((X^n+1)\) – Application to the HQC Cryptosystem](https://opencryptology.org/2025/2180/versions/20261114T034642Z.pdf), ePrint 2025/2180, revision indexed as 2026-11-14. Its §1 contributions and §2.1 use covariance and Cantelli for the random-product/binomial and uniform-slice models, alongside earlier exact-distribution work cited there. That problem includes random factor supports and is not interchangeable with the conditional theorem here. We make no claim about its numerical or scheme-specific conclusions, and no conclusion for fixed-weight inputs.

No exhaustive priority claim is made.
