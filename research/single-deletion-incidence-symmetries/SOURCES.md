# Prior work, precise scope, and verification limits

**Problem.** [Francisco Pernice, On the Symmetries of the Deletion Channel, Allerton 2022, final conference PDF](https://allerton.csl.illinois.edu/files/2022/12/2022-114-paper_868.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/Allerton49937.2022.9929324), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03633). Definition II.3 specifies Bernoulli marks, II.4 includes variable output lengths, II.6 quantifies separately over an arbitrary output bijection, II.8 restricts the single global group to each input length; see also §IV.B around Conjecture IV.3 and Proposition IV.4. The conjecture's compact labeled statement is an orbit bound, its preceding discussion suggests exact group equality. The conjecture is about the nondegenerate channel; footnote 2 specifies the interior probability interval. The vacuous endpoints are discussed in PROOF.md. This note does not prove that row entropy is a complete invariant, and does not approximate capacity. Pernice explicitly attributes the general notion of a channel group to Polyanskiy.

**Essential existing theorem.** [P. L. Erdős, P. Sziklai, D. C. Torney, A finite word poset, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 8(2), #R8](https://doi.org/10.37236/1607) ([full text](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/EJC/Volume_8/PDF/v8i2r8.pdf)), Theorem 2. The paper was accepted in 2000 in the 2001 volume. It classifies P_n on **all** word lengths through n over arbitrary finite alphabets and describes the n=2 independent mixed-pair swaps. Its Lemma 1 reconstructs an individual word of length at least 3 from its distinct one-deletion outputs. These primary results must not be presented as new. The all-length channel result follows by identifying transition support with subsequence order. The fixed-input-length support result needs the elementary recovery of that order via length-n upper neighborhoods. The exactly-one-deletion question requires handling simultaneous unknown row and column labelings without the intervening ranks, which is not implied just by bottom-up reconstruction from known labels. [Ligeti and Sziklai, Automorphisms of subword-posets, Discrete Math. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2005.10.007) classify other, different posets (DNA-strand quotient and principal alternating-word ideals); these are not our two-rank incidence.

**Closest coding and metric literature.**
- [A. A. Kulkarni and N. Kiyavash, Nonasymptotic upper bounds for deletion correcting codes, IEEE TIT 59 (2013), §III.A](https://publish.illinois.edu/kiyavash/files/2015/06/Kulkarni_it_trans_2013.pdf) already introduced exactly the arbitrary-alphabet deletion-ball hypergraph (even for more than one deletion). The single-deletion incidence is not a new object. At n=2 distinct input labels ab and ba with the same neighborhood are retained here, as required by the channel; an ordinary simple hypergraph merging duplicate hyperedges loses these permutations.
- [Kulkarni, Kiyavash, Sreenivas, On the Varshamov–Tenengolts construction on binary strings, Discrete Math. 317 (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2013.11.003) ([author-hosted PDF](https://rsree.ise.illinois.edu/Coding_Information_Theory_files/1-s2.0-S0012365X13004524-main.pdf)), Lemma 2.1 and equation (8), use single-deletion indistinguishability and the common-insertion/common-deletion characterization in the binary setting.
- [Bar-Lev, Etzion, Yaakobi, On the size of balls and anticodes of small diameter under the fixed-length Levenshtein metric, IEEE TIT 69 (2023)](https://yaakobi.net.technion.ac.il/files/2024/02/77_Levenshtein_Ball_IT23.pdf) explicitly discuss the graph metric and neighborhoods: adjacent equal-length words share a one-deletion subsequence. We give the standard distance argument only to make the rigidity proof self-contained, not as new metric theory.
- [Ruth and Lladser, Levenshtein graphs: Resolvability, automorphisms & determining sets, Discrete Math. 346 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2022.113310) ([open full text](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10495517)), Definitions 1.1, Theorem 5.1, Lemma 5.6: all word lengths in an interval, including consecutive lengths as a special case, but the graphs include within-length substitution edges. They classify the automorphisms of this richer graph, and prove reconstruction from upper neighbors. Removing substitution edges can enlarge the group; indeed n=2, q≥3 has extra pair swaps in our incidence. Their whole group theorem therefore cannot be quoted verbatim for the two-rank deletion-only problem. On the unbounded edit graph, related generators were also studied by Houghten, Ashlock and Lenarz ([author's reference page](https://www.cosc.brocku.ca/~shoughten/binaryedit.html)).
- [Fazeli, Vardy, Yaakobi, Generalized Sphere Packing Bound](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.6496), §III.C, Theorem 7/Corollary 8, already use graph automorphisms for linear-program symmetry reduction. The paper treats bounds on deletion-correcting codes, but the theorem on automorphism averaging holds for generic groups/subgroups and is not an exhaustion theorem for the above bipartite deletion incidence.
- A nearby public [2026 deletion-channel entropy formalization](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Blueprint/D5/S3/Entropy/DeletionChannelEntropyOrdering.html) gives a conditional entropy ordering/counterexample in a selected ensemble. It is not a classification of the row/column permutation group. Pernice's [research page](https://sites.google.com/view/franciscopernice/research) lists the original work.

**Finite tests.** Run python -B -O verify.py. Starting from the bipartition only, individualization/refinement enumerates all isomorphisms of small exact support/mass graphs, then checks each bijection at its leaves. Tests cover all lengths 1–6 (binary), 1–4 (ternary), 1–3 (quaternary), both full support and only one-deletion support; n=0 full support; and eight weighted test cases. The distinct n=2 input labels are retained even when they have identical incidence. The separate exhaustive column-order tests cover 110,487 ordered word pairs. All checks explicitly raise on a mismatch; these are sanity checks, not a proof at arbitrary length or an external review.
