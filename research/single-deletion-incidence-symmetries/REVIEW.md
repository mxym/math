## Adversarial checks

1. Two distinct top words share an output exactly when they have a common subsequence one shorter. Two distinct bottom words share an input under exactly the same condition: delete both exceptional positions from their common superword, or merge the two single exceptional letters into a common subsequence. In the latter case, when the insertion gaps agree either order works. The fixed-length distance in the resulting graph is $m-\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)$: in a length-$t$ path at least $m-t$ original occurrences survive, and a longest marked common subsequence gives a path of that length by replacing the unmatched occurrences, preserving all previously matched placements. Each side map is therefore an isometry for this metric. Constants are identified as top degree-one words, and their lower singleton neighbors determine the corresponding bottom constants. Distance to a fixed constant is $m$ minus the number of its letters. Thus fixing the constants preserves the whole count vector on each side; there is no assumption that an unknown permutation is a permutation of positions.
2. In the binary alphabet, after this normalization, one-one words on the two sides induce a path with $2n-1$ vertices. Its only length-preserving actions are identity and simultaneous reversal; even when $n=2$ the middle vertex is fixed and the two endpoints may be exchanged. Fix the path pointwise. In the weight-$k$ ($k\ge 2$) top words, the positions of the ones are their nondecreasing preceding-zero counts. The pieces obtained by deleting one occurrence of a one constitute the **set** of single removals from a $k$-multiset, all in the same known zero count. If at least two different values occur, coordinatewise maximum of their multiplicity vectors returns the multiset. If all entries have one value, the nonempty remaining $(k-1)$-multiset returns that value as well. This latter clause fails at $k=1$, precisely the reason the path was needed. Once the top weight-$k$ words are fixed, adding a zero into any gap of a bottom word gives the complete family of $k+1$ possible incremented gap vectors. Their coordinatewise minima recover every original coordinate, including zero. The terminal $k=n$ has no lower vertex of weight $n$ and requires none. This addresses both labelings without circular induction.
3. For any two letters, count preservation restricts both maps *onto* the induced binary incidence. A word's projection to this pair is detected by its maximal LCS equality against all length-$n$ tests in that pair; among candidates of the same projected length, different words are incomparable. More generally if $u$ is not a subsequence of $w$, append repeated copies of a letter different from the first unmatched symbol of $u$ after greedy matching $w$. This cannot finish $u$, and supplies a length-$n$ distinguishing superword of $w$. This works for the empty projection and for no padding. As binary tests transform uniformly within the pair, their orientation carries over to projections of *all* top words, not just binary inputs.
4. If a triangle of letter pairs had a single reversed edge, order the blocks of its endpoints on either side of the remaining letter: the resulting pairwise orders form a directed cycle, which cannot be the first-occurrence order in any image. Two reversed edges reduce to one by reversing all three comparisons. The words with block sizes $(n-2,1,1)$ in every order exist for $n=3$ as well. Every pair sign is thus the same. All pair projections, with occurrences labeled within each character in order, determine the total order and hence the word. Bottom maps are then forced by distinct columns (or by the earlier three-superword reconstruction result cited below).
5. At $n=2$ an independent swap of $ab,ba$ is possible for each unordered pair. Relabeling letters conjugates the swap at $\{a,b\}$ to the swap at its image. Thus the first factor in the semidirect product is elementary abelian of rank $\binom q2$. The pair and induced input groups agree because no observable output columns are equal. At $n=1$ the input group is $S_q$, with the empty output fixed. For uniform single deletion the incidence generators preserve the actual number of deletion positions yielding the output; equality of probabilities implies preservation of the support, so no extra permutations are possible. For the separate optional strictly positive position weights, a one-exception word gives the individual weight when sent to a constant; this detects non-palindromic weights and a reversed index pairing proves sufficiency. No zero-weight claim follows.
6. For the **all-length** channel with interior rates, an output word's minimum input length over its positive column is its own length. This is invariant under the required length-preserving input bijection, proving that even an unrestricted output witness preserves length. The unique supported output the same length as its input is the input itself, so the two actions coincide and preserve subsequence order. The two choices supplied by the old word-poset theorem cannot vary with maximal word length after fixing the images of letters and of one mixed two-letter word. For just the inputs of length $n$ but all output lengths, reverse containment between sets of length-$n$ superwords recognizes the whole word order, by the greedy padding argument above. Positive symbol-dependent deletion probabilities restrict the alphabet permutation by evaluating the no-deletion event on a constant. All stated endpoint and low-rank qualifications are valid.

## What the sources actually establish

- [Erdős, Sziklai & Torney, *A finite word poset* (2001), Theorem 2 and Lemma 1](https://real.mtak.hu/9920/1/ErdosLSz-EJC01.pdf): all lengths up to $n$ and all subsequence comparabilities; the word of length at least three is determined by its *labeled* distinct immediate predecessors. The latter alone cannot pin both unlabelled rank permutations. At $n=2$, the entire nonempty full poset already has the two required ranks. No novelty at this endpoint. Their explicit list of independent pair flips with arbitrary alphabet permutations gives the natural semidirect product by conjugation.
- [Pernice, *On the Symmetries of the Deletion Channel* (2022 Allerton), Definitions II.5–II.6 and §IV.B](https://allerton.csl.illinois.edu/files/2022/12/2022-114-paper_868.pdf): the formal IV.3 asks for binary orbit size at most four; the preceding discussion proposes the four actions and their orbits. The deletion channel in this question is the all-length channel, not a fixed-length experiment conditioned on one deletion. Proposition IV.4 concerns input actions restricted to index permutations; the paper also reports numerical entropy tests. The four-element full-channel statement for interior rates is a short application of the 2001 result. It is not a new classification of the full subsequence poset, an entropy converse, or a capacity computation.
- [Ruth & Lladser, *Levenshtein graphs: resolvability, automorphisms & determining sets* (2023), Theorem 5.1](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10495517): this does include length windows $[n-1,n]$, but with **within-rank substitution edges**. Deleting these edges can enlarge the group: at $n=2$, $q=3$, the two orders of one pair can be swapped alone in the pure deletion incidence, giving order 48 rather than 12. Thus the result itself cannot be applied to the weakened observation without an additional argument. Their demonstration of rigidity uses distances supplied by substitutions (e.g. Eqs. (9)–(10) and the resolving set); that proof does not simply apply unchanged. Lemma 5.6 already states that three distinct immediate superwords determine a word, a prior stronger version of the bottom-label separation once top labels are fixed.
- [Kulkarni, Kiyavash & Sreenivas, *On the Varshamov–Tenengolts construction on binary strings* (2014), Lemma 2.1 and (8)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2013.11.003) already explicitly identify the binary common-superword and common-subword adjacency relations and the derived single-deletion confusability graph. [Bar-Lev, Etzion & Yaakobi, *On the size of balls and anticodes of small diameter under the fixed-length Levenshtein metric* (2023)](https://yaakobi.net.technion.ac.il/files/2024/02/77_Levenshtein_Ball_IT23.pdf) give the standard metric and anticode-size results, not a classification of the support-incidence automorphisms.
- The older [Houghten, Ashlock & Lenarz, *Bounds on optimal edit metric codes*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228762620_Bounds_on_optimal_edit_metric_codes), Theorem 1, likewise concerns the **full edit graph**, including replacements. Searches for rank-selected subword posets, single-deletion hypergraph and channel symmetry, FLL isometry groups, the published conjecture and subsequent papers did not locate an explicit earlier all-$q,n$ classification of the bare two-rank incidence. This is bounded evidence, not proof of priority or a claim to classify every automorphism of the projected fixed-length confusability graph.

## Independent finite replay

The accompanying standard-library program constructs raw unweighted incidence directly by deleting each position, then computes the number of color-preserving automorphisms by individualized simultaneous equitable refinement **without** any word or alphabet invariants. Each completed vertex bijection is checked against the actual adjacency sets, with the expected count used only at the end. All 19 counts agree, covering binary $n=1,\dots,9$, ternary $n=1,\dots,6$, and quaternary $n=1,\dots,4$. These numerical checks do not replace the general argument.
