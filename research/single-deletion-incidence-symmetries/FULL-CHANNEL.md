# The deletion-channel group from its support

The central automorphism classification for the full finite word poset is a prior theorem. We give the short channel corollary and a fixed-input-length support reduction. The [main theorem](PROOF.md) treats the more restrictive observation scheme in which only single-deletion incidence is retained.

**Convention and question.** Let Σ be a finite alphabet of cardinality q≥2. Write u≼v when u is a (not necessarily contiguous) subsequence of v, ε for the empty word, P_n for all words of lengths 0 through n, and R for word reversal. For a nondegenerate memoryless deletion channel each occurrence of a∈Σ is deleted independently with a fixed probability d_a strictly between zero and one. Every retained index set has positive probability, so W(y|x)>0 iff y≼x. This includes ε and the unchanged word x.

Pernice, [Allerton 2022](https://allerton.csl.illinois.edu/files/2022/12/2022-114-paper_868.pdf), Defs. II.6 and II.8, asks for length-preserving **input** bijections g for which an arbitrary output bijection h makes h Ch(gx) equal in law to Ch(x) for every finite x. Write b=h⁻¹, so W(by|gx)=W(y|x). The conjecture immediately before IV.3 asks if the four group elements generated by binary complement and reversal exhaust the symmetries; the labeled IV.3 asks the weaker orbit bound 4. For a fixed interior homogeneous deletion probability both hold; see §IV.B, especially footnote 2 for the intended parameter interval. This does not address the capacity or characterize coincident row entropies.

**The already known word theorem.** [P. L. Erdős, P. Sziklai and D. C. Torney, A finite word poset, EJC 8(2), #R8, Thm 2](https://doi.org/10.37236/1607) explicitly identifies the automorphisms of the entire P_n for n>2 as uniform alphabet permutations followed optionally by reversal. These commute; each decomposition is unique. At n=2 there is one independent swap ab↔ba per unordered pair of distinct letters in addition to the alphabet permuting. In modern notation the group at n=2 is (C₂)^{q choose 2} ⋊ S_q; alphabet permutations act on the swap coordinates. In particular, the binary case n=2 already has four elements. P₁ has group S_q, P₀ the trivial group.

**All-length application.** For any y, |y| is the minimum input length among x with y in the support of Ch(x). Hence b preserves length since g does and g is bijective. For a given x the only length-|x| element in its support is x, so bx=gx. Thus g preserves subsequence order in both directions. Restrict to P_n for all n≥3 and apply the attributed theorem. The alphabet permutation is fixed by the action at rank one, and after removing it the reversal choice is fixed by the image of a single word ab, a≠b. The same pair of choices therefore works for every input length. Conversely for the homogeneous channel each alphabet permutation and reversal permute retained-position sets and preserve their probabilities, and hence preserve transitions with all their embedding multiplicities. The global binary group is C₂×C₂, with orbit {x, complement(x), Rx, complement(Rx)} after repetitions removed. Its induced action has size 1,2,4 at n=0, n=1, n≥2 respectively (as a group, not necessarily as each individual orbit). At d=0 the channel is the identity and at d=1 it is constant; in each case every length-preserving input bijection qualifies. The orbit bound then fails for n≥3, so no endpoint claim is possible.

**Finite data with two independent permutations.** Restrict the inputs to exactly Σⁿ and the observable outputs to P_n. Allow any input permutation g of Σⁿ and any output permutation b of P_n, not initially constrained to preserve output length, to satisfy
     
    y≼x  iff  by≼gx   (x in Σⁿ, y in P_n).

Define C_n(y) to be the length-n inputs containing y. For arbitrary u,w in P_n,
     
    u≼w  iff  C_n(w) ⊆ C_n(u).                                (1)

The forward implication is transitivity. For its converse, if u is not a subsequence of w, the greedy algorithm for matching u left-to-right in w matches its longest attainable prefix and stops short at the next required letter a. (Inductively its chosen index for the j-th match is the earliest possible.) Append n−|w| copies of any letter other than a to w. This is a length-n word containing w but still not u. The empty prefix, zero padding, and an already full length w are allowed. In particular all the C_n(y) are distinct.

Now g maps C_n(y) onto C_n(by), so (1) makes b an automorphism of P_n. Its preservation of lengths also follows from lengths being ranks in this poset. On top words C_n(x)={x}, whence b(x)=g(x). Conversely any poset automorphism gives such a pair by restriction. Thus all possible pairs, the input group, and the full poset automorphism group are canonically isomorphic (uniqueness of the output map given g follows from distinct columns). The three groups are as listed above, including the n=0 and n=1 degeneracies. If arbitrary longer output labels are formally included in a fixed-n model, those are zero columns; their permutations do not belong to the observable pair group and never enlarge its induced input group.

For the weighted channel with rates d_a and n≥3, the support bound implies that g is an alphabet permutation π followed optionally by R. Using aⁿ as both input and output gives (1−d_a)ⁿ=(1−d_{π(a)})ⁿ and hence d_a=d_{π(a)}. These conditions suffice: a permutation preserving rates preserves each mask probability, and reversal reverses the mask without altering the product probability. Thus the group is the product of the permutations of the individual equal-rate classes, times a central C₂ generated by reversal. The same argument works at binary n=2 and in the all-length model. At n=2 over an arbitrary alphabet the independent pair reversals also preserve transitions: for mixed input ab, reversing ab swaps positions but not the probabilities to ε, a and b, with the full word transported simultaneously. Thus replace S_q by the rate-preserving subgroup in the semidirect product above. At n=1 only the rate-preserving alphabet permutations remain; at n=0 there is no input-rate constraint and the observable group is trivial.

**Attribution and limitations.** The all-length upper bound is a short application of the 2001 theorem, not a new theorem on word-poset automorphisms. Pernice already singled out the generators and treated coordinate-permutation symmetries; this proof neither reconstructs the deletion probability from capacity nor solves a coding rate problem. The case of full output support and the case in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) have different observations and must not be conflated.
