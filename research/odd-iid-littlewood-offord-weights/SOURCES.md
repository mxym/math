# Original questions, prior results, and scope

1. Tomas Juškevičius and Valentas Kurauskas, [*On the Littlewood–Offord problem for arbitrary distributions*, Random Structures & Algorithms 58 (2021), 370–380](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20977). [Authors' manuscript, §5, Conjecture 1, p. 11](https://web.vu.lt/mif/v.kurauskas/files/2020/08/general_200704.pdf); [full arXiv version, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08770v2). Their statement compares nonzero real coefficients of iid lattice vectors with sign coefficients of the **same law**. Corollary 1(b) already proves exact sign optimality for even $n$, with balanced signs. Their §4 shows how Bernoulli biases can favor different sign proportions. Their §5 Conjecture 2 concerns a different reference law under an atom cap. Neither that conjecture nor their proved even theorem is refuted here.
2. Mihir Singhal, [*Erdős–Littlewood–Offord problem with arbitrary probabilities*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0012365X22002114), [author's preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.02886), Theorem 1.2, solves the common Bernoulli-law problem: some signs always suffice, for every $p,n$. Lemma 3.2 proves the small-$p$ balanced regime by the binomial product used in our §1. We give that argument for self-containment and to see uniqueness. The problem of optimizing the Bernoulli sign count is not the same as proving a non-Bernoulli fixed-law counterexample. The new mechanism is to combine local optima for two biases within one integer and use a nonunit coefficient to shift a term between digit positions.
3. Vsevolod Lev, [*On the Number of Solutions of a Linear Equation over Finite Sets*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1998.2878), compares representation counts when the underlying sets can vary with their cardinalities fixed; suitably balanced arithmetic progressions attain the uniform-size bound. The uniform result in this note compares coefficients on the **same fixed set** for all independent samples, and that set need not be an interval. Constructions with digit blocks to separate linear totals have much older precedents; for example [Hegarty on the base-expansion method for sum-dominant sets](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/83466) studies sizes of sum/difference sets, not maximum probabilities of individual totals. The random selection in [UNIFORM.md](UNIFORM.md) uses the classical [bounded-differences inequality of McDiarmid](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781107359949.008).
4. Valentas Kurauskas, [*An asymptotically optimal bound for the concentration function of a sum of independent integer random variables*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11043v1), gives a multiplicatively asymptotic estimate under separate maximum-atom caps and formulates a different exact conjecture (Conjecture 1.3, attributed to Juškevičius in 2023). That comparison can replace the input law by a cap-dependent interval law. Our result varies the coefficients while preserving precisely the original one-dimensional law, not merely its cap. The repository's [three-summand cap theorem](../three-summand-point-concentration/README.md) and the present counterexample are therefore compatible.
