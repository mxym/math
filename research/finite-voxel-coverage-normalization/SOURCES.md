# Sources and comparison

* [Penrose and Yang, *Random coverage of a manifold with boundary*, v2, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2) give the full-boundary weak law in Theorem 2.1 and the interior law in Proposition 2.11. Remark 2.4 asks for an appropriate transformation for boundary-touching intermediate targets where the conventional full-boundary normalization degenerates. Their [revision history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19278) records the July 2026 version. In the present theorem the ambient body is Euclidean and smooth and the target has positive volume; intermediate contact requires no shape classification, but the output transform is algorithmic rather than an explicit geometric affine limit.
* [Janson, *Random coverings in several dimensions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02399201), and [Penrose, *Random Euclidean coverage from within*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-022-01182-5) established foundational covering laws. [Penrose–Higgs, *Random coverage from within with variable radii, and Johnson–Mehl cover times*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10687-026-00529-8), including its spatial independence approximations, concerns coverage with random growth radii, not an oracle formula for every fixed positive-volume subtarget. [Higgs–Penrose–Yang](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11009-025-10165-7) cover a second random point sample. Neither full coverage nor a limit for a randomly sampled second target automatically controls every point of an irregular fixed target at the fluctuation scale.
* General blocking, localization and Poisson approximations are not new. For example, [Ferreira, Pereira and Martins](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00637) use local dependence conditions to study maxima and clustering of spatial random fields. [*Covering a compact space by fixed-radius or growing random balls*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12671) gives concentration estimates in general compact metric spaces, not this computable transform. The steps specific here are the offset-averaged deterministic local functional, fine discretization controlled by a uniform level-band estimate for arbitrary target subsets, Poisson truncation, and deterministic quantile inversion, not the general independence principle or the Gumbel distribution.
* Historical searches and a separate July–19 November 2026 screen covered the original authors and versions, intermediate targets, rare spatial blocks, local Poisson/clump approximation for Boolean coverage, discretization of complete coverage, and nonlinear deterministic transforms of covering radii. The recent [Penrose record paper, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19104v3#S2) studies nested-sample record times, a different observable. No identical theorem was located in the checked material; indices, date filters and accessible full texts do not certify novelty.
