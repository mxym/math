# A finite local normalization for covering an arbitrary fixed target of positive volume

Let $d\ge2$ and $k\ge1$ be fixed. Sample $n$ independent uniform points in a compact smooth Euclidean domain $A$; rescale to $|A|=1$. Let $B\subset A$ be any compact set of positive Lebesgue measure. It may touch the boundary on any set and need not itself have any boundary regularity. The radius $R_n$ is the maximum over $b\in B$ of its $k$th closest sample distance. We give a nonlinear, deterministic normalization for $R_n$; it uses only voxel volumes of $A$ and voxel intersections with $B$, not the distribution of $R_n$ or a simulation. Unlike a contact-profile formula it requires quasipolynomially many operations. This is a localization result, not a claim that the elementary affine normalization usually sought always exists.

Write $\omega_d$ for unit ball volume. Set $h=(\log n/n)^{1/d}$, $\alpha=(1/(4\omega_d))^{1/d}$, $\beta=(8/\omega_d)^{1/d}$, and use thresholds $r\in[\alpha h,\beta h]$. Set $a=\lceil\beta\rceil+3$, $H=2a+1$, $D=10+2\cdot5^d H^d$, $J=\lceil n^D\rceil$, $\delta=h/J$, and $L=\lceil\log\log n\rceil$ (all constructions are for large $n$). Fix an axis-aligned microscopic grid of side $h$ and subdivide it into fine voxels of side $\delta$, using the same origin and half-open cells. For each of the $L^d$ offsets of the microscopic grid modulo $L$, group its cubes into coarse blocks $C$ of $L^d$ cubes; the underlying microscopic and fine grids themselves never move. Let $W_C$ be the union of the block and its $a$ layers of neighboring cubes in the maximum-coordinate distance. Only blocks meeting a fixed bounding box of $A$ matter. For every fine voxel $v$ put $w_v=|v\cap A|$, and mark it if $v\cap B\ne\varnothing$. Write $z_v$ for its center and $V_C=\sum_{v\subset W_C}w_v$. Put
$$K_n=\left\lceil10e\left[(L+2a)^d+1\right]\log n\right\rceil.$$

Here is the complete deterministic finite functional. For a list $v_1,\ldots,v_j\subset W_C$ let $I_C(r;v_1,\ldots,v_j)$ be one if any marked $v\subset C$ has fewer than $k$ list entries with $\|z_{v_i}-z_v\|\le r$ (including multiplicities), and zero otherwise. An unmarked block has value zero even for $j=0$. Compute
$$
 \widehat p_C(r)= e^{-nV_C}\sum_{j=0}^{K_n}\frac{n^j}{j!}
 \sum_{v_1,\ldots,v_j\subset W_C}
 w_{v_1}\cdots w_{v_j} I_C(r;v_1,\ldots,v_j),\qquad
 \widehat\Lambda_n(r)=\frac1{L^d}\sum_{\text{offsets}}\sum_C \widehat p_C(r). \tag{1}
$$
Empty products have value one. There are only polynomially many fine voxels at fixed $d,A$; $K_n=O_d(\log n(\log\log n)^d)$. Thus direct enumeration at a specified $r$ takes at most $\exp(O_{d,A,k}((\log n)^2(\log\log n)^d))$ arithmetic, comparison and elementary exponential operations with exact voxel volume/intersection oracles. This is a real arithmetic operation count, not a bit complexity assertion or an efficient practical numerical recommendation. The functional adds rare *local* configurations of polylogarithmically many points, not configurations of the full $n$-point sample.

For any deterministic $r_n$ in the above window with $\widehat\Lambda_n(r_n)\to \tau\in(0,\infty)$,
$$\mathbb P(R_n\le r_n)\ \longrightarrow\ e^{-\tau}. \tag{2}$$
In particular the monotone transformation $-\log\widehat\Lambda_n(R_n)$ converges to the standard Gumbel law. It can be extended constantly outside the threshold window (and also clipped to finite values diverging at the ends), without affecting the limit. The result also holds for a Poisson sample of mean $n$.

## Spatial decomposition

First use Poisson intensity $n$ on $A$. Let $E_i$ mean that some target in microscopic cube $i$ is not $k$-covered at $r$. Write $p_i=\mathbb P(E_i)$ and $M=\sum_i p_i$. This event reads only the cube buffered by $a$ layers, a cube of side $Hh$. There are $O_A(h^{-d})$ relevant cubes. At any target center a ball of small radius $s$ meets $A$ in volume at least $\omega_d s^d/3$ uniformly, by the smooth tangent half-space estimate and compactness. Nets within the target in a single cube at mesh $r/2$ have bounded size (fixed $d,\alpha$). A failed center implies fewer than $k$ points in a net-point ball of radius $r/2$. Therefore, uniformly on the window, $\max_i p_i\le n^{-c_d}$ for large $n$ and some $c_d>0$; the fixed polylogarithmic factor in the Poisson low-count bound is absorbed here.

Write $p_C=\mathbb P(\bigcup_{i\subset C}E_i)$ and let $\Lambda$ denote the average over offsets of $\sum_Cp_C$. Put $m_C=\sum_{i\subset C}p_i$, so $\max m_C\le L^d n^{-c_d}=o(1)$. Color cubes by indices modulo $2a+2$ in each coordinate; one of $F=(2a+2)^d$ colors within a block contributes at least $m_C/F$, and the events within this color inspect disjoint buffers. Consequently
$$m_C/(2F)\le 1-e^{-m_C/F}\le p_C\le m_C,\qquad M/(2F)\le\Lambda\le M . \tag{3}$$
The first inequality holds for all sufficiently large $n$. For offset $s$, remove the microscopic cubes within $a$ layers of a face of their coarse block, and let $m_s^*$ be the sum of their $p_i$. Each microscopic cube is removed for at most $O_d(a/L)$ of the offsets. Hence $\operatorname{avg}_s m_s^*\le C_d(a/L)M$. The no-failure events in the resulting cores of all blocks are independent for fixed $s$. If $M$ is bounded, their product differs from $\exp(-\sum_C p_C)$ by $O(m_s^*+M\max_Cm_C)$ (use the sum of removed probabilities and $\log(1-p)=-p+O(p^2)$). Restoring missing cubes changes the global probability by at most $m_s^*$. Write $P_0=\mathbb P(\bigcap_i E_i^c)$. For a shift with $m_s^*=o(1)$ these bounds give $P_0\ge e^{-M}-o(1)$. Taking logs, now Lipschitz on the common bounded-away-from-zero range for $P_0$ and $\exp(-\sum_Cp_C)$, and averaging over *all* shifts proves
$$-\log P_0=\Lambda+o(1)\quad\text{if }\Lambda=O(1). \tag{4}$$
This argument does not assume that a spatial cluster of holes has a unique center or that its location is uniformly distributed.

## Uniform microscopic anti-concentration

For any nonempty target subset $T$ of one microscopic cube and $j\ge k$ define $\Phi(\mathbf x)$ as the supremum over $\overline T$ of its $k$th distance to sites $x_1,\ldots,x_j$. Strict failure on $T$ is the same as $\Phi>r$. Consider configurations restricted to a single cube $W$ of side $Hh$ per coordinate point. For all real $s$ and $e>0$,
$$ |\{\mathbf x\in W^j:|\Phi(\mathbf x)-s|\le e\}|
 \le C_d j\,5^{dj} \,(e/(Hh))\,|W|^j . \tag{5}$$
To see this, choose a maximizing target $b$ for a configuration. For each point choose a direction from a Euclidean unit-sphere net of chord mesh one, cardinality at most $5^d$, making dot product at least $1/2$ with the unit direction from $b$ to that point (arbitrary if equal). Such a net follows by packing disjoint radius-$1/2$ balls around a maximal separated family. In the resulting product direction $\mathbf v$, each distance from $b$ increases by at least $t/2$ under $\mathbf x\mapsto\mathbf x+t\mathbf v$, for all $t\ge0$, by projection onto the initial unit direction. Thus $\Phi(\mathbf x+t\mathbf v)\ge\Phi(\mathbf x)+t/2$. In each line parallel to this fixed $\mathbf v$, configurations in the level band for which this inequality holds have diameter at most $4e\sqrt j$ in Euclidean arclength: any two ordered points are separated by at most $4e$ in $t$. One may define these measurable qualifying sets using the inequality for all nonnegative rational $t$ and continuity. The perpendicular projection of a cube of side $Hh$ in $\mathbb R^{dj}$ has $(dj-1)$-volume at most $\sqrt{dj}(Hh)^{dj-1}$: project the forward coordinate faces, using the sum of the absolute unit-normal components. Fubini and the finite product-direction union yield (5).

For the Poisson sample in $W\cap A$, the probability of this band, with at least $k$ points, is bounded by summing (5) times $n^j/j!$, dropping the density factor $e^{-n|W\cap A|}\le1$. Uniformly for $e=C_d\delta$ it is at most
$$ C'_d(\delta/h)(1+5^d n|W|)\exp(5^d n|W|)=O(n^{-6}), \tag{6}$$
since $n|W|=H^d\log n$. The bound also holds for all intensities $n(1+O(n^{-1/3}))$. If fewer than $k$ points occur, take the local radius as infinity; finite bands ignore these outcomes. No regularity or metric complexity of $T$ entered the bound.

## Explicit evaluation, fixed sample, and quantiles

In each microscopic cube, round every target in it and each sample site in its buffered cube to the center of its fine voxel. All distances change by at most $2\sqrt d\,\delta$, hence so does any finite local threshold defined using those sites. The buffer contains every possible relevant rounded or unrounded site for all radii in the window, with room to spare. With fewer than $k$ sites, failure for a nonempty target is unchanged. By (6), the probability of a changed indicator in that microscopic cube is $O(n^{-6})$, uniformly over deterministic $r$ in the window. Upon taking unions per coarse block and summing, each microscopic cube contributes only once per offset; the discrepancy in the mean local sum is $o(1)$.

The probability of a rounded coarse failure using the full Poisson sample from $W_C\cap A$ is given by (1) with the sum extending to infinity, by the Poisson law of total mass and iid voxel selection proportional to volume. Its mean number of sites is at most $(L+2a)^d\log n$. Truncation at $K_n$ costs $O(n^{-6})$ per block by the Poisson Chernoff bound, still $o(1)$ summed globally. In particular
$$\sup_{\alpha h\le r\le\beta h}|\widehat\Lambda_n(r)-\Lambda(r)|=o(1).\tag{7}$$
If $\widehat\Lambda_n(r)$ is bounded, (3),(4),(7) give (2) for Poisson input. At intensities $n_\pm=n\pm n^{2/3}$ the same exact local formula applies with the same grids and radius. The ratios of the summand density weights for $n_\pm$ and $n$ are $1+o(1)$ uniformly over all blocks and $j\le K_n$, since their log ratios are $O(n^{-1/3}(K_n+(L+2a)^d\log n))=o(1)$. Thus their truncated sums are between $1\pm o(1)$ times the original (including any zero sums); (6),(7) continue to hold for them. Monotonicity in sample size and Poisson concentration then imply (2) for iid samples.

At $\alpha h$, pack at least a constant times $|B|h^{-d}$ disjoint radius-$\alpha h$ balls centered in $B$ by a maximal separated set and the union volume bound for double-radius balls. Each such sampling ball is empty with independent probability at least $n^{-1/4}$ for Poisson input. Thus $P_0\to0$, implying $\Lambda(\alpha h)\to\infty$ by (3),(4) on every bounded subsequence. At $\beta h$, a net at mesh $\beta h/\log n$ within the target in each microscopic cube costs $O((\log n)^d)$ per cube. Each shrunken ball there has mean at least $(8/3-o(1))\log n$. This shows $M(\beta h)\to0$, hence $\Lambda(\beta h)\to0$. For each fixed $n$, the exact $\Lambda$ is continuous on the window: bands in each of finitely many microscopic thresholds have probability tending to zero as $e\downarrow0$ by (5) and the convergent Poisson series, and a coarse-block threshold is a maximum of such local thresholds on the window. Therefore all jumps of the nonincreasing $\widehat\Lambda_n$ on the window are $o(1)$ by (7). Every positive level is approximated there to $o(1)$ for large $n$. Bracket any desired level $e^{-x}$ by deterministic thresholds approximating $e^{-x+\epsilon}$ and $e^{-x-\epsilon}$, apply (2), and send $\epsilon\downarrow0$ to obtain the claimed Gumbel law for the monotone transform. There is no claim of a universal simple centering, rate, limiting point process, polynomial-time bit algorithm, or a result for null-volume targets.
