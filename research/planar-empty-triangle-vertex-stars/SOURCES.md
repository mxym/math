# Versions, earlier claims, and search scope (19 November 2026)

**The rate was stated before.** Reitzner–Temesvari, [*Stars of Empty Simplices*, August 2018 v1, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08734v1), stated order \(n^{d-1}\) for the expectation of the largest one-vertex star without excluding the plane. Reitzner's [Oberwolfach 2018 report, pp. 3265–3266](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46776?nt=1), likewise announced the rate without the planar exception. Neither the order conjectured here nor the idea of examining the star conditional on its anchor is new.

**What changed.** In the v1 proof, pp. 10–11, the displayed spherical integral involves an inverse sine power \(l+2\). For two planar triangles sharing an edge the off-diagonal case is \(d=2,l=0\): the angular integral with integrand \(\sin^{-2}\phi\) on the circle diverges, and the cited lemma itself assumes a power less than \(d\). The [May 2019 v2, Theorem 1.5(2), discussion immediately below, and Eq. (19)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08734v2) treats this case separately, proving a linear lower bound and an \(O(n\sqrt{\log n})\) upper bound. The authors explicitly leave the necessity of the logarithm open; Conjecture 1.6 includes the linear planar rate. The [October 2023 lead-author CRM slides, slide 24/26](https://www.crm.cat/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Matthias_Reitzner_Empty_Simplices.pdf), likewise distinguish the two dimension ranges. The [2024 journal article](https://doi.org/10.1215/00192082-11081246) was checked via [TU Wien's institutional bibliographic record and abstract](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/196465), which explicitly attributes a sharp one-vertex order only to \(d\ge3\). The final full journal body was not accessible in this comparison; all precise theorem/equation numbering here refers to the identified preprint version, not a claim about unchanged journal text.

**Different statistics.** RT v2 Theorem 1.1 treats the maximum over facets, i.e. common *edges* in the plane. [Bárány–Marckert–Reitzner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3928) and [Temesvari](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07481) are earlier common-edge/common-facet work. The total number of empty triangles supplies a linear lower bound via the mean degree over vertices, not an upper bound for the maximum. Classical variance tensorization is the standard Efron–Stein tool; the proof uses it twice and derives its fourth-moment consequence explicitly.

**Later-work gate.** Checked both all-date and July–19 November 2026 searches, including the title, author names, common-vertex and maximum-degree formulations, anchor counting, planar empty-triangle incidence and the logarithmic gap. Rechecked the [version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.08734) and the original sources above. [Andričík et al., August 2026 v2, Theorems 1.1–1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08762v2) count deterministic planar holes under exclusion of larger holes, not the random vertex maximum. [Bhattacharya–Das–Islam–Sen, Theorems 1–2, Computational Geometry 130 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comgeo.2025.102197) address the change in the total number of empty simplices under point deletion. They do not give this uniform anchor fourth-moment estimate or the resulting linear maximum. No later matching proof was located in the sources reviewed; terminology, indexing, inaccessible work or independent results may limit this search.
