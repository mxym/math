# Primary sources, overlap and scope

1. **The conjecture.** Pavel Exner, [*An optimization problem for finite point interaction families*](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ab3d82), *J. Phys. A: Math. Theor.* **52** (2019), 405302. In [§2, Eqs. (2.2)–(2.3), and §5, Conjecture 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.01229v2#S5), the interval has periodic boundary conditions and length 2π; the term **plus** α times the total squared site trace defines the repulsive model for α>0. Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 treat small and large coupling, respectively. For a non-equidistant set the conjectured strict inequality concerns every finite positive coupling. Theorem 2.1 already deals with attractive interactions. Sections 3–4 concern a particle in higher-dimensional ambient space, a different problem.
2. **Contemporary statement of status.** Exner, [*An optimization problem for δ′ leaky stars*](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15771v1#S2), October 2026, §2, Proposition 2.3 and Remark 2.2(a), explicitly describes the general repulsive **δ** comparison as open and records local optimality. Its Eq. (2.3) uses a **minus** sign in the form, so the repulsive coefficient there is negative, unlike the 2019 convention used here. The regular configuration's trigonometric secular equation is classical; see also Remark 2.2(b). We make no claim about the paper's δ′ star or δ′ loop questions.
3. **Substantial earlier tools.** Gustav Karreskog, Pavel Kurasov and Isak Trygg Kupersmidt, [*Schrödinger operators on graphs: symmetrization and Eulerian cycles*](https://doi.org/10.1090/proc12784), *Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.* **144** (2016), 1197–1207. [Authors' preprint, Eq. (32)](https://staff.math.su.se/kurasov/PDFARCHIV/2015-Report3.pdf) gives the upper comparison with the flower graph obtained by identifying all vertices, with the **same individual edge lengths** and the total coupling concentrated at the resulting vertex. This directly supplies a short route to the present result. On the flower the positive fundamental frequency t is the unique root before π/max(l_j) of
   \[
   2t\sum_j\tan(tl_j/2)=N\alpha.
   \]
   Indeed the normalized cosine arcs in the proof live on each petal, and their endpoint derivatives sum to this value; the positive eigenfunction gives the lowest eigenvalue. Jensen at *this flower frequency* gives \(N\alpha\ge2tN\tan(tL/(2N))\), strictly for unequal lengths. By monotonicity of the regular frequency equation the flower, and hence the original loop, then has energy strictly below the regular value. Thus the conjecture is an elementary consequence of earlier comparison machinery, rather than evidence of a new spectral principle.
4. Pavel Kurasov and Andrea Serio, [*Optimal Potentials for Quantum Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00023-019-00783-6), *Ann. Henri Poincaré* **20** (2019). Their [full text](https://d-nb.info/1183221843/34) treats optimization of potentials and coupling distribution on a **fixed** metric graph. Theorem 6.1 identifies the corresponding flower supremum, Eqs. (18)–(19) feature the cosine arcs and tangent sums in their positive regular/singular problem, and Example 8.1, Eqs. (49)–(50), calculates a regular path configuration with the related scalar tangent equation. These structures are prior. The position problem varies the individual lengths with fixed total length and equal fixed coupling.
