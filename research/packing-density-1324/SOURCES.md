# Prior work and scope of the comparison

- W. Stromquist, [*Packing Layered Posets Into Posets*](https://walterstromquist.com/POSETS.DOC), 1993. The original memorandum proves layered replacement and uses a last-block recursion for 132. Its final section explicitly distinguishes 1324 as unresolved. M. Albert, M. Atkinson, C. Handley, D. Holton and W. Stromquist, [*On packing densities of permutations*](https://doi.org/10.37236/1622), EJC 9 (2002), Theorem 2.2, Proposition 2.10 and §4, give the published replacement result, an obstruction to bounded layer counts including this case, and a discussion of the limiting structure. Layered attainability, unbounded layer count, and the geometric ratio for the shorter pattern 132 are not results claimed here.
- A. L. Price, *Packing densities of layered patterns*, Ph.D., University of Pennsylvania, 1997. Price's optimization over prescribed layer counts and its 1324 approximation predate this note. [Stromquist’s 2005 presentation](https://walterstromquist.com/DensitiesAtAltitude.ppt), slides “1324” and “Open Problems,” attributes approximate layer proportions and a density near .244 to Price and asks for a better computation. The full dissertation was not independently available in this comparison; Price's results were cross-checked in the cited contemporary and later accounts.
- J. Sliačan and W. Stromquist, [*Packing Densities: An Update*, PP2017 abstract](https://pp2017.github.io/assets/pdf/abstracts/talks/walter_stromquist.pdf) and [conference slides, p. 4](https://github.com/PP2017/PP2017.github.io/blob/281f092e23a2f162da807e575afac97d7b318938/assets/pdf/slides/walter_stromquist.pdf). They already announced a calculation of at least 100 decimal places with Mathematica, displayed the common leading digits and raised algebraicity. The abstract's caveat about an inexact upper bound occurs in the **1342** discussion, not in its 1324 paragraph. The abstract and slides do not supply a proof of the recursive structure or its uniqueness. We do not infer what unpublished calculations the authors may have had.
- The same authors, [*Improving bounds on packing densities of 4-point permutations*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/4263/pdf), DMTCS 19(2) (2018), §3.1 Theorem 2, exhibit a symmetric construction with geometric 132/213 end tails and prove $0.244054321 < p(1324) < 0.244054549$; the end of §3.1 also reports the alternative Permpack upper bound $0.244054540$. Their §5 calls determining the 1324 part ratios a challenge. See also [Sliačan’s doctoral thesis, §5.3.1](https://oro.open.ac.uk/55735/1/thesis.pdf) and their [public scripts and certificates](https://github.com/jsliacan/permpack/tree/6c2d7e11e0abebd1f42c74a541f9b22e7738caf2). Their symmetry ansatz, qualitative infinite layering and high-precision numerical value are predecessors, not new observations here. Our non-geometric correction is compatible with use of exactly geometric tails as a *lower bound*.
- A. Kabela, D. Král’, J. A. Noel and T. Pierron, [*Density maximizers of layered permutations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01723), EJC 29(3) (2022), develop the layered-permuton setting and study finite versus infinite layer counts for other parameter ranges, including patterns with large non-singleton end layers. W. Stromquist's [2024 AMS slides on joint packing densities](https://walterstromquist.com/Walt_Stromquist_GreatLimitSet3.pdf) concern joint densities of three-point patterns, a different optimization.

**What is certified here.** The exact scalar fixed point, the unique center and full equality conditions among layered permutons, and asymptotic but not eventual geometricity give a constructive characterization of the packing density rather than a closed-form algebraic expression. The rigorous numeric replay is optional. No new digit record, all-permuton equality classification or priority guarantee is asserted.
