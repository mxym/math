# Source map and limits

- Geoffroy Couteau and Adi Rosén, [Random Sources in Private Computation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/074), ASIACRYPT 2022, pp. 443–473, [mirrored author full text](https://opencryptology.org/2023/074/versions/20240114T061341Z.pdf). The question is Conjecture 13, §5.1, printed p. 15; the feasibility hypothesis is the honest-majority divide, and the disputed factor is between minimum randomness over all protocols and minimum randomness when exactly or at most t parties possess independent fair tapes. Section 2 and Definition 4, pp. 3–4, specify the synchronous point-to-point, static perfect simulation model, with a polynomial-time simulator. Section 3, pp. 5–6, distinguishes a static set selected for a particular functionality from a placement fixed across arbitrary functionalities. The formal open problem does not introduce a maximum over inequivalent placements of one fixed labelled function. We answer the standard minimum over protocols using t sources, spelled out also in [AICR c/0102](https://aicr.info/c/0102/). The same construction can refute a claim uniform over all F for any one predetermined set, by relabeling F. It does not refute an independently posed worst-placement version. Theorem 1 of Couteau–Rosén already establishes t-source feasibility; the new issue here is the asymptotic *total coin cost* for an honest-majority functionality.
- The source's §5.1 explicitly notes that Kushilevitz–Mansour's XOR uses exactly t sources and does not answer Conjecture 13, because XOR lacks the feasibility obstruction. See Eyal Kushilevitz and Yishay Mansour, [Randomness in Private Computations](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480196306130), SIAM J. Discrete Math. 10(4):647–661, 1997, §3.2 for the sequential XOR setup. Section 2 of this note recalls the cut-masking variant, including exact simulation and a standard binary BCH-column shortening, to make the combined protocol self-contained. These are not claimed as new XOR results, and no improved arbitrary-parameter XOR bound is needed here.
- Vipul Goyal, Yuval Ishai and Yifan Song, [Tight Bounds on the Randomness Complexity of Secure Multiparty Computation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/799), CRYPTO 2022, [author full text mirrored](https://opencryptology.org/2022/799/versions/20220621T030444Z.pdf), §3.1 for the worst-case total coin count and §4.2 Theorem 2 for the t²/2 lower bound on nonconstant, one-bit symmetric functions with at least three inputs and t≤n−2. It permits additional helper parties. The lower bound here uses instead a direct emulation of the two fixed-input roles into two actual XOR participants, with an explicit loss of two in the guaranteed threshold. The resulting lower bound is on unrestricted randomness for F, not on a subsequence of rare inputs or on a restricted linear protocol class.
- Benny Chor and Eyal Kushilevitz, [A Zero-One Law for Boolean Privacy](https://doi.org/10.1137/0404004), SIAM J. Discrete Math. 4(1):36–47, 1991: finite-domain Boolean functions admit half-size privacy precisely in the XOR-of-local-functions class. The selected function is not in that class, and this note re-proves the needed special case by an equal-size two-party grouping and the transcript product argument for AND. This is a functionality feasibility boundary, not a deduction about a deployed scheme.
- Adi Shamir, [How to Share a Secret](https://doi.org/10.1145/359168.359176), CACM 1979, is the polynomial-sharing foundation. Ivan Damgård and Jesper Buus Nielsen, [Scalable and Unconditionally Secure Multiparty Computation](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-74143-5_32), CRYPTO 2007, [IACR proceedings PDF](https://iacr.org/archive/crypto2007/46220565/46220565.pdf), is the standard double-sharing method for degree reduction; an explicit modern formulation is [Scalable MPC for Privacy-Preserving ML, USENIX Security 2024](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity24-liu-fengrun.pdf), §3 online/offline phases. The mask is not a new multiplication primitive. The additional use here places both nonlinear input owners in the designated t sources, handles the no-honest-source branch separately, and combines the low-degree mask contributions in a private parity computation so the nonlinear result is never opened separately. This converts the known building blocks into a counterexample to the conjectured universal lower bound.
- [Randomness in Private Sequential Stateless Protocols](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1448), ASIACRYPT 2024, addresses one-party corruption in a sequential stateless model, including an AND protocol with different structure. [On Randomness Complexity of 1-Private Protocols](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol374-icalp2026/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.79/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.79.html), ICALP 2026, improves one-private AND and covers other one-private protocols. These do not give the growing-t counterexample or settle the separate upper-bound/compilation question.

The focused duplicate search checked the source's full and proceedings records, the named source-count and randomness-complexity follow-ups, the double-sharing line and the public c/0102 statement. No identical claim about the lower half of Conjecture 13 was located. This is a scoped check rather than a priority claim. No active-corruption, noisy-channel, concurrent-execution, computational, or worst-placement guarantee is asserted.
