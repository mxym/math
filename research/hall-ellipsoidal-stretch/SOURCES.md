# Sources and limits of the comparison

- G. R. Hall, [*Acute triangles in the n-ball*](https://doi.org/10.2307/3213533), J. Appl. Probab. 19 (1982), 712–715, evaluates the probability for an area/volume-uniform **ball** (in two dimensions $4/\pi^2-1/8$, in three $33/70$). These evaluations are prior. Gabriel Khan, [*Hall’s Conjecture on Extremal Sets for Random Triangles*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05913), proves the local maximizing statement in the plane, a near-ball result in dimension three and a large-isoperimetric-ratio planar bound. The original extremal question compares **all** bounded convex bodies; this note does not resolve that question.
- [ProofAtlas: Hall’s Random-Triangle Conjecture](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/halls-random-triangle-conjecture/), the research map for a source recorded 2 August 2026, already reports a strict disk-versus-noncircular-ellipse comparison in the plane. That planar endpoint statement must not be credited here as a first. The comparison here is monotone along an entire stretch ray, is valid for solid ellipsoids in every dimension, and specifies when nonuniform spherical input laws and even unequal radii are covered. The public ProofAtlas page is an account of its own research record, not evidence for a full proof of Hall’s conjecture.
- Akopyan, Edelsbrunner and Nikitenko, [*The Beauty of Random Polytopes Inscribed in the 2-Sphere*](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2021.1980459), Lemma 4, use the halfspace test for an obtuse vertex on a sphere; the angle-to-halfspace conversion and spherical projection distributions are classical. [Drivas and Retakh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18414) consider random-triangle probability over arbitrary input laws. [Greenwell](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06591) studies unequal concentric radii in the plane, with **no common deformation**, and [Jandhyala’s author-posted note](https://vamshij.com/research/why-the-sphere-beats-the-ball/) determines the radial three-dimensional comparison. The [Gaussian triangle computations by Edelman and Strang](https://math.mit.edu/~edelman/homepage/papers/focm.pdf) treat triangle shape distributions, not the stated monotone stretch inequality for arbitrary prescribed shells.
- The owner’s [#179, radial obtuse triangles](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/179) identifies the preferred radius distribution in all sufficiently high dimensions **before** affine deformation. The present theorem instead takes the three radii as given and compares a common anisotropic stretch to the reference Euclidean metric. It is not an independent rederivation of the radial optimizer. The [six-point Sylvester results](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/198) concern whether six uniform points are convex-hull vertices, an affine invariant, and have no implication for acuteness, which is not affine invariant.
