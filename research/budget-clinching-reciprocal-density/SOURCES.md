# Sources, scope, and bounded prior-work comparison

Checked 19 November 2026. No external peer review or claim of global priority.

## Original question and model

* Yiding Feng and Jason D. Hartline, *An End-to-end Argument in Mechanism Design (Prior-independent Auctions for Budgeted Agents)*: [FOCS 2018 proceedings paper](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2018-Papers/pdfs/59f404.pdf), [full arXiv version v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.01977v2), [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01977). The full version's introduction, p. 5, conjectures extension of the middle-ironed optimal-welfare result to regular distributions. Definition 2.1 uses concavity of the CDF for welfare. Definition 4.1, Figure 3(a), and Theorem 4.1 specify the two-agent, common-public-budget auction and the proved **uniform** result. Section 4 already uses a highest-type allocation ceiling in the welfare relaxation. Appendix A discusses additional formats with price jumps; the negative comparison in this package is to Definition 4.1, not every such extension.
* Feng's [2021 Northwestern dissertation](https://www.mccormick.northwestern.edu/computer-science/documents/yiding-feng_tech-report.pdf), Chapter 3: Definitions 3.1 and 3.5 and Figure 3.3(a) confirm the regularity and allocation conventions. Theorems 3.11 and 3.14 treat continuous and discrete uniform distributions, respectively; Theorems 3.16 and 3.18 concern the revelation gap. The [EconCS Bench problem contributed by Jason Hartline](https://github.com/aieconcs/econcs-bench/blob/main/revelation-gap-public-budget-welfare/PROBLEM.md) also distinguishes the structural extension from the broader prior-independent revelation-gap question. This note does not settle the latter.

## Which results are being compared?

1. **Explicit strict comparison.** Neither the uniform theorem nor the prior-independent bounds compare a four-band auction to the best Definition 4.1 mechanism at our nonuniform distribution. The example satisfies both the stated welfare regularity and strictly increasing hazard; the conclusion concerns welfare, not revenue.
2. **Strictly concave reciprocal.** The comparison gives an improving allocation at a distribution-dependent budget. It does not assert failure at every budget. The 2018 theorem is compatible with failure along a sequence of smooth, strictly increasing-hazard distributions approaching uniform.
3. **Affine reciprocal.** The uniform member and the upper-ceiling relaxation are prior. Here the identity involving an antiderivative of the squared density leads to explicit nonnegative budget and ceiling multipliers that attain the welfare upper bound also for nonconstant affine reciprocals. A comparison restricted to rivals that always exhaust the item would not give the same theorem.

[Dobzinski, Lavi and Nisan](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S089982561100131X) analyze multi-unit auctions with budgets and Pareto efficiency. Bhattacharya et al., [Incentive Compatible Budget Elicitation in Multi-unit Auctions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.3501), study private-budget elicitation and approximation in a Bayesian setting. [Recent work on liquid welfare and revenue monotonicity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06278) concerns other objectives. These are not the expected-welfare optimum in the 2018 two-bidder DSIC model.
