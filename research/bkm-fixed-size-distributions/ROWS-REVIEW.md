The support intervals are exact: top row k feasible iff k≤n≤mk, and for r≥2 row r=k feasible iff rk≤n, realized by C_k even when k=0. For the fixed-top-row slice, fill rows of capacity k from the top. If the first lower row shorter than k has a positive row below it, the bottommost positive donor is removable, and the preceding full row makes the target row addable; otherwise the shape is already the lexicographic endpoint. This respects the cap. For a fixed lower row, move surplus below r from the bottom, then move all surplus in rows 2,…,r−1 above k in order from the last such row. These legal moves reach C_k; the positive reversed transitions follow from reversibility. Every adjacent nonempty cut has the explicit legal endpoint edge (move a bottom box to row one for r=1, or from row r of C_(k+1) to row one for r≥2). The endpoints are related by the coordinate slack condition.

The ordered proposal coupling from the first-row theorem is defined for all tight coordinates, so no row is distinguished prior to censoring. If only one proposal leaves its respective row slice, its only possible direction from R is left growth or right shrinkage. Every other coordinate of that proposed diagram then has moved weakly in the safe direction for restoring its original values. Hence the stationary pair has the desired conditional marginals by slice irreducibility. Right growth implies left growth and left shrinkage implies right shrinkage by the same integer comparison. Detailed balance gives the stated nonzero adjacent flux ratios and weak log-concavity; zeros outside contiguous support cause no difficulty.

For exactly m rows the down prefactor is x_i/t and the up prefactor 1/m. Residues at infinity give both probability sums with no assumption that x_m=0. Hook and Weyl products multiply to the displayed Schur–Weyl law, and its ratio across a legal bond equals U/D by cancellation. The factors at any tight row compare with the same sign of pairwise shifted differences as before; all factors are nonnegative and zeros exactly describe blocked moves. Thus the matched-tight-row coupling works within the finite cap. Standard word RSK uses weak row increase, giving precisely the stated word statistic, not strictly increasing subsequences or nonuniform word laws.
