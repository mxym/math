# Sources, scope, and limited prior search (2026-11-19)

* J. Baslingker, M. Krishnapur and M. Madiman, [Log-concavity in one-dimensional Coulomb gases and related ensembles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15116v2), §7, questions (i)–(iii), equations (30)–(32), pp. 20–21. In (31) the power is two, the sum of all integer particle locations is prescribed, and the number of particles exceeds the excess above the minimum sum. Their (ii) asks separately about coordinate marginals and row sums, and (iii) means longest weakly increasing subsequence of a uniform word, with length and alphabet fixed. Their earlier positive results on size mixtures in the same paper do not remove the conditioning.
* Y. Li, [Log-concavity of longest increasing subsequence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36483), October 2026, §§4–5: the origin of the ordered proposal, individual slice restoration and stationary comparison method used here. Li's Theorem 1.1 concerns the first row under ordinary Plancherel measure (uniform permutations). The [prior repository note](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/af1b5757288006b7228f3040073b40230855e8fe/research/jack-matching-logconcavity/PROOF.md) extends that first-row result to every positive Jack parameter and proves two matching specializations. Neither is a first-two-row-sum or fixed-alphabet theorem.
* S. Matsumoto, [Jack deformations of Plancherel measures](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.5619): the fixed-size Jack mass formulas and parameter conventions are classical. K. Johansson, [Discrete orthogonal polynomial ensembles and the Plancherel measure](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906120), Proposition 1.5: classical connection of random words and the Charlier ensemble. A. Borodin and G. Olshanski, [Markov processes on partitions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0409075), and [Meixner polynomials and random partitions](https://m.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=mmj&option_lang=eng&paperid=263&wshow=paper): classical z-measure dynamics and Meixner specialization. We claim neither the ensemble, its level weights, nor the classical tableau formulas as new.
* Other similar names do not indicate equivalent results. For example [Log-concavity of subsequence counts of words](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22147) studies a *single given word* and its counts of distinct subsequences, not the random-word statistic in (32). [Xia's 2026 result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18235) concerns the ordinary-permutation/first-row case.

Targeted primary-source searches compared fixed-size/fixed-trace Meixner and Charlier ensembles, Vandermonde-square conditioning, individual and summed Plancherel rows, and concomitant random-word/Schur–Weyl/LIS questions. The counterexample for row sums is elementary, and search cannot exclude earlier observation or unpublished work.
