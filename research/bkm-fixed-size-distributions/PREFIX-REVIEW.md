At n≥9, a one-row shape cannot contribute to S=2,3,4. Direct enumeration of positive splits in weakly decreasing order gives exactly a column for S=2, one two-cell-first-row hook for S=3, and for S=4 either a 3+1 first pair (so a hook), or 2+2 followed by some more twos and then ones. The transposed latter shapes are the two-row shapes (n−j,j), j≥2. Hook dimensions and the usual two-row ballot difference give the three stated mass formulas. In the full integer sum, reflection j↔n+1−j negates the ballot difference (with zero middle difference if fixed); expansion of its square, followed by Vandermonde, gives twice the Catalan number. Thus the missing j=0,1 terms have squares 1,(n−1)^2. Catalan growth dominates 1+(n−1)^2+(n−1)^4 from the verified n=9 boundary by the respective ratio bounds in the proof (in particular (9/8)^4<2). As a numerical cross-check independent of both the hook formula and Catalan evaluation, the accompanying script generates all standard tableau dimensions through the Young lattice, obtaining w_2=1,w_3=64,w_4=5581 at n=9 and matching every asserted formula through n=23. The strict sign is opposite log-concavity. This concerns the sum of the first *two*, not an individual row.
