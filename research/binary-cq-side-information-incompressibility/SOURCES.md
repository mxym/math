# Primary sources and exact question

**Target.** Lasse H. Wolff, Matthias Christandl, Bergfinnur Durhuus, Christoph Hirche and Andreas Winter, [Incompressible quantum information, arXiv:2611.28361v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.28361v1), Nov. 20, 2026, §II. Equation (1) asks, for every input source and loss tolerance, for an encoder on the quantum system B with output dimension depending only on |A| and the allowed loss, not on |B|. The encoder may depend on the source. Theorem 1 rules this out for quantum A, including a two-dimensional quantum A via Appendix D, and for a classical reference with at least three symbols via Appendix E. The paragraph immediately following Theorem 1 explicitly leaves the two-symbol classical case open. Appendix H, Corollary 23 supplies the positive special case of two conditional states proportional to projectors via Jordan's lemma; here the conditional states are positive definite but not proportional to projectors. The prior Appendix G result for an information-bottleneck optimum (including Corollary 21 and the finite auxiliary gaps) is a different fixed-source optimization and does not yield a robust dimension-independent compression obstruction.

Our binary source family is indexed by k, not a single fixed source on an infinite-dimensional input. The estimate is uniform over all encoders for each source, even those designed with full classical knowledge of that source. A classical label A identifying the prepared conditional state is *not* furnished to the encoder. The theorem applies in an unbounded subsequence of dimensions, enough to negate the universal bound of Eq. (1). The ordinary quantum dimension bound is the direct specialization with no flag. The optional finite flag extension measures maximum quantum dimension per flag; it allows an arbitrarily large finite flag set and bounds loss of information in the full flag-plus-quantum register, not just its quantum marginal.

**Known ingredients, precisely separated.** [M. B. Hastings, Random unitaries give quantum expanders, arXiv:0706.0556](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0556), [Phys. Rev. A 76, 032315 (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.76.032315), supplies two independent Haar unitaries and their adjoints in its Hermitian degree-four case. The second eigenvalue bound tends at most to √3/2 with arbitrarily small fixed slack, in probability. Wolff et al. use the same input in Proposition 4 and Appendix C Proposition 5 and in Appendix D Eq. (25). Their Eq. (7) uses the Kraus commutator identity and their Proposition 4 proof uses the rank cost of a compression/decompression Kraus product. The present note does not claim these as new: the new step is a realization of a uniformly expanding sign family using only one binary cq source, by the fixed nonorthogonal-frame pencil and its uniform perturbation estimate. Finally Wolff et al. Lemma 3 / Eq. (3) and Appendix A convert the trace-recovery lower bound into base-2 mutual-information loss; the underlying recoverability is [Fawzi–Renner, arXiv:1410.0664](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0664).

**Closest other comparisons.** [Koashi–Imoto, Operations that do not disturb partially known quantum states](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101144) gives the structure of exact preservation of state families. Exact noncompressibility at each finite dimension would not give a positive dimension-independent error tolerance; our lower bound is quantitative. [Bluhm–Rauber–Wolf, Quantum compression relative to a set of measurements, arXiv:1708.04898](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04898), Theorem 5.1 and Lemma 5.2, studies compression with a finite classical record, stabilizing a fixed measurement set tested on all possible states. Their finite-dimensional compactness tolerance can depend on the entire measurement set and dimension, and the witness input state can depend on the compression map. They use the same low-rank Kraus/flag structure, but their theorem is not a uniform gap on a fixed pair of input states. [Buscemi–Sutter–Tomamichel, An information-theoretic treatment of quantum dichotomies](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08539), §§2–4, analyzes general state-pair conversion and relative-entropy rates under different asymptotic resource restrictions. One-way conversion or many-copy interconversion does not itself constrain an intervening one-shot quantum output dimension for a recovery of the same fixed pair.
