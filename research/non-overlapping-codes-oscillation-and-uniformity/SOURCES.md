## Which assertion changes?

[Chee, Kiah, Purkayastha and Wang, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 59 (2013), 4668–4674](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2013.2252952); [author's journal PDF](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/hmkiah/docs/papers/cbfcode.pdf), [arXiv:1209.0236v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.0236v2). The notation matters. Their Eq. (5), p. 4670, maximizes the cardinality of a particular construction over the integer run length; it is not the maximum over arbitrary non-overlapping codes. Theorem 4.2, Eq. (9), p. 4672, states a liminf for that restricted maximum. Choosing lengths indexed by the run parameter, as in its proof, establishes a subsequence, not an all-length estimate at the same height. Theorem 4.1, Eq. (6), p. 4671, is a separate restricted-to-unrestricted ratio assertion. Theorems A and B and the short ratio proof above evaluate the former and contradict the latter for $q\ge8$. Theorem 1.1, Eq. (1), for unrestricted codes is unaffected. Both the journal and preprint texts were checked. No claim is made about a formal erratum having or not having appeared.

## The phase, constants and synchronizing-code constructions are classical

* V. I. Levenshtein, “Decoding automata which are invariant with respect to the initial state,” *Problemy Kibernetiki* **12** (1964), 125–136, [scanned Russian original, §2, printed pp. 131–133](https://publ.lib.ru/ARCHIVES/P/%27%27Problemy_kibernetiki%27%27_%28seriya%29/%cf%f0%ee%e1%eb%e5%ec%fb%20%ea%e8%e1%e5%f0%ed%e5%f2%e8%ea%e8.%20%c2%fb%ef%f3%f1%ea%2012.%281964%29.pdf#page=132). His $W_k(n)$ is the **same code after reversal**, not merely analogous. Equation (12) lower-bounds the larger optimum by this code; p. 132 counts it as $(q-1)^2$ times the number of length $n-k-2$ strings avoiding $0^k$. In Lemma 2 and Eqs. (16)–(18) he chooses $k=\lfloor\log_q(n(q-1)^2/(q\log q))\rfloor$; writing $a=1-1/q$ gives $z=na q^{-k}$ in $[\log q/(q-1),q\log q/(q-1))$. The phase-dependent lower estimate is $az e^{-z}$; the equal endpoint values and the subsequence approaching the maximum at one are already identified. This is exactly the lattice optimizer in our notation. Lemma 2 states **lower bounds**, not a matching upper limit for $\max_k|W_k(n)|$. Our proof supplies the two-sided estimate, including control of the maximizing $k$ outside compact phase windows, to compare the later restricted theorems. The old calculations strongly anticipate it; no priority for the old profile, either constant, or even their tuning is asserted.
* See also [Levenshtein, *Combinatorial problems motivated by comma-free codes*, J. Combin. Des. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.10071), [author's manuscript](https://www.keldysh.ru/papers/2003/source/article/golomb4.pdf), §4, Theorem 9, Eqs. (23)–(24), and Theorem 10, Eq. (25); [Levenshtein 1970](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi1773) for the general upper bound. Multiple synchronizing words are not a new idea here.
* [Gilbert, *Synchronization of binary messages* (1960)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1960.1057587) treated prefix synchronization. Levenshtein 1964, p. 132, expressly credits Gilbert's Theorem 5 and Appendix 3; he notes the factor of two between the binary estimates for the different admissible classes. For the precise historical comparison we used the original Levenshtein pages and the subsequent accounts below, rather than claiming a line-by-line verification of Gilbert's full text.
* [Guibas–Odlyzko, *Maximal Prefix-Synchronized Codes* (1978)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0135034), and [Odlyzko, *Enumeration of strings*, §6.4, Theorem 6.1, manuscript pp. 16–17](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/arch/string.enumerate.pdf#page=16): for binary prefix-synchronized codes optimized over the marker, the normalized size is asymptotic to $z e^{-z}$ on the same lattice, with endpoints $(\log 2)/2,1/e$. These are **twice** the one-run values here. Such a code forbids misplaced copies of the *selected marker* in concatenations; a non-overlapping code forbids every nonempty proper prefix-to-suffix match, even shorter than the marker. For example, the binary word 0010 by itself synchronizes with marker 001 in all concatenations, but overlaps itself on its last 0. Fixing the marker $0^k$ recovers the one-run code; optimizing all markers changes the class. [Kashyap–Neuhoff, *Periodic Prefix-Synchronized Codes*](https://ece.iisc.ac.in/~nkashyap/Papers/period2_codes.pdf), particularly §II-A, studies period-one/two marker schemes and rate, delay and span, not the all-alphabet cross-bifix optimum. For long Bernoulli runs and lattice oscillations see also [Gordon–Schilling–Waterman (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00699107).
* [Blackburn, *Non-overlapping codes*, arXiv:1303.1026v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.1026v3), Lemma 3, Construction 2 and Theorems 4, 5, 8: the fixed-alphabet estimate, exact divisibility cases, fixed-length large-alphabet limit and joint constant-factor bounds are distinct from B's simultaneous additive error relative to $(q-1)/(qe)$. Its multi-prefix construction is antecedent, not our contribution. [Wang–Wang, arXiv:2108.06934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.06934), §III, Theorem 3, treats generating functions and a fixed-$k$ exponential growth rate; it does not assert the optimized all-length liminf at issue here. [Blackburn–Nasr Esfahani–Kreher–Stinson, arXiv:2211.10309v4, Theorem 35](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.10309v4), gives the binary subsequential lower bound and credits Levenshtein. See also [Stanovnik–Moškon–Mraz](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-023-01344-z) on representations and finite optimization.

## Parallel-work and repository check

A focused **1 July–19 November 2026** search for arXiv versions, publication records, corrections and later citing papers supplemented unrestricted historical searches for non-overlapping, cross-bifix-free, strongly regular and prefix-synchronized codes, optimizing run length, leading constants, joint alphabet/length growth and lattice oscillations. [Blackburn, arXiv:2611.21913v1 (16 November 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21913v1) explicitly distinguishes a binary subsequential $1/(2e)$ construction from a $5/(6e)$ unrestricted limsup upper bound (Theorem 1); it does not assert our restricted liminf correction, ratio contradiction or B. We found no direct statement of the quantitative bound B and no direct correction of the two 2013 statements in the searched indexed results. This bounded search does not establish global priority or exclude unindexed or parallel work.

The [earlier fixed-alphabet repository manuscript (#52)](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/52) proves a limit for unrestricted $C(n,q)$ for fixed $q$. It supplies the additional upper bound used **only** for the optional two-sided uniform rate and exact ratio-limit statements. The refutations of Eqs. (6) and (9), and the varying-alphabet $1/e$ limit, do not depend on that upper bound. Its marker construction approximates a target mass for each fixed alphabet; B controls the rounding and endpoint losses uniformly. Its older text and files are unchanged by this note.
