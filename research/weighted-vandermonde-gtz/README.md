# Sharp original-weight sampling for weighted univariate polynomial spaces

**Research draft · 18 November 2026.** Exactly as many observations as the polynomial dimension suffice for the lower sampling bound below, even when the nodes are prescribed and the observation weights cannot be changed. The result gives a sharp structured case of the real Goreinov–Tyrtyshnikov–Zamarashkin (GTZ) conjecture at every rank. It **does not resolve the unrestricted real conjecture**; the all-real rank-two consequence is already known.

**中文摘要。** 对任意有限实节点和预先指定的正权重，可以选出与多项式空间维数相同的原始观测点，使其加权平方和支配给定概率分布下的平方均值。相同结论适用于共圆（或共线）复节点。白化后最小奇异值的常数是锐的；这不解决一般实矩阵的 GTZ 猜想。

Research, proof development and a separate adversarial review were AI-assisted. [Review and reproduction](REVIEW.md) records what was checked. There has been no external peer review or formal verification, and we make no priority claim.

## Statement

Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be real numbers (repetitions allowed), $w_i>0$ fixed observation weights, and $p_i>0$ any probabilities summing to one. Suppose at least $r$ nodes are distinct. Then some $r$ original indices $I$, at distinct nodes, satisfy **simultaneously** for every real or complex polynomial $f$ of degree $<r$
$$
\sum_{i\in I} w_i |f(x_i)|^2 \ \ge\ \sum_{j=1}^n p_j w_j |f(x_j)|^2 . \tag{1}
$$
The selected weights are the original $w_i$, not quadrature weights which may be chosen after selecting the nodes. The conclusion also holds with all $x_i$ on one generalized circle (line or circle) in the complex plane. Zero-probability inputs follow by adding a small positive uniform measure and taking a constant subsequence of index sets.

The [unrestricted real GTZ conjecture (RA–18)](https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/randomized-and-low-rank-approximation/RA-18/problem.tex) asks whether every real $n\times r$ isometry $U$ has a square row submatrix with $\|U_I^{-1}\|_2\le\sqrt n$. Here is the connection to (1). Take $p_i=1/n$. If $F_{ik}=\sqrt{w_i}x_i^k$, $0\le k<r$, (1) is $F_I^*F_I\succeq F^*F/n$. Thus the column-whitened $U=F(F^*F)^{-1/2}$ has $\sigma_{\min}(U_I)\ge1/\sqrt n$. Column basis changes and independent row phases do not matter. The guarantee applies at every rank and allows arbitrary node spacing, not a condition-number bound for unwhitened monomials. For example this covers **any contiguous band of columns** of a unitary Fourier matrix, including arbitrary row weights and restriction to any full-rank node set. The constant in this structured GTZ theorem is best possible; see below.

## 1. Remove redundant moments without increasing total probability

Combine coincident nodes by keeping an index with largest $w_i$ at that node and assigning it probability mass $\sum_{j:x_j=x_i}p_j w_j/w_i$. This preserves all weighted moments and does not increase total mass. The ultimately selected rows correspond to actual indices.

For distinct nodes use the real weighted moment vectors $w_i(1,x_i,\ldots,x_i^{2r-2})$, understood as **vectors of length** $2r-1$ (one per node). When more than $2r-1$ nodes are active, there is a nonzero real relation $\sum_i c_i w_i x_i^k=0$ for $0\le k\le2r-2$. The constant moment and positive $w_i$ force both signs. Multiplying by $-1$ if necessary, assume $\sum_i c_i\ge0$. For $t=\min_{c_i>0} p_i/c_i$, change the masses to $p_i-tc_i$. This deletes at least one node, leaves every mass nonnegative, preserves every moment, and does not increase total mass.

This is elementary conic Carathéodory reduction with a mass objective. An explicit relation on any $2r$ distinct nodes is $c_i=1/(w_i L'(x_i))$, $L(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{2r}(z-x_j)$, by comparing leading coefficients in Lagrange interpolation of $z^k$. It makes the algorithm exact over rational input. Continue until $q\le2r-1$ active nodes remain. Their preserved covariance $F^*D_pF$ is positive definite, so $q\ge r$. If their positive total is $s\le1$, divide by $s$: the new probability covariance dominates the original covariance. Therefore it suffices to establish (1) on this reduced instance with strictly positive probabilities summing to one.

## 2. Weighted complement reduces the rank

We recall the weighted complement identity in [Holden, §7, Weighted duality](https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/holden-ra18-2026-09-13/manuscript/ra18.tex); the explanation here is self-contained. Let $F\in\mathbb C^{q\times r}$ have full column rank, $D_p=\operatorname{diag}(p_i)$, $H=F^*D_pF$, $V=D_p^{1/2} F H^{-1/2}$. Its columns are orthonormal. Let $W$ be any orthonormal basis matrix of their orthogonal complement. For complementary index sets $I,J$ of sizes $r,q-r$ and $0<p_i<1$,
$$
 F_I^*F_I\succeq H
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G_J^*G_J\succeq G^*D_pG,\qquad
 G=(I_q-D_p)^{-1/2}W. \tag{2}
$$
Indeed $F_I^*F_I\succeq H$ means $V_I^*D_{p,I}^{-1}V_I\succeq I_r$. The square matrix $D_{p,I}^{-1/2}V_I$ then also has its *row* Gram at least $I_r$, and conversely. Since $V_IV_I^*=I_r-W_IW_I^*$, this is equivalent by the Schur complement to $W_I^*(I_r-D_{p,I})^{-1}W_I\preceq I_{q-r}$. Subtract from $W^*(I_q-D_p)^{-1}W=I_{q-r}+G^*D_pG$ to obtain (2). The right-hand criterion is invariant under any invertible right change of basis of $G$, so an unnormalized full-rank basis for the same complement is enough.

Here the reduction survives because the dual of a weighted Vandermonde is again a weighted Vandermonde. Write $m=q-r$, $P(z)=\prod_{i=1}^q(z-x_i)$, and set
$$
 \widetilde W_{i,k}=\frac{x_i^k}{\sqrt{p_iw_i}\,P'(x_i)},\qquad 0\le k<m .
$$
It has rank $m$ by the Vandermonde determinant. It spans $\ker(F^*D_p^{1/2})$ since $\sum_i x_i^a/P'(x_i)=0$ for $0\le a\le q-2$ by Lagrange interpolation, and $r-1+m-1=q-2$. This is the familiar [duality of generalized Reed–Solomon evaluation codes](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/halljo/classes/codenotes/grs.pdf). Consequently the dual problem has the *same* real nodes and the *same* probabilities $p_i$ but polynomial degree $<m$ and weights
$$
 w_i'=\frac1{p_i(1-p_i)w_i|P'(x_i)|^2}. \tag{3}
$$

Induct on $r$. If $r=1$, select an index of largest $w_i$. If $q=r$, taking all $q$ works since $p_i\le1$. Otherwise $1\le m=q-r\le r-1$, and all $p_i<1$. The induction hypothesis supplies $m$ indices $J$ satisfying the right side of (2) for (3). The complementary $r$ reduced indices $I$ work for the original rank. This is induction on degree, not an appeal to an unsolved lower-rank instance. It completes the real-node proof. Real Gram domination also tests polynomials with complex coefficients by splitting real and imaginary parts.

A Möbius map sends any generalized circle containing the finitely many complex nodes to the real projective line and can be chosen to avoid a pole at all of them. Under $x=(az+b)/(cz+d)$, degree-$<r$ polynomial spaces transform by $f(z)=(cz+d)^{r-1}g(x)$ with $g$ ranging over all polynomials of degree $<r$. Absorb $|cz+d|^{2r-2}$ into $w$ to deduce the circle statement. This is not a proof for arbitrary complex nodes or arbitrary complex isometries.

## 3. Sharpness and further consequences

Already for $n=r+1$, any distinct real nodes yield an equality example after row weighting. Choose $w_i=|P'(x_i)|^{-2}$. Then the unit orthogonal complement of $F$ has all coordinate magnitudes $1/\sqrt n$ by the same interpolation identity. Deleting any one row of the whitened isometry leaves a minimum squared singular value $1/n$. On the complex unit circle, equally spaced $r+1$ nodes have this property even with $w_i=1$.

More generally, for every $n>r$ choose positive multiplicities $m_i$ summing to $n$ at $r+1$ distinct real nodes and set $z_i^2=(1-m_i/n)/r$; these positive values sum to one. For each of the $m_i$ copies take $w_i=1/(m_i z_i^2 |P'(x_i)|^2)$ with $P$ the polynomial on the $r+1$ distinct nodes. The direction-compressed and whitened isometry has orthogonal complement with squared moduli $z_i^2$; uncompress by dividing each row by $\sqrt{m_i}$ and duplicating it. Any nonsingular selection omits one group and uses all other groups once. On such a selection the row Gram minus $I_r/n$ is diagonally congruent, up to row signs, to $I_r-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/r$: write its diagonal-minus-rank-one form as $D-uu^T$, $D_{ii}=1/m_i-1/n$, $u_i^2=z_i^2/m_i=D_{ii}/r$. Hence it is positive semidefinite and singular. This realizes the [unequally duplicated simplex extremizer, Holden §8](https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/holden-ra18-2026-09-13/manuscript/ra18.tex) **within the polynomial class**. If pairwise distinct original nodes are required, perturb the duplicated nodes to distinct points: whitening stays continuous at a full-column-rank matrix and the finite maximum of minimum singular values is continuous. Thus over distinct-node instances the infimum of the best available minimum singular value is still $1/\sqrt n$ (equivalently, the supremum of the best inverse norm is $\sqrt n$).

Rank two **recovers, but does not claim priority for,** the known all-real-two-column theorem [Sengupta–Pautov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05944): after choosing a first coordinate nonzero on all finitely many nonzero rows, their directions are $(1,x_i)$. A higher-rank consequence independent of the rational normal curve: in any real rank $r$, if the real linear span of the individual row outer products has dimension $d\le r+2$, the same moment-preserving, mass-nonincreasing compression leaves $q\le d$, and its weighted complement has rank at most two. Applying the just-proved weighted rank-two result in (2) completes this class (zero rows may be discarded, normalizing the remaining probability). This extends the earlier criterion of at most $r+2$ distinct row directions to a dimension bound on their quadratic span. In particular all real rank-three frames whose row directions lie on a homogeneous projective conic, including a reducible one, satisfy GTZ, with no cardinality limit on the row directions. It does not include six generic directions in the projective plane. More generally at fixed rank $r$ the whole conjecture reduces by this compression to a weighted finite-dimensional semialgebraic test with at most $r(r+1)/2$ atoms; for $r=3$ the only remaining support size is six, with independent row outer products. Probabilities are variables, so this is **not** the unweighted six-row case alone. Indeed, if no index set works for a fixed positive probability vector, then each candidate difference has a strictly negative eigenvalue; finitely many such inequalities persist for nearby rational probabilities. For $p_i=m_i/N$, replace row $i$ by $m_i$ **unscaled** copies: the equally weighted mean of their outer products is the same target. Any selection using one row twice is singular and cannot dominate the positive definite target. Any remaining selection fails by construction. Whitening gives a counterexample in the ordinary isometry formulation.

## Prior work / limitations

* [Goreinov–Tyrtyshnikov–Zamarashkin 1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0024-3795(96)00301-1): original inverse-norm hypothesis; [Sengupta–Pautov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05944): real rank two; [Nesterenko 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14050): rank-two equality; [Nesterenko](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02387): series–parallel graph-based real extremals and sharpness within a structured family.
* [Sidney Holden, *A recursive counterexample to the complex extension in RA-18* (2026 manuscript)](https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/holden-ra18-2026-09-13/manuscript/ra18.tex): weighted complement equivalence, real $r+2$-direction theorem, unequal duplications, unbounded general complex counterexample. The present argument uses that algebraic duality; it combines moment support reduction, dual polynomial closure and strictly decreasing rank for all weighted real-line / circle polynomial evaluation spaces. Realification of a general complex frame is not a counterexample to the original real conjecture.
* Positive moment compression and dual generalized Reed–Solomon bases are standard ingredients, not new. Arbitrary changed weights or nodes in Gaussian quadrature, design or exact discretization, e.g. [Bartel et al.](https://smai-jcm.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/smai-jcm.136/), differ from the original-weight $r$-point conclusion. The [de la Garza / complete-class phenomenon](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3575) gives saturated designs on the design interval under stated hypotheses; the nodes of such a design need not belong to a prescribed finite sample, and the allocation is not fixed in advance. This is a lower sampling bound only, not near-isometry or a dimension-independent interpolation Lebesgue constant.
* For one-sided sampling, compare [Limonova–Malykhin–Temlyakov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00848): their left discretization result allows a number of points of the order of the dimension; their Proposition 4.1 with exactly the dimension many points is an **upper** estimate. The general equal-weight lower estimate in [Krieg–Ullrich, §4.1, (4.1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02066) vanishes when the sample count equals the dimension.
* Literature searches through 18 November 2026 did not identify a prior exact finite-original-support theorem with all the hypotheses and the constant of (1); this does not establish priority. The argument proves neither the unrestricted real GTZ conjecture nor its general rank-three case.
