The target is the drawn (leaf-up) discrete cladogram of Aldous–Janson arXiv:2303.02529v3 §6.1.1, not the continuous edge lengths, the maximum tree height, or ordinary root-up width. Their definition (72) gives height zero for leaves and one above the higher child at each split; (73) counts the sum of positive integer edge lengths. They explicitly conjecture the wrong order n log^4(n) in OP14 and ask about the drawn profile in OP15.

The crossing sum proof checks exactly: across a parent–child edge it is the parent height threshold indicator minus the child height threshold indicator. Internal nodes contribute twice as parents, and all but root once as children; leaves contribute zero. This gives the internal-fringe sum plus one extra root. Thus the length immediately is at most (2n−2) max depth, already ruling out n log^4 n using Ma. The linear asymptotic and global ℓ¹ LLN require the remaining estimates.

Size-biasing the exact root split gives the transition 1/[H_(s−1)(s−t)] to t<s, including the symmetric split with both children the same size. Each fixed size can be visited at most once, and a uniform label is in a clade of that size with probability m/n. Hence the expected fringe count per leaf equals visit probability divided by m, with subtree law T_m at each fixed address. Direct primary checks: (51), Thm 18 of A–J II for the exact limit; Ma arXiv:2609.14329v2 Thm1.1 covers the entire finite range 1≤p<∞; Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel arXiv:2505.24821v3 Cor.1.4 actually gives O_m(n) size-count variance, not just a weak convergence assertion.

In [M,2M), every one-step term for states 1,…,M−1 is at least 1/(2M H_2M). The aggregate hazard is at least 1/(4H_2M) and the chain cannot return after downward exit, so the bound on mean total visits follows at arbitrary start. Multiplying the m-th contribution by E h_m^p / m, bounded in the bin by C_p(1+log(2M))^(2p)/M, makes the dyadic high-size tail uniformly negligible. For shape counts, the stopped skeleton at sizes ≤m indeed leaves independent size-m fringe trees. Conditional binomial variance plus the prior size-count variance yields O(n), sufficient for convergence of finite truncations in every finite L^p by uniform boundedness. Jensen correctly pads the large-clade list with zeros even though its count is random. The additional root term is negligible in ℓ¹ L^p. The deterministic limit profile is summable and its coordinate sum induces the length limit. No fluctuation or rate result claimed. The expected-value constant may also be deduced once the telescoping is observed via BCM's established weighted summability statement, which the sources discussion should note separately from the new interpretation and full-profile LLN.
