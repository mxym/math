# Source and priority notes

1. N. Fernando and B. Kousik, *A further study of polynomial \(g_{n,q}\) over finite fields*, [arXiv:2606.01037v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01037v1), 31 May 2026, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.01037). Equation (1.1) defines \(g_{n,q}\) by the translation sum, and §2 sets the notation \(S_r\). **Question 6.6**, [PDF pp. 17–18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01037v1#page=17), asks which even \(e>2\) make the given \(g_{n,4}\) a PP of \(\mathbb F_{4^e}\). Its exponent is \(n_{e,e/2-1}\), since \((3e-2)/2=e+(e/2-1)\). The two printed terms \(S_e S_{3e/2-1}\) are both present in the PDF and arXiv HTML; they cancel in characteristic two. The proof rederives the formal polynomial from (1.1), so it does not depend on assuming a correction or an omitted coefficient.
2. The same paper's **Theorem 3.4** proves that \(g_{n_{e,2},4}\) is a PP when \(e>3\) is even and \(10\nmid e\). Its Example 3.5 is \(e=6,n=137249\), also the exact instance \(a=e/2-1=2\) of Question 6.6. Its Table 1 lists the \(e=4,n=2441\) observation under Question 6.6. The all-parameter classification, the reverse implication for the \(a=2\) subfamily, and the independent defining-polynomial derivation are the claims here; the positive instances just cited are not claimed new.
3. N. Fernando and X. Hou, *From \(r\)-linearized polynomial equations to \(r^m\)-linearized polynomial equations*, Finite Fields and Their Applications 37 (2016), 14–27, [DOI 10.1016/j.ffa.2015.08.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ffa.2015.08.003), [arXiv:1503.03162v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.03162). Its Table 1 already lists \(g_{2441,4}\) as a permutation over \(\mathbb F_{4^4}\), without a reference in that row; it is the pre-existing finite instance motivating Question 6.6. Its method of splitting into trace fibers also underlies Proposition 3.1 of source 1. No originality is claimed for trace-fiber splitting or the elementary fixed-subfield calculation in isolation.
