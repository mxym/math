# A linear-size extremal kernel for the signature of a line graph

Working manuscript, AI-assisted and internally checked; not externally peer reviewed. All graphs under optimization are finite simple and connected. Write $c=m-n+1$, $s=n_+(A(L(G)))-n_-(A(L(G)))$, and $M=Q(G)-2I$. The signature of a real symmetric matrix counts its positive eigenvalues minus its negative eigenvalues, ignoring any zeros. The standard incidence identity gives s=sig M-c+1. Paone–Paone Conjecture 5.4 asks 2s≤c+1. Their existing family gives f(c)≥floor((c+1)/2) for all c≥1; their spanning-tree estimate f(c)≤c includes c=0,1. We do not solve the general conjecture.

**Theorem.** For c≥2, every connected graph G has another simple connected graph H with the same cyclomatic number and s(L(H))≥s(L(G)), on at most 19(c-1) vertices. H is obtained from a connected multigraph kernel (loops and parallel edges allowed) with all degrees at least three by (i) realizing each edge by a fresh path of length in {3,4,5,6} joining its endpoints and (ii) optionally adding one leaf at each kernel vertex. In particular f(c) is exactly computable on this explicit finite set. Moreover f(c)=floor((c+1)/2) for all c≤5, including all forest attachments and subdivisions; no graph-order restriction.

### 1. Pendant forests: an extremizer only needs single leaves

As in Paone–Paone Lemma 3.5 and Prop. 3.6, for one branch T rooted at its vertex adjoining the host put B=Q(T)-2I+e_r e_r^T, sigma=sig B, g=(B^-1)_rr. With child quantities (sigma_i,g_i), root scalar after congruence is x=-1+sum_i (1-g_i), sigma=sum_i sigma_i+sign x, g=1/x. When there are no children x=-1. Inductively all g are rational odd/odd since x is -1 modulo 2 in the local ring of rationals with odd denominator. Thus x never vanishes. Eliminating a complete branch from a graph contributes sigma to the signature and adds 1-g to the old host diagonal at its port, as relative to the host without that branch. The host itself may well be singular.

For t=-sigma we claim t≥0; t=0 implies g≥1; t=1 implies g≥-1. Induct on vertices. Let T0=sum_i t_i, then t=T0-sign x. If T0=0, x≤-1, giving t=1 and g≥-1. If T0=1, at most one child has nonzero budget, so x≤1 by induction. A positive pivot gives t=0 and g≥1; a negative pivot is harmless. For T0≥2 one has t≥1, with equality possible only for a positive pivot, hence g>0.

Now aggregate all branches at a host vertex: put T0=sum_i t_i and y=sum_i(1-g_i). At cost T0 in signature we add y ee^T. If T0=0, y≤0 and deleting all is at least as good by Loewner monotonicity of the signature. If T0=1, y≤2 and replacing the entire collection by one leaf with shift +2 and cost 1 is at least as good. If T0≥2, deleting the collection is at least as good since arbitrary rank-one diagonal increase can raise sig by at most 2, by interlacing, including zero modes. These comparisons work against every surrounding symmetric matrix, so apply at every 2-core vertex. We are left with a nonempty connected 2-core K and a set S of its vertices carrying one pendant leaf. Eliminating the leaves gives
    sig M(G_reduced) = sig(M(K)+2 diag(1_S))-|S|.

### 2. Marked degree-two links can be rounded away

Call the remaining leaf supports marked. Vertices of K having degree ≥3 are the branches. Since c≥2 there is at least one branch, and the maximal internal-degree-two paths between branches (possibly a common endpoint) partition the core edges. Temporarily use signed links on these paths; a link of sign e=±1 contributes e to the out-of-diagonal quadratic form coefficient /2, and degrees count both incidences regardless of sign. A loop of sign e contributes 2e to the diagonal, with degree counting both ends.

Take a maximal consecutive run of m≥1 marked internal vertices on one such path. Its ends are joined to unmarked internal or branch vertices, which need not be distinct and may themselves be marked branch vertices. On the run the shifted core has precisely the positive definite tridiagonal 2I+A(P_m) by switching the internal coordinates (the original links have sign +). Its signature m cancels the leaf cost for these vertices. The two inverse corner entries on the diagonal equal m/(m+1), the off-corner (-1)^(m-1)/(m+1); this follows from det T_j=j+1 and cofactors, including coincident corners at m=1. Consequently at the two external endpoints, in two formally independent terminal coordinates, the Schur correction equals
     (1/(m+1))*[[-m,e],[e,-m]],  e=(-1)^m
for originally positive links. Replace this by the signed unit link [[0,e],[e,0]]. The difference is (m/(m+1))*[[1,e],[e,1]], positive semidefinite. If endpoints coincide take the pullback along (z,z); the same conclusion holds. All marks on internal vertices are disjoint runs and their Schur signatures exactly pay for themselves, so doing this simultaneously cannot decrease sig. Degrees at the surviving vertices are unchanged. Marks on branch vertices remain as above.

A signed unit link can be realized by an odd-length ordinary path: a length-1 link has sign +, a length-3 path has sign -, the two internal degree-two vertices pivot out as a hyperbolic pair (their path adjacency has inverse itself). To avoid accidental loops/parallel direct edges one can use length 5 for + and length 3 for -, with disjoint internal vertices. More generally removing four consecutive unmarked internal vertices and replacing the five links by one of the same sign, with path endpoint form unchanged and signature offset zero, is the P4 Schur identity (corner inverse 0 on the diagonal, -1 off-diagonal). Thus the signed or realized paths between branch vertices may be replaced, without signature change, by ordinary paths whose lengths are canonical representatives modulo four in {3,4,5,6}; one may increase length by four to reach a canonical representative if needed. This also works for a loop by pulling back the terminal form, and for zero couplings to other interior components because internal vertices have no outside neighbors. All these canonical paths realize a simple graph even for loops or repeated kernel edges.

Put b=number of branches and e=number of kernel edges, so e=b+c-1 and 2e≥3b. Hence b≤2c-2 and e≤3c-3. There are ≤5e internal vertices, ≤b marked pendant leaves and b branches, giving **19(c-1)** vertices with the uniform nonminimal {3,4,5,6} choice.

### 3. Sharp cases c≤5

Delete the b branch vertices from the matrix on the realized unmarked paths (retaining the optional mark shifts +2 on branches until the last step). The remaining principal matrix consists of zero-diagonal path adjacencies, each of signature zero. Extension by one vertex can increase signature by at most one by interlacing. Hence sig M≤b-h, where h counts the retained marked leaves. Together with b≤2c-2 this yields the desired 2 sig M≤3c-1 for c=2,3. For c=4,5 a failure needs b=2c-2=6,8 respectively, every branch cubic, no branch marks, and equality sig M=b in this argument.

To analyze this last possibility, eliminate every internal path adjacency block. For even vertex-length (odd number of edges) path interiors, the block is invertible and has zero signature, diagonal endpoint correction 0 and off-diagonal correction +1 or -1. For odd vertex-length interiors the kernel is spanned by the alternating odd-coordinate vector (1,0,-1,...,±1). On its orthogonal complement its invertible part has balanced inertia: bipartiteness pairs ± nonzero eigenvalues; also its generalized inverse has all same-parity entries zero by bipartite symmetry, so both endpoint diagonals and the corner correction vanish. Its kernel couples to the terminals by the equality constraint z_u±z_v=0. Simultaneously the remaining matrix has the form [[B,D],[D^T,0]], whose signature equals that of the compression of B to ker D^T (pair the independent coupled kernel directions hyperbolically via congruence; the remaining kernel is zero). B has diagonal deg(v)-2 plus signed odd-loop contributions, and off-diagonal the signed sum of odd links. Therefore sig M=b requires ALL even-link constraints to vanish identically and B to be positive definite. In particular all nonloop links must be odd. An even loop whose constraint is identically zero does not alter the argument.

Call the vertices without a topological kernel loop ordinary. Their diagonal in B is 1. For distinct ordinary vertices a,b, strict positive definiteness of the 2-by-2 minor gives |B_ab|<1, hence B_ab=0 by integrality. All nonloop paths between ordinary vertices are odd and contribute ±1, so any two adjacent ordinary vertices are joined by at least two parallel kernel edges. In a cubic kernel any ordinary vertex therefore has at most one *distinct* ordinary neighbor. Each looped vertex has only one nonloop incidence and is a leaf in the underlying simple kernel for b≥2. Deleting these leaves leaves a connected graph on the ordinary vertices if nonempty, hence there can be at most two. With two, their at least two mutual links leave at most one looped neighbor per ordinary vertex: b≤4. With one, at most three looped neighbors so b≤4. With zero, connectedness gives b≤2. This contradicts b∈{6,8}. Thus the same upper inequality holds for c=4,5, and the previous sharp lower examples finish. Explicitly Paone–Paone Theorem 5.3 gives signatures 1, 1, 2, 2, 3 for c=1, 2, 3, 4, 5 respectively: the odd cases are their amplifier family, and the even cases append their neutral 4-cycle module. At c=0 the path on three vertices attains zero. Proposition 5.2 gives the already known upper bounds for c=0 and 1.
