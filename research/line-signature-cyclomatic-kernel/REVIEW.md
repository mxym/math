The incidence Gram matrices share their nonzero eigenvalues; subtracting 2I changes the unequal numbers of zeros into negatives, giving the stated signature offset. The conditioned tree-root Schur pivot is odd in the localization at 2 and cannot vanish. The induction on the signature cost has exactly three cases: zero means additions cannot raise the host diagonal; budget one allows at most +2 and is dominated by one leaf; budget at least two cannot be recovered through any rank-one shift, whose signature gain is at most two. These bounds hold regardless of the other attachments and a possible zero eigenvalue in the host, so every attached forest may be reduced at its fixed root in the core.

A block of consecutive marked degree-two vertices has positive Cartan block 2I+A(P_m); its m positive directions pay for precisely those m leaves. Its endpoint Schur correction has diagonal -m/(m+1) and coupling (-1)^m/(m+1). Replacing it by the corresponding unit signed link is a positive rank-one update, preserved for a loop by pulling back the two terminal coordinates to the same coordinate. Different runs are separated by a surviving point, so this can be done simultaneously, with endpoints keeping the same incidence-counted degrees. Odd paths realize either sign with zero internal signature cost. Elimination or insertion of a zero-signature P4 internal block gives the mod-four normalization, also valid with a shared endpoint. All canonical paths of lengths 3–6 use disjoint interiors, so they realize a simple graph even from a multigraph kernel. Its at most 2c−2 branches and at most 3c−3 links plus at most one leaf per branch yield 19(c−1) vertices.

Deleting all b branches leaves only disjoint interior path blocks of signature zero. Principal interlacing bounds the increase by b, and branch leaves still cost their number. For c=2,3 this implies the desired bound; for c=4,5 only the extreme no-leaf cubic kernel could violate it. Zero eigenvectors of an odd interior create explicit single linear constraints on endpoint coordinates; the nonsingular part has balanced inertia and same-parity pseudoinverse entries vanish by bipartite symmetry. In the aggregate block, rank of the coupling enforces the compression dimension, so equality with signature b requires no nonzero constraint and positive definiteness of the integer effective matrix. Every distinct pair of ordinary branch vertices has diagonal 1, hence positive-definite 2-by-2 minor forces integer off-diagonal 0. A connection between them requires at least two opposite odd signed links, so at most one distinct ordinary neighbor is possible in the cubic kernel. Looped vertices are individual leaves in the underlying simple kernel as they have only one remaining incidence. Deleting them leaves a connected ordinary graph of at most two vertices if nonempty, implying at most four branches total, against six or eight. The lower examples and small c≤1 are existing input, not new constructions.
