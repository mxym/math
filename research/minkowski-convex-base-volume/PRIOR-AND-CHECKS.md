# Prior work, duplicate search and validation

## Phrasing and priority

- [Fradelizi, Madiman, Marsiglietti and Zvavitch, *The convexification effect of Minkowski summation*, published text](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/37001), EMS Surv. Math. Sci. 5 (2018), 1–64, §4.2. Their Theorem 4.5 treats three **convex** compact summands in all dimensions; Prop. 4.14 disproves the uncorrected extension to arbitrary compact sets; Prop. 4.15 proves the **hull-corrected** estimate on the line. Remark 4.16(1) asks whether Prop. 4.15 extends to higher dimensions and in particular asks about convex A. No claims about the other items of Remark 4.16.
- [Fradelizi, Madiman and Zvavitch, *Sumset Estimates in Convex Geometry*, published text](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10575771), IMRN 2024(15), 11426–11454, §3.3; [preprint §3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01565). The paragraph **immediately before Conjecture 3.11** identifies the all-dimensional hull-corrected inequality as unknown. Conjecture 3.11 asks the convex-body base version. Theorem 3.12 proves the latter in every dimension if A is convex compact, B a zonoid, C compact (or vice versa in B,C). Our note uses neither a zonoid assumption nor mixed volume machinery; it does not answer adjacent Question 3.10 about higher iterates. Numbering was crosschecked against the published version, not deduced from an index.
- [Barthe and Madiman, *Volumes of subset Minkowski sums and the Lyusternik region*, 2024, §5 last bullet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06518), [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-023-00606-w). Separately states the convex-base version as open for arbitrary compact summands, explicitly recording the zonoid result. Its *fractional* theorem and the characterization of the entire Lyusternik region are different questions.
- [Fradelizi, Langharst, Madiman and Zvavitch, *Weighted Brunn–Minkowski theory I*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.13522), concluding section, eq. (52). Notes this conjecture and discusses failure of its Gaussian-measure analogue; our assertion uses translation-invariant Lebesgue measure.
- [R. Skorupinski (Aug. 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07702) and [Fradelizi et al. (Aug. 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12681) disprove **multiplicative log-submodular** conjectures. The two sides are products, not sums, and the conjectured order is reversed. These are not disproofs or proofs of additive Conjecture 3.11. Recent author's [publication list](https://www.math.kent.edu/~zvavitch/Research.html) was checked for similarly named work.
- Related language can mislead: [Kampf and Kiderlen, *Large parallel volumes of finite and compact sets*](https://www2.math.ethz.ch/EMIS/journals/DMJDMV/vol-18/09.pdf) studies dilation by a Euclidean ball, and asymptotic deficits comparing a set with its own convex hull, not this comparison with an arbitrary compact summand. Nearest-point/support-plane characterizations for convex sums are standard; no novelty of the projection characterization itself is asserted.
- Search included the number and exact formula of Conj. 3.11; FMMZ Remark 4.16; hull-corrected and set-increment formulations; combinations of these with supermodularity, Lyusternik region, normal cone, projection, exterior, convex hull, parallel volume, nonconvex and the names of the authors; searches for arXiv, public code/formalization and repository hits, and a subject literature search. No positive identification of an existing all-dimensional proof was made. This does **not** certify priority or that the finding is new: unindexed/independently circulated papers, other terminology and discrete/lattice formulations remain possible. The subsequent internal AI review is recorded in [REVIEW.md](REVIEW.md). There has been no external validation or priority certification.

## Exact supporting computations

Run from this directory with `python -B -O verify.py`. Failure raises an explicit exception, including under -O. Results:
```
PASS: 3375 hull-corrected area cases (1026 equalities); 160 exterior-area cases (34 equalities).
```
For the first check, the base is a union of unit lattice squares, chosen by any nonempty subset of four square origins; the other two summands are any nonempty subsets of four points. Each actual sum is a union of unit squares of integer origins, so the double-area computation is exact. Convex hulls use a monotone chain plus the shoelace formula. For the exterior theorem, deterministic cases include a point, segment and polygons as the reference K and sets of unit lattice cells as E. Exact polygon clipping with Python rational arithmetic gives the two exterior measures directly, independently of the map used in the proof. These finite checks cannot establish the theorem or priority.
