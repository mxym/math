## Two independent routes

An alternative proof makes the stronger exterior comparison particularly transparent. In dimension d≥1 choose a countable dense sequence of unit vectors u_i (in dimension one both signs suffice). Write h_M(u)=max_{m∈M} u·m. For each i select one b_i∈B with u_i·b_i=h_B(u_i); no measurability in u is required. A compact convex K is precisely the intersection of {x : u_i·x≤h_K(u_i)}: strict separation of any x outside K persists in an open neighbourhood of the separating direction because h_K is Lipschitz on the sphere. Decompose E∖K into the countably many Borel layers L_i on which i is the **first** violated inequality. Translate L_i by b_i. For y=x+b_i with x∈L_i,
- u_i·y>h_K(u_i)+h_B(u_i);
- for j<i, u_j·y≤h_K(u_j)+h_B(u_j).

Since h_{K+conv B}=h_K+h_B, the i-th shifted layer is outside K+conv B, and i is its first violated shifted inequality. All shifted layers lie in E+B and are disjoint. Countable additivity and translation invariance now give the exterior-volume bound for arbitrary nonempty compact B directly. This proof does not assume a finitely generated hull, uniqueness of maximizers, measurable selection on the whole sphere, volume continuity under Hausdorff convergence, interior, or a regular boundary.

I independently checked the author's different projection proof:
1. The compact convex projection P_K exists, is unique and continuous (also for a point, segment or flat). The variational inequality at p is necessary and sufficient by differentiation along segments and squared-distance expansion.
2. For finite B choose the first maximizer of n·b at n=x−P_Kx. Its selection depends only on the full vector n. Weak inequalities for later indices and strict inequalities for earlier indices cut out Borel sets. For q=P_K(x)+b, every z=k+v, v∈conv B satisfies (x+b−q)·(z−q)≤0. Here maximization on conv B follows from linearity. Therefore q=P_{K+conv B}(x+b) and n=x+b−q≠0. If image points coincide, their residuals and then their selected b and preimages coincide, including positive-volume tie regions. Finite unions of disjoint translated Borel pieces have the claimed measure.
3. For F⊂B a finite δ-net, conv B⊂conv F+δD. One cannot just take the exterior of K+conv F, a smaller obstacle. The draft instead uses K+δD as reference in the finite step, so its obstacle contains the target obstacle while E+F⊂E+B. Thus its exterior is a subset of the target exterior. The final lower bounds tend monotonically upwards along δ=1/m to |E∖K|; no unproved assertion about arbitrary Hausdorff-limit volumes.
4. Translate C so that 0∈C. E=A+C contains A, but need not contain K=conv A. The part A+B is inside both E+B and K+conv B; count it disjointly from the exterior. Then |E∖K|≥|E|−|K|. The nested-set increment corollary uses K⊂E explicitly and the same disjointness for K+B. All compact differences here are Borel; the compact convex hull is closed in finite dimension. Empty summands must not be admitted: for B empty already the exterior comparison need not hold. No positive-volume or full-dimensional assumption is needed. The authors' 2018 triple gives a sharp hull correction and invalidates using the nonconvex base volume instead.

The two maps really can choose different translations; the supporting-index proof is not an alternative phrasing of nearest-point reconstruction.

- [Barthe & Madiman, §5, final bullet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06518#page=16) records the open ordinary additive increment inequality plus the zonoid case. Their fractional result should not be credited here. Their published [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-023-00606-w).
- Finite-scale, nonconvex structuring elements are a material scope distinction. [Kiderlen & Rataj, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03339#page=8) handles general compact structuring elements but estimates the **infinitesimal** outer Minkowski content with finite-perimeter hypotheses; this is not the exterior inequality for arbitrary positive radii and possibly degenerate compact sets.
