## Commonness estimates

1. **Fixed graphs and the zero case.** In the centered-edge expansion the single-edge component integrates to zero. In a forest component with at least two edges, two distinct leaves can be integrated; their neighbors can be equal, in which case the bound is the single integral of $z^2$, or distinct, when the remaining bounded factors give the square of the $L^1$ norm of $z$. For a cyclic subgraph, fix the variables outside a shortest chordless cycle. The remaining multipliers have modulus at most one; Schatten Hölder bounds its trace by the fourth-power trace because the cycle length is at least four. In the block computation the nonnegative square $2\|(pI+D)z\|^2$ can be dropped; $s^2\le s$ and $\|D\|\le1$ give $s+\operatorname{Tr}L^4\le4s+\Delta$. Since $\Delta\ge2p^2s$, (1) follows uniformly when $p\ge1/4$. If $\Delta=0$ then $\operatorname{Tr}L^4=0$ and the self-adjoint compact operator $L$ vanishes. Normalizations and the sparse-color proof use no division by a zero density. At $p=0,1$ the graphon is constant a.e.
2. **The rooted error terms (3).** Gluing two copies at exactly one vertex (the same part) gives a simple bipartite pattern with twice the number of edges. Its density is the conditional second moment of the rooted count by Fubini on the independent nonglued variables. Thus the variance estimates cost only $C_{132}+2C_{66}$ and $C_8$, and the constants $2^{140}$ and $2^{14}$ are conservative. The unrooted $(13,22)$ and $(13,23)$ biclique completions bound $m$ and $n$ below by $p^{286}$ and $p^{299}$ respectively; adding edges can only decrease the product since this part uses $0\le W\le1$.
3. **Potentially dangerous bridge correlation (4)–(5).** The rooted codegree square is $c(x)=\int K(x,y)^2dy$. Integrating $K-p^2$ against $y$ gives $pz+Lz$. This explains both the sign and the coefficient $2p^3z$ in (5). With $r(x)=\int(K(x,y)-p^2)^2dy$, the exact mean is $\int r=\Delta-2p^2s$. The potentially negative term $\int zr$ costs at most $p\int r$ because $z\ge-p$; the cancellation with $p\Delta$ leaves the *positive* $4p^3s$. The term $\langle z,Lz\rangle$ can have either sign and only its lower bound $-\eta s$ is used. At small $s$ the other inequality $p\Delta-2^7\sqrt{s\Delta}$ is used instead. These two regimes yield the stated uniform $2^{-25}\Delta$. In the fixed H term, $L$ acts on the mean-zero $g$ rather than acting at size one. The loss is at most $2^{-675+70+7}\Delta=2^{-598}\Delta$, while $mB\ge 2^{-597}\Delta$. In particular, the sign is not inferred from positive correlation of arbitrary rooted patterns.
4. **Convexity (6).** The $M$ squares are independent **conditional on the common root**, giving exactly $\int bc^M$, not a product of unrooted densities. With $n>0$, the tilted Jensen measure $b/n$ is legitimate and its mean of $c$ is $p^4+I/n\ge0$. Equivalently one may multiply the pointwise tangent at $p^4$ by $b$ and integrate; this directly yields the linear term without division by $n$. Since (4) is nonnegative and $p^4\le1$, replacing $M I/p^4$ by $M\kappa\Delta$ loses nothing in the inequality direction.
5. **Pointwise complete-bipartite lower bound (7).** Conditional on root $x$, each of the $\beta$ independent opposite vertices is drawn from the measure with density $W(x,y)/d(x)$. With $Z=\int\prod_i W(u,Y_i)du$, the exact rooted biclique density is $d^\beta\mathbb E[Z^{\alpha-1}]$. Jensen, independence of the $Y_i$ (not independence of unrooted graph counts), and Jensen for $K^2$ give the displayed formula. The power $d^{\beta(2-\alpha)}\ge1$, so dropping it is a lower bound. Here the new root is on the former face side, $\alpha=23,\beta=13$, giving exponent 143. At a zero-degree root $c=0$ and no division by $d$ is needed. Jensen on $c^{M+143}$ now provides (7). Its far-regime dividend relative to $p^{4M}$ is at least $p_0^{572}(1+M\delta)\ge1$.
6. **Sparse color.** In the far regime $q^{67+4M}\le p^{4M}q^{67}$, so $p^{67}+q^{67}\le1$ suffices. In the near regime the complement has centered operator $-L$, hence identical $s$ and fourth-power trace even when its own $q$ is tiny. Its rooted first moment at $q^4$ is the difference of fixed counts on patterns of 71 and 67 edges (the latter multiplied by $q^4$), so it costs $C_{71}+C_{67}$ times the *dense-color* square excess. The convex tangent is applied without dividing by $q$. After dividing by $p^{4M}$ only, use $p^{-4}<4$ and $(q/p)^{4M-4}<3^{-(4M-4)}$ to absorb both error terms. This proves the density-specific **strong** bound even when a color is negligible; it does not just prove the symmetric random-color benchmark.

## Rank-layer input and rooted uniformity

Read the immutable [OpenAI kernel source](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-counterexample-to-Sidorenkos-conjecture-September-23-2026/build/sections/kernel.tex) (Git blob `bbe11be8fffe968012060ec43e6aff597e922831`) and [Table 1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-counterexample-to-Sidorenkos-conjecture-September-23-2026/build/sections/complex.tex) (blob `178b0e9da21867a727ea847dae8e537b9d719a70`).

- Eq. (3.6) is an expectation of a product of normalized rank-layer functions of independent additive matrix differences. For any one fixed matrix tuple and both fixed types, averaging only over the other tuple already gives one: component differences are independent uniform matrices for the active coordinates. This gives exact row and column means **before** and after thinning and dilution. On the symmetric crossed kernel the two factors use separate draws and give exact row degree one.
- Source Table 1 has faces 0 and 1 incident to both points 0 and 1. For its four edges, (3.4) has just one possible complete square. Lemma 3.1 is uniformly valid over subsets of the original 66 distinct incidences and sign choices; it may be restricted to these four by integrating additional independent unused variables. The four distinct edge packages are independently sampled conditional on the four types; repeating a type does not identify its independent matrix labels or its edge packages. For each nonempty coordinate subset, the single-incidence conditional first sign moments in (3.7) vanish even if the coordinates in a given package are dependent. Thus the product of its four such conditional moments is zero. There are finitely many coordinates, possible packages and types, all fixed independent of the prime. This gives rectangular square density tending to one with the same parameters used for the strict deficit.
- **Alternative independent derivation of the needed uniform conclusion.** The exact rectangular marginals imply that for *every* left-root $x$ the like-side codegree $K_X(x,x')$ has $x'$-mean exactly one. Its square mean $C^X(x)$ is therefore at least one, and likewise for $C^Y$. Simultaneously translate the group component at every square vertex by the same group tuple: this fixes every difference but sends the root to any desired matrix tuple of its type. Consequently $C^X,C^Y$ depend only on the type. If $\pi_{\min}>0$ is the least positive left-type prior, then
  $$
   0\le \sup_x(C^X(x)-1)\le [t_{\rm rect}(C_4,W_r)-1]/\pi_{\min}
  $$
  on the support; the analogous bound holds on the right with its own positive minimum. This uses only the **unrooted** rectangular square limit above and positivity; it does not assume uniformity in a growing matrix space. The conditional-type proof in the manuscript gives the same result directly.
- For a fixed symmetric root $(x,y)$, the two crossed rectangular products use the two opposite sets of coordinates of the other three independent product-space vertices. This gives the exact factorization $c_r(x,y)=C^X_r(x)C^Y_r(y)$. For each fixed finite $M$ the sup error in $c_r^M$ tends to zero. By nonnegative row sums equal to one, the operator on that error is an $L^\infty$ contraction. Therefore the bridge loss is at most $t(H,V_r)\|c_r^M-1\|_\infty$ regardless of the size of the rooted H integrand. Neither ordinary cut convergence of a uniformly bounded sequence nor independence of $h_r$ and $c_r$ is assumed.
- The original parameters are chosen in the right order: sufficiently large fixed even dimension, 66 coordinates and sign parameter, finite thinning/score scale and priors, then a sufficiently small positive dilution, and only then take the prime large. Fix $M$ independently **before** that prime limit. Equations (3.24) and (3.25) yield a product limit strictly below one for the core. Scaling the selected *single* finite kernel by its maximum lowers the edge-density benchmark and graph density by the requisite powers. The finite-simple-host sampling argument handles noninjective maps by a union bound, not by pretending the repeated edges are independent. No concrete host or rate in the prime was computed, and the deep rank-layer lemma was not reproved.

## Antecedents and remaining limitations

- [Grzesik–Lee–Lidický–Volec, journal p. 908](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548322000074) ask an **unnumbered** converse question for ordinary bipartite common graphs. There is no connectedness restriction there. Their **different Conjecture 1.3** on adjoining independent apices explicitly assumes connectedness. This construction concerns only the unnumbered question and strengthens the earlier [disconnected note](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/0deb2d848775ea6a76b5aee6da61374e9129286c/research/bipartite-strongly-common-non-sidorenko/README.md). It is not a 2-connected separation, an answer to apex Conjecture 1.3, or a determination of commonness for OpenAI's 35-vertex core itself.
- [Král’–Volec–Wei, Theorems 18, 23 and 24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05800) already use path connections, forests, spectral estimates and dense bipartite padding in proving commonness. Theorem 23 assumes a core of girth at least 50 and guarantees ordinary commonness after a specified augmentation; the present core has girth four. Its conclusion does not give this strong inequality or preserve this core's strict Sidorenko deficit. [Lee–Noel, eq. (2)](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v33i2p1/pdf/) distinguishes the strong bound and notes what was known before the September counterexample. [Král’ et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09422) show the equivalence with commonness **for every color count**. Here there are two colors. No worldwide novelty or exhaustive literature coverage established.
- A preliminary version of the finite checker originally **silently skipped** its incidence-data validation if an adjacent checkout was absent. Fixed by embedding and always testing the Table 1 triples, reconstructing all 66 edges and doing a BFS of all 35 vertices, and checking the distinguished square. The revised script was copied alone into an empty directory and passed with `python3 -B -O`; a deliberately corrupted duplicate face failed. Its 128 small-kernel checks and finite constants are diagnostics, not a proof.
- [controls_1.py](controls_1.py) is a separate implementation: ten exact weighted two-atom tests integrate the *actual* 13-point/22-face graph via the 8192 point assignments, including complements; a nonconstant rank-one test at $p>3/4$ has $0<\Delta<2^{-2706}$ and verifies the true local bridge gain with exact rationals; a nonuniform-type rectangular 4-by-6 model directly checks type-dependent square densities, exact normalized marginals, the factorization on all 24 symmetric kernel roots and three weighted bridge moments. These are not computations of the finite-field lemma, the huge graph's full density, or a violating finite host.
