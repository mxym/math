## Proof checks

1. A valid finite downward-closed structure includes every singleton and the empty face. For a nonempty ground set every vertex lies in a maximal face, so a single vertex misses at most M−1 facets. A set of vertices is a face iff at least one facet contains all of it; the respective missed-facet sets cover all labels exactly for nonfaces.
2. For (a,b)=(M+N+1,j), the projectors for (a,b) and (−b,a) sum to I, while every ray in this whole collection is different: each positive slope is different, each negative slope is different, and the slope types are separated. The effects are real rational symmetric Hermitian matrices on **one** complex qubit, with no ancillary Hilbert dimension or postselection.
3. The normalized pairs of effects carry total scalar weights w and ε, not 2w and 2ε. One setting uses weight at most (M−1)w+ε<1. If M=1, the formal w=2 exceeds 1 but is never assigned to an outcome or parent. Edgeless-on-singletons, identical-incidence vertices, the full simplex, and N=1 cause no other issue; N=0 is separately compatible. Counting two outcomes per missed facet, two private and one null gives 2M+1.
4. For a face the maximum distinct shared mass is (M−1)w and private mass is Nε; the probability weight of the scalar residual is at least 1/[2(2M−1)]. The deterministic output maps give simultaneous non-null marginals exactly, and normalization forces the specified null marginal. Merging latent parent atoms with the same reported Cartesian tuple still produces a finite joint POVM.
5. For a nonface fix one eligible setting per shared label; representatives need not be different. An atom below a nonzero ray effect has range contained in that ray: evaluating the positive quadratic form on the orthogonal complement and taking a positive square root proves it. An atom contributing to two marked events for distinct rays must vanish, even though the rays need not be perpendicular. Overlap of events asking for distinct outcomes of the same setting is empty. The sum of all selected marginals is MwI>I, whereas a positive parent would bound it above by I. This argument is in operator order and does not assume trace additivity over intersecting positive atoms without first removing the zero atoms.
6. Distinctness persists under classical postprocessing as well as relabeling. A positive multiple of a private rank-one projector cannot be written as a nonnegative combination of another setting's effects: a summand with nonzero coefficient must have range on that ray. The other setting has no rank-one effect on the ray and its null effect is full rank. No claim of inequivalence under independently changing the ambient qubit basis is necessary.
7. Equivalent literature-based nonface proof: retain the two outcomes of missed facet j in the representative setting and merge **every other outcome** into null. This is the lossy sharp basis with efficiency w and null effect (1−w)I. A common parent for the original family gives one for all M extracted basis measurements by the same simultaneous classical maps, including when a single original setting is used more than once. The known 1/M threshold contradicts w>1/M. Only M≥2 arises for nonfaces. Thus the adaptive grouping of shared basis labels according to facet complements, not incompatibility of lossy sharp bases, is the relevant combinatorial step.

## Prior scope and overlap

* [Andrejić and Kunjwal (2020), Defs. 1–3 and Conj. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00785), [published](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.043147): Def. 1 distinguishes general POVMs from binary qubit POVMs; Def. 2 allows differing outcome sets; Def. 3 specifies compatible subsets exactly. The quantifier on POVMs in Conj. 4 does not state a binary restriction. The authors' concrete construction studies binary cases. The present result should specify finite vertices, not infinite complexes (Def. 3 describes finite hyperedges; no inference about arbitrary infinite ground sets).
* [Kunjwal (2025), §§5.1, 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10063): Def. 5.1 uses O₁×⋯×O_N, Def. 5.2 assigns **different** measurements to vertices, and §5.3 explicitly restates the conjecture for every finite N without imposing binary outcomes. A sufficient condition specifically for binary POVMs is listed separately.
* [Kunjwal, Heunen and Fritz (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5948): finite complexes realized using POVMs, but with growing Hilbert dimension. The assertion that arbitrary complexes have quantum realizations is not the new part.
* [Skrzypczyk and Cavalcanti (2015), §VII Eq. (18), Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04926) ([journal](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.92.022354)): rank-one PVMs with no shared outcomes (maximal overlap <1) and a heralded null outcome have common-efficiency compatibility threshold 1/n. Appendix E supplies the parent at threshold. Its sufficiency extends to inefficient POVMs in general. This prior deserves credit; it does not state how to encode every finite complex on one qubit. The elementary support proof here is an alternative in the special case used and must not be presented as a new lossy-measurement threshold.
* [2026 coplanar criterion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38836) addresses an Andrejić–Kunjwal conjecture about jointly measuring coplanar unbiased binary measurements, not realization of arbitrary complexes. [2026 Quantum paper on n-wise simulability](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2026-10-08-2231/) compares notions of simulability/compatibility; not the present representation result. [McNulty 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15954) uses the anti-commutativity graph for binary observables to study robustness, a different graph invariant.
* I independently searched variants of arbitrary joint-measurability structures, qubit multi-outcome realizability, incompatibility hypergraphs, simplicial complexes, losses and finite measurement dimensions. I found no prior source giving the facet construction or universality with unbounded finite outcomes in dimension two. Ordinary web/arXiv and source-paper checks are not exhaustive. The author's repository/attachment scan is a separate record, not part of this independent external-prior search.

## Finite regression (not a substitute for the proof)

The independent standard-library verifier enumerates full-vertex downward-closed face families directly. It builds the actual rational symmetric matrices and checks PSD, normalization and margins for every face and each whole marginal. For nonfaces it checks a lossy basis extraction, positive determinant of every sum of distinct selected rank-one effects (hence ray distinction), and the excess over I. It tests the private support obstruction to postprocessing in every ordered pair.
