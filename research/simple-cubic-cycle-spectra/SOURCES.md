# Sources and boundary of the result

* Ryan McCulloch, Brendan D. McKay, Alireza Salahshoori and Thomas Zaslavsky, [*The Cycle Counts of Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-026-03047-3), *Graphs and Combinatorics* **42**, article 53 (2026); [arXiv:2507.02260v3, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02260v3). The published and v3 lists in (b,d) agree. They posed the inverse problem for 2-connected simple cubic graphs and for the planar subclass, proposed exception lists and reported finite positive occurrences. We compare those literal lists; we do not infer why the data differ from our census. The general-class correction is an additional 16–21, and the planar correction is the additional 42, *not* 16–21 a second time. Their Theorem 1 already excludes 16 for every inseparable graph. Their Theorem 2 determines the spectrum when **parallel edges are allowed**, not the simple cubic spectrum. Their Corollary 10 treats nonplanar inseparable graphs below 24 cycles. Their Lemma 4 is exactly the path-count increment on adding an ear used in our enumeration. We give the open-ear completeness argument explicitly; the classical source is Hassler Whitney, [*Non-separable and planar graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1932-1501641-2) (1932).
* The authors' [linked catalog](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/graphs.html) provides data for other parts of the original paper, including multigraphs encoded via subdivisions. No such dataset is required to run the present computation. Its graph searches and planarity certificates are generated by the scripts here.
* For an extremal but different question, Clark and Entringer, [*The minimum number of cycles in graphs with given cycle rank and small connectivity*](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%2003/vol-003-paper%2014.pdf), determine minima given rank and connectivity. We do not assume any numerical lower bound by order in our stopping rule.
* The previously established [cofinite simple-cubic construction](../simple-cubic-prescribed-cycle-count/PROOF.md) is used only for k≥2679. See [#188](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/188) and the two finite-scope corrections [#190](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/190) and [#191](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/191). The earlier [#184 cycle appendix](../logarithmic-subtree-counts/CYCLES.md) treats cubic multigraphs or simple **subcubic** graphs and does not imply the classification here.
