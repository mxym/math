# Sources, prior results and scope

* Aadityan Ganesh, Jason Hartline, Atanu R. Sinha and Matthew vonAllmen, [*Fundamental Limits of Throughput and Availability*, EC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3670865.3673553), [full author's version, arXiv:2402.19292v3, introduction, §§2.1 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19292v3). Their unnumbered conjecture asks for a unit-jump Poisson demand with availability and throughput simultaneously no greater, at the same supply and for independent up-to-unit demands. Availability is strict ($S<\kappa$); throughput is expected capped total **divided by** $\kappa$. At $\kappa=2$ our exact finite-population bound implies their Poisson comparison for every positive availability, with their limiting interpretation at zero. This is not a statement about the different sellout events or stock parameters used in their welfare applications. Their proved general concentration bounds are not refuted or required here.
* Shuchi Chawla, Nikhil Devanur and Thodoris Lykouris, [*Static pricing for multi-unit prophet inequalities*](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2023.0031), [arXiv v4, §5, Lemmas 2–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.07990v4). Their pricing analysis reduces to sums of Bernoulli demands, optimizes the success probabilities, and obtains equal-bias binomial and limiting Poisson comparators. In particular, the possibility of equal unit Bernoulli extremizers is not new. Ganesh et al. explicitly credit their work for the unit-demand benchmark. The [repository's matched-tail comparison, (3)](../prophet-secretary-stockout/PROOF.md) gives the fixed-$n$, prescribed-availability binomial lower bound for **zero/one** demands at all integer supplies, by relative log-concavity. Neither Bernoulli-only argument covers arbitrary real-valued demands; a fractional sum need not have integer support.
* Helmut Finner, [*A Generalization of Hölder's Inequality and Some Probability Inequalities*, Annals of Probability 20 (1992), 1893–1901](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176989534). The product bound on functions omitting one coordinate in our proof is the classical functional Loomis–Whitney instance of generalized Hölder; we include its short derivation. The application to the one-coordinate deletion shortage functions and the resulting two-unit frontier are the claim, not a new functional inequality.
* [Fractional supply and the rare-stockout frontier](../soup-kitchen-fractional-supply/PROOF.md), repository [PR #141](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/141). Its Theorem A disproves the ordinary Poisson comparator for noninteger supplies above one. Its Theorem B includes the *leading term as stockout tends to zero* at supply two; that endpoint asymptotic also follows from earlier bounds. This does not yield the full interior curve at fixed population size. No claim is made here for integer supplies above two, uniqueness among all extremizers, or the minimum welfare of a pricing policy.

On 19 November 2026 we compared the original question and later primary literature under its title, authors, DOI, two-unit and finite-population terminology, Poisson availability–throughput frontiers, truncated independent bounded sums, and projection/entropy tail inequalities. No primary paper located supplied this fixed-population frontier for arbitrary independent fractional requests. This is a bounded comparison, not a priority claim.
