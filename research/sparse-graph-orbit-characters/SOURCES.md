# Sources and scope

- Damiano Abram, Amos Beimel, Yuval Ishai, Eyal Kushilevitz and Varun Narayanan, [Cryptography from Planted Graphs: Security with Logarithmic-Size Messages](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1929), [TCC 2023 proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-48615-9_11). The [December 19, 2023 full version](https://opencryptology.org/2023/1929/versions/20240114T061341Z.pdf), §4.3 on printed pp. 26–28, gives Theorem 4.7 and then the weighted no-hints expression Eq. (3). Immediately after Eq. (3), the authors identify α as an edge subset of at most D and describe node permutations, not arbitrary edge permutations. The unnumbered heuristic on p. 28 expects cancellation for most H and all choices of α; in context we read that as the nonzero choices in the preceding sum. It does not give a numerical uniform threshold. Our Theorem 1 proves such a threshold for the isolated character sum; the larger allowable D is not an extension of the source's low-degree theorem.
- [AICR c/0084](https://aicr.info/c/0084/) is an explicitly AI-written adaptation. It states instead a maximum over all nonzero edge sets α without a degree cutoff. Only this unrestricted formalization is contradicted by the full-edge example. The adaptation separately suggests proving the polylogarithmic-weight restriction; that is included in (1). Neither the adaptation nor the full-edge example is evidence that a cryptographic primitive is insecure.
- Helmut Finner, [A Generalization of Hölder's Inequality and Some Probability Inequalities](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176989534), Ann. Probab. 20 (1992), 1893–1901; Dmitry Gavinsky, Shachar Lovett, Michael Saks and Srikanth Srinivasan, [A Tail Bound for Read-k Families of Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.1478), Random Struct. Algorithms 47 (2015), 99–108, [published version](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20532). The latter Definition 1 and Theorem 1.1 directly yield (3) with r=Lw/M and deviation t/2 for indicators, and explicitly note the general random-subgraph-count application of the read-k framework. Our elementary Hölder proof specializes their method. We claim neither that inequality nor orbit–stabilizer or Walsh orthogonality as new; the application here is the character, exact edge load and isomorphism-class/complement union. 
- Joanna B. Fawcett, E. A. O'Brien and Jan Saxl, [Regular orbits of symmetric and alternating groups](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~obrien/research/regular-orbits.pdf), §4.2 before Lemma 4.8, recalls the pair-permutation module and its alternating-4-cycle Specht span. This is the known representation-theoretic counterpart of the 4-cycle space used in §4. We give an elementary derivation of the maximal-character classification and its probability in the present notation; the construction of that module itself is not a novelty claim.
