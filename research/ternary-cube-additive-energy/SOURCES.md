# Prior-work and repository boundary

AI-assisted research check, 18 November 2026; not external peer review or proof of bibliographic priority.

**Question.** For the cube over three consecutive integers with addition in the integers (not the elementary abelian group), find the optimal exponent for ordered quadruple energy, uniform over all powers of the cube. We address this single alphabet and energy order. The answer is an implicit isolated root pair, with certified decimals, and the ball construction approaches the exponent; finite non-singleton sets do not attain equality.

1. [Kane–Tao, *A bound on partitioning clusters*](https://doi.org/10.37236/6797) initiated the binary ordinary-energy benchmark (Theorem 7, exponent log₂6). The separate higher-energy extension on the binary alphabet was subsequently proved for all orders by [Kovač, *On binomial sums, additive energies, and lazy random walks*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2023.127510). Neither fixed ternary ordinary energy nor its value follows simply from the binary exponent.
2. [De Dios Pont, Greenfeld, Ivanisvili and Madrid, *Additive energies on discrete cubes*, Discrete Analysis 2023:13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09352), Proposition 21 and §4.3 provide the precise equivalence to the three-coefficient Fourier polynomial inequality, its full quartic expression, and (Proposition 6) the strict improvement on the full ternary cube. Their §4.3 test $(x,y,z)=(1,1/2,1/2)$ in their mass variables already has symmetric endpoints and gives a lower bound; neither the three-variable reduction, the quartic calculation, nor the insight to test equal endpoints is a contribution of this note. Their indexing uses t_2 for our t_3.
3. [Shao, *Additive energies of subsets of discrete cubes*, Proc. R. Soc. Edinburgh A, online 2024, vol. 156 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1017/prm.2024.126) states the published finite bound t_3≥2 log₂(2.5664)≥2.71949 from the previous paper, records the Fourier equivalence (Proposition 2.1), proves the full cube is not sharp for any alphabet n≥3, and treats the large-alphabet problem. [Beker–Crmarić–Kovač](https://doi.org/10.1017/prm.2025.10015) treat related Gowers norms, including binary sharp cases and asymptotics. [Xinyu Long](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16444) constructs lattice balls providing asymptotic lower bounds when the alphabet grows; this is not the sharp ternary question. Our explicit fixed-ternary example happens to be a Hamming ball (also a truncated Euclidean ball about the all-ones word). Neither balls nor the method of types are claimed new. The sharp ternary *sumset-size lower bound* of [Becker–Ivanisvili–Krachun–Madrid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04486) concerns a different statistic; it does not give an upper bound of this sharpness for the sum of squared representation counts.
4. [Shao–Sun, *Higher additive energies on discrete cubes*, October 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07490), Corollary 1.1 solves the large-alphabet leading correction for fixed energy order and their Proposition 5.1 credits the same finite-dimensional equivalence. Their introductory ternary discussion recalls the strict lower bound, not an exact value; we do not claim to improve their resolved asymptotic problem.
5. What this note establishes for the fixed ternary problem: a globally valid strict endpoint-balancing inequality in the critical exponent interval; locating and certifying the unique nontrivial maximizing scalar at threshold; the resulting exact all-dimensional ternary exponent, finite-set strictness and an explicit asymptotically attaining fixed-alphabet ball radius. We do not classify all near-extremizers, nor claim the full conjectural landscape of discrete cube energies is resolved.
