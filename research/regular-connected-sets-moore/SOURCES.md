# Published question, prior ingredients, and scope

1. **The questions.** S. Cambie, J. Goedgebeur and J. Jooken, [*The maximum number of connected sets in regular graphs*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v32i1p20/pdf/), *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* 32(1), P1.20 (2025). Section 1 defines both regular-graph limsup bases without a factor of 1/2. Conjecture 2 (p. 3) asserts both same-order extremality of a Moore graph and strict domination of every larger order at the same degree. It imposes no girth constraint on the competitors. Section 6 (pp. 16–17) asks the separate large-degree question for connected sets. The authors already compute small-degree extremizers and develop repeated-gadget/board constructions; the note does not resolve their cubic or fixed-degree asymptotic problems. Their existing observations on nonmonotonicity are not the new conclusion here.
2. **Earlier convergence question.** J. Haslegrave, [*The number and average size of connected sets in graphs with degree constraints*](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.22793), Proposition 2.6 and concluding discussion. Haslegrave's base is normalized by an extra factor of 1/2; the paper asks for its rate of convergence to 1. Normalized mean size of a connected set is an altogether different parameter.
3. **Universal count bound.** A. Björklund, T. Husfeldt, P. Kaski and M. Koivisto, [*The traveling salesman problem in bounded degree graphs*](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/mkhkoivi/publications/talg-2011.pdf), *ACM Trans. Algorithms* 8 (2012), Lemma 2.4. Their closed-neighborhood projection bound, including the additive singleton term, is exactly the upper bound on ordinary connected sets used here. They also give the sharper numerator $2^{d+1}-2$ for connected domination. The projection inequality is the Chung–Frankl–Graham–Shearer inequality, recalled in their Lemma 2.2. K. Kangas, P. Kaski, J. H. Korhonen and M. Koivisto, [*On the number of connected sets in bounded degree graphs*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v25i4p34/pdf/), EJC 25(4), P4.34 (2018), Corollary 7, give another proof. Their Theorems 1–2 improve low-degree estimates; their Theorem 3 gives the general lower base $(3\cdot2^{d-5})^{1/(d-3)}$, a deficit of order $1/d$, not the rate here.
4. **Products and local probabilities.** Brown and Mol, [*On the roots of the node reliability polynomial*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.08496), Lemma 4.2, give the connected-set polynomial for general lexicographic products: an occupied support with at least two vertices lifts to a connected selection precisely when that support is connected, independently of the internal selection within each nonempty fiber. This is an exact precedent for that step of the construction. Armada, Canoy and Go, [*Forcing Subsets for γc-sets and γt-sets in the Lexicographic Product of Graphs*](https://www.ejpam.com/ejpam/article/download/3485/841), Theorem 3.1, give the corresponding characterization for connected factors; our fibers are independent and we check neighboring support directly. See also [*On the Roots of Connected Domination Polynomials*, arXiv:2609.06569v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06569v5) for a clique substitution in a different problem. The positive-association inequality for increasing events under a product measure is due to [Harris](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100034241); we do not assume the overlapping windows are independent.
5. **Moore graphs and the comparison.** The projective-plane incidence examples and their attainment of the girth-six Moore bound are classical; see, for instance, [Gács, Héger and Weiner, *On regular graphs of girth six arising from projective planes*](https://heger.web.elte.hu/publ/Gacs-Heger-Weiner-k6-graphs-from-projplanes.pdf). The note recalls the subspace counts and edge-rooted breadth-first bound to identify the comparator unambiguously. Counting isolated survivors is the familiar obstruction to connectivity; what is used here is a two-term bound with controlled overlaps and an explicit subtraction of singleton selections, compared to the occupied-strip construction.
6. **Search boundary (19 November 2026).** Searches included the exact CGJ questions, later citations, high-degree connected and connected-dominating counts, Moore graph/node-reliability/lexicographic-product methods, projective-plane comparators, and strip/percolation constructions. The accessible sources located did not supply either the stated two-sided large-degree order or a counterexample to the stated Moore comparison. The construction, comparison, and same-order regular completion are the candidate additions, not the projection inequality, graph-product transfer, projective-plane graphs, or Harris's inequality.

The repository's domination-sequence example uses monotonicity of connected dominating sets for a different purpose; its large-palette cubic-coloring work concerns a different conjecture of Cambie and Jooken. The later [#170](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/170) and [#171](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/171) additions concern prophet inequalities and hyperbolic frames, not this extremal problem.
