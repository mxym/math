# Failure at the Moore graph's own order

For $Q=2^k$ with $k\ge2$, $d=Q+1=4t+1$, and $n=2(d^2-d+1)$, one can require that the competing $d$-regular graph has *exactly* the order of the incidence graph $M$ of $\mathrm{PG}(2,Q)$. Thus it is the finite-order extremality assertion $c_d(n)=c(M)$ in Cambie–Goedgebeur–Jooken Conjecture 2 that already fails, not only comparison of $M$ with larger orders.

In the width-four construction set $m=2d$. Its uniform $t$-fold blow-up has $4mt=2d(d-1)=n-2$ vertices. Modify its fiber sizes at the base vertices $(0,0)$ and $(0,3)$, putting $t+1$ twins there instead of $t$. These base vertices are nonadjacent and in opposite parts of the checkerboard bipartition, whose two parts initially have equal size because $m$ is even. The two resulting parts thus have size $n/2$ each, and the graph has order $n$. A vertex originally has $4t$ neighbors counted with the uniform sizes. Extra neighbors occur exactly when its base location neighbors an enlarged fiber. The neighborhood of each of the two enlarged locations consists of four ordinary fibers of size $t$, and their open neighborhoods are disjoint since the centers belong to opposite parts. These assertions hold also for the new twins themselves, which have the same neighborhoods as the other clones at their own location. Exactly $4t$ vertices in each part now have degree $d$, and all others have degree $d-1$.

Let $L=n/2-4t=d^2-2d+2$ be the number of deficient vertices in each part. On those two sets form the bipartite graph of currently absent cross-edges. Each of its vertices has degree at least $L-(d-1)\ge L/2$ for $d\ge5$. This balanced graph has a perfect matching: for a nonempty left subset of size at most $L/2$, the neighbors of even one vertex suffice for Hall's inequality; for a subset of size more than $L/2$ every right vertex must see it. Add a perfect matching of absent edges, making the graph simple, bipartite and $d$-regular on $n$ vertices.

Uniformly random clone selections still give independent occupancies in the base; each vacancy probability is at most $2^{-t}$. The same four-column guard and product inequality give at least $2^n(1-1820\,2^{-4t})^m$ connected dominating selections before, hence also after, completing the degrees. Since $m/n<1/(4t)$, the root lower bound $2(1-1820\,2^{-4t})^{1/(4t)}$ and its gap upper bound $1820\,2^{-4t}/t$ both remain valid whenever $1820\,2^{-4t}\le1/2$. Comparing with the Moore bound in the previous note gives the strict same-order comparison for all sufficiently large $k$.

For an explicit comparison choose $Q=2^{14}$, $d=16385$, $t=4096$, $m=32770$, and $n=536903682$. Since $4t=d-1$, the competing graph has connected-dominating gap at most $(3640/4096)2^{-d}=(455/512)2^{-d}$. Since $n<2^{30}$, both $(n-1)2^{-(d+1)}$ and $2^{d+1-n}$ are less than $1/32$. The girth-six Moore graph's ordinary connected-set gap therefore exceeds $(480/512)2^{-d}$. In particular the constructed graph strictly beats $M$ even after requiring connected domination of its counted subsets. This is a certificate by inequalities, not an exact evaluation of either count.
