# A critical-sample supremum for an arbitrary fixed floor

Private proof for review. Fix $d\ge2$ and a compact convex base $F$ with nonempty relative interior in a supporting hyperplane. Let $K$ vary over all positive-volume convex bodies in its positive half-space having exactly $F$ as the bottom face (no restriction on overhang); volume and base volume may be normalized as usual. Let $Q_n$ ask that all $n$ independent volume-uniform samples lie on the boundary of their hull with $F$. The answer below concerns $n\le d+1$ only. In particular the three-sample planar conclusion does not find an optimizer within the vertical-strip subclass.

For $2\le n\le d$ the supremum is one and is not attained. For $n=d+1$ the supremum is
$$
 1-\frac{2(d+1)}{d!\,\kappa_d}\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
 \frac{\Gamma(d/2)^d}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((d+1)/2)^{d-1}},
$$
again not attained; $\kappa_d$ is the volume of the unit ball. At $d=2$ this is $1-8/(3\pi^2)$. If a single supporting point is allowed as marking instead of a nondegenerate floor, upper half-ellipsoids whose base is centered there attain the critical value. This argument is a direct use of the classical Busemann random simplex inequality, not a strengthening of it.

**Point comparison.** Fix $p\in F$ and translate $p$ to 0. Reducing $F$ to the point $0$ only enlarges the success event. Sampled points have positive height a.s. so the anchor is always extreme. With $d+1$ sampled points, in general position either every point is a vertex, or just one is interior to the simplex formed by the remaining $d+1$ points: all subsets of at most $d+1$ of the $d+2$ labeled points are affinely independent a.s., and a full-dimensional polytope has at least $d+1$ vertices. Consequently the point-marked probability is
$$
 1-\frac{d+1}{d!\,|K|}\mathbb E|\det(X_1,\ldots,X_d)|.
$$
Multiply each of $X_1,\ldots,X_d$ by an independent uniform random sign. Their absolute determinant is unchanged and their new law is uniform in $E=K\cup(-K)$, of volume $2|K|$, since the two sets occupy opposite half-spaces up to a null set. Apply the real Busemann random simplex inequality **for compact measurable sets**: the determinant expectation for $d$ uniform samples in $E$ is at least that for the centered ball of the same volume. Equality requires an origin-centered ellipsoid up to a null set. The convex-body-only version would not suffice for general $E$. The requisite scope is explicitly recorded in Saroglou–Wannerer, *Complex and quaternionic analogues of Busemann's random simplex and intersection inequalities*, [2025 paper, introduction around (1)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-025-03279-3) ([author version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01057)).

For a unit ball the mean radial factor is $d/(d+1)$; directions are uniform and independent of radii. The expectation of the absolute determinant of $d$ independent uniform unit directions is $\Gamma(d/2)^d/(\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((d+1)/2)^{d-1})$. Indeed Gram–Schmidt gives the product of successive distances to the preceding spans. For a span of dimension $j=1,\dots,d-1$ the squared distance has beta distribution $((d-j)/2,j/2)$, by the Gaussian representation of a uniform direction, and its conditional square-root mean is $\Gamma((d-j+1)/2)\Gamma(d/2)/[\Gamma((d-j)/2)\Gamma((d+1)/2)]$. These constants multiply and telescope. Scaling from the unit ball to volume $2|K|$ scales the determinant by $2|K|/\kappa_d$. This proves the upper bound. If equality were possible for a positive-area floor, the point comparison would force equality in Busemann for *every* $p\in F$. The open positive-height part then agrees with a half-ellipsoid centered at $p$ up to a null set; taking closures recovers precisely $K$, since it is a convex body. Thus the bottom face would be an ellipsoid section with center $p$, impossible for two distinct choices of $p$. At $2\le n\le d$ every positive-volume floor-body has $Q_n<1$: after choosing $X_1$ the open pyramid over $F$ below $X_1$ has positive volume, and $X_2$ in it is interior irrespective of later samples.

**Approaching the bounds while fixing the floor shape.** Translate the prescribed $F$ to contain 0 in relative interior. For small $e>0$ take within the unit upper half-ball the convex body cut out by
$$
 y\ \ge\ e\,\operatorname{dist}(x,eF),
 \qquad x\in\mathbb R^{d-1}.
$$
Its whole bottom face is $eF$, and the bodies converge in volume to the half-ball by dominated convergence, hence their uniform laws converge in total variation. The marked bases $eF$ tend to the point $0$. For a limiting sample in general position, every successful point has positive distance from the hull of all the other points and $0$, and remains a vertex for sufficiently small bases; a failed point is already strictly interior to a simplex and the added floor contains $0$. Thus the probabilities converge to that with point marking at the center of the half-ball's base. If $n\le d$, the sampled vectors in the half-ball are linearly independent a.s. and the limit is one. If $n=d+1$, random signs give the entire unit ball, hence equality in the point comparison. Finally dilate the base coordinates by $1/e$ and rescale heights to restore the prescribed total volume. The floor is exactly $F$ and the probabilities are unchanged.
