# Independent internal review of the critical-sample flat-floor supremum

For fixed full-dimensional floor F in its hyperplane, no overhang restriction, the asserted n=d+1 supremum and nonattainment are valid. The known n=2 supra-unit approach for this wider class is not new; higher n≤d is included as a point-collapse consequence.

Shrinking the marking to p∈F only helps the vertex event. With d+1 random samples plus one supporting point, general position forces at most one interior point and only a random point can fail. Conditioning on the other d independent samples, the failure event for that point is exactly the simplex formed by them and p; normalization by K volume is correct. Independent sign changes about p create the uniform law on K∪(−K) and do not affect absolute determinant. The reverse halfspaces overlap only in a null hyperplane; volume explicitly doubles. The needed real-function Busemann result for an arbitrary compact/measurable set, including equality up to null sets, was verified in Saroglou–Wannerer arXiv:2409.01057v2 Thm1.1 and Remark1.2. The convex-only wording would not suffice. Centered ellipsoidal equality for two distinct p would force the full bottom face of K to have each as unique center, impossible. Closed convex bodies with positive volume are the closure of their interiors, and equality of two such bodies almost everywhere implies equality as sets.

The mean absolute determinant is radial factor (d/(d+1))^d times the independent spherical Gram–Schmidt product. The square distances use the claimed beta parameters; the gamma ratio product telescopes, and equal-volume replacement at volume 2|K| scales determinant by 2|K|/κ_d. In d=2 independently using E[r]=2/3 and E|sin θ|=2/π gives anchor failure aggregate 8/(3π²). For the approximating convex bodies y≥e dist(x,eF) within the upper unit half-ball, choose e small enough that eF lies within the flat unit disk. Full face is then precisely eF, and dominated convergence of positive-height interior yields volume and uniform density convergence. For almost every limiting finite configuration the strict vertex/cell-membership inequalities are stable as eF collapses. At n≤d, a full-dimensional pyramid over nondegenerate F gives positive failure probability for two of the samples; generic point anchor plus at most d samples succeeds almost surely. Horizontal expansion and separate height scaling restore F and total volume without changing probabilities. No true maximizer in SubPrism, no n>d+1 result, and no new Busemann-type inequality claimed.
