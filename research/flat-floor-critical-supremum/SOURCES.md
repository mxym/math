# Prior work and limits of the comparison

- **The probability model.** Jean-François Marckert and Ludovic Morin, [*The Sylvester question in* $\mathbb R^d$: *convex sets with a flat floor*, arXiv:2411.08456v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.08456v2), [EJP journal record](https://doi.org/10.1214/26-EJP1563). Definitions 1.1–1.3 distinguish the entire marked face from a vertical-prism restriction. Their Theorem 1.7 already gives the unattained two-point supremum one for unrestricted bodies with fixed floor; Theorem 1.5 instead treats the prism restriction. Their Conjecture 1.8 is specifically planar. We evaluate the unrestricted supremum at three samples in the plane, and give the critical-sample counterpart in higher dimensions; we do not attribute a separate high-dimensional conjecture to them. Numbering checked against the author version, not collated independently against the publisher PDF.
- **The extremal determinant theorem is classical.** Christos Saroglou and Thomas Wannerer, [*Complex and quaternionic analogues of Busemann's random simplex and intersection inequalities*, introduction, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.01057v2), [Math. Ann. paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-025-03279-3). Equation (1) over the reals is Busemann's random simplex inequality. The remark explicitly covers positive-volume compact or measurable sets and gives ellipsoidal equality up to null sets. Our sign-reflected set need not be convex. We use this existing theorem directly; the floor-to-point comparison and realization with a nondegenerate prescribed face are the application.
- **Different models.** [Marckert's disk and circular-segment paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3512) uses two fixed endpoints on an arc for its bi-pointed probabilities. [Marckert–Morin's conditioned-chain work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26330) is about chains in a triangle. Neither statement is an optimization over all bodies for fixed nondegenerate floor. The all-random Sylvester event has no deterministic marking. Searches combined the original title, DOI and author names with “flat floor,” “bipointed,” “anchored convex position,” “supporting point,” “boundary vertex,” “half disk,” “expected simplex volume,” and “Busemann random simplex.” No earlier explicit statement of this critical-sample fixed-floor supremum was located. The determinant inequality, its equality cases, and standard ball moments are not claimed as new.
