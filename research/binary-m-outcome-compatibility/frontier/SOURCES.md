# Antecedents and scope

* **This repository.** The immediate source is [the previously merged common-visibility proof (#109)](../PROOF.md). Its bound is pointwise in the noise of the second measurement and its qubit witness is pointwise sharp. Allowing a separate visibility on the first measurement therefore reads off the frontier from that proof; it does **not** require a new inequality. The new question addressed here is which general POVM pairs attain the frontier at an interior point. The proof shows that their characterization is the same at *every* interior point and at the worst common visibility. [The separate basis-pair result (#111)](../../jointly-measurable-noise-basis-pairs/README.md) allows optimizing jointly measurable **noise pairs** for two rank-one basis measurements; neither the measurement class nor that noise model is the one considered here.
* [Bluhm, Evert, Klep, Magron and Nechita, §6.2, Theorem 6.5, Conjecture 6.6 and Remark 6.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17706v1) give the common threshold for real qubits, the candidate value in all finite complex dimensions and the complementary qubit example padded with zero labels. Section 7.1 exhibits a numerical relaxation with an extracted optimizer for three labels. This is neither an exclusion of other optimizers nor an if-and-only-if condition on arbitrary finite-dimensional POVM pairs. Their §4.2 analyzes matrix extreme points, a different notion from attaining maximal incompatibility. [Designolle, Eq. (15)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10381) also gives the common-noise upper bound in the heterogeneous-outcome case. The value, padded example and upper obstruction are not claimed as contributions.
* **Two binary observables.** [Bluhm and Nechita, Corollary 8.9 and §9.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.01508) give the complete universal quarter-disk in all finite dimensions at least two, including its unequal visibilities. [Heinosaari, Kiukas and Reitzner, Proposition 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.04554) give the binary optimum and, for two projections, a necessary and sufficient spectral condition; at uniform noise their bias is zero and the spectral point is $1/2$, corresponding to a complementary qubit block. Their bias parameter changes the *noise distribution*, not the relative visibilities. More generally, the common-visibility classification for arbitrary binary effects also follows by combining [Wolf, Pérez-García and Fernández's equivalence of binary incompatibility and CHSH violation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.2998) with [Kaniewski's CHSH rigidity for binary contractions, §I.B and Proposition A.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.06845). Indeed common visibility multiplies the CHSH value of fixed Alice observables by the visibility, so a worst pair attains the maximal unnoised violation; compression to the reduced-state support preserves this value, and on the support the two observables are complementary Pauli observables up to multiplicity. Sharp compressed observables lift to reducing subspaces of the original contractions. We make **no novelty claim for the binary/binary case**.
* **Two mutually unbiased bases and the same curve.** [Kiukas, McNulty and Pellonpää, §II.B and Proposition 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.07239) explicitly identify the identical function as the trade-off for two noisy mutually unbiased bases with $m$ outcomes each. Their Proposition 1 also implies the sharp upper obstruction for the padded qubit pair from classical fidelity of the two output distributions. [Carmeli, Heinosaari and Toigo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3509) treat conjugate observables. [Bluhm and Nechita, *Compatibility of quantum measurements and inclusion constants for the matrix jewel*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.04514) study asymmetric inclusion constants and give general bounds. These are important prior uses of the curve or formalism. They are not statements characterizing every optimizer for one binary and one $m$-outcome arbitrary POVM when $m>2$.
* **Matrix tools.** The unsquared fidelity characterization and rectangular factorization are standard; see [Watrous, Theorem 3.19 and Lemma 3.21](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/TQI/TQI.double.3.pdf). The passage from a sharp compression to a reducing subspace, the two-label projection decomposition and the connected-support irreducibility test are elementary linear algebra. No invention of these methods is claimed.

Searches by source titles and conjecture number, mixed outcome counts, matrix jewels, fixed-outcome and uniform-noise incompatibility, unequal visibility regions and equality/maximally incompatible pairs identified no independent matching **all-pair equality characterization for $m\ge3$**. This is not a priority guarantee. In particular it is not a characterization of all extreme points, a quantitative stability theorem, a statement about optimized noise, or a result for arbitrary pairs with both outcome counts greater than two.
