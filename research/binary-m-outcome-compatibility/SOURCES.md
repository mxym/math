# Primary sources and limits of the (2,m) result

**Original question.** [Bluhm, Evert, Klep, Magron & Nechita, arXiv:2512.17706v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17706v1) (19 December 2025), Definitions 2.13, 2.15, 2.16 and Conjecture 6.6. The latter uses m=k+1. The sentence immediately before Conjecture 6.6 expressly gives every finite d≥2 and complex measurements; §7.3 also highlights the complex-qubit special case. Definition 2.16 is the minimum over pairs of their *maximum* compatible visibility. The common model is fixed uniform outcome noise, including positive identity noise added to initially zero outcomes. Bluhm et al. proved the exact real-qubit case in Theorem 6.5, proposed the same formula and padded complementary qubit bases in Conjecture 6.6, and supplied numerical evidence (§7.1); the explicit upper witness and the target number are not claimed as contributions here. The case m=2 in every d≥2 already follows from [Bluhm & Nechita, *Joint measurability of quantum effects and the matrix diamond*](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5049125) as summarized in Bluhm et al. Proposition 2.18(6,7).

**Other primary antecedents.**

* [Designolle, Farkas & Kaniewski, *Incompatibility robustness of quantum measurements: a unified framework*, NJP 21 (2019)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.00448), §III.B, Eq. (31), calls this specific noise model *random robustness*. Its universal lower bound in Eq. (35) has denominator 1+√(n_A n_B) inside the half-sum, hence 1+√(2m) here. Appendix C.2 also studies the effect on upper bounds of adding impossible outcomes. The paper's optimization for generalized robustness does not imply the same outcome under this fixed noise.
* [Designolle, *Most incompatible measurements and sum-of-squares optimisation*, NJP (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/ae72ba) ([technical version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10381)), Eq. (15) explicitly records the present expression as a **conjecturally tight upper bound** in the (2,n) setting. Eq. (20) treats equal outcome counts with an explicit parent; Eq. (21) treats all-binary settings. The distinct fixed-outcome generalized robustness work cited there is [Tavakoli et al., *Mutually unbiased bases and symmetric informationally complete measurements in Bell experiments*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03225), Appendix B.4, Proposition B.12 (the generalized, not random, lower bound for arbitrary unequal outcome counts). Likewise [Designolle & Farkas (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20728) address several unbiased measurements and generalized robustness, not the universal (2,m) random robustness.
* [Kiukas, McNulty & Pellonpää, *Amount of quantum coherence needed for measurement incompatibility*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07239), §II.B and Proposition 3, already give **the same scalar function** f_m(s) as the sharp curve for two uniformly noisy mutually unbiased bases of size m. See also [Carmeli, Heinosaari & Toigo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3509) on Fourier-conjugate observables. Bluhm et al. explicitly note the same known diagonal threshold in Remark 6.7. The Hellinger necessary condition in Kiukas et al. Proposition 1 already gives the upper obstruction for the padded qubit pair: the two computational basis states produce second-outcome distributions whose classical fidelity is exactly f_m(s), while the coherence of the first noisy binary observable is s. Neither the curve itself nor this witness obstruction constitutes the new all-pairs lower bound. Their sufficient criterion (Proposition 2) imposes positivity of additional Schur products; universality for all noisy binary POVMs is not its statement.
* [Watrous, *The Theory of Quantum Information*, §3.2, Theorem 3.19 and Lemma 3.21](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/TQI/TQI.double.3.pdf) give the unsquared fidelity variational formula and a rectangular factorization identity used in the argument. Both are standard, not claimed as contributions.
* The [trureturing public source entry](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Library/QuantumBounds/bluhm2025inclusion.html) and its [four-binary source page](https://www.trureturing.org/research/bluhm-2025-four-qubit-compatibility-degree/) attribute work on Appendix A.1's candidate for an explicit matrix certificate and on Conjecture 6.10 (four binary qubit measurements), not the present two-measurement problem.

**Increment.** This note establishes the lower bound simultaneously for *all* complex binary/m-outcome pairs, at every finite d≥2 and m>2. The setting allows degenerate effects; no rank-one, unbiased-marginal, dimension-two, or projectivity hypothesis is imposed. No general (n,m) with n,m>2, no classification of all optimal pairs, no different robustness definition, and no new result for the MUB subproblem is claimed.

**Search scope.** Searches by paper title/arXiv ID, conjecture numbers, free simplex/interval inclusion constants, dichotomic arbitrary measurement / heterogeneous outcomes, random incompatibility robustness, coherence and joint measurability, the older MUB tradeoff, and neighboring 2026 papers located no prior universal (2,m) lower bound equal to the conjectured value when m>2. Public search and citation indexing can lag, and the argument may have relatives under different terminology. Priority is not established.
