# Scope and prior-work search

Checked current public sources in November 2026. Search terms included combinations of both forms of the authors' accented names, cube central slices, critical points of slice volume, two-coordinate/two-block/two-level normals, multiplicity two, uniqueness, non-diagonal saddle, normal limits, Laplace–Pólya ratio, and zero of F_{n,2}. No earlier complete proof of this **specific positive-coordinate open-arc uniqueness** was located. This is not proof of priority.

* Gergely Ambrus, [Critical central sections of the cube](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14778), Theorem 3 and §5: all critical normals in dimension four are diagonal or signed permutations of (1,1,2,2). This already gives the n=4 case. The Gaussian heuristic at the end suggests equality of the two chosen large coordinates when the remaining coordinates are small. Our family starts by assuming exactly that equality; the heuristic does not count roots for their size relative to the other block.
* G. Ambrus and B. Gárgyán, [Non-diagonal critical central sections of the cube](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2024.109524), Advances in Mathematics 441 (2024), 109524. In §5, (5.1)–(5.4), Lemmas 5.1–5.3 and the paragraph before Table 3, the authors obtain existence, explicitly suspect uniqueness, and tabulate small-dimension roots; their §6 shows the constructed direction is an ambient saddle. The adjacent conjecture that there are no such points with k≥3 large entries is **different**. Their Conjecture 1.3 about *all* local extrema is also **different**.
* The same authors' [Estimates on the decay of the Laplace–Pólya integral](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.70157) treats ratio bounds at lattice offsets and excludes local extrema among intermediate-order *diagonal* directions. L. Pournin, [Deep sections of the hypercube](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04637) and [Local extrema for hypercube sections](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15054), study volumes as distance to the center varies for diagonal/subdiagonal normals. Neither resulting diagonal statement is a uniqueness result on the interior of this arc.
* M.-C. Brandenburg et al., [Critical moments of slices and slabs of the cube](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25643), develop generic piecewise formulas, critical-point computations and low-dimensional examples, including four-dimensional volume formulas. Credit for exact piecewise methods is not claimed here. [Brandenburg–Meroni](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09265) classify low-dimensional combinatorial types, not critical normal directions in all dimensions. [Fodor–González Merino](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01504) study the main diagonal under a Gaussian weight inside the cube, a different measure. The same AG authors' 2026 [simplex paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12714) concerns a different body.
