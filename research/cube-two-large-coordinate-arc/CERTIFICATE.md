# How to audit the computation

Purpose: prove that the continuous derivative has exactly one zero, not merely one sign change on a sampled grid. No numerical quadrature or root solver enters the proof. The finite polynomial operations in `verify.py` are part of the proof and `independent.py` rederives the integer/rational sign certificates through a different algorithm; the differential estimates in PROOF.md are separate mathematical inputs.

1. Fix the scale by setting the small coordinates of the normal equal to one, using the two large coordinates to produce the triangular law. The derivative sign is exactly the sign of the truncated-moment polynomial functional, including at every positive knot. The two excluded geometric endpoints are (a) all entries equal at x=2 and (b) vanishing smaller entries in the limit x to infinity. Neither is counted. No whole-sphere critical points are lost on the open arc: symmetry reduces the tangent gradient to a line, and the arc has nonzero speed on its interior.
2. For 2 through 63 small coordinates, the verifier computes the two truncated-power sums using binomial coefficients on each closed knot interval. A truncated term freshly activated at the left endpoint has value zero there, so the two one-sided polynomial pieces agree at the knot. A knot strictly beyond 2 is counted once. For every open cell the change of variable u=(x−L)/(H−x) maps exactly onto the strictly positive real axis. After multiplication by a positive power of 1+u, zero sign changes among coefficients means zero positive roots; one sign change means exactly one positive root (Descartes, including parity and multiplicity). No larger sign-change count is accepted. The last unbounded cell uses translation instead. The script checks left and right derivative signs as well as every individual dimension's total count. For example, in dimension 4 the only interior root is x=4, corresponding to a normal proportional to (2,2,1,1). This recovers the known small-dimensional case.
3. For all remaining dimensions the central limit estimate is in derivative norm; an ordinary distributional CLT would not suffice. The two exact roots of the rescaled functional at t=0 and the equal-coordinate endpoint t=12/m may be arbitrarily close. To avoid a nonuniform perturbation argument, certify strict concavity all the way to zero, which fixes the sign past the second root. To cover the remaining intervals, certify negative values, then strictly positive first derivative, then positive values. The distant tail is handled with the fourth moment; finite support poses no problem.
4. All four inequalities reduce to exact polynomial coefficient checks at h=0 and h=1/64 because the signed Edgeworth-polynomial expression is affine in 1/m. The rigorously bounded order-1/m² remainder and the Taylor truncation remainder together are smaller than the relevant margin before drawing the conclusion. Positivity of every transformed coefficient proves strict positivity on an entire closed interval: constant controls the left endpoint and leading coefficient controls the right. It is stronger than just checking two endpoint values. This yields eight finite coefficient checks for infinitely many m.
5. Reproduce with `python -B -O verify.py` (Python 3.10+; no dependencies). All assertions are explicit conditional exceptions, unaffected by optimization. The program neither downloads nor loads any precomputed sign list. It builds the coefficients from scratch. Local replay currently reports 1,023 finite cells and eight large-dimensional positivity certificates. That run is the author check. See [independent internal review](REVIEW.md) and [two-verifier replay](CHECKS.md) for the separate implementation. Neither is a proof-assistant kernel or external validation.

Independent reviewers should rederive the factors 4m and 2m, the normalization of the Gaussian derivative error in §3, the two t-derivative error constants in §4, and the endpoint/Descartes counting convention. The limiting location and ambient saddle type are not needed to certify uniqueness; the saddle assertion is credited to AG.
