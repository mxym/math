# Sources and attribution

1. Claude Carlet and Darrion Thornburgh, [Resolving a conjecture on quadratic APN functions and a new quadratic (n,n)-function associated to crooked functions](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23888v2), arXiv:2608.23888v2 (7 September 2026). Corollary 4.9 establishes exact degree of each nonzero ortho-derivative component, and Proposition 5.12 records the semi-bent expansion of the adjoint and divisibility of its split-plane counts. Corollary 5.13 gives \(m\ge n\) and Open Problem 5.16 asks for improvement. The present argument reproves the needed parity and tensor facts directly. It does not use the asserted nonvanishing at nonzero inputs that follows Theorem 5.5 in the preprint.
2. Sophie Hannah Bénéteau, Nicolas Goluboff, Lukas Kölsch and Divyesh Vaghasiya, [On the Walsh spectra of quadratic APN functions](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12008), Theorems II.3–II.4 (radical partitions and component amplitudes), II.12–II.13 (application of Heden's tail inequalities). The original tail theorem is Olof Heden, [On the length of the tail of a vector space partition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2009.05.026), *Discrete Mathematics* 309 (2009), 6169–6180; see also Sascha Kurz, [Heden's bound on the tail of a vector space partition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01113). For minimum dimension two and next dimension \(d\ge4\), Heden's alternatives are: if \(2^{d-2}\nmid m\), then \(m=s=(2^d-1)/3\) or \(m>2^{d-1}\); if \(2^{d-2}\mid m\), then \(m\ge2^d\). Heden's small branch is realized by a spread in a \(d\)-space at the partition level. Kurz's discussion after Theorem 10 already replaces the lines in a divisible tail by their three points and applies point-set divisibility. These are partition constraints, not APN realizability claims.
3. Daniel Heinlein, Thomas Honold, Michael Kiermaier, Sascha Kurz and Alfred Wassermann, [Projective divisible binary codes](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.08291v1), §5, including its Table 3 and the single- and double-simplex short constructions. For the final spectrum at divisibility 8, see Thomas Honold, Michael Kiermaier, Sascha Kurz and Alfred Wassermann, [The lengths of projective triply-even binary codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05957). The general code tools are surveyed in Heinlein–Honold–Kiermaier–Kurz–Wassermann, [On projective q^r-divisible codes](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.10147), §3 for the partition link and Theorem 5.13 for the general short-length spectrum. Its binary specialization \(r=d-1\) gives \(N=a(2^d-1)+b2^d\) up to \(N=(d-1)2^d\); if \(N<3(2^d-1)\) is a positive multiple of three this leaves only the simplex or double-simplex length. The single- and double-simplex structures are separately credited to the earlier §5 above. See also Sascha Kurz, [Divisible Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11763) (short lengths, residual codes and the quadratic moment obstruction). The use of a projective tail code, quadratic moment gaps and the small simplex structures is rooted in this literature. The APN-specific addition is coupling these constraints to the right-inverse polar tensor and wedge rank, which rules out one or two short simplex tails for whole parameter ranges.
