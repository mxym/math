The exact moment weighting is instancewise, not uniform over stable permutations. Fix Tan's invariant odd core and mark for each residual participant the core partners that would prefer them to their own successor. Mutual acceptability preserves the original absence of mixed blocks, and any unacceptable residual proposed blocking pair fails the cutoff on at least one side. Alternating-edge comparison between an acceptable stable M_0 and an arbitrary completed stable matching N forces each noncommon alternating-cycle edge to have exactly one endpoint preferring either matching as required: each edge of each matching needs one, and the two lower bounds exhaust the vertices. Thus a new unacceptable N edge cannot have both endpoints preferring M_0. This extends to all even stable permutations by the two possible splittings. Checking both directions of stability proves the reduction to the existing all-even signed identity on a solvable residual. The case of no remaining agents is a single empty term.

The conditioned successor scores give the product of predecessor-below-successor factors and the noncycle edge factors, using disjoint ordered scores. A transposition needs no extra factor. Both parities of long cycles have degree two and contribute m edges. The Young–Studebaker common-profile proof compares W times the first-order sum over those m edges on its symmetric good set; neither this integral nor its remainder estimates depend on long-cycle parity. Their reference diagonal law and their uniform common-profile estimate therefore apply exactly. The cycle-count prefactor simplifies via the displayed product, with local logarithm expansion giving e^(-x²/2) for x=m/sqrt(n).

The signed EGF gives H0=(1+z)exp(-z+z²/2) before marking. Differentiation of the odd marked term yields the L_j terms; at +1 each leading coefficient contributes 1/(2j) after division by j!, while the zero at -1 removes one pole order. This yields a leading term 2e^(-1/2)(1/2)^(rising r) m^(r−1)/(r−1)! and a uniform polynomial bound, including r=1 and even m. The uniform kernel error must first sum unsigned; the estimate has order O(n^((r−1)/2)log^8(n)) = o(n^(r/2)). After grouping signs the remaining common-profile error is o(n^(r/2)). The even lattice has spacing 2/sqrt(n); the resulting line of moments matches independent Beta(1/2,1/2) and Rayleigh(1), whose product is moment determinate.
