For odd n without a singleton, the long-vertex index is odd; the residual after removing the odd core is even and its signed identity remains valid. The chi-square/Dirichlet and common-profile calculations depend on n+m being even and n−m being matched, not on separate parity. Starting A at m=1 and taking two steps yields the displayed ratio; the product bound and local logarithm expansion give the same Gaussian scaling and shifted mesh. Removing a singleton makes the residual even, with A at m=0 equal to one, giving the stated small singleton bound. An odd solvable instance has X=1, so its conditional-moment correction is exactly P_n, not zero.

For odd solvability, a permissible singleton is unique by odd-core invariance and still participates in blocking. Fixing it contributes the independent score factor product(1−x_i) for every compatible stable permutation, and the completed solvable all-even residual gives signed mass one, justifying the normalization n. With S² chi-square, the log radial density relative to its positive mode rho has first derivative zero and second at most −1 throughout its support; the uniform central expansion −t²+O(log³(N)/sqrt(N)) and shifted Gaussian tails give exp(b²/4) for b=0,1,2 with the stated errors. Combining sqrt(N)−rho with the bounded second tilt gives the weighted bad-region bound by Cauchy–Schwarz, not a false uniform pointwise bound on V.

In the critical window the parity-independent first-order addition of m long edges is still symmetric after multiplying by V. The full remainder can be summed against the unsigned count, yielding the stated O(N^(-3/4)log^8 N) rather than losing the small signed answer. Log-expanding the product(1−x_i) cancels the q/2 term exactly and removes S, leaving h−q²/4 near −1/2. Its upper bound 1/4, the bounded second radial tilt and the q,h means and variances give a weighted error o(N^(-1/4)) uniformly in the common profile. Thus its leading normalization is e^(-1/2+1/4) e^(-k/sqrt(N)), with summable signed coefficients for the final use. Separating unsigned and signed remainders is essential.

In the scalar sum, d_0=1,d_1=0, other d_k negative and sum zero, so subtraction of the constant is exact even after extending A by zero. At k/sqrt(N)→t the Gaussian shift is exp(−2t²) and the radial tilt adds exp(−t). The elementary first-order bound and k^(−3/2) singularity control both tails of the Riemann sum. Multiplying the radial e^(−1/4), coefficient e^(1/2)/(2sqrt(pi)), and singleton multiplicity n gives exactly C n^(3/4)exp(−sqrt(n)). The original Glitzner–Manlove arXiv:2505.06717v3 Conjectures 1 and 3 indeed predict, in this odd IC model, order 1/n and sqrt(n/log n), respectively. This is distinct from the known even solvability asymptotic.
