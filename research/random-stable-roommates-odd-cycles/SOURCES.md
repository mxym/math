# Sources, antecedents and search boundary

**Which predictions are being tested?** Stephan Mertens, [*Random stable matchings*](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/2005/10/P10008), *J. Stat. Mech.* P10008 (2005), §3.1 Conjecture 2, predicts an order for the expected number of participants on stable odd cycles, conditional on nonsolvability. Its Conjecture 1 concerns a different quantity: the probability a perfect stable matching exists when the population is even. Frederik Glitzner and David Manlove, [*Perspectives on Unsolvability in the Roommates Problem*, arXiv:2505.06717v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06717v3), conjecture a solvability probability asymptotic to $e\sqrt{3/\pi}/n$ for odd populations (Conjecture 1) and fit the odd-population odd-cycle mass to $1.74\sqrt{n/\log n}$ (Conjecture 3). Their §3.6 explains the relationship with the earlier even-population prediction. Their odd solvability convention, including its comparison with known exact finite probabilities, follows Mertens. We work only with their impartial-culture model, not two-culture, geometric, attribute or correlated rankings.

**Deterministic structure.** Jimmy J. M. Tan, [*A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a complete stable matching*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-6774(91)90028-W) and [*A generalization of the stable matching problem*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0166218X93E0154Q), supplied stable-partition existence, the invariant odd cycles, and resolutions of long even cycles. Glitzner–Manlove, [*Structural and Algorithmic Results for Stable Cycles and Partitions in the Roommates Problem*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3789257), establish the correspondence between reduced partitions and perfect stable matchings of a truncated sub-instance. We explicitly check that mutual truncation also preserves the all-even cyclic permutations and the signed count after completing the lists. This avoids an assumption that all stable permutations are equally sampled.

**The integral and the signed formula.** Mertens, [*Small random instances of the stable roommates problem*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.06635), *J. Stat. Mech.* P06034 (2015), Proposition 3.1, Proposition 3.2, Corollary 3.3, and eqs. (19)–(21), already provides the integral and the signed formulas for solvability, including the odd-population formula with one fixed point. His small odd-size probabilities are the benchmarks used by the finite checker. His singleton must be considered in the stability test; this point is explicit after (21e).

**Previous probability estimates.** Boris Pittel, [*On random stable partitions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00182-018-0635-9) ([author's preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.08340v1)), §4, estimates the number of reduced stable partitions and bounds large odd-cycle mass. In particular his Corollary 4.3 already gives a rare-singleton estimate for even populations and Corollary 4.4 gives a superpolynomial tail bound beyond $\sqrt n\log n$. The singleton estimate in the even proof is an alternative derivation, not a novelty claim. Counting partitions in expectation is not the same as the law of the invariant mass per instance. Byron Chin and Marcus Michelen, [*The random stable roommates problem typically has no solution*](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07612v1), prove that even solvability tends to zero. It suffices for our passage to conditioning at even sizes. Their argument and prior Pittel work also supply degree-two integral domination.

**Direct analytic antecedent.** Caden Young and Ian Studebaker, [*Sharp asymptotics for the solvability probability of random stable roommates*](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07741v1) (October 2026), Theorem 1.1, give the sharp solvability probability for even populations. Their Appendix A proves the all-even signed identity for each instance using the graph of stable matchings; §§3–6 give the Gaussian–Dirichlet estimates, matching-only common kernel, signed cycle count and cancellation window. These are central inputs, not claimed anew. Proposition 4.1 there is stated for even long cycles; the invariant-mass proof checks the extension to mixed cycles rather than citing that stronger statement as written. The odd-solvability proof separately controls an exponentially renormalized singleton weight before applying signed cancellation. The earlier even solvability equivalent alone does not determine the odd prefactor. The candidate additions here are the marked instance-law limit and the odd-population solvability asymptotic, not the unweighted profile, the stable matching graph, or the previous even result.
