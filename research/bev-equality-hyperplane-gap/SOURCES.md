# Source anchors and scope

- Srikanth B. Pai, [*A proof of the Braun–Etzion–Vardy bound for binary subspace codes*, arXiv:2609.33719v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33719v1), 27 September 2026. Equation (1) records the translation identity; Theorem 3.6 proves the general bound. The paragraph after Example 3.7 and §4 explicitly leave the classification of the equality codes open without the hypothesis that the ambient space is a codeword. This note proves two parts of that classification, not the full unrestricted problem. The [arXiv abstract record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33719) showed only v1 in the checked record.
- Michael Braun, Tuvi Etzion, Alexander Vardy, [*Linearity and complements in projective space*, arXiv:1103.3117](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.3117), §3, Definitions 2–3, Lemma 6, Theorem 6, Example 1, Remark 3 and Theorem 7; [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2012.08.010). The coordinate and Fano constructions are there. **Remark 3 already states precisely the pencil plus avoiding-hyperplanes construction in all \(n\ge4\)**, leaving verification of its linearity to the reader. Theorem 7 gives direct products. We do not claim either construction itself as new; the point is the rigidity converse above the sharp threshold and the complete small-dimensional alternative list.
- Srikanth B. Pai and B. Sundar Rajan, [*On the Bounds of Certain Maximal Linear Codes in a Projective Space*, arXiv:1410.2725v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.2725v1), Theorem 2; [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2015.2449308). Their equality classification when the full ambient space is a codeword is the known input in §6. It does not classify all ambient-full-free equality sets.
- Pranab Basu, [*Equidistant Linear Codes in Projective Spaces*, arXiv:2107.10820v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.10820v1), Theorem 14, §4 Construction, Proposition 18, Theorem 19 and Proposition 20. The Fano example is the dimension-three equidistant equality case, while the pencil in (2) is not equidistant for \(n\ge4\). Proposition 20 already gives the pencil/top alternatives for an equidistant distance-two subgroup of size \(2^{n-1}\). That subgroup is obtained here from the density of the hyperplane layer, and the hyperplanes are then forced as well. A direct argument for the two-space dichotomy is supplied.
- Mahak and Maheshanand Bhaintwal, [*Some results on linear subspace codes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ffa.2025.102596), *Finite Fields and Their Applications* 104 (2025), 102596. The abstract concerns the number of one-dimensional words equal to \(n-1\), or to \(n-2\) with the ambient space absent. This is different from the count of hyperplanes studied here.
- W. Edwin Clark and J. Pedersen, [*Sum-free sets in vector spaces over GF(2)*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(92)90019-Q), and A. A. Davydov and L. M. Tombak, [*Quasiperfect linear binary codes with distance 4 and complete caps in projective geometry*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi669), give prior general structural context for dense sum-free sets. No large-cap classification or Kneser theorem is used here; the simple half-size sum-free lemma and the dimension-five circuit analysis are proved directly.

Bounded search conducted on 2026-11-27 using the exact Pai arXiv number, the BEV author combination, binary linear subspace code with “equality”, “hyperplane”, “pencil”, “classification”, and the exact original title. We found the original construction and the nearby prior results above, not a source giving the sharp hyperplane gap or the same complete dimension-four classification. This is not a claim of exhaustive priority. Neither a \(q>2\) extension nor classification of the admissible group operations is included.
