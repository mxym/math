This treats one accepted cost, at most k distinct predictable thresholds and a forced last draw. It is not a multiple-accepted-item problem, not uniform in a growing threshold budget, and does not claim all lower tails.

For an atomless cost law the probability p formulation and its truncated quantile mean A(p) are exact. The two-step interchange difference is ps(A(p)−A(s)), so deterministic schedules sort nondecreasing by p and coalesce into at most k blocks. For adaptivity, the fictitious rejection path changes each actual survival-history distribution by exactly the product of past rejection probabilities; this factors only over observed-alive histories and preserves the cap almost surely while all factors are positive. If a threshold has zero rejection probability, the schedule may extend by its same value without changing any future cost or its count. Thus the expected costs really are mixtures of deterministic schedules, without claiming the actual threshold after seeing the current value is allowed.

Karamata gives A(p) asymptotic, and M_n scaling follows from fixed-multiplier convergence with Potter domination and negligible integrable upper tail. For B_N(v) at log(v/b_N)=w→∞, the trial on either side of (1±ε)w/N separates the exponentially penalized failure term from the monotone successful cutoff threshold; Potter bounds Q(w/N)/b_N polynomially in w and w²/N→0. At slowly varying r=0 and bounded v/b_N≥1−o(1), a slowly growing multiplier provides the upper bound, and splitting at tiny fixed η/N gives the lower bound from either the accepted expectation or the survival cost. Subsequence reasoning covers arbitrary bounded and unbounded alternating stages.

Enlarging lengths in optimized blocks decreases cost, giving the lower bound by k applications with each enlarged to n (even if an original fixed cutoff was disadvantageous, its cost is above its optimized map). For positive r, uniform convergence and Potter give the exact iterated log input. In the attainable upper allocation, m/n→0 but log(n/m)=o(the pertinent iterated log); induction therefore produces the same rate for the long first block. For r=0, constant-fraction block lengths can be substituted by bounded-factor slow variation, and induction at each finite stage with a bounded/divergent subsequence split matches the nonrecursive k-step expression. No pure-log power rate is thereby asserted for general unbounded slowly varying factors.
