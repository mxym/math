# Sources and distinctions (19 November 2026)

**Which question?** The closing paragraph of [Arjun Ramachandra, *Dimension Dependent Correlation Gap Bounds under Restricted Independence*, arXiv:2609.02659v1 (September 2026), §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02659v1#S5), suggests a follow-up with positive correlations as a relaxation of pairwise independence. It is an informal direction; it does not specify fixed correlation values, or the stronger association or FKG conditions. We use the explicit coordinatewise inequalities P(i,j both present)≥p_i p_j. Under that interpretation the factor is exactly 4/3. Higher-order positive upper intersections are achieved as a property of our construction.

* The older *pairwise independent* conjecture is **not** the assertion proved here. Compare [Ramachandra–Natarajan, *Tight Probability Bounds with Pairwise Independence*, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 37 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1408294), Proposition 2.5 and the concluding questions; [their 2025 correlation-gap paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orl.2025.107255); [their five-element counterexample](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19663); and [Ramachandra's 2026 dimension-dependent theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02659). The latter gives 4/3 for four elements and asymptotic worst gap e/(e−1) under the equality constraints. The two-fair-bit union example and its gap are classical in this literature, not a new obstruction.
* [K. Natarajan, D. Padmanabhan, A. Ramachandra, *Distributionally robust optimization through the lens of submodularity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04890), §§4.1–4.2 and the proof of Theorem 4.2, explicitly distinguishes positive upper, lower and two-sided orthant dependence, studies fixed-marginal optimization with lower bounds on joint events and gives algorithms for maximum-of-affine objectives (including bounded-rank functions). The authors use conditional comonotone coupling in their optimization proof. We do not claim to introduce these admissible classes, to invent common-uniform coupling, or to compute an individual optimum. Our comparison and examples give sharp factors uniform over marginals, dimensions and monotone objectives.
* The union upper bound splitting off one distinguished event is the classical star bound of Kounias, used explicitly by [Ramachandra–Natarajan, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00516); see also [D. Hunter, *An upper bound for the probability of a union*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/abs/an-upper-bound-for-the-probability-of-a-union/092D711504BA968EF0D1D903A2685D60). It is the upper-bound ingredient for the sharp general lower-envelope theorem.
* [J. Esary, F. Proschan and D. Walkup, *Association of Random Variables, with Applications*](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177698701) defines positive association in terms of all increasing functions. Under association, the independent-law guarantee for monotone submodular objectives of [S. Agrawal, Y. Ding, A. Saberi, Y. Ye, *Price of correlations in stochastic optimization*](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1110.1011), together with the elementary union obstruction in the proof, yields the different sharp asymptotic factor 1−1/e. This is a consequence of older tools, not a new claim about FKG.
* For arbitrary real marginals, the randomized distributional transform at atoms is standard: [L. Rüschendorf, *On the distributional transform, Sklar’s theorem, and the empirical copula process*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jspi.2009.05.030), §2. Common-shock transformations of copulas are also established techniques; for example [F. Durante, M. Hofert, M. Scherer, *Multivariate hierarchical copulas with shocks*](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/mawi.inst.zawa/forschung/durante_hofert_scherer.pdf). The bound here concerns a particular domination coupling simultaneously for all increasing objectives, not the introduction of shock models.
