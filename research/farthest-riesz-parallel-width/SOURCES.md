# Question, dependencies and scope of the search

## Exact question and known results

- [R. S. Laugesen and I. E. Pritsker, *Potential Theory of the Farthest-Point Distance Function* (2003)](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-2003-039-0), [authors' copy](https://math.okstate.edu/people/igor/distfunc.pdf). See the higher-dimensional question in §2 on p. 378 of the journal. The probability measure comes from the Newtonian potential in dimensions at least three, not the logarithmic potential used in dimension two. The general compact set is assumed to have at least two points.
- [S. J. Gardiner and I. Netuka (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11854-007-0006-0) settled the planar upper bound. Its equality class is different; in particular planar constant-width bodies need not be disks to attain equality.
- [B. Kawohl, C. Nitsch and G. Sweers, *More on the Potential for the Farthest-Point Distance Function* (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11118-014-9454-1), [April 2014 preprint](https://api.newton.ac.uk/website/v0/events/preprints/NI14040). The published §6 explicitly states the unrestricted higher-dimensional question. Equation (6) normalizes the Riesz measure; Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 2.4 give the boundary flux upper estimate and the relation to supporting directions. Theorems 4.1 and 5.1 already give the full bound and ball rigidity for constant-width bodies and for centrally symmetric sets, respectively. The paper gives a second planar proof, a convex-hull reduction, an explicit ball density and an example showing that arbitrary inclusion does not order the masses. Here the support chord comparison and their symmetry theorem are used as inputs. The exact closed-body flux needed for the expansion is justified in PARALLEL.md; no historical priority is asserted for that identity. Compare also [B. Kawohl, *Two dimensions are easier* (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00013-016-0953-8) and his [presentation, Open Problem 6](https://www.cirm-math.fr/ProgWeebly/Renc1489/Kawohl.pdf).
- W. Wise's [2013 paper on the geometry of the measure](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11118-013-9333-1) treats geometric formulae for polytopes and examples. Her [2014 Oklahoma State dissertation](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.14446/15196), Ch. 3, §3.4, Conjecture 3.1 and Proposition 3.13, proves the result for $C^2$ constant-width bodies; Ch. 3 also treats the density in an exterior region for $c$-round smooth sets (not the self-mass expansion along a Minkowski enlargement). [Pritsker, Saff and Wise](https://math.okstate.edu/people/igor/PritskerSaffWise.pdf) use a Riesz representation in reverse triangle inequalities and polarization, different observables.
- The inputs from classical convex geometry are the mixed area-measure polynomial, weak continuity, the mixed-volume integral, Alexandrov–Fenchel concavity and the surface-area Brunn–Minkowski equality theorem: see [R. Schneider, *Convex Bodies: The Brunn–Minkowski Theory*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139003858), in particular Chapters 5 and 7, and [C. Richter and E. Saorín Gómez, Theorem 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12220-021-00766-2). The latter article concerns the *isoperimetric quotient* of parallel bodies, not the mass in this note.

## What the partial results add, and how the search was bounded

Large outer parallels of a fixed nonsymmetric, non-constant-width body need not belong to either classical exact class for any finite enlargement. The signed coefficient in PARALLEL.md describes these directions, including nonsmooth ones. WIDTH.md gives an explicit neighborhood in the smooth topology of the **even** support function, including the case when the central symmetral is close to a ball. Qualitative persistence near any one *strict* prior example should not itself be treated as a new type of bound.

The search compared the original problem, the two versions of Kawohl–Nitsch–Sweers, the Wise dissertation and later open-problem material. Combinations of the authors' names and “farthest-point distance”, “Riesz mass/measure”, “outer parallel bodies”, “asymptotic”, “second variation”, “central symmetral”, “near constant width” and “mixed volume” did not identify an existing statement of these two theorems. This is not a comprehensive citation index or a proof that none exists.
