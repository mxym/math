# Independent internal check of the width criterion

Differentiability of the positive even support makes each face of the central symmetral a singleton, hence both summand faces are also singletons and their chord is 2X. The component along u between a boundary point at support u and any farthest site is nonnegative and no greater than 2h. The denominator in boundary flux is d^n, bounded from below by the n-th power of the opposite chord, so its coefficient is 2^(1−n) times phi. If phi is a support, it is even so its body is origin-symmetric. The Alexandrov–Fenchel Minkowski concavity for V(L[n−1],H)^(1/(n−1)) and reflection yield the claimed comparison of the two surface-area integrals, both in ordinary area convention. The radial projection of the central symmetral has Jacobian h/|X|^n and bijects boundaries, so the latter integral is s_m.

If phi−epsilon is a support, decompose H and apply the same inequalities to each summand separately. Surface-area equality for K and −K forces homothety for n≥3 and hence central symmetry up to translation; KNS then gives the ball equality statement. The radial ball density is positive at radii 0<r<R (indeed −Δ(R+r)^(2−n)=(n−2)(n−1)R/[r(R+r)^n]), so a proper closed subset with the same convex hull cannot preserve that mass.

Along a geodesic the covariant bounds on f and its first three derivatives give |q''|≤2(delta+4delta²)≤4delta, q≥(1−delta)². Combining the first two log f terms as delta/(1−delta)² yields the printed lower bound for log psi curvature. For delta=1/[8(n+1)] this is strictly greater than −1/2. Thus Q_psi has uniform positive minimum and a small constant can be subtracted before invoking the support-function condition. This constrains the even support in C³, not the odd part except for existence of K, and it is distinct from the large-outer-parallel result for arbitrary convex K.
