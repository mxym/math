# Independent internal check of the large-parallel-body expansion

The fundamental-solution convention matches the KNS normalization. On a differentiable chart point for the restricted farthest distance, each maximizing unit vector agrees on tangential directions (apply the support inequality to both signs along the chart). All have the same norm and nonnegative normal component; thus the maximizing vector is unique. The exterior homothetic flux proof may use a sequence of regular layers; exterior distance cutoffs and coarea give the closed-set mass in the limit. On those layers the farthest-vector choices converge at the regular original points by compactness; the homothetic area factor converges to one, and uniform domination follows from the diameter lower bound. Consequently the equality in (2), rather than merely the KNS upper inequality, is justified.

The distance identity for an outer sum exactly adds t, by maximizing over the ball as well as K. Uniform expansion of |tu+p−z| gives the stated w as first coefficient. At almost every normal both supporting p and the opposing z are unique. Upper and lower trial comparisons at z and the maximizing z_t, using u·(p−z_t)≤w and z_t→z, squeeze the quadratic transverse correction. Differentiating h(u)+h(−u) tangentially gives P(p−z); signs are opposite for the derivative of h(−u). Expansion of the normalized power and cosine gives R=m(m+1)w²/8−(m+2)|∇w|²/4. The contact-fiber upper and lower envelopes are measurable (closed compact graphs), uniformly bounded and converge at all regular normals; this is what makes TV convergence of the normalized area measure enough, without requiring smooth K.

The first two coefficients of the area-measure polynomial for smooth supports follow from the determinant expansion; the integrated trace-square minus square-trace and sphere Bochner identity give A₂ total (m−1)/2 times integral of mh²−|∇h|². Weak coefficient interpolation, uniform/H¹ support approximations, and approximation of w as a test justify the identities for lower dimensional and nonsmooth K too. The constant and t^−1 coefficients are s_m and zero. Direct substitution at t^−2 cancels the even L² term, and leaves −(m+3)/2 even Dirichlet plus (m−1)/2 times the negative odd spectral deficit exactly. For m>1 the odd lowest eigenfunctions are precisely linear forms and the even Dirichlet vanishes precisely on constants, so only translated balls, including a point, have zero deficit. A sufficiently large parallel body follows for each fixed K, not an unrestricted bound for K or monotonicity claim.
