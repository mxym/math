**Quantifiers and literature.** [Cooper–Hollars v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15486v1), Definitions 1.1–1.3, Conjecture 1.1, Lemma 3.1, Theorem 1.7 and §7.1 concern inclusion-maximal independent subsets, not maximum ones. They establish $\Omega_c(n^{1/3}/\log n)$ for fixed $c$ and state this as their best example for Question 7.1 ($\beta=i\tau/n$). Their core is a complete join of $m$ copies of $mK_m$, plus random cross edges to an independent reservoir, and their transversal estimate uses Janson. The present manuscript retains their independent reservoir, uses one disjoint-clique core, and obtains $\Omega_c(\sqrt{n\log n})$ via simultaneous expansion and greedy covering. With $i\ge cn$ we also get $\beta\ge c\tau$; the construction-specific upper bound is not an upper bound on the maximum of $\beta$ or on all $c$-hollow graphs. In particular it neither proves nor disproves $\tau=o(n)$. Complementation gives the maximal-clique formulation related to [Erdős–Gallai–Tuza (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(92)90681-5) and Erdős Problem 611; do not advertise this as solving Problem 611. [Alon, arXiv:2103.05998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05998) concerns maximum independent sets and does not yield this fixed-$c$ statement. One recent maximum-set item often returned in searches, [arXiv:2608.18963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18963), was withdrawn in September 2026. A targeted search in both graph/complement vocabulary located no subsequent improvement for precisely the fixed-$c$ maximal-set bound. The firm comparison is to Cooper–Hollars' **stated** bound, not a certification that no overlooked work exists.

## Derivation and adversarial checks

1. **All orders and the hollow condition.** For $f(m)=m\lceil m\log m\rceil$, $f(m)\le an<f(m+1)$ implies $a n/f(m)\to1$, since $f(m+1)/f(m)\to1$. Thus $m^2\log m\sim an$, $\log n/\log m\to2$ and $m\log m\sim\sqrt{a n\log n/2}$. The unused integer vertices go *directly into* the independent reservoir at the start; they are not isolated vertices adjoined after the construction. Since $s>c$ and $\lambda\le(1/2)\log(s/c)$, there is a strictly positive fixed margin $s e^{-\lambda}-c$. For every partial choice $S$, its reservoir nonneighbor count is binomial using independent edge sets indexed by reservoir vertices; the uniform mean exceeds $(c+\eta)n$ eventually. Hoeffding's exponent is at least $2\eta^2 n$. There are $(\ell+1)^m$ partial choices, logarithm $O(m\log m)=o(n)$. No maximal independent set with given clique part can omit any reservoir nonneighbor of that part. Some partial choices need not occur as parts; that is harmless.

2. **The claimed simultaneous events.** For any fixed pool of $h=\lceil m/2\rceil$ cliques, a given reservoir vertex has at least $h\ell p$ expected neighbors, at least $\lambda m\log m/2$. Since $t<\lambda m\log m/8$, the failure probability is at most $\exp(-\lambda m\log m/16)$ by Chernoff. The multiplicity $n2^m$ is negligible relative to this exponent. For $m/8\le r\le t$, the author's single-binomial-term bound is correct: setting $u=\log m$ and $y=r/(mu)$ gives $k\le2u\sqrt y\le r$, $rp/k\ge\lambda\sqrt y/2$, and $(1-p)^r\ge e^{-2\lambda yu}$. Replacing first the base by a value below one, and then increasing the exponent, preserves the lower-bound direction. Independent crosses from each candidate yield a *binomial* count of good vertices for fixed $(P,R)$. The mean is $\ge m^{7/4}u/2$; thus the probability of $\le t$ is at most $\exp(-m^{7/4}u/16)$. The logarithm of the number of all $(P,R)$ is at most $m\log2+\log(t+1)+t\log n=O_c(mu^2)$. There is no assumption that success counts for different $R$ or $P$ are independent. Simultaneity is precisely what licenses choosing a pool *after observing the graph or previous rounds*.

3. **Independent lower-tail proof not using the author's single-binomial-term argument.** To verify the most delicate exponent by a different route, choose $d=\lambda^4/2^{20}$, $0<\lambda\le1$. For a fixed $R$ divide $k$ disjoint blocks, each of size $b=\lfloor r/k\rfloor\ge r/(2k)$, discarding leftovers. For large $m$, $1\le k\le2u\sqrt y$ uniformly in the required range; this lower bound on $b$ follows from $k\le r$. For a candidate vertex, $pb\le pr/k\le\lambda\sqrt y\le1$. The chance that *each* block contains a neighbor is at least
   $$(1-e^{-pb})^k\ge (pb/2)^k\ge (\lambda\sqrt y/8)^{2u\sqrt y}.$$
   These block events are independent and imply degree at least $k$. With $v=\sqrt y\le\lambda^2/1024$, $2v\log(8/(\lambda v))$ is increasing in $v$ on this interval. Its upper bound is $(\lambda^2/512)[\log8192+3\log(1/\lambda)]\le[\log8192+3/(2e)]/512<1/4$. Hence the same $m^{-1/4}$ success lower bound holds. This explicit $d$ also satisfies the other slack and smallness requirements in the manuscript. A much poorer constant would suffice.

4. **The greedy process for a mixed transversal candidate.** At each round, among $>t$ distinct eligible *vertices* in a pool of entirely unused cliques, some vertex is outside the forbidden clique vertices $F$; there is no need for $>t$ distinct cliques. Used cliques and forbidden vertices are different restrictions, and neither is double-counted. Starting at $r_0\le dmu$, the potential drop at every high round is at least $\tfrac12\sqrt{u/m}$, including the final crossing below $m/8$. Telescoping on every prefix bounds all high rounds by $2\sqrt d\,m<m/8$. Initially and inductively before each requested round there is an unused half-pool; a first failure of availability would require more than this many used cliques. Low rounds handle fewer than $m/8$ residual elements, one fresh clique per element at most. Consequently the total number of used cliques is less than $m/4$, safely below $m-h+1$ for large $m$, for even and odd $m$. Since $t<\ell$, no clique can be completely forbidden; arbitrary unselected-clique completion loses none of the domination previously gained.

5. **Maximality and the family upper bound.** The set of one representative from every clique is independent, and adjoining exactly its reservoir nonneighbors gives an independent set disjoint from $T$. Each omitted clique vertex sees its representative; every omitted reservoir vertex sees a representative by the definition of the extension. This is an inclusion-maximal independent set in the original graph, not just in $G-T$. Since all $|T|\le t$ were arbitrary, $\tau>\lfloor d m\log m\rfloor$, giving a fixed positive constant at every large $n$. Every maximal set is determined by its partial clique choices, hence there are at most $(\ell+1)^m$ of them. Sampling with replacement gives a transversal of size at most $k$, where $(\ell+1)^m(1-c)^k<1$. This establishes $O_c(m\log m)$ only for graphs with this specific representation and large-MIS property.
