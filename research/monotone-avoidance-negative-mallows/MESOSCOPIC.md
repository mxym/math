# The empirical negative-tilt plot at its mesoscopic vertical scale

Use the notation and hypotheses of GROUND.md (fixed $d\ge2$, fixed $b>0$, weight $e^{-b\operatorname{inv}/n}$). Let $j_i$ be the canonical colour at position $i$ and $\phi_b$ the positive $L^2(W)$-normalized Dirichlet ground state of $H_b=-\Delta_E/d+bV$. The mesoscopic empirical plot, including its canonical colours, converges in probability:
$$\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \delta_{(i/n,\,j_i,\,(\sigma(i)-i)/n^{1/3})}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal L(T,J,d X_J),$$
where $T$ is uniform on $[0,1]$, $J$ uniform on $[d]$, $X$ has density $\phi_b^2$ in the relative Lebesgue measure of $W$, and $T,J,X$ are independent. In particular this describes the collapsing diagonal after magnifying its signed displacements, rather than giving only a first-order permuton. The convergence concerns an empirical measure, not a claim of convergence at every fixed time or of a stationary time process.

**Bounded probes.** First use a fixed time interval partition with lengths $w_l>0$, and a bounded continuous real probe $f_l(j,z)$ constant as a choice of function on the $l$th time interval. Let $A_n$ be its integral against the empirical plot and $B=n^{1/3}$. Set $g_l(x)=d^{-1}\sum_j f_l(j,d x_j)$, a bounded continuous multiplication operator on $W$. Write $\lambda_l(s)$ for the bottom Dirichlet eigenvalue of $H_b-sg_l$ and $\lambda(0)=b^{2/3}\Lambda_d$. For each real $s$ we need only
$$\limsup_n B^{-1}\log\mathbb E_b e^{s B A_n}
\le \lambda(0)-\sum_l w_l\lambda_l(s).\tag{1}$$
There is no need for an endpoint gluing lower bound for an asymmetric probe.

Run the **upper** block construction of GROUND.md §2 separately within each macroscopic interval, with the same $\lfloor TB^2\rfloor$ time blocks and rough footrule fraction $\theta>0$. In a retained block event at $t$ of colour $j$ with local mate at $t'$, multiply the weight by $\exp[s f_l(j,(t'-t)/B)/B^2]$. For an event with no local mate use the upper factor $\exp[|s|\|f_l\|_\infty/B^2]$, since the actual signed displacement may then be unknown. A residual interval fragment of length at most $TB^2$ uses only the same trivial reward bound and can drop its nonnegative cost and constraints. With finitely many intervals, log contribution of these fragments is $O(T)$, negligible at speed $B$ for fixed $T$. Thus this product genuinely bounds the *true* weighted count from above.

For compactly varying initial $D/B$ and tight paths, all except $O(RB)$ block-end positions have locally determined mates. The colour-$j$ identity $N^V_j(t')-N^V_j(t)=D_j(t)$ and interval regularity of §1 give $|(t'-t)/B-dD_j(t)/B|\le d\epsilon$, tending to zero in probability uniformly on bounded path sets. Restrict there to use uniform continuity; the $O(RB)$ exceptional bounded rewards vanish on the block integral scale. Joint path convergence and convergence of each colour-event measure to $du/d$ then yield the limiting reward $s\int_0^T g_l(X_u)du$. The per-block multiplier is bounded by $e^{|s|\|f_l\|_\infty(T+o(1))}$, so the same wall test and rough confinement argument applies, including sequences of starting states escaping to infinity. The upper row limit at each $l$ is now the row supremum for killed Brownian motion with potential $b[(1-\theta)V+\theta\|x\|_1/2]-s g_l$. Its long-time log upper rate is minus the corresponding ground eigenvalue, as in §2 (the perturbation is bounded). Conditioning at successive block and interval boundaries uses only independent unrestricted word increments. As $T\to\infty$ the interval rates add with weights $w_l$.

As $\theta\downarrow0$, these bottom eigenvalues tend to $\lambda_l(s)$. Indeed the perturbation in the linear confining potential is bounded in absolute value by $C\theta bV$ on the chamber, by homogeneity and the strictly positive minimum of $V$ on its closed unit section. Normalized near minimizers have uniformly bounded $V$-expectation by coercivity, the bounded probe, and a fixed test-function upper bound. The form comparison in both directions follows. Subtract (1) of GROUND.md for the unperturbed normalizer; this proves (1) above.

The unperturbed confining operator on the connected chamber has an isolated simple ground eigenvalue with positive eigenfunction, by compact resolvent and positivity improvement of the Dirichlet heat semigroup. The bounded-potential perturbation therefore has derivative $\lambda_l'(0)=-\int g_l\phi_b^2$. For instance normalized minimizers as $s\to0$ converge up to sign in $L^2$ to $\phi_b$ by the spectral gap; Rayleigh quotients between the two minimizers give the derivative without analyticity. The right side of (1) vanishes at zero and has derivative $a=\sum_l w_l\int g_l\phi_b^2$. Chernoff with small positive and negative $s$ gives $\mathbb P_b(|A_n-a|>\delta)\le e^{-c_\delta B}$ for all sufficiently large $n$ (constant can depend on the probe). This is precisely convergence of integrals for every step-in-time probe to the claimed product law.

Bounded Lipschitz probes on the full product are uniformly approximated by step-in-time probes (uniformly in $j,z$). Tightness of the displacement marginals in probability follows by applying the probe conclusion to continuous compact cutoffs tending increasingly to one, since the candidate limit is a probability distribution. A countable convergence-determining bounded Lipschitz family, or finite test neighborhoods together with this tightness, now yields the asserted weak convergence in probability.
