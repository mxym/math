# The negative weak Mallows tilt under bounded decreasing subsequences

Fix $d\ge2$ and $b>0$. For permutations $\sigma$ avoiding the decreasing subsequence of length $d+1$, set $Z_n(b)=\sum_{\sigma}\exp(-b\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)/n)$. In $E=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\sum x_i=0\}$ with its Euclidean metric put $W=\{x_1>\cdots>x_d\}$ (relative to $E$), and $V(x)=d^{-1}\sum_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)$. Define the honest continuum number
$$\Lambda_d=\inf_{f\in C_c^\infty(W),\ \int f^2=1}\int_W \big(d^{-1}|\nabla_E f|^2+V f^2\big)\,dx.$$
Then
$$\log Z_n(b)=2n\log d-b^{2/3}\Lambda_d n^{1/3}+o(n^{1/3}).\tag{1}$$
In particular this is a stretched-exponential, not merely polynomial, correction to the known exponential growth. Under this negatively tilted law, $\operatorname{inv}/n^{4/3}\to(2/3)\Lambda_d b^{-1/3}$ in probability. For the uniform avoiding permutation at any fixed $z>0$,
$$\lim_{n\to\infty} n^{-1/3}\log\mathbb P\{\operatorname{inv}\le z n^{4/3}\}=-\frac{4\Lambda_d^3}{27z^2}.\tag{2}$$
The coefficient is spectral, not a claimed elementary formula. The argument below for (1) also works for positive $b_n\to b$, with the same limiting normalized correction. Do not extrapolate to arbitrary bias or moving $d$.

## 1. Two words, their state and the effective operator

Take independent iid uniform $d$-letter words $U,V$ of length $n$, giving each pair mass $d^{-2n}$. Set $D_i(k)=N^U_i(k)-N^V_i(k)$ from prefix counts. End matching is $D(n)=0$. Match equal ordinal occurrences of each colour in the two coordinate orders $U,V$. Every true permutation in the sum has a unique such pair using its canonical colours (longest decreasing subsequence ending here). Precisely the equal-content pairs satisfying the following tests are canonical: at every $j$-event in $U$ (here $j$ is a colour) with ordinal also located in $V$, the number of $j-1$'s before it in $U$ exceeds that before it in $V$, for $j>1$. This asserts a northwest witness of the preceding colour. Iterating witnesses gives descending chains of prescribed lengths, and excludes a point of colour $\ge j$ northwest of one of colour $j$ by descending back to the same increasing chain. In fact every colour $i<j$ has such a witness. At the matched event positions $t,t'$ define
$$L_{ij}(t)=N_i^U(t)-N_i^V(t'),\quad i<j=U_t=V_{t'}.$$
Since the equal-colour chains are increasing, the number of pairs of these colours with the $i$-point northwest of this $j$-point is exactly $L_{ij}(t)$ on a canonical pair; no lower colour is southeast of it in an inversion of the opposite orientation. Thus all these scores are nonnegative and $X=\operatorname{inv}=\sum_{t,i<U_t}L_{i,U_t}(t)$. Another bound, not using this score decomposition, is $2X\ge\sum_k\sum_i|D_i(k)|$: for each colour the sum over levels of absolute rank-count differences equals the sum of its ordinal displacements (the one-dimensional increasing matching). Adding over colours gives the Spearman footrule, at most twice the inversion count by sorting by adjacent swaps.

Write $B=n^{1/3}$ and use time $k/B^2$, space $D(k)/B$. The walk has mean zero and, on $E$, covariance $2I/d$ per step. On a block of length $\ell/B^2\to T\in(0,\infty)$, from deterministic starts $D(0)/B\to x$, the linearly interpolated walk converges to Brownian motion of generator $\Delta_E/d$ started at $x$. Individual cumulative letter counts within the block divided by $B^2$ converge uniformly to time divided by $d$, on either axis. For every fixed $R<\infty$ all count increments for **all subintervals of length at most $RB$** on a block differ from their lengths divided by $d$ by $o_{\mathbb P}(B)$, by Hoeffding and a union bound over $O_T(B^4)$ intervals. These conclusions hold jointly. They concern unrestricted iid blocks; conditioning imposed below is by restriction in expectation, not by an independence assertion about conditioned words.

Here is a practical consequence. If $t,t'$ lie in the block and are matched $j$-ordinals, then $N^V_j(t')-N^V_j(t)=D_j(t)$, with past counts included on both sides. When $|D|/B$ is bounded by $K$ and interval errors are at most $\epsilon B$, $|t'-t|\le RB$ for $R>d(K+2)$ and small $\epsilon$, by looking at the first $\lfloor RB\rfloor$ positions toward $t'$ if necessary. Consequently
$$ |L_{ij}(t)-(D_i(t)-D_j(t))|\le 2\epsilon B.\tag{3}$$
Indeed subtract the two oriented $V$-count increments of colours $i$ and $j$ over that interval; their common length terms cancel. For $t$ farther than $RB+1$ from either end of the block, a mate in the same block exists whenever the state is bounded as above: the preceding and following $V$ intervals contain more than $KB$ letters $j$ and bracket the ordinal. This statement also makes sense at an arbitrary state on an isolated block: using local prefixes, a local $j$ in $U$ with ordinal $h$ is matched to ordinal $h+D_j(0)$ in $V$, when in range.

The event-weighted sums on the diffusive block scale become time integrals. Explicitly, under a joint uniform convergence coupling for the walk paths and the two empirical letter CDFs, for either word $w=U,V$,
$$ B^{-3}\sum_{t\text{ in block}}\sum_{i<j}\mathbf1_{\{w_t=j\}}(D_i(t)-D_j(t))
\longrightarrow\int_0^T V(X_u)du.\tag{4}$$
This uses weak convergence of each scaled counting measure to $du/d$, and uniform convergence to the continuous Brownian integrand. The same holds with the positive part applied to each difference separately. Replacing $D(t)$ by $D(t-1)$ changes each scaled sum by $O(1/B)$. Local regularity and (3) hold on events tending to probability one, for each fixed $R,\epsilon$. These elementary invariance facts will be used with compactly varying starting states by passing to convergent subsequences.

## 2. Upper bound: arbitrary incoming states and the walls

Fix $0<\theta<1$ and $T>2$. Split into consecutive blocks of length $\lfloor TB^2\rfloor$, leaving a remainder. On each complete block discard edges without their ordinal mate there, and retain the adjacent-colour strict tests for those with a mate there. All quantities can be computed from the block words and incoming $D$, without individual past counts. Let $J$ be the sum of **positive parts** of $L_{ij}$ for these matched events. Define a block weight
$$w=\mathbf1_{\{\text{all retained adjacent tests pass}\}}
\exp\{-b B^{-3}[(1-\theta)J+(\theta/2)\sum_{t\text{ in block}}\|D(t)\|_1]\}.$$
On any canonical pair the product of the block weights is at least $e^{-bX/n}$: the retained scores are nonnegative and form a partial sum of $X$, while the footrule part is separately bounded by $X$. Bounding the actual canonical sum by this unrestricted product, dropping also the endpoint, gives
$$ Z_n(b)\le d^{2n}\left(\sup_y \mathbb E_y w\right)^{\lfloor n/\lfloor TB^2\rfloor\rfloor}.$$
Here $y$ ranges over lattice states in $E$, and independence of fresh unrestricted blocks permits the row-supremum bound.

If $\|y\|/B\to\infty$, diffusive tightness of block increments implies that the rough footrule penalty forces $\mathbb E_y w\to0$. For a bounded convergent scaled start $x$, use the joint convergence just described, passing as needed to an a.s. uniform coupling/subsequence. A continuous limit path which exits the *closed* chamber has a negative adjacent gap on an interior interval, on which there are upper-colour events away from block ends. At such events the mate exists and (3) excludes the finite path when the regularity error tends to zero. The error can be taken to zero in probability for every fixed $R$ and the compact bound can be increased arbitrarily by tightness. Brownian motion from the chamber boundary has probability zero to remain in the closed chamber for positive time; from the interior, touching a wall before $T$ and then remaining in the closed chamber likewise has probability zero by the strong Markov property; touching exactly at $T$ has probability zero. On closed-chamber limit paths all the positive-part differences in (4) are just the differences themselves. Ignoring the $O(RB)$ end times for an upper bound on the weight loses zero limiting integrated score, and nonnegative penalty from any additional matched events there can only help. Boundedness $0\le w\le1$ now gives
$$\limsup_n\sup_y\mathbb E_y w\le \sup_{x\in W} P_T^\theta\mathbf1(x),$$
where $P_T^\theta$ is the killed Brownian Feynman–Kac semigroup with potential $b[(1-\theta)V+\theta\|x\|_1/2]$.

For completeness $V$ is positive on the closed trace-zero chamber away from the origin, with $V(x)\ge c_d\|x\|$. These linearly confining potentials give Dirichlet Schrödinger operators with compact resolvent (local Rellich compactness plus tail controlled by the potential term). Their lowest eigenvalue is positive by the energy form. For $T>2$, the row-sup above is bounded by $C_\theta e^{-(T-2)\lambda_\theta}$, where $\lambda_\theta$ is the bottom of the corresponding quadratic form: $P_1^\theta$ maps $L^2$ boundedly to $L^\infty$ by the free heat kernel, and $P_1^\theta\mathbf1\in L^2$ since staying within distance $\|x\|/2$ from $x$ gives exponential damping in $\|x\|$, while the complementary Gaussian path probability decays even faster. Use the spectral $L^2$ bound in the middle. The variational principle and homogeneous spatial rescaling give $\lambda_\theta\ge[b(1-\theta)]^{2/3}\Lambda_d$. Take $n\to\infty$, $T\to\infty$, $\theta\downarrow0$ to obtain the claimed upper bound on $(\log Z_n-2n\log d)/B$.

## 3. Lower bound: two independent half-paths and a polynomial-cost seam

We give the construction for even $n=2m$. Choose any bounded smooth connected domain $K$ with closure inside $W$ and any closed ball $A$ of positive radius in $K$. Fix $\eta>0$ small, set $h=\lceil\eta B\rceil$, and prescribe initial prefixes of equal length $\ell_0=d+h\sum_{i=2}^d(i-1)$ on each of the two axes of a half. The $U$ prefix is sorted increasingly, with counts $a_i=1$ for $i>1$ and $a_1=\ell_0-(d-1)$. The $V$ prefix is sorted decreasingly, with $b_i=1+(i-1)h$ for all $i$. Then $D_0/B=(a-b)/B$ tends to an interior point of $W$ of norm $O_d(\eta)$, with consecutive gaps bounded below by a positive multiple of $\eta$. Importantly $a_s\le b_s$ for every $s>1$ and $a_r>0$ for all $r$.

Next use one entrance block of length $\lfloor B^2\rfloor$ to reach $A$, requiring the trajectory to stay in a bounded connected open tube $K_0$ with closure in $W$ containing the scaled start limit and a path to the interior of $A$. This costs only a positive constant in probability at fixed $\eta,K,A$, by Brownian support. After that split the remainder of the half into blocks of lengths differing by at most one, each divided by $B^2$ tending to $T$ (take the floor of remaining length divided by $TB^2$ for their number). Require the trajectories to stay in $K$ and end in $A$ at each complete block. Consequently throughout the body of each half all states scaled by $B$ are bounded by some fixed $K_*$ and adjacent gaps are at least $\kappa>0$, depending on the chosen sets. One may use polygonal time interpolation here.

For both words on every body block, impose the interval count regularity with a fixed $R>d(K_*+3)$ and error $\epsilon B$, with $\epsilon$ arbitrarily small compared to $\kappa$, as well as the following score regularity: the difference between the discrete left side of (4) and $B^{-3}\sum_{t}V(D(t))$ is smaller than $\epsilon$ times the scaled block length. Each of these extra requirements has probability tending to one under the unrestricted block law; (4) applies to both terms, with uniformity for compactly varying starts and the two possible lengths. The unit-scaled entrance block is subject to the same requirements. Weight each half by $\exp[-bB^{-3}\sum_{t\text{ in body}} V(D(t))]$ on these good paths (zero otherwise). By a uniform weak-convergence lower bound, each $T$-block expectation going from $A$ back to $A$ is at least $p_T-o(1)$, where
$$p_T=\inf_{x\in A}\mathbb E_x[e^{-b\int_0^T V(X_u)du};\ X_{[0,T]}\subset K,\ X_T\in\operatorname{int}(A)]>0.$$
Indeed the event here is open in the continuous uniform path topology and $V$ is bounded on $K$; the extra restrictions fail with vanishing probability. Use compactness of starting states in $A$ for the infimum claim. The same argument gives a fixed positive entrance-block lower bound. The prefixed symbols themselves have joint iid probability $d^{-2\ell_0}$. Hence the total expected good-half weight is at least $d^{-2\ell_0}c_0(p_T-o(1))^{m/(TB^2)+O(1)}$ (equivalently use the integer number of complete blocks, whose ratio to $m/(TB^2)$ tends to one).

Take two independent such halves $(U^a,V^a)$, $(U^b,V^b)$. Restrict to equal *relative* terminal vectors $D^a(m)=D^b(m)$, not fixed individual contents. The sum of products of their weights on this event is at least the square of the total half weight divided by $(2n+1)^d$, by Cauchy–Schwarz over possible states. Join them into full words
$$ U=U^a\,\mathrm{rev}(V^b),\qquad V=V^a\,\mathrm{rev}(U^b).$$
They have equal full contents; this rearrangement preserves the original product iid measure of two full words and is a bijection before restrictions. The full $D$ on the second half retraces the second half-sample's path in reverse, with no translated offset. Source word $U$ there uses the old $V$ letters; this is why score regularity was imposed on both words. A reversal changes pre- vs post-step evaluation of $D$ by at most 2 per letter and colour.

**Canonicality including both joins.** In either full-word direction (reverse and swap for the other end), when an $s$-ordinal is among the first $b_s$, $s>1$, its mate in $V$ lies in the descending prefix with no preceding $r=s-1$. Its $U$ occurrence, whether in the ascending prefix or after it, has at least one preceding $r$. The reverse-swapped test is exactly the original strict test: the complementary pre-counts switch. This covers all ordinals with either occurrence in an endpoint prefix/suffix, because $a_s\le b_s$. The central region has linearly many copies of each colour for large $n$ by interval regularity, so the two end treatments do not interfere. For ordinals whose two occurrences lie in the body, every interval of length at most $RB$ there has error at most $2\epsilon B$ (it crosses at most one block boundary, including the midpoint). Formula (3) with this doubled error works also for occurrences in different blocks. Indeed a too-long separation would contain an interval of length $\lfloor RB\rfloor$ inside the body, contradicting $|D_j(t)|\le K_* B$. All adjacent scaled gaps exceed $\kappa$, hence every remaining test succeeds for small $\epsilon$.

**Energy at the joins.** For every body-matched ordinal, (3) approximates each $L_{ij}$ with error at most $4\epsilon B$. The $O(B)$ ordinals handled by endpoint prefixes and suffixes have both times within $O(B)$ of the respective end: in the body any sufficiently large constant multiple of $B$ positions after entrance contains more than $\max_i(a_i+b_i)$ copies of each colour, by summing fixed-$RB$ interval bounds (and analogously at the terminal end). For those ordinals the actual pre-count differences are $O(B)$, by complementing full contents at the far end, and the body approximate scores if any are $O(B)$ too. This argument applies to all $j$ including $j=1$ (which contributes no pairs); for $i<j$ always $j>1$. Their total error is $O(B^2)$, not a positive rate per block. Combining all body terms with score regularity and reversal,
$$X\le\sum_{k\text{ in both bodies}}V(D(k)) + C_d\epsilon nB +O(B^2+n).$$
Here the $O(n)$ part suffices for the one-step pre/post discrepancy and is negligible after multiplying by $b/n$ and dividing the log by $B$. The body count regularity contributes at most $O(\epsilon B^3)$ per unit of diffusive block time, as required. Thus the genuine canonical ensemble has at least the weighted joined mass computed above times $d^{2n}\exp[-C_d b\epsilon B-o(B)]$.

Let $\lambda_K$ denote the bottom Dirichlet eigenvalue of $-\Delta_E/d+bV$ on $K$. The bounded smooth connected domain heat semigroup has a strictly positive ground state, and $\liminf_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\log p_T\ge-\lambda_K$. For example the strictly positive unit-time interior kernel from $x\in A$ to a smaller ball is bounded below uniformly, and spectral expansion of the integral of the remaining kernel over two interior balls has a positive ground-state contribution. Consequently the lower bound for the normalized log correction is at least $-\lambda_K-O_d(\eta)-C_d b\epsilon$: take $n\to\infty$ first and $T\to\infty$ with the geometry fixed (then $c_0$, seams and polynomial costs do not affect this scale). Send $\epsilon\downarrow0$ at fixed $\eta$, then $\eta\downarrow0$. Exhaust $W$ by bounded smooth connected domains (or choose such a domain containing a compactly supported near-minimizer); the limiting infimum eigenvalue is $b^{2/3}\Lambda_d$. For odd $n$, append a maximum to any avoiding permutation of $n-1$; no extra inversion or decreasing chain is created. Use the even result with $b_{n-1}=b(n-1)/n\to b$, for which all the block estimates above are unchanged. The factor $d^2$ in comparing the two leading exponentials does not affect this scale. This finishes (1).

## 4. Inversions and the uniform lower tail

Let $Y_n=\operatorname{inv}/n^{4/3}$. Under the $b$-tilt, for each fixed $u<b$ the normalized log-mgf $B^{-1}\log\mathbb E_b e^{u B Y_n}$ tends to $\Lambda_d[b^{2/3}-(b-u)^{2/3}]$ by (1). Differentiability at zero and elementary Chernoff bounds on both sides yield exponentially small tails outside each fixed neighborhood of $(2/3)\Lambda_d b^{-1/3}$. Under the uniform law the normalization satisfies $\log Z_n(0)=2n\log d+O_d(\log n)$ by Regev's bounded-height asymptotic. Markov gives for every $b>0$ the upper exponent $b z-b^{2/3}\Lambda_d$ in (2); minimizing gives $-4\Lambda_d^3/(27z^2)$. For the reverse bound choose $0<z'<z$, $b'=[2\Lambda_d/(3z')]^3$, and restrict to $|Y_n-z'|<\delta<z-z'$. The tilted law gives that interval probability tending to one, and reverting to uniform costs at worst the factor $[Z_n(b')/Z_n(0)]e^{b'B(z'-\delta)}$. Send $\delta\downarrow0$, then $z'\uparrow z$.
