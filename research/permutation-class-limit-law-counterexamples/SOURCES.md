# Original questions, decisive input, and limits of the comparison

1. **Logical question.** Braunfeld and Kukla, [*Logical limit laws for layered permutations and related structures*, ECA 2:4 (2022), Article S4PP2, p. 7](https://ecajournal.haifa.ac.il/Volume2022/ECA2022_S4PP2.pdf), pose Questions 5.3 (sum closed) and 5.4 (all proper permutation classes) separately. The latter is printed as a **question**. It uses first-order logic of the two orders, not the theory of a single bijection. The classical failure for the class of *all* permutations (Foy–Woods) is expressly excluded. The [positive 231-avoidance result of Albert–Bouvel–Féray–Noy](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/11751) also discusses the open proper-class distinction.
2. **The essential input, not a new construction.** Pantone and Vatter, [*Permutation classes need not have proper growth rates*, arXiv:2610.30369v1 (21 October 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30369v1). Proposition 5.2 gives the large grid classes’ growth $9w$. Proposition 6.3 and the final proof of Theorem 1.1 (§6.3, pp. 21–22) give the particular subclass $C$ with lower growth at most $8(w+513)$ and upper growth at least $9w$ for every integer $w\ge4105$. Their Proposition 6.4 concerns finitely based superclasses of the limit-superior class $Q\subset C$ and yields the infinite-basis observation in the logical example. **Their §3 already uses the bounded-descent class, its increasing-run encoding and its integer growth.** Our use selects a second such class with growth strictly between the two subsequential bases to obtain an observable. The other example uses reflection and a larger class of sums of bounded blocks to expose the oscillation away from the typical diagonal. Neither the oscillating growth phenomenon itself nor the word encoding is attributed to this note.
3. **Nearby first-order theorems, July–November 2026 check.** Markström–Räty, [arXiv:2610.22218v2 (19 October)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22218), Theorem 1, prove convergence for *small* permutation classes (upper growth below the specified constant near 2.20557). Falgas-Ravry–Markström–Räty–Skerman, [arXiv:2610.34328v1 (25 October)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34328v1), Theorems 1.2–1.4, study polynomial/small classes and their sets of limit probabilities. Neither positive theorem covers these two large classes or is contradicted. Uniform samples for a fixed length-three avoidance class and the unrestricted symmetric group are different models.
4. **Deviation question.** Borga, Féray and Winkler, [*Permutons*, arXiv:2611.12104v1 (9 November), §8.3, Problem 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1#S8.SS3), ask whether uniform permutations in classes with exponentially many members of every size have a permuton LDP, and specifically whether speed $n$ is universal. Their §4.1 discusses positive results; Budzinski–Dubach–Féray–Kammoun–Maïda [arXiv:2604.23751v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23751) and [AofA 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.AofA.2026.18) prove speed-$n$ LDPs for avoidance of one pattern of size three. The [owner's earlier #222](../geometrically-griddable-permuton-ldp/README.md) establishes a positive rate theorem for geometrically griddable permutation classes. The present example concerns general classes, and denies only speed-$n$ universality, not every speed.

**Comparison cutoff: 19 November 2026.** Retrospective and July–19 November searches included the above authors, preprint histories and formulation-specific terms for proper classes, oscillatory growth, TOTO nonconvergence, nested deviation probabilities and speed-$n$ permuton LDPs. The current accessible Pantone–Vatter preprint did not state these two applications, and the November survey poses its universality question. No second primary source stating either conclusion was located; unindexed parallel or later work may exist. This is a bounded source comparison, not a priority certificate.
