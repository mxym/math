# Sources, comparison and limits

* Stefan Steinerberger, [*Kaczmarz Kac Walk*, §1.2 and §2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06614v1) ([journal record](https://doi.org/10.1090/qam/1711)). Section 2.3 describes numerical four-direction clustering, proposes a nonlinear angular density equation and analyzes its linearization around the constant density. Its full nonlinear gain uses different semicircles for the two opposite pivots. [The author's problem list of March 31, 2026, §56, pp. 54–55](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf#page=54) also gives this equation and asks about singularity formation. Our comparison is specifically with these two open texts; we do not claim to have checked the publisher's typeset equation. These proposals and the identification of undamped Fourier modes divisible by four in the printed linearization are prior.
* Carl Graham and Sylvie Méléard, [*Stochastic particle approximations for generalized Boltzmann models and convergence estimates*](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1024404281), *Annals of Probability* **25** (1997), 115–132. They established quantitative propagation of chaos for generalized bounded collision models by interaction graphs and backward trees. Neither the general approach nor its fixed-time $1/m$ scale is claimed here. Our proof specializes the coupling, identifying the two-input law for this particular planar projection and its sharp density relaxation.
* The [finite-particle study in this repository, #165](../kaczmarz-kac-absorption/README.md), treats exact eventual absorption, and identifies the finite planar cross labels with the classical voter model. See also [Pickering and Lim (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.93.032318). These finite-population results do not predict a deterministic many-particle angular density at long kinetic time. [Detherage and Shah, 2411.16101v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16101v3) and their [later pivoting analysis, 2505.02023v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02023v4) concern finite full-column-rank orthogonalization and its quantitative rates, not an iid planar density limit.
* The integral transform uses the ordinary probability-integral transform on the circle with a quarter-turn-invariant density, and the elementary Fourier series of a square wave. They are standard tools, not new general harmonic-analysis identities. The special feature here is that the sign of a dot product is the semicircle test, so this nonuniform change of coordinates preserves the kernel and gives the sharp decay exponent for all initial densities.
