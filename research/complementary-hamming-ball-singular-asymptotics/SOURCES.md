# Source scope and de-duplication

**Complementary-ball bound being refined.** [Salman Beigi and Marco Tomamichel, arXiv:2611.05943v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05943v1), §3 equations (43)–(44) specify the normalized binary-Hadamard projectors with real cutoffs (equivalently floored). Lemma 5, citing Polyanskiy–Samorodnitsky, gives an unspecified positive exponent for all sufficiently large n and any spatial support no larger than the corresponding ball. Appendix A Lemma 12 gives an explicit ball-versus-ball bound for every positive n and both radii in [0,1/2] with (1−2p)^2+(1−2q)^2>1. In natural logarithms the decay constant there is (sqrt((1−2p)^2+(1−2q)^2)−1)^2/4. Their Eqs. (134)–(137) give the exponential-tilt factorization; the present note explicitly optimizes that bound, and works with the actual integer cuts. This is a quantitative strengthening of the ball ingredient, not the solution of a separately stated open conjecture. Theorem 4 of that paper concerns the full parallel BB84 monogamy game and already establishes its robustness threshold. We do not identify the two-projector overlap with the full-game probability or its exponent.

**The same block problem.** [Pierre-Antoine Bernard, Nicolas Crampé and Luc Vinet, arXiv:2103.15742v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.15742v1), published as [Nucl. Phys. B 986 (2023), 116061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2022.116061), §5 Eq. (45) and §§5.2–5.3 Eqs. (67), (70)–(71) give the chopped correlation matrix of the Hamming scheme and its irreducible Terwilliger blocks. On the binary cube the adjacency eigenspaces are the Hadamard weight layers; choose the low-spatial-weight and low-Fourier-weight sets in (45) (or modulate to reverse a band sign convention). This is the same Gram matrix, and the known blocks correspond to §2 of our proof. Their entropy/spectrum calculation via a commuting tridiagonal operator is not a proof of the fixed-exterior prefactor or sector ordering used here.

**Strong individual Krawtchouk asymptotics are classical.** [Mourad E. H. Ismail and Plamen Simeonov, J. Comput. Appl. Math. 100 (1998), 121–144](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0377-0427(98)00183-6), §3, treats strong Krawtchouk asymptotics via saddle analysis. See also [Diego Dominici, arXiv:math/0501042v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0501042v2), formulas and region diagram in §4 (WKB). Our direct saddle proof is limited to a compact positive strict-exterior region and is included for normalization, sign and a uniform bound rather than as an original scalar asymptotic.

**Exact radial large-deviation exponent already follows from the ensemble literature.** [Kurt Johansson, Ann. Math. 153 (2001), 259–296](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2001/153-1/p05), §5, gives the Krawtchouk ensemble with a binomial weight and its extreme-particle relation. [Sayan Das and Evgeni Dimitrov, arXiv:2103.15227v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.15227v2), [JFA 283 (2022), 109487](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2022.109487), §6.2 Eq. (6.9) and Proposition 6.9, give the exact rightmost-particle logarithmic rate and an explicit rate function for the relevant symmetric binomial ensemble (their parameter θ=1), not a sharp leading constant.

The precise overlap comparison is as follows. In the radial n-block let F be the full spatial matrix of Fourier columns y=0,…,b in the orthonormal radial weight basis. It has orthonormal columns. Row x equals the square root of binomial weight times the vector of normalized Krawtchouk polynomials at x. The law on (b+1)-subsets of {0,…,n} with mass det(F_I)^2 has the symmetric Krawtchouk-ensemble form, since the determinant of consecutive-degree polynomial evaluations is a constant times the Vandermonde. It is normalized by Cauchy–Binet. Its probability of no point in {0,…,a} is det(I−F_[0,a]^T F_[0,a]), also by Cauchy–Binet. If G=F_[0,a] F_[0,a]^T and λ is its largest eigenvalue, the probability of at least one point in the ball is between λ and Tr G≤(a+1)λ, using 1−∏(1−λ_i) for eigenvalues λ_i∈[0,1]. Here λ equals the squared radial-block norm. The symmetric binomial ensemble is invariant under x↦n−x, so this is the right-tail event max≥n−a. The proportional dimensionless parameter in §6.2 is M=(1−q)/q>1 with N=b+1 particles and grid endpoint n; the threshold tends to (1−p)/q. The branch t>b_M=(M+1)/2+sqrt(M) is exactly p<1/2−sqrt(q(1−q)). Thus their exponent already describes the radial logarithmic scale (equivalently use their general Proposition 5.2 with grid parameter n−b=M N+O(1), which has the same Stirling bounds as §6.2; thresholds converging to a fixed t are squeezed by continuity). It does not select a spin sector of the full cube or give the relative constants between sectors.

**Concentration threshold and other asymptotic regimes.** [Yury Polyanskiy and Alex Samorodnitsky, arXiv:1606.07491v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07491v3), Theorem 9, gives the uncertainty threshold and a strict-exterior exponentially small upper bound for suitable support sizes, not the sharp spectral prefactor in the two-ball case. [Nir Weinberger, arXiv:2610.12358v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12358v2), Appendix F, makes the same concentration-operator identification, proving the threshold for a clipping statistic. Its §§4–5 use radial and harmonic concentration constructions near the boundary or on the concentrated side, without the exterior singular hierarchy above. [Michel Ledoux, EJP 10 (2005), Prop. 5.9](https://doi.org/10.1214/EJP.v10-282) gives Krawtchouk ensemble tail inequalities; these also do not give the full-cube singular hierarchy.

This is a bounded primary-source de-duplication, not a priority certificate. The distinct contribution asserted here is the operator-level strict-exterior prefactor and sector hierarchy, via a simultaneous corner tilt and uniform Hilbert–Schmidt localization. The closed-form finite tilt is a refinement of the proof already identified above, not a new exponential-tilt technique. The unified repository tree at 087f3be2c0284f68ac71746dfac17273af8ce629 was also screened; its neighboring Walsh-support and Fourier-overlap results concern different objects.
