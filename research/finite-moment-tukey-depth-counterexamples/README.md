# Finite moments do not make Tukey depth identify a probability law

For each fixed integer \(k\ge1\) and dimension \(d\ge2\), there are two **distinct, smooth, everywhere positive densities** with finite \(k\)-th absolute moments, identical mixed moments through degree \(k\), and exactly the same Tukey halfspace depth at every point. In particular, the expectation subquestion of [Nagy–Schütt–Werner, Problem 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.10925#page=43) and [Nagy's 2025 GeMS Problem 1](https://gems.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/Depth_problems_update.pdf) has a negative answer.

[The proof](PROOF.md) starts with a distribution whose greatest depth is less than one half. Around a bisecting hyperplane both sides have too much probability to determine anyone's depth. A Fourier perturbation changes only these irrelevant halfspace probabilities while preserving nonnegativity and as many finite moments as prescribed. This differs from the earlier examples without finite expectation.

The pair is chosen **after** \(k\). No counterexample with compact support, with a finite exponential moment, or with a single pair having moments of all orders is asserted. This is compatible with the uniform simplex law itself being identifiable. See the [prior-work note](SOURCES.md) and [independent internal review](REVIEW.md). The argument is analytic, not a finite computation or formal proof. AI assistance and internal checking do not constitute external peer review or verification of priority.

**A halfspace whose mass differs.** In formula (4) take a direction with nonzero angular factor and a threshold at which the tail integral of the nonzero function \(g\) does not vanish. That halfspace has different probabilities for the two laws. A common rigid motion sends it to any prescribed proper halfspace and preserves depth equality and equality of the moments through degree \(k\). Thus such a halfspace mass is not recoverable even under the stated regularity and finite-moment assumptions. Without those restrictions, nonrecoverability is already implicit in Nagy's earlier examples and the Cramér–Wold theorem.
