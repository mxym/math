# Formulation, prior results and scope

- The expectation subquestion in [Nagy–Schütt–Werner, *Data depth and floating body*, arXiv:1809.10925, Problem 12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.10925#page=43) asks if the existence of \(\mathbb E X\) suffices for uniqueness from all halfspace depths. The [2019 journal version](https://doi.org/10.1214/19-SS123) is titled *Halfspace depth and floating body*. [Nagy, 2025 GeMS open problems, Problem 1](https://gems.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/Depth_problems_update.pdf) describes the known counterexamples there as lacking finite first moment, and repeats the question. The simplex is a separate positive subquestion; no assertion is made here that the uniform simplex is a counterexample.
- [Nagy, *Halfspace depth does not characterize probability distributions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09207) gives the earlier unrestricted negative answer, using alpha-symmetric laws. That work deserves priority for unrestricted non-identification. The distinction here is the prescribed finite absolute moment, equality of all low mixed moments, and preservation of the complete depth despite a perturbation of smooth positive densities. See also [Nagy's recent review of depth](https://doi.org/10.1002/wics.70038) and its characterization discussion.
- [Koshevoy, *Lift-zonoid and multivariate depths*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-57338-5_16) and other earlier broad identifiability claims are critically discussed in [Nagy (2020), *The halfspace depth characterization problem*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-57306-5_34), and the [author's account, summary of paper (B)](https://chres.is.cuni.cz/media/documents/2023/01/09/Nagy_ThesisShort.pdf). Existence of all linear exponential moments/bounded support is much stronger than any single specified finite order, even apart from the cautions in that discussion. The present construction does not settle identifiability for compactly supported laws. [Kong–Zuo (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmva.2010.06.007) assume globally smooth depth contours in their identifiability theorem; that is not the same as a smooth density. [Laketa–Nagy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03959) derive partial reconstruction despite unrestricted nonuniqueness.
- Projection slicing is classical, not a new result of this note. See [Boman–Lindskog, *Support theorems for the Radon transform and Cramér–Wold theorems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.4373), especially the Fourier-transform identity for projections and their counterexamples illustrating dependence on tail hypotheses. Here a high, finite order of frequency vanishing allows localization in both direction and offset, sufficient decay of a **signed** inverse transform, and its domination by a strictly positive reference density. The inactive depth halfspaces are the reason this leaves the infima unchanged. [Molchanov–Nagy (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26108) studies a different notion of depth from translates of convex test bodies.

The adjacent owner result [#186, uniform simplex identification](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/186) settles the **opposite kind of statement for a named reference measure**, by local contour retrieval and moment testing on a compact simplex. Here the approximating reference is smooth and has positive polynomial tails; unbounded support permits a nonzero Fourier perturbation and the counterexample does not apply to the simplex law. For comparison on restricted-direction Cramér–Wold questions, [Cuesta-Albertos–Fraiman–Ransford](https://personales.unican.es/cuestaj/CramerWold.pdf) prove sharp results for projected **laws** and discuss the role of moment determinacy. Agreement of selected full projections is not by itself agreement of every Tukey depth; localization in offsets and the strict gap above maximal depth supply that additional step here.
