# Sources and scope

- [Pednekar and Podder, *On the Approximate Non-Deterministic Degree of Total Boolean Functions*, arXiv:2605.23336v1 (22 May 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23336v1), §1.1 Conjectures 2–3 and §3.5 Lemma 9, Corollaries 6–7. Their literal Conjecture 2 gives a constant-one comparison with approximate degree; the parity example in the proof here is a direct consequence of known exact parity witnesses, not a new separation of polynomial orders. Their Conjecture 3 is a polynomial comparison and is implied unconditionally by the later v3 Theorem 12 below. Their Lemma 9 is the squared-polynomial alternation argument used in §4; Lemma 10 embeds an endpoint predicate inside a long run near the middle, and Corollary 7 obtains the quadratic approximate-degree bound in its restricted symmetric class. We use every run to obtain a cubic deterministic bound uniformly for all symmetric functions. Corollary 6 gives a sixth-power bound there, and Corollary 5 covers bounded alternation with a sixth power.
- [Kothari, Kovacs-Deak, Wang and Yang, *Rational degree is polynomially related to degree*, arXiv:2601.08727v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08727v3), §5.3 (72)–(75), Appendix B Definition 13 and Theorem 12. Their Conjecture 3 is the all-function \((2,2)\) deterministic exponent pair, not the Pednekar–Podder polynomial comparison. They already prove that an AND projection has size \(O_\epsilon(d^2)\) in this setting and combine it with certificate arguments to obtain exponent pair \((3,3)\). Our endpoint lemma records a short explicit constant for this known bound. Their Fact 2 footnote records the known even parity exact rational degree \(n/2\).
- [Iyer et al., *On the Rational Degree of Boolean Functions and Applications*, arXiv:2310.08004](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08004), §3.1 and §1.1 parity example; known exact rational degree, not a result claimed here.
- [Nisan and Szegedy, *On the degree of Boolean functions as real polynomials*, Computational Complexity 4 (1994) 301–313](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01263419), classical symmetrization and Markov-type approximate-degree argument; [Paturi, *On the degree of polynomials that approximate symmetric Boolean functions*, STOC 1992](https://doi.org/10.1145/129712.129758), known degree estimates and symmetrization. The polynomial bound for an endpoint and the Chebyshev upper witness are standard.

Bounded 2026-11-27 checks used the two arXiv identifiers, their author names and exact titles, and combinations of “approximate nondeterministic degree”, “affine”, “degree one”, “slab”, “junta”, “symmetric functions”, “runs” and “projection-AND”. We found the ingredients above but no matching statement of the exact two-error paired-affine maximum, the exact mixed affine/exact endpoint, or the all-symmetric cubic joint estimate. No priority claim is made. These restricted results do not settle the full deterministic conjecture or a general best exponent.
