# Independent internal check of the fidelity power and scalar-kernel classification

Reviewed frozen manuscript 9de0c079e2f071b22815d6aa4ba0f47cc0de2311b7bb6169de6bbcb18fe8a9b7. PASS for both theorems in their exact stated scope; not external peer review.

* The trace–determinant formula for squared qubit fidelity follows by squaring the sum of the two square roots. In the real square of orthogonal Pauli directions, the inputs to the flagged root overlap are \(1,\,1-r^2/2,\,1-r^2\). Square roots and trace norms split by the common direct-sum flag. Differentiating \(g(x)=(1-\epsilon+\epsilon\sqrt{x})^{2\alpha}\) gives the stated sign bracket; the rational choice of flag weight makes it negative everywhere on the positive interval for any prescribed exponent. The alternating sign vector eigenvalue is the strict midpoint gap. For the explicit full-rank rational example the adjacent squared overlap is \((21+5\sqrt{17})/50\), opposite is \(16/25\), and the eigenvalue is \((4-\sqrt{17})/5\). Positive rational \(r\) tending to zero keeps all blocks strictly positive and makes the witness cluster at one state. No one ensemble is asserted to work for all exponents at once.
* Three-state root-fidelity positivity is a prior theorem, and nonnegative entrywise powers at least one preserve positivity on three rows (the FitzGerald–Horn result). The three pure-vector path with squared edges one half and one orthogonal nonedge has smallest eigenvalue \(1-\sqrt 2\,2^{-\alpha}\); hence the lower endpoint of the powers is exact. Four or more entries fail by their principal four-state minor. At \(\alpha=1\), the qubit trace–determinant formula is an actual Hilbert–Schmidt Gram inner product with one extra determinant coordinate, so four states and qutrit dimension are both minimal.
* For an arbitrary finite real-valued scalar function \(f\), all root fidelities in \([0,1]\) occur for pairs of pure qubits; the two-row test therefore gives \(|f(r)|\le f(1)\) everywhere without any regularity. The common/group/private orthogonal flag construction has diagonal entries \(D=f(1)\), within-group off-diagonals \(f(b)\), and across-group entries \(f(a)\). The difference of the two group sums gives quadratic form \(2n[D+(n-1)f(b)-nf(a)]\). Letting \(n\) grow proves monotonicity on \([0,1)\).
* The mixed common/private flag plus Fourier/standard basis test instead has within-group off-diagonals \(f(a)\) and across-group entries \(f(a+s/\sqrt n)\), for every exact \(s\in[0,1-a]\). Thus positive increments at every step of length at most \((1-a)/\sqrt n\) are bounded by \(2D/n\). For a fixed \([a,b]\subset[0,1)\), choose steps of uniform length at most \((1-b)/\sqrt n\). There are \(O(\sqrt n)\) of them, so the total possible increase tends to zero. This establishes constancy with no measurability or assumed endpoint continuity. Testing orthogonal states with all-one coefficients forces the constant nonnegative. Fidelity one is equality of density operators, so the remaining possible jump is an equality-class Gram kernel and does preserve PSD even with repeated points.
* As stated, this is a universal dimension-and-list theorem, not a classification of kernels for each fixed Hilbert dimension. The prior [Fannes–de Melo–Roga–Życzkowski paper](https://www.rintonpress.com/xxqic12/qic-12-56/0472-0489.pdf) gives the four-state obstruction for the **root** overlap already and uses two mutually unbiased bases; its four-mixed-state numerical suggestion for the **square** is different. Its separately numbered Holevo entropy conjecture is not the claim refuted. Bures distances and other similarities are not treated by this audit.
