# Primary-source comparison, November 19, 2026

**Target and notation.** In [Fannes, de Melo, Roga and Życzkowski, *Matrices of fidelities for ensembles of quantum states and the Holevo quantity*, QIC **12** (2012) 472–489, journal text](https://www.rintonpress.com/xxqic12/qic-12-56/0472-0489.pdf) ([authors' paginated copy](https://chaos.if.uj.edu.pl/~karol/pdf/FMRZ12.pdf)), their $F$ is the **square** of the trace norm of the product of square roots. Proposition 1 proves positivity of the matrix of *root* fidelities for three states and states failure with four (the end of this proof points to numerical examples). Their Proposition 4 with the two qubit MUBs also immediately gives a pure four-state witness specifically for the root exponent: within each basis the off-diagonal entries are zero and across bases they equal $1/\sqrt2$, so the signed eigenvalue is $1-\sqrt2$. These facts do not decide the four-state squared-fidelity question. Proposition 4, Eq. (34), studies the entrywise powers of squared fidelity. Their two unbiased bases and alternating-sign test, Eqs. (35)–(38), show that a universal exponent in all dimensions and list sizes must be at least 1. Proposition 5 treats pure states. The **unnumbered numerical suggestion** on journal p. 484, immediately before Figure 4, concerns four mixed states and squared fidelity. The authors also report failure already for longer lists (their Figure 4 explicitly plots six qutrits). Proposition 6 proves positivity at exponent 1 for any number of qubits. The April 2011 [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.2271) makes the same four-state suggestion before Fig. 3; the corresponding powers and qubits propositions there are numbered 5 and 7, respectively. Their *numbered* Conjecture 1 concerns the entropy/Holevo bound for three root fidelities. This is a different statement; see also [a recent separate record about that bound](https://www.trureturing.org/research/fannes-demelo-roga-zyczkowski-2012-root-fidelity-holevo-bound/). No Holevo bound is resolved here.

**The earlier withdrawn-in-revision assertion.** [Li, Pereira and Plosker, arXiv:1503.00304v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.00304v1), Definitions 1.1 and 5.2, use the root convention, then square it to form their matrix. Their Conjecture 5.3 (March 1, 2015) proposed positivity for lists of *any* finite length. This broad assertion already conflicts with the 2012 report for longer lists. The fidelity-matrix conjecture is absent already from [v2, March 26, 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.00304v2), whose §5.3 instead discusses maximal channel fidelity, and absent from the [final arXiv revision](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.00304). No reason for the deletion is ascribed. We do not present the any-length counterexample at exponent 1 as a new disproof of a standing 2015 journal conjecture.

**Known mathematical ingredients and other kernels.** The three-state positive side above the root exponent uses [FitzGerald–Horn, Theorem 2.2, *On fractional Hadamard powers of positive definite matrices* (1977)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-247X(77)90167-6) following the earlier three-state root-fidelity theorem. The explicit pure three-state path is an elementary test, not the main advance. The unflagged qubit square comes from two unbiased bases as in Fannes et al.; the Bloch/determinant formula, interval bound $R\le1$, the block sum law and feature-map Gram identities are standard. [Roga’s dissertation, Eq. (70)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5065) also records the qubit Bloch formula. [Liu and Rebentrost, Appendix D, Eqs. (D1)–(D4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.07405) construct a mixed-state positive kernel using *superfidelity*, with the density matrix itself and its purity complement as feature coordinates. It is not a universal scalar function of Uhlmann fidelity. [Nuradha–Mishra–Leditzky–Wilde](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16101) study multivariate fidelities, notably SDP optimizations over several states; these are not the entrywise matrix in our theorems. A measured or Hilbert–Schmidt affinity kernel and the Gram matrix of one jointly chosen collection of purifications are also different from pairwise optimized Uhlmann fidelity.

**Direct antecedents and limits on attribution.** [Fannes and Vanpeteghem, *A three state invariant*, §2 (2004)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0402045) explicitly use orthogonal direct sums to form affine mixtures of root fidelities of triples, and describe their attainable region. Fannes et al. acknowledge Vanpeteghem when stating their Proposition 1. [Roga's dissertation, §§3.1.2–3.1.3, following Eq. (3.22)](https://chaos.if.uj.edu.pl/~karol/prace/Roga_PhD.pdf) separately discusses numerical failures of the root-fidelity matrix, including with pure states, and states general failure for squared fidelity before treating the pure-state exception. Neither nonpositivity for unspecified length nor the block sum operation is claimed as a novelty.

Two close papers require special caution. [Fannes, *Multi-state correlations and fidelities* (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219887812600213) also studies small state ensembles and fidelity matrices in connection with joint-purification Gram matrices. [Sun, Luo and Lei, *Gram Matrices of Mixed-State Ensembles* (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10773-021-04908-8) explicitly report that the Gram matrix based on quantum fidelity need not be positive, in contrast with quantum affinity; see the [publisher's first-page preview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353429155_Gram_Matrices_of_Mixed-State_Ensembles). We had the first paper's abstract and the second paper's publisher preview, not their full texts. In particular we do not infer which witness or fidelity power Sun et al. used, or assert that either paper excluded the four-state ordinary-fidelity special case. No claim of historical priority is made for that case or the classifications.

The fixed-source [trureturing mathematical blueprint, Theorem 1.4](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/609e95e447fb1045172423c4d7bcf2a6e321eef0/Blueprint/D5/S3/Quantum/Divergence/FannesRootFidelityHolevo.md) has exactly three states as hypothesis, and an entropy inequality as conclusion. This citation identifies its statement only, not independent certification of its Lean development. Its bibliography's [Zhang–Wu–Fei](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.5979), [Shirokov 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05671) and [2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05335) address Holevo/entropic estimates; [Uhlmann](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0007018) studies simultaneous two-state decomposition; [Nuradha et al., §5.6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16101v2#S5.SS6.SSS1) concerns log-Euclidean multivariate fidelity and oveloh information. We did not identify either classification here in these materials. [Sra's October 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15889) bounds three-block matrices with $\|\sqrt A\sqrt B\|_p^p$ by varying the **Schatten norm**; this differs from raising the fixed trace norm to successive scalar powers. [Zhang and Fan (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11128-025-04814-6) develop different positive kernels from super- and operator fidelity. These increase the importance of distinguishing Hilbert-space inner products, substitute fidelities and the actual Uhlmann quantity.

**What this note proves.** For the numerical four-state suggestion there is an explicit, minimal, rational full-rank qutrit counterexample, with an exact negative ordinary-fidelity eigenvalue. With a common orthogonal flag tuned to the prescribed exponent it rules out *every* positive exponent already on four arbitrarily close states. In the arbitrary-function result, private and shared flags vary the base overlap and short increment independently. The induced monotonicity and uniform increment estimates force a complete rigidity statement for any finite-valued scalar kernel on all finite state spaces. Neither a new MUB construction nor a new positivity-preserver theorem on ordinary nonnegative PSD input matrices is claimed.
