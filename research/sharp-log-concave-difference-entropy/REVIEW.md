# Independent review: a one-dimensional entropy bound for differences

**Verdict:** Pass for the mathematical statements in [the proof](PROOF.md). This is an independent AI-assisted mathematical review, not external peer review, Lean verification, or evidence of priority.

The key comparison has an intuitive meaning. To distinguish a distribution from its translate, consider two independent observations, one from each law, presented in random order. For a log-concave law, the larger observation is the best candidate for the right-shifted sample. Using both observations cannot be worse than using a single threshold. On the line, this familiar statistical fact compares the concentration of an independent difference with concentration of the original law in an interval. The entropy conclusions are consequences of that comparison; the statistical fact is **not new**.

## Alternative proof of the delicate step, including lattice ties

Write CDFs for the original law and its right translate by a positive shift h. Denote them by P and Q; write the corresponding masses or densities as p,q, so q(x)=p(x-h). For x<y,
p(x)q(y)−p(y)q(x)≥0. This follows from concavity of log p on its positive support; if the allegedly subtracted positive term is nonzero, x−h and y belong to the interval support and both middle points are in that same support. The argument does not divide by zero.

The concentration gap at any threshold a is P(a)−Q(a). If independent U and V have laws P and Q, respectively, put T=Pr(V>U)−Pr(V<U), counting a tie as zero. Pair x<y with y>x: the contribution to T from an unordered pair is the nonnegative determinant above. The difference P(a)−Q(a)=Pr(V>a)−Pr(U>a) counts precisely the same determinants but **only for pairs on opposite sides of a**. Consequently T≥P(a)−Q(a). The discarded nonnegative determinants on the two same-side regions give an exact nonnegative remainder.

For independent copies with symmetric difference D, T=Pr(D<h)−Pr(D>h). On the lattice, this is precisely Pr(−k<D≤k), not the mass of the 2k+1-site symmetric interval. In the continuous case it is Pr(|D|≤h). This proves (1) and (2) by varying the threshold. It is the signed-area form of the classical concave ROC bound: twice the area under the interpolated ROC curve minus one dominates the maximum gap of its ordinate and abscissa. Linear interpolation accounts for half-weighted ties in AUC. See [Zhang–Mueller (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11336-003-1119-8), [Mueller–Zhang (2006)](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/m3lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/1376/2024/11/2006-Mueller-and-Zhang-ICML-proceedings.pdf), or [Bandos–Rockette–Gur (2010), Appendix (A2)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2980542/). This is independent of counting the overlap of the two product measures, but the two derivations rest on the same likelihood-ratio order.

## Ranked masses, strictness, and limits

* A largest-mass selection of k sites of a unimodal summable law can be consecutive, including a plateau or a finite support shorter than k (pad with zero). The comparison thus gives all even ranked inequalities for the difference versus two copies of the mass list divided by 2. For odd ranks use concavity of the ranked cumulative sum and linearity between consecutive *even* ranks on the split side, including the first segment from rank 0. No convolution-closure theorem is needed.
* Rank 1 is strictly larger because g(0)+g(1) dominates max p but g(0)−g(1) is half the strictly positive squared shift distance of the nonzero summable mass sequence. A one-point support is included. Merely writing 'strict Schur concavity' for a countable alphabet would be inadequate: the candidate correctly proves strictness on a threshold interval by its positive-part transform, and at order infinity by rank 1. The layer-cake constants for subunit orders, superunit orders, and Shannon were checked separately; log-concavity gives the geometric tails needed for finiteness.
* In the geometric family, the power-sum ratio is (1+q^α)/(1+q)^α, the Shannon gap is log(1+q)−q log(q)/(1+q), and the min-entropy gap is log(1+q). These known examples tend to log 2 but never attain it. The support-size statement at order 0 is separate from taking a difference of infinite entropies.
* In the continuous section, superlevel sets of b(x)=f(x/2)/2 have finite length for every t>0. If a chosen representative has a singleton strict superlevel set at a boundary, its contribution to J is zero and it can be treated like the empty case. Changing units jointly before using the Shannon integral identity is legitimate. Equality at any single 0<α<1 implies J_g=J_b everywhere on t>0 by positivity of the weight and continuity, then Shannon equality. Theorem 4 of the source paper gives exactly the asserted affine one-sided exponential family. No such classification at infinity is asserted.

## Exact checks and literature boundary

The independent `check_roc.py` uses integer trapezoidal double areas, reconstructs every translated ROC polygon rather than computing overlaps, and separately computes differences by all ordered pairs. It passed 736 input families of support lengths 1 through 32, 12,880 translated polygons, all threshold triangles and all ranked sums. A full-interval unimodal but non-log-concave sequence (ten 1s, a 10, ten 1s) correctly fails. The author's distinct checker passed 2,111 cases. Neither test is a substitute for proof.

[Han–Zhan–Zhou–Zhu (October 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15716v1) ask (63) with exactly the interval-support assumption used here, and (61) for fixed dimension. Their acknowledgments credit **Jiange Li for suggesting the integer-valued and positive-sum directions**; cite this distinction. Their Theorems 3–5 already give all-dimensional, weighted, continuous α≥1 results, the finite-order equality classification there, and high-dimensional failure at each fixed α<1. This manuscript covers unweighted iid differences on the lattice and the line only. It does not prove either positive-sum question, different-law question (60), or the higher-dimensional subunit question.

[Melbourne–Tkocz, §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10930) already gave sharp discrete orders 2 and infinity, finite support order zero and the geometric sharpness limits for all orders. [Melbourne–Palafox-Castillo, Thm 2.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.06997v1) has the monotone-input orders ≥2; [Fu–Li v4, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09206v4) covers the positive-sum order-two identity and monotone positive-sum min-entropy. [Bobkov–Marsiglietti–Melbourne, §§7–8](https://doi.org/10.1017/S096354832100016X) treats related atom/variance and concentration comparisons. [Alqasem–Aravinda–Marsiglietti, Thm 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05439v1) is a nonsharp variance-derived reverse EPI at r>1; it does not imply (63). [Marsiglietti–Melbourne on Littlewood–Offord](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25869v2) and [Gaxiola–Melbourne–Pigno–Pollard](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05793v1) treat related but different concentration extrema. Melbourne–Tkocz's Remark 6.3 already notes the continuous **symmetric** input and α≤1 positive-sum implication; the scope here must not claim this symmetric subclass is new. I found no earlier paper explicitly deducing the two entropy conclusions in their full stated range; the statistical comparison is known and this search cannot establish priority.
