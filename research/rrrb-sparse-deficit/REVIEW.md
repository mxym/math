# What follows from prior work, and what was checked

**The leading constant is not claimed here.** Chao, Cohen Antonir, Li and Yu, [*Edge inducibility via local directed graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24064), Theorem 1.6 and the paw example in §5, give normalized edge inducibility $1/4$ for the triangle with a pendant edge ($K_3^+$). Its fractional independence number is $2$ and its automorphism group has order $2$, so there are $m$-edge graphs with $(1-o(1))m^2/2$ unlabelled induced paws along a sequence. Their construction uses a total order on auxiliary vertices; the ordered multiscale idea is antecedent, not a priority claim of this note.

Here is the implication for this graphon problem. On each induced paw the two undirected Hamilton paths each give two ordered RRRB tuples. A finite simple graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, viewed as its adjacency step graphon, thus satisfies $F\ge4(\#\mathrm{paws})/n^4$ and $p=2m/n^2$. Other induced shapes and colliding vertex labels cannot decrease this inequality. These hosts have $F/p^2$ arbitrarily close to $1/2$ from below. For each such witness, shrinking its support preserves its ratio for every lower density. Together with $F/p^2\le1/2$, this proves the limiting constant without the quantitative argument. The cited paw theorem does not state a logarithmic deficit for every graphon. Conversely, an RRRB tuple is not necessarily an induced paw.

* Basit, Granet, Horsley, Kündgen and Staden, [*The semi-inducibility problem*, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09842v2), Theorems 1.9, 2.7, §7.2 and §9.1 study this nonalternating cycle, including a high-red-density regime. They count unoriented cycles, hence a factor of two relative to this note. The [previous repository note](../rrrb-lower-saturation/README.md) treats an explicitly identified high- and intermediate-density interval. Neither the present estimate nor those dense results determine the full curve.
* Wang, Zhao and Lu, [*Edge version of the inducibility via the entropy method*, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17502v2), give the general edge-constrained growth exponent and related results for paths and cycles. This is distinct from the fixed-density graphon deficit. [Balogh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03433) study other fixed-density colour patterns, including the alternating four-cycle; that is not RRRB.
* Searches for the fixed-density one-blue cycle, its path-minus-cycle formulation, sparse semi-inducibility, and the paw/edge-inducibility logarithmic correction did not locate a matching graphon rate theorem. The repository check covered the full tree at main `0bbd0f585b682695c4fbb646e87b3fd247e6b681` plus 48 tag trees, including preprints, formalizations, searchable archives and PDFs. The additional 33 files through `926fff8ab62315fe68babca6732cb7d4e6b9d7d1` concern the trine bound, Laplacian entropy, and the two preceding RRRB notes. The release inventory covered 47 releases with 175 assets. Searches have language, format and indexing limits and do not establish novelty. Third-party results and rights remain with their authors.

**Review and reproducibility.** An independent internal AI review checked the conditional marginal and joint bridge bounds, the $7/(200K)$ constant, the normalized interval law, the two bridge matchings, dilution for *every* sufficiently small $p$, and the prior paw normalization. The single-first-class collision argument in [STEPS.md](STEPS.md) arose during that review and was re-derived and checked separately in preparing this note.

Run `python3 verify.py` (standard library, assertions enabled) and `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS` in this directory.
