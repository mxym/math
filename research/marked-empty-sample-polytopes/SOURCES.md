# Source and scope comparison, 19 November 2026

* [Reitzner–Temesvari, *Stars of empty simplices*, arXiv:1808.08734v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08734v2), Theorem 1.4 and §3.2, pp. 8–9: the coefficient of the expected **total** number of empty sample simplices already involves the integral of the section volume to power d+1 over hyperplanes. Their affine Blaschke–Petkantschin reduction is the pertinent prior. Normalizing this weight is not a newly discovered integral. Here it describes the hyperplane of the **volume-maximizing** empty simplex, with the conditional limiting vertex law and independence from its centered maximum.
* [Balko–Scheucher–Valtr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12533v2) treat the expected number of k-holes, not the joint process of the extreme-volume holes. [Balogh–González-Aguilar–Salazar](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925772112001599) maximize the number of vertices of a planar hole, a different statistic.
* [Bobrowski–Schulte–Yogeshwaran](https://doi.org/10.5802/ahl.156) develop general Poisson process approximations by Palm coupling and stabilization. The Poisson and Palm principles are prior. The problem-specific steps here are the coupling in descending vertex count, exclusion of a smaller nearly coincident hull after affine normalization, and identification of the extremal physical marks.
* [Peres–Yang, arXiv:2610.09790v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09790), Theorem 1.3 and §3.3: leading almost-sure maximum empty volume for arbitrary convex bodies avoiding the points, not the centered process or vertex marks for a fixed count of **sample** vertices. Searches of indexed papers/preprints, including July–19 November 2026, used largest empty simplex/convex polygon/polytope, fixed vertex count, k-holes, sample-vertex volume extremes, joint marked Poisson/Gumbel process, and section law. No exact duplicate was located; this is bounded search evidence, not a priority certificate.

The new information is simultaneous convergence, independence across counts and bins, and the extreme objects' marks.
