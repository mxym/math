# Original model, adjacent results and search coverage

* Ekbatani, Niazadeh, Nuti and Vondrák, [*Prophet Inequalities with Cancellation Costs*, STOC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3618260.3649786), [author-hosted conference PDF](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/12758/files/Prophet-Inequalities-with-Cancellation-Costs.pdf), [long first version §§1.1,4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v1#S4). These are the original model, fixed-number structural questions, proof of logarithmic loss up to constants, and threshold-after-which-to-buy-back policy.
* Same authors' expanded [April 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v2) and [October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v3) versions ([EC 2026 program](https://ec26.sigecom.org/program/main-technical-program/)). The later manuscript establishes the exact unrestricted factor at every fee through a differential equation. Sections 2–3 also guarantee that this factor is attained by a finite scaled-Bernoulli instance; an upper bound on the number of exact knot iterations is in Theorem 2.9(iii). These exact-attainment facts are prior and do not identify the horizon necessary and sufficient for attaining a given **asymptotic loss**.
* Their [Lemma 3.2 and Eqs. (11)–(12)](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v3#S3.SS2) are used explicitly for the expectation of each hard family. In [§9.1, Theorem 9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v3#S9.SS1), approximately log₂(1/f) arrivals and activations of probability one half give the hard direction at the specific crossover value a=1/ln 2. Its general geometric-mesh idea is not new. In [§9.2, Lemmas 9.2–9.4 and Theorem 9.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v3#S9.SS2), the authors choose a quantile threshold and subsequently replace only for a positive immediate gain. They use a Poisson relaxation for the number of passers and obtain a horizon-independent guarantee. Indeed, if one instead retains the independent passer indicators in Lemma 9.3, their estimate already implies a guarantee of the same asymptotic order and leading profile: for atomless laws and failure probability c it gives `O/M ≥ (1−c)∏(1−c p_i) ≥ (1−c)(1−c B_N)` by AM–GM, before using `0≤B_N−B_{N−1}≤1`. The displayed comparison follows by testing the positive part on the event that the maximum passes, so its expectation on that event is at least its signed expectation there. Our direct record policy is different: it takes every superior passer, even if the increase does not cover the fee, and charges the cost against the maximum among the *other* variables. This immediately handles atoms and yields the stated constant in the upper direction. We do not claim that threshold strategies or the idea of counting the passers are new.
* [Zhang's preceding repository note](../prophet-buyback-three-arrivals/README.md) establishes the residual sharp three-arrival ratio and the unrestricted small-fee leading coefficient, including the pure quadratic knots used at one edge of the present construction. The new target is the full finite-horizon and small-fee joint limit with an additive error uniform in every integer horizon, not either endpoint. The interpolating exponential–quadratic knots keep both the sum of squares of the log-step deviations and the loss from the early offers uniformly bounded.

**Search scope, 19 November 2026.** We compared the original and expanded versions, [the authors' 2026 conference listing](https://ec26.sigecom.org/program/main-technical-program/), and citing/adjacent literature using small cancellation fee, logarithmic horizon, number of arrivals/variables, scaled Bernoulli, growth of the hard instance, phase transition and finite-horizon prophet inequality with buyback. We found no matching simultaneous theorem. Work on a random or unknown *supply* horizon, [discounted retrieval of declined observations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06805), or [multiple-choice minimization](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.55) changes the feasibility rules or objective.
