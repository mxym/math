# Random additive chores: a fixed law for each coefficient below two, and a failing subsequence

The model is that of [Manurangsi–Suksompong](https://doi.org/10.1137/25M180161X): n agents, m indivisible chores, independent disutilities from a *single fixed* nonatomic distribution on [0,1] across all agent–chore pairs, utility comparisons made by adding disutilities. An allocation is a partition of all chores and is envy-free (EF) when each agent's own total cost is at most her valuation of each other's bundle. A PDF-bounded law has a density bounded below and above by finite strictly positive constants on [0,1]; continuity at zero is not assumed.

**Theorem.** For every fixed real $1<c<2$ there exist one PDF-bounded law $D_c$ and integer sequences $n_j,m_j\to\infty$ such that $m_j\ge c n_j$, $m_j/n_j\to c$, and the probability of an EF allocation tends to zero along this sequence. In particular there is no coefficient $c<2$ (including $c\le1$, by taking a larger ratio) that can universally replace 2 in their Theorem 1.1. Together with their known sufficient condition this identifies the **best distribution-independent coefficient** for this whole class. It does not locate the threshold for the uniform law, any smooth density with a positive limit at zero, or every fixed PDF-bounded law. The distribution can depend on the proposed replacement but, once chosen, is the same for every size.

## 1. Deterministic implications for singleton chores

All individual costs are strictly positive and each agent has distinct costs almost surely. If at least one agent receives no chore while there is a chore, the holder of a nonempty bundle envies the empty bundle; so every EF bundle is nonempty. If $s$ agents hold singletons, $m\ge s+2(n-s)$, and at most $m-s\le 2(m-n)$ chores **can be in nonsingleton bundles**.

An agent's *certified prefix* is a possibly empty list consisting of her first $K$ chores in strictly increasing order of cost, all having cost less than the sum of her two cheapest **distinct** chores. We will select such prefixes for only a subset of agents. The following deterministic lemma uses no randomness or independence.

Start all directed requests $R^0_{i\to z}=0$. At round $t+1$, agent $i$ requests a chore $z$ at position $\ell$ in her certified prefix if for **every** earlier chore $h$ in her prefix some other agent $k\ne i$ sent $R^t_{k\to h}=1$. For $\ell=1$ the test is vacuous. Unlisted chores receive no requests from this agent.

**Ownership implication.** At any finite round, if a requested chore $z$ is singleton in an EF allocation, its owner must be the requester $i$. Proof by induction, considering any particular alleged EF allocation. If an earlier chore is singleton, let $h$ be the *earliest* such chore in $i$'s ranking. It is cheaper than any possible nonsingleton bundle (the prefix inequality) and cheaper than any other singleton. Agent $i$ must own it to avoid envy. But an earlier-round request by a different agent requires that other agent to own $h$ by induction. This contradiction rules out all earlier singleton chores. If $z$ is now singleton it is itself $i$'s cheapest singleton, and cheaper than any nonsingleton, forcing its ownership as claimed. This argument also covers the first round. In particular, any chore with two or more requests in a round **cannot be singleton**. Only this sufficient certificate of being nonsingleton will be used.

## 2. Finite-depth analysis of independent random prefix lists

Fix $c\in(1,2)$, the retention probability $\rho=9/10$, and a positive integer $K$. Consider $m/n\to c$. Independently each agent is active with probability $\rho$; each active agent has a uniform ordered list of $K$ distinct chores independent of other agents (inactive lists empty). For any fixed number $t$ of rounds, the proportion of chores receiving at least two requests converges in probability to $f_t$ specified by
$$
 p_0=0,\qquad \lambda_{t+1}=\frac{\rho}{c}\sum_{\ell=0}^{K-1}p_t^\ell,\qquad
 p_{t+1}=1-e^{-\lambda_{t+1}},\quad f_{t+1}=1-(1+\lambda_{t+1})e^{-\lambda_{t+1}}. \tag{1}
$$
This is a finite-depth limit, not an assertion that the final finite-graph process converges to a particular infinite fixed point.

Here is a derivation with dependencies explicit. Form a bipartite incidence graph on chores and agents with each active agent's edges marked by their positions in its ordered list. Around a fixed chore, a bounded-depth neighborhood converges to a tree: at a new chore there are independent Poisson$(\rho/c)$ numbers of **new** agents at each of the $K$ possible incidence positions. At a new agent reached at position $\ell$, each of the other $K-1$ positions leads to a new chore with the indicated edge mark. For clarity, to expose a neighborhood, query previously unexposed rows for occurrences of the next chore. If $b$ distinct chores have been queried, remaining rows are conditionally independent and have not hit those $b$ chores. The probability a given remaining row hits a specified new chore at a specified position is $\rho/m+O_{b,K}(m^{-2})$: the unconditional joint event of that hit and any prior hit costs $O_{b,K}(m^{-2})$, and prior avoidance probability is $1-O_{b,K}(1/m)$. The mutually exclusive positions give the independent Poisson limits by the multinomial limit; the remaining coordinates of the rows so found are exchangeable uniform on the remaining eligible labels. For any fixed bound $B$ on the exploration size, the probability of an unintended coincidence among these new labels and previously exposed but unqueried labels, or two neighborhoods meeting, is $O_{B,K}(1/m)$. Conditional Poisson limits are unchanged by the bounded past, and so give independent offspring at distinct queried chores.

One can remove the bound $B$: at fixed depth the expected size of each explored neighborhood is uniformly bounded. For instance the expected number of simple paths of $2j$ steps from a root chore is at most $n^j m^j (K(K-1)/(m(m-1)))^j=O_{j,K,c}(1)$, since each of $j$ distinct rows must contain its two specified distinct items. Odd path lengths add a final row containing one specified item, at cost at most $nK/m$; the sum over the fixed depth stays bounded. Markov now bounds the chance of exceeding $B$, and $B$ may tend to infinity *after* $m,n$. The same argument explores two distinct uniformly random root chores with asymptotically independent limit trees.

The request of an agent reached at rank $\ell$ uses only its $\ell-1$ earlier chores. At each such chore it queries **other** agents: the previous incidence is omitted. On the limit tree these events use independent branches. At depth $t$ the probability of finding at least one requester on such a branch is $p_t$. Thus the reached agent sends its request upward with probability $p_t^{\ell-1}$. Independent Poisson thinning at the $K$ marks yields total requests Poisson$(\lambda_{t+1})$ and both probabilities in (1). The root event is determined in bounded depth for fixed $t$. The one-root limit gives convergence of its indicator mean; the two-root limit gives covariance tending to zero. This proves the claimed convergence in probability of their average. No two requests for different chores in the *same* agent's list were assumed independent on the finite graph.

## 3. Finite constants yielding an obstruction for each $c<2$

First consider the numerical recursion at infinite list length: $\lambda_{t+1}=(\rho/c)/(1-p_t)$, $p_0=0$. This is only used to choose finite constants. The sequence $p_t$ increases to 1: it increases by monotonicity of the recursion starting at zero, and a limit $p<1$ would satisfy $-(1-p)\log(1-p)=\rho/c$, impossible since the left side is at most $1/e<0.45\le\rho/c$. Consequently $f_t\to1$. Since the maximum permissible fraction of chores in nonsingleton bundles is
$$ 2(m-n)/m\ \longrightarrow\ h_c=2(1-1/c)<1,$$
choose a **fixed finite** round $t$ with $f_t>h_c$ by a positive margin using this infinite-length recursion. For this fixed $t$ the finite-$K$ recursion converges to the stated infinite-$K$ values as $K\to\infty$ by induction: the preceding $p_{t-1}^{(\infty)}<1$ at each fixed round, so the geometric tails tend to zero uniformly on a compact subinterval of $[0,1)$. Choose a fixed $K\ge2$ so the margin remains positive. By (1), whp more than $2(m-n)$ chores are certified nonsingletons, ruling out EF whenever these independent lists of length $K$ can be provided.

## 4. A single two-sided PDF-bounded disutility law realizes the prefixes

Choose $\epsilon=1/100$, $B=1000(K+100)$ and scales $z_j=2^{-2^j}$, $j\ge1$. The disjoint bands $J_j=[z_j,3z_j/2]$ lie in [0,1] and $\sum_{i>j}z_i=o(z_j)$. Set $g=B$ on their union, $g=1$ elsewhere, $H=\int_0^1g(x)dx=1+(B-1)\sum_jz_j/2$, and let $D_c$ have Lebesgue density $g/H$. This is one fixed nonatomic distribution with positive lower bound $1/H$ and finite upper bound $B/H$, including in every neighborhood of zero. Band endpoints may be given either density value.

Put $m_j=\lfloor H\epsilon/z_j\rfloor$ and $n_j=\lfloor m_j/c\rfloor$. Both diverge and $m_j/n_j\to c$, $m_j\ge c n_j$. For one agent's independent $m_j$ draws, the expected number below $z_j$ is
$$
 m_j\big(z_j+(B-1)\sum_{i>j}z_i/2\big)/H\ \longrightarrow\epsilon.
$$
The probability of any such draw is at most $2\epsilon$ eventually. The number in $J_j$ is binomial with mean $m_j Bz_j/(2H)\to B\epsilon/2=5(K+100)$ and variance at most the mean. For all sufficiently large $j$ the mean $M$ is at least $2K$ and 400; Chebyshev gives probability of fewer than $K$ at most $M/(M-K)^2\le4/M\le1/100$. By a union bound, with probability $\gamma_j\ge0.97>\rho$ there is no lower draw and at least $K$ band draws. Then each of the first $K$ chores costs at most $3z_j/2$, strictly less than the sum of the two cheapest, which is at least $2z_j$.

For iid continuous draws the order permutation is independent of their sorted values, by exchangeability (including for this discontinuous *density*). The high-probability event just defined uses only the sorted values and occurs independently from agent to agent. Retain each eligible agent independently with conditional probability $\rho/\gamma_j$, using auxiliary coins solely to extract certificates. Retention indicators are then iid Bernoulli$(\rho)$ and the retained ranked lists are independent uniform lists of $K$ distinct labels as required by §2. Every request made by them is valid for the original costs, regardless of discarded agents. The existence of an EF allocation in the costs would forbid the excess certificates for *every* auxiliary coin realization; hence whp impossibility in the joint experiment implies whp impossibility for the original market. This completes the proof.

**Prior scope.** The single-round obstruction from others' first-choice chores is the argument of Manurangsi–Suksompong (Lemma 3.1); the contribution here is the iterative prefix ownership implication, its finite-depth application, and the fixed-law subsequence construction. The positive assertion at $m\ge2n$ is their theorem and is not reproved. No priority guarantee.
