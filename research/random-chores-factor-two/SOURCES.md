# Source, scope and duplication checks

* [Manurangsi and Suksompong, *Asymptotic Fair Division: Chores Are Easier Than Goods*, SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics](https://doi.org/10.1137/25M180161X), Theorems 1.1–1.2 and concluding discussion; [IJCAI conference version](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0444.pdf); [authors' preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20704). All costs for all agents and chores are independent from one common nonatomic law on [0,1], and disutilities add. A PDF-bounded law has fixed positive lower and upper density bounds; no continuity of the density at zero is imposed. Their Theorem 1.1 already supplies the entire positive side (high-probability EF, including an algorithm, at $m\ge2n$). Their Theorem 1.2 gives nonexistence for *all* nonatomic laws when $m\le(\nu-\epsilon)n$ with $\nu$ approximately 1.1256; Lemma 3.1 uses chores that are first choice for more than one agent. Our worst-case law answers a different quantifier and does not replace their all-law lower result.
* [Gan, Suksompong and Voudouris, *Envy-Freeness in House Allocation Problems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00468), Algorithm 1 and Theorem 2.2. Removing houses whose demand cannot be satisfied, then considering the next best remaining houses, is prior work for the one-house-per-agent model with surplus houses left unassigned. Under strict preferences a first-choice collision already prevents both claimants from receiving that same house. We do not claim to originate conflict deletion. In the chore model all chores must be allocated, bundle sizes are endogenous, and a prefix can be used for the singleton ownership implication only when it is cheaper than *every* nonsingleton bundle. The argument combines these certified prefixes with a finite-depth random-incidence calculation and a fixed cost law.
* Goods, chores, ex-ante envy-freeness and envy-freeness *up to one* or *up to any* item are distinct problems. The result here is exact envy-freeness in a realized complete allocation, with no discarded chores, payments or ties introduced.
