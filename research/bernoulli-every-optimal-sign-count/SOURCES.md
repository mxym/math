# Original question and boundaries

1. Tomas Juškevičius and Valentas Kurauskas, [*On the Littlewood–Offord problem for arbitrary distributions*, Random Structures & Algorithms 58 (2021), 370–380](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20977), [authors' manuscript, end of §5, pp. 11–12](https://web.vu.lt/mif/v.kurauskas/files/2020/08/general_200704.pdf). After the numbered conjectures they ask whether varying the common Bernoulli bias for one given length can yield every possible maximizing sign count. Their [full version, §4, following Lemma 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08770v2) identifies the nontrivial odd-length question and reports numerical evidence at sizes through 31. They had already established even-length balancing. At bias $1/2$ there is an uninformative tie, whereas our realizations have $0<p<1/2$ and are strict modulo reversal.
2. Mihir Singhal, [*Erdős–Littlewood–Offord problem with arbitrary probabilities*, Discrete Mathematics 345 (2022), 113005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2022.113005), [full manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.02886v2). Theorem 1.2 proves the reduction from arbitrary nonzero real coefficients to signs; Lemma 3.2 gives the small-bias balanced case, used here for the smallest imbalance. Propositions 3.3–3.5 describe large odd lengths at a **fixed** bias. In particular Proposition 3.3 already realizes each fixed odd denominator as the optimal imbalance for all *sufficiently large* odd lengths depending on that bias. This does not prescribe every target at each given finite length, including targets that grow with the length. The new result supplies strict, explicitly tuned witnesses uniformly, including those targets. The method uses the characteristic-function approach of Singhal with new effective uniform comparisons; it is not a new reduction to signs.
3. J. N. Darroch, [*On the distribution of the number of successes in independent trials*, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 35 (1964), 1317–1321](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703287). His theorem controls every mode of a sum of independent Bernoulli variables, including those with different success probabilities. Here it bounds the candidate phase after translating a signed sum.
4. Arnaud Marsiglietti and James Melbourne, [*A note on the Littlewood–Offord problem for discrete log-concave distributions*, arXiv:2510.25869v2, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25869v2#S3.Thmtheorem1), establish majorization by a sign choice for independent finitely supported log-concave integer variables; their Proposition 3.3 bounds common-bias Bernoulli concentration. Neither selects a bias giving each prescribed finite sign count.

The repository's [optimal sign count at fixed even-denominator bias](../bernoulli-optimal-sign-period/README.md) proves Singhal's separate eventual-periodicity conjecture. The quantifiers are reversed: there the bias is fixed and the sample size grows; here the sample size and target are fixed and the bias may be chosen. The [odd iid non-Bernoulli lattice-law counterexamples](../odd-iid-littlewood-offord-weights/README.md) concern the distinct numbered Conjecture 1 in the same Juškevičius–Kurauskas paper. The [three-summand concentration theorem](../three-summand-point-concentration/README.md) varies laws under atom caps, not sign counts of one Bernoulli law.
