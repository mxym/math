# When the catropy of a segment-uniform geometric grid reaches class growth

Let $M$ be a finite, nonempty $0/\!\pm1$ matrix. Its cell graph has one vertex per nonzero cell; two distinct cells are adjacent if they share a row or column. For each connected component $K$ of this graph write $g_K=\operatorname{gr}(\operatorname{Geom}(M_K))$, restricting to those cells. A **star component** means that all of its cells lie in one row or all lie in one column; a single cell counts. This terminology refers to its bipartite row–column incidence graph, not its cell graph (which is complete). Put $g=\max_K g_K$. All logs are base two.

Choose positive masses $p_e$ of total one on the nonzero cells, assign each row and column its total mass as height and width, and distribute $p_e$ uniformly along the appropriate full cell diagonal. This gives a permuton $\mu_p$. Conversely the dimensions of a permuton of this form are forced by the cell masses and the uniform marginals (empty strips can be discarded). Define its catropy by $C(p)=\lim_n H(\sigma_n^{\mu_p})/n$, where $\sigma_n^{\mu_p}$ is the permutation given by $n$ independent samples. This is the segment-uniform model of Borga–Féray–Winkler, *Permutons*, §8.3 Problem 8, not arbitrary measures on the segments.

**Theorem.**
1. The limit exists for every $p$. The function extends continuously to the closed mass simplex. A finite formula below approximates its value and its supremum with a uniform explicit error tending to zero.
2. The supremum of $C(p)$ over strictly positive masses equals $\log g=\log\operatorname{gr}(\operatorname{Geom}(M))$ **if and only if some star component has $g_K=g$**. Otherwise the supremum is strictly less. The growth numbers appearing in this graph criterion are given by Bevan's matching-polynomial theorem; for a star of $k$ cells, $g_K=k$.
3. For each connected component let $c_K$ denote its optimized catropy under total mass one (effectively specified by item 1). Its maximum is attained and every maximizing mass vector has full support in $K$. The overall supremum is $\max_K c_K$; it is attained with *all prescribed cells positive* iff all $c_K$ are equal. In particular the growth bound is attained with all cells positive iff every component is a star of the same size; then the conditional law within each star is uniform and the positive component totals are arbitrary. Always allowing zero cell masses instead makes the supremum a maximum by compactness. We do not give closed symbolic values or unique optimizing masses within general nonstar components.

## 1. Encoding the sampling law and computing its rate

We use the established trace encoding of geometric grid classes (Albert–Atkinson–Bouvel–Ruškuc–Vatter, §§4–7; Bevan, §2). For a consistently oriented grid, assign signs to the rows and columns so that the product of their signs in each nonzero cell is its slope sign. A cell diagonal has a base endpoint specified by these signs. For a uniform draw on it, its fractional position from that endpoint is a uniform $U$ independent of the cell label. Sorting the iid positions $U$ thus gives an iid word of cell labels with law $p$. Adjacent letters in distinct rows **and** columns may be interchanged without changing the gridded permutation. Conversely the gridded permutation determines the relative order of every pair of occurrences in the same row or column, after orienting that strip by its sign. Occurrences of the same letter are also ordered. These are exactly the relations of the word's **heap**: take the transitive closure of the word orders on pairs of equal or adjacent labels. Its linear extensions are precisely the word representatives under the permitted swaps. Repeated equal labels cannot yield multiplicities among representatives of a single extension since their chain orders them. Thus the length-$n$ trace (equivalence class of words) and the gridded sampled permutation are in bijection. Forgetting the grid loses only $O_M(\log(n+1))$ bits, since the strip boundaries in each of the two ordered sets of ranks have at most $(n+1)^{\#\mathrm{rows}+\#\mathrm{columns}}$ choices.

For any signs, bisect every row and column. Each original segment gives two segments of equal mass $p_e/2$ in opposite half-cells. These form the consistently oriented double refinement $M^{\times2}$ (the fractional base directions may alternate by strip). Conditional on its refined label, the position from the corresponding base is again uniform. This refinement preserves the set of ungridded realizations; gridded multiplicities remain polynomial in $n$. Use the refined alphabet $A$ with law $u=(p_e/2,p_e/2)_e$, or use $A$ and $u=p$ unrefined when consistent orientations are available. The same analysis applies on a simplex face by simply omitting zero-probability labels; this is also the natural deletion of empty cells and collapsed empty strips.

For an iid word, let $T_n$ be its trace and $L_n(u)=H(T_n)$. This is a finite explicit function: for each trace $t$, its probability is $\ell(t)\prod_{a\in A}u_a^{N_a(t)}$, where $N_a(t)$ counts occurrences and $\ell(t)$ counts the linear extensions of its heap. It can for example be computed by enumerating all length-$n$ words. Entropy is subadditive since traces multiply by concatenation. Its rate $h(u)=\inf_n L_n/n$ satisfies the following uniform bounds, for $s=|A|$:
\[
0\le L_n(u)/n-h(u)\le s\log(n+1)/n. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed cut a word of length $kn$ into $k$ independent length-$n$ blocks. From the whole trace and the count of each letter in each block one recovers all the block traces: for each label assign the ordered occurrences to the blocks in the indicated counts, then restrict the heap or equivalently its order on dependent pairs. Thus $kL_n\le L_{kn}+ks\log(n+1)$; let $k\to\infty$. The sampling rate is $C(p)=h(u)$ by the entropy bound for forgetting the grid. Formula (1) gives uniform convergence of continuous functions even on the boundary, and the same two-sided bound on maximizing $L_n/n$ versus maximizing $C$. These are effective approximations for specified computable masses and for the supremum: on the compact probability simplex a finite grid suffices in the latter computation. For instance at total variation distance $\eta$ of mass vectors a coordinatewise coupling differs in at most $n\eta$ probability over an $n$-sample, and the output entropy discrepancy is at most $H_2(t)+tn\log s$ where $t$ is the output disagreement probability (or use the supremum of that expression over $0\le t\le\min(n\eta,1)$). Here $H_2$ denotes binary entropy. This procedure need not be efficient.

For original components of masses $s_K$, labels from different components commute also under refinement. The trace is the tuple of component subtraces. The vector of component counts has entropy $O_M(\log(n+1))$; given counts, the subtraces are independent iid traces of those lengths with the corresponding conditional label laws. Dividing by $n$ and using (1), or conditioning on binomial counts, yields $C(p)=\sum_{K:s_K>0}s_K C_K(p|K)$ where $p|K$ is normalized. Likewise the count of all length-$n$ traces is the convolution of the component counts: the number of count vectors is polynomial. Polynomially many griddings and the existing component growth rates yield $g=\max_K g_K$. Within any connected original component, the refined dependence graph is either connected or consists of two isomorphic connected copies covering the original. Indeed every edge joining two original cells (sharing one strip) lifts to a perfect matching between their two halves, and swapping both halves is a graph automorphism. Lifting a path visits a lift of each original cell from either starting lift. Thus there are at most two components and in the split case they are exchanged and each maps isomorphically onto the original. If the original cell graph is noncomplete, each lifted component is noncomplete, since it contains lifts of two nonadjacent original cells. If the original graph is complete, its cells all lie in one strip: any other cell adjacent to two distinct cells in the same row must lie in that row, and likewise for columns. It is then consistently orientable without refinement.

## 2. Maximizing laws have full support on a connected component

We give the argument for a finite trace alphabet, calling two distinct labels dependent when they cannot be swapped. Suppose $x$ is missing from the support but a dependent neighbor $a$ has probability $v>0$. Insert $x$ with mass $\varepsilon$, rescaling the old alphabet by $1-\varepsilon$. Then
\[
h((1-\varepsilon)u+\varepsilon\delta_x)
\ \ge\ (1-\varepsilon)h(u)+H_2(\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\log(1+2/v). \tag{2}
\]
For clarity, a length-$n$ new word is generated by a mask of fresh letters of size $K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\varepsilon)$ and an independent old word $Y$ of the remaining length $N=n-K$ with law $u$. Conditional on $K$, the mask is uniform on $\binom nK$ choices. The output trace determines $K$, the old trace (erase $x$, retaining dependent pair orders), and the projection to $\{a,x\}$. With the full old word $Y$ known, this projection fixes the numbers $k_j$ of insertions between consecutive $a$'s, including the two end gaps. If an old gap contains $t_j$ non-$a$ letters, its $k_j$ insertions can occupy weakly ordered slots in at most $(t_j+1)^{k_j}$ ways. The conditional entropy of the hidden mask is therefore at most the expected $\sum_j k_j\log(t_j+1)$. Given $Y,K$, the numbers of insertions in its $N+1$ individual slots form a uniform weak composition, so $E[k_j|Y,K]=K(t_j+1)/(N+1)$. The mean logarithm of the gap-slot count at a uniform old slot is at most $\log(1+2/v)$: scan from that slot to the nearest $a$ in each direction, with each truncated non-$a$ run having expected length at most $(1-v)/v$ under the iid old word, and apply Jensen. This also handles $N=0$. The trace entropy is
\[
 H(K)+E L_N(u)+H(T_n\mid K,\mathrm{trace}(Y))
 \ \ge\ H(K)+E L_N(u)+H(T_n\mid K,Y).
\]
The last conditional entropy is at least $E\log\binom nK-EK\log(1+2/v)$ by the hidden-mask bound. Using $H(K)+E\log\binom nK=nH_2(\varepsilon)$ and $L_N\ge Nh(u)$ proves (2) on division and passage to the limit. In particular adding a neighboring label increases the rate for sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$.

For the original grid masses, if one absent cell touches one present cell in a strip, each absent half touches a positive-mass half of the present cell in the refined alphabet. Add both missing halves successively: for desired total new mass $\varepsilon$, add the first half with mixing proportion $\varepsilon/(2-\varepsilon)$ and the second with $\varepsilon/2$. The final probabilities are precisely $\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2$ and $(1-\varepsilon)u$. In both uses of (2) a neighbor has mass bounded below as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, and the current rate is bounded by $\log|A|$. The $H_2$ term hence makes each increase strict for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. If a maximizing vector on a connected original component missed any cell, some absent cell would touch a present one; this contradicts maximality. Continuity in (1) guarantees at least one maximizing vector on the closed simplex, now necessarily interior. The component decomposition gives the attainment criterion for the overall supremum. Although component catropies can be approximated, this tie criterion does not assert a general decision algorithm for equality of two computable reals.

## 3. A strict entropy gap for a connected noncomplete dependence graph

Let the finite dependence graph on a trace alphabet be connected and noncomplete, and let every label have positive probability. Choose an induced path $a,b,c$ with three distinct labels, so $ac=ca$, and neither $a$ nor $c$ commutes with $b$. Let $V$ be a word visiting every label in a walk of the dependence graph ending at $b$ (repetitions allowed), and let $V^R$ be its reversal. Its length is $d$. Importantly, in a longer word any occurrence of
\[
           V\ (abc)\ V^R \tag{3}
\]
has two identifiable *separating occurrences*: the last $b$ in the left guard (meaning its last position) and the first $b$ in the right guard. Each is comparable in the heap with **every** occurrence: the left guard is a chain, and any earlier letter precedes its own label in that guard, hence its last $b$; dually use the right guard for anything later. Each separator is comparable to all three internal letters as well. The same holds if the middle trace is $acb$ (represented by $acb$ or $cab$). A separating occurrence at rank $j$ means an element with exactly $j-1$ predecessors and comparable with all elements; it occupies position $j$ in every representative. It is unique at that rank.

Use disjoint deterministic word windows of length $L=2d+3$ starting at positions $1,1+L,\ldots$ within the length $n$ word. Call a window **eligible from the trace** if at its two designated ranks (positions $d,d+4$ relative to that window, counting from one) there are separating occurrences labelled $b$, and the intervening three-letter trace is $abc$ or $acb$. No guard word is required in this intrinsic definition; (3) just ensures its occurrence. Switch either internal trace to the other at any eligible window. This is well defined because the two separators split the whole heap as an ordinal sum of a prefix, $b$, the internal trace, $b$, and a suffix. More explicitly, in the recomposed trace each original prefix element still precedes the left $b$ by relations inside the prefix and that $b$, and dually each suffix element still follows the right $b$; both internal traces lie between the two $b$'s by dependence. The prefix and suffix retain their induced heaps. Therefore other disjoint designated windows have unchanged eligibility and type (every outside element is comparable to all the replaced internal elements in both versions). The switches partition length-$n$ traces into finite classes, each class a full cube of binary choices, one for every eligible window.

All traces in the cube have identical label counts. Their linear-extension counts factor over the ordinal separators. Each internal $abc$ is a chain with one representative whereas $acb$ has two. Accordingly, conditional on this cube, the law of the iid trace on its $j$ choices is a product of Bernoulli$(2/3)$ choices, while the law of a uniform length-$n$ trace on them is a product of fair choices. Relative entropy to the uniform law, using its chain rule on the cube partition and dropping the nonnegative term for the class index, gives
\[
 \log |\mathcal T_n|-L_n(u)
 \ \ge\ (1-H_2(2/3)) E_u[j]
 \ \ge\ (1-H_2(2/3))\lfloor n/L\rfloor\Pr_u(VabcV^R). \tag{4}
\]
The last factor is positive, and uses just the indicated literal word probability in each fixed window. Independence across windows is not needed. Thus the iid trace entropy rate lies strictly below the log of the length growth. In contrast, for a complete dependence graph with $k$ labels there are no swaps, so the trace rate is the ordinary single-letter entropy and its maximum is $\log k$ at the uniform law.

## 4. Comparing the optimal rates

For a nonstar connected original component, every maximizing vector is interior by §2. All of its refined components are connected noncomplete and have positive label laws. Apply (4) within each. If there are two, they are isomorphic; their growth rates coincide. The weighted trace entropy decomposition in §1 therefore puts the attained maximum strictly below $\log g_K$. A star is consistently orientable and yields the free-word case: $c_K=\log g_K=\log |K|$, with the unique uniform conditional mass vector. Since $C$ on the whole grid is the weighted average of the component catropies and $\log g=\max_K\log g_K$, the optimized rate equals the overall log growth precisely when at least one largest-growth component is a star. If all masses must be positive, equality with that growth rate requires and suffices that every component attain it; equivalently all are stars of the same size with uniform conditional masses. This proves the assertions.

The results concern the growth-equality and segment-uniform optimization parts of Problem 8; no assertion is made about general singular permutons or a neighborhood large-deviation principle. The known count-growth formula and trace encoding are inputs, not new claims.
