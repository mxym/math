# Sources and scope

## What was known and what is shown here

* **Question and upper bound.** J. Borga, V. Féray and P. Winkler, [*Permutons*, v1, 9 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12104), end of §5.2 and [§8.3, Problem 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1#S8.SS3), ask about the entropy of permutations sampled from uniformly populated geometric-grid segments as the masses and cell dimensions vary. Their class-count bound is $C\le\log_2\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal C)$. This note determines when equality holds in the supremum and when a strictly positive mass vector attains it. It does not settle their wider singular-measure questions.
* **Known encoding and growth.** M. Albert, M. Atkinson, M. Bouvel, N. Ruškuc and V. Vatter, [*Geometric grid classes of permutations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.6319), Propositions 4.1–4.2 and 7.1: consistent orientations via refinement and the bijection between finite traces and gridded permutations. D. Bevan, [*Growth rates of geometric grid classes of permutations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4246), §2 and Theorem 1.2: the geometric class growth is the square of the largest zero of the matching polynomial of the row–column graph of the double refinement. Neither the encoding nor the growth computation is claimed here. The limit shape of a uniform permutation from a *monotone* grid class, studied in [Alshammari and Bevan's enumeration and limit-shape paper](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/15958), is a different sampling question from the Shannon entropy of independent points from a fixed geometric-grid permuton.
* **Overlap on graphs of functions.** B. Maga, [*On the Sampling Entropy of Permutons*, Random Structures & Algorithms 68, e70086; first published 25 July 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.70086), [author version, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18518). For a finite piecewise continuously differentiable measure-preserving graph the sampling rate is the integral of log absolute derivative, and under bounded derivative agrees with Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy. In particular, after transposing coordinates if needed, a one-strip star has slopes of absolute value $1/p_e$ on intervals of lengths $p_e$. Maga's formula therefore already gives the single-star rate $H(p)$ and its uniform maximum; entropy does not change on transposition since sample permutations invert. The multivalued nonstar case is not in general a graph in either orientation.
* **Uniform traces versus iid letters.** S. Abbes and J. Mairesse, [*Uniform and Bernoulli measures on the boundary of trace monoids*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5879), explicitly show in their introduction, on the three-letter example with one commuting pair, that no iid choice of successive letters induces the uniform law at infinity. Their notion of a Bernoulli measure uses multiplicative prefix-cylinder probabilities on the boundary; it need not arise by taking iid letters before quotienting. See also [Abbes, Information and Computation 255 (2017), §§5.2–5.3](https://www.irif.fr/~abbes/pdf/2017-IC.pdf). The example and the distinction are prior. Inequality (4) of the proof supplies instead a positive **per-letter, finite-trace entropy deficit** on every connected noncomplete dependence graph with full support. Merely distinguishing two probability laws does not show that their relative entropy grows linearly. Abbes, Mairesse and Chen [Theorem 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.03762) prove strict loss in *counting growth* upon removal of an action in irreducible concurrent systems; this is not the neighboring-letter insertion inequality (2) for *optimized iid sampling entropy*. [Krob, Mairesse and Michos](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0112012) distinguish word and trace distributions in their study of average parallelism, a height/length statistic rather than this Shannon optimization.

## Search and limits

The late-2026 comparison specifically searched **1 July–19 November 2026** by geometric-grid/catropy, independent permuton sampling and entropy, and random trace/heap iid growth, checking arXiv version histories and publisher records rather than relying on the dates of search results. The November survey still lists the question. Two further contemporaneous near matches were checked: [Maga–Szepessy, 9 November, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12789) concern order patterns along trajectories of a dynamical map, not independent draws from a permuton; [*On first-order limiting probabilities for small permutation classes*, 25 October, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34328) concerns logical probabilities for uniform class members (including geometric-grid encodings), not these iid sample entropies. The July RSA article and its earlier author preprint are the same line of work, not separate priority records. No same-scope statement of the general strict gap, interiority or equality/attainment classification was identified. This is a bounded comparison, not a guarantee of originality.

The repository comparison used the full tree at [main following #217](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/c1bf3fcc1b42d72a55ba050b73e480741af65292) and its tags, including preprints, research, formalizations and release inventories. The bounded keyword/content scan read 15,769 main blobs and 297 additional tagged blobs, including 36,543 text members, 162 PDF extractions and 160 archive visits with no extraction failures. The two target-keyword hits were citations of the Borga–Féray–Winkler survey in the repository's [layered-pattern maximizer note](../layered-permuton-all-maximizers/), not results on catropy. The earlier [1324](../packing-density-1324-unrestricted/) and other packing papers maximize a *pattern probability*, not the entropy rate of sampled permutations.

Of the 175 uploaded assets recorded for the earlier 54 releases, 102 repeated repository content and 73 attachment-only assets were separately checked by digest and size (10,756 text members, 19 PDFs and 42 archive visits). The newer planar flat-floor, eventual random-hull and 1324 scoped source archives repeat screened tagged work; the two most recent layered-permuton tags respectively identify the already-screened #217 tree and its seven research files plus two metadata files. The two latter published attachment lists contained only GitHub-generated source archives. Keyword scans need not detect every equivalent reformulation, and this inventory does not cover unpublished work outside the repository. Third-party sources retain their own rights.
