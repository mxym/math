# Source and scope comparison (19 November 2026)

**Question addressed.** [Matthias Reitzner's 16 October 2023 CRM slides, 25/26](https://www.crm.cat/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Matthias_Reitzner_Empty_Simplices.pdf) explicitly ask for the limit of the expected maximum facet degree divided by \(n\), as well as distributional information. The present note gives the mean limit and law at that scale. It does not compute a finer fluctuation scale in \(d\ge3\), nor an exact decimal for the planar square or disk.

**Direct predecessors.**

1. [Bárány, Marckert and Reitzner, *Many Empty Triangles Have a Common Edge* (2013 journal version)](https://www.renyi.hu/~barany/cikkek/133.pdf): Theorems 1–2 give an expectation lower bound of order \(n/\log n\) and divergence in probability. Their close-pair method and conditional expected-degree estimates already identify very short edges as useful candidates. The result here is not the first use of close pairs.
2. [Daniel Temesvari, *Moments of the maximal number of empty simplices of a random point set*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07481) obtains moment lower bounds for stars sharing a facet. [His dissertation, Chapter 5, Theorem 5.1.1 and Remarks 5.1.2–3](https://d-nb.info/1182682162/34) gives order \(n\), reports computations of M. Meckes and D. Temesvari for the disk/square, and gives moment orders. It does not identify the normalized limiting degree law in the passages checked.
3. [Reitzner–Temesvari, *Stars of Empty Simplices*, v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08734v2): Theorem 1.1 gives the linear expectation order, Remark 1.2 records the numerical 0.70–0.95 window, Remark 1.3 gives the order of moments. In §2, especially Lemma 2.2, they already prove a Poisson limit for the **number of small clusters** used to produce high-degree bases. This is distinct from the normalized degree of a common facet or its maximum. The older [August 2018 v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.08734v1) likewise gives the expectation order. Bibliographic/version check: [published 2024 article](https://doi.org/10.1215/00192082-11081246), [institutional record and abstract](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/196465). The abstract claims order \(n\) for the facet degree; access to the final full journal body was not available in this comparison, so precise numbering is for the indicated preprint.
4. [Brown–Silverman, *Rates of Poisson convergence for U-statistics*](https://doi.org/10.2307/3212911) treats classical rare interpoint distances, including the planar uniform model. Our scalar factorial-moment calculation for selected short pairs is included for normalization and self-containment, not as a new Poisson principle. The additional ingredients are the conditional second-moment proof for their empty-triangle degree marks, transferring it to all close sample pairs, and a tail estimate excluding the remaining edges. In higher dimension the leading value comes from nearly flat facets, not from a new close-pair theorem.

**Not the deterministic problem.** The common-edge divergence question for *arbitrary configurations*, for example [Bárány, Oberwolfach Report 3/2024, Problem 11](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/4109/OWR_2024_03.pdf), is not addressed.

**Late literature.** Checked terminology for maximum degree/number of empty triangles or simplices sharing a facet/edge, interpoint-distance Poisson limits with marks, the Meckes–Temesvari constants, and random versus deterministic stars, both all-date and restricted to July–19 November 2026; checked preprint revisions and the original bibliographic record. [Andričík et al., August 2026 v2, Theorems 1.1–1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08762v2) bound counts in deterministic planar configurations excluding larger holes. [Bhattacharya–Das–Islam–Sen, Theorems 1–2, Computational Geometry 130 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comgeo.2025.102197) study changes in total counts under deletion. Neither yields the limiting distribution of the largest iid common-facet star. No matching law in the sources examined; incomplete indexing or inaccessible publications can limit the conclusion.
