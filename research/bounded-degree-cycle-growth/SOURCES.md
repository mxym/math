# Sources, attribution and limits

* **Which conjecture?** Martin Knor, [*On the number of cycles in k-connected graphs*](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/AMUC/_vol-63/_no_2/_knor/knor.pdf), *Acta Math. Univ. Comenianae* 63 (1994), 315–321 ([author's copy](https://www.math.sk/knor/PREP/p011.pdf)). The class inequalities and order-by-order minimum are defined in §1. The final, unnumbered conjecture in §4, p. 321, imposes a fixed maximum degree as well as a minimum degree. The upper construction in his Theorem 10 for the different uncapped class need not have a fixed maximum degree. R. J. Faudree, “Connectivity and cycles,” §5.10, pp. 134–136, in [*Topics in Structural Graph Theory* (CUP, 2013)](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/02314/toc/9780521802314_toc.pdf), records Knor's question separately from the cubic conjecture described below.
* **Direct methodological precedent.** R. E. L. Aldred and C. Thomassen, [*On the number of cycles in 3-connected cubic graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jctb.1997.1771), JCTB 71 (1997), 79–84 ([author-hosted text](https://www.maths.otago.ac.nz/webdata/resources/robert_aldred/spc3c.pdf)). Theorem 3.1 establishes the superpolynomial lower bound for cubic graphs asked for by Barefoot, Clark and Entringer, with a stronger exponent than our displayed one at $D=3$. Their proof supplies the underlying strategy: extract disjoint chords on a long cycle (Observation 2.8 in the cubic setting), partition their ends, take a monotone subsequence, and use ladder switches/bypasses in separated parcels (Lemmas 2.4–2.6 and §3). The fourth-root loss from circumference to the logarithm of the count is also present there. The present note adapts this proof rather than claiming a new ladder strategy: it replaces the cubic pairing step by bounded-degree terminal packing and works through the ambient 3-connected detours with arbitrary attachment positions. Merely applying the **statement** of their cubic theorem does not cover a class whose minimum degree is at least four.
* **Length input, not count input.** G. Chen, J. Xu and X. Yu, [*Circumference of graphs with bounded degree*](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539703436473), SIAM J. Comput. 33 (2004), 1136–1170, give the base $2(D-1)^2+1$ used here. Jackson and Wormald had already proved a polynomial-in-order circumference bound. There are subsequent improvements, including [Chen, Gao, Yu and Zang](https://doi.org/10.1137/050633263); optimizing this input is not necessary to test the conjecture.
* **A limitation already implied by prior work.** P. J. Owens, [*Bipartite regular graphs and shortness parameters*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X8580025X), Discrete Math. 55 (1985), 101–106, gives shortness exponent less than one for the class of $r$-connected $r$-regular bipartite graphs, for each $r\ge4$. For $r=\delta$ these are admissible for Knor's parameters. On a subsequence the circumference is at most $n^\beta$ for some fixed $\beta<1$; summing the bound $nD(D-1)^{j-1}$ for walks of length $j$ without immediate reversals bounds the number of cycles by $2^{O_D(n^\beta)}$. This prior implication rules out a uniform exponential-in-order strengthening for each triple. It does not give the superpolynomial lower bound.
* **Related studies, different questions.** [Nguyen's 2024 work on unavoidable topological minors](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_dissertations/6431/) uses a qualitative size threshold (see Thm. 2.1.7 and the chord arrangements in §2.1); a guarantee for each prescribed ladder size with no polynomial dependence on order is not the needed count. [AlBdaiwi](https://doi.org/10.1515/ms-2017-0074) ([author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.01807)) asks about the extremizer for **Hamiltonian cubic** graphs. [Alahmadi, Aldred and Thomassen](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2019.04.005) study cycles in 5-connected triangulations and discuss stronger exponential-growth questions with additional hypotheses. Counting [Hamiltonian subsets with a growing degree parameter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07467), counting distinct lengths, and [prescribing a cycle count](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-026-03047-3) are different problems.
* **Owner work.** [#200](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/200) concerns the large-average-degree factorial scale and a minimum-degree-four case *without* fixing maximum degree, explicitly leaving the conjecture here outside its scope. [#197](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/197) and [#193](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/193) are inverse spectrum results, not an order-by-order minimum.
