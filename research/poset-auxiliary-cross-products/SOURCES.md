# Primary sources, attribution, and scope

- Swee Hong Chan, Igor Pak and Greta Panova, [*On the cross-product conjecture for the number of linear extensions*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/on-the-crossproduct-conjecture-for-the-number-of-linear-extensions/044B8106B0B02D7C13219FA9AE438EDF), *Canadian Journal of Mathematics* 77 (2025), 535–562, published online January 19, 2024, DOI [10.4153/S0008414X24000087](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X24000087). We checked the **Cambridge journal text**: it specifies CPC1 and CPC2, proves the universal factor-2 replacements in Lemma 3.5, gives a width-three family in Proposition 7.1, derives the signed relabeling in Proposition 7.2, and asserts the width-two auxiliary inequalities in Corollary 7.3. Only the latter numerical assertions are contradicted here. The article itself correctly shows that an *unrestricted* signed generalization fails. [arXiv:2306.09240v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09240v1) and [author-hosted manuscript](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/QuantCPC26.pdf) are also available.
- The same authors, [*The cross-product conjecture for width two posets*](https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/8679), *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* 375 (2022), 5923–5961. We checked §3.2 in the [author-hosted manuscript](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/CP-v57.pdf) and [arXiv:2104.09009v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09009v3). In both sources, Theorem 3.3 includes a negative first gap, and Corollary 3.4 imposes no same-chain hypothesis on the two marked ranks. Their §3.3 application actually uses two ranks on the same constituent chain. The positive-domain main Theorems 1.4 and 1.6 are distinct from these auxiliary statements. The AMS publication details were checked separately; the publisher's typeset full text could not be directly retrieved in this check. We therefore pin the exact 2022 wording to those two open primary versions, not to an unread publisher PDF. Cambridge Corollary 7.3 expressly cites the earlier Theorem 3.3 for its conclusion.
- S. Felsner and W. T. Trotter (1993, Conjecture 8.3) and G. Brightwell, S. Felsner and W. T. Trotter, [*Balancing pairs and the cross product conjecture*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01110378), *Order* 12 (1995), 327–349, Conjecture 3.1: the original question concerns **positive** adjacent gaps and a different arrangement of terms. [Chan and Pak, *Linear extensions of finite posets*](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/LEsurvey11.pdf), §9.7, summarizes the partial results including the positive width-two theorem. The older Graham–Yao–Yao inequality is an independent classical theorem: see [Graham, A. C. Yao and F. F. Yao, *Some monotonicity properties of partial orders*](https://fanchung.ucsd.edu/ron/papers/80_06_partial_orders.pdf); original and subsequent proofs are cited in [§3.3 of the width-two paper](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/CP-v57.pdf). The five words here do not contradict that result. In particular, no claim about a proof of the full positive-gap conjecture is made.
