# Sources and comparison

1. Stefan Steinerberger, [*Kaczmarz Kac Walk*](https://doi.org/10.1090/qam/1711), *Quarterly of Applied Mathematics* **83** (2025), 655–666; [open author text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06614v1), §§1.2, 1.4, 2.3. The walk skips parallel/antiparallel pairs. The author describes the one-extra-row singular-value pattern and the four planar directions, but does not explain their general finite terminal law. The formulated observation is about this particular walk, not arbitrary row projections or Kac's random rotations. See also his [March 31, 2026 problem list, §56, pp. 54–55](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf#page=54). The continuum equation in that section is not needed or analyzed here.
2. Isabel Detherage and Rikhav Shah, [*A Kaczmarz-Inspired Method for Orthogonalization*](https://doi.org/10.1090/qam/1719), *Quarterly of Applied Mathematics* **84** (2026), 31–43; [open version 3, Eq. (1) and final directions preceding Eq. (23)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16101v3). Their Corollary 2.8 gives an affirmative determinant-time bound depending on the initial determinant. The **conjecture**, not that theorem, replaces the initial determinant dependence by $\log(\kappa(A)/\varepsilon)$. It concerns uniform random pair draws, an unnormalized determinant volume with unit columns, and constant success probability. The article already recognizes the deterministic Gram–Schmidt strategy for independent input. We checked the precise conjecture in the open v3; no accessibility claim about the publisher's PDF is needed.
3. Detherage and Shah, [*Matrix Factorizations with Uniformly Random Pivoting*, v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02023v4), Eq. (10), Theorem 4.3, Remark 6 and Corollary 4.4. The inverse-diagonal potential, its exact mean contraction, and its specialization to this walk are theirs. Their $n^2\log(n/\delta)$-scale approximation bound for moderately conditioned inputs is also prior. The new issue here is whether the additional log dimension can be *necessary* for the determinant objective: the disjoint-block construction gives a matching minimax leading coefficient at fixed parameters. Compare also [Detherage's dissertation](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ihd/dissertation/dissertation.pdf), Chapter 3, Eq. (3.10), Theorem 3.4.3 and Remark 6. These results assume full column rank/positive definite Gram data; they do not describe absorption of overcomplete row systems.
4. William Pickering and Chjan Lim, [*Solution of the multistate voter model and application to strong neutrals in the naming game*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.93.032318), *Physical Review E* **93** (2016), 032318; [open paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02757). The complete-graph voter mean for distinct starting opinions is $(m-1)^2/m$ in units of $m$ updates, hence $(m-1)^2$ individual pair draws. The quotient by planar orthogonal crosses is the application in this note. Coupon collection and the backward coalescence representation of voter dynamics are classical.

**Comparison boundary (November 19, 2026).** Searches for the names, article IDs, DOIs, overdetermined/planar absorption, coupon-collector determinant lower bounds, and random pivoting located the later contraction paper and thesis above. No earlier account of the stated block obstruction or finite overcomplete terminal proof was found in the sources checked. This does not establish priority. The two comparisons should not be conflated: an approximate contraction for full column rank does not yield exact finite absorption for the overcomplete case, and the very loose uniform absorption bound does not explain fast approximate convergence in practice.
