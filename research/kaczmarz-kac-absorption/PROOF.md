# Finite absorption and a sharp bounded-condition determinant time for the Kaczmarz–Kac walk

**Yongxian Zhang. Research draft; exact arithmetic.**

We consider independent uniform ordered pairs of distinct labels. The target unit vector is replaced by its component orthogonal to the source, normalized; when target and source are parallel or antiparallel, do nothing. In particular an orthogonal pair is left unchanged. Steinerberger, *Kaczmarz Kac Walk*, §§1.2, 1.4, 2.3 [open article](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06614v1) poses qualitative questions on the time evolution of overcomplete row configurations, revisited in [his 2026 list, §56](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf#page=54). The independent unit-column version (transpose of the real row version) is studied by Detherage and Shah, [arXiv:2411.16101v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16101v3). Its final directions conjecture a determinant-accuracy time controlled by the initial condition number without an additional logarithmic dependence on the dimension. We separate two time scales: the time to a fixed determinant accuracy on uniformly conditioned inputs, and exact (not approximate) absorption for arbitrary finite inputs.

## 1. A coupon obstruction and the minimax determinant time

Write $n$ for the number of linearly independent unit columns, $V(A)=\sqrt{\det(A^*A)}$, $\kappa(A)=\sigma_{\max}(A)/\sigma_{\min}(A)$ and $M=\binom n2$. For fixed $K>1$, $0<\varepsilon<1$, $0<p<1$, let $t_n$ be the least integer $t$ such that every such input with $\kappa(A)\le K$ satisfies $\Pr(V(A_t)\ge1-\varepsilon)\ge p$. The dimension of the ambient real or complex space can be any $d\ge n$; the lower example is real square. Then
$$t_n=M\log n+O_{K,\varepsilon,p}(M).$$
The logarithm is natural. In particular the conjectured $O(n^2\log(\kappa(A)/\varepsilon))$ determinant time at constant probability fails, even for fixed $\kappa(A)=2$ and $\varepsilon=1/10$.

**Lower example.** In each of $s=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ orthogonal planes with axes $e,f$, take $e,he+v f$, where $0<h<1$ and $v=\sqrt{1-h^2}$. Add an orthogonal unit column if $n$ is odd. The Gram block has eigenvalues $1\pm h$, giving $\kappa=\sqrt{(1+h)/(1-h)}$. A cross-block pair does nothing. The first selection of a designated within-block pair restores orthogonality within that block; the vectors remain in their own planes and never change there afterwards. The determinant volume is exactly $v^{U_t}$, where $U_t$ counts designated pairs never sampled in the first $t$ steps. In particular the rational choice $(h,v)=(3/5,4/5)$ at $\varepsilon=1/10$ succeeds if and only if all designated pairs have been sampled. The expected exact success time for this example is $M\sum_{j=1}^s1/j$, by the successive geometric waiting times with hit probabilities $j/M$.

For the general lower bound take $h=(K^2-1)/(K^2+1)$; $V(A_t)\ge1-\varepsilon$ is equivalent to $U_t\le b$, where $b=\lfloor\log(1-\varepsilon)/\log v\rfloor$ is fixed. The mean is $\mu=s(1-1/M)^t$, and distinct nonhit indicators have nonpositive covariance since $(1-2/M)^t\le(1-1/M)^{2t}$ for $n\ge3$. Thus $\operatorname{Var}U_t\le\mu$. For $t=\lfloor M(\log n-C)\rfloor$ we have $\mu\to e^C/2$. Choose $C$ large enough that this limit exceeds $b$ and $\mu/(\mu-b)^2<p$ for every $\mu$ sufficiently close to that limit. Chebyshev's inequality gives failure of the uniform guarantee at this $t$. For $t=O(n^2)$ instead, $\mu$ grows linearly and success probability tends to zero. The volume is nondecreasing: on a nontrivial update, subtracting a multiple of the source leaves volume unchanged and normalization divides by a factor at most one. Hence the one-time lower bound also bounds the hitting-time quantile from below.

**Matching upper bound from known potential contraction.** For the positive definite, unit-diagonal Gram matrix $B=A^*A$, set $\Gamma(B)=\operatorname{tr}(B^{-1})-n$. Detherage and Shah [*Matrix Factorizations with Uniformly Random Pivoting*, Eq. (10), Theorem 4.3 and Remark 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02023v4) established, in a more general setting, the exact identity $\mathbb E\Gamma(B_t)=(1-1/M)^t\Gamma(B_0)$. See also [Detherage's dissertation, Eq. (3.10), Theorem 3.4.3](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ihd/dissertation/dissertation.pdf). For clarity its pair case can be recovered directly. The update gives $B'=S^*BS$ for a change of basis supported on the selected two coordinates $J$, with $S_J^*B_{JJ}S_J=I_2$. Diagonals outside $J$ of $B'^{-1}$ are unchanged, and inside $J$ their sum is $\operatorname{tr}(B_{JJ}(B^{-1})_{JJ})$, since $B_{JJ}=S_J^{-*}S_J^{-1}$. Hence the change in $\Gamma$ is the sum of $b_{ij}(B^{-1})_{ji}$ over distinct $i,j\in J$. The sum of these changes over all unordered pairs is $\operatorname{tr}(BB^{-1})-\operatorname{tr}(B^{-1})=-\Gamma(B)$, as claimed.

Its eigenvalues $\lambda_j$ have mean one, so $\Gamma\ge0$. The scalar inequality $-\log\lambda\le\lambda^{-1}-1$ gives $-2\log V\le\Gamma$. At time zero $\lambda_{\max}\ge1$ and $\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\le K^2$, so $\Gamma(B_0)\le n(K^2-1)$. Therefore
$$
\Pr(V(A_t)<1-\varepsilon)\le \frac{n(K^2-1)e^{-t/M}}{-2\log(1-\varepsilon)}.
$$
This yields the matching upper bound, including its leading coefficient. The prior contribution is the contraction; the lower example rules out the proposed faster rate. This is not a floating-point bound, a result about sharp dependence when $K$ or $\varepsilon$ varies with $n$, or a lower bound against arbitrary adaptive pivoting.

## 2. Exact absorption in arbitrary rank

Use real unit rows, now numbered $1,\dots,m$, spanning a space of dimension $r$. With probability one, in finitely many steps all rows are signed copies of the vectors of an orthonormal basis of their original span, and every subsequent step fixes them. If $m=r+1$ the positive singular values of the terminal matrix are therefore $\sqrt2,1,\ldots,1$ (the sole positive value is $\sqrt2$ if $r=1$). In general they are $\sqrt{k_1},\ldots,\sqrt{k_r}$ for positive integer axis multiplicities summing to $m$. No law for these multiplicities at general rank is asserted.

Here is an input-independent word of length $L=\binom m2$: in order $i=1,\ldots,m-1$ project each $j=i+1,\ldots,m$ against pivot $i$. After each stage the processed rows are grouped on orthogonal axes, and each remaining row either lies on one of these axes or lies in their common orthogonal complement. Induct on the stages. If the next pivot lies on an old axis, it changes no remaining row. Otherwise it adds a perpendicular axis; vectors on old axes stay put, and vectors in the old perpendicular space are either parallel to the new pivot (hence skipped) or are projected into the perpendicular space of all the axes. A pairwise update preserves the total span, including in the skip case. This proves the claim for that word from every starting state. The terminal states are precisely those in which every pair is parallel or orthogonal: a pair neither parallel nor orthogonal would change upon selection. For $m\ge2$ each nonoverlapping block of $L$ draws has probability $[m(m-1)]^{-L}$ to equal this word, independently between blocks. The absorption time is thus bounded by $L$ times a geometric waiting time. The assertion for $m=1$ is immediate. This crude tail is not an algorithmic efficiency estimate. In ambient dimension at least $m$, no shorter fixed word works on *all* inputs: it would miss an unordered pair, and that pair can be the only nonorthogonal, nonparallel one, with all other unit rows orthogonal to it and to one another.

## 3. The planar law

In dimension two, give each row the label of its unoriented orthogonal cross, its angle modulo $\pi/2$. Projecting a nonparallel target against a pivot leaves a direction perpendicular to the pivot, so the target adopts the pivot's label. If target and pivot are parallel or orthogonal, their labels agree and copying still describes the update. This quotient is exactly the complete-graph voter model. Consensus is equivalent to absorption, not to all four signed directions being occupied. The terminal cross is an initial cross, with winning probability equal to its initial proportion: each label count is a bounded martingale by symmetry of the ordered pair, and tends at absorption to zero or $m$.

For pairwise distinct initial labels the time $T$ to reach one cross has the law
$$T\ \stackrel{d}{=}\ \sum_{k=2}^m \operatorname{Geom}\!\left(\frac{k(k-1)}{m(m-1)}\right),\qquad \mathbb E T=(m-1)^2,$$
where the summands are independent and are supported on $1,2,\ldots$. For a fixed horizon $t$ trace the ancestors of all $m$ labels backward through the $t$ ordered updates. A lineage at the target jumps to the pivot; it merges with a different lineage exactly when both labels of the ordered update are presently occupied. The number of lineages thus decreases from $k$ with probability $k(k-1)/(m(m-1))$ at each step, otherwise stays at $k$. The reversed update list has the same law as iid forward draws. Consensus by $t$ is exactly one ancestor at the end of this reverse exploration for distinct initial labels. Successive holding times of this pure-death chain give the displayed distribution. With repeated initial labels, identifying the colors in the same construction makes consensus no later. The voter facts and their expectation are classical; compare [Pickering and Lim, *Physical Review E* 93, 032318 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.93.032318), whose macroscopic time unit is $m$ updates. The reduction from the walk to these labels is the application here. We give no kinetic or mean-field interpretation as part of this theorem.
