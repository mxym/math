# Algorithmic specialization, contraction, and descent

## 1. Statement and representations

Let \(f(x)=(-1)^{p(x)}\) on \(\mathbb F_2^n\), with \(p\) its reduced (squarefree) binary phase polynomial, \(s=|\operatorname{supp}(\widehat f)|\) and \(L=\|\widehat f\|_1\) for normalized Walsh coefficients. For \(d=\deg_2 p\ge2\), put
\[
 D(f)=\min\left(n,\ 1+\left\lfloor \frac{2^{2d-1}-5}{3}\log_2 L\right\rfloor\right).
\]
For \(d\le1\) an affine phase takes at most one query. A parity tree's internal query is a nonzero ambient binary linear form. A compact description consists of input data, a fixed random table, and an algorithm which, given an admissible prefix of branch labels, returns the next query or leaf. It may recompute the polynomial-size local state along that prefix. This is not an assertion that the explicit tree has polynomial size in the succinct input.

Our main theorem is a randomized output-sensitive construction from sparse ANF, with polynomial-size compact compilation and failure probability \(\delta\in(0,1)\), using the list of monomials of \(p\) (after cancellation of duplicates). An exact sparse Walsh list gives a separate variant. For Walsh input the coefficients are given as integers \(a_\xi=2^n\widehat f(\xi)\); validity as a Boolean phase is promised. The tree is exact for all choices of the coins in the Walsh model and has depth at most \(D(f)\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\). In the ANF model the *same* good event certifies both exactness on all inputs and depth at most \(D(f)\). In both models even on a bad draw the algorithm terminates in at most \(n\) queries along every path. All running times below count bit operations; basic binary linear algebra is exact. In the explicit version the bound is the cost of writing the random table plus a polynomial in the input and confidence lengths times the number of actual output vertices. This is an output-sensitive bound, not a polynomial upper bound on that number. The compact version stores the same table and an initial chart, without serializing the tree.

## 2. The rank facts used from Zhang

On an affine restriction \(q\) of the phase to \(r\) variables, let \(T_j\) be the alternating \(j\)-linear form whose value on distinct coordinate vectors with index set \(S\) is the degree-\(j\) ANF coefficient \(c_S\) of \(q\). Equivalently, for \(v_1,\ldots,v_j\) over any extension of \(\mathbb F_2\),
\[
 T_j(v_1,\ldots,v_j)=
 \sum_{\iota:[j]\hookrightarrow[r]}
     c_{\operatorname{im}\iota}\prod_{i=1}^j (v_i)_{\iota(i)}.       \tag{1}
\]
This is the degree-\(j\) polar form of the homogeneous part; if \(\deg q\le j\) it is the ordinary \(j\)th finite difference on binary directions. The latter identity follows by expanding a single monomial: a surviving term assigns each of the \(j\) directions to a different factor, and there are exactly \(j\) factors in a contributing monomial. In particular \(T_j|_{U^j}=0\) is equivalent to lowering degree to \(\le j-1\) on **every** affine coset of the binary subspace \(U\) when the degree is at most \(j\). Lower terms do not matter, and the \(j\)th difference does not depend on the offset.

For \(j\ge3\), set \(A(\mathbf t)=T_j(t_1,\ldots,t_{j-2},-,-)\) in the coordinate basis and let \(R\) be its generic rank over the field of rational functions in all anchor coordinates. If \(\deg q=j\), Zhang's Lemma 3.4 and Lemma 3.10 give
\[
 R\ \le\ 2^{j-2}\max_{\mathbf h\in(\mathbb F_2^r)^{j-2}}
          \operatorname{rank}A(\mathbf h)
 \ \le\ 2^{2j-3}\log_2\|\widehat{(-1)^q}\|_1 .                    \tag{2}
\]
If \(\deg q<j\), \(T_j=A=0\). We use (2) as an existing theorem, not as an algorithm for finding the maximizing binary anchors. The Fourier norm and sparsity do not increase on affine restriction: for \(y\mapsto a+My\), with \(M\) injective, the new normalized coefficient at \(\beta\) is
\[
 \sum_{\xi:M^{\mathsf T}\xi=\beta}(-1)^{\xi\cdot a}\widehat f(\xi).
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
Triangle inequality proves the norm claim and grouping proves the sparsity claim.

## 3. Sampling in one variable preserves the right rank

Work over \(K=\mathbb F_2(z)\). Choose every coordinate of every anchor independently and uniformly from the \(2^\ell\) polynomials of \(z\)-degree \(<\ell\). A finite subset of a field is enough for Schwartz's polynomial zero bound; it need not itself be a field. Some \(R\)-minor of \(A(\mathbf t)\) is a nonzero polynomial of total degree at most \(R(j-2)\le n^2\). Hence the specialized matrix \(A\) has rank \(R\) with probability at least \(1-n^2 2^{-\ell}\). It can never have larger rank: all \((R+1)\)-minors vanish identically even before the substitution. If \(R=0\), every draw succeeds.

On any successful draw, let \(U=\ker A\) over \(K\). Then
\[
 T_j(K^r,U,\ldots,U)=0.                                            \tag{4}
\]
Here is the specialization step in Zhang's Schur-complement argument. Write the anchors as \(a_i\). Choose a complement \(S\) to \(U\), on which the restriction of the symmetric bilinear form \(A\) is nonsingular; symmetry and \(U=\ker A\) guarantee this. In the fixed basis \(S\oplus U\), perturb the anchors by \(a_i+s_iw_i\) with arbitrary \(w_i\in K^r\) and independent \(s_i\). The \((R+1)\)-minors are still zero, because the generic identities allow **any** specialization, including these perturbations. The upper-left block \(P(s)\) is invertible over \(K(s)\) and at \(s=0\). With off-diagonal block \(B(s)\) and lower-right \(E(s)\), Schur complementation therefore yields \(E=B^{\mathsf T}P^{-1}B\), with \(B(0)=0\), in the formal power series ring at zero. There is no presumption about an algebraically independent set of specialized \(a_i\).

For a nonempty set \(I\) of perturbation indices use subscripts \(I\) for the coefficient of \(\prod_{i\in I}s_i\). Induct on \(|I|\): (a) \(E_I=0\) when at most one \(w_i\), \(i\in I\), is outside \(U\); (b) \(B_I=0\) when all those \(w_i\) lie in \(U\). For (a), the squarefree coefficient on the right uses nonempty disjoint proper subsets of \(I\) for the two \(B\) factors. At least one of these uses only directions in \(U\), so its coefficient vanishes by (b) at smaller size. When \(|I|=1\) there are no such terms. For (b), a scalar entry of \(B_I\) has free arguments \(\sigma\in S,u\in U\). Swap \(\sigma\) with any \(w_i\), \(i\in I\), using symmetry. This is an entry of \(E_I\) for a new list of directions with at most one outside \(U\); apply (a) at the same size. This gives (4) by choosing all perturbation indices, allowing one arbitrary \(w_i\), and using two remaining arguments in \(U\). The case \(R=0\) is immediate since then the generic matrix, hence \(T_j\) itself, is zero.

## 4. Constructive \(z\)-adic descent

Compute a kernel basis \(\Lambda\) for \(A\) over \(K\) by Gaussian elimination on its linear equations (not an ambient change of coordinates). If the kernel is zero, return the binary zero space. Otherwise we use the valuation \(v(P/Q)=\operatorname{ord}_{z=0}P-\operatorname{ord}_{z=0}Q\). At each step take a nonzero entry of minimum valuation among the active columns and all as-yet unselected rows. Record its row, move its column to the next position, and divide that column by the entry. It is now integral at \(z=0\) in every row, including earlier selected rows where it was already zero. Subtract multiples of this column from later columns to zero their entries in the selected row. Continue until each column has a pivot, which is possible because the original columns were independent. The basis remains a basis throughout; prior normalized columns are not changed. At the end every column is integral, and on the selected rows in pivot order the matrix is triangular with diagonal ones. Taking residues at zero therefore gives an equally large binary subspace \(U_0\), not a possible lower-dimensional specialization of an unnormalized kernel. No ambient row addition is used: marking a pivot row only changes the set scanned, not the coordinates of \(T_j\). If an identity minor is desired, right multiply by the inverse of the selected triangular row submatrix. Its determinant is 1, so its inverse is integral and this is an additional column basis operation. In fact the selected rows achieve the **global** minimum valuation of an original nonzero maximal minor: every maximal minor is multiplied by the same \(\det C\) under the net invertible column operation \(C\), and after normalization all have valuation at least zero while the selected one has valuation zero. Thus no enumeration of maximal minors is needed.

If the draw was successful, (4) holds for every column tuple and a coordinate vector in the free slot. Those evaluations are identities over \(K\) with integral entries. The residue map is a ring homomorphism on entries regular at zero, so \(T_j(\mathbb F_2^r,U_0,\ldots,U_0)=0\). By Section 2, all cosets lower the degree; \(\operatorname{codim}U_0=R\). Even on a bad draw this descent returns a rank-preserving residue subspace for the *sampled* kernel; no vanishing conclusion is asserted then.

There is no hidden exponential precision. Entries of \(A\) have degree at most \(n\ell\). A polynomial basis of its kernel can be formed by choosing a nonsingular \(R'\)-minor of the computed rank \(R'\), writing each nonpivot solution by Cramer's rule, and clearing the common determinant. The resulting basis entries have degree at most \(R'n\ell\). In the subsequent valuation elimination an active entry after \(h\) pivots is a Schur-complement entry of the original basis matrix after those row/column selections: it is a bordered \((h+1)\)-minor divided by the corresponding \(h\)-minor. This follows from the determinant formula for \(a-bD^{-1}c\), or inductively by ordinary elimination. Pivot normalization forms ratios of those entries. Keeping fractions reduced by polynomial gcd bounds all degrees by \(O(n^3\ell)\), including each intermediate operation up to a constant factor. Field arithmetic, valuation comparison, and extraction of a residue are polynomial-time over these binary polynomials. This replaces the search over *all* maximal minors in the existential valuation proof.

## 5. Computing the contraction from either compact representation

The algorithms compute (1) for anchor arguments and two coordinate vectors; doing so for all pairs builds \(A\). They also compute the quadratic polar matrix with \(j=2\). They do not construct an order-\(j\), \(r^j\)-entry tensor.

### Exact sparse Walsh list

Maintain the exact Walsh list of the current phase by (3). In \(r\) variables write \(a_\xi=2^r\widehat{(-1)^q}(\xi)\) and \(M_j=2^{r+1-j}\), using \(1\le j\le r\). The identity of real multilinear polynomials
\[
 q(y)=\frac12-\frac1{2^{r+1}}\sum_\xi a_\xi\prod_{i:\xi_i=1}(1-2y_i)
\]
holds because both sides agree on the cube. Its real multilinear coefficients are integers, by integer Möbius inversion of the \(0,1\) values of \(q\). Thus
\[
 c_S \ =\ \left(\frac{\sum_{\xi:\,S\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\xi)}a_\xi}{M_j}\right)\bmod2,\qquad |S|=j,                    \tag{5}
\]
where the omitted sign \((-1)^{j+1}\) is irrelevant modulo two.

For each frequency \(\xi\), compute the injection sum in (1) with all chosen columns in \(\operatorname{supp}(\xi)\) and with \(c_{\operatorname{im}\iota}\) omitted. This takes a dynamic program over subsets of assigned argument slots: scan those columns; skip a column or assign it once to one still-unassigned slot, multiplying by that slot's entry at the column. Initialize the empty set with 1, and use the full set at the end. Lift binary polynomials in the arguments to integer polynomials by lifting their coefficients as \(0,1\), and do this DP modulo \(2M_j\) coefficientwise. Accumulate the outputs with weights \(a_\xi\). Each coefficient of the result is divisible by \(M_j\), by (5) after expanding the same injection sum; dividing by \(M_j\) and reducing mod 2 gives precisely (1), with no lifting ambiguity. There are \(2^j\) states, and all intermediate degrees are at most \(j\ell\); integer coefficient widths are at most \(r+2\).

No supplied \(d\) is necessary. If \(d\ge2\), restricting to a coordinate \(d\)-flat of any degree-\(d\) monomial with all other coordinates fixed to zero gives a phase with an odd number of ones: its top coefficient is the parity of its values. Adding any affine phase maintains odd parity for \(d\ge2\), so each of the \(2^d\) unnormalized Walsh sums is \(2\bmod4\), nonzero. By (3) this implies \(2^d\le s\). Hence start with ceiling \(J=\lfloor\log_2 s\rfloor\), treating \(s=1\) directly as a signed character. Every stage \(j\le J\) has \(2^j\le s\), so the contraction algorithm is polynomial in \(n,s,\ell\). Restricting and renormalizing exact coefficients in (3) uses integer arithmetic with \(O(n+\log s)\) bits; the output sums divided by the appropriate power of 2 are integers since they are again unnormalized Walsh coefficients of a sign function.

### Sparse ANF and an affine chart

Keep the original monomials; keep the current affine chart \(y\mapsto a+My\). Each original monomial of length \(b\le d\) becomes a product of \(b\) affine linear forms in \(y\). The factoring here is for one monomial **before** Boolean reduction. Do not expand the restricted ANF, and do not treat its unreduced higher powers as multilinear.

For this monomial, put the available factor indices in \([b]\), the argument slots in \([j]\), and run a DP over masks \((I,J')\) of factors already assigned to variables and slots already used. Scan the \(r\) columns \(u\) of the affine chart. Either skip \(u\), or choose one unassigned slot \(t\notin J'\) and a **nonempty** subset \(V\subseteq[b]\setminus I\) of factors whose coefficient of \(y_u\) is 1. Multiply by \((v_t)_u\) and move to \((I\cup V,J'\cup\{t\})\). At the end require every slot used, and multiply by the constant terms of the factors still outside \(I\). All operations are in \(\mathbb F_2[z]\). After scanning \(u\) columns the entry at \((I,J')\) sums over maps \(\pi:I\to[u]\) that assign a variable to each factor in \(I\), and bijections \(\eta:\operatorname{im}\pi\to J'\) that label the used columns by distinct argument slots. The weight is the product of selected affine-variable coefficients, multiplied by the corresponding argument entry at each used column. Initially there is only the empty map. In the induction, column \(u\) either is unused or has precisely the nonempty fiber \(V=\pi^{-1}(u)\) with its newly chosen slot \(\eta(u)\). These cases are disjoint and exhaust the maps. With all \(j\) slots filled the union of selected columns has size \(j\); choosing several factors at one \(y_u\) contributes one copy of \(y_u\) after Boolean reduction \(y_u^h=y_u\) for \(h\ge1\). Factors left outside \(I\) choose their constants. Summing therefore gives exactly (1) for the Boolean-multilinearized monomial, including cancellations across inputs. Each pair of disjoint masks \((I,V)\) has at most \(3^b\) possibilities in total; slot masks and choice of \(t\) contribute at most \(j2^j\). There are at most \(j\,3^b2^j\) transitions per column up to polynomial overhead. Each state is a binary polynomial of degree at most \(j\ell\), so schoolbook multiplication and addition take polynomial bit work over that degree.

Consider the only nontrivial case \(d\ge2\). Some binary affine 2-flat has nonconstant quadratic phase, because a function all of whose second differences vanish is affine. One can see this by taking two coordinate variables occurring together in a nonzero ANF term, whose second difference is a nonzero Boolean polynomial, and choosing values where it equals 1. On that flat the Walsh norm is 2, so \(L\ge2\) by (3). If \(4^d\ge 6n+4\), the bound \(D(f)=n\) follows; just query all coordinates. Otherwise \(6^d\) is polynomial in \(n\), since \(4^d<6n+4\). As \(j\le d\), the ANF DP is polynomial in the input length, \(n,\ell\), even for non-fixed \(d\). Degree \(d\) is known directly from the normalized sparse ANF list.

## 6. One depth bound for the entire tree

Start with degree ceiling \(J\) from Section 5. Process stages in decreasing \(j\) with \(j\ge3\), skipping if \(j\) exceeds the local dimension. At each affine domain use the sampled contraction, construct \(U_0\), and query a basis of its annihilator in local coordinates. Those forms are linearly independent; solving a linear system lifts them to ambient forms with at most a known label flip from the chart offset. The residual domains are exactly the affine cosets of \(U_0\), so lifting neither adds nodes on a path nor invalidates the separation. All unconditional dimension reductions consume their respective codimensions in queries, and no path ever consumes more than \(n\) independent queries. Domains, charts, coefficients and offsets can all be updated by binary linear algebra and (3).

At ceiling \(2\), compute the binary quadratic polar matrix. Symplectic elimination produces an isotropic space containing its radical plus one vector from each hyperbolic pair, of codimension \(r_2=\operatorname{rank}/2\). Query its annihilator; if the degree ceiling is valid, the remaining functions are affine. For rank \(2r_2\) the normalized Fourier norm of a quadratic phase is \(2^{r_2}\) (Zhang's Lemma 2.12; it also follows by multiplying the transforms of the hyperbolic pairs and the linear radical part). Therefore this stage costs at most \(\log_2 L\) on a successful full build, before one final query. In the ANF model the affine candidate at a leaf is obtained by evaluating the original polynomial at its chart's zero and unit vectors; on a valid degree ceiling this gives its exact constant and linear coefficients. In the Walsh model, a one-element spectrum is an exact affineness test. If the test fails after the quadratic stage, query the remaining independent coordinates and evaluate the phase at the resulting point. This fallback guarantees correctness on *every* draw in that model, not just on the good event. The ANF model has no claimed correct fallback on a bad draw.

For the probability statement choose independent anchor tables for each stage \(j\); a table has \(j-2\) rows of \(n\) independent polynomials of degree \(<\ell\), and a local chart of dimension \(r\) uses the first \(r\) entries from each row. Condition on the tables for all larger stages. The affine domains and their chart functions at the start of stage \(j\) are now fixed, even though they depend on previous branch labels and previous tables. Domain trees use disjoint cells, so there are at most \(2^n\) such domains. A fixed determinant witness for each domain has failure probability \(\le n^2 2^{-\ell}\) marginally under the new table. Domain failures can be correlated; the union bound does not assume independence. There are at most \(n\) stages, so \(\ell=n+\left\lceil\log_2((n+1)^3/\delta)\right\rceil+1\) suffices for a probability \(1-\delta\) that **every** used contraction at **every** node has generic rank. This justifies reusing the same table within a stage. Skipped zero-query stages still use only their own table. The number of random bits is polynomial in \(n,\log(1/\delta)\).

On this simultaneous event the degree ceilings are all correct by induction, with no queries above the true initial \(d\), since those tensors are zero. At each stage \(3\le j\le d\) the number of queries is at most \(2^{2j-3}\log_2 L\) by (2) and (3). Together with the last two stages, the integral depth is at most
\[
 1+\left\lfloor\left(1+\sum_{j=3}^d2^{2j-3}\right)\log_2 L\right\rfloor
   =1+\left\lfloor\frac{2^{2d-1}-5}{3}\log_2 L\right\rfloor .
\]
Combining with the unconditional \(n\)-query cap gives \(D(f)\). All tree labels are deterministic once the tables are fixed. The running times are polynomial per node using the two DPs, linear algebra, and the polynomial degree bounds above. Computing a label from a prefix needs at most \(n\) extra zero-query stages and \(n\) actual queries.

For a dense truth table the exact Walsh transform and ANF computation are polynomial in the table length. The explicit tree has at most \(2^{n+1}-1\) vertices; a fixed-confidence attempt is therefore polynomial in that length. In the Walsh construction every draw is exact; compare the returned depth against \(D(f)\) and repeat independent draws until it passes. This check is exact on the dyadic \(L\): for an integer test depth \(b\ge1\) compare \(2^{b-1}\) against the rational power \(L^{(2^{2d-1}-5)/3}\) when needed; the exponent is an integer, and its bit-expansion cost is polynomial in \(2^n\). The success probability is at least \(1-\delta\), so fixed \(\delta<1/2\) gives constant expected repetitions. No deterministic derandomization or always-shallow, always-correct sparse-ANF compiler is claimed.
