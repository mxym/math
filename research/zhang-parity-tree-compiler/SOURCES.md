# Scope and sources

1. Shengyu Zhang, [*Parity Decision Trees of Depth \(O(4^d\log\|\widehat f\|_1)\) for Boolean Functions of \(\mathbb F_2\)-Degree \(d\)* (arXiv:2611.03452v1, 2026-11-02)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03452v1). Theorem 1.3 gives the depth already, Lemma 3.4 and Lemma 3.10 give the two rank bounds used here, Theorem 3.12 gives the generic radical vanishing identity, and Lemma 3.14 gives the dimension-preserving existential descent. The degree-reduction criterion and quadratic base are Lemma 2.8 and Proposition 2.13, with the quadratic Fourier norm in Lemma 2.12. Remark 3.17 says the proof made no time claim from a succinct representation of the phase; [§4 first question](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03452v1#S4) leaves efficient construction open without prescribing the representation or ruling in/out construction randomness. The present answer specifies its representations, randomness, global success event and output costs. It uses Zhang's spectral bound as stated, not the threshold-weight variant as a computational input, and makes no claim on the separate optimal-depth-dependence or XOR log-rank questions.
2. Hing Yin Tsang, Chung Hoi Wong, Ning Xie, and Shengyu Zhang, [*Fourier sparsity, spectral norm, and the Log-rank conjecture*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.1245), FOCS 2013. Prior degree reduction, Fourier sparsity bounds and parity trees; the randomized compiler here targets the later bound of Zhang (2026), not a new existence bound. Nikhil S. Mande and Swagato Sanyal, [*On Parity Decision Trees for Fourier-Sparse Boolean Functions*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2020.29), gives related Fourier-folding bounds. It does not use the degree-sensitive 2026 radical algorithm.
3. J. T. Schwartz, [*Fast Probabilistic Algorithms for Verification of Polynomial Identities*](https://doi.org/10.1145/322217.322225), JACM 27(4):701–717, 1980, Lemma 1. The generic-minor zero bound is standard (also due to DeMillo–Lipton and Zippel), as are Schur complements, valuations, Gaussian elimination and subset DP. The algorithmic contribution combines one-variable rank specialization with effective descent and contraction evaluation in the two explicit representations. None of those standard tools is itself claimed as new.

The current arXiv listing still has v1 only; no matching compiler was located in the searched sources. This is not an absolute priority claim.
