# The Gold-inverse gcd conjecture at exponent index six

## Theorem

For every \(n\ge1\), put
\[
 G_n^{(k)}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_{2^n}^{\times}}
           (-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_{2^n}/\mathbb F_2}(x^{2^k+1}+x^{-1})}.
\]
Then
\[
                     G_n^{(6)}=G_n^{(\gcd(6,n))}.
\]
This proves the full extension-degree assertion at the fixed index \(k=6\), including noncoprime extension degrees. It does not prove the assertion at arbitrary \(k\). In addition, it gives the \(k=6\) instance of Ahmadi–McGuire–Rojas-León's refined \(L\)-polynomial factorization conjecture.

## 1. Curve, normalization and exact zeta reconstruction

Let \(D_k\) be the smooth projective model of the characteristic-two Artin–Schreier equation
\[
                         y^2+y=x^{2^k+1}+x^{-1}.
\]
The right side has odd pole orders \(1\) at zero and \(2^k+1\) at infinity. In particular it is not \(h^2+h\) over the algebraic closure (every pole of such a nonconstant rational function has even order), so the cover is geometrically connected. The standard Artin–Schreier ramification formula gives one totally ramified point above each pole, with different exponents \(2\) and \(2^k+2\), and no other ramification. The two points are rational because their base places are rational and total ramification exhausts the degree two. Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
  2g_k-2=-4+2+(2^k+2)=2^k,\qquad g_k=2^{k-1}+1.
\]
Thus \(g_1,g_2,g_3,g_6\) are \(2,3,5,33\).

Over \(\mathbb F_{2^n}\), the map \(y\mapsto y^2+y\) has image equal to the trace-zero subspace and two-element nonempty fibers. Hence each nonzero finite \(x\) contributes \(1+(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(x^{2^k+1}+x^{-1})}\) points. Including the two pole points gives
\[
                 \#D_k(\mathbb F_{2^n})=2^n+1+G_n^{(k)}.
\]
The minus sign in the analogous displayed count in the accessible Ahmadi–McGuire–Rojas-León author manuscript §5 is a typographical error. The present positive sign follows directly from the count and also agrees with their listed \(L_1\): \(G_1^{(1)}=1\) and \(\#D_1(\mathbb F_2)=4\).

The zeta numerator \(L_k(T)\) is normalized by \(L_k(0)=1\), with
\[
 \frac{L_k(T)}{(1-T)(1-2T)}
  =\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\#D_k(\mathbb F_{2^n})\,T^n/n\right),\qquad
 \log L_k(T)=\sum_{n\ge1}G_n^{(k)}\,T^n/n.
\]
Write \(L_k(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{2g_k}a_j T^j\). Formal differentiation gives the Newton recurrence
\[
 a_0=1,\qquad j a_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}G_\ell^{(k)}a_{j-\ell}.
\]
By the curve zeta functional equation \(L_k(T)=2^{g_k}T^{2g_k}L_k(1/(2T))\), one has
\[
                       a_{2g_k-j}=2^{g_k-j}a_j\quad(0\le j\le g_k).
\]
Thus the counts only for \(1\le n\le g_k\) fix the **entire** degree-\(2g_k\) numerator. In particular, the integer computations through degree 33 are sufficient, not a finite-extension heuristic.

## 2. Independent exact determination of the counts

[COUNTS.tsv](COUNTS.tsv) contains both field moduli and the exact integers \(G_n^{(k)}\) for all 43 necessary \((k,n)\) pairs. It is generated by two separate point counters.

* [count_field.cc](count_field.cc) uses polynomial shift-and-reduce multiplication. For degree \(n>1\) it chooses the smallest odd monic modulus for which the class \(g\) of \(X\) has order \(2^n-1\), checking the full power and the proper power for each prime dividing \(2^n-1\). This order means all nonzero residues are unit powers of \(g\), certifying the quotient is a field. In degree one it directly uses the generator \(1\). With \(x=g^j\), the trace terms are those of \(u_j=(g^{2^k+1})^j\) and \(v_j=(g^{-1})^j\). For a fixed multiplier, both the next field element after 64 steps and the 64-bit word of trace values are linear in the present field element. The program builds those maps on a bit basis and evaluates them using byte tables. The population count of the XOR of the two words is the number of negative signs, and a final mask counts the remaining positions. Therefore it computes the defining sum exactly without storing a full field table.
* [count_recurrence.cc](count_recurrence.cc) uses carryless polynomial products followed by long-division reduction. It chooses the modulus in descending order, first testing irreducibility by Frobenius and polynomial gcd, and then the generator's multiplicative order. The moduli differ for every \(n\ge3\). For each of the multipliers \(a=g^{2^k+1}\) and \(a=g^{-1}\), it forms its minimal polynomial \(P_a\) by multiplying the conjugate linear factors along its Frobenius orbit. If \(P_a(T)=\sum_{i=0}^r c_iT^i\), with \(c_r=1\), then \(s_j=\operatorname{Tr}(a^j)\) satisfies \(\sum_i c_i s_{j+i}=0\), by multiplying \(P_a(a)=0\) by \(a^j\) and taking trace. Initial bits come from direct powers, and a prefix is checked against direct traces. The large count then steps linear feedback states, not field elements. From each 64-bit output word it consumes 31 trace positions and retains the following \(r\le33\) bits as the next state. This independently computes the same sign count including the final partial block.

The verifiers reconstruct the known small numerators and the new one by exact integers:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_1(T)&=1+T+2T^3+4T^4,\\
 L_2(T)&=L_1(T)(1+2T^2),\\
 L_3(T)&=L_1(T)(1-4T^3+8T^6),\\
 L_6(T)&=L_1(T)(1+2T^2)(1-4T^3+8T^6)R(T^6),\\
 R(U)&=1-4U+256U^3+131072U^6-8388608U^8+134217728U^9.
\end{aligned}
\]
The 67 expanded coefficients of \(L_6\) are in [L6_COEFFS.txt](L6_COEFFS.txt), one per line starting at degree zero, with SHA-256
\(\mathtt{a21c3fc5b0c125bb6f308abb4185ec8471ac2e8c67c9cd782ecc23030469e078}\).
The coefficient list for \(R\), starting at degree zero and including zero entries, is
\[
 (1,-4,0,256,0,0,131072,0,-8388608,134217728).
\]

The reciprocal coefficient relation is verified in [verify.py](verify.py). There is also a direct exact Weil-root check on the new factor, independent of numerical root solving. Set \(z=8U\). Then \(2R(z/8)=(1+z)S(z)\) with
\[
 S(z)=2-3z+3z^2-2z^3+2z^4-2z^5+3z^6-3z^7+2z^8 .
\]
For \(x=z+z^{-1}\) this gives \(z^{-4}S(z)=x(2x^3-3x^2-5x+7)\). The cubic takes the consecutive values \(-11,7,1,-1/2,1\) at \(-2,-1,1,3/2,2\); it has a root in each of \((-2,-1),(1,3/2),(3/2,2)\), hence these are all its roots. Together with \(x=0\), each gives two unit-circle \(z\) values; the remaining factor gives \(z=-1\). Thus the zeros of \(R\) have absolute value \(1/8\), and those of \(R(T^6)\) have absolute value \(1/\sqrt 2\). The other factors have the same required radius: with \(z=\sqrt2 T\), \(L_1/z^2\) reduces to \(x^2+x/\sqrt2-2\), whose two real roots are in \((-2,0)\) and \((0,2)\); \(1+2T^2\) becomes \(1+z^2\); \(1-4T^3+8T^6\) becomes \(1-\sqrt2 z^3+z^6\), with the roots in \(z^3\) conjugate and of unit norm.

## 3. Why this settles every extension degree

The formal logarithm of a polynomial in \(T^d\) with constant coefficient one contains only powers divisible by \(d\). The exact factorization above therefore gives:

| \(\gcd(6,n)\) | Relevant quotient | Coefficient of \(T^n\) in its logarithm |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | \(L_6/L_1=(1+2T^2)(1-4T^3+8T^6)R(T^6)\) | zero, since \(n\) is divisible by neither 2 nor 3 |
| 2 | \(L_6/L_2=(1-4T^3+8T^6)R(T^6)\) | zero, since \(3\nmid n\) |
| 3 | \(L_6/L_3=(1+2T^2)R(T^6)\) | zero, since \(2\nmid n\) |
| 6 | \(L_6/L_6=1\) | zero identically |

The coefficient in the logarithm is \((G_n^{(6)}-G_n^{(\gcd(6,n))})/n\), proving the result for all \(n\). Splitting \(L_6/L_1\) as a polynomial in \(T^2\) times a polynomial in \(T^3\) also exhibits the refined factorization for the two prime factors of \(6\).

For an independent finite-to-infinite algebra check, write \(L_k(T)=\prod_i(1-\alpha_{k,i}T)\). Taking logarithms gives \(G_n^{(k)}=-\sum_i\alpha_{k,i}^n\). On each progression \(n=r+6j\) for \(r=1,\ldots,5\), let \(e=\gcd(6,r)\). The difference \(G^{(6)}_{r+6j}-G^{(e)}_{r+6j}\) is a sum of powers of \(\alpha_{6,i}^6\) and \(\alpha_{e,i}^6\) with fixed coefficients, so is annihilated by the monic characteristic polynomial whose roots are those values with multiplicity. Its order is at most \(66+2g_e\), even if roots coincide. Consequently equality of its first \(66+2g_e\) terms forces equality on the whole progression by induction. The verifier regenerates the log coefficients from the *expanded* numerators and checks the first \(70,72,76,72,70\) terms for residues \(1,2,3,4,5\), respectively (360 comparisons, maximum index 453). This check does not use a test of the quotient's support to decide the comparisons. Residue zero is the tautological \(e=6\) case. A changed last input count that preserves Newton integrality fails this verification.

## 4. What is and is not new

The Gold-inverse conjecture is due to Johansen, Helleseth and Kholosha. Ahmadi–McGuire–Rojas-León compute the earlier \(L_k\) through \(k=5\); these earlier cases are not claimed here. Carlet–Thornburgh list the gcd-dependence statement as Conjecture 5.19. The result above gives the genus-33 numerator and the fixed-\(k=6\) result, not a proof of their general conjecture. The count, proof and check use only the Artin–Schreier curve over finite fields and do not address implementations or schemes.
