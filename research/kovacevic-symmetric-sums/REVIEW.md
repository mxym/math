# Adversarial mathematical review

**Verdict: PASS for the stated symmetric subclass.** This was an independent AI-assisted reconstruction and finite stress test, not external peer review. It does not certify novelty, uniqueness, or the unrestricted Kovačević conjecture.

1. **Candidate and comparison.** The interior letters of the exceptional input each identify their residue mod r, leaving an independent sum of n−1 fair bits. The two endpoint choices feed a *single* residue with a binomial of order n. These slice masses sum to one, including r=1. On each slice the centered log-binomial has expectation zero. Because Q has full support, the entropy identity follows by expanding relative entropy with no division by the possibly zero masses of P. The direction is a lower bound on the supporting score, not on conditional entropy.
2. **Conditioning.** Reflecting each input through r/2 shows that its magnitude determines an equiprobable pair, except at zero magnitude where either independent sign yields the same value. Independence of inputs permits independent magnitudes and independent added signs. Only a linear expectation is conditioned; the argument does not assume conditional entropy is an upper bound for unconditional entropy.
3. **Signed tail bound.** If two sign configurations both exceed n−2j, twice the number of agreeing coordinates is greater than 2(n−2j), so their Hamming distance is at most 2j−1. Kleitman's odd-diameter bound applies since 2j−1<n. Its right-hand side divides by 2^n to give the *one-sided* probability for Bin(n−1,1/2) to be strictly below j. Only in the absence of integer ties can the two disjoint extreme tails be complemented directly into the desired middle block for the floor. The symmetric increasing-toward-the-center log-binomial score is a nonnegative sum of precisely these middle-block indicators, up to a constant. No block is needed for n−1=0,1.
4. **Integer ties.** As a separate check, μ_n−μ_{n−1}=log 2−(H_n−H_{n−1}); adding an independent fair bit gives a mixture of the old law and its shift, so the entropy increment is nonnegative. Together with k(n−k)/n²≤1/4 this proves the needed midpoint inequality. This agrees with the bit-deletion calculation in the proof. When all amplitudes have absolute value one, the binomial calculation is exact, including endpoints. Otherwise the sum never reaches either endpoint; one can move a single non-full amplitude by ±δ small enough for *every* sign configuration at once by finiteness. Already off-grid points stay in the same interval, and the two perturbed scores at each tie are the adjacent interval values, including for zero amplitude. Negative amplitude merely permutes sign vectors. Only a score comparison, not an off-lattice entropy inequality, is used.
5. **One unrestricted input.** The sum of the other n−1 inputs is reflection symmetric. Reflecting only the exceptional marginal consequently reflects the output. Mixing those marginal laws preserves independence of the other inputs, and concavity of Shannon entropy gives the required direction. This does not support iterating the argument with two arbitrary asymmetric inputs. As a check, the score G is itself reflection invariant, so its expectation is unchanged under this single symmetrization.
6. **Attribution.** Theorem 2.1 of Kovačević's original paper gives the attainable distribution and the unrestricted n=2 case; Theorem 2.3(a) already gives the sharp bound with up to two unrestricted marginals and the others supported on the two endpoints (possibly biased). This is a distinct, overlapping class. The ternary unrestricted result is **Theorem 7 in §4** of the [2026 submitted PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11831), not “Theorem 4.1”; it states attainment. Its §5 obstruction concerns a proof method, not the entropy maximum. Ordentlich and Yu previously established the continuous differential-entropy result under symmetry and the signed-sum comparison used here. A principal finite-alphabet step is reconciling the two binomial scores at atoms on the interfaces. No claim is made about priority or uniqueness.
