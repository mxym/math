# Sources, scope and prior-work checks

- M. Braverman, K. Efremenko, G. Kol, R. R. Saxena and Z. Zhang, [*Round-vs-Resilience Tradeoffs for Binary Feedback Channels*, ITCS 2025, Conjecture 3, Theorems 2 and 4](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22). Their normalized conjecture allows arbitrary nonnegative edge weights of sum one. The equivalence with achieving noise resilience 7/23 uses **all** such weights. Equal nonzero weights alone do not imply the channel result.
- The authors’ [full ECCC report, revision 1 of 26 November 2024](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/revision/1/download/), Conjecture 1.3, §11.1 and §11.2, Theorem 11.1, is essential prior work: all unweighted cliques and all equal-weight graphs with ≥10⁹ edges. The threshold is an edge count, not a vertex count; §11.2 uses maximal matchings and a high-weight vertex argument. K₃ and K₅ were already equality examples. The [revision history](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/) records that §11 was added after the 2022 version. The separate degree-square question (Conjecture 1.5) is not the statement proved here.
- C. S. Edwards, [*Some Extremal Properties of Bipartite Subgraphs*](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1973-048-X), Canadian J. Math. 25 (1973), 475–485; and *An improved lower bound for the number of edges in a largest bipartite subgraph*, Recent Advances in Graph Theory (1975), 167–181. The connected Edwards–Erdős bound is classical, not a new ingredient. See also [Erdős–Gyárfás–Kohayakawa, *The size of the largest bipartite subgraphs*](https://www.ime.usp.br/~yoshi/MSs/Edwards/Edwards.pdf) for a different proof and [Poljak–Turzík](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(86)90192-5) for weighted bounds. [Gutin–Yeo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05536) treat other lower bounds on maximum weighted cut; these are related tools, not the statement about expected absolute random imbalance.
- D. de Caen, [*An upper bound on the sum of squares of degrees in a graph*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(97)00213-6), Discrete Math. 185 (1998), 245–248. We give a short incidence-matrix proof for convenience.

**Search scope (18 November 2026).** Checked the conference paper and full report (including §11 and the revision history), and searched the paper title, author combinations, one-round resilience and 7/23, maximum cut versus average/random imbalance, equality/extremizers and unweighted small orders. No subsequent published or preprint proof covering all unweighted orders and the exclusion of all other equality examples was located. This is a search result, not a proof of priority. Work on the so-called graph imbalance multiset conjecture concerns differences of adjacent vertex degrees rather than the BEKSZ quantity.
