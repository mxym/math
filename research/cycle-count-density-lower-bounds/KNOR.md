# Knor's degree-four gap

**Statement.** Among n-vertex simple 3-connected graphs with minimum degree at least four and no maximum-degree restriction, the minimum number of undirected simple cycles is Θ(n³) as n tends to infinity. Knor explicitly identifies the missing lower-bound case before Theorem 11 (1994, p.319). This is not his final conjecture with fixed maximum degree.

**Upper bound.** Knor's Proposition 2 gives O(n³). Concretely, for each n≥8 join three independent vertices to all vertices in disjoint copies of K₂, replacing one K₂ by K₃ if needed so that the private orders sum to n−3. The three-vertex deletion connectivity requirement holds: after at most two deletions at least one public and one private vertex remain, every private vertex is adjacent to the surviving public one, and all public vertices are adjacent to every surviving private vertex. Each private vertex has degree ≥4, and each public one degree n−3≥5. A simple cycle uses at most three private components (between successive public vertices) or stays in one such component. Each component has bounded size, so there are O(n³) cycles uniformly over all n.

**Lower bound.** Fix a 3-connected graph G with δ(G)≥4; let H be a minimally 3-connected spanning subgraph. By Mader's theorem as quoted in Knor Lemma 4, H has at least 2n/5 degree-three vertices. Each needs an edge of F=E(G) minus E(H), counted at most twice, so m=|F|≥n/5. If Δ(G)<n/40, the greedy disjoint-edge-triple bound in the main proof (now subtracting 2Δ and 4Δ) gives at least (n/5)(3n/20)(n/10)/6=n³/2000 distinct cycles, by Knor Lemma 6.

If Δ(G)≥n/40, select a vertex v of maximum degree and write D=G−v. This is 2-connected with δ(D)≥3, hence M=|E(D)|≥3(n−1)/2. Each distinct pair a,b of neighbors of v has at least M−|V(D)|+1 paths in D: add the path a−w−b through a fresh vertex w, giving a simple 2-connected graph even when ab already exists. Apply Alahmadi et al. Lemma 1 to the edge aw in the augmented graph. Every such cycle is exactly awb plus a path from b to a in D, and the cycle-rank bound is actually M−|V(D)|+2. Thus each neighbor pair contributes ≥(n−1)/2 cycles of G through v. Different pairs have different incident cycle edges at v. The total is at least binom(ceil(n/40),2)(n−1)/2=Ω(n³). Both regimes exhaust G. The exponent matches the old upper construction; we do not identify an optimal cubic constant or constant-order extremizers.
