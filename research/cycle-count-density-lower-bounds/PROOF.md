# Cycle lower bounds in terms of order and average degree

Let c(G) count undirected simple cycles by edge set, n=|V(G)|, and d=2|E(G)|/n. All graphs below are finite, simple and undirected. Vertex k-connectivity requires at least k+1 vertices.

**Theorem.** For k in {2,3} and each fixed 0<η<k there exists d₀(k,η) such that every k-connected G of average degree d≥d₀ satisfies
$$
 c(G)\ \ge\ n^{k-\eta}\;\big\lfloor \eta d/(8k)\big\rfloor!.
$$
In particular Open Problems 2 and 3 in §7 of Alahmadi, Aldred, de la Cruz, Ok, Solé and Thomassen, *The minimum number of minimal codewords in an [n,k]-code and in graphic codes*, Discrete Appl. Math. 184 (2015), 32–39, have affirmative answers for graphs with the original sufficiently-large-average-degree quantifier. Specifically, for each fixed ε>0 and all sufficiently large d, put F(d)=floor((3d/8)^(1−ε))!. Then a 3-connected graph has at least F(d)n² cycles and a 2-connected one has at least F(d)n cycles, in particular retaining the coefficient 1/2 in Theorem 8. For ε≥1 this is a weaker conclusion; for 0<ε<1 the factorial argument in F(d) is o(d). For these corollaries take η=1. This theorem does not assert the k-th power of n with that factorial, does not answer the question on the optimal onset of the wheel minimum, and concerns graph cycles, not all linear codes.

**1. Two order bounds from prior tools.** Theorem 4 of Alahmadi et al. gives c(G)≥binom(|E|−n+2,2) for a 2-connected graph, in particular ≥n² if d≥18. The given-rank bound also occurs in earlier work of Clark and Entringer.

For k=3 take an edge-minimal 3-connected spanning subgraph H by deleting edges. By Halin's edge bound, |E(H)|≤3n for n≥8 (we only need this weak form; d≥18 implies n≥19). Put F=E(G) minus E(H), m=|F|. Then m≥6n. Choose three pairwise vertex-disjoint edges of F. There are at least m(m−2n)(m−4n)/6 unordered such triples: a chosen edge meets fewer than 2n edges, and two chosen edges together meet fewer than 4n, including the chosen edges themselves. This gives at least 8n³ triples. Knor, *On the number of cycles in k-connected graphs* (1994), Lemma 6, states that adjoining three nonedges not forming a claw to a 3-connected graph produces a cycle through all three. Apply this in H augmented **only** by the selected triple. It is not necessary, and is not asserted, that arbitrary independent edges of a 3-connected graph lie on one cycle. The intersection of the resulting cycle with F is exactly that triple. Thus triples give distinct cycles and c(G)≥n³. This is the residual-edge mechanism of Knor's Theorems 9 and 11, used here with the large average degree providing many residual edges without a maximum-degree hypothesis.

To spell out the needed disjoint-edge case of Knor's lemma, label the three nonedges uᵢvᵢ. By set Menger in H, link {u₁,v₁,u₃} to {u₂,v₂,v₃} by three pairwise vertex-disjoint paths. Together with the three new edges, these give one cycle or two. In the latter case one cycle has one added edge (necessarily u₃v₃); the other has two (u₁v₁, u₂v₂). In each cycle delete its one or two added edges mentally to mark its one or two constituent original path segments, keeping segment endpoints. Both cycles have at least three vertices: an added edge is a nonedge of H. Apply set Menger again in H between the two cycle vertex sets, trimming linkage paths so their interiors avoid both cycles. Two of the three attachment vertices on the two-segment cycle lie on the same segment; use their long complementary arc (which contains both added edges). On the one-segment cycle take the arc through its single added edge. Joining the chosen arcs by the corresponding two disjoint linkage paths yields a simple cycle through all three edges, as required.

**2. A factorial degree bound independent of order.** The classical Mader density theorem implies that every graph with average degree ≥4s has an s-connected subgraph (we do not need the stronger versions). Set s=floor(d/4). In any j-connected graph any two distinct vertices x,y have at least (j−1)! simple x–y paths. Indeed this is trivial at j=1. At j≥2, at least j−1 neighbors of x differ from y, and after deleting x the remaining graph is (j−1)-connected; prefix the edge from x to each such neighbor to all its paths to y. The resulting families are disjoint according to the first edge. This weak form and proof are contained in Pardeshi's 2026 path-count lemma; no priority is claimed for this elementary induction.

In the s-connected subgraph choose a vertex v and two distinct neighbors a,b. After deleting v there are at least (s−2)! paths from a to b, yielding distinct cycles with the two fixed edges at v. Hence c(G)≥(s−2)!. No graph-minor lifting or regularity of G is assumed.

**3. Interpolation with the correct universal quantifiers.** Write θ=η/k. At d≥18 the positive c(G) is at least both n^k and (floor(d/4)−2)!, hence at least
$$
 n^{k(1-\theta)}\big[(\lfloor d/4\rfloor-2)!\big]^\theta .
$$
The logarithm of its factorial factor divided by d log d tends to θ/4 by Stirling's formula, whereas log(floor(θd/8)!)/(d log d) tends to θ/8. Thus the former exceeds the latter for every sufficiently large d depending only on θ, uniformly for all n. This proves the theorem.

**4. Why the power is nearly best possible, not asserted at the endpoint.** The shared-separator clique example is already in Alahmadi et al.'s discussion of Problem 2 and, in related form, Knor's Proposition 2. For fixed q>k glue arbitrarily many copies of K_q along the same K_k, with no other vertex overlaps or edges between private sets. This is k-connected, has n=k+t(q−k), and average degree tends to q+k−1, which can be made larger than any fixed lower threshold. A simple cycle visits private vertices in at most k of these copies if it meets the separator, since the successive separator vertices delimit at most k intervals, each in one copy; other cycles stay in one copy. Thus there are O_q(n^k) cycles. This prevents replacing k−η by a power strictly above k at all sufficiently large average degrees. It does not exclude or establish a factorial times n^k.
