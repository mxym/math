The questions are Alahmadi et al., DAM (2015) §7 Open Problems 2–3, with original sufficiently large average degree, and Knor's 1994 stated missing minimum-degree-four cubic lower-order problem without fixed maximum degree. For the pure order lower bounds, the 2-connected cycle-rank comparison is prior, and deleting to a 3-connected spanning core leaves ≥6n extra edges at d≥18. Greedily selecting three vertex-disjoint extra edges gives ≥m(m−2n)(m−4n)/6 triples; the known Knor Lemma 6 applies to the augmented core (not arbitrary matching edges in G). I checked the self-contained disjoint case: initial opposed terminal sets linked by three disjoint paths yield one cycle or two; the single-edge cycle must carry the only new edge with ends in opposite terminal sets, and the other two are on the second cycle. A second three-way linkage between cycles, trimmed to avoid both interiors, gives two attachments in the same old segment of the two-edge cycle. Take the complementary arc containing both extra edges and the arc in the other cycle containing its extra edge. Only the marked triple is outside the core, making witnesses for different triples distinct.

The degree-only factorial lower follows from the standard Mader high-average-degree theorem and the known degree-per-first-step path induction. The two initial fixed edges at a vertex of that subgraph preserve injectivity of cycles counted, with the factorial index offset two. Taking the weighted geometric mean of the separate order and degree bounds does not assume they are witnessed by disjoint sets. Stirling's d log d comparison is strict for fixed positive η and yields a degree threshold independent of n. With η=1, the linear-in-d factorial argument dominates the required sublinear argument for 0<ε<1 and constant/bounded cases for ε≥1. Glued cliques show why larger order exponent is impossible at arbitrarily large fixed d but do not establish the exact endpoint with the same factorial factor.

For Knor's degree-four case, I verified the original minimal-core degree-three count ≥2n/5 (his Lemma 4), thus m≥n/5. If Δ<n/40 triples give ≥n³/2000. Otherwise deleting a maximum-degree vertex leaves a 2-connected graph of minimum degree at least 3 and linear cycle rank. Adding a degree-two path between any two former neighbors preserves 2-connectivity and is simple; the original Alahmadi Lemma 1 then makes its cycles through the new edge exactly the sought old paths. These are disjoint across neighbor pairs after reinstating the center, giving Ω(n³). The prior join model with blocks of size at most three supplies O(n³) with all required properties and works for every large n. Internal review only.
