# Sources and what follows from them

* **The density questions.** A. Alahmadi, R. E. L. Aldred, R. de la Cruz, S. Ok, P. Solé and C. Thomassen, [*The minimum number of minimal codewords in an [n,k]-code and in graphic codes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2014.11.015), Discrete Applied Mathematics 184 (2015), 32–39 ([author manuscript, §6–7](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/128144478/KAU3.postscript.pdf)). Theorem 8 gives a linear order factor for 3-connectivity at sufficiently large average degree. Their §7 asks whether a quadratic order factor is available (Problem 2) and whether the theorem works for 2-connectivity (Problem 3). Their shared-triangle example limits the order exponent to three. We do not answer the separate Problem 1 about the onset of the wheel extremum. Lemma 1 (cycle rank as an edge-through-cycle lower bound) and Theorem 4 (the 2-connected cycle-rank bound) are direct prior inputs. See also L. Clark and R. C. Entringer, [*The minimum number of cycles in graphs with given cycle rank and small connectivity*](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%2003/vol-003-paper%2014.pdf).
* **The degree-four question and the three-edge method.** M. Knor, [*On the number of cycles in k-connected graphs*](https://www.math.sk/knor/PREP/p011.pdf), Acta Math. Univ. Comenianae 63 (1994), 315–321. Proposition 2 is the cubic upper construction. Lemma 4 quotes Mader's low-degree-vertex theorem for minimal connectivity; Lemma 6 yields the cycle through three adjoined edges used here. Knor already obtained cubic order when the minimum degree is at least five (Theorem 9 and Corollary 10) or is at least four **and the maximum degree is bounded** (Theorem 11). The sentence immediately before Theorem 11 identifies the missing unrestricted-maximum-degree case at minimum degree four. Our high-maximum-degree count covers that case, without claiming a sharp leading constant or Knor’s separate general conjecture. The low-maximum-degree branch deliberately follows Knor’s residual-edge method.
* **Structural inputs.** R. Halin, *Untersuchungen über minimale n-fach zusammenhängende Graphen*, Math. Ann. 182 (1969), 175–188: the edge bound for a minimally 3-connected simple graph is at most 3n−9 for n≥8. See Theorem 3 in [*Constructing Minimally 3-Connected Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.3390/a14010009) and the attribution in [*Deletable edges in 3-connected graphs and their applications*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X2600018X). W. Mader, [*Existenz n-fach zusammenhängender Teilgraphen in Graphen genügend großer Kantendichte*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02993903), Abh. Math. Sem. Univ. Hamburg 37 (1972), 86–97, gives the density-to-connectivity tool; the weaker 4s form suffices ([Diestel, ch. 1](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/home/diestel/books/graph.theory/preview/Ch1.pdf)). Mader's *Ecken vom Grad n in minimalen n-fach zusammenhängenden Graphen*, Arch. Math. 23 (1972), 219–224, is the different result behind Knor's Lemma 4.
* **Degree alone and the original factorial scale.** R. Pardeshi's [2026 public manuscript, pinned LaTeX](https://github.com/rajveersinh-is-dev/cycle-spectra-connectivity/blob/0ea823d5c1a0340be89ce06957d6c6c4c9a238db/paper/main.tex) already contains the path-count induction with a stronger numerical bound. The degree-only bound in this note is a direct application of known tools, not claimed as a new path theorem. There is also a different prior route at the weaker scale of the two 2015 questions: Kim, Liu, Sharifzadeh and Staden, [*Proof of Komlós's conjecture on Hamiltonian subsets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06784), Thms. 1.2 (earlier Tuza bound) and 1.3, give an exponential-in-average-degree count even with cycles distinguished only by vertex set. Once one has the pure order bound, interpolation with this prior exponential already suffices for the original sublinear factorial argument: for fixed ε>0, log(floor((3d/8)^(1−ε))!)=o(d). It does not yield the present linear-in-d factorial argument. Cambie, Gao and Liu [also count Hamiltonian vertex subsets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07467), incorporating order and structural parameters under their degree hypotheses; that count need not distinguish different cycles on the same vertices.
* **Owner corpus.** [#197](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/197) and its [sources](../simple-3-connected-cycle-spectrum/SOURCES.md) already cite Knor, Pardeshi and Alahmadi et al. for the separate prescribed-count problem: for which integers does **some** 3-connected graph realize that many cycles? Earlier [#184](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/184), [#188](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/188) (scope corrected in #190/#191) and [#193](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/193) likewise give inverse realizations or spectra, not the universal lower bounds considered here.
