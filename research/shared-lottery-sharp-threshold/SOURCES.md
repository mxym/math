# Sources and scope

* Nick Arnosti and Carlos Bonet, [*Lotteries for Shared Experiences*, EC 2022 extended abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/3490486.3538312). In [arXiv:2205.10942, §4.3.1, pp. 15–16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.10942), equations (19)–(21) reduce incentives to a conditional weighted threshold event, and **Conjecture 1** poses the group-request comparison. Their Proposition 8 and its proof introduce the same relaxation to independent Poisson counts and establish the comparison on the rank-based classes (22); Proposition 7 and Example 1 give other small-group/dominance results. The [author-hosted manuscript](https://nickarnosti.com/ResearchPapers/Arnosti-Bonet_SharedExperiences.pdf) numbers these two threshold statements **Conjecture 4.14** and **Proposition 4.15**, with unchanged objective (20). The [author's 2024 problem description](https://nickarnosti.com/blog/openproblem/) separates this exponential-score model from a different, corrected reciprocal-probability variant. The present theorem concerns the former only.
* Weibo Fu, Yanjun Han, Guanyang Wang, Jun Yan, Peng Zhang and Zhengqing Zhou, [*Sharp small-deviation inequalities for sums of independent nonnegative random variables*, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23980v1#S1). The sharp finite-dimensional large-slack branch is the direct input to our proof. See also Zipei Nie and Jiaye Wei's [unit-slack result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24528), Zhi Ling's [Samuels theorem and Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18392v2#S1), and Yanjun Han and Zipei Nie's [conditional-expectation refinement](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22816v2#S1). These are prior probability results; we claim neither their inequalities nor their proofs. The [original problem of Uriel Feige](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539704447304) concerns independent nonnegative sums, not the ticket mechanism.
* The stronger 2026 theorem is convenient, not logically necessary. Even a dimension-free $1/e$ unit-slack Feige inequality for **arbitrary iid** nonnegative mean-one variables suffices: for $t<s<1$ add independent unit Poisson jumps of mean $s-t$ to the normalized compound sum, calling the result $W$. Split $W$ into $M$ iid blocks of mean $s/M$. For $M$ large, $s+s/M<1$, so this iid inequality gives $e^{-1}\le\Pr(W<s+s/M)\le\Pr(W<1)=e^{-(s-t)}\Pr(Z_t<1)$. Let $s\uparrow1$. Martín Egozcue and Luis Fuentes García's [2025 note](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19949) discusses the iid problem; its explicitly stated Proposition 1.1 concerns a common two-point law. John Elton's [2009 note](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3528) addresses integer-valued iid gumball machines. Our compound blocks are not generally two-point, and our main proof does not rely on either note. We have not located the ticket application in those sources.
* Shuyuan Jia, Kangning Wang and Renzhe Xu, [*Persuasion by Parts*, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02845v1#S4.SS3), is another recent economics application of Feige's inequality: it bounds product-experiment persuasion compared to joint persuasion. Its variables and objective are not the Arnosti–Bonet group requests.

On 19 November 2026 we searched the original title, DOI, author pair, both conjecture numbers, the weighted individual lottery, group requests, exponential and compound-Poisson thresholds, and citations around the sharp small-deviation papers. No primary source located explicitly gave the conclusion for all conditional profiles. This is not a priority guarantee. The authors' original problem list, and not the absence of a search result, is the source of the conjecture.
