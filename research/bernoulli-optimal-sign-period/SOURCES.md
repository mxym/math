# Sources and comparison

* Mihir Singhal, [*Erdős–Littlewood–Offord problem with arbitrary probabilities*, Discrete Mathematics 345 (2022), 113005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2022.113005); [author's arXiv manuscript, §4, Conjecture 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.02886v2). Theorem 1.2 reduces arbitrary nonzero real coefficients for a common Bernoulli law to signs. Proposition 3.4 and its proof already give the even-denominator leading slope and the maximizing target's residue offset for large odd sample sizes. Equations (7)–(8) supply the Fourier representation. Our contribution is to resolve the discrete rounding by expanding an actual adjacent-pair difference an order further and excluding midpoint ties arithmetically; this yields the least positive period preserving odd sample sizes. We do not change the conclusions about other denominators or irrational biases.
* J. N. Darroch, [*On the distribution of the number of successes in independent trials*](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703287), Annals of Mathematical Statistics 35 (1964), 1317–1321. The proximity of a Poisson-binomial mode to its mean is used after the signed sum is shifted by the number of negative signs.
* Tomas Juškevičius and Valentas Kurauskas, [*On the Littlewood–Offord problem for arbitrary distributions*](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20977), [authors' manuscript, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08770v2). They analyze the odd Bernoulli case and raise questions about the best sign proportion. Their different §5 Conjecture 1 asks whether arbitrary coefficients can beat all signs for one and the same iid lattice law; our [non-Bernoulli odd-order examples](../odd-iid-littlewood-offord-weights/README.md) concern that question, not the sign count for Bernoulli variables. The [three-summand cap theorem](../three-summand-point-concentration/README.md) varies the underlying laws under atom caps.
* Arnaud Marsiglietti and James Melbourne, [*A note on the Littlewood–Offord problem for discrete log-concave distributions*, arXiv:2510.25869v2, Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25869v2#S3.Thmtheorem1). Their majorization theorem gives a sign comparison for independent finitely supported log-concave integer laws, and their Bernoulli results bound concentration; these do not select the optimal odd sign imbalance in the regime considered here.

On 19 November 2026, title-, author-, theorem-, citation- and topic-based searches for further work on the sign-count conjecture, optimal iid Bernoulli imbalance, even denominators and arithmetic periodicity found no earlier proof of this eventual rounding rule.
