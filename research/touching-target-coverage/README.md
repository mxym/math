# Random coverage where two domains touch

Consider random balls centered at uniform sample points of a smooth Euclidean domain A. How large must their common radius be to cover a target B several times when B touches the boundary of A on a set of zero surface area?

* **An explicit coverage law for regular contact.** For each fixed multiplicity, if B is a smooth positive-volume domain and the surface area of its low contact-height sublevels is regularly varying, the probability of coverage is asymptotically the exponential of minus the sum of two intensities. The first is the known interior intensity. The second is an explicit boundary constant times a Laplace integral of the contact-height profile. At ordinary quadratic tangency, isolated contacts give the interior limit; a contact curve can yield a mixture; higher-dimensional contact manifolds can dominate despite having zero surface area. See [CONTACT.md](CONTACT.md) for the dimensions, multiplicities and constants.
* **Affine centering and scaling need not exist.** For one fixed connected C³ target in dimension three, touching at just one point, the ordinary volume-scale radius at single coverage has two subsequential limits of different affine types: Gumbel and a two-component extreme-value law. No positive affine normalization of the volume-scale radius has a nondegenerate full-sequence limit. A *different* connected C² example, also touching at one point, has first-order coefficients with almost-sure liminf 1 and limsup 7/6 for every fixed multiplicity. See [OBSTRUCTIONS.md](OBSTRUCTIONS.md).

The motivating statement is [Penrose–Yang, Remark 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2.SS2). The positive theorem does **not** give an explicit contact formula for every compact target or general ambient manifold. The examples do **not** rule out nonlinear transformations.
