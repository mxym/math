# Primary precedents, differences, and limits of the search

* [Penrose and Yang, *Random coverage of a manifold with boundary*, v2, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2) is the source of the question. Its [version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19278) lists September 2025 and the July 3, 2026 revision. Theorem 2.1 gives a one-time law at the full boundary normalization; its boundary term uses the actual surface measure of contact. Remark 2.4 points out that this degenerates for zero-area contact when d > 2 or k > 1 and asks for the appropriate transformation in intermediate cases. Proposition 2.11 gives the interior-normalized distribution after excluding the ambient boundary collar. Theorem 2.6 uses a relative-interior closure condition on boundary contact for its strong law. Our counterexamples do not contradict that theorem. We explicitly use the flat boundary case of Theorem 2.1 (including its constant), Proposition 2.11, and the known interior and positive-contact distinction.
* [Penrose, *Random Euclidean coverage from within*, esp. Theorems 3.1–3.2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-022-01182-5), previously determined full-domain distributional limits, including a two-component law. Independence arguments, interior/boundary competition, clumping, and Gumbel distributions are not themselves new. [Janson, *Random coverings in several dimensions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02399201) is a foundational homogeneous random-covering treatment.
* [Penrose and Higgs, *Random coverage from within with variable radii, and Johnson–Mehl cover times*, esp. §4.2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10687-026-00529-8), study a Poisson Boolean model near the boundary and relate a flat floor to a lower-dimensional random-radius covering. Their Remark 2.8 discusses two-component extreme-value distributions. Our baseline uses this established half-space geometry. The second layer here comes from raising the *target* a small distance above the floor: conditional on a rare cell failure, it can cover the entire cluster of insufficient centers, producing the contact-height factor.
* [Higgs, Penrose and Yang, *Covering one point process with another*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03832) study coverage of a second random sample, not an entire deterministic positive-volume touching target; their different competing limits are not the two subsequences of the same fixed target constructed here. [Penrose, *Record times for coverage thresholds and maximal spacings*, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19104v3#S2), asks how frequently coverage radii improve in a nested sequence, not how to normalize this one-time proper-target radius. It explicitly leaves an intermediate target out of the record analysis.
* Searches through 19 November 2026 included the titles, authors, arXiv identifiers and revised original text, then historical and specifically July–November 2026 searches for boundary-touching targets, singular/zero surface contact, coverage of a subregion, tangential and intermediate coverage thresholds, competing one-time extremes, and failure of affine normalization or almost-sure leading scale. No exact parallel regularly varying contact law or either single-point obstruction was located. The Laplace estimate for quadratic minima, covering nets, Poisson couplings and convergence of types are standard ingredients. Search engines and accessible publications cannot prove priority.
