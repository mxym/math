# An interior/contact competition for random multiple coverage

Let $d\ge2$, $m=d-1$, and $k\ge1$ fixed. Let $A$ be a compact smooth Euclidean domain and $B\subset A$ a compact positive-volume smooth domain, both closures of their interiors. Use the ordinary Euclidean distance. Suppose that the nonempty contact set $Z=B\cap\partial A$ has zero surface measure. Near $Z$ use inward normal coordinates $(x,v)$ on $\partial A\times[0,v_0]$. The lower boundary of $B$ is $v=g(x)$, with $B$ on the $v\ge g(x)$ side, $g\ge0$ smooth and $Z=\{g=0\}$. More precisely we use this description on a fixed relatively open neighborhood of $Z$; on a smaller such neighborhood it holds for all sufficiently small $v$, and all points of $B$ sufficiently close to $\partial A$ project there. This follows by the implicit function theorem, since at contact the two outward normals coincide by containment, and by compactness. We can smoothly continue $g$ positively outside the smaller neighborhood without changing its small sublevels. Put
$$V(z)=\mathcal H^m\{x\in\partial A:g(x)\le z\}.$$
Assume $V$ varies regularly at zero with index $\rho$, i.e. $V(cz)/V(z)\to c^\rho$ for every $c>0$. Here $0\le\rho\le m/2$: compactness gives monotonicity of $V$ and, at any minimum, an $O(|x-x_0|^2)$ upper bound for $g$. Write $|\cdot|$ for volume in the relevant dimension and $\omega_j$ for the unit $j$-ball volume.

Sample $n$ iid uniform points in $A$ and let $R_{n,k}$ be the least radius covering all of $B$ at least $k$ times. The following also holds for a Poisson sample of mean $n$. Put $\lambda=n/|A|$, $u=\lambda\omega_d r^d$, $y=u/2$, $t=\omega_m\lambda r^m$, $D=d+k-3+1/d$. With $c_d,c_{d,k}$ as in [Penrose–Yang, (2.3),(2.4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2.SS1), set
$$
 J_I=\frac{c_d}{(k-1)!}|B|\lambda u^{d+k-2}e^{-u},\quad
 \gamma=c_{d,k}(\omega_d/2)^{1/d}(d/m)^D,\quad
 \Theta=\lambda r\,y^{m+k-2}e^{-y},\quad
 J_T=\gamma\Theta\int_{\partial A} e^{-tg(x)}\,dS(x).
$$
On any sequence with $u\to\infty$ and $J_I+J_T\to\tau\in(0,\infty)$,
$$ \mathbb P(R_{n,k}\le r)\longrightarrow e^{-\tau}.\tag{1}$$
The integral in $J_T$ is asymptotic to $\Gamma(1+\rho)V(1/t)$. In particular one can use $-\log(J_I+J_T)$ as a transformation on the increasing large-$u$ branch (with $r=R_{n,k}$): the limit is standard Gumbel. Equivalently take $r_n(x)$ such that the sum is $e^{-x}$ on that branch. Each summand is decreasing for sufficiently large $u$, and the interior term alone diverges at $u=(1/2)\log\lambda$, while both vanish at $u=3\log\lambda$. On any sequence tested in (1), $u\asymp\log\lambda$, $r\to0$, $t\to\infty$: the same bounds follow by monotonicity. These statements do not require a fixed proportion between $J_I$ and $J_T$.

This addresses the intermediate null-surface-contact question of [Penrose–Yang, Remark 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2.SS2) for this regular class of full-dimensional Euclidean targets, not for arbitrary compact targets. Their Theorem 2.1 gives the positive-surface contact law and the half-space coefficient; their Proposition 2.11 supplies the interior contribution. We use these results explicitly.

## A rare cell at a flat floor, then at a small positive height

Take homogeneous Poisson intensity $\lambda$ on the upper half-space. Let $P$ be a target parallelepiped on its floor, of side scale $b_0$ and shape in a fixed compact family of nonsingular linear images of the unit cube. Write $\theta=|P|\Theta$. If $y\to\infty$, $r/b_0\to0$ and $\theta\to0$, then
$$\mathbb P(P\text{ is not }k\text{-covered})\sim\gamma\theta.\tag{2}$$
Here and below uniformity means that the assertion holds on each such sequence. We give the amplification argument because a fixed-domain weak limit alone does not give its small tail.

Tile a dilate $jP$ with $j^m$ copies of $P$, $j=\lceil\theta^{-1/m}\rceil$. First ignore an $O(r)$ strip on both sides of every tile face, taking cores inset by $2r$ in Euclidean distance. Core events are independent. The union of face strips, enlarged by $r/y$, has area at most $Cj^m |P| r/b_0$. Nets on the floor of mesh $r/y$ control failures: a net-point half-ball of radius $r(1-C/y)$ has mean $y-O(1)$, and the probability of fewer than $k$ points there is $O(y^{k-1}e^{-y})$. Therefore failure anywhere in the strips costs $O(j^m|P|(r/b_0)r^{-m}y^{m+k-1}e^{-y})=o(1)$, since $r^{-m}y^{m+k-1}e^{-y}$ is a fixed constant multiple of $\Theta$. The single-tile cost is likewise $O(\theta r/b_0)=o(\theta)$.

To evaluate coverage of $jP$, scale by $1/(jb_0)$; the intensity and radius are $\lambda_*=\lambda(jb_0)^d$ and $r_*=r/(jb_0)$, leaving $y$ unchanged. If the normalized target has volume $v=|P|/b_0^m$, then
$$v\,\lambda_*r_* y^{m+k-2}e^{-y}=j^m\theta\to1,\qquad
 \lambda_*r_*=(2/\omega_d)^{1/d}\lambda_*^{m/d}y^{1/d}.$$
Hence $\lambda_*\to\infty$, $y/\log\lambda_*\to m/d$, and the boundary parameter $y-(m/d)\log\lambda_*-D\log\log\lambda_*$ stays bounded. The fixed normalized target can be placed wholly within a flat relatively open face patch of a bounded smooth domain, with an additional fixed buffer (take a smooth domain coinciding with the half-space in a neighborhood of the patch). Coverage there has exactly the half-space law for all small $r_*$. [Penrose–Yang Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19278v2#S2.SS2) applies to this target in the boundary, of zero $d$-volume and with zero relative-boundary $m$-area. Thus the no-failure limit is $\exp(-\gamma)$, by the two scaling identities. For varying shapes take a convergent subsequence and bracket the limiting parallelepiped by its factors $1\pm\delta$, then let $\delta\downarrow0$ (couple using the same half-space process). The previous seam bound, independent cores and $j^m\theta\to1$ yield (2), first for cores and then whole cells. Using a different fixed fraction of a cell height for scaling or trimming an $O(r)$ boundary strip does not alter (2).

For a cell at a fixed height $h\ge0$ above the floor translate its plane to height zero. This gives an independent baseline process in the half-space above the target and supplement in depths $[-h,0]$ below it. If $0\le th\le M$ for fixed $M$, then
$$
 \mathbb P(P+h e_d\text{ is not }k\text{-covered})
       =(\gamma+o(1))\theta e^{-th}.\tag{3}
$$
The supplement projected to the target plane is a Poisson process of intensity $\lambda h$ with disks of radii between $\sqrt{r^2-h^2}$ and $r$. Here $h/r=O_M(1/y)$. Conditional on baseline failure, with probability tending to one the set of its insufficient centers is contained in a ball of radius $\delta r$ about any chosen one, and all points of the cell already have at least $k-1$ baseline neighbors. For the second statement apply (2) at multiplicity $k-1$ and the same radius: the failure probability is $O(\theta/y)$ if $k\ge2$; for $k=1$ it is automatic. For the first, use a mesh $r/y$ and shrunken balls as above. For two insufficient centers separated by $\delta r$ to $3r$, for fixed $\delta>0$ the corresponding net-point half-balls have union mean at least $(1+c_\delta)y$, since translating a half-ball tangentially by a fixed positive fraction of its radius increases union volume strictly. At most $C y^m$ partner net points lie within $4r$ of each. Both deficient implies their union has at most $2k-2$ points, hence this pair event summed over the near pairs is $O(|P|(y/r)^m y^{m+2k}e^{-(1+c_\delta)y})=o(\theta)$. For separations above $3r$ shrunken balls are disjoint, and the total bound is $O(\theta^2)$. Conditional on baseline failure the claimed conclusions follow from (2). A measurable insufficient center can be chosen, for instance by enumerating a dense subset (strict failure is relatively open).

On this good event, if no projected supplemental point falls within $r$ of the chosen center, it remains insufficient. If one falls within $\sqrt{r^2-h^2}-\delta r$, it covers every deficient center and restores full coverage. The conditional failure probability is bracketed by the two zero-count probabilities. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves (3) uniformly for $th\le M$, including $h=0$. This avoids assuming an independent thinning of individual uncovered continuum points. In (2),(3) comparison intensities and radii $\lambda(1+o(1/y)),r(1+o(1/y))$ change the leading probabilities by factors $1+o(1)$ for bounded $th$.

## Collar reduction and assembly

Poissonize first. The following elementary estimates are used uniformly on the sequences of (1). Write $F(z)=V(z)$ on small $z$. Regular variation gives
$$ F(L/t)\le C L^{\rho+1}F(1/t)\quad (L\ge1,\ L/t\text{ sufficiently small}).\tag{4}$$
Indeed $F(2z)/F(z)\le 2^{\rho+1}$ eventually and dyadic iteration suffices. For larger $L$ the exponential weight below absorbs the finite total area; $F(1/t)$ is bounded below by a constant times $t^{-m/2}$. Integration by parts, or layer cake followed by dominated convergence using (4), gives $\int e^{-tg}dS\sim\Gamma(1+\rho)F(1/t)$. In particular $\Theta F(1/t)=O(1)$, and for fixed $c>0$ and $M\ge1$,
$$\int_{g>M/t}e^{-ctg}\,dS \le C_c e^{-cM/2}F(1/t)$$
for all large $t$ (enlarge the constant for a polynomial factor, or replace $c/2$ by $c/3$). Also $\Theta F(Cr)=O((1+y)^{\rho+1})$, since $tr\asymp y$.

For centers of $B$ at depth $\ge3r$, extend the Poisson process to all Euclidean space, which does not change these events. In full space the omitted centers of $B$ at depths $\le3r$ can be ignored: a mesh of size $r/u$ in the ambient strip has $O(r(u/r)^d)$ points and shrunken full balls there have failure probability $O(u^{k-1}e^{-u})$. Their product is $O(r\lambda u^{d+k-2}e^{-u})=o(1)$. By the interior theorem (PY Prop. 2.11 applied with target $B$ inside a larger smooth body), the deep-target coverage probability is $\exp(-J_I)+o(1)$, allowing $J_I\to0$ by monotonicity; take subsequences for varying $J_I$. The deep event inspects only original points at depth $\ge2r$.

Fix $1/2<\alpha<1$, let $H=r y^{-\alpha}$. Centers of $B$ with $H\le v\le3r$ fail with probability $o(1)$: a mesh $r/u$ there has at most $C F(Cr)r(u/r)^d$ points, by packing in the widened collar whose projections satisfy $g\le Cr$. The latter follows by Lipschitzness on a fixed tube; here the mesh is $o(r)$. Uniform smooth half-space comparison for the shrunken balls gives mean at least $y+c y^{1-\alpha}-O(1)$, since the disk mass gains at least a constant times $u\min(v/r,1)$ over a half-ball and curvature costs $O(ur)$. Thus the union bound is at most $C\Theta F(Cr)y^{C_k}e^{-c y^{1-\alpha}}=o(1)$.

On $0\le v\le H$ it suffices to test the lower graph. Indeed in normal-coordinate charts frozen in a tangential cell of side $\ell=r^{3/4}$, the tangent metric is Euclidean after a nonsingular linear change, the normal coordinate orthogonal with unit length. On the $O(\ell+r)$ neighborhood the metric, volume density and physical chord lengths of close pairs have relative errors $O(\ell+r)$. This follows from bounded second derivatives of the smooth normal chart, a uniform bound on its inverse differential, and Taylor's formula; distinct nonlocal points cannot approach within $O(r)$ by compact embeddedness. In frozen coordinates Euclidean squared distances from a sample $(x',w)$ with $w\ge0$ to $(x,v)$ and $(x,g(x))$ differ by $(v-g)(v+g-2w)\le H^2$ when $g\le v\le H$. Thus if the graph is covered at $r(1-\eta_n)$ the thin columns are covered at $r$, while coverage of the columns implies coverage of their graph at $r$. Here $\eta_n=C(\ell+r+(H/r)^2)=o(1/y)$, uniformly. We may apply this column argument locally without requiring any independence between different heights. The shallow event only inspects original points of depth $\le H+2r(1+o(1))$; for tests at radii $r(1+o(1))$ in fact only $H+r(1+o(1))<2r$ is needed. Thus the remaining thin-column event is independent of the deep event.

The graph with $M/t< g\le H$ has failure probability uniformly bounded by $C e^{-c'M}+o(1)$, including the small radius reduction just described. Cover its parameter domain by a mesh of size $r/u$; use a maximal separated net within the set so that counts can be estimated by disjoint smaller balls on $\partial A$. Distances on the graph are Lipschitz comparable to the parameter distance. At a net center the shrunken ball has mean $\ge y+c tg-O(1)$ by the half-plane volume bound and $g\le H$. Within each small packing ball the value of $tg$ changes by at most a constant since $t r/u=O(1)$ and $g$ is Lipschitz. Summing the Poisson low-count bound gives at most
$$ C (u/r)^m y^{k-1}e^{-y}\int_{g>(M-C)/t}e^{-ctg}\,dS
       \le C' e^{-c'M}\Theta F(1/t).$$
All constants may depend on the fixed bodies and $d,k$. Replacing the chart estimates by one of the finitely many original charts causes no change to this net bound.

It remains to assemble the cells on $g\le M/t$ for fixed $M>0$. Use finitely many boundary charts and grid cubes of side $\ell$. We describe the seam choices to allow a general (possibly irregular) zero set. Each initial chart working box can have its faces independently moved within a fixed small interval while still lying in an extended chart; choose the boxes so their innermost choices cover $\partial A$. Give overlaps to the first box in a fixed ordering; keep grid cells wholly within an assigned piece and discard cells meeting any moved original chart face. Randomize working faces uniformly in their intervals and each grid offset uniformly modulo $\ell$. A given surface point has probability $O(\ell)$ to be within $C\ell$ of a moved chart face: this follows in its chart from bounded inverse metric and the coordinate-wise density of each face. Likewise the probability of being within $Cr$ of any grid face is $O(r/\ell)$, using the finite number of charts. The union of these regions with enlarged widths also contains the $r/u$-neighborhoods of all discarded regions and grid seams. Hence one may choose the faces and offsets (depending deterministically on $n,M$) so that their enlarged bad set inside $\{g\le(M+1)/t\}$ has area $O((\ell+r/\ell)F((M+1)/t))$, by integrating probabilities. The area of the global domain is not used in place of this weighted sublevel estimate. A width $2r$ or any fixed larger multiple of $r$ may be discarded from each remaining cell for independent cores.

For use in sublevel comparisons, $|\nabla g|\le C\sqrt g$ near its zeros: use nonnegativity and Taylor upper bounds along the negative gradient direction. Thus in an $O(\ell)$-neighbor of $g\le M/t$, variation of $g$ is at most $C_M(\ell/\sqrt t+\ell^2)=o(1/t)$, since $\ell\sqrt t\to0$. The $r/u$ packing enlargement of the bad set of graph centers $g\le M/t$ therefore lies inside $g\le(M+1)/t$. Its failure cost is $o(1)$ by the half-ball net bound $C\Theta$ times the enlarged bad area. Discarding these centers also removes only $o(1)$ from the intended intensity sum. Retain whole good cells whose central value $h_i=g(x_i)$ is $\le M/t$. This classification differs from the desired sublevel only in a region where $tg=M+o(1)$ (and the bad set); its failure cost is $o(1)$ by the same net bound. Indeed after a packing enlargement the relative sublevel shell has area $o(F(1/t))$, by bracketing it by $F((M+\epsilon)/t)-F((M-\epsilon)/t)$ and then letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$. This works also for $\rho=0$.

In each such cell, section the graph at height $h_i$ in the frozen orthonormal tangent/normal chart. The center change in height from the actual graph is $o(1/t)=o(r/u)$ uniformly, and the change of intensity in these coordinates has relative size $O(\ell+r)$. All neighborhoods of core centers lie inside the enlarged normal chart. Coupling by two flat homogeneous Poisson intensities and two comparison radii, and (3), gives core failure probability
$$ (\gamma+o(1))\Theta |P_i|\,e^{-t h_i},$$
where $|P_i|$ is the frozen surface area of the cell (relative error $o(1)$). Indeed $\max_i |P_i|\Theta\to0$: the bound $F(1/t)\gtrsim t^{-m/2}$ and $\Theta F(1/t)=O(1)$ give $O((\ell\sqrt t)^m)=o(1)$. Core trimming costs $O(\Theta |P_i|r/\ell)$ per flat cell by the baseline net bound. We can inset the true chart cells by a fixed sufficiently large multiple of $r$ so all neighborhoods of distinct cores are disjoint in physical space; path comparability and the $1+O(\ell)$ local charts justify this for neighbors, while the original chart boundaries have already been discarded with a larger buffer. Their Poisson tests are therefore independent. The sum of these probabilities is
$$ \gamma\Theta\int_{g\le M/t} e^{-t g}\,dS+o(1),$$
by uniform $o(1)$ variation of $t g$, the negligible seams and shell, and the bounded total $\Theta F(M/t)$. Their maximum tends to zero. Taking products, including the independent deep law, controlling the previously excluded regions, and finally $M\to\infty$ proves (1) for Poisson input. The same comparison bounds apply at the same deterministic $r$ for intensities $\lambda(1\pm n^{-1/3})$: $u$ changes by $o(1)$, $t$ by $1+o(1)$, and the contact integral by $1+o(1)$ by regular variation. Poisson-count concentration and monotonicity give (1) for the fixed sample.

## Consequences for ordinary quadratic contact

Suppose $Z$ is a smooth compact $p$-dimensional contact manifold, $0\le p\le m-1$, and the Hessian $Q$ of $g$ in the $q=m-p$ normal directions of $Z$ inside $\partial A$ is positive definite. Then the contact integral is asymptotic to
$$ (2\pi/t)^{q/2}\int_Z(\det Q)^{-1/2}\,d\mathcal H^p .$$
This is the usual quadratic Laplace formula: in normal coordinates $g(z,w)=w^TQ(z)w/2+o(|w|^2)$ uniformly; rescale $w$ by $t^{-1/2}$ and dominate by a Gaussian on the compact tube. In particular $V$ has index $\rho=q/2$. The full boundary-side intensity scales as a positive constant times
$$\lambda^{(m-\rho)/d}u^{\,m+k-2+1/d-m\rho/d}e^{-u/2}.$$
For $p=0$ the interior wins; for $p\ge2$ the contact region wins despite its zero total surface area. If $p=1$, the leading scale is $u\sim\log\lambda$ and the contact log-log coefficient is $d+2k-3$, versus the interior's $d+k-2$. Thus the intensities mix for $k=1$, while for $k\ge2$ the contact region wins. All constants and the mixed CDF in (1) use Euclidean volume and surface area; no universal threshold shape is asserted beyond the stated hypotheses. In dimension two a proper null-measure clean contact manifold can only have $p=0$.
