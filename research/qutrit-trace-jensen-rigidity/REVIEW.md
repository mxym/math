**Negative type.** For zero-mass contrasts only the midpoint kernel matters with sign opposite the Jensen gap. The six-degree polynomial from the two repeated eigenvalues has the stated opposed derivatives: the pure x derivative is 1440; the other combination gives 1440−3840+2304=−96. The scalar eigenvalue is killed because each derivative test contains an x derivative. Continuity suffices: averaging the family of kernels at base values a+r preserves conditional positivity, smooths the two repeated eigenvalues by the same translation, and smooths the complement eigenvalue by a separate scale that is still killed. On compact subintervals f_delta is therefore a polynomial of degree at most 5 and converges uniformly, forcing the same condition on f. Different intervals agree on overlap.

The diagonal line gives a polynomial with leading coefficient nonzero when the degree m is at least 3. The two derivatives of positive orders 1 and m−1 yield a PSD 2-by-2 matrix with a zero diagonal (order 2m−2>m) but a nonzero off-diagonal (order m), impossible. Thus the local polynomial is quadratic at most. To extend beyond 1/2, the second difference contrast based at x in (0,b) has zero norm against its local polynomial midpoint kernel: second differences on both factors kill all quadratic terms. Hence the global difference from the polynomial obeys the local midpoint relation around every point in (0,(1+b)/2), and is affine there by continuity, vanishing on the previous interval. Iterating covers (0,1); subtracting the known smaller repeated eigenvalues recovers f itself. The two-point contrast imposes nonnegative quadratic coefficient; the converse is the exact squared Hilbert–Schmidt identity. Padding by zeros and full-rank approximation extend the implication to larger dimension.
