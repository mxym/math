# Rigidity of negative-type trace Jensen gaps on qutrit density matrices

Let f:[0,1]→ℝ be continuous. For density matrices ρ,σ put
$$J_f(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr} f(\rho)+\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr} f(\sigma)-\operatorname{Tr} f((\rho+\sigma)/2).$$
Then the following are equivalent:

1. For all finite families of **positive definite real symmetric 3×3** density matrices, and real coefficients with ∑cᵢ=0, ∑ᵢⱼ cᵢcⱼ J_f(ρᵢ,ρⱼ)≤0.
2. f(x)=a x²+b x+c on [0,1], with a≥0.

Consequently the same characterization holds on the full complex qutrit state space, and on the full state space in each fixed dimension d≥3. In (1) only qutrits having a real 2×2 block and a scalar block are actually used. For a>0 the square root of J_f is the Hilbert–Schmidt distance multiplied by √a/2; a=0 gives the identically zero pseudometric. This is a classification of the *trace* Jensen construction, not of Jensen gaps for arbitrary spectral functionals (e.g. log Tr ρ^q).

## 1. Signed convolution tests on a qutrit pencil

Set K_f(ρ,σ)=Tr f((ρ+σ)/2). The endpoint terms drop out of any zero-sum contrast, so (1) says K_f is conditionally positive semidefinite: its quadratic form on every signed finite sum of point masses of total mass zero is nonnegative. The associated bilinear form obeys Cauchy–Schwarz on this vector space (apply positivity to μ+tν). In particular a zero-norm contrast is orthogonal to every zero-mass contrast.

For a∈(0,1/2), introduce
$$R_a(s,x,z)=\begin{pmatrix}a+s+z&x&0\\x&a+s-z&0\\0&0&1-2a-2s\end{pmatrix}.$$
These are trace one and strictly positive on a neighborhood of (s,x,z)=0. Write v=(s,x,z). The midpoint of two such matrices equals R_a((v+w)/2). Thus φ_a(v)=Tr f(R_a(v)) yields a conditionally positive kernel φ_a((v+w)/2).

First suppose f is smooth on the involved spectral intervals. If L is a homogeneous constant-coefficient differential operator of positive order k and φ_a is smooth up to order 2k at zero, then
$$2^{-2k} L^2\phi_a(0)\ge0.$$
Indeed replace each derivative by its forward difference with common step h (including sums and products of difference operators). Each nonconstant monomial gives a signed combination of point masses of mass zero. Applying the mixed stencil separately to the two entries of the kernel gives a nonnegative number. After division by h^{2k}, its limit is the displayed expression, since each differentiation of the midpoint produces a factor 1/2. More generally two derivative tests of positive orders r,s have a positive semidefinite 2×2 Gram matrix with mixed entry 2^{−r−s}L_rL_s φ_a(0). This follows by separately normalizing the two stencils and passing to the limit of finite positive semidefinite matrices. These facts only require the corresponding Taylor expansions at zero when used there.

Use k=3 and the two individual tests
$$L_+=\partial_x^3,\qquad L_-=\partial_x(\partial_s^2-\partial_x^2-\partial_z^2).$$
Both kill every function of s alone, so the third eigenvalue of R_a makes no contribution. The other eigenvalues are a+s±√(x²+z²). Their summed Taylor expansion through degree six exists even at x=z=0: the odd powers of the square root cancel. The degree-six part is f^{(6)}(a)P/6!, where for u=x²+z²,
$$P=(s+\sqrt u)^6+(s-\sqrt u)^6=2(s^6+15s^4u+15s^2u^2+u^3).$$
Direct differentiation gives
$$L_+^2P=1440,\qquad L_-^2P=-96.$$
For the latter, applying ∂x²(∂s²−Δ)², with Δ=∂x²+∂z², to the displayed polynomial gives respectively 1440, −3840 and 2304 from its three terms ∂x²∂s⁴, −2∂x²∂s²Δ and ∂x²Δ². Consequently the two required signs force f^{(6)}(a)=0.

## 2. Removing regularity and identifying the local polynomial

Fix a compact interval I⊂(0,1/2) of positive length and choose δ>0 smaller than its distance from the endpoints of (0,1/2). Let η_δ be a smooth nonnegative probability density supported on (−δ,δ). For a∈I average the kernels from R_{a+r} against η_δ(r)dr. All are defined on a common neighborhood of v=0, by compactness, and their positive quadratic-form inequalities persist under averaging. The eigenvalue function after smoothing is
$$f_\delta(a+s+\sqrt u)+f_\delta(a+s-\sqrt u)+g_\delta(1-2a-2s),$$
where f_δ(y)=∫f(y+r)η_δ(r)dr and g_δ(y)=∫f(y−2r)η_δ(r)dr are smooth locally on the spectral ranges. The two derivatives above annihilate the last term, regardless of its different smoothing scale. Therefore f_δ^{(6)}=0 throughout I for every sufficiently small δ. With the usual shrinking mollifier family f_δ→f uniformly on I by continuity. Polynomials of degree at most five form a closed finite-dimensional subspace of C(I), so f is a polynomial of degree at most five on I. Taking nested intervals exhausting (0,1/2) and using uniqueness of a polynomial on an interval, f agrees with a single such polynomial on (0,1/2).

Restrict next to the diagonal trace-one line
$$A(t)=\operatorname{diag}(t,(1-t)/2,(1-t)/2),\qquad
\psi(t)=f(t)+2f((1-t)/2),\quad 0<t<1.$$
Its midpoint kernel ψ((t+u)/2) is conditionally positive. On (0,1/2) it is generated by a polynomial. If f there has degree m≥3, then ψ has exactly degree m on that interval: the leading coefficient factor is 1+2(−1/2)^m≠0. At any interior point use the pair of derivative tests of orders 1 and m−1 on the scalar line. The Gram entry of order 2(m−1) is zero since 2(m−1)>m, while the off-diagonal entry is the nonzero mth derivative times 2^{-m}. This contradicts positivity. Thus f equals a polynomial Q of degree at most two on (0,1/2).

## 3. Propagation to the entire spectral range

No analytic-continuation hypothesis is necessary. On (0,1/2), ψ agrees with the known polynomial R(t)=Q(t)+2Q((1−t)/2). Suppose ψ=R on (0,b), initially b=1/2. For x∈(0,b) and sufficiently small h>0, consider μ=δ_{x+h}−2δ_x+δ_{x−h}. It has zero mass and first moment. Its squared norm against the midpoint kernel ψ is zero: all involved midpoint arguments lie in (0,b), and a quadratic polynomial in the midpoint has zero double contrast of this form. By Cauchy–Schwarz its contrast against δ_y−δ_z is zero for any y,z∈(0,1). Taking z in (0,b) to evaluate the known value gives, writing t=(x+y)/2,
$$(\psi-R)(t+h/2)-2(\psi-R)(t)+(\psi-R)(t-h/2)=0.$$
Here the second difference of R is constant in its center. For each x∈(0,b) this holds throughout t∈(x/2,(1+x)/2) and all sufficiently small h (depending on x but not t). The union of these intervals is (0,(1+b)/2). A continuous function with this local midpoint identity is locally affine: on a sufficiently short interval apply the midpoint identity to any two points and use dyadic interpolation. Since the union is connected, these affine functions agree where they overlap, so ψ−R is affine on that union and is zero on its nonempty open subinterval (0,b). It is therefore zero throughout (0,(1+b)/2). Iterating b↦(1+b)/2 gives ψ=R on (0,1).

For every such t, (1−t)/2 belongs to (0,1/2), where f=Q already. Subtracting that term yields f(t)=Q(t); continuity includes 0 and 1. If its quadratic coefficient is a, direct expansion gives
$$J_f(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac a4\operatorname{Tr}(\rho-\sigma)^2.$$
The two-point zero-sum contrast forces a≥0, and a≥0 is sufficient in every dimension. For d>3, zero-padding embeds the qutrit space isometrically for the Jensen gap, since the extra f(0) terms cancel. Even if only full-rank d-states were assumed, approximate the padded states by mixing with the full-rank maximally mixed state and use continuity. This completes the characterization.
