# Independent adversarial review: extremal trace-scaled Laplacian entropy

**Verdict:** PASS as an internal mathematical review of the three stated finite, simple, connected-graph conclusions in the reviewed manuscript. The updated §4 attribution to Sakai–Iwata was also checked. The accompanying public PROOF.md reproduces that mathematical text with presentation and review-status edits. This review concerns Shannon and finite real orders strictly greater than one only. It is neither external peer review nor a guarantee of priority.

## Steps checked without assuming the candidate

1. For a common trace c, define P(t)=prod(t+x_i). The entropy difference equals the integral of log(P/Q) because evaluating the primitive at R leaves, after canceling the list length and sum, O(1/R). Even for unequal numbers of zero eigenvalues the lower endpoint logs are locally integrable. A single strict positive-coefficient difference makes the integrand pointwise positive on t>0 if all other coefficient differences are nonnegative. The trace c>0 is fixed **within** each compression or tree comparison; scaling by 1/c simply divides the unnormalized entropy difference by c. This does not compare different edge counts: that is the job of inequality (5). [Csikvári Thm 8.1](https://real.mtak.hu/14004/1/application_Kelmans.pdf) has exactly the necessary direction for absolute coefficients and allows arbitrary vertex pairs. No assumption that every move strictly decreases entropy is needed.
2. To keep connectivity, compress onto u from an adjacent v with a distance-two target, retaining uv. Each lost edge vw is replaced by the path v-u-w; connectivity is preserved. After u is universal, only off-u pairs are compressed; u is a common neighbor. The degree-square increment on an incomparable pair is exactly 2pq, positive regardless of the chosen direction, giving termination. In the induced comparable graph with no isolates, a maximum-degree v must be universal: if w misses v, its neighbor z is distinct from v; N(z) outside the pair is contained in N(v) outside the pair by degree and comparability, contradicting w's nonadjacency. Restriction preserves comparability. Removing isolates/universals gives precisely a birth sequence. This accounts for connected, not arbitrary, threshold endpoints.
3. For a dominating birth at j, its added star matrix annihilates every contrast constant on the older j+1 coordinates, acts as identity on contrasts of shorter prefix, and as j+1 on the centered contrast of prefix length j+1. These nonzero mutually orthogonal contrasts plus constants span the full space. Therefore after the final universal vertex the claimed spectrum contains exactly one zero. In the auxiliary graph the loop at j contributes two to its degree, old incident edges contribute j-1, and later births each add 1 to the older index. These are exactly the nontrivial contrast powers, with r=max J, total degree 2s. An ordered loop endpoint carries probability 1/s, not 1/(2s); this gives the correct correction +k log 2 inside sum mu log mu. Concavity follows from h''(u)=-1/[u(u+1)] and the continuous value at zero. Replacing r by min(s,N) enlarges the upper bound since the derivative of R h(2s/R) is positive.
4. Recomputing the normalized entropy difference from scratch gives (5): subtract the star's (N+1)log(N+1)/(2N) from the cone value and factor 2(N+s). After inserting (4), the surplus divided by s is precisely D(x)+delta_N−(k/s)log 2. At x=1 the two cases agree. The derivative signs give min D=log(32/27); the sparse-case value is 17 log 2−10 log 3 > log 2. When N<3s, delta_N > (1+log N)/N, hence s delta_N > (1+log(3s))/3. k≥8 gives s/k≥9/2. For 1≤k≤7, it suffices that 3s e (32/27)^(3s)>2^(3k); e>2 and s≥k(k+1)/2 give exactly the seven finite tests, including the tight first three where a floor of one is not by itself enough. Each integer numerator strictly exceeds its denominator. N=1 implies s=0; s=1 and n=3 are covered without a missing endpoint. Every m>N is strictly higher than the star, irrespective of equality in any earlier step. Equality is up to graph isomorphism.
5. For trees, Zhou–Gutman and [Mohar](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2006.12.005) compare **all** coefficients between star and path. tr(L²)=sum d_i²+2N, so e2=(4N²−2N−sum d_i²)/2. Positive integer degree lists with that trace have unique squared-sum minimizer up to permutation (degrees 1 and 2, precisely two 1s), and unique maximizer (one N, other 1s), forcing respectively the path and the star. At n=2,3 the star and path coincide, which is consistent. This proves entropy strictness for all nonisomorphic trees without any assertion of strictness in a Kelmans step.
6. For a nonstar the largest normalized eigenvalue is below the unique largest star value: if m>N use lambda_max≤n; if m=N, equality would make the complement disconnected, hence the tree contains a complete bipartite cut. Both sides cannot have size ≥2 since that creates a cycle; singleton side forces the star. Comparing positive spectra alone gives F(0)=F(1)=0 and F'(1)=S(star)−S(G)<0. Since the only possible negative coefficient bases are a,b and the greatest base has a strictly negative coefficient, the ordered distinct-base list has at most 3 sign changes including ties; the positive multiplicities do not change that count. The supplied Rolle induction counts repeated zeros correctly, and F is negative eventually. To have a zero beyond 1 the two negative endpoint signs require at least two further roots counted with multiplicity, contradicting 3. Dividing log power sums by 1−z reverses the inequality as required.
7. Independent existing route: use [Sakai–Iwata, Thm 1, Eq. 296](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.07678) or [Thm 2, Eq. 253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.00019). Over the **N**-coordinate positive-support simplex, a one-large-atom vector v(t) minimizes H_z at fixed Shannon entropy for z>1. Along v(t), both entropies strictly increase from 0 to their common uniform endpoint as t increases. Therefore a strict Shannon advantage implies strict H_z advantage relative to v(1/(2N)), including the uniform endpoint. Taking the fixed order as 2 in the second paper instead yields the previously implicit z>2 case from Dairyko et al. Thm 3.8. The graph contribution is Shannon and thereby the interval (1,2), not discovery of a new entropy-interpolation theorem. No positive-order claim below 1 is included here.

## Independent validation

`python3 -BO independent.py` uses the standard library only, no floating point or optimizable Python assertions. It constructs raw labeled-graph Laplacians, compares exact Faddeev–LeVerrier coefficients for every nonidentity Kelmans move starting at connected graphs on 2–5 vertices, and verifies connected endpoint reduction and tree equality. It separately builds the threshold graph from edge pairs, constructs the endpoint multiset by explicitly enumerating both formal orientations, and checks the actual Laplacian-eigenvector equations using that multiset for every birth word through order 12. It then compares the entropy inequality as integer powers after clearing denominators, as well as three Rényi orders. All passed (see CHECKS.txt). The finite checks are supporting checks, not a stand-in for the general argument in steps 1–7.

Proposition 3.5 and Theorem 3.8 already treat order two. [Lin–Zhou](https://cam.tju.edu.cn/research/downAchiev.php?id=326), §4, compare via graph parameters especially conjugate degrees, with equality for threshold graphs; their lower bound does not establish the all-connected star claim (for a path its conjugate degrees have only two nonzero entries). [Star-relevant graph calculations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05491) study special star augmentations, not an arbitrary connected graph. Degree entropy and a normalized symmetric Laplacian are different problems.
