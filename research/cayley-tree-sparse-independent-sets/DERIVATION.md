# Independent derivation of the two critical scales for Cayley-tree coefficients

This is an independent derivation accompanying the main proof. Let P be uniform on labeled trees, X=i_k(T), L=X/E_P X; Q the L-size-biased tree law (equivalently preselect a uniform k-set then condition tree on its independence). Let m=n−k≥1. Either ∑binom(deg,2) or ∑binom(deg−1,2) can measure collisions after centering: their difference is the deterministic n−2.

**Coefficient vs. path.** Exact Bedrosian gives E X=binom(n,k)(1−k/n)^{k−1}. For positive path denominator B=binom(n−k+1,k), direct cancellation gives E X/B = ∏_{j=0}^{k−2}[1+kj/{n(n−k−j)}]. For k→∞, k=o(n), the relative error from replacing the denominator by n² is O(k/n) and the largest summand is O(k²/n²)=o(1). Thus log(E X/B) ∼ k³/(2n²). For k=o(n^{2/3}) the ratio X/B tends to 1 in L¹ by Wingard and the exact product, including bounded k. At k∼t n^{2/3} the ratio tends to exp(t³/2) in L² by the normalized concentration below. For k≫n^{2/3} below half, the one-claw term of the reviewed macroscopic comparison suffices for divergence in probability: on the good event N=Ω(n) the relative bonus is N (k)_3/[(n−k+1)(n−2k+2)(n−2k+3)]. This use of the path bound is distinct from the larger n^{5/6} scale at which relative-to-annealed concentration fails.

**Two replicas.** For fixed k-sets S,T with overlap r, write s=k−r and c=n−2k+r. Permitted graph = join K_c with the disjoint union of r isolated vertices and K_{s,s}. For c≥1,s≥1, its nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues are n (c times), c (r times), m (2s−2 times), n−r (once): the s,s part's internal eigenvalues are shifted by c; component indicators supply the c-eigenspace; the outer clique and global contrasting vectors account for n. Dividing the Kirchhoff product by n^{n−1} gives the pair probability. For s=0 the events coincide and the same closed formula holds. At c=0,r>0 and s>0 the graph disconnects; c=r=0 gives K_{k,k}, the same formula by direct bipartite eigenvalues. Consequently
  E_P L² = E g(H),
  g(r)=(1−r/n)[1+(r−θ)/(n(1−k/n)²)]^r,
where H is the intersection size of two uniformly sampled independent k-subsets of [n], θ=k²/n. The second moment method itself and its computability by Matrix–Tree are *explicitly noted* in Basit–Galvin (EJC 2021, pp.3–4 in author PDF); don't claim the idea is new. The phenomenon below is about the growing k regimes, not a contradiction to their warning about large variance for typical linear k.

**Hypergeometric asymptotics and tails.** For k/n→0 and θ→∞, Z=(H−θ)/sqrt(θ) tends to standard normal by the hypergeometric CLT (variance=θ(1−k/n)² n/(n−1)). Write λ_n=θ^{3/2}/n. If λ_n→λ<∞, expansion on |Z|≤K and then K→∞ gives log g(H)=λ_n Z+o_P(1). The prefactor tends to one since H≤k=o(n). Check: multiplying the quadratic log remainder by H gives O_P(θ²/n²); the extra linear term (H−θ)²/n=O_P(θ/n) also vanishes. For bounded θ, H is tight and g(H)→1 in probability. For integrability when k=O(n^{5/6}), B=max(4eθ,log n); on h>B the factorial-moment bound P(H=h)≤(eθ/h)^h≤4^{-h}, whereas g(h)^2≤exp(O(hk/n))=exp(o(h)). On h≤B, g(h)^2≤exp(t(H−θ)_+), t=2B/[n(1−k/n)²]. Using e^{tx_+}≤1+e^{tx} and the elementary centered mgf bound E exp(t(H−θ)) ≤ exp(θ(e^t−1−t)) for t≥0 gives sup E g(H)^2<∞ (t→0 and θ t²=O(1)). The mgf follows by expanding (1+(e^t−1))^H since E binom(H,j)=binom(k,j)(k)_j/(n)_j≤θ^j/j!. This handles θ bounded or oscillating by subsequences. Thus E_P L²→1 for k=o(n^{5/6}), and →exp(s^6/2) for k∼s n^{5/6}. When k≫n^{5/6} but k=o(n) the event Z∈[1,2] separately gives E g(H)→∞; no such divergence alone proves failure of concentration in probability.

**Exact planted degree law.** For fixed planted S, C=[n] outside S. Direct degree formula for the complete split graph K_m ∨ kK₁:
  Σ_T ∏_i x_i^{deg_T(i)−1} = (Σ_{i∈C}x_i)^{k−1}(Σ_i x_i)^{m−1}.
A published exact source is Martin–Reiner, Theorem 4 in [their paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0302213) at y_i=x_i: the conjugate of the sorted split-graph degree partition has first m column lengths n and remaining n−1−m column lengths m, while the product runs from column 2 to n−1. A self-contained alternative: fix root v∈C, direct edges toward v with edge weight x_{parent}; each tree then contributes x_v times its excess-degree monomial. Write U=Σ_C x and V=Σ_i x. The directed Matrix–Tree minor in order S,C\{v} has blocks UI, −1 x_{C\{v}}^t; −1 x_S^t, VI−1 x_{C\{v}}^t. Schur complementation and rank-one determinant give U^k V^{m−1}(x_v/U), including m=1 by the vacant-block case. Divide x_v. Thus under Q the multiset of n−2 Prüfer digits as a *count vector* agrees with independent b=k−1 uniform-complement and u=m−1 unrestricted draws. This is not a claim that the specific classical Prüfer code has its first b digits restricted.

**Collision under the null.** W=Σ_i binom(deg(i)−1,2) = number of equal unordered draw pairs. With independent uniform draws Y_j, N=n−2, W_j collisions up to j, let D_j=W_j−W_{j−1}−(j−1)/n. These are martingale differences. Distinct pair indicators are uncorrelated, including pairs sharing a digit by uniformity, so Var(W_j)=binom(j,2)(1−1/n)/n. Predictable Var D_j=(j−1+2W_{j−1})/n−((j−1)/n)^2. Its sum has mean Var W∼n/2 and differs from this mean by O(sqrt(n)) in L² by Minkowski with the preceding exact variance. Given the new digit alone, its previous matches have Bin(j−1,1/n) law; the centered increments have uniformly bounded fourth moments. Thus the sum of normalized fourth moments tends to zero (implying conditional Lindeberg in probability), the conditional variance sum normalizes to one, and the martingale array CLT yields (W−E_PW)/sqrt(n/2) ⇒ N(0,1).

**Planted shift and an independent CLT proof.** Couple Q's artificial independent draws X_j to uniform Y_j by changing only restricted draws whose Y_j lands in S; resample an independent uniform complement label. Distinct pairs (X_j,Y_j) are independent over j. Let A_ij=1_{X_i=X_j}−1_{Y_i=Y_j}. Nonzero requires at least one recolored endpoint. For k=o(n), a union bound on which endpoint was recolored and on X- or Y-collision bounds E A_ij²≤2(k/n)(1/m+1/n)=O(k/n²). Only O(kn) pairs meet a restricted draw. For overlapping covariance use conditional expectations at a shared pair (X_i,Y_i): projection is zero if j is unrestricted, and otherwise equals 1_{X_i∈C}/m−1/n. This is constant at restricted i. At unrestricted i its variance is k/(n²m), and it can be shared only by two restricted partners. Disjoint pairs are independent. Hence Var(W_Q−W_P)=O(k²/n+k³/n²)=o(n) for k=o(n). The expectation shift is precisely Δ=binom(b,2)(1/m−1/n). It follows that under Q at k∼s n^{5/6}, the P-centered statistic (W−E_PW)/sqrt(n/2) ⇒ N(s³/sqrt(2),1). For dense k, the following *exact* Q variance is simpler (same conditional projection):
  Var_Q W = [binom(u,2)+ub](n−1)/n² + binom(b,2)(m−1)/m² + u b(b−1) k/(n²m).
Only an unrestricted shared draw and two restricted other draws contribute nondiagonal covariance. In particular Var_Q W=O(n+k²/m) even at m=1 (there W is deterministic). These exact first and second moments refer to W conditioned on each fixed S; mixing over S preserves them by relabeling invariance.

**Readout of the three regimes.** E_P L²→1 is exactly L² convergence to one. At critical scaling set λ=s³/sqrt(2), Z_n=(W−E_PW)/sqrt(n/2), G(z)=exp(λz−λ²/2), G_B=min(B,G). The limit of E_P[L−G_B(Z_n)]² equals E[(G(Z)−G_B(Z))²] for Z standard Gaussian: the cross term is E_Q G_B(Z_n), whose limit is the tilted Gaussian expectation; the square norm of the limit G is exactly the critical second moment. As B→∞ this goes to zero. Thus (Z_n,L) converges jointly to (Z,G(Z)), with lognormal log parameters (−s⁶/4,s⁶/2). Using a bounded truncate is essential: positive exponential moments of Z_n need not themselves converge because rare stars have W of order n². This normal-shift likelihood principle is standard; see Banerjee–Ma [Prop. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00567) for a general Gaussian second-moment conditioning method. Identifying the single Cayley-degree statistic and the exact scale are the model-specific inputs. For k≫n^{5/6}, k≤n−1, the above shift satisfies Δ²/n→∞ and Δ²/(k²/m)→∞ since m≤n. Chebyshev separates P and Q using W halfway between their means. On the null-typical side E_P[L 1_{side}]=Q(side)→0, while its complement is P-negligible. Hence L→0 **in P-probability** though E_P L=1; convergence to zero in L¹ would be false since E_P L=1.

**Scope and finite checking.** `independent.py` enumerates all 18,248 labeled trees of orders 2 through 7 via Prüfer sequences. Direct independent-set masks verify 55 feasible fixed-pair overlaps, annealed/path ratios, total second moments, planted W means and variances, and all 1,965 nonzero monomial coefficients of the split-graph degree polynomials across 21 sizes.
