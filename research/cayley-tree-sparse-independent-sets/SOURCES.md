# Sources and scope

**Same random tree, different conclusions.** [Basit and Galvin, *On the independent set sequence of a tree*](https://doi.org/10.37236/9896) ([author PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~dgalvin1/pdf/journal/random_trees_J.pdf)) specifically note that the variance of the size-$k$ count can be calculated by the Matrix–Tree Theorem and is often very large compared to its squared mean. [Heilman, *Independent Sets of Random Trees and Sparse Random Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225) studies the very same planted-set law (§3); Lemma A.1 gives the classical fixed-independent-set enumeration attributed to Bedrosian. These are direct precedents for the model, first moment and second-moment method. The exact normalization here uses the exponent $k-1$.

**Known enumerative and probabilistic inputs.** [Martin and Reiner, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0302213) gives the complete split graph's degree enumerator on setting its two weight vectors equal; see also [Richard P. Lewis's degree-aware multipartite Prüfer enumeration](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(99)90111-5). The representation used in the proof is for degree multiplicities, not for the order of letters of a conditioned ordinary Prüfer word. The diverging-variance hypergeometric CLT is classical; see [Lahiri, Chatterjee and Maiti](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378375807001188). The general Gaussian change-of-measure and second-moment matching principle is already in [Banerjee and Ma, Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00567). Neither the norm-matching argument in isolation nor the planted degree enumerator is claimed as a new method.

**What is compared.** The model-specific claim is the location and distribution of the transition for a single prescribed sequence of coefficient sizes, including the separation of the ordinary and size-biased laws above it. The separate finite path factor follows by combining subcritical concentration with the classical exact expectation. Basit–Galvin's published Problem 17, pp. 13–14 after (18), and Heilman's Question 8.3 ask instead about a typical-tree lower bound beating the path exponentially at positive densities; see the [separate repository note](../cayley-tree-path-coefficient-gap/). The repository's earlier [mode results](../cayley-tree-independent-set-modes/) concern the maximizing coefficient index, not the count at a prescribed index. Lognormal phenomena in other random graph models, such as [the total hard-core partition function on a percolated hypercube](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07080), concern a different graph law and statistic.

**Search boundary.** Reviewed sources on random labeled-tree independent-set counts, concentration relative to the mean, sublinear coefficient regimes, planted independent sets and detectability, overlap moments, split-graph spanning-tree degrees and Gaussian likelihood conditioning. The repository review used [main at #129](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/17e8dbd459b9b62f47cab49b17f643daa54629e7), including notes, preprints, formalizations, releases and earlier [Cayley inventory](../cayley-tree-independent-set-modes/SOURCES.md). The two research additions since the previous complete #127 inventory were the Gaussian modal-index law and the macroscopic path gap. Searches by subject and terminology did not locate the stated Cayley coefficient trichotomy. This is a bounded literature and repository check, not a guarantee of priority, completeness or external peer review.
