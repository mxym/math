# Exact question and adjacent work

- [S. P. Fekete and H. Meijer, *On Minimum Stars and Maximum Matchings* (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004540010007), §7, Conjecture 1, predicts $4/\pi$ for the ratio of an optimal site-centered star to an optimal unrestricted Steiner star across all planar Euclidean inputs. The circular lower-bound examples already belong to the prior work. This is not the Gilbert–Pollak spanning-tree/network ratio.
- [A. Dumitrescu, C. D. Tóth and G. Xu, *On stars and Steiner stars. II*, SODA 2009](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611973068.35), [author manuscript, end of §2](https://adriandumitrescu.org/star2.pdf): their Conjecture 1 is the exact fixed-$n$ formula for **every planar point set**, not just for a circle. Compare [their fuller manuscript, Corollaries 3(ii), 4, and Conjecture 1](https://adriandumitrescu.org/star.pdf). The first circular theorem assumes the Weber center is the center of the circle; the second theorem assumes all rays from the Weber point are regularly spaced. The present result drops these two restrictions on circular inputs and allows arbitrary weights, with $n$ counting the occupied sites rather than the sum of multiplicities.
- [D. Ismailescu and J. Park, *Improved upper bounds for the Steiner ratio* (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disopt.2013.10.004), §§2–4, bound the unrestricted planar ratio and the spatial analogue. Their Lemmas 2.3–2.4 credit earlier existence and equal-nodal-cost results for extremal finite configurations. This draft does not claim those reductions. [S. Steinerberger (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19311) studies measures with constant distance potential on planar curves; constant-potential equilibrium theory itself is classical and is not the cardinality estimate proved here.
- A related geometric formulation involves tangent polygons of ellipses. [G. B. Mathews, *Polygons of minimum perimeter circumscribed to an ellipse*](https://rcin.org.pl/impan/dlibra/publication/88078/edition/109149/content) proves Steiner’s structural characterization involving a confocal ellipse. [Baranzini, Bialy and Sorrentino, introduction, formula (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06915v2) recall an **upper** bound on the least circumscribed polygon perimeter in terms of the perimeter of the body. Neither statement directly gives the needed lower bound for the nodal distance sum. [Solynin and Zalgaller](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v159-n1-p05.pdf) compare the logarithmic capacity **of a polygon** at fixed area, a different functional.
- The repository's [earlier Euclidean star result](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/154) and [dedicated archived version](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24359374) derive the unrestricted sharp dimension constant for $d\ge3$ and a separate, nonsharp upper bound for general planar inputs. That archive does not contain this weighted finite-cardinality circular theorem. This draft does not change the archive or settle the remaining general planar predictions.
