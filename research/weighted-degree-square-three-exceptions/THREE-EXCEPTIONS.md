# Three arbitrary exceptional weighted degrees: a finite symbolic extension

**AI-assisted proof; independently reviewed internally, not externally peer reviewed.** Statement, ambient weights, original source, nomenclature and limitations as in [two exceptions](TWO-EXCEPTIONS.md). This lemma is about the separate squared-degree conjecture, not the main cut–imbalance bound. It is not the general weighted theorem.

**Theorem.** If all but at most three vertices of a nonnegative weighted loopless graph have the same weighted degree, then its maximum cut satisfies 8WC >= 4W² + D, where D is the sum of weighted degree squares.

**Base cases.** Every graph on at most four vertices satisfies the inequality: pad to four, then a random two-versus-two cut gives C>=2W/3. Since d_i<=C and sum d_i=2W, F=W/2+D/(8W)<=W/2+C/4<=C. We will repeatedly use the zero-weight contraction, component, pendant-edge, rank-one and two-exception facts from the preceding notes. Convex positive homogeneity gives subadditivity F(w+w')<=F(w)+F(w') on the same vertex set, by convexity at the midpoint.

**Reduction.** For n>=5 there are a=n−3>=2 ordinary vertices. Average over every permutation of them. As their degrees were equal, D and W do not change; C cannot increase. Three classes of variables are: one ordinary-pair weight A, three ordinary-to-exception weights H_i, three exceptional pair weights B_ij. In total there are seven independent parameters. It suffices to check the extreme points of the compact nonnegative seven-dimensional polytope cut out by C<=1, by the argument in TWO-EXCEPTIONS.md.

**Boundary.** If A=0, contract the independent ordinary class to a single vertex, giving a graph on at most four vertices. If B_ij=0, contract exceptions i,j; only two exceptional vertices remain and all remaining ordinary degrees are unchanged. If some H_i=0 and neither previous case applies, exception i is adjacent only to the other two exceptions j,k, and the three exceptional edges are positive. Let m be the minimum of those three weights. Subtract a triangle of weight m per edge on the exceptions to get a nonnegative residual graph G'. Some exceptional edge in G' is now zero; thus the preceding contraction case applies (including when the missing edge is incident to i). Moreover an optimal cut of G' may always be taken nonmonochromatic on i,j,k: if all three are on the same side and a positive residual edge is incident to i, flipping only i strictly improves the cut, impossible. Otherwise i is isolated and can be assigned freely. The same cut therefore attains the triangle's optimum 2m. Thus C(G)=C(G')+2m, and F(G)<=F(G')+F(mK3)<=C(G). No merger across an exceptional and an ordinary vertex was used. This covers intersections of boundary faces as well.

**Interior extreme points.** Normalize A=1 for the following algebra (rescaling a positive point does not affect its active maximizing cut types). Write h_i=H_i/A and B_ij for the rescaled exceptional weights. For additional clarity, each statement about extreme point ranks refers to the original C<=1 normalization, before this change of units. Up to global reversal the three exceptional signs have four sign types: all equal, and each type with one singleton opposite the other two. For a type s, orient either representative and let v_s=sum h_i s_i. Let x be the ordinary signed count on the equally spaced grid {-a,-a+2,...,a}. Twice the signed edge objective (which is minimized to maximize the cut), apart from a constant -a, is
   x²+2v_s x+2sum_{i<j} B_ij s_i s_j.
Each type supplies at most two maximizing cut normals, because the minimizer in x of a strictly convex quadratic on this grid consists of at most two adjacent counts. At a positive extreme point the tight cut functionals span all seven dimensions. All four sign types must therefore participate, and at least three of them have two minimizing counts. For those types v_s is an integer congruent to a+1 modulo 2 with |v_s|<=a−1. The four signed fields r=h_1+h_2+h_3 and v_i=r−2h_i obey r=v_1+v_2+v_3, so the fourth field is also an integer of the same parity, whether or not it has two grid minimizers. Given positive h_i, each |v_i|<r. If any mixed field exceeded a−1 in modulus, then the all-equal field r would also exceed it; at most two types could have double minima, contrary to the rank count. Thus all three mixed fields are double and have rounding error 1 (squared distance to the count grid). All three h_i=(r−v_i)/2 are integers. For the all-equal type the squared rounding error is 1 when r<=a+1, otherwise (r−a)².

Write E_ij=B_ij−h_i h_j. The minimum for sign type s, still omitting the common −a, equals
   error_s − sum_i h_i² + 2 sum_{i<j} E_ij s_i s_j.
Because the three mixed types tie with the same error 1, comparing them yields E_12=E_13=E_23=E. Comparing any mixed type with the all-equal type gives 8E=1−error_all. If r<=a+1 then E=0: all the weights are products of the list (1 repeated a times, h_1,h_2,h_3), already covered.

**Only remaining explicit family.** For r>a+1 write r=a+2t+1 with an integer t>=1 and z=t(t+1)/2. The interior extreme weights have B_ij=h_i h_j−z. Each |r−2h_i|<=a−1, so h_i>=t+1. Write h_i=t+1+y_i with y_i>=0; then a=t+2+sum y_i. Sort y_1>=y_2>=y_3, and set y_1=i+j+k, y_2=j+k,y_3=k, and t=u+1, where i,j,k,u are nonnegative integers. The mixed type computation shows the minimum signed edge sum is exactly (1−a−sum h_i²+2z)/2. The common ordinary degree is a−1+r, the exceptional degrees are h_i(a+r−h_i)−2z, and the edge mass is
   2W=a(a−1)+2ar+2sum_{i<j} h_i h_j−6z.
Consequently the target gap 8WC−4W²−D is exactly the polynomial
   2W(a+sum h_i²−1−2z)−a(a−1+r)²−sum_i (h_i(a+r−h_i)−2z)².
After the stated substitutions its 70 nonzero monomial coefficients in u,i,j,k are integers between 2 and 516 inclusive (only positivity is used), and the constant coefficient is 96. The accompanying [elementary integer polynomial expansion](three_check.py) reconstructs the polynomial from these formulas; no optimizer, search, floating point, external data or third-party package is needed. Positivity proves the claim on this entire unbounded-parameter family. Together with the rank-one and zero faces this proves the three-exception theorem.

**Second verification.** The [internal review](REVIEW.md#an-independent-symmetric-polynomial-certificate-for-the-last-family) gives an alternative decomposition into eight nonnegative terms directly from a particular cut, without sorting the three parameters. Its [independent verifier](independent.py) certifies the identity on a degree-determining interpolation grid and recovers the first expansion separately.

No classification for four or more arbitrary exceptional vertices is inferred. This argument does not suppose that vertices of a general graph have large degree multiplicities. Its finite symbolic identity is distinct from the much larger numerical certificate for Conjecture 1.3.
