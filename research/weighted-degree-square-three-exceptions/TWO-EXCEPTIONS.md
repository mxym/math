# A certificate-free case of the degree-square Max-Cut conjecture: two exceptional degrees

**AI-assisted proof; independently reviewed internally, not externally peer reviewed.** This is about BEKSZ ECCC Conjecture 1.5 (ITCS Conjecture 5), *not* their already separately addressed Conjecture 1.3.

Let w_ij>=0 be aggregate weights of a finite undirected loopless graph, W the total, C the largest cut, d_i weighted degree and D=sum_i d_i². Write F(w)=W/2+D/(8W), with F(0)=0.

**Theorem.** If all but at most two vertices have the same weighted degree, then C>=F(w). The weights may be arbitrary nonnegative real numbers. The two exceptions can have different degrees. No assumption of constant edge weights within or across vertex types is imposed on the input graph. This also applies after omitting isolates. It does not assert the full degree-square conjecture or classify equality.

**1. Auxiliary facts.** F is continuous, nonnegative, positively homogeneous and convex on nonnegative weight vectors: each term d_i²/W is quadratic-over-linear, a convex function, and d_i<=W makes the zero extension continuous. Identifying any two nonadjacent vertices preserves the total and the degrees off the pair, replaces the pair of degrees u,v by u+v, and cannot increase the maximum cut. Thus the degree-square inequality on the quotient implies it on the original, with arbitrary parallel weights aggregated. On disjoint unions use C=sum C_j and D/(8W)<=sum D_j/(8W_j) over positive components. Appending a pendant edge of weight t to a vertex of degree d cannot break the inequality: C and W both increase by t, D increases by 2dt+2t². Then
  8(W+t)[F(w)+t−F(w')]=t(4W+D/W−2d)+2t² >= 0
for W>0 since d<=W. If W=0, the resulting single edge directly satisfies the assertion.

The [rank-one lemma](RANK-ONE.md) proves the inequality on every complete product-weight graph w_ij=x_i x_j, including arbitrary zeros and all graph orders; its proof is elementary and should be read along with this note. Any positive three-vertex instance is of that form by taking x_i=sqrt(w_ij w_ik/w_jk). With a missing edge it is bipartite, where C=W and D<=2W². The cases at most three vertices therefore need nothing further.

**2. Exact reduction to four variables.** For at least four vertices, label the two exceptions 1,2, choosing extra arbitrary labels if necessary. Let the remaining a>=2 vertices have the common degree. Average the original weighted graph over permutations of these a vertices. Total and each degree are unchanged, and the maximum cut does not increase by convexity. It is enough to prove the bound on the averaged graph, where A is the weight on each pair of ordinary vertices, H and J are ordinary-to-exception per-edge weights, and B is the exception-to-exception weight. In fact we prove it for *every* such four-parameter graph, regardless of accidental further equalities in degree.

Consider the closed polytope K of nonnegative quadruples (A,H,J,B) for which all cut weights are at most 1. It is bounded since each parameter occurs on an edge and is <=1. If F<=1 at every extreme point, then F<=1 throughout K by convexity; scaling a graph of positive total to have C=1 proves the homogeneous claim. The zero-total case is immediate.

**3. All strictly positive extreme points have product weights.** Represent a cut by signs. Up to reversing signs, fix exception 1 to +1, write the sign on exception 2 as t in {-1,+1}, and let x be the signed count of the ordinary vertices, ranging from -a to a in steps of two. Minimizing the signed sum over edges is equivalent to maximizing the cut. Twice that signed sum, apart from the common constant -Aa, is
     Q_t(x)=Ax²+2(H+tJ)x+2tB.
For fixed t, each possible x gives one cut functional in the four edge parameters. If A>0, strict convexity of Q_t in x shows that its minimum on this finite grid is attained at most twice, and twice only at two adjacent grid points equidistant from the real minimizer. Thus there are at most four distinct functionals achieving C across the two values of t (fewer if the branch minima differ).

At a strictly positive extreme point of K the active cut functionals must span four dimensions; otherwise a nonzero direction annihilating them provides a sufficiently small feasible two-sided perturbation, since every nonactive inequality has positive slack and there are finitely many cuts. Consequently both t-branches have two adjacent minimizers and attain the common cut maximum. Put r=(H+J)/A and s=(H−J)/A. The two adjacent signed counts in each branch are at distance exactly one from -r and -s respectively, so the minimizing values of Q are A−Ar²+2B and A−As²−2B. Equating them gives
     4B=A(r²−s²)=4HJ/A.
It follows that the averaged graph has product weights: take x_ordinary=sqrt(A), x_1=H/sqrt(A), x_2=J/sqrt(A). The rank-one lemma applies. No enumeration of the size, parities or locations of the two adjacent minima is needed.

**4. Boundary.** If A=0, the ordinary class is independent. Contract it to one vertex and apply the three-vertex case. If B=0, contract the two exceptions into one; with A>0 the result is a product-weight graph using the single exceptional weight (H+J)/sqrt(A). If H=0 or J=0 and A>0, the corresponding exception only has a possible edge to the other exception. Deleting it leaves a product-weight graph with just one exception, and the pendant-edge calculation restores the assertion. These alternatives exhaust the boundary, including repeated zeros. This proves the theorem.

Their one-round channel equivalence concerns a different graph inequality. See [scoped search/reduction notes](SOURCES.md); no universal or novelty claim follows from searches or sampling.
