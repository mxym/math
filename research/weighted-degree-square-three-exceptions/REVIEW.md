# Independent internal review: weighted degree-square cut bound with three exceptional vertices

**Verdict: PASS for the two- and three-exception theorems and the product-weight lemma.** This is a proper subclass of the separate BEKSZ degree-square conjecture. No conclusion about all weighted graphs, all equality cases, or communication rate. No external peer review or assurance of historical priority.

## Premise and prior

[BEKSZ, ITCS 2025, §1.3, Conjecture 5, p. 22:7](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol325-itcs2025/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22.pdf) normalize nonnegative real edge weights to total 1. Multiplication by an arbitrary W>0 gives C≥W/2+D/(8W), D=∑dᵢ². Their [ECCC revision 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/revision/1/download/) numbers it 1.5. BEKSZ give equal-weight odd cliques as tight examples. Their one-round feedback equivalent cut–imbalance conjecture is *different*, ECCC 1.3/ITCS 3. For unweighted connected simple graphs the Edwards–Erdős bound suffices: D≤2W(n−1). Classical weighted spanning-tree bounds ([Gutin–Yeo](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1411913)) should not be conflated with this problem: on a triangle with weights 1,ε,ε the minimum spanning tree weighs 2ε, while D/(8W) tends to 1/4. Regular weights are elementary from a random balanced cut. Here edges may be arbitrary subject only to the repeated ordinary *degrees*, not necessarily constant on vertex classes.

The public named-conjecture and degree-square/weighted Max-Cut searches found the BEKSZ question and nearby results, not an existing proof for the scope reviewed here; this is not evidence of absolute priority. The related repository tracks #110/#115/#123/#127 address the separate imbalance problem. Last delta checked to main 5ac928b56f6e87907a1a9fe95afef3c1bd30bc37 (#130), exactly three intervening Cayley-tree commits since #127.

## Global reduction and product weights

For positive W, F=W/2+D/(8W) is convex by quadratic-over-linear and homogeneous; at the origin the extension is continuous since dᵢ≤W and ∑dᵢ=2W. Subadditivity follows and C is itself the maximum of finitely many linear functionals. Symmetrization over the equal-degree vertices keeps *every degree* and W, and can only decrease C. On the averaged graph C≤1 and nonnegative weights form a compact polytope (each parameter occurs on a cut, hence ≤1). Maximize convex F there at a vertex; normalize a nonzero input by C. At a strictly positive vertex the tight cut normals must have full rank: otherwise a small two-sided perturbation annihilates all of them and is feasible by strict slack elsewhere. Contracting two nonadjacent vertices keeps W, raises D by 2dᵢdⱼ, and cannot increase C since cuts lift. A pendant edge of weight t gives exactly C+t, and the paper's increment check for F holds also for isolated attachment. For n≤4, pad to 4 vertices: a random balanced cut gives C≥2W/3; singleton cuts give D≤2WC, hence F≤W/2+C/4≤C. Handle W=0 directly.

For weights xᵢxⱼ, normalize ∑xᵢ=1 by homogeneity, omit zero masses, and write Q=∑xᵢ², Sⱼ=∑xᵢʲ. For sign discrepancy g, C=(1−g²)/4, W=(1−Q)/2, D=Q−2S₃+S₄. The goal is (1−Q)g²≤2S₃−S₄−Q². Sort x decreasing and greedily cancel against the residual sign. At the *last* crossing, incoming residual ≤xₖ. Thereafter residuals subtract every remaining term; hence residual ≤xₖ−z with 0≤z=∑_{j>k}xⱼ≤xₖ. Then (xₖ−z)²≤xₖ²(1−z)≤∑_{i≤k}xᵢ³. For independent size-biased X,Y (xᵢ selected with probability xᵢ), E[(X−Y)²(1−X−Y)]≥0 even though X can exceed 1/2: the integrand vanishes when equal, and otherwise the labels differ so X+Y≤1. Expanding yields S₃(1−Q)≤2S₃−S₄−Q². Trivial W=0 covers the one-atom case.

## Two and three exceptions

For two, the signed ordinary count has a strictly convex quadratic objective on a step-two grid for each of the two sign classes. Rank four forces each class to supply two *adjacent* minimizers, both classes tying globally. Each minimum has rounding error exactly one; completing the square yields AB=HJ, hence product weights. On the boundary one contracts independent ordinary vertices (A=0), or the exceptional pair (B=0), or removes and restores a pendant exception (H or J zero). These reductions do not assume the exceptional degrees are distinct.

For three, A=0 again contracts to ≤4. A zero exceptional-pair weight permits direct contraction to two exceptions with ordinary degrees unchanged. For Hᵢ=0 and all three exceptional edges positive, remove their largest common triangle (m=min B). The residual therefore has a zero exceptional edge. If all exceptional vertices in a residual optimum were monochromatic, and i had a positive remaining incident edge, flipping i would strictly improve: no ordinary edge touches i. Otherwise i is isolated and may be flipped at no cost. Thus there exists a residual optimum also attaining 2m on the triangle; the exact additive C and subadditive F then transfer the inequality. This crucial additivity is not assumed for arbitrary triangles.

With seven positive variables normalize A=1 after the active-rank argument. Four sign types contribute ≤2 tight normals each. Rank seven forces all four active and ≥3 double; double means field of opposite parity from the count grid and magnitude ≤a−1. The fields r=∑h and vᵢ=r−2hᵢ satisfy r=∑vᵢ, so the fourth has the same parity as the first three. Positivity gives |vᵢ|<r; hence if any mixed field exceeds a−1 in magnitude, it and r are both nondouble, impossible. Thus all mixed types round with error 1; the all-equal error is 1 up to and including r=a+1, then (r−a)². Equal mixed optima force all Eᵢⱼ=Bᵢⱼ−hᵢhⱼ equal; tying the all-equal type gives 8E=1−error. The zero case is product. Otherwise r=a+2t+1, t integer ≥1, E=−t(t+1)/2, and hᵢ=(r−vᵢ)/2≥t+1, hence hᵢ=t+1+yᵢ, a=t+2+∑yᵢ. No claim that every such set of parameters defines a vertex is required.

## An independent symmetric polynomial certificate for the last family

Put S=∑yᵢ, P=∑_{i<j}yᵢyⱼ, R=y₁y₂y₃, U=S²−3P=(1/2)∑_{i<j}(yᵢ−yⱼ)². Put exceptions 1,2 on the plus side, 3 on the minus side, and p=y₃+1 of the a ordinary vertices on the plus side. This cut is always feasible (0≤p≤a), and its weight is C★=p(a−p)+(a−p)(h₁+h₂)+ph₃+B₁₃+B₂₃. No maximization is required. Directly from these edge counts the gap G★=8WC★−4W²−D satisfies

    3G★ = 6(S²+P)U + (8t+6)S³ + (28t+30)SU
         + (32t²+60t+24)S² + (16t²+48t+30)U
         + (24S+36t+54)R + 12(t+1)(4t²+7t+2)S
         + 24t(t+1)²(t+2).

All terms are nonnegative; the last is positive. The identity is independently certified in independent.py on the product grid t=1,…,5 and yᵢ=0,…,4. This is exhaustive polynomial interpolation, not experimental evidence: W,C★ have total degree ≤2, the exceptional degrees also degree ≤2, and a times the ordinary degree squared degree ≤3, so both sides have total degree ≤4; vanishing on the full grid implies a polynomial identity by repeated univariate interpolation. The checker's extra finite maximum-cut comparisons are not needed for this argument. Separately, recovering the author's sorted-parameter polynomial from 70 direct-cut evaluations on a total-degree Newton grid confirms all 70 coefficients positive, range 2–516, constant 96.

The bounded exhaustive stress checks in CHECKS.txt cover 13,608 small two-/three-exception parameter graphs, including 1,824 simultaneous-optimum triangle subtractions, and 22,995 candidate rank-one input partitions. These finite examples supplement, not replace, the unbounded mathematical proof above.
