# Primary references and limits of the claims

- **Original question.** M. Braverman, K. Efremenko, G. Kol, R. R. Saxena, Z. Zhang, [Round-vs-Resilience Tradeoffs for Binary Feedback Channels, ITCS 2025, §1.3, Conjecture 5, p. 22:7](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol325-itcs2025/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22.pdf); [ECCC TR22-179, revision 1, Conjecture 1.5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/revision/1/download/). They normalize total nonnegative edge weight to one and give the equally weighted odd cliques as tight examples. Their one-feedback equivalence instead concerns ITCS Conjecture 3 / ECCC Conjecture 1.3. Repository [#110](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/110), [#115](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/115), [#123](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/123) and [#127](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/127) concern that other problem.
- **Classical cases.** For a regular weighted graph a uniformly random balanced cut suffices. For an equal-edge connected simple graph the [Edwards–Erdős bound](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1973-048-X) and $\sum_i d_i^2\le2W(n-1)$ already imply the degree-square inequality (use unit weights and apply componentwise). This is not a new consequence for unweighted graphs. [Gutin and Yeo, Lower Bounds for Maximum Weighted Cut](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1411913) treat weighted matchings, bipartite subgraphs and spanning-tree bounds. The individual minimum-spanning-tree bound does not imply the degree-square estimate for arbitrary weights; see the example explained in the [review](REVIEW.md). The recent [Weighted Max Cut in Subcubic Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05178) constrains combinatorial degree and girth, not the number of vertices outside one common *weighted degree*.
- **External screening, 2026-11-19.** Searches checked the originating conference and ECCC versions, named conjecture/local numbering and author/title combinations, maximum weighted cut versus squared degrees, product weights, exceptional/equal degree classes, odd cliques and feedback follow-ups. We did not identify a prior proof of exactly the two scopes in this note. Search cannot rule out unpublished, differently formulated, unindexed or recent work.
- **Scope of this note.** There is no claim of solving the unrestricted degree-square conjecture, giving an optimal-code classification, or improving a channel threshold. Independently checked proofs cover arbitrary product weights and the class with at most three exceptional vertices. Other conjectural or unreviewed extensions are excluded.
