## Independently reconstructed counting model

Write C for the clique and Z for its h=6bq independent auxiliary vertices. For X⊂C, let M(X)={z∈Z : N(z)∩X=∅}. For ordinary domination the correct unnormalized polynomial identity is
```
D_G(t) = sum_{X⊆C} t^(|X|+|M(X)|) (1+t)^(h−|M(X)|).
```
Indeed, if X is nonempty it already dominates all clique vertices; precisely the members of M(X) must themselves be selected, and every other auxiliary is free. If X is empty, M(X)=Z, only the full auxiliary set is eligible, and it really dominates C because every clique vertex has at least one incidence neighbor. Thus the empty-clique term is exactly t^h, not zero and not (1+t)^h. Distinct incident *group pairs* give distinct, nonadjacent z; |M| is the sum of their indicators even when they share dozens of clique neighbors. At t=1 followed by fair normalization, the exact conditional factor is 2^(−|M|). This justifies conditioning on the group-hit pattern rather than the precise X. In either pure event the selected clique contains at least 2b or 3b distinct vertices, respectively. Every auxiliary, selected or not, has a selected clique neighbor. Hence the clique supplies open domination internally and a connected induced core, and each selected auxiliary attaches to that core. This verifies both variant inclusions, without assuming an arbitrary ordinary set is connected or total.

The finite point–line pair has degree q in both parts; same-x points, or parallel distinct lines, have zero common neighbors, all other pairs have at most one. Hence an incident-degree list has sum qu and sum of factorial moments ≤u(u−1); Cauchy yields |Γ(U)| ≥q²u/(q+u−1). Centering the degree list around u/q gives sum of squared deviations ≤(q−1)u. Both are slightly stronger than the bounds used.

## Explicit finite probability estimates

* One exact missing-group pattern on a chosen side has probability `w × product(pi/(1-pi)) ≤ w`: even the singleton missing group has odds one, and every other group's odds are smaller. Opposite group misses remain independent. After integrating each opposite indicator, a neighbor position incurs at most a factor 127/128, using just one of its groups (pi≥1/64). Independent auxiliary indicators, not the adjacent incidences, were integrated before this product. No normalization by the rare event “is dominating” occurs.
* For 1≤u≤q, the relative contribution is at most the geometric series with ratio 7·2^80·2^(−2^32)<2^(−12), giving <1/4095. For q<u≤2^−16b, the penalty is at least b/256 in base-two exponent. The weighted small-support count is ≤2^(30·2^−16b), by the ordinary generating function (1+7δ)^b and δ^u≥δ^(δb), so the net is ≤2^(−b/512). This applies on either side despite unequal block sizes. For the remaining supports u,v>δb, the error-to-main ratio is sqrt(q³/(uv))<1/16. Hence |M|≥uv/(2q)≥128b. Sum against the original **probability distribution** on the patterns, not their unweighted number. Since w>(1/4)^b, the remaining relative cost is ≤2^(−126b). These exhaust all mixed samples; double-counting small supports simply makes the union bound weaker. In total the mixed cost/w < 2/4095+3/4096 <1/100.
* Enumerating the 2^k−1 nonempty bit patterns within each group independently gives per-position constrained mass 63/128 on both sides, shifts 23/42 and 37/42 over the fair baseline 7/2, and variances 608/441 and 412/441 for the constrained side. The opposite side adds 7b/4 to variance and the auxiliaries h/4. Thus the proposed looser ≤2bq and Chebyshev leakage 2048/q<1/100 are valid. Even at q=2^40 all arguments are finite.
* Center of the middle window is N/2+5b/7, the side centers differ by b/3, and all three exact widths in integer counts are 75,557,863,725,914,323,419,136 for this b. For any family containing both phase events and contained in the dominating event, *each* outer-window mass is at least .99w, and the middle-window mass is ≤.03w. The overlap of pure phases is allowed throughout; no unauthorized subtraction/additive lower bound is needed. Comparing averages produces one index per window with a strict valley. The triples may depend on the family. Connected dominating sets are upward closed by adding vertices adjacent to the original set, so their positive support is an interval; adjacent log-concavity would imply unimodality. This is precisely the connected coefficient-shape consequence, not a theorem about roots.

## Scope and prior work

* [Alikhani–Peng, after Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.2251#page=7) and [Galvin–Zhang, Conjecture 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.12731) concern the weak unimodality of counts of **all** dominating subsets in finite graphs, not the sequence of *minimal* dominating sets. [Alikhani–Jafari, Conjecture 2.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.02222#page=5) is the **total** domination counterpart. [Shao–Zhao, v5, §5 Open Problem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06569v5#page=14) call the connected domination unimodality and log-concavity issues open among several different analytic questions. The counterexample addresses exactly its coefficient-shape clause.
* [Omar 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14494) proves (among other things) threshold-graph log-concavity. Our graph is split but not threshold: on one incidence pick the two auxiliaries for the same point group and different line groups, and a clique vertex from each of those latter groups; the induced graph is the path auxiliary–clique–clique–auxiliary. The graph has no universal vertex, its minimum degree is only 3, and all three windows lie strictly between N/2 and 3N/4, so known early and late monotone ranges are not challenged. [Burcroff–O’Brien](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2023.113508) prove special families/monotone portions, not all split graphs. [Du–Heilman–Panova](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02193) give graph/tree failures of ordinary log-concavity, which do not imply nonunimodality. [Alikhani–Griswold](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00109) concerns integer roots. [Newton–Brimkov, Nov. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11352v1) disprove unimodality for ordinary/connected/total **zero forcing**, not domination.
* [Bhattacharyya–Kahn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.1752) is a direct two-phase nonunimodality precedent for bipartite *independent-set* counts: it matches phase weights and separates their typical sizes. [Kanté–Limouzy–Mary–Nourine](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2053#page=6) use split-incidence graphs for minimal transversal/dominating-set enumeration. Neither the overarching two-phase idea nor the incidence skeleton is claimed as new. Neither cited statement handles ordinary domination's independently selectable unmet auxiliaries with this mixed-weight bound. No prior resolution of the three coefficient questions was located in the targeted search. This does not establish global priority.
