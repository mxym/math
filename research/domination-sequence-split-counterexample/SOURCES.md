# Problem statements and prior work

Literature search snapshot: 18 November 2026. A search that finds no matching result does not establish priority.

* The [Alikhani–Peng preprint, §3 p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.2251#page=7) states the unnumbered universal conjecture immediately after Theorem 6, for ordinary domination by a subset (not just minimal dominating sets). The preprint is from 2009; the paper appeared in *Ars Combinatoria* 114 (2014), 257–266. [Galvin–Zhang, Conj.1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12731) reiterates it and proves it for powers of paths and cycles. [Beaton–Brown](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11813) prove it on paths, cycles, complete multipartite and almost all graphs; they give failure of log-concavity, not a unimodality counterexample. [Burcroff–O'Brien](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2023.113508) prove sufficient conditions including families of spiders and products. [Omar, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14494v1) gives overlap and transversal methods and a threshold-graph result. Split graphs need not be threshold graphs; here opposite auxiliary neighborhoods are not nested. No universal vertex is present. This construction is not an outcome of the constant-density random-graph model and does not contradict an almost-all result.
* For the total variant, [Alikhani–Jafari, Conjecture 2.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.02222#page=5), in their 2016 arXiv version, quantifies over total domination polynomials (the ordering of the authors differs between preprint and [journal version](https://doi.org/10.1080/09720529.2019.1616908)). It is distinct from the integer-root question in the same work. [Their follow-up](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06702) treats positive examples. For the connected variant [Shao–Zhao, open discussion in §5 of v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06569v5) discusses universal unimodality/log-concavity among separate connected-domination-root questions. We address only the coefficient-shape clause, not their root questions.
* Methodological precedents are genuine: [Bhattacharyya–Kahn 2013, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.1752) balanced two modes with different typical sizes to disprove unimodality of the *independent-set* sequence on a bipartite graph. Their many-mode extension appears in §3. The present use of distinct concentrates is analogous and should not be called new. But ordinary domination is not independence, and their partition does not account for the selecting-an-uncovered-auxiliary mechanism here. [Kanté, Limouzy, Mary, Nourine, Definition 14 and Lemmas 15, 17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.2053#page=6) explicitly define the same general split-incidence skeleton; they study reductions involving *minimal* transversal, total/connected and ordinary dominating sets, rather than this shape problem. We do not claim to introduce that skeleton. The affine incidence bounds used here are elementary second moments, not new incidence theorems. What specifically needs appraisal for novelty is the finite weighted mixed-phase domination estimate in conjunction with the balanced block choice and the common sandwich for all three enumerators.
* [Alikhani–Griswold 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00109) constructs a graph for a different question, integer domination roots; [Newton–Brimkov 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11352v1) refutes the zero-forcing unimodality conjecture and its own connected/total forcing variants, not domination. Here “connected domination” means a connected *induced subgraph on the selected vertices*, not merely the graph's being connected.
* Searches combined exact conjecture titles and author pairs with split graph, graph incidence, counterexample, disproved, domination / dominating-set sequence, nonunimodal / non-unimodal, connected and total variants, GitHub/Lean/Zenodo, including 2026 and November records. No matching earlier general result was located; unindexed or differently named work is not excluded.
* [Du–Heilman–Panova, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02193) produce examples of trees and other graphs whose dominating-set sequences are not log-concave. Non-log-concavity alone does not imply nonunimodality; the present graph is not a tree.
