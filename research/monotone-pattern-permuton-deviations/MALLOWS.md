# Explicit near-uniform Mallows limits for a fixed bound on decreasing subsequences

Use the class and assumptions in LDP.md, and weight each allowed permutation $\sigma$ of length $n$ by $q^{\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)}$ with $q=e^{\beta/n}$, $\beta$ fixed. Write $Z_{n,d}(\beta)$ for the unnormalized sum of weights. For $\beta\le0$ the limiting permuton is the increasing diagonal and $n^{-1}\log Z\to2\log d$. For $\beta>0$, put
$$G(t)=\int_0^t\log(2\sinh(\beta u/2))\,du,\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log Z_{n,d}(\beta)=2[G(1)-dG(1/d)].\tag{6}$$
For $0<p<1$ let
$$z_p=\frac{e^{\beta p}-1}{1-e^{-\beta(1-p)}},\qquad
h_p(x)=\frac{z_p e^{-\beta x}}{1+z_p e^{-\beta x}},\quad h_0=0,\quad h_1=1.$$
The permuton converges in probability to the deterministic measure (2) of LDP.md with
$$ f_i(x)=h_{i/d}(x)-h_{(i-1)/d}(x),\qquad g_i(y)=f_i(1-y),\qquad 1\le i\le d.\tag{7} $$
These bounded positive densities (including $d=1$) describe $d$ explicit increasing quantile curves of equal masses. For the increasing-pattern counterpart reflect the plot and use $\operatorname{inv}\mapsto\binom n2-\operatorname{inv}$, i.e. reverse the bias parameter as well as applying the known deterministic normalization factor.

## 1. Exact canonical subset and equality of exponential free energies

Given axis words $u,v$ with matching colour totals, let $A(u)$ count the pairs of positions whose earlier letter is strictly smaller. Pair by colour ordinals as before. An inverted point pair oriented from smaller to larger colour in the x-order contributes $+1$ to $A(u)-A(v)$; one oriented the other way contributes $-1$. Noninversions contribute $0$, and there are no same-colour inversions. Thus $\operatorname{inv}\ge A(u)-A(v)$ for every such word pair; equality holds for **every canonical pair**, because along a decreasing subsequence heights strictly increase. The canonical pairs are in bijection with the allowed permutations. Define the surrogate sum $S_{n,d}$ by summing $q^{A(u)-A(v)}$ over *all* pairs with matching content. The original free sum is the sum of these exact same weights restricted to canonical pairs, in particular $Z\le S$.

For profiles in (1), let $D=\sum_{i<j}\big(\int F_i f_j-\int G_i g_j\big)$. This is the continuum version of the signed statistic divided by $n^2$ and is continuous in the profile topology, because all limiting marginals are atomless and their empirical cumulative functions converge uniformly. Set $v(\mu)=\mu^{\otimes2}\{x_1<x_2,\ y_1>y_2\}$, the oriented inversion probability, so $\operatorname{inv}/n^2$ is its empirical version (not twice that value). It is continuous along convergent permuton sequences and their rank-grid approximants, by atomlessness of the limiting axis marginals. For any profile, $v(\Phi(f,g))\ge D$: partition ordered pairs of points by their two colours as in the finite identity (or take empirical marginal quantile grids and pass to the limit).

The fixed-bin entropy upper count of LDP.md §2, applied now to all word pairs with the continuous signed term, yields
$$\limsup_n n^{-1}\log S_{n,d}\le\max_{(f,g)\in\mathcal A}(E(f,g)+\beta D).$$
In detail, choose a maximal weighted array for each fixed dyadic resolution using the maximal signed exponent in that array, extract a realizing subsequential profile, then pass down the dyadic entropy approximants on the compact profile space with the continuous term retained. In the opposite direction the unnormalized open-neighborhood lower count in LDP.md §§3–4 and continuity of $v$ give
$$\liminf_n n^{-1}\log Z_{n,d}\ \ge\ \sup_{(f,g)\in\mathcal A}\{E(f,g)+\beta v(\Phi(f,g))\}.$$
For $\beta>0$ these bounds and $Z\le S$ imply equality at exponential order; in particular under probability proportional to the surrogate weight the event that the pair is canonical has probability $e^{-o(n)}$. This is not a finite-size equality of enumerators nor a bounded-ratio claim. For $\beta\le0$, the unnormalized LDP and $v\ge0$ yield the free energy $2\log d$ and exponential concentration outside any neighborhood of the increasing diagonal: the latter is the unique zero of the untilted rate and has $v=0$.

## 2. Content and projected binary words

For a fixed content vector $(n_1,\ldots,n_d)$ the surrogate sum is
$$ {n\brack n_1,\ldots,n_d}_q\,{n\brack n_1,\ldots,n_d}_{q^{-1}}
=\left[\frac{\prod_{j=1}^n 2\sinh(\beta j/(2n))}
{\prod_i\prod_{j=1}^{n_i}2\sinh(\beta j/(2n))}\right]^2.$$
The first equality with the content sum is the ordinary Gaussian multinomial identity for inversions in rearrangements of a multiset (reversing a word interchanges increasing and decreasing unequal-letter pairs); the second is the product expression for these polynomials, with all exponential prefactors cancelling. Thus the normalized log of each content term equals $2[G(1)-\sum_i G(n_i/n)]+o(1)$ uniformly over all contents. To see uniformity through zero, subtract $\log u$ from the integrand of $G$: the remainder extends continuously to zero, and the normalized partial Riemann sums of $\log(j/n)$ differ from the corresponding integrals by $O(\log(n+1)/n)$ by monotonicity or Stirling. Since $G$ is strictly convex on $[0,1]$ (the derivative is strictly increasing on the interior), and there are polynomially many contents, (6) follows, and $n_i/n$ is within any prescribed tolerance of $1/d$ for all $i$ except with exponentially small surrogate probability.

Conditional on the content, the two words are independent. In the first, merge the letters $1,\ldots,j$ into a single low category, and the rest into a high category ($1\le j<d$). The statistic $A$ is the sum of the two internal word statistics and the number of low-before-high pairs in this binary projection. The internal words can be interleaved uniquely according to the binary word, so it has probability proportional to $\exp[-(\beta/n)\sum_{t\in T}t]$, where $T$ is its set of low positions of prescribed cardinality $m=\sum_{i\le j} n_i$. (The omitted constant depends only on $m,n$.) The second word's projection has exactly the law of the reversal of the first word's projected law. We require only the marginal laws for these finitely many cuts, not their mutual independence.

For completeness, if $m/n\to p\in(0,1)$, let $z=z_{n,m}$ be the unique positive constant making $\sum_{t=1}^n z e^{-\beta t/n}/(1+z e^{-\beta t/n})=m$. The binary projection is the independent Bernoulli subset with these inclusion probabilities conditioned to have size $m$. For $m/n$ in a compact subinterval of $(0,1)$ the odds are bounded above and below uniformly. That size event costs at most $e^{o(n)}$ in conditioning: some $k$ within $2\sqrt n$ of the mean has probability $\gg n^{-1/2}$ by Chebyshev and averaging. The ratio of adjacent size probabilities lies between $(n-k)\min_t w_t/(k+1)$ and $(n-k)\max_t w_t/(k+1)$, with $w_t=z e^{-\beta t/n}$, by counting all single insertions into weighted $k$-subsets. In this window both bounds stay between positive constants, giving $\mathbb P(|T|=m)\ge e^{-O(\sqrt n+\log n)}$ before conditioning.

Unconditional prefix counts deviate by more than $n\epsilon$ from their expectations at any prefix with probability at most $2n e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$ by Hoeffding and a union bound. Their expectations divided by $n$ converge uniformly in the prefix fraction to $\int_0^x h_p(t)dt$: Riemann sums and strict monotonicity in $z$ give $z_{n,m}\to z_p$, since $\int_0^1h_p=p$. The same convergence is uniform with $p=m/n$ in a compact interior interval, by compactness. For every fixed tolerance around the target cumulative profile with $p=j/d$, take a small fixed content tolerance first; then these concentration statements, including the exact-size conditioning cost, give exponentially small probability of failure. The complementary word uses the reflected densities. Taking consecutive differences of the projected cumulative profiles recovers all individual colours, so the surrogate pairs concentrate exponentially at (7) in the weak marginal topology. Conditioning on canonicality has only subexponential cost by §1. Finally (2) identifies the resulting unique permuton limit. This proves the assertion without assuming a priori that the full unconstrained colour words are usually canonical.

For $d=2$, (6) reduces to $2\int_0^1\log(2\cosh(\beta t/4))dt$ by the duplication formula for $\sinh$. By the change of variable $y=e^s$ and symmetry this is exactly equation (6) of Budzinski et al., Proposition 8. The $d=2$ asymptotics and constrained Mallows shapes are prior results.
