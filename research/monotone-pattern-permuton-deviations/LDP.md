# A permuton large-deviation principle for avoidance of every fixed monotone pattern

Fix $d\ge1$. Let $\sigma_n$ be uniform among permutations of $[n]$ having no decreasing subsequence of length $d+1$. Its permuton satisfies a full-sequence large-deviation principle at speed $n$. We give the rate below in terms of **unconstrained pairs of $d$-colour profiles**; no closure of an unspecified set of attainable data is needed. Reflection gives the result for avoidance of the increasing pattern of the same length. The $d=2$ permuton LDP is already known (Budzinski–Dubach–Féray–Kammoun–Maïda); the one-entry deviation probabilities for general $d$ are different observables (Madras–Pehlivan).

## 1. Profiles and rate

An admissible pair consists of nonnegative measurable functions $f_1,\dots,f_d$ and $g_1,\dots,g_d$ on $[0,1]$ such that
$$\sum_i f_i(x)=\sum_i g_i(y)=1\quad\text{a.e.},\qquad
\int f_i=\int g_i=p_i.\tag{1}$$
Regard these as tuples of subprobability measures with the product weak topology. The profile space $\mathcal A$ is compact: the matching totals and uniform total marginals are closed conditions, and imply that every component has a density between zero and one. With $F_i(x)=\int_0^x f_i$, $G_i(y)=\int_0^y g_i$, let $\Phi(f,g)$ be the permuton obtained by coupling the two marginals of each colour in increasing quantile order and adding the submeasures. Equivalently,
$$\Phi(f,g)([0,x]\times[0,y])=\sum_i\min\{F_i(x),G_i(y)\}.\tag{2}$$
This includes $p_i=0$. Weak convergence of profiles gives convergence of the distribution functions at every coordinate by atomlessness, hence $\Phi$ is continuous. Put
$$E(f,g)=\int_0^1 -\sum_i f_i\log f_i+\int_0^1-\sum_i g_i\log g_i,\qquad
I_d(\mu)=2\log d-\max_{(f,g)\in\mathcal A:\ \Phi(f,g)=\mu}E(f,g),\tag{3}$$
using $0\log0=0$ and maximum $-\infty$ on the empty set. In (3) the integrals are with respect to the corresponding coordinate Lebesgue measure and logs are natural. The entropy is upper semicontinuous: replacing densities by their dyadic interval averages gives continuous entropy functionals decreasing by concavity to $E$ by $L^1$ convergence. Thus (3) is a good rate. It concerns macroscopic permutation measures, not individual far-off-diagonal entries or a refined fluctuating path limit.

## 2. Canonical layers and the upper bound

Give each point its height, the length of the longest decreasing subsequence ending there. Points of the same height form an increasing chain. Every point of height $i>1$ has a point of height $i-1$ strictly to its northwest (earlier first coordinate, larger second). Conversely, if a decomposition into numbered increasing chains has this witness property, those labels **are the heights**. Indeed a point $u$ strictly northwest of $v$ cannot have label $s\ge r$, where $r$ is the label of $v$: when $s=r$ this contradicts the chain property; otherwise iterate northwest witnesses from $u$ down to label $r$ and obtain the same contradiction. Thus labels strictly increase along decreasing subsequences, and a label-$r$ point has longest ending subsequence exactly $r$.

Reading canonical layer labels in the two coordinate orders produces two $d$-letter words of the same content. They determine the permutation by matching equally numbered occurrences of each letter. Conversely arbitrary such pairs always generate permutations of width at most $d$, but these pairs are **not** unique without the canonical height condition. For an upper count, inject from the uncoloured permutation to its canonical pair. The empirical colour submeasures in each axis approach (1) along convergent subsequences; the total empirical marginal is the uniform rank grid. Formula (2) holds exactly for rank atoms, so any empirical profile limit yields the corresponding permuton. Square embedding instead of atoms costs at most $1/n$ in Wasserstein distance with sup ground metric.

For a closed set $C$ of permutons and a fixed dyadic partition, prescribe counts of all letters in each bin on each axis. There are polynomially many arrays for fixed partition, and the number of admissible pairs with those counts is bounded by the product of the separate bin multinomials. With $h(z)=(\sum_i z_i)\log(\sum_i z_i)-\sum_i z_i\log z_i$ for nonnegative vectors, the normalized log bound is at most the sum of $h$ at the two lists of normalized bin masses, up to $o(1)$ for the polynomial number of arrays. On any nonempty dominant array choose a realizing permutation from $C$. Extract a subsequence limit of its empirical profiles; by (2) it maps into $C$, and the masses of the fixed bins converge. Thus the unnormalized upper exponent is at most the maximum dyadic entropy over $\Phi^{-1}(C)$. If the latter set is empty there are eventually no such permutations by compactness. Otherwise compactness, continuity of each dyadic entropy, and monotone decrease show upon refining that the upper exponent is at most $\max_{\Phi^{-1}(C)}E$. Indeed maximizing profiles for increasing refinements have a limit subsequence, on which each fixed coarser entropy is an upper bound. This accounts also for profiles with zero-mass colours.

## 3. Sorting colour curves without changing the entropy

For the open-set lower bound fix any admissible $(f,g)$ with $\Phi(f,g)$ in the given open set. Omit colours of zero mass and number the remaining $k\le d$. Approximate each density by its averages on a common fine finite partition, then mix its average with a small fraction of the constant density $p_i$. Both axes retain (1) and the exact colour totals. As the partition is refined and the fraction tends to zero these approximations converge in $L^1$ to the original, so their entropies converge by boundedness and continuity of $-z\log z$; (2) gives weak convergence of their permutons. We may therefore first work with positive piecewise constant densities bounded below for each remaining colour, losing arbitrarily little in shape and score.

Each colour now traces the continuous strictly increasing polygonal curve $y=c_i(x)=G_i^{-1}(F_i(x))$ from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$ with finitely many positive slopes bounded above and below. At every $x$ relabel these curves from highest to lowest $y$, breaking ties by the pre-existing label. Carry along their masses. The sorted curves $c'_1\ge\cdots\ge c'_k$ are again strictly increasing continuous polygonal bijections: they are order statistics of the $c_i$, switch only at finitely many pieces or coincidence-interval endpoints, and inherit common lower and upper slope bounds. Denote the relabelled axis densities by $f'_i,g'_i$. In the first axis the densities are merely permuted pointwise. In the second axis they are likewise permuted almost everywhere: split the graphs at all switches; on each piece the submeasure travels unchanged to its new label, and through each fixed $y$ each strictly increasing sorted graph passes exactly once. Away from finitely many vertex heights the assignment of the $k$ old graph branches is a bijection onto the $k$ sorted branches there. On coincident pieces use the same fixed tie assignment on both axes. Therefore both integrated entropies are unchanged (the individual colour masses may change). All $f'_i,g'_i$ are still bounded below by a positive constant and above by one. Each sorted curve is the increasing coupling of its new marginals. Consequently the represented permuton is exactly unchanged too. This is a rearrangement to guarantee northwest witnesses, **not** a counting bijection between finite word pairs.

## 4. Robust witnesses and full-sequence lower count

Translate the *raw* coordinates of colour $i$ on its sorted curve by $(+i\delta,-i\delta)$ for fixed small $\delta>0$. We use these coordinates only to define ordered boxes, then assign the true ranks by sorting. For each sufficiently large $n$ choose positive integers $n_i$ summing to $n$ with $n_i/n=\int f'_i+O_k(1/n)$. Place $n_i$ labelled points at mid-quantiles of the normalized first marginal of colour $i$, with second coordinates on $c'_i$. Their two unshifted empirical marginals tend uniformly in CDF to the chosen marginals (the quantile grid error for each colour is $O_k(1/n)$). The maximal first-coordinate mesh on every colour, including the end gaps, is $O(1/n)$ by its positive density, with constants allowed to depend on the smooth profile. Every point $(x,c'_i(x))$ of colour $i>1$ therefore has a sampled point of colour $i-1$ within $O(1/n)$ of $x$ in the first coordinate and with second coordinate at least $c'_i(x)-O(1/n)$, by ordering and the Lipschitz bounds. After translation its predecessor is **strictly northwest**, with both coordinate gaps exceeding $\delta/2$ for all large $n$.

Use any fixed finite consecutive partitions covering the shifted x and y ranges with widths at most $\eta<\delta/4$. In each axis bin place its colour multiset in any order, independently over all bins and both axes; concatenate to get the axis words, and match each colour's occurrences by ordinal. Each original sample point still belongs to the same two bins: within either axis its colour occurrence index is preserved, and the cumulative bin counts for that colour fix its bin in that axis. Its witness belongs to a strictly earlier x-bin and a strictly later y-bin. Thus *every* word pair produced has canonical heights by §2. All outputs are distinct permutations, not weighted colourings. This remains valid for colour masses that were initially zero because they were omitted before assigning consecutive heights to active colours.

All outputs are close to the original sorted-profile permuton. One explicit coupling keeps the same colour index and ordinal. Before arbitrary within-bin scrambling, an unshifted empirical coordinate distribution across all points differs uniformly from Lebesgue by $O_k(1/n)$. A raw shift by at most $k\delta$ and a permutation of slots within a bin of raw width $\eta$ displace a normalized coordinate rank from its original unshifted real coordinate by at most $2k\delta+2\eta+O_k(1/n)$: an original coordinate below this margin stays below, and one above stays above, and one uses the empirical marginal CDF. Equivalently bracket the new rank by original empirical CDF values at the original coordinate plus and minus this margin. The unshifted point measure converges to $\Phi(f',g')$. Thus a small enough $\delta$ and $\eta$ keep *all* these outputs in the given open set for all large $n$.

Their number is exactly the product of the horizontal and vertical bin multinomials. For each fixed choice of shift and bins, the exponent per $n$ tends by Stirling to
$$\sum_J h\!\left(\left(\int_J f'_i(x-i\delta)\,dx\right)_i\right)
+\sum_B h\!\left(\left(\int_B g'_i(y+i\delta)\,dy\right)_i\right),\tag{4}$$
extending the densities by zero. Interval boundaries have zero limiting mass. By concavity and positive homogeneity of $h$, (4) is at least the two integrals of $h$ applied pointwise to the respective shifted density vectors. As $\delta\downarrow0$ these integrals converge to $E(f',g')$: translations are continuous in $L^1$, the densities are uniformly bounded on a common compact interval, and $h$ is continuous on the resulting bounded cube (including the zero vector). The choice of partitions may vary with $\delta$; the inequality is valid for every such choice. Thus the unnormalized lower exponent is at least the original $E(f,g)$ on any open neighborhood of its profile's permuton, sending smoothing errors to zero last. The construction works at **every sufficiently large $n$**, not just a subsequence or multiples of a finite template size.

## 5. Normalization and conclusion

The maximum of $E$ is $2\log d$, attained by $f_i=g_i=1/d$. Taking the whole space in the two count bounds yields the exponential growth $d^2$, which is a known consequence of Regev's much sharper bounded-strip asymptotic. Subtracting $2\log d$ gives the open lower and closed upper inequalities with (3). In particular $I_d$ vanishes only at the increasing diagonal permuton, since both entropy maxima require the uniform colour distributions almost everywhere; this agrees with known first-order concentration. No closed evaluation of the optimal profile at a general prescribed permuton is required or asserted.
