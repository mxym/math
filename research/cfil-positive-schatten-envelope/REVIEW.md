# Independent internal review of the two-positive-operator envelope

**Review date:** 18 November 2026. The argument and the independent adversarial review were AI-assisted. This is an internal technical review, not an external referee report, formal verification or priority determination. The checks below found no gap in (1)–(10), the finite-dimensional sharpness assertion for every admissible data triple, or the stated Schatten-class extension. This is about the two positive inputs in [CFIL (5.5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05599), not the many-operator question resolved in 2026.

## An alternative proof of the axis cancellations

Let $k$ be a nonnegative integer. Apply (3) to the polynomial $v^{k+1}$ and compare the coefficient of $s^k t$. On the operator side, all $k+1$ words with one occurrence of $B$ have trace $\operatorname{Tr}(A^kB)$ by cyclicity. On the planar side the binomial coefficient is $k+1$. After canceling it,
$$
(k+1)(k+2)\int x^k y\,d\mu=\operatorname{Tr}(A^kB)=(k+1)(k+2)\int x^k y\,d\nu .
$$
The last equality follows directly from (4). The $x$-marginals of the two **finite** positive measures $y\,d\mu$ and $y\,d\nu$ are supported on a compact interval and have identical moments. Polynomial approximation therefore yields $[y f(x)]=0$ for every continuous $f$. Interchanging $x,y$ gives $[x g(y)]=0$ for every continuous $g$. These identities imply all of (7), including the exponential tests and $[xy]=0$. No derivative under an infinite measure is needed.

## Reconstruction and failure-mode checks

1. The actual [Heinävaara theorem, preprint Thm 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03227) requires local integrability of |f(v)/v|; all univariate test functions used satisfy this (unlike an uncompensated exponential at the origin). The [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-024-01309-6) is in Invent. Math. 239 (2025), 505–526. Coordinate-supported positive tests outside [0,||A||] and [0,||B||] imply the rectangle support, including the possibility of mass on its axes. Deleting the origin from both measures does not change the test integrals. Both weighted first moments are finite, and this suffices for sigma-finiteness off zero.
2. In (5), first subtract 1 in both planar integrals; the radial formula independently gives a subtraction of Tr I in both terms. One may then subtract the integrals. Each exponential of a negative semidefinite matrix is positive definite; the product is not required positive as a matrix, but has nonnegative real trace. Golden–Thompson in its usual trace direction proves that the difference is nonnegative for all s,t≥0. Positive s,t and negative semidefinite exponent arguments are permitted without commutation.
3. The independent moment proof above avoids all axis differentiation. The original Duhamel proof also works: the first derivative of the radial exponential carries a factor $h$ and is uniformly bounded after multiplying by $w(h)$. On the planar side the corresponding coordinate is integrable.
4. The kernel k(v)=exp(−v)−1+v is nonnegative; its exact product expansion in (8) has no unpaired constant at the origin. The compensations from point 3 and the one-variable marginal identities make its bracket exactly L. For 1<r,l<2, the scaling integrals with powers s^(−r−1) and t^(−l−1) are positive and finite at both ends. Integrating two *positive* integrals separately via Tonelli is legitimate; each mixed moment is finite by domination by C(x+y). The same domination uniformly for 1≤r,l≤2 gives the closed-square endpoints. Coefficients outside this square are not claimed. In fact, (9) for **arbitrary** exponents would be false: compare [Cha–Lee, arXiv:2603.19927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19927), at (5,5) where the normalized symmetrized moment exceeds the clustered trace.
5. Check of the sensitive endpoint: the coefficient of s²t² in Tr(sA+tB)^4 is 4Tr A²B²+2Tr ABAB. Hence the (9) defect at r=l=2 is exactly ||AB−BA||_HS²/6, not its negative. The finite-matrix strictness claim in [CHECKS.md](CHECKS.md) follows from this fourth-order coefficient in the *unaveraged* GT power series (positive, proportional to h⁴s²t² for noncommuting matrices), GT nonnegativity at every scale, strict monotonicity of E for 0<u<1, and rank-independent doubling. Noncommutation of positive squares is equivalent to that of their positive square roots. This does not in itself give an infinite-dimensional equality classification from approximation.
6. For p in [2,4], applying [IM Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.02329) to p(p+1) μ is permitted on an arbitrary measure space. The power moments X,Y and trace norm of the *sum* agree exactly, whereas only the desired upper comparison is needed for the overlap. Because E is increasing, substitution of the larger overlap is in the upper-bound direction. IM Corollary 2.3 has exactly the scalar factor in (2), including the factor of two.
7. For doubling, normalize the **2p**-power traces rather than the p-power traces. Applied to A²,B², the exponent-$p$ input then has S=1 and u=Tr(A^p B^p). Norms of AB,BA coincide (adjoints); ALT with exponent p/2≥1 gives Tr(BA²B)^(p/2) ≤ Tr(B^(p/2) A^p B^(p/2)). Cross term in squaring E_{2p} is exactly 2 u^(1/p). This propagates the sharp envelope with no coefficient loss. All exponents p≥2 are reached from the closed base interval. Spectral truncations need not be joint: take the span of the two finite ranges for each index, then S_p and S_2 convergence and continuity of the scalar envelope suffice.
8. The dimension-two diagonal extremizer works for *every* feasible triple, including S>0 with either trace zero or Z=0; |X−Y|≤2Sd by Z²≤XY. For d=0 both inputs may be equal scalars. In each coordinate the two p-powers have the indicated common prefactor, so their root-sum raised to p totals S E_p(u)^p. The result asserts existence of a commuting extremizer, not that all extremizing pairs have dimension two.

## Record of attribution and limits

- [CFIL, §5.2, (5.5) and Theorem 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05599): the conjectured overlap is indeed Tr(B^(p/4)A^(p/2)B^(p/4)), which equals Z; the doubling idea and the positive dyadic cases were already known. Heinävaara [research statement dated 22 November 2023, Question 3.14](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~oeh/research_statement_Otte.pdf) independently calls out this extension after introducing his measure. This is a useful primary reference for both the question and the fact that the isometric embedding alone does not identify the nonlinear term.
- [Audenaert, *Trace inequalities for completely monotone functions and Bernstein functions*, Thm 4 and Cor. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.3057): important **methodological** prior for combining Golden–Thompson, Tr(P_iQ_j) and compensated fractional-power integration. His power estimate for 2≤p≤3 is a **lower** bound with a linear mixed trace, not this sharp upper envelope. [Li–Zhou 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20937) likewise treats complementary McCarthy inequalities. [Dinh 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17782) proves among other things the integral upper mixed moment comparison for integer (n,2), not a fractional interior of the square; it is relevant prior for the endpoint, not a full proof of (9).
- [Chen–Ivanisvili–Madrid–Wang, Thm 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04116): coefficients α fulfil ||AB||_(p/2)≤α²||A||_p||B||_p etc. In the normalized commuting case X=Y=1, the minimum cross coefficient is z^(1/p); its p'-powered coefficient matrix has norm 1+z^(1/(p−1)). At p>2 and 0<z<1 this gives a strictly weaker bound, not (2). [Teng Zhang 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00459) applies the same tracial measure to **two signs / Clarkson–McCarthy** bounds, not this p/2-by-p/2 mixed-overlap envelope. [Dinh–Le–Vuong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31851) use the pinched comparison, not this spectral pairing. No earlier theorem establishing the full result was identified; searches are necessarily finite.
