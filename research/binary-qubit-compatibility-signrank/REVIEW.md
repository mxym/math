## Reproduction of the main argument

1. A complex binary qubit measurement is exactly (E,I−E), 0≤E≤I. Under E=e₀I+e·σ the coordinate e₂ permits imaginary off-diagonal parts: no realness restriction has entered. The determinant is its Minkowski norm; g=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and complement is u−e with u₀=1. Theorem 3, Eq. (55) of [Busch and Schmidt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.4167) applies to all e,f in the qubit effect interval L, not just the spacelike, noncommuting pairs appearing in intermediate lemmas. Its two product terms have minus signs on the original left; moving them and the determinant radicals to the score side gives exactly the stated K≥0. Coexistence of two effects is equivalent to a four-cell parent via intersections of the two designated events. Positivity gives the four-cell operator decomposition in the candidate. No joint parent shared by three or more settings is assumed.

2. In coordinates, the score is the product of the two nonnegative determinant features plus
2 Σ_{i≤j}(2−δ_ij) g_ii g_jj (T_e)_ij(T_f)_ij − (Σ_i g_ii(T_e)_ii)(Σ_j g_jj(T_f)_jj).
Indeed contracting the two symmetrized tensors pairs the four bracketed factors in the two required ways. Thus ten upper-triangular coordinates plus the radical, eleven in total, suffice over the reals; the feature bilinear form may be indefinite. Scaling or swapping the distinguished binary outcome is not silently assumed.

3. Weak inequality K≥0 must be made strict before Forster applies. The candidate's row replacement handles the only rows on which the raising linear coordinate could vanish: trace E=0 or 2 for 0≤E≤I forces E=0 or I. Replacing that row alone by I/2 does not alter any *cross* sign, since the original and replacement pair with all column POVMs. Self-consistency of a replacement in a duplicated set is unnecessary: we are building a real witness matrix, not a new realization. Every nontrivial row now has h(e)=e₀(1−e₀)>0. Choosing 0<δ<min_{K<0}(−K/h) makes every previously zero score strictly positive and preserves all negative entries; if there are no negative entries any positive δ works. Because h is already coordinate T₀₀, all columns remain linear combinations of the same eleven feature columns. This is also valid if a column itself is deterministic and its unshifted feature vector vanishes.

4. The sign-rank of the cross-part compatibility sign matrix is therefore ≤11 for arbitrary finite boxes. [Forster (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0000%2802%2900019-3) gives the square-matrix bound n/‖S‖ (and its rectangular counterpart √(ab)/‖S‖). [Alon, Moran and Yehudayoff, Appendix A](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/alon16.pdf) explicitly repeat the square formulation and the Hadamard consequence. The optional argument is also sound: strict signs persist through small perturbation, Forster position gives Σuᵢuᵢᵀ=(a/r)I for unit rows, and ||U||_F ||S||₂ ||V||_F=√(ab)||S||₂ bounds ΣSᵢⱼ〈uᵢ,vⱼ〉. The inequality between the sum of squares and sum of absolute values uses unit factors.

5. The two parts of the proposed graph are disjoint copies of F₂⁷; thus the distinguished 'zero' row has no loops and can be compatible with all opposite-part measurements. The parity character table satisfies HHᵀ=128I, so any realizing sign table has rank at least ceil(√128)=12, a contradiction. Within-part adjacencies do not participate. Taking no such edges makes the graph triangle-free, so its flag complex just adds the singleton and empty faces. The elementary alternative is valid on all 2¹² anchor neighborhoods: orient a dependence to have at least one negative coefficient, assigning exactly its nonnegative coefficients to positive weak labels yields a strictly positive value for a linear combination that should vanish.

## Independent finite verification

The separate checker uses rational-spectrum effects such that sqrt(det(E)det(I−E)) itself is rational. It checks the signed flattened tensor against the full biased-pair criterion of [Yu, Liu, Li and Oh, Thm. 1, Eqs. (4)–(5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.1538) in a *different* parametrization; only the singular sharp-projection endpoint of Eq. (5) is handled by commutation. It verifies deterministic-row replacement, weak-zero conversion and exact Gaussian-elimination rank after the single shift in the full 59×59 test matrix. There are 3,481 ordered pairs, including 286 zero cells after row replacement. The rank 11 of that *specific real score* does not lower-bound its sign-rank. The checker builds the Sylvester table recursively and independently tests its signs against the binary inner-product definition and its norm via exact orthogonality.

## Scope and precedent

The pair criterion and the spectral bound are established prior theorems. The contribution under audit is the finite-rank feature representation with a boundary-preserving strict sign realization and its explicit quantum incompatibility application. Qualitatively, finite pair-coordinate dimensions and semialgebraic sign-pattern counting already suggest nonuniversality at bounded outcome and dimension, by general classical tools (see [Sauermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11575)). That generic route does **not** furnish the stated 11-dimensional bound or this 256-vertex parity witness. This review does not certify any claim of the first possible witness or the smallest one. The exact 256-vertex consequence is a negative result **only** under the extra binary restriction, fully consistent with [the unrestricted result](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/118). [Andrejić–Kunjwal](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.043147) do not impose two outcomes in Conjecture 4; [Kunjwal, §5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10063) expressly specifies finite measurement sets. [Heunen–Fritz–Reyes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.3698) and [Kunjwal–Heunen–Fritz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5948) allow growing Hilbert dimension in their general realization results. [Grinko–Uola](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07711) and [Yoshino et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38836) concern other binary/coplanar criteria. I searched for sign-rank, Forster/Hadamard and compatibility/incompatibility graph representations of binary qubit measurements without finding this quantum application; this is not an exhaustive bibliographic search. No outside communication or publication was undertaken by this reviewer.
