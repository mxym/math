# Independent review: odd-dimensional one-shot exclusion of projective measurements

**Independent internal AI review:** no mathematical blocker found; not external peer review or priority certification. The reviewed manuscript has SHA-256 `1192c526d2f77f993d302b7dc7ad901af7a46789c54ce1750c5a1f1404823a45`; the public proof changes its opening description and adds explicit credit for the known Gram threshold. The arbitrary-dimension statements are proved analytically, with supplemental finite checks.

## Scope and attribution to prior results

[Manna, arXiv:2510.26255v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26255v2), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ae91a4), §III and §V, concerns one use of a labeled measurement with its classical output retained: no quantum postmeasurement system is returned. The reference is retained when supplied. The §V odd-dimensional question asks to retain nonidentifiability; the preceding sentence distinguishes the measurements and the conditional states for the prescribed probe. The conservative reading in the abstract is nonidentifiability **with that input**. The reviewed proof gives both prescription-specific nonidentifiability for every observed label, and as a separately proved strengthening, nonidentifiability of the entire triple for **any** probe (not necessarily of each pair for every probe). The bases may depend on the input. The positive witnesses are pure entangled states; the even-dimensional Theorems 2–3 explicitly present Schmidt expansions with nonzero coefficients. Nothing here implies a perfect separation for every entangled mixed state or a quantitative advantage for faithful mixed states; the positivity domination remark in the proof is valid.

The threshold is **already explicit** in [Havlíček–Barrett, Phys. Rev. Research 2, 013326 (2020), Eq. (3) and Claim 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01927): three pure states with all squared overlaps at most 1/4 are antidistinguishable, using the earlier [Caves–Fuchs–Schack compatibility conditions](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.66.062111). [Johnston–Russo–Sikora, Quantum 9, 1622 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17047) give both the general Gram decomposition criterion (Theorem 3.2) and the sufficient bound in Corollary 5.4, Eq. (17), which at n=3 again gives absolute overlaps ≤1/2. The proof here uses strict <1/2 for its positive-definite two-coordinate blocks and verifies each effect is nonzero with positive probability on each of the two alternatives. Neither the threshold nor the Gram test is new. The two-dimensional block is Manna’s Theorem 1.

[The 2025 four-scenario measurement-discrimination/exclusion paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.111.022221) and [the qutrit unitary-channel result](https://doi.org/10.1103/d183-k1x3) are different. [Vijayan–Sudarsanan Ragini (Nov. 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04105) study channel-exclusion criteria and include the family of label permutations of a fixed POVM (§III.1); the three working bases are not such a family. A bounded search for the pure-probe odd-dimensional measurement conclusion did not locate an earlier proof. This is not a worldwide priority claim.

## Independent mathematical checks

1. **Product failure:** Condition on each outcome. An independent ancilla only randomizes guesses; achieving zero average error forces at least one label at that outcome to have zero probability, with the all-zero case harmless. A zero diagonal expectation of a positive operator puts the vector in its kernel. Thus a successful product strategy is equivalent to a singular transversal, and every nonsingularity requirement is open. Separable inputs are convex mixtures of products: the same decoder would have to have zero error on each positive-weight component, which is impossible.
2. **All-probe identification:** Writing the arbitrary purifying ancilla vectors as the columns of a matrix A, the marginal in these coordinates is R=(A* A)^T (star is conjugate transpose), and the conditional vector is A conjugate(u). Therefore the Gram entry for x,y is u_y* R u_x. Orthogonality for every label and pair, including any zero vectors, is necessary and sufficient for a zero-error decoder. Compactness of input density matrices proves closedness of feasibility as bases vary. No bound on the ancilla is silently assumed.
3. **Qutrit:** Cayley transform endpoints are I,C,C² at zero, I,I,C at the midpoint, and all I at one. For positive Schmidt weights, normalization is well-defined and the conditional triples at zero are orthonormal. Continuity supplies *for each given spectrum* a neighborhood with strict overlap bound. Rational nonsingularity tests are not required to hold at zero: the transversal determinants are nonzero at one, as are the individual numerator overlaps for every positive spectrum. The six real equations separate Sym(3) at the midpoint by reading its three diagonal and three independent off-diagonal entries. Real parts of a Hermitian prospective R suffice since the basis is real and trace is real. Finitely many roots may be avoided together with the strict small-parameter condition. The summands H_x of the Gram matrix have positive-definite two-index blocks; their pullback is a three-effect exclusion POVM, positive on each nonexcluded input and zero on the excluded one. Extending by positive fractions of identity on the unused complement changes no result.
4. **Qubit:** Each pair of conditional rotated states has inner product c²−s²<0 in the chosen phase convention; neither is orthogonal to the unrotated state because c>0. The two weighted projectors onto s e ± c f sum to the diagonal with entries 1 and c²/s²; adding the remaining effect gives the identity. The possible determinants are 1, cos θ and cos 2θ, all nonzero. Pure Schmidt rank two gives a maximum two-dimensional ancillary span but three nonzero candidate vectors. Schmidt rank one reduces to product, and a successful identification implies successful exclusion by a derangement of the answer.
5. **Direct sums and padding:** Unnormalized strictly positive block weights are handled by dividing the block by its trace; conditional rays are unchanged. An identifying input marginal has some principal block of positive trace, which would contradict that block’s no-identification result. Before mixing, padded labels force their own diagonal entries of R to vanish; all transversals remain invertible. Both impossibilities are open. To avoid wrongly assuming perfect **exclusion** is open in the enlarged class, mix the active anchor and the zero-Schmidt labels using a common orthogonal column action. Projection to the Schmidt support at every mixed label is **exactly** the anchor vector times the corresponding first-row coefficient. Nonzero coefficients of arbitrarily small rotations therefore preserve conditional triples exactly at positive probabilities, simultaneously with the open requirements.
6. **Exact example:** `independent.py` uses rational Cayley inversion, exact determinant computations and positive definite certificates; it imports no candidate verification routines and has no Python `assert` guards. At t=1/1000 it checks three positive Schmidt spectra, 27 transversals and a linear combination of the six identifying constraint matrices equal to identity. It additionally checks a rank-3 probe in dimension five: every mixed posterior ray, all 243 transversals, strict Gram factor tests, and an explicit linear combination of constraints bounded below by I/2 by rational diagonal dominance. Nonreal auxiliary vectors separately test the conjugation/order in the marginal criterion. Run `python -B -O independent.py` (SymPy 1.14.0). These are instances, not a substitute for the compactness and rational nonidentity arguments. The candidate’s verify.py also succeeded without -O; its intermediate assert is not counted as an optimized-mode verification.

No mathematical blocker found. The Gram threshold is prior, the triple may depend on the probe, and exclusion is not identification.
