# Perfect one-shot exclusion of three projective measurements in odd dimension

This AI-assisted research note addresses the **pure-probe odd-dimensional question** in S. Manna, [arXiv:2510.26255v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26255v2), [J. Phys. A 59, 315304 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ae91a4), §V. Manna’s Theorems 1–3 give the preceding even-dimensional constructions. His protocol is a *single use*, with the classical measurement output available but no access to the measured system after the measurement. The unknown setting $x$ has positive prior (equal priors suffice). All conditional states below are on the retained ancilla, not on the measured system.

**Theorem.** For every pure entangled state $\psi$ on $\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d$, $d\ge2$, there exist three ordered rank-one projective measurements (three orthonormal bases, each with the same $d$ classical outcome labels) such that:

1. With the **specified** probe $\psi$, for every classical outcome $a$ the three possible ancillary states have positive probability, can be perfectly excluded with a three-effect POVM, all its effects nonzero, and cannot be perfectly identified. The construction even makes each conditional pair nonorthogonal. Exclusion here means reporting a setting that was not used, not reporting the setting used.
2. No product probe, optimized separately from $\psi$, can perfectly exclude the three settings.
3. No probe at all can perfectly **identify all three** settings, even allowing arbitrary ancillary dimension and mixed probes. This last statement is about joint, not necessarily pairwise, identifiability. There is no claim of a non-perfect advantage bounded away from zero uniformly over Schmidt spectra, or a claim about repeated uses.

The three bases can depend on the prescribed state. The new block is three-dimensional and can be combined with Manna's known two-dimensional block. The proof of point 1 is distinct from the impossibilities in points 2–3.

## Two tests for impossibility

Write $u_{xa}$ for column $a$ of basis $x$ in the fixed input coordinates. On an unentangled probe with reduced input $\sigma$, the outcome probabilities are $u_{xa}^*\sigma u_{xa}$. An independent ancilla gives no extra information about $x$ once $a$ is known. Perfect exclusion is possible iff for every $a$ some $x(a)$ has $u_{x(a),a}^*\sigma u_{x(a),a}=0$ (Manna, Lemma 2). Thus **no product probe works** iff every *transversal* $(u_{x(a),a})_{a=1}^d$ spans: a zero expectation forces the vector to be in $\ker\sigma$, and conversely a vector orthogonal to a nonspanning transversal gives a product probe. Shared randomness or separable mixtures also cannot turn strictly positive failure into zero.

For arbitrary assisted **identification**, pure probes suffice: purify a hypothetical mixed probe with a further retained ancilla; the original protocol would work by ignoring it. For a pure probe $|\Psi\rangle=\sum_j|j\rangle|v_j\rangle$ with input marginal $R$, the subnormalized ancillary vector for outcome $a$ and setting $x$ is $\sum_j\overline{(u_{xa})_j}|v_j\rangle$. The inner product of the vectors for $x$ and $y$ equals $u_{ya}^*R u_{xa}$. For a fixed classical $a$, perfect identification requires all the nonzero conditional vectors to be orthogonal (a zero vector imposes no constraint). Consequently, necessity is
$$
R\succeq0,\quad\operatorname{Tr}R=1,\qquad u_{xa}^*R u_{ya}=0\quad (a,\ x\ne y).
$$
Conversely any such $R$ has a purification and permits outcome-by-outcome orthogonal discrimination. There is no ancillary dimension left in this criterion. The set of basis triples admitting such an $R$ is closed, by compactness of the set of density matrices and continuity. Nonexistence of a successful product excluder is likewise open by the finite collection of nonvanishing determinants. These openness statements concern the **two failures only**, not preservation of exact exclusion.

## The qutrit block

Take Schmidt coordinates for the prescribed pure probe, with normalized strictly positive squared coefficients $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3$. Put $L=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)$. When the basis vectors are real, the ancillary vectors for that specific probe are $L^{1/2}u_{xa}$. Let
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&1\\1&0&-1\\-1&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q(z)=(I+zJ)(I-zJ)^{-1}
=I+\frac{2zJ+2z^2J^2}{1+3z^2}.
$$
These are real rotation matrices ($J^*=-J$ and $J^3=-3J$). $Q(0)=I$, $Q(1)=C$ is the cyclic permutation $e_1\mapsto e_2\mapsto e_3\mapsto e_1$, and $Q(-1)=C^2$. Set
$$
U_1=I,\qquad U_2=Q((1-t)(1-2t)),\qquad
U_3=Q((1-t)(6t-1)).
$$

*For the specified probe.* At $t=0$ the three bases are $I,C,C^2$: at every $a$, the three normalized ancillary states form an orthonormal triple. For all $t$ sufficiently close to zero (depending on $L$), all pairwise absolute overlaps at each outcome are $<1/2$ and each such triple remains linearly independent. Probabilities are positive by $L>0$. We may also avoid zero overlaps: the unnormalized inner products $u_{xa}^TLu_{ya}$ are rational functions of $t$ with real coefficients, nonzero at $t=1$ when each equals $\lambda_a>0$. Their zeros are finite. This ensures that no conditional pair, hence no conditional triple, is perfectly identifiable.

For completeness, three independent unit vectors $v_1,v_2,v_3$ with all off-diagonal Gram entries of magnitude $<1/2$ are perfectly excludable with nonzero effects. Write $V=[v_1\ v_2\ v_3]$ as a bijection onto their span and $G=V^*V$. For each missing index $x$, let $H_x$ have its other two diagonal entries $1/2$, its two corresponding off-diagonal entries equal to those of $G$, and all other entries zero. Each nontrivial $2\times2$ block is positive definite, $\sum_xH_x=G$, and $F_x=(V^{-1})^*H_xV^{-1}$ form the required POVM on the span: $F_xv_x=0$, while both other states have positive probability for that effect. Extend to a larger ambient ancilla by splitting the orthogonal complement identity among the effects. The threshold is prior: [Havlíček–Barrett, Eq. (3) and Claim 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01927) record the squared-overlap bound $1/4$ using [Caves–Fuchs–Schack](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.66.062111); [Johnston–Russo–Sikora](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17047) give the Gram criterion (Theorem 3.2) and the absolute-overlap bound $1/2$ for three states (Corollary 5.4, Eq. (17)). The strict version used here displays effects with positive probability on both nonexcluded alternatives.

*Against every product probe.* The 27 transversal determinants are rational in $t$, without real poles. Each is one at $t=1$ (all three bases coincide with $I$). No determinant is identically zero. Avoiding the finitely many zeros ensures all transversals span.

*Against arbitrary assisted identification.* All vectors are real, so the real symmetric matrix $X=\operatorname{Re}R$ of any putative solution satisfies $u_{xa}^T X u_{ya}=0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}X=1$. Take just the six real linear forms, indexed by $a=1,2,3$, for $(x,y)=(1,2)$ and $(1,3)$. Their determinant in the six real symmetric coordinates is a rational function. At $t=1/2$ the bases are $I,I,C$; the six forms read the three diagonals and $X_{12},X_{23},X_{31}$. The determinant is then $\pm1$, so away from finitely many exceptions the six equations force $X=0$, a contradiction.

All three conclusions hold simultaneously for sufficiently small positive rational $t$, avoiding finitely many zeros. One may in particular search $t=1/n$; an exact example for $\lambda=(1,2,3)/6$ is $t=1/1000$ (see verify.py). The argument for general positive $\lambda$ is analytical, not inferred from this example.

## The qubit block (Manna's Theorem 1)

In a two-dimensional Schmidt block with positive coefficients $s_0,s_1$, take the eigenbasis and its real planar rotations through $\theta,-\theta$, where $0<\theta<\pi/2$ and $\tan\theta>\max(s_0/s_1,s_1/s_0)$. At either output, up to signs/order, the conditional states are $e$ and $c e\pm s f$ with $e,f$ orthonormal, $c^2+s^2=1$ and $0<c<s$; the new coefficient ratio is $(s_{\rm other}/s_{\rm current})\tan\theta>1$. Take the effect for excluding $e$ to be $(1-c^2/s^2)|f\rangle\langle f|$ and effects for the other two states to be $(2s^2)^{-1}$ times the projectors onto $s e\mp c f$, respectively. They are nonzero, positive, sum to $I$, and each excludes the prescribed label. Every conditional pair has nonzero overlap. A transversal determinant is $\cos(\alpha-\beta)$ with $\alpha,\beta\in\{0,\theta,-\theta\}$; all are nonzero since $\pi/4<\theta<\pi/2$. To identify the three settings, a pure probe with input marginal of rank two gives three nonzero conditional vectors in a two-dimensional Schmidt span for each output, hence cannot work. A rank-one input marginal makes the probe product and identification would imply exclusion (derange the guessed label), already ruled out.

## Assembling dimensions and handling zero Schmidt coefficients

On a Schmidt support of dimension $k\ge2$, partition the coordinates into pairs if $k$ is even, or one triple and pairs if it is odd. Form the three global bases as the corresponding direct sums of bases just built, using distinct output labels for the blocks. Given a label, the prescribed probe steers the ancilla into precisely its corresponding Schmidt block. This proves the first claim on the active support. A global transversal is a block-diagonal collection of invertible block transversals. For identification, every positive-trace principal block of a prospective $R$ would, on normalization, satisfy its local identification constraints because each $u_{xa}$ is supported in a single common block. No positive-trace block works; since the traces add to one there cannot be such an $R$. Off-block coherences do not contribute to these equations.

If the ambient input dimension $d$ exceeds $k$, first pad all three bases with the same orthonormal complement basis. This preserves nonsingularity of every transversal, and every new common outcome forces $R_{jj}=0$ if perfect identification were possible. Thus both failures persist. At this interim stage, the new outcomes have zero probability for the prescribed probe. To ensure *all* $d$ outcomes have positive probability and retain all the conditional properties, fix one active label $a_*$. In each of the three bases, mix its $a_*$ column and the $d-k$ new columns by the **same** real orthogonal matrix $O$ close to identity whose entire first row is nonzero (for instance a product of small rotations between the first coordinate and each remaining coordinate). The physical basis rotations can depend on $x$ through its old $a_*$ column; they are just definitions of the three rank-one PVMs. The projection of each of the new mixed columns onto the Schmidt support is its first-row coefficient times the old $u_{x,a_*}$. Therefore, for the given probe, every affected outcome has positive probability for each setting and gives exactly the **old** conditional triple up to normalization/phases. Its exclusion measurement still works, not merely approximately. The unchanged labels retain the same properties. Taking $O$ sufficiently close to identity preserves the two open impossibility conditions proved above. This treats all pure entangled states, not just full Schmidt rank. The probe's local Schmidt bases can of course be chosen separately on the two subsystems.

## Scope note: mixed inputs are a different question

Manna's stated odd-dimensional future problem concerns pure entangled probes. The introduction's broader wording also asks about all entangled states as a possible analogue of Piani–Watrous. The latter's discrimination result concerns a *quantitative advantage*, not perfect measurement exclusion. A faithful mixed entangled state cannot provide the above zero-error separation for any channels. Indeed, if $\rho_{AB}\ge c\,\sigma_A\otimes\sigma_B$, $c>0$, positivity gives the same domination after each $\Phi_x\otimes{\rm id}$. If the exclusion error on $\rho_{AB}$ vanishes with a given decoder, all the nonnegative product-probe contributions also vanish, contradicting product failure. For faithful $\rho$ one may use normalized identity factors. This elementary observation is not used in the positive pure-probe theorem; no classification of mixed probes or statement about approximate advantages is asserted.
