# Scope and prior-art notes

**Source and model.** Satyaki Manna, [J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 59, 315304 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ae91a4), [arXiv:2510.26255v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26255v2), revised 10 August 2026. The full v2 PDF checked here has SHA-256 36801c37a7a6e983fc7d5df25e859a5084b1068ae9f87cb870b3766fcdd39566. Some mirrors return v1's different title with the word “pure” missing; the published question and v2 concern the **pure-state odd-dimensional extension**. Section III models a single use without access to the measured system after the measurement. Alice learns the outcome and conditionally decodes with the reference; §III.A Eq. (5), Lemma 2 treat all product inputs, §III.B Eqs. (6)–(8) the assisted optimization; §IV Theorems 1–3 actually construct measurements for the prescribed probe, at equal priors. Section V requires retaining nonidentifiability in odd dimensions. Claims 1–3 in the draft distinguish these quantifiers. The parity question is not the claim that all mixed entangled inputs provide perfect separation. The introductory analogy to [Piani–Watrous](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.102.250501) must not conflate their optimized success advantage with a perfect zero-error task.

**Precedents.** [Manna–Suresh–Kachhawaha–Saha (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.111.022221), [arXiv:2410.10632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10632), separate four operational settings according to access to a reference and the postmeasurement quantum state. Their qutrit example concerns a pair, their examples of discrimination/exclusion advantages are not a three-PVM witness for every prescribed odd-dimensional pure entangled input. The qubit block used here is Manna's 2026 Theorem 1, not a new result. [Puchała–Pawela–Krawiec–Kukulski (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05856) already develop orthogonality/SDP criteria for perfect one-shot identification of von Neumann measurements. The reduced-input orthogonality condition used here follows directly from purifying the probe. [Johnston–Russo–Sikora, Theorem 3.2](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-02-04-1622/) equate excluding pure states with a decomposition of their Gram matrix; [Johnston–Lovitz–Russo–Sikora](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17411) extend such criteria to list classification. In particular, [Havlíček–Barrett, Eq. (3) and Claim 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01927), following [Caves–Fuchs–Schack](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.66.062111), give the sufficient bound of $1/4$ on all three squared overlaps; [Johnston–Russo–Sikora, Corollary 5.4, Eq. (17)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17047) specializes to the same bound at $n=3$. Neither this threshold nor the Gram exclusion criterion is new. We use those ideas in conjunction with a rational qutrit path that gives three different exclusions/impossibilities simultaneously. [Manna–Das Bhowmik (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/d183-k1x3) treat *unitary channels* (quantum output available); their qutrit separation goes in another direction and does not imply this all-probe measurement result.

**New November preprint.** [Vijayan–Sudarsanan Ragini, arXiv:2611.04105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04105), submitted 3 November 2026, was checked in full. They give weak/strong channel-exclusion criteria via process POVMs, support obstructions, generalized measure-and-prepare examples, an unlabeled-POVM permutation criterion and more. Their permutation result (§III.1) concerns the ensemble of *all permutations of the same* fixed POVM. Here the cyclic permutations occur only at the limiting parameter; at the working nonzero parameter the three measurements use genuinely perturbed different bases and satisfy additional probes/identification constraints. Their Choi support/common-noise no-go is related in principle to the elementary mixed-input scope remark, but concerns noise in the channels, not in the probe. The qutrit strict Gram construction here guarantees all three exclusion outcomes occur with nonzero probability, even for each fixed observed label.
