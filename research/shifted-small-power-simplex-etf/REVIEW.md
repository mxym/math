# Independent internal audit: small-power shifted spherical energies

**Verdict: pass.** The argument gives an explicit sufficient interval and classifies all minimizers for the fixed finite real parameters in both parts of Glazyrin–Park Conjecture 5.2. It does not prove a dimension-independent threshold or identify the optimal exponent. This is an AI-assisted internal mathematical review, not external peer review. The proof reviewed before Markdown reformatting had SHA-256 `892be58a16d6f0213e668d23469bc4ce10901c17f1ef92300337304e62c0e714`.

1. **Domain and normalization.** [The primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.06096), §5, fixes a system of size $N$ on the real sphere. Its first kernel is $|s+1/d|^p$, not a function of $|s|$; the second is $|s^2-\alpha^2|^p$. Part (ii) assumes an ETF of size $\ell$ exists; unlike its continuous Theorem 5.1(ii), it does not require the ETF to be maximal. Repetition and zero off-diagonal terms are allowed. Both proposed constructions use multiplicities that differ by at most one; the authors' comparison is an ordered sum, twice the unordered one used in the lemma. The statement quantifies $p_0$ after the parameters are fixed. It does not ask for the uniform phase-transition threshold of Conjecture 5.1.
2. **Clique exclusion and integer margin.** Put $D_{ij}=(1+(\ell-1)y_i\cdot y_j)/\ell$. Replacing each inner product by $(\ell D_{ij}-1)/(\ell-1)$ in the squared norm of a sum of $s$ unit vectors gives $s(\ell-s)/(\ell-1)+2\ell\sum_{i<j}D_{ij}/(\ell-1)$. If $s=\ell+1$ and the absolute values of all the $D_{ij}$ are less than $\eta$, its upper bound is $(\ell+1)(\ell^2\eta-1)/(\ell-1)<0$, impossible. Turán's equality case is needed on the **indices**, not only on distinct points: any graph other than the balanced complete $\ell$-partite graph has at least $M+1$ nonedges. Equality $|D|=\eta$ belongs to the nonedges. Writing $b=4\ell^2(M+1)$, $p_*=[2b(M+1)]^{-1}$ gives $(M+1)(1-2p_*b)=M$. The inequalities $b>\log b$ and $e^{-x}\ge1-x$ make $(M+1)\eta^p>M$ strict, including $p=p_*$.
3. **The extremal graph is not yet the extremal configuration.** Let $h$ be the largest absolute cross-part value. The transversal formula with $s=\ell$ bounds the norm of every transversal sum by $\ell\sqrt h$. Subtracting two such sums which differ only by their representatives in the same part gives distance at most $2\ell\sqrt h$. Thus the defect $1-D$ of a within-part pair is at most $2\ell(\ell-1)h$. This bound is less than one, so $D^p\ge D$ for the exponents in question. There are exactly $M$ such pairs. If $h>0$, one cross-part pair alone costs $h^p\ge\sqrt h>bh>2M\ell(\ell-1)h$, paying strictly more than the total possible within-part defect. The first strict inequality uses $h<b^{-2}$. If $h=0$, both transversal sums vanish; within each part all points coincide and distinct parts have inner product $-1/(\ell-1)$. This also handles singleton parts correctly. If $M=0$, vanishing of the energy itself forces all pairwise contacts. If $N<\ell$, subsets of a simplex give zero; no full equality classification for this degenerate case is needed.
4. **Projector lift and equality.** For unit vectors, $\operatorname{tr}(xx^Tww^T)=(x\cdot w)^2$ and $\|xx^T-I/d\|_F^2=1-1/d$. Taking the Frobenius norm squared of the ETF sum of projectors gives $\alpha^2=(\ell-d)/(d(\ell-1))$. Substitution yields $D=( (x\cdot w)^2-\alpha^2)/(1-\alpha^2)$, with positive denominator since $d\ge2$. The assumed ETF supplies an attainable unlifted candidate. Conversely, the sum of the $\ell$ distinct lifted representatives is zero, hence the sum of their projectors is $(\ell/d)I$. They span and are tight as well as equiangular. Each real rank-one projector has exactly two unit representatives. This proves both directions without a uniqueness claim for ETF equivalence classes.
5. **Prior implications.** The qualitative fixed-size spherical case and the maximal-ETF and orthogonal-ETF subcases should **not** be called wholly new, even including the equality description. The inequality $1-t^q\le(2\arccos t/\pi)^2$, $0\le t\le1$, $0<q\le1/8$, allows one to combine [Lim–McCann, finite incidence Theorem 2.1(a)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.08698) and [Lim–McCann, local angle stability Theorem 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13052). The derivation is recorded in the sources note: convergence of configurations with bounded small-power cost forces a balanced repeated full basis, then the local inequality and pointwise comparison exclude nonbasis minimizers. Diagonal terms do not affect the transfer as $1-1^q=0$. The weighting in the local theorem allows unequal positive basis masses. At fixed $N$, matching labels gives the required strong convergence. This reasoning provides no explicit threshold as a function of the input parameters. For some $N$ one already has much stronger explicit ordinary bounds. It is the lift dimension that matters: it is $\ell$ for part (i) and for a maximal real ETF, but $d(d+1)/2>\ell$ for a nonmaximal ETF. A lower-dimensional orthogonal system is then not a full orthonormal basis in the prior theorem. The orthogonal case instead reduces directly to the ordinary $2p$ power on the original sphere. We located no prior full treatment of nonmaximal, nonorthogonal real ETFs with arbitrary nonzero remainders; this is not proof that none exists.
6. **Independent diagnostics.** `independent.py` checks the Turán margin and the full complement-component characterization by exhaustive labeled graph enumeration through six vertices; it also checks the threshold arithmetic and the affine projector identities over the rationals. Those finite checks supplement the proof; they do not establish a conclusion for all parameters or manufacture an ETF for every parameter pair.
