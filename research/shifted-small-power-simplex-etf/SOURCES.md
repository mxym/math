# Earlier results and what this note adds

**Question.** [Glazyrin and Park, *Repeated Minimizers of p-Frame Energies*, §5, Conjecture 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.06096) ([journal article](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1282702)), asks for sufficiently small positive exponents with the real dimension, frame size and point count fixed. The two kernels are $|s+1/d|^p$ and $|s^2-\alpha^2|^p$. Part (ii) assumes the existence of an ETF, not necessarily a maximal one. The authors themselves suggest sparsity as the mechanism. Their Theorem 5.1 proves a **continuous** result for the simplex and maximal real ETFs on $0<p\le2$; the case of equal numbers of copies ($m=0$) for these two families follows by taking empirical measures. This does not settle finite cardinalities with nonzero remainder. Also see [Park's dissertation](https://repository.gatech.edu/bitstreams/8547891b-4093-442a-b0c4-494ba7e124ff/download), Ch. 3, Conjecture 2.

## Which qualitative conclusions follow from earlier work?

For unit $y$, form the latitude lift $z(y)=(1,\sqrt{\ell-1}\,y)/\sqrt\ell$. Then $z(u)\cdot z(v)=D(u,v)$ and the $\ell$ vertices of any simplex in the theorem map to orthonormal vectors. If the ambient space of $z$ has dimension $\ell$, a minimization theorem for unrestricted vectors in that space gives a lower bound for the constrained $z$-images, and a feasible simplex attains it. If the only unrestricted minimizers are balanced repetitions of orthonormal lines, then the constrained ones have precisely the asserted form: antipodal representatives cannot both lie on the latitude with first coordinate $1/\sqrt\ell$. The lift ambient dimension is $\ell=d+1$ in the spherical case, and $d(d+1)/2$ for the real-projector lift. Thus this argument works directly for maximal real ETFs; it does **not** give a full basis when $d<\ell<d(d+1)/2$. Orthogonal ETFs instead reduce directly to ordinary frame energies with power $2p$ on the original sphere.

**A deduction from Lim and McCann, not a theorem they state in these terms.** The qualitative ordinary small-power conclusion (and its equality cases) at fixed dimension $\ell$ and fixed $N\ge\ell$ follows by combining two prior results. [Lim–McCann, finite orthogonality incidence theorem, Theorem 2.1(a)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.08698), classifies systems with the largest number of orthogonal pairs. [Lim–McCann, *Maximizing expected powers of the angle between pairs of points in projective space*, Theorem 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13052), proves strict local maximality of the squared normalized-angle energy around a measure supported on all lines of an orthonormal basis with positive, not necessarily equal, masses (up to projective symmetries). Here is the transfer:

* Write $t=|x\cdot y|$ and $L(t)=2\arccos(t)/\pi$. For $0<q\le1/8$, one has $1-t^q\le L(t)^2$, with equality at $0,1$. On $[0,1/4]$, $1-L^2\le (4/\pi)\arcsin(t)\le2t\le t^q$, using convexity of arcsine. On $[1/4,1]$, $1-t^q\le4q(1-t)$ by the mean-value theorem whereas $L^2\ge(8/\pi^2)(1-t)$.
* If ordinary nonbasis configurations could cost at most $M$ for arbitrarily small $q>0$, a sequence with $q\downarrow0$ would have a convergent labeled subsequence by compactness. Each nonorthogonal pair in the limit contributes asymptotically one, so the limit has at most $M$ nonorthogonal pairs. The first Lim–McCann result forces it to be a balanced repetition of an orthonormal basis of lines.
* Equal-mass empirical measures converge to this limiting measure in the $W_\infty$ metric by matching labels (individual signs may be aligned). The second Lim–McCann theorem gives strictly less squared-angle energy nearby unless the system is projectively equivalent to the limiting balanced basis. The displayed pointwise comparison then gives strictly more than $M$ in ordinary power energy for any nonbasis system nearby, since both inequalities are equalities for the reference basis. This is a contradiction.

Thus for **all fixed $N$** the qualitative assertions, including equality, in the spherical, maximal-ETF and orthogonal-ETF cases are deductions from prior theorems; the argument above gives no explicit threshold for the exponent. We do not attribute the shifted-kernel statement itself to Lim and McCann. Their discrete large-angle-exponent result and their continuous bounds are different from a dimension-independent bound on small ordinary powers.

**Divisible cardinalities need no new small-power estimate.** In fact the equal-multiplicity lower bound for $m=0$ is immediate even for nonmaximal ETFs and $0<p\le1$: since $|D|\le1$, $|D|^p\ge D$. Formula (1) in the proof gives $\sum_{i<j}D_{ij}=N(N-\ell)/(2\ell)+(\ell-1)\|\sum_i y_i\|^2/(2\ell)\ge M$. Balanced repeats attain equality. Thus the substantive regime for the quantitative finite problem is $m>0$.

**Stronger known subcases.** For $\ell<N<2\ell$, Glazyrin–Park's ordinary frame-energy Theorem 3.2 already yields optimality at power one in dimension $\ell$, and hence for all smaller positive powers since $|t|^q\ge|t|$ on $[-1,1]$ for $0<q\le1$. This transfers via the full-basis lifts above. At a multiple of the dimension the ordinary problem has the much larger known interval $0<q<2$ ([Ehler–Okoudjou](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.0140)). [Xu–Xu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10861) treat ordinary $N=d+1$ configurations, and [Ben-Av et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1539915) give stronger thresholds for ordinary planar systems. These results should not be counted as new consequences of the present bound.

## Claimed scope and search boundary

The explicit $p_*$ in the proof works under a single elementary contact condition and covers, in particular, **nonmaximal nonorthogonal real ETFs**. The projector embedding, the ETF trace formula, and Turán's theorem with its equality case are standard tools; see also [Cohn–Kumar–Minton on projective simplices](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.3188). The exact nonmaximal ETF used in the verifier is an instance of the classical symmetric conference-matrix construction, not a new frame. No optimal threshold, new ETF existence theorem, global priority, or solution of the Fejes Tóth angle conjecture or Glazyrin–Park Conjecture 5.1 is asserted.

[Park (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32973) proves support and stability results for measures minimizing the ordinary kernel. [Bilyk et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12283) consider three-point energies. Neither is a treatment of the fixed-$N$, shifted two-point comparison above. A search on 18 November 2026 included the original title, authors and citing literature; variants of repeated simplex, ETF and frame-potential terminology; angle powers; and small-power sparsity. No earlier full result for the nonmaximal nonorthogonal case with arbitrary nonzero remainders was identified. This is a bounded search, not proof of absence.
