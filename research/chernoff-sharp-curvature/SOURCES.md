# Primary sources, prior results and limits of the screen

- F. Balabdaoui and J. A. Wellner, [*Chernoff’s density is log-concave*](https://doi.org/10.3150/12-BEJ483), Bernoulli 20 (2014), 231–244. [Full text, §3, Conjecture 3.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3993999/). The introduction and the numbered conjecture specify the threshold by the curvature **at zero**. They already established ordinary log concavity and gave numerical and partial evidence for the sharper statement. Their numerical value 3.4052 is rounded; its approximate numerical identification is not new here.
- X. Zhang and Q. Zhou, [*Chernoff’s Density Is Strongly Log-Concave*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18619), July 2026, [v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18619v1), equations (1)–(3), (9) and Theorem 1 (4)–(7). They prove strict positivity everywhere and the quantitative tail estimate $\kappa(x)>4|x|$, hence a global positive curvature lower bound. Their theorem does not identify its infimum with $\kappa(0)$. This note **uses their theorem** outside a compact interval. The present author should not be confused with Xianyang Zhang. Only v1 appeared in the inspected submission history.
- P. Groeneboom, [*Brownian motion with a parabolic drift and Airy functions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00343738) (1989), supplied the Airy representation. P. Groeneboom and J. A. Wellner, [*Computing Chernoff’s distribution*](https://doi.org/10.1198/10618600152627997) (2001), developed previous methods for numerical density and distribution-function evaluation. Neither the Chernoff factorization nor deriving values by Fourier inversion is an innovation of this work. [DLMF §9.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.2) gives the Airy differential equation and its initial values; [§9.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.9) describes the real negative Airy zeros.
- In the standard convention $Z=\arg\max(W(t)-t^2)$ the present candidate supplies the missing **sharp** global curvature constant, uniquely attained at the origin, by rigorous compact-interval enclosure combined with the existing tail theorem. The proof is computer-assisted with transparent integer arithmetic; this is not a formally verified proof in a proof assistant or a purely analytic derivation.
