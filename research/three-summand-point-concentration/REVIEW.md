The conditional height function sums to one over integer locations. Picking the k highest locations and the (k+1)st level as a threshold proves the exact linear extremum under one cap at any fixed target; ties, finite support, zero residual, and deterministic variables do not require any limiting argument. Sequential marginal replacements preserve independence; expanding each as a difference of nested indicators gives exactly the nonnegative weights of the eight rank boxes.

In two chains the uniform slice is a uniform interval whose endpoints advance by zero or one. In three chains the third-coordinate ordered likelihood ratio increases by log-concavity of the two-chain trapezoid, and the other-two sum has the same sequence of masses truncated to a sliding interval, hence both are stochastically increasing. Coupling by common quantiles makes the third coordinate advance by zero or one and likewise the remaining rank. The nested two-chain coupling therefore yields a path uniform at each rank and the LYM inequality. Symmetry and unimodality give the central rank height, and unit strict drop gives uniqueness of that maximal rank. The coefficient of (1−x) times the product at centered K is one if all widths ≤K (no two numerator shifts contribute), and the longest width otherwise covers both target ranks uniformly; thus the even-box drop is exactly zero or one. The reflected canonical intervals move the midpoint by +1/2 for each changed bit relative to the chosen sacrifice, so one corner loses exactly its drop and all others are at a maximizing rank.

In the only nontrivial case all four even-box weights and drops are positive. If they all achieved maximum, LYM forces every centered triple in each. When three widths are positive, stepping by opposite adjacent coordinates gives equality of the increments on the indicated rank-index band. Adjacent increments of one coordinate have a common neighbor in the second because c≥1, a≤K, b≥K−c; every other increment has an incident neighbor, and switching pairs propagates the common step to all coordinates. Corners of the same parity differ in two coordinates and share a nontrivial array, propagating the step when all short widths are positive. For exactly one zero short width, the two corners with this coordinate enlarged suffice and share a two-point array. When two short widths vanish but not the third, one parity corner violates balance so already has zero drop. If all three short widths vanish, the three pair corners equate gaps directly. Nested consecutive-size progressions then extend at a single end, shifting each midpoint by ±h/2; the four center equations are inconsistent on both bit parities. Thus some integral-center count loses at least one, giving exactly the minimum weighted loss in the formula. Scope is three arbitrary heterogeneously capped integer variables and the largest atom, not arbitrary many summands or an interval concentration inequality.
