# Original question, prior bounds and scope

- Valentas Kurauskas, [*An asymptotically optimal bound for the concentration function of a sum of independent integer random variables*, arXiv:2603.11043v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11043v1), pp. 2–6, attributes Conjecture 1.3 to Tomas Juškevičius (reference [13], personal communication, 2023). The paper defines the canonical consecutive high-mass atoms and one endpoint remainder, with a separate sign choice for each summand. Theorem 1.1 gives a multiplicative $1+\delta$ upper bound when the sum of the canonical variances exceeds a threshold depending on $\delta$; it is not the exact finite three-variable statement. Definition 2.1 and the discussion of extremal distributions already isolate capped laws of the shape used in §1 of the present note. The new work is not the discovery of those marginal shapes.
- The uniform-set extremum is classical: see Vsevolod F. Lev, [*On the Number of Solutions of a Linear Equation over Finite Sets*](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1998.2878), JCTA 83 (1998), 251–267. Gabriel's discrete rearrangement inequality as formulated in Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya, *Inequalities*, Theorem 374, is restated in Kurauskas, Theorem 2.16: apart from two marginals, the formulation requires symmetric decreasing rearrangements. A freely placed single residual atom need not satisfy that hypothesis. The separate uniform-box bound in our proof is classical; the sharp weighted cost of imposing a shared target across the eight nested boxes is the extra issue.
- Mokshay Madiman, Liyao Wang and Jae Oh Woo, [*Majorization and Rényi Entropy Inequalities via Sperner Theory*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2019.03.002), Theorem 1.4, controls ordered support compression when the sequence of masses in physical support order is log-concave (including two-point and uniform laws). A residual atom between two larger atoms need not be log-concave in that order. Kurauskas, pp. 3–4, explains the existing exact case when every reciprocal cap is either in $[1,2]$ or an integer. Sperner methods, products of chains and the corresponding single-box antichain bound are prior tools. For the reduction to extreme capped marginals also compare Madiman, Melbourne and Roberto, [*Bernoulli sums and Rényi entropy inequalities*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00896), §4.
- Tomas Juškevičius and Valentas Kurauskas, [*On the Littlewood–Offord problem for arbitrary distributions*, authors' paper](https://web.vu.lt/mif/v.kurauskas/files/2020/08/general_200704.pdf), Random Structures & Algorithms 58 (2021), 370–380, obtain sharp common-cap results for even numbers of summands. Kurauskas 2026, Lemma 2.17, also gives an exact result under strong balance. Neither the paired equal-cap hypothesis nor the reciprocal/Bernoulli cases describe all distinct nonreciprocal caps for three summands.
- Aistis Atminas and Valentas Kurauskas, [*Rearrangements of distributions on integers that minimize variance*, v2, Introduction and (1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07899v2), discuss an inequality about concentration after rearranging **given mass spectra**; their proved theorem optimizes **variance**. Our result optimizes the point concentration over all laws subject to given caps and does not establish the general fixed-spectrum rearrangement assertion. Hajaiej, Han and Hua, [*Discrete Schwarz rearrangement on lattice graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70583), [Theorems 5.7 and 5.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.01003v2), treat nonincreasing distance kernels; that is not three freely placed, nonsymmetric capped marginals.
- The [sharp Kolmogorov–Rogozin inequality and Leader–Radcliffe conjecture](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.13132) concern interval concentration, a distinct problem with generally different extremizers. No interval, higher-dimensional, or arbitrary-number-of-summands theorem is claimed here.
