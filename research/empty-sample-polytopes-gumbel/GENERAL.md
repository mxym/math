# Poisson exceedances and Gumbel maxima for every fixed number of vertices

Fix $d\ge2$, $k\ge d+1$, $m=k-d$, and a full-dimensional compact convex body $K$. Normalize $|K|=1$; equivalently all hull volumes below mean fractions of $|K|$. In an iid uniform $n$-point sample, a $k$-hole is an unordered $k$-tuple in convex position whose hull contains no other sample point in its interior. Let $M_{n,k}$ be the maximum volume of such a hole (zero if none). For every fixed $x\in\mathbb R$ put
$$
 z_n=d\log n+(m-1)\log\log n+x,\qquad v=z_n/n,\qquad
 \lambda=c_{d,k,K}\frac{d^{m-1}}{\Gamma(m)} e^{-x}.
$$
Here $c_{d,k,K}$ is the established coefficient of $n^d$ in the mean number of **all** $k$-holes. The number of $k$-holes of volume greater than $v$ converges in law to $\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$. Thus $\Pr(nM_{n,k}-d\log n-(m-1)\log\log n\le x)\to e^{-\lambda}$. This is a one-threshold limit for each fixed $k,d,x$, not a joint point-process statement. It specializes to the simplex law, including $\lambda=2e^{-x}$ for planar triangles. We prove the result directly using the first-moment chart of [EXTREME-COUNTS.md](EXTREME-COUNTS.md).

## 1. A regular truncated family

Write $L=\log n$. For fixed $H>0$, $\eta>0$ restrict to convex-position tuples with volume $V\in(v,v+H/n]$ satisfying both:
1. Every $d$-tuple of vertices has absolute determinant at least $\eta$ after projection to the first $d-1$ coordinates (difference columns, no factorial).
2. Every $d+1$ vertices span a simplex of volume at least $\eta V$.

For the height-polar representation $h=r\theta$ in EXTREME-COUNTS.md, condition 1 is independent of $h$, and condition 2 is invariant under dilation of all heights: each simplex volume and hull volume acquire the same factor $r$. Consequently these cutoffs give another bounded density factor $q_\eta(V)$ with $q_\eta(V)\to q_\eta(0)$, by the same section-boundary argument. Further $q_\eta(0)\uparrow q(0)$ as $\eta\downarrow0$. Indeed every fixed projection minor is nonzero almost everywhere: for generic absolute-continuous anchors and horizontal coordinates these are nonzero determinant polynomials. Conditional on such projections, the full determinant of any fixed $d+1$ labels is a nonzero linear form in $\theta$: there is at least one nonanchor label in it, and its height coefficient is its complementary nonzero projection minor, up to a common coordinate factor and sign. Its zero set on $S^{m-1}$ is null (also for $m=1$ where it is empty). There are finitely many conditions. This reasoning concerns the normalized limiting chart, not the original physically flattened limiting tuple. The integrands are bounded in a fixed finite integration volume. Positivity of the prior $c$ shows that $q_\eta(0)>0$ for sufficiently small $\eta$.

Let $W$ count regular truncated empty hulls in a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity $n$ on $K$. Its mean is
$$
 \lambda_n=\frac{n^k}{k!}\int_v^{v+H/n} V^{m-1}q_\eta(V)e^{-nV}dV
 \ \longrightarrow\ \lambda_{\eta,H}=\frac{d^{m-1} q_\eta(0)}{k!}e^{-x}(1-e^{-H}).
$$
This follows by setting $V=v+y/n$; the untruncated mean tends to $\lambda$ by the same argument over $y>0$, using the domination of EXTREME-COUNTS.md. The binomial analogues have identical limits and $c=q(0)\Gamma(m)/k!$. In the remainder the constants may depend on $K,d,k,x,H,\eta$ but not $n$.

**Slabs and shells.** Choose any $d$ labels as anchors. Their lifted points lie on a vertical graph $y=b+\alpha\cdot x'$ of bounded slope by condition 1 and the bounded body. Each other vertical height relative to this plane has absolute value at most $C V$, since the absolute height times the anchor projection determinant is $d!$ times a contained simplex's volume. The whole hull is in the resulting $O(v)$ slab. For definiteness let its base normal be that of the largest projected anchor, with deterministic tie-breaking.

If the anchor positions and the horizontal coordinates of all other vertices are specified, the **Lebesgue measure of the $m$ remaining vertical heights** achieving the volume band is at most $C v^{m-1}H/n$. Indeed, in the coordinates of the first-moment chart $h_i=J(y_i-b-\alpha\cdot x_i')$ and the volume is $rU(u,\theta)$; $J\ge\eta$ and $U\ge1/(d!\sqrt m)$, even for an anchor that is not the maximal one, because the base simplex with any extra vertex is contained in the hull. Integration of $r^{m-1}dr$ over that band gives the bound, with the Jacobian $J^{-m}$ uniformly bounded. No boundary or convex-position indicator needs to be enforced in this upper estimate.

## 2. Linear recovery of vertices on each regular shape family

Suppose $R$ is a convex polytope with exactly $k$ vertices satisfying condition 2. Normalize it to $P$ by an affine map taking any fixed $d+1$ of its vertices to the standard simplex. In these coordinates the labeled vertex tuples lie in a compact bounded region: each extra vertex's coordinates are ratios of signed simplex volumes by Cramer's rule, with numerator at most $|R|$ and denominator at least $\eta|R|$. The normalized volume is between $1/d!$ and $1/(d!\eta)$. Condition 2 is affine invariant, so all normalized full minors are bounded away from zero, also in every subsequential limit. In particular the limiting configurations retain their $k$ vertices: the signs of all full determinants are eventually fixed at their nonzero limiting values. The $d$-subsets that are facets are exactly those for which all other vertices lie strictly on one side. The facet lists, hence the labels of extreme vertices, therefore persist. Condition 1 need not be affine invariant and is **not used** in this vertex-recovery assertion.

For any other polytope $S$ with exactly $k$ vertices, if $e=|R\triangle S|/|R|$ is sufficiently small, in the normalized coordinates its vertices can be matched to those of $P$ with all distances at most $C_{d,k,\eta}e$. To prove uniformity, consider any sequence of normalized $P_i$ and competitors $S_i$ with $e_i\to0$. Pass to a convergent vertex tuple for $P_i$ as above, with limit $P_*$. The $S_i$ eventually contain a fixed ball by separation, using an interior ball of the fixed standard simplex in all the $P_i$: omitting a point of a smaller ball cuts off half a fixed-radius ball. Their bounded volumes then bound their diameters by cones from a disk of that fixed ball. Extract a convergent subsequence of their labeled vertex tuples. The limiting hull equals $P_*$, since at every point outside the two limiting boundaries the respective hull indicators converge, these boundaries are null, and $|P_i\triangle S_i|\to0$. Equivalently, this follows from convex Hausdorff convergence and the common interior ball. Since the limit $P_*$ has exactly $k$ extreme vertices, each must be a distinct limiting vertex of $S_i$, and relabeling matches the two limiting tuples. The matching facets are eventually the same $d$-subsets, by the nonzero full determinant signs.

For each facet of $P_*$ take a fixed small disk $Q$ in its relative interior, and a short two-sided normal cylinder above it. All other facet inequalities are strictly satisfied there, also for $P_i,S_i$ at large $i$. The two matching planes are graphs of affine functions $g_i,\tilde g_i$ over the limiting facet plane, with graph heights inside the cylinder on $Q$. The symmetric difference in the cylinder has volume $\int_Q|g_i-\tilde g_i|$, at most $|P_i|e_i$. This integral defines a norm of the affine-function difference and controls every coefficient. At each limiting vertex there exist $d$ incident facets with linearly independent normals: if not, a line orthogonal to their normal span would contain a small segment through the point satisfying all constraints, contrary to extremality. The corresponding linear systems remain uniformly invertible along the subsequence. Thus the two nearby vertex solutions differ by $O_{P_*}(e_i)$, including at vertices with more than $d$ incident facets. A contrary sequence with unbounded ratio of optimal matching error to $e_i$ would have just such a subsequence, a contradiction. This proves the stated uniform linear bound.

In original coordinates the union of these $k$ possible vertex neighborhoods has volume $O(|R|e^d)$: take the inverse image of the $k$ balls of radius $Ce$ about the normalized vertices, whose inverse Jacobian is $|R|/|P|\le d!|R|$. This estimate controls *free* vertices even when several vertices are shared between the hulls.

## 3. The planted-hull size-bias coupling

Draw an independent ordered regular truncated tuple with density proportional to $e^{-n|R|}$ (its convex hull is $R$). Delete Poisson points in its interior and insert its $k$ vertices. The number $W^s$ of regular truncated empty hulls after this change includes $R$ and has the size-biased law of $W$: $\mathbb E[WF(W)]=\lambda_n\mathbb E[F(W^s)]$. This is the Poisson tuple identity summed over a distinguished tuple; its inside is void with probability $e^{-n|R|}$ and the outside process stays independent. In the finite window the identity follows immediately by conditioning on total Poisson count and using $(j)_ke^{-n}n^j/j!=n^k e^{-n}n^{j-k}/(j-k)!$. For fixed distinguished vertices the remaining Poisson points have no boundary coincidences a.s.; one only needs upper bounds when a shared planted vertex is on a boundary.

We estimate the expected count of lost old hulls plus newly created ones other than $R$, uniformly conditional on each admissible distinguished $R$. We use repeatedly
$$
 n^k e^{-nv}\,v^{m-1}/n=O(1),\qquad nv\asymp L .
 \tag{1}
$$

**Lost hulls.** An old hit can be lost by deletion of a vertex in $R$ or by an inserted blocker. For an arbitrary measurable $D\subset K$, the old expected count with a specified vertex in $D$ is $O(|D|)$. Include this label among the $d$ anchors, integrate it over $D$, integrate other anchors and the free horizontal coordinates in bounded ranges, and apply the shell bound $C v^{m-1}H/n$ in the free vertical variables. Multiply by $O(n^k e^{-nv})$ and use (1). For a fixed $z\in K$, old expected hits containing $z$ are $O(v)$. In the same tuple integral, condition on all horizontal coordinates and write the first $d$ vertical coordinates as $y_i=b+\alpha\cdot x_i'$, Jacobian the absolute projected minor, bounded above. Write the remaining vertical coordinates as their displacements from that graph. All are $O(v)$, and their joint shell has measure $O(v^{m-1}H/n)$ independent of $b$ in the upper estimate. To contain $z$ one needs $|z_d-b-\alpha\cdot z'|\le Cv$, so the integration in $b$ contributes $O(v)$; horizontal coordinates and $\alpha$ range in fixed bounded sets. Equation (1) gives the result. Summing over the finitely many vertices, the old losses thus have expectation $O(v)$.

**Far new competitors.** Call $S$ near if it meets the closed $R$ and their unoriented base normals have angle at most $vL^3$; otherwise call it far. For far $S$ write $I=|R\cap S|$. Uniformly $nI=o(1)$: if the hulls meet, projection in the two-normal plane bounds their slab overlap in the bounded body by $C v^2/\sin\angle\le C v/L^3$; otherwise $I=0$. A newly created far hit with no planted vertices must have had an old blocker in $R\cap S$. The expected count is bounded by integration of $n^k e^{-n(|S|-I)}(1-e^{-nI})/k!$ over possible admissible tuples: the outside and inside Poisson restrictions are independent, and all inserted-point exclusion conditions can be dropped for an upper bound. This is $o(1)$ times a bounded original expected count.

If there are $1\le j\le d$ shared planted vertices, fix their roles and take all $j$ among the $d$ anchors; there are $d-j$ free anchors and $m$ further free vertices. For each of the last $m$ points, its vertical variable ranges over length $O(v)$ given the base and its horizontal coordinate, by the determinant cutoff. Ignoring the narrower shell and using the far survival bound, the integral is at most
$$
 C n^{k-j}v^m e^{-nv+o(1)}=O(n^{-j}L)
$$
using the definition of $z_n$. For $d<j<k$ choose $d$ of the planted points as anchors. All $k-j$ free vertical variables are then confined to $O(v)$, giving $O((nv)^{k-j}e^{-nv+o(1)})=o(1)$. The finitely many role assignments cost only a constant. All $k$ shared gives the distinguished hull itself. Thus all far creations other than $R$ have vanishing expected count.

**Near competitors with $0\le j<k$ shared vertices.** Near hulls lie in one slab of the distinguished base of width $O(vL^3)$ by their intersection, bounded diameters, small relative normal angle and the two original slabs. Put $\delta=20k\log L/L$. If $|S\setminus R|\ge v\delta$, the cost of avoiding that region in the modified process is at most $L^{-10dk}$ for large $n$. With each free point in the common slab the expected number is bounded by $C(nvL^3)^{k-j}L^{-10dk}=o(1)$. If instead $|S\setminus R|<v\delta$, then $||S|-|R||\le H/n$ implies $|R\triangle S|/|R|\le2\delta+H/(nv)=O(\delta)$. By §2 all free vertices must be in a set, depending only on $R$, of volume $O(v\delta^d)$. Their expected tuple count is at most $(Cnv\delta^d)^{k-j}=o(1)$, since $d\ge2$ yields $nv\delta^d=O(L^{1-d}(\log L)^d)\to0$. These are upper bounds even for all modified hits in those regions.

It follows that $\mathbb E|W+1-W^s|=o(1)$. For the generating function $F_n(z)=\mathbb E z^W$, $0<z\le1$, size bias gives $F_n'(z)=\lambda_n\mathbb E z^{W^s-1}$. Therefore $|F_n'(z)-\lambda_n F_n(z)|\le\lambda_n\Pr(W^s\ne W+1)=o(1)$ uniformly; integration with $F_n(1)=1$ gives convergence to $\exp(\lambda_{\eta,H}(z-1))$. This proves the truncated Poisson law.

## 4. Fixed sample size; remove truncation

Independently choose the Poisson count on an iid point sequence. With probability tending to one it lies in $[a,b]$ where $a=\lfloor n-n^{2/3}\rfloor$, $b=\lceil n+n^{2/3}\rceil$. The admissibility thresholds still refer to $n$. Across that entire block the expected number of hits using only the first $a$ points and later lost is $o(1)$: the bounded density factor gives $O(1)$ initially by (1) and $(n-a)v=o(1)$, and the probability any later point enters any one hit is at most $(b-a)(v+H/n)=o(1)$. A hit that ever uses a later vertex yields a tuple with at least one label above $a$, of which there are $O((b-a)b^{k-1})$. Its volume falls in the band with probability $O(v^{m-1}H/n)$, and all its first $a$ non-tuple points must avoid it, with conditional probability at most $e^{-(a-k)v}$. By (1) the resulting bound is $O((b-a)/n)=o(1)$. Thus the truncated fixed-size count agrees with the coupled Poisson-size count with probability tending to one.

The binomial mean of discarded hits tends to $\lambda-\lambda_{\eta,H}$ by the restricted and unrestricted first moments. Let $H\to\infty$ and $\eta\downarrow0$; this difference tends to zero, so Markov's inequality removes the cutoffs. This proves the one-threshold count convergence, and the zero-count event gives the maximum law. ∎
