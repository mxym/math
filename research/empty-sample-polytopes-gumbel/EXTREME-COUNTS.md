# A first-moment profile for large empty sample polytopes

Let $K$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body in $\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge2$. Normalize its volume to one by an affine change of coordinates; volumes below mean fractions of $|K|$. The sample consists of $n$ independent uniform points. For fixed $k\ge d+1$ a $k$-hole is an unordered set of $k$ sample points in convex position whose hull contains no other sample point in its interior. Let $c_{d,k,K}>0$ be the limit of the expected total count divided by $n^d$, as in [the leading-constant theorem](../empty-k-holes-leading-limit/PROOF.md). For fixed $x\in\mathbb R$ and $m=k-d$, the expected number of $k$-holes with volume greater than
$$
 v_n=\frac{d\log n+(m-1)\log\log n+x}{n}
$$
tends to $c_{d,k,K}d^{m-1}e^{-x}/\Gamma(m)$. For planar triangles the limit is $2e^{-x}$, since $c_{2,3,K}=2$. This is an expected count only, not a limit distribution.

**Small-volume density.** Use the largest-projected-anchor chart of the leading-constant theorem, without the $n$ rescaling. In a labeled tuple, take the $d$ anchors whose projections onto the fixed first $d-1$ coordinates have largest simplex volume; their index set is a.s. unique. By exchangeability fix its first $d$ labels and multiply by $\binom{k}{d}$. With $b_1,\ldots,b_d$ the anchors, $B=(b_2-b_1,\ldots,b_d-b_1)$, $J=|\det(\pi B)|>0$, each of the remaining $m$ points has coordinates $b_1+B u_i+(h_i/J)e_d$, whose absolute Jacobian is one. The anchor-selection indicator $A(u)$ is independent of the heights, and forces $u_i\in[-1,1]^{d-1}$. Call the volume in these chart coordinates $U(u,h)$ and the convex-position indicator $I(u,h)$; they coincide with physical volume and convex position since this affine change has determinant of magnitude one. In particular $U(u,h)\ge\max_i|h_i|/d!$ by taking the simplex over the anchors.

Write $h=r\theta$, $\theta\in S^{m-1}$ with its surface area measure (two atoms of mass one for $m=1$). For $r>0$, vertical dilation gives $U(u,r\theta)=rU(u,\theta)$ and $I(u,r\theta)=I(u,\theta)$. Change variables from $r$ to $v=rU(u,\theta)$ with Jacobian in the radial measure $r^{m-1}dr=v^{m-1}U(u,\theta)^{-m}dv$. The volume law of a random $k$-tuple, *restricted* to convex position (not divided by its probability), consequently has density $v^{m-1}q(v)$ with
$$
 q(v)=\binom{k}{d}\int_{K^d}\int A(u) I(u,\theta)U(u,\theta)^{-m}
 \prod_i {\bf1}_K\!\left(b_1+Bu_i+\frac{v\theta_i}{J U(u,\theta)}e_d\right)du\,d\theta\,db .
$$
Here the $u$ integral may be confined to the above cube. Since $U(u,\theta)\ge1/(d!\sqrt m)$ and $|K^d|=1$, $q$ is bounded by a constant depending **only on $d,k$**, independently of the body. The limit $q(v)\to q(0)$ as $v\downarrow0$ follows by dominated convergence: almost every anchor is interior, so $E=b_1+\operatorname{im} B$ intersects the interior of $K$; $E\cap\partial K$ is the relative boundary of the full-dimensional section and has zero $(d-1)$-measure. Thus for almost every $u$ the section points avoid $\partial K$ and the indicator products converge. This is the same section argument as in the leading-constant theorem. No uniform rate of this convergence across bodies is required.

Conditional on a tuple of volume $v$, emptiness has probability $(1-v)^{n-k}$, with equality of boundary events immaterial almost surely. Thus the total expectation is $\binom nk\int_0^1 v^{m-1}q(v)(1-v)^{n-k}dv$; rescaling $t=nv$ gives $c_{d,k,K}=q(0)\Gamma(m)/k!$ by dominated convergence, since $(1-v)^{n-k}\le e^k e^{-nv}$. For the restricted count, integrate from $v_n$ instead. Put $s=nv_n$ and $nv=s+y$; extend the integrand by zero outside $0\le y\le n-s$. After factoring out $\binom nk n^{-m}s^{m-1}e^{-s}$, the integrand tends pointwise to $q(0)e^{-y}$ because $s^2/n\to0$. It is dominated by $C_{d,k}e^k(1+y)^{m-1}e^{-y}$ for $s\ge1$. Since the extracted factor tends to $d^{m-1}e^{-x}/k!$, the stated expectation follows. ∎
