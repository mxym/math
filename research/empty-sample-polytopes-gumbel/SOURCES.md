# Scope, sources and checks (19 November 2026 UTC)

**The statistic matters.** Here $M_{n,k}$ is the maximum volume fraction of a convex hull with exactly $k$ sample vertices, empty of the rest of the independent uniform sample in its **interior**. Dimension and vertex count are fixed. The simplices are not required to be Delaunay, homothetic to a fixed simplex, or facets of any prespecified tessellation. The count convergence is at each fixed threshold, not a joint point-process theorem. The pathwise bounds in ALMOST-SURE.md concern simplices only.

**Direct predecessors and proper credit.**
- [Arizmendi–Salazar, *Large Area Convex Holes in Random Point Sets*, SIAM J. Discrete Math.; Thms 1.1–1.3 and §6](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1024184), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04307): planar leading-order bounds for different empty-set classes including sample polygons. Their leading-constant question is prior motivation, not an attributed conjecture of the exact Gumbel law or either pathwise second-order endpoint.
- [Peres–Yang, arXiv:2610.09790v2, 13 October 2026, Thm 1.3 and §3.3, eq. (3.7)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09790v2): unrestricted empty convex bodies for independent points; almost-sure leading coefficient $d$ and polynomial probability bounds for fixed relative deviations. The theorem allows arbitrary vertices, and does not state the sample-vertex one-threshold law at additive scale $1/n$ or the fixed-$k$ log-log centering. Its leading and quantitative results are not claimed here.
- [Balko–Scheucher–Valtr, *Tight bounds on the expected number of holes in random point sets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12533v2), and [Reitzner–Temesvari, *Stars of empty simplices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.08734): total expected counts/leading constants, including the planar simplex constant and fixed-body simplex limits; not the large-volume exceedance distribution.
- [Owner #221](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/221): earlier limiting $n^{-d}$ total mean for fixed $d,k$. The bounded volume-density calculation here deliberately reuses its largest-projected-anchor chart and boundary nullity; the first-moment profile is a refinement of that argument. [Owner #231](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/231) already proved the sample-vertex a.s. leading limit using thin targets, angular separation, corner localization and Borel–Cantelli. The a.s. log-log control adapts that construction with matched corner occupancies. The dependence proof in GENERAL.md is needed separately for a one-threshold limiting law.
- [Janson, *Maximal Spacings in Several Dimensions*, Ann. Probab. 15 (1987), 274–280](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176992269): a classical limit law for greatest empty homothets of a specified shape, without the present sample-vertex condition. [Chenavier, *A general study of extremes of stationary tessellations with examples*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5675) concerns extremes of tessellation cells; enforcing an empty circumball in the Delaunay model is stronger than an empty simplex.
- The tuple integration, Palm construction and Poisson approximation framework are standard: [Last–Penrose, *Lectures on the Poisson Process*, Chap. 4](https://stoch.math.kit.edu/img/Last/lastpenrose2017.pdf); [Bobrowski–Schulte–Yogeshwaran, *Poisson process approximation under stabilization and Palm coupling*](https://doi.org/10.5802/ahl.156). Related [Schulte–Thäle Poisson-driven order statistics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.5282) treat scores from fixed tuples: whether our simplex is empty also depends on points **outside that tuple**. The issue handled directly here is the near/far and shared-vertices contribution under planting, not a new Mecke or Stein principle.

**Late-2026 comparison.** Searches through 19 November, repeated specifically for July–November, included largest empty sample simplex/triangle/polygon; large-area convex holes with vertices in the sample, extremes for empty polygons with fixed vertex count and large-$k$-hole volumes; volume exceedances, Poisson/Gumbel, second order/log-log, maximal spacings and Delaunay extrema. We checked the current arXiv history and the text of Peres–Yang's October v2, in particular §§1.2 and 3.3; it distinguishes unrestricted bodies and the classical fixed-shape results itself. Earlier and later primary statements returned by those searches were compared by emptiness region, vertex constraint and normalization. The unrestricted comparison was also checked against Peres–Yang §§1.2 and 3.3 and Arizmendi–Salazar §§4–6: these describe first-order unrestricted or sample-polygon estimates, not the all-fixed-$k$ distributional formula. No matching fixed-$k$ Gumbel theorem was located. This search cannot rule out unpublished, unindexed or unrecognized equivalent results.
