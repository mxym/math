# Independent review: Watanabe comparison for flat-off-a-hub covers

**PASS** for the frozen proof (SHA-256 `dcabe02f531b7bee511cf68c7615e4e9e71167247f30a236d0f61a0a421b2e0d`). The sharpness assertion is for constant *inclusion ranks*, not arbitrary constant raw occupations. AI internal mathematical review, not external peer review or a priority determination.

1. **Gauge and conditional independence.** Use the bipartition with the hub on the left, complement the occupied sets on the right, and choose a gauge on the whole hub complement from the flatness hypothesis. Conditional on the hub set A, the exact remaining problem consists of identity inclusions within each component and boundary conditions that the right-neighbor sets contain the transported images of A. Components are independent given A. Their ordinary completion counts are individually independent of the particular fixed hub set by simultaneous relabeling inside the component.

2. **Compression.** Fix off-pair memberships at every vertex for two labels i,j. Terms with a failed off-pair inclusion cannot be revived by this shift, and an already exceeded rank makes both terms infeasible. On the remaining pair, enforce the necessary inequalities of ranks (0,1,2); when they hold, only edges joining two rank-one vertices require a choice, and their choices must agree. A boundary that requires both labels at rank one is infeasible before and after; rank zero and rank two boundary cases are unchanged. For single demands the shift aligns all forced colors toward i, so it cannot lose any contribution in a component. Repeated downward shifts reach common nested requirements since the sum of label indices strictly decreases at effective steps.

3. **The last shift.** In a component with initially disagreeing demands, consider the first shift after which all demands agree (not necessarily the end of the complete sorting procedure). The preimages of their common value are exactly the version with i and the one with j, with the same off-pair set B; both versions occur. For each ordinary reduced completion at the pinned B, every vertex has exactly one outstanding label. Connectedness of this component makes them a single agreement component: zero orientations before, exactly one afterwards. All other terms are nondecreasing. The reduced completion count is positive by nested sets, since full ranks and the boundary rank were interior and all edge inequalities persist on reducing every inclusion rank by one. On the original right raw occupancies this is (M−a_v)−1=(M−2)−(a_v−1), as required. Choice of i,j and B may vary from one component to another: their **counts** do not, by relabeling. A common full boundary has at least the reduced count, by adjoining i throughout. No particular coupled injection between different choices of removed labels across the components is needed.

4. **Product and orbit.** For a conflicting component 0, write its ordinary/gained pinned counts as A₀,B₀, and the other two products as A',B'. The difference is (A₀−B₀)A'+B₀(A'−B'), with both terms nonnegative. This proves the precise direction of the frozen bound. A closed walk decomposes into excursions from the hub; inside each complement component all edge maps are identities, so the returning maps are exactly generated by the relative boundary permutations. An invariant hub set gives agreement in each component; different agreement sets in different components are harmless. For the other hub sets at least one component conflicts. The reduced ordinary coefficient is the common pinned count times binom(M−2,p−1), including binom(0,0)=1. Consequently summing the pinned inequalities yields exactly (1).

   A set invariant under the whole monodromy group is a union of its label-orbits. Counting sets containing exactly one of a chosen same-orbit pair gives 2 times the reserve unit after division. For a same-orbit triple, the partial-intersection count is 3[binom(M−3,p−1)+binom(M−3,p−2)]. At positive feasible occupations on a component with an edge, every rank is strictly between zero and M. Thus the deficit is positive on a nonflat restriction. Deleting zero raw occupation vertices introduces no new constraint or multiplicity. Singleton components are flat and multiply the ordinary and gained counts by the same positive number; infeasible profiles have zero on both sides and are excluded from the equality characterization.

5. **Sharpness.** If all transformed ranks equal p, inclusions along edges become equalities. Transport consistency identifies entire assignments bijectively with the common invariant p-sets. Ordinary and reduced assignments likewise correspond to arbitrary p-sets and (p−1)-sets. This particular equality argument does not require the localization hypothesis. It says nothing about equality of the quantitative bound at *every constant raw occupation*. For example, on a transposition-twisted 4-cycle with one pendant leaf at the hub, M=3 and all raw occupations 1 give P=36, Z=32, R=1, whereas the moved-set right side is 2. Conversely with M=4 and group generated on a K₂,₃ by (12)(34) and (13)(24), all inclusion ranks 2 give P=6, Z=0, R=2; the whole group fixes no 2-set, although each nonidentity permutation individually fixes two. The numerical group action itself is elementary, not a separate theorem.

6. **Base-graph exception and prior work.** Crown graphs at d≥5 have minimum degree d−1, yielding the claimed treewidth bound by degeneracy, and remain connected after deleting two vertices: same-side pairs retain a common neighbor and opposite sides have surviving edges. Three matched pairs induce a 6-cycle. A nonredundant gluing of smaller graphs at an edge/vertex cannot give the indicated 3-connected graph. The theorem adds a class of *particular covers* of such graphs; it does not control every cover of them.

   [Watanabe's original Conjecture 1, Eq. (4)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2445v3) is coefficientwise. [Watanabe 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14892v1) already gives the active-flatness comparison for the stated structural classes (Theorems A,C), 2R / odd-order 3R and element/rank-dependent constants on width two (Theorem B and Cor. 4.5), two-sheet comparison on general bipartite bases (Remark 2.3, crediting Csikvári), finite seams, and saturation/trace and mixed-holonomy examples (§4). In particular Example 4.7 treats a single transposition on any even cycle, not merely one on C₄. Lemma 5.1 cites Frankl's simultaneous shifting and KMS rearrangement; the present proof uses a related two-label operation but counts a pinned boundary problem on an arbitrary ordinary complement via agreement components. The fixed-set argument is an extension of the saturated example, not the source of the earlier sharp constants. The unrestricted conjecture and the full quantitative chordal-bipartite question are not thereby proved. Ruozzi's evaluated activities do not imply fixed-coefficient domination (§1.4); large-palette *minimum* DP coloring counts also concern a different extremum. Additional source searches for single-vertex/incident-edge twists, deleted-vertex gauge, and DP extremality located no earlier general hub theorem, not a guarantee of priority. The repository increment after #106 up to #107 (87064bfba5f342811529128b59e5f7a5a0a687dc) only adds the finite cut-dictionary package. The author's full historical extraction was inspected through its reports, not independently repeated.

7. **Independent verification.** The second checker counts on each individual sheet the binary upward-closed subsets of the hub complement containing that sheet's required neighbors. It obtains the prescribed coefficient of the product of these sheet polynomials by exact integer dynamic programming. It enumerates the generated permutation group, its label orbits and its fixed subsets separately, checks every elementary shift for each tested boundary state, and includes disconnected complement, nonabelian groups and zero-sheet reductions. Its 606 profiles, 2,175 pinned choices and 13,146 single-shift tests pass under Python optimization. The author's independent-set checker also reproduced its frozen output under optimization. Neither program replaces the all-graph proof.
