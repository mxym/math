# Prior-work and scope check (18 November 2026)

[Y. Watanabe (2011), A conjecture on independent sets and graph covers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2445) asks for coefficientwise domination of the projected multivariate independence polynomial of every finite cover of a bipartite graph by that of the trivial cover. It is a statement at every prescribed occupation vector, not at one choice of positive activities. [N. Ruozzi (2012), Theorem 4.1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/03afdbd66e7929b125f8597834fa83a4-Paper.pdf) proves the evaluated partition-function comparison for log-supermodular factorizations, applicable here after complementing one bipartition. His Lemma 3.7 even keeps a single Hamming-weight slice in one step, but the general induction marginalizes others. It does not prove coefficientwise domination on all simultaneous vertex slices. We do not claim to settle the original conjecture.

[Y. Watanabe (October 2026), Theorems A–C, Corollary 4.5, Examples 4.7–4.9, §7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14892) proves the exact coefficient inequality and flatness rigidity for covers on bases with an active graph of treewidth at most two, chordal-bipartite active graphs, and the stated finite seam compositions. On width two the work introduces the reduced (M−2)-sheet coefficient, 2-fold deficit (3 for odd holonomy order), stronger profile-dependent moved-subset constants and sharp examples. It also proves a first homogeneous defect for arbitrary bases. Its simultaneous nest-column compression (Lemma 5.1), Boolean inclusion-rank interpretation, root/edge fibres and holonomy formalism are prior work. It explicitly leaves the unrestricted coefficient conjecture open; §7 also asks for the 2R lower bound for arbitrary nonflat chordal-bipartite covers. The new result only gives the requested lower bound when gains can be confined to incident edges of *one* hub. It is a hypothesis on a particular cover, different from the earlier conditions on a base graph. It extends to base graphs of unbounded treewidth and outside the earlier seam class, not to every cover of those bases.

The proposed bound uses the number of p-sets moved by the entire hub group (at least one element moves the set), not the minimum over individual group elements and ranks in Watanabe's width-two interpolation. Its exactness on uniform *complemented inclusion ranks*, not general uniform raw occupations, is the elementary fixed-set count, generalizing the saturated cycle calculation in Example 4.8. [P. Csikvári, Extremal regular graphs (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01295) and [Y. Zhao, The bipartite swapping trick on graph homomorphisms (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3704) are precedents for switching/counting two-label components; Watanabe credits Csikvári for the all-base two-sheet coefficient comparison in §1.4 and also treats its rigidity in Remark 2.3. Watanabe’s Example 4.7 gives the transposition coefficient identity on every even cycle; Example 4.8 gives a saturated cyclic-holonomy example. His Lemma 5.1 also credits Frankl’s shifting method. The new step is the conditional last-sort deficit with reserved sheets at a hub and its sum over the whole stabilizer action, not a claim to invent swapping, rearrangement or first quantitative cover deficits.
