# Independent sets in a bipartite cover with twists at one vertex

Fix a bipartite graph and prescribe exactly how many vertices of an independent set must lie above each base vertex in an $M$-sheeted cover. [Watanabe's 2011 conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2445v3) says that the trivial cover, $M$ disjoint copies of the base graph, maximizes this count for every occupation vector.

**Result for a particular class of covers.** Suppose there is a vertex $z$ such that, after deleting $z$, the sheet labels can be chosen to make every remaining edge permutation the identity. The trivial cover then maximizes every coefficient. At a feasible occupation profile, the two counts are equal **if and only if** the cover is flat on the induced graph of positive-occupation vertices. This is a condition on a cover, not on the size or treewidth of its base. It gives examples on unbounded-treewidth crown graphs, but not a theorem for all covers of those graphs.

**Quantitative bound.** Suppose the base is connected, has an edge, $M\ge2$, and all occupations are positive and feasible. Let $p$ be the hub occupation; $\Gamma$ the permutation group on sheet labels generated by transports around closed walks at the hub; and $f_\Gamma(p)$ the number of $p$-subsets invariant under this *whole group*. Write $P,Z$ for the trivial and gained counts, and $R$ for the trivial count after deleting two sheets and lowering all raw occupations by one. Then
$$
P-Z\ \ge\ \frac{\binom Mp-f_\Gamma(p)}{\binom{M-2}{p-1}}\,R.
$$
Nonflatness gives at least $2R$, and an orbit of size at least three gives at least $3R$. The bound is exact when **all inclusion ranks equal $p$**: raw occupation $p$ on the hub's side, $M-p$ on the other. Equal raw occupations alone do not guarantee equality of this bound.

The proof pins the hub set and considers the ordinary components of its complement separately. A shift of two labels aligns the neighbors' boundary requirements without losing assignments. In a component where they initially disagree, the last shift to agreement recovers a whole reduced-cover contribution.

[Watanabe 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14892) already proves exact comparison and rigidity for width-two and chordal-bipartite bases and their specified seam compositions, with quantitative constants and sharp examples on width two. His shift/compression and reduced-sheet reserve are important precedents. The result here is instead the flat-off-one-vertex cover class and the pinned deficit from counting subsets moved by at least one member of the full hub group. It does **not** resolve the unrestricted conjecture or the whole chordal-bipartite quantitative question.
