# Watanabe's coefficient comparison for covers whose gains can be localized at one vertex

Throughout G is finite simple bipartite. An M-cover is specified by bijections of [M] on the oriented edges (reverse edges use inverses). Selecting a_v independent vertices in the fibre over v is feasible only if 0≤a_v≤M and a_v+a_w≤M on every edge. Write Z(a) for the resulting count and P(a) for that of the trivial cover. The latter is the coefficient of x^a in the Mth power of the ordinary independence polynomial. All feasible profiles do have positive ordinary count (nested prefixes after complementing one bipartition class).

**Theorem.** Suppose the given cover restricts to a flat cover on G−z for some vertex z; flat means a change of labels in each vertex fibre makes all its gains identity. Then Watanabe's inequality Z(a)≤P(a) holds for every a. For a feasible a equality holds if and only if the cover on the induced active graph (the vertices of positive a) is flat. There is no restriction on the size, treewidth, degree or induced cycles of G, but the localization condition on the cover is essential to our argument.

If G is connected with at least one edge, M≥2, and a is positive feasible, choose the bipartition L,R with z∈L. Use inclusion ranks p_v=a_v on L and p_v=M−a_v on R. All ranks are between 1 and M−1. Write p=p_z. Let Γ≤Sym([M]) be the subgroup of transports of all closed walks based at z. Write f_Γ(p) for the number of p-subsets of [M] invariant under **every** element of Γ. Let R(a) be the trivial-cover coefficient for M−2 sheets, raw occupation a_v−1 at each v (including the natural empty choice when M=2). Then

    P(a) − Z(a) ≥ (binom(M,p) − f_Γ(p)) R(a) / binom(M−2,p−1).       (1)

The bound is an equality for constant **inclusion** ranks on every connected graph in the stated class: raw left occupation p and raw right occupation M−p. Constant raw occupation on both sides does not generally have this property. In particular on a nonflat cover the deficit is ≥2R(a), and ≥3R(a) if Γ has an orbit of size at least three. This does not solve the conjecture on arbitrary covers or the full chordal-bipartite quantitative question of Watanabe (2026), §7.

## Sorting boundary requirements

Relabel fibres so all gains in G−z are identity. Complement the sets selected on R. For fixed physical hub selection A⊂[M] of size p, the remaining states are subsets X_v of sizes p_v. They satisfy ordinary inclusions X_l⊆X_r on each remaining edge lr and at every neighbor w of z the additional requirement T_w=π_w(A)⊆X_w, where π_w is the gain z→w. The components of G−z are independent conditional on A.

Fix a component C. For prescribed boundary sets T_w of size p at its neighbors of z, write F_C(T) for the number of completions on C. Write F_C^* for the canonical completion count if all T_w are the same p-set; this number is independent of the common p-set. For distinct labels i,j, orient a sort toward i: replace j by i in every T_w containing j but not i; other requirements remain as they were. **F_C(T) cannot decrease.** Indeed fix every vertex's chosen labels other than i,j. Its remaining required count is k_v in {0,1,2}, or else this term is zero. Off-pair inclusions are checked unchanged. Pair inclusions require k_l≤k_r; if so the only further restrictions are that adjacent vertices with k=1 must choose the same one of i,j. Thus each connected component of C[{v:k_v=1}] carries one free binary choice, possibly forced by one or more boundary requirements. A k=0 or k=2 boundary feasibility condition is independent of the sort, as is a two-label demand at k=1 (infeasible). At a single-demand, k=1 boundary vertex the sort directs all forces to i. It cannot introduce a conflict: inside every binary component, number of orientations stays the same or increases (from zero to one in the conflicting case). Summation gives the claim. By sorting towards smaller labels until none of the requirements changes, each T_w becomes [p]: the sum of its label values over all w strictly decreases at each effective step. Therefore F_C(T)≤F_C^*. This argument imposes no constraints on the internal topology of C beyond canonical gains.

## The deficit of a conflicting component at one pinned hub set

If p is interior and the T_w in a connected C are not all the same, use the sorting sequence and stop at the first step after which they are all equal, say T*. Just before this last sort toward i from j, there must be both a requirement containing i but not j and one containing j but not i; all T_w then agree outside {i,j}, say on B=T*−{i} of size p−1. To verify: any requirement changing goes from the j-only to the i-only state; as the sorted result is common, unchanged requirements must be i-only and one must exist or the previous requirements would already be common. Let r_C be the number of canonical completions on C with label set [M]−{i,j}, ranks p_v−1, and common boundary requirement B. Relabeling makes r_C independent of this pair and B. For each such off-pair completion all k_v equal one. The binary agreement graph is the whole connected C, forced inconsistently before the last sort, and with exactly one orientation after it. All other off-pair states have nonnegative gain. The entire difference F_C^*−F_C(T) is therefore at least r_C.

Whether or not the original requirements conflict, F_C^*≥r_C: starting from a reduced canonical completion, adjoin i everywhere and omit j, using the common requirement B∪{i}. Feasibility and interior ranks guarantee r_C≥1, by using nested prefixes after relabeling B to [p−1]. In particular there is no strictness claim solely from disconnected neighbor sets lying in different components.

## From fibres to the orbit count

For each C the products of transports π_{w'}^{-1}π_w with w,w'∈N(z)∩C together generate Γ as C varies. In fact a walk from z back to z without an intermediate visit to z travels through one such C with all internal gains identity; conversely C supplies a path for every such pair. Thus some C has conflicting requirements for the pinned set A if and only if A is not Γ-invariant. If all are nonconflicting then the pinned count is already the canonical product, because each component may be freely relabeled.

When C_0 conflicts, with F_C=F_C(T), nonnegativity gives

    ∏ F_C* − ∏ F_C ≥ (F_C₀* − F_C₀) ∏_{C≠C₀} F_C* ≥ ∏ r_C .

Writing A₀, B₀ for the ordinary and gained counts in C₀ and A′, B′ for the two products over the other components, the difference is (A₀−B₀)A′+B₀(A′−B′); the second term is nonnegative. In the reduced canonical graph the number of completions conditional on any fixed (p−1)-set at z is exactly ∏r_C, so R(a)=binom(M−2,p−1)∏r_C. Summing the pinned deficits over all p-subsets proves (1).

If Γ moves at least one pair of labels i,j in the same orbit, an invariant A must contain both or neither; at least 2 binom(M−2,p−1) sets violate this. If an orbit contains a triple, any A containing exactly one or two of its three labels violates invariance. The total is 3[binom(M−3,p−1)+binom(M−3,p−2)] = 3 binom(M−2,p−1), with out-of-range binomials zero. In general f_Γ(p)=[t^p]∏_O(1+t^|O|) over label-orbits. Uniform inclusion ranks p_v=p force equality of the transported endpoint sets along all edges, so that every assignment is determined by a Γ-invariant hub set; conversely every such set extends uniquely. The trivial and reduced counts are binom(M,p), binom(M−2,p−1), proving sharpness. This saturated calculation itself also works on covers not satisfying the localization assumption.

For arbitrary profiles remove zero-occupation vertices, which impose no restrictions in the original independent-set model. Infeasible profiles are zero on both sides. In the active graph, components missing z have flat restriction. A singleton component, including an isolated hub, always contributes the same positive factor for feasible ranks. If an active component containing z has an edge then all its ranks are interior; a nonflat restriction forces positive deficit by (1), and a flat one gives equality. Positive feasible factors elsewhere preserve strictness. These observations prove the stated full equality criterion.

## Beyond the known base graph classes

For d≥5 the crown graph K_{d,d} minus a perfect matching contains an induced C6 (take three corresponding pairs). It has minimum degree d−1, hence treewidth ≥d−1. It stays connected on deleting at most two vertices: every same-side pair still has a common neighbor (there were d−2), and both sides have an edge between them. Hence it cannot be a nontrivial vertex- or edge-seam sum of smaller graphs (such a sum has a vertex separator of size at most two). Thus these base graphs are outside the chordal-bipartite, width-two, and finite-seam classes of Watanabe. Any assignments of gains to the edges incident to one hub with all other gains trivial satisfy our hypothesis, and need not be flat or commute. Conversely, our theorem says nothing about arbitrary covers of these crown graphs.

Watanabe's 2026 paper already contains the 2R, odd-order 3R, and moved-subset constants on its structural classes, and the singly twisted even-cycle examples, including C4. Our claim is the unrestricted-base, single-hub transport class and its bound involving the full orbit partition. See [the prior-work comparison](SOURCES.md). The two-label sorting argument is a Kempe-component compression, not a claim to invent two-colour switching, the Boolean inclusion transformation, or sheet reservation.
