# Scope and source comparison

Comparisons checked 19 November 2026; this is a bounded literature and repository search, not a claim of priority.

- [A. S. Lotnikov, arXiv:2608.14848v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14848v1), English translation of the June 2026 thesis. §1 credits the 2019 first-moment question to D. Zaporozhets and A. Tarasov and states its all-moments extension as Conjecture 2. §§4,7–9 treat centrally symmetric planar bodies, nonsimplex centrally symmetric counterexamples in higher dimensions, and sufficiently large moment orders for a fixed planar body. §10 Proposition 14 explicitly gives the independent radial decomposition, with density $d r^{d-1}$; its cone-measure form and the associated Kingman-type identities are prior, not a new tool here. §11 Theorem 9 gives the projection-wise **mean** inequality for every triangle. Its comparison has no free threshold. The present simplex result neither reproves the full centrally symmetric theory nor classifies arbitrary bodies. [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14848) listed only v1.
- [E. Shen, arXiv:2608.06470v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06470v4), the November 9 revision. Theorem 1.3 and §3, Corollaries 3.10 and 3.11 (HTML numbering), prove the first and second Euclidean moments for **every planar convex body**, using projected estimates. §4 Example 4.13 gives a six-vertex three-dimensional first-moment reversal, then products extend the failure beyond dimension three. That polytope is not a tetrahedron. The [official version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06470) lists the September v3 and a later v4, which split off the former §5.
- [M. Kukushkin, arXiv:2608.14833v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14833v1) independently proves the strict general planar mean comparison via a one-dimensional Gini inequality and exact polynomial signs, not a tetrahedron theorem or an all-threshold comparison. [A. S. Tokmachev, arXiv:2607.08869v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08869v1) proves **one-point** convex order for planar bodies with coincident interior and boundary centroids, and for circumscribed polytopes whose incenter and both centroids coincide. That gives all powers for those bodies, including regular simplices; there is no such centering hypothesis in our signed **two-point projection** comparison.
- [A. S. Tokmachev (2022)](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/znsl7205) treats fixed-perimeter extremal boundary mean distances, not the simplex dimension threshold. The uniform simplex divided-difference identity used here is classical; see [C. de Boor, “Divided Differences”, Eq. (52)](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~deboor/sat/papers/2/2.pdf). Projecting a pair, taking a stop-loss transform, and averaging powers over directions are standard tools.
- July–November 2026 searches using the conjecturers' names, author names, the four arXiv identifiers, triangle/tetrahedron/simplex, surface versus interior distance moments, random chord and convex/stop-loss order led back to the works above. We did not locate an exact prior for the no-centering simplex dimension classification; that negative search cannot exclude independent, unindexed or later work. In particular, we do not present first-moment failure for general higher-dimensional bodies as new.

## Owner research corpus

At [main after #243](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/0b0a1adb5175edcf45a7172af8864e668b7965f8), the snapshot plus the 60 publicly advertised tag trees supplied 15,992 distinct main blobs and 299 tag-only blobs. Names/identifiers plus terms for mean distances, interior and boundary distances, chord moments and stop-loss order were searched in supported source/text formats (including Lean), PDFs with extractable text, and archives: 36,742 text units, 162 PDFs and 160 archives. All 175 manual assets across 59 **published** releases were separately size- and SHA-256-checked; their supported contents yielded 10,884 text units, 38 PDFs and 46 archives. This does not cover unpublished releases, OCR-only pages, unrelated terminology, or private projects.

The repository's [Hall radial-stretch work](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/201) and [true-area](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/212) / [true-arclength](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/216) results concern the probability of obtuse angles, not the present pair-distance order. A simplex stability note bounds distances of support points in a different problem; a [star-Steiner result](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/164) concerns optimizing the star center. [#240](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/240) concerns maximum empty sample-polytope volume; #241–243 add permutation/prophet work. None of these comparisons supplies external review.
