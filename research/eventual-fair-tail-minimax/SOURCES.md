# Source comparison and limits

**Original question and the meanings of optimal.** [B. B. Pokhodzeĭ, 1984, pp. 115–123, journal record](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=znsl&option_lang=rus&paperid=4790&wshow=paper), [complete original scan](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=znsl&option_lang=rus&paperid=4790&what=fullt), [1986 English translation](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01095642). The full Russian paper was read. On p. 116 the algorithm selects a joint dyadic interval/depth and appends fair suffix digits. Lemma 1 constructs the mixture by right-endpoint lower step approximants for a continuous decreasing density; the canonical representation minimizes expected delay to a uniform tail (p. 118). Lemma 2 proves $n+O(1)$ expected coin cost for a bounded density in this class, by finiteness of the selector entropy. Lemmas 3–4 treat the Gaschütz minimum-of-uniforms generator; the reflected cubic is one instance of that established order-statistic construction. Lemma 5 and pp. 121–122 treat Marsaglia’s exponential construction. These mixtures, exact examples, and the $n+O(1)$ result are prior.

On pp. 122–123 the first numbered problem calls for extension beyond the monotone class and asks about bounded variation; problem 2 asks whether the intermediate model includes finite generators. Problem 3 asks for arbitrary positive excess tolerance over the optimal algorithm in the intermediate model. Its short wording does not put a supremum or a limit over precision in the statement, and does not say when a discrete selector must release partially determined output. We consequently give explicitly quantified theorems under two timing conventions. Exactness at every precision for a monotone law in the streamed convention, and vanishing asymptotic excess in the upfront convention, are different conclusions. Minimum expected *time until copying starts* is neither of these criteria. The two-bit minimax concerns a larger class including nonmonotone positive smooth densities and compares with the exact optimal coin cost of each prefix, not with $n$ or with differential entropy.

**Earlier and adjacent theory.**

* D. E. Knuth and A. C. Yao, *The Complexity of Nonuniform Random Number Generation*, in *Algorithms and Complexity* (1976), 357–428; [original volume catalogue](https://books.google.com/books/about/Algorithms_and_Complexity.html?id=SNEmAAAAMAAJ), [Knuth's addendum to the reprint](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/aaerr1.html). Pokhodzeĭ p. 115 explicitly credits their unrestricted optimal generating algorithms. The binary-expansion criterion, discrete entropy-plus-two bound, and ordinary optimal coin trees are classical. A modern exact statement is [Saad et al., POPL 2020, Theorem 2.9](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rinard/paper/popl20.sampling.pdf). That classical two-bit bound is for the discrete optimum **over entropy**; the minimax constant here is for an eventually-copying generator **over the discrete optimum**. No transcription of the 1976 p. 427 is relied upon.
* V. Nekrutkin, [*Binary decompositions of probability densities and random-bit simulation*, Monte Carlo Methods Appl. 26 (2020), 163–169](https://doi.org/10.1515/mcma-2020-2063); [publication index and abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/mcmeap/v26y2020i2p163-169n5.html). This is directly relevant prior work: the author's abstract describes drawing a special discrete label to obtain initial output bits, then continuing with fair bits, and studying its complexity. We do not claim invention of density decomposition, interval selection or copying. We verified the author abstract, publication metadata and references, not the full subscription article; those records do not suffice to exclude all overlap in its technical details. The new claims proposed here are the specific prefix persistence criterion, near-two smooth obstructions and matching asymptotic minimax bound.
* P. Flajolet and N. Saheb, [*The complexity of generating an exponentially distributed variate*, J. Algorithms 7 (1986), 463–488](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-6774(86)90014-3), determine the additive constant for a specified exponential generator, not a worst-law comparison with each exact discrete optimum. [Devroye and Gravel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02539v6) analyze continuous-law approximation using Wasserstein accuracy. Here the real always has its exact target law; the approximation concerns expected input cost.
* C. T. Li and A. El Gamal, [*Distributed Simulation of Continuous Random Variables*, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 63 (2017), Proposition 2, §III and Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.05875), and [*A Universal Coding Scheme for Remote Generation of Continuous Random Variables*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05238). The positive hypograph construction, dyadic decompositions into conditionally uniform laws, and averaging under grid shifts/scalings are prior. The one-dimensional shifted interval tile count is a specialization of this geometric averaging, not a new dyadic decomposition principle. Their objectives are the entropy of shared labels or remote message length. Here the horizontal index determines *which later output bits cost one each*: its depth is subtracted from the label cost. The separate vertical-scale estimate against the discrete prefix cost, the BV stabilization and the worst-target lower construction yield the eventual-copy minimax.
* [Han–Hoshi’s interval method](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.556116) and the [author seminar exposition](https://algo.inria.fr/seminars/sem94-95/hoshi.pdf) give classical interval partitions for exact random generation. The result here uses a fixed shifted vertical grid above a varying density and a horizontal selector for which the remaining suffix is uniform; no novelty is claimed for dyadic interval decoding itself.
* [Kindler–Romik, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 17 (2004), 624–633](https://doi.org/10.1137/S089548010343106X) classify the smooth laws computable by an unrestricted-precision **finite graph**. [Draper–Saad, July 2026 v1, §9, around (63)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14503v1#page=18), distinguish precision-uniform excess from per-draw loss and ask about the size of finite generators. Neither the mixture selector nor its initial decision tree is required to be finite here. A maximally efficient streamed interval selector says nothing about a finite-state implementation.
