# A persistent cost gap for every eventual-copy selector, and a sharp oscillation of the unrestricted optimum

Use the streamed and upfront conventions and the exact discrete prefix optimum $C_n$ of the accompanying paper. For every $0<\eta<1$ there is a strictly positive smooth density, arbitrarily close to 1 in the uniform norm, for which **every** almost surely eventual-copy selector satisfies
$$\liminf_{n\to\infty}(E T_n-C_n)\ \ge\ 2(1-\eta).$$
This includes streamed selectors, regardless of memory, locking-time integrability or optimality of the joint interval draw. The density is not monotone. On a *different* smooth law there can be oscillation of $C_n-n$ itself of size greater than $2(1-\eta)$, arbitrarily close to the universal upper bound 2 for bounded positive smooth densities. We do not assert that 2 upper-bounds all possible selector deficits.

**Homogeneous dyadic holes.** Fix integers $q_0$ and $s$ so that $2^{-q_0}+2^{-s}<\eta$. On the initial grid of depth $n_0=q_0$, reserve the last cell for a mean correction, and declare every other cell live. Define $r_j=s+j+2$, $n_j=n_{j-1}+r_j$ ($j\ge1$). At stage $j$ partition each live parent into $2^{r_j}$ equal dyadic children of depth $n_j$; remove one fixed interior child (the second, say), and keep the others live. Work up to the countable set of grid endpoints. The surviving set $F$ has length
$$|F|=(1-2^{-q_0})\prod_{j\ge1}(1-2^{-r_j})>1-\eta.$$
Each live cell at generation $j$ has the same future removal schedule scaled to that cell. Every dyadic interval $u$ with $|I_u\cap F|>0$ contains an entire later removed cell: on a finer live grid some full live cell inside it has a positive surviving portion, hence loses a child in the next step.

**Persistent gap construction.** In each removed cell place a nonzero smooth mean-zero function supported in its interior, taking both signs. A difference of two translated, congruent smooth bumps suffices. Set the perturbation zero elsewhere, including the reserved cell, and add it to density 1. Take positive amplitudes so the sum at each finite stage $j$ (disjoint supports) has $C^j$ norm at most $\delta 2^{-j-2}$ with $0<\delta<1$ arbitrarily small. The series is smooth by uniform convergence of every derivative, positive, normalized, equals 1 on $F$, and every bin intersecting $F$ in positive measure has essential infimum strictly below 1. Each such bin has average exactly 1: dyadic intervals are nested or disjoint off endpoints, and it cannot be inside a removed cell, so every bump that meets it integrates to zero there.

At least $2^n|F|$ length-$n$ bins meet $F$ in positive measure. For each such bin $u$, at time $k=n+j$, $j\ge0$, the optimal discrete tree has exactly $2^j$ length-$k$ input strings which have returned $u$ (Knuth–Yao floor criterion). Consider any exact eventual-copy selector, and count the strings returning $u$ by $k$ for which its interval selection has finished at an output length $K<n$. These correspond to the following disjoint events. A locking input leaf of depth $c$ selects a dyadic ancestor interval of depth $K<n$. It then copies the $n-K$ suffix digits of $u$; this has cost $c+n-K$ and is eligible precisely when that cost is at most $k$. It contributes $2^{k-(c+n-K)}$ padded strings. Its resulting subdensity, constant across the entire bin $u$, is $2^{K-c}$ (per unit Lebesgue length). If multiple leaves select the same interval, count the leaves separately. All these subdensities, being disjoint events under the same exact generator, sum to at most $\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{I_u} f<1$. Each eligible term is an integer multiple of $2^{-j}$; equivalently the total number of such padded strings is at most $2^j-1$. Output already printed before locking is permitted; when $n>K$ the last required suffix digit must be supplied by direct copying, which is why this count still applies.

Across all these bins the remaining input strings returning some such $u$ at first hit without a previously completed lock ($K\ge n$) have total probability at most $\Pr(K\ge n)$. Formally pad with infinite fair continuations when that event is not yet determined: on a length-$k$ cylinder that has already returned $u$, either its first hit used copying after a lock at $K<n$, or every continuation locks at $K\ge n$. Thus, at deadline $n+j$, the probability deficit relative to the optimal tree is at least $2^{-j}|F|-\Pr(K\ge n)$. Other bins and all deadlines have nonnegative deficits. By the expectation tail sum, for every fixed integer $J\ge0$,
$$E T_n-C_n\ge |F|\sum_{j=0}^J2^{-j}-(J+1)\Pr(K\ge n).$$
The asserted lower limit follows by taking $n\to\infty$, since $K<\infty$ almost surely, then $J\to\infty$. Infinite expected costs present no exception.

**Oscillation of the unrestricted optimum.** For $z\ge0$ write $D(z)=\sum_i i2^{-i}b_i(z)$ for its terminating-preferred bits as in the main paper. Indices $i$ below a finite bound do not occur on a bounded interval and the positive-index series converges uniformly there, so $D$ is bounded and is continuous at every nondyadic positive $z$. At 1 its right limit is $D(1)=0$ and its left limit is $\sum_{i\ge1}i2^{-i}=2$. For the piecewise constant dyadic-bin averages $m_n$, the optimal leaf of height bit $2^{-i}$ in a bin has probability $2^{-n-i}$ and input cost $n+i$. Summing proves exactly
$$C_n-n=\int_0^1D(m_n(x))\,dx.$$

Use the same deleted/live geometry but replace the mean-zero pair by **one** nonnegative smooth bump inside each deleted cell, of sign $(-1)^j$ at stage $j$, identical after translation at that stage. One may use a rescaled version of $\exp(-1/(t(1-t)))$ on an interior interval, extended by zero; it is strictly monotone on either side of its maximum. Choose a strictly positive common amplitude so that the stage sum has $C^j$ norm at most $\delta2^{-j-2}$ and its total absolute integral $A_j$ is at most $A_{j-1}/4$ for $j>1$. Take $A_1$ sufficiently small to allow compensation: in the reserved initial cell put a single such smooth bump with integral $-\sum_j(-1)^j A_j$, whose amplitude is bounded by $\delta$ by taking $A_1$ small. Disjoint supports and the derivative bounds again give a smooth positive normalized density, as uniformly close to 1 as desired.

If $I$ is any single live cell at stage $j$, the integral of each later stage $\ell$ over it is *exactly* $(-1)^\ell A_\ell/N_j$, where $N_j$ is the number of live cells at stage $j$: they contain equal numbers of congruent removed descendants at stage $\ell$. Earlier bumps and the correction do not meet $I$. Therefore $m_{n_j}(x)-1$ for $x\in F$ has sign $(-1)^{j+1}$, since the first later term dominates the sum of absolute later remainders. As $f$ is continuous and equals 1 on $F$, these averages tend to 1. Outside $F$ almost every point either lies in an open constant plateau or has a nondyadic value of $f$: on each of the countably many bumps every level except its outside plateau has finitely many preimages. Thus $D(m_{n_j})$ converges off $F$ almost everywhere to $D(f)$; on $F$ it tends to 0 along the above-1 subsequence and 2 along the below-1 subsequence. Bounded convergence gives two limits separated by $2|F|>2(1-\eta)$. This is sharp as a supremum of possible oscillations: the classical bound $H(p^{(n)})\le C_n\le H(p^{(n)})+2$ and
$$H(p^{(n)})-n=-\int_0^1m_n\log_2 m_n\longrightarrow-\int_0^1 f\log_2 f$$
show that $\limsup(C_n-n)-\liminf(C_n-n)\le2$. Here $p^{(n)}$ denotes the discrete prefix law. No finite-state claim or equality of the two examples is intended.
