# Optimal binary prefixes with an eventual fair tail

**Model and historical scope.** A distribution-generating fair-bit decision tree prints an infinite binary expansion of a real in $[0,1]$, including the option of printing already determined digits for free. Equivalently, finite strings can be attached to nodes; initial deterministic digits may be printed at the root. A tree may be countable; no finite-memory or effective access hypothesis is imposed on the law. An interval selector has an almost surely finite locking time when it has output the prefix of a dyadic interval indexed by $(K,J)$ (depth and index), and from then on copies fresh independent input bits. In the *streamed* convention the selector may emit interval digits before it finishes; in the *upfront* convention it must finish the entire interval selection before its first digit is made available. The same selector tree can be read either way by withholding or releasing labels. The distinction is one of **timing**, not distribution. No copy may be declared until its future bits really are fresh and uniform.

Write $p_u$ for the mass of a binary interval $I_u$, $T_n$ for coins used when its first $n$ digits are printed, and $C_n$ for the minimum possible expected fair-coin cost of the discrete length-$n$ law. Knuth–Yao's optimal DDG criterion requires precisely one first-hit leaf of probability $2^{-c}$ for each term of this size in the terminating-preferred binary expansion of each $p_u$, and no others. It applies also to a countable selector when its entropy is finite; the pointwise floor/tail-sum argument for the lower bound applies without this extra assumption. We call a tree prefix optimal if $E T_n=C_n$ for every $n$.

Pokhodzeĭ, *Zap. Nauchn. Sem. LOMI* 137 (1984), [full text, pp. 115–123](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=znsl&option_lang=rus&paperid=4790&what=fullt), p.116, models a joint interval/depth draw followed by fair digits. Lemmas 1–2 give a canonical mixture for decreasing continuous densities, minimum expected uniform-tail delay and $n+O(1)$ cost for bounded densities. Problem 3, p.123, asks for arbitrarily small excess over optimal cost in the intermediate model; it does not itself specify a supremum over $n$, a limit over $n$, or whether already determined digits may be announced during the joint draw. We prove separate results for those precise readings, not a claim about an unstated convention. In particular optimality of prefix coin costs is not minimality of $E K$ over mixtures.

**Theorem.** For bounded continuous monotone densities a single streamed selector can simultaneously (i) attain every $C_n$, (ii) itself be optimal for its discrete joint law $(K,J)$, and (iii) terminate with $\Pr(K>n)=O_f((n+1)2^{-n})$ and finite expected input cost. With all digits withheld until that same selector finishes, $E T_n-C_n\to0$. Nevertheless under the upfront convention the reflected cubic density $3(1-x)^2$ has $E T_1-C_1\ge1/8$ for every selector. For general bounded continuous densities the exact criterion below characterizes streamed prefix optimality with almost surely eventual copying; it can fail even for strictly positive $C^\infty$ densities.

**1. Canonical atoms and persistence.** With $h=2^{-|u|}$, let $b_i(z)\in\{0,1\}$ be the coefficient of $2^{-i}$ in the binary expansion of $z\ge0$, terminating in zeroes at a dyadic value. Put $m_u=p_u/h$ and
$$S(u)=\{i: b_i(m_v)=1\text{ for every dyadic descendant }v\text{ of }u,\text{ including }u\}.$$
The set increases under descent. The canonical input atoms at $u$ have weights $h2^{-i}$ for the present bits of $m_u$. These weights are always at most 1. If $f$ is bounded, all indices are bounded below by one fixed integer even when some indices are negative. Put
$$L_n=\sum_{|u|=n} h\sum_{i\in S(u)}2^{-i}.$$
The greatest possible probability of copying by output depth $n$ in a prefix-optimal streamed selector is $L_n$, simultaneously for all $n$ if nontermination of the selector is provisionally allowed. Hence an almost surely terminating selector exists if and only if $L_n\to1$.

For necessity, each already locked input path ending with the first $n$ printed digits $u$ is a canonical atom. Each further $d$-letter refinement of that atom has weight $h2^{-i-d}$, because the algorithm copies fresh bits. This must occur in the canonical expansion of every corresponding descendant by prefix optimality. Summing these distinct weights gives the upper bound. In applying this argument to any selector, its output before locking consists solely of initial bits of the selected interval and the selector may lock just after finitely many deterministic digits. If an implementation consumes extra coins *after* printing all $K$ interval digits but before copying, the lock-path need not itself be a first-hit path for $K$; the following qualification handles it. At depth $n$ count only atoms already in direct-copy mode *at first hit of $n$*, and for a locked path with $K<n$ this condition always holds. Thus for such general implementations $\Pr(K<n)\le L_n$, sufficient for the necessity of the iff. The sharp finite-depth statement is about first-hit copy mass (the construction locks at first hit).

For attainment start at the root with the single probability-one atom. At a first-hit atom at $u$, if its index is newly persistent, declare the selector finished and copy thereafter. Its half-weight at each child is an available canonical bit by persistence. Inherited copy atoms continue likewise. Remove their half-atoms in the two children, leaving two canonical sub-expansions. The remaining parent capacities are also a canonical sub-expansion, and their sum equals the total child demand. Removing any subset of bits preserves the terminating-preferred convention (a subset cannot acquire an eventually all-one tail).

Partition the remaining multiset of child powers of two into the parent capacities without splitting an atom. List both in decreasing order, breaking child ties deterministically, and place each list end-to-end on an interval of their common total length. For a parent boundary $s$ ending at a capacity $w$, the total length left strictly beyond $s$ is less than $w$ (the unused parent digits form a non-eventually-all-one tail). A child interval of length $a$ starts at a multiple of $a$. If $a\le w$, $s$ is also a multiple of $a$ and cannot be interior. If $a>w$, the next multiple of $a$ strictly above $s$ is at least $s+w$, beyond the available length. Thus no boundary cuts a child interval. Within a parent each assigned child interval is a relative dyadic code interval: its left endpoint is aligned modulo its length after subtracting the parent start (also an appropriate multiple). Decode this partition using fair bits and emit the child digit only on reaching its assigned leaf. The leftover endpoints/null nondecoding rays have probability zero. Equal-weight whole-parent transitions print a digit deterministically; for bounded $f$ only finitely many consecutive digits can be printed with no fresh input since $2^{-c}\le \|f\|_\infty2^{-n}$ on every positive-probability prefix path. Recursive construction over finite levels gives one tree for all precisions; every next level is reached almost surely. Within zero-cost chains first-hit weights still agree with the designated canonical atom weights, including for multiple letters emitted together. At each depth every persistent bit has now locked and all first-hit atom weights at each $u$ are exactly its canonical expansion. This proves the criterion and sharp simultaneous attainment in the sense above.

The first-lock leaves for each value $(K,J)=(n,j)$ are a subset of the canonical powers for the single prefix $u$ indexing $j$; no repeated powers and no eventually all-one tail occur. If $L_n\to1$ these leaves form a complete tree for the joint selector and are precisely the binary digits of its probabilities. Hence this joint selector itself is a Knuth–Yao optimal tree, not merely an arbitrary sampler subsequently annotated with digits.

**2. Monotone densities.** Write $B=\|f\|_\infty$. A bin of depth $n$ whose average has bit $i$ on but $i\notin S(u)$ has a descendant whose average has that bit off. For a monotone continuous function the two averages cannot be on different sides of a bit transition without the closed bin meeting a boundary point of a level set $\{f=t\}$ for a grid level $t$ which is a multiple of $2^{-i}$ in $[0,B]$. Indeed each such level set is an interval, possibly a single point; a bin wholly inside a level plateau is constant and never witnesses a change. Endpoints on the dyadic grid may be counted for both adjacent bins. There are at most $1+B2^i$ levels, at most two endpoints per level, giving at most $4(1+B2^i)$ problematic bins. For each fixed present digit the uncertified mass is at most $\min\{2^{-i},4\,2^{-n}2^{-i}(1+B2^i)\}$. Sum this over indices bounded below, splitting at $i=n$. It yields $1-L_n=O_B((n+1)2^{-n})$ and hence $E K<\infty$. Every prefix receives the correct probability, so the generated real has density $f$.

The entropy of the discrete selector is finite: $H(K,J)\le H(K)+E K$ in bits, since $0\le J<2^K$, and any nonnegative integer with finite mean has finite entropy by the geometric reference law (for example $H(K)\le E K+1$). The optimal selector's expected completion cost $E T_{\rm lock}\le H(K,J)+2$ is finite. Withholding all interval digits until completion gives the very same exact draw of $(K,J)$, followed by fresh bits. For $K\le n$ the cost of its first $n$ digits agrees with the streamed cost (at $K=n$ the construction locks exactly at the first hit); when $K>n$ the increase is between zero and $T_{\rm lock}$. Dominated convergence proves the asserted $o(1)$ excess above the **exact** $C_n$.

**3. Why an upfront uniform excess need not be small.** Reflect the decreasing density to $f(x)=3x^2$ without changing either cost convention. The first digit is 0 with probability $1/8$. In any dyadic-uniform mixture the total mass assigned to a given interval $I$ is at most $|I|\inf_I f$. The root can receive no mass. The left half likewise has capacity zero. In the left half any quarter interval has capacity at most $3/64$ (the right quarter starts at $1/4$), and any interval of length at most $1/8$ has capacity at most $3/32$. In particular no single left-side interval can receive as much as $1/8$. An upfront tree cannot finish any leaf specifying such an interval in at most three flips: that leaf would already have probability at least $1/8$. Thus at coin depth 3 it has returned no 0; the optimal discrete Bernoulli tree returns 0 on one of the eight coin strings. The pointwise floor deficits for other outputs and all depths are nonnegative, and summing them with weights $2^{-k}$ gives the excess expected cost. This alone gives $E T_1-C_1\ge1/8$, independent of selector size or its optimality for $(K,J)$. It is an obstruction to the *uniform-in-precision* upfront convention, not to a limit as $n\to\infty$.

**4. Smoothness alone need not give eventual copying at exact optimality.** Let $D\subset(0,1)$ be a closed nowhere-dense set of positive length with no isolated points, for example a translated and scaled Smith–Volterra–Cantor set. In each bounded complementary gap inside its convex hull place a $C^\infty$ function $g_j$ supported in its interior, with integral zero, taking both signs. It may be obtained by subtracting two disjoint congruent nonnegative smooth bumps. Enumerate the gaps; rescale each nonzero pair so that $\max_{r\le j}\|g_j^{(r)}\|_\infty<2^{-j-2}$ for $j\ge1$. Then $f=1+\sum_j g_j$ is smooth, strictly positive, integrates to one and equals one on $D$. Every neighborhood of every non-endpoint $x\in D$ contains a full complementary gap: the perfect nowhere-dense set has two of its points in the neighborhood with a complementary point between them. Therefore within every sufficiently small dyadic cell containing such an $x$ in its interior are open intervals of densities above and below one. By continuity and $f(x)=1$ there are descendant averages arbitrarily close to 1 on either side (choose gaps in arbitrarily small neighborhoods and dyadic subcells inside the respective sign intervals). The integer bit with weight 1 is absent just below 1 and each fixed fractional bit is absent sufficiently close above 1. Larger bits are absent near 1. Therefore no atom of any height can persist on such a cell. Endpoints and dyadic rationals form a null exception. Integrate the unlocked mass density: on this portion of $D$, $m_{u_n(x)}\to f(x)=1$ and no bit locks, while elsewhere the residual is nonnegative. Fatou gives $\liminf_n(1-L_n)\ge |D|>0$. Thus no prefix-optimal streamed selector with an almost surely finite uniform tail exists for this smooth law. This concerns the eventual-copy restriction, not ordinary generation on a countably infinite decision tree, and does not rule out arbitrarily small positive excess.
