# Sharp two-bit worst-case asymptotic selector excess

In the conventions of the accompanying notes, for every continuous density $f$ of bounded variation on $[0,1]$ there is an upfront dyadic-interval selector, with finite expected coin cost for its joint draw, such that
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}(E T_n-C_n)\le2.$$
Combining with the separate persistent-gap construction gives, for either the streamed or upfront class,
$$\sup_{f\text{ strictly positive smooth}}\ \inf_{G\text{ an exact eventual-copy selector for }f}\ \limsup_{n\to\infty}(E_G T_n-C_n(f))=2.$$
The same equality holds replacing $\limsup$ by $\liminf$. This is asymptotic and worst over densities; it is neither a uniform bound at every precision for the upfront class nor a bound on *every* selector. It gives no finite-state cardinality bound.

**Randomly shifted dyadic cover.** A shift is used to prove existence and then fixed once: it costs no input randomness. Let $B=\|f\|_\infty$, and take $a$ uniformly in $[0,L)$, where $L>2B$ is a power of two. For a given $x$ put $z=f(x)$. Decompose the vertical interval $(a,a+z)$ up to null sets into its maximal dyadic tiles. For definiteness use the global dyadic grid on the line; strict containment of the closed tile in the open interval and noncontainment of its parent suffice. For fixed $x$, with probability one neither endpoint is dyadic. Fubini implies that for almost every fixed $a$ these endpoint exceptions occur for at most a null set of $x$. For each possible vertical tile $I$ of length $w$ let $U_I$ be the set of $x$ where the inequalities specifying that it is maximal hold strictly (its two endpoints inside and at least one parent endpoint strictly outside). This set is relatively open by continuity. On almost every vertical fiber the $U_I$ select precisely its maximal tiles. Partition $U_I$ up to null endpoints into maximal dyadic horizontal cells. Equivalently, use the first depth at which the closure of a horizontal cell relative to $[0,1]$ lies inside $U_I$; disjointness, coverage up to dyadic endpoints, and the estimates below hold with this convention. The resulting rectangles have disjoint interiors in each fiber up to null sets and joint area one. Each has mass $w2^{-K}$ for its horizontal depth $K$. It may be chosen by one leaf of exactly $K-\log_2w$ coins: these are nonnegative integers and exhaust Kraft mass. Then print that horizontal cell's $K$ digits and copy fresh coins. Integration in the vertical coordinate gives the exact desired law.

Define $A(a)=\int_0^1\sum_{I:\,x\in U_I}|I|(-\log_2|I|)\,dx$. The sum has a universal finite bound in absolute value depending on $B$: at a single scale there are at most two maximal tiles in a given interval (each must have an unavailable sibling, i.e. touch the left or right boundary of the run of contained tiles). The negative-index scales are finite. For $z>0$ let $w_0$ be the largest power of two not exceeding $z$. For $w\le B$ the expected count of contained vertical tiles of length $w$ in the shifted interval is $(z/w-1)_+$ by averaging the left endpoint modulo $w$. Subtract twice the expected number of contained parents; the parent calculation is valid by the choice of $L$ (and identically zero for lengths larger than $z$). The resulting expected number of maximal tiles is $0$ for $w>w_0$, $z/w_0-1$ at $w=w_0$, and $1$ at each $w=w_0 2^{-j}$ with $j\ge1$. Thus the mean vertical contribution at $x$ is $-z\log_2w_0+2w_0$. Put it equal to 0 when $z=0$. The ideal binary-atom cost $D(z)=\sum_i i2^{-i}b_i(z)$ is at least $-z\log_2w_0$ since no digit weight exceeds $w_0$. Hence
$$E_a A(a)\le\int_0^1D(f(x))\,dx+2\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=\int D(f)+2.$$

**Finite expected horizontal depth for almost every shift.** In a fixed depth-$n$ cell $J$ let $v_J=\sup_J f-\inf_J f$ (using the closure). For an eligible vertical tile of length $w$ at $x\in J$ to fail the containment test across $J$, the range $a+[\inf_J f,\sup_J f]$ must meet a point of $w\mathbb Z$: all of the upper-endpoint tests for the tile and its parent compare to that grid. The lower-endpoint tests at $a$ are independent of $x$. Except for null shifts, the failure probability is at most $\min(1,v_J/w)$, including $v_J=0$. At most two tiles at scale $w$ are eligible at a point. The expected mass at $x$ lying in rectangles with $K>n$ is therefore bounded by $2\sum_{w\le B}\min(w,v_J)$ over dyadic $w$. For $0\le v\le B$ this is at most $C v(1+\ln(B/v))$ for an absolute $C$, with value zero at $v=0$. Summing over $J$ with weight $2^{-n}$, use $\sum_J v_J\le \operatorname{Var}(f)$ and concavity and monotonicity of $v(1+\ln(B/v))$ on $[0,B]$. We get $E_a\Pr(K>n)=O_f((n+1)2^{-n})$ (the constant-density case has no failure). Summation shows $E K<\infty$ for almost every shift. Kraft decoding the rectangles then has finite expected cost $E K+A(a)$.

Choose such a good shift with $A(a)\le\int D(f)+2$, possible by the preceding average bound. Upfront rectangle sampling has $E T_n=n+A(a)+E[(K-n)_+]$; one may instead optimally sample the merged discrete law of $(K,J)$, which can only lower the expected initial cost without changing the subsequent cost. The endpoint relaxation for a deterministic root cell has no effect on the limit. For the dyadic-bin averages $m_n$, $C_n-n=\int D(m_n)$ and $m_n\to f$ by continuity. The function $D$ is lower semicontinuous on $[0,B]$: away from dyadic thresholds this follows by uniform control of the digit tail; at a positive dyadic value $z$ with least nonzero digit weight $w$, the terminating value equals the right limit while the left limit adds $2w$, as replacing $w$ by the entire tail $w/2+w/4+\cdots$ increases the cost by $\sum_{j\ge1}j w2^{-j}=2w$. At zero $D(z)\to0$. It is bounded below on $[0,B]$. Fatou gives $\liminf_n(C_n-n)\ge\int D(f)$. This proves the upper bound. An upfront selector is allowed in the streamed class by withholding its digits; the lower bound from the smooth examples holds for every streamed selector even with a different locking distribution and without integrability assumptions. The two equalities follow.
