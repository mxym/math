For almost every shift, vertical endpoints are not dyadic for almost every horizontal position by Fubini. Relative-open eligibility permits the disjoint maximal dyadic horizontal partition for each vertical tile; the rectangle law integrates to the original density. A rectangle's mass is an exact dyadic atom and the masses exhaust one, so Kraft coding samples them with the indicated input depths. At one vertical size, averaging the uniformly shifted grid counts contained tiles, then subtracts twice the contained-parent count, exactly counting maximal ones. The negative digit indices are bounded and at most two tiles occur at any one scale, so the averaged cost summation is legitimate. Its value for height z is z times the coarsest digit index plus twice the coarsest weight, which is no more than D(z)+2z.

For the horizontal partition, if a tile eligible at one point stops being eligible over its whole depth-n bin, some upper endpoint test for the tile or its parent changes along the range of f. These thresholds lie on the tile-length grid; the lower endpoint tests do not depend on position. Uniform shift bounds this event by the ratio of oscillation to grid spacing capped at one. Summing twice the tile mass over scales gives the stated logarithmic modulus, and bounded variation makes its dyadic average summable in n. Thus almost-every shift has finite expected horizontal depth and a finite cost selector; selecting a fixed shift with average vertical cost no more than the upper bound incurs no random input. Merging labels and applying optimal discrete coding can only reduce mean initial cost. Integrable horizontal tail vanishes, and the terminating binary-cost map is lower semicontinuous (a left-hand dyadic carry has larger cost). Fatou therefore compares the delayed cost against the correct exact prefix benchmark, even if that benchmark oscillates. Together with the arbitrarily-near-two lower examples this proves supremum of infima equal to two, for each cited staging and limit-limsup convention; it is not a uniform early-prefix assertion.
