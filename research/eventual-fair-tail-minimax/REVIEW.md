* Each first-hit leaf already copying at output bin u splits with equal weights in every descendant; exact Knuth–Yao optimality makes its place in each respective canonical binary expansion compulsory. The construction attains the sharper first-hit bound by locking before proceeding to the next digit.
* For sufficiency, forced uniform halvings use exactly the matching descendant bits. Sort remaining child and parent atoms by weight. At any internal parent boundary s with last capacity w, the remaining total is strictly less than w by the terminating-preferred binary representation. A putative crossing child atom smaller than w aligns with s, and one larger than w cannot even fit its right endpoint before the total. Relative dyadic alignment then gives the requisite prefix tree within every parent. Its leaf weights are exactly canonical, even across deterministic emissions, and indefinitely many no-coin output digits along one positive cylinder would contradict bounded density. All finite-depth laws are therefore correct and exactly optimal. First-lock leaves for each fixed interval label are a subset of distinct canonical powers at that bin, hence canonical for that label itself; this proves optimality for the joint selector, not just its marginals.
* Monotonicity bounds the number of intervals meeting digit-threshold endpoints by O(1+B 2^i); intervals contained wholly in an exact-height plateau do not suffer a bit change. Combining this with the total possible mass of one bit and summing on either side of i=n gives the stated locking tail. Finite mean depth bounds entropy of depth and interval index, hence the optimal selector's mean cost. Withholding the emitted prefix costs nothing extra on K≤n and an integrably dominated amount on K>n. For the exact upfront uniform-in-n obstruction, no dyadic interval contained in the left half of the reflected cubic can carry a full mass 1/8. Any completed left-output selector path by three input coins would require at least that mass. The associated first-digit tail deficit is at least 1/8 and cannot be canceled by other depths because the floor optimality lower bounds hold pointwise.
* For failure even with a strictly positive smooth density, the mean-zero opposing bumps in complementary gaps have summably small derivatives at every fixed order. At almost every point of the positive-measure Cantor set all containing dyadic cells have descendant averages tending to 1 from both sides; every potentially positive binary digit is absent on one such side. Thus no bit persists there and the residual mass is at least the measure of that set in the limit. An unrestricted countably infinite sampler is not excluded.
