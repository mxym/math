# Original questions, related results and scope

* Swee Hong Chan and Igor Pak, [*Computational complexity of counting coincidences*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114776), *Theoretical Computer Science* (2024), §6.8, Conjecture 6.5; see also their [author manuscript](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/DominoPermanent16.pdf). The subject is the count of all nonempty tree **subgraphs** of a simple planar graph. The conjecture requires polynomial size in the bit length of the count. Connectedness is not required; the paper explicitly uses isolated vertices. Conciseness is not counting complexity, and the problem about **spanning** trees in their Example 5.21 is different.
* Éva Czabarka, László Székely and Stephan Wagner, [*The inverse problem for certain tree parameters*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2009.07.004), *Discrete Applied Mathematics* **157** (2009), 3314–3319 ([full author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2749697/)). Their Theorem 1 already determines the unrestricted connected-tree spectrum exactly: all positive integers except 34 explicitly listed numbers, the last of them 89. Their construction in §3 gives caterpillar hosts for every sufficiently large count and also reports a finite caterpillar exception list. Their standard two-coordinate rooted-subtree recursions are a direct ingredient here; they use a different arithmetic construction, involving sums of shifted pentagonal numbers. The theorem as stated does not bound the host order by a logarithm. We do **not** claim the first eventual connected-tree realization; our conclusion couples maximum degree three with optimal asymptotic order. We do not require the hosts constructed here to be caterpillars.
* Lucas Alves de Almeida, [*The inverse problem for the number of connected induced subgraphs*](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00029v1) (November 2026, author preprint). Theorems 1.1–1.2 classify counts of **connected vertex sets** and give sharp host orders allowing arbitrary connected graphs. That is a different function on general hosts, though it agrees with the subtree count on trees. Its coning and near-coning constructions do not require subcubic tree hosts. The same work also discusses **all connected subgraphs**, which can contain cycles. Neither general-graph count is the planar all-tree-subgraph function here.
* Ryan McCulloch, Brendan D. McKay, Alireza Salahshoori and Thomas Zaslavsky, [*The Cycle Counts of Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-026-03047-3), *Graphs and Combinatorics* **42**, 53 (2026), §2: Lemma 7 realizes a tree as the inner dual of a 2-connected outerplanar graph, and Theorem 8 gives the cycle–subtree bijection. The cubic chord construction uses the authors' convention that parallel edges and 2-cycles are allowed. The logarithmic-order consequence and a simple **subcubic**, not cubic, version are in [the appendix](CYCLES.md); the unrestricted cycle-range and graph duality results are theirs.
