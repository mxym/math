# Size consequence for the known inner-dual cycle correspondence

McCulloch, McKay, Salahshoori and Zaslavsky, [*The Cycle Counts of Graphs*, Section 2, Lemma 7 and Theorem 8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-026-03047-3) (2026), give the following prior construction and proof of its count. Retain only its quantitative consequence here.

For a tree T with v≥3 vertices place v−1 noncrossing chords, none sharing endpoints, in a circle as in their Lemma 7. The inner dual of the resulting outerplanar graph is T, and their Theorem 8 bijects cycles with subtrees of T. The outer circle is a spanning cycle, so the graph is 2-connected. It has 2(v−1) vertices (the chord endpoints), and is cubic.

**Their convention permits parallel edges and cycles of length two.** The cubic result above is about loopless multigraphs, not simple cubic graphs. If a simple witness is wanted, subdivide each edge once, using a different vertex for every original edge. Subdivision preserves 2-connectivity and cycle count (including a length-two cycle becoming a length-four cycle); it results in a simple graph of maximum degree three. To preserve outerplanarity when subdividing an interior chord, one should instead subdivide only boundary edges involved in parallel pairs: each such pair is one chord and one circular edge for v≥3, the chords being pairwise disjoint and the circular edges forming a simple cycle. Insert one new vertex in the circular edge of each pair. The graph is still outerplanar, is simple and 2-connected, and has at most 3(v−1) vertices. It need no longer be cubic.

Hence every sufficiently large k is the number of cycles of a 2-connected outerplanar cubic loopless multigraph on no more than 596 log₁₇ k vertices, and of a 2-connected **simple subcubic outerplanar graph** on no more than 894 log₁₇ k vertices. For either class logarithmic order is necessary since k≤2^{|E|}≤2^{3|V|/2}. Without the logarithmic bound, eventual cycle-count existence via this correspondence is prior. No statement about the missing counts in their Section 6 for simple **cubic** graphs follows from subdivision.
