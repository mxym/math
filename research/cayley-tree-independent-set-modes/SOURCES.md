# Primary sources and scope of comparison

* [Steven Heilman, *Independent Sets of Random Trees and Sparse Random Graphs*, Journal of Graph Theory 109 (2025), 294–309, Question 8.2](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225). For each random **labeled** tree, locate its own maximum coefficient; the published question chooses the greatest maximizing index in a tie. It is not the question of maximizing an expected coefficient. Lemma A.1 credits Bedrosian for the tree count with a prescribed independent set; Theorem 2.10 recalls Pittel's distinct result on the largest feasible cardinality. The arXiv preprint numbers the question differently.
* [Abdul Basit and David Galvin, *On the independent set sequence of a tree*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12562), Theorems 1.4/1.7 in the authors' paper: long terminal and initial intervals of coefficient monotonicity, starting/ending at approximately 0.347n/0.280n. We neither prove nor improve their adjacent-coefficient inequalities. Heilman's paper also proves partial monotonicity and emphasizes distinctions between planted and uniform models. [Heilman, *Tree/Endofunction Bijections and Concentration Inequalities*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06724) uses tree/function relationships to establish concentration: this methodology is not new. Our use of independent parent pointers conditioned to a tree costs a factor n, harmless for fixed exponential deviations. Bounded differences is classical. More directly, [Pemantle–Peres, *Concentration of Lipschitz Functionals of Determinantal and Other Strong Rayleigh Measures*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548313000345) already gives exponential concentration of log Z about its tree expectation, since changing one edge costs at most log(1+λ). The parent-map proof is an elementary alternative; pressure concentration itself is not new.
* [Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*](https://www2.math.uu.se/~svantejs/papers/sj264.pdf), Theorem 7.1 (local limit at the root), Theorem 7.12(ii) (empirical fringe frequencies), Example 10.2 (conditioned Poisson offspring for uniform labeled trees, and size-biased Poisson along the infinite spine). These two limits are different. The second is used for an additive pressure limit; the first is used to bound the occupation at a uniform vertex. Those limits and their interpretations are not new.
* [D. Ralaivaosaona, M. Šileikis and S. Wagner, *A Central Limit Theorem for Almost Local Additive Tree Functionals*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-019-00622-4), especially [arXiv Theorem 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00467): the logarithm of the **total** number of independent sets is an almost-local additive functional and satisfies a nondegenerate CLT over conditioned trees. Their §6.1, equations (34)–(44), explicitly use the unit-activity tree toll and exponential level-width bounds. We use the tree factorization in its activity-weighted form, and do not claim a new zero-field log-count limit or CLT.
* [A. Dembo, A. Montanari and N. Sun, *Factor models on locally tree-like graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.4821), especially Theorem 1.12 and §§1.2.3,2.4: general hard-core free-energy results for locally tree-like graphs under branching/uniqueness hypotheses, with ordered boundary root marginals. The one-ended Poisson Kesten tree has branching number one (finite side branches), and one finite-weight spin chain on its spine. The presence of deterministic pressure and truncation by opposing boundary conditions is established methodology, not a claim of conceptual priority.
* [E. Andriantiana, V. R. Misanantenaina and S. Wagner, *The average size of independent sets of graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08290): independent-set mean as a logarithmic derivative; sharp deterministic tree extrema attained by path/star. This is not a random-tree modal location. [E. X. Fang, J. Lu, E. Nevo, Y. Yao and H. Zheng, *Unimodality of Independence Polynomials for Sufficiently Large Forests*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20961), equations (4.1)–(4.2), give the same conditional odds and first-log-derivative recursions used here. Their Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 1.1 prove a uniform hard-core size CLT/linear variance and unimodality, respectively. These already localize every mode to O(√n) of the individual forest's own mean: e.g., Chebyshev puts most mass in an O(√n) window; unimodality bounds the distance of a mode to this window by comparing monotone intervals of equal length. This observation transfers to **each sufficiently large deterministic forest**, but it does not identify a single deterministic leading coefficient for a random labeled tree. [W. Li, K. Vallier and T. Zhang, *Unimodality of Forest Independence Polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07943), crediting Zhang–Li, state unimodality at all sizes. Our result neither establishes the classical tree/forest unimodality assertion nor relies on it for the fixed-ε exponential bound.
* [B. Pittel, *Normal convergence problem? Two moments and a recurrence may be the clues*](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoap/1029962872) is not needed for calculating the modal coefficient; Heilman Theorem 2.10 records the independently established maximum-cardinality result with coefficient ρ satisfying ρe^ρ=1. The optional annealed comparison uses the classical expected coefficient; the distinguishing assertion is a *strict linear separation* from its modal location, not the classical expectation formula or its elementary calculus.

The paper and preprint searches covered author sites, arXiv, JGT and cross-citations under “independent set sequence”, “Cayley”, “mode”, “occupancy”, “independent set average size”, “random labeled trees”, “hard-core”, “quenched/annealed”, “fringe” and “Galton–Watson”, and specifically the two September/October 2026 forest-unimodality manuscripts. This is a research-status description, not an assurance of priority.
