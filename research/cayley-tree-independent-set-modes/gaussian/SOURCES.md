# Sources and scope of the Gaussian result

* [Steven Heilman, *Independent Sets of Random Trees and Sparse Random Graphs*, JGT 109 (2025), Question 8.2](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225), asks whether the modal coefficient index of a uniform labeled tree concentrates. The [linear-scale result (#125)](../README.md) identifies $\mu$; the [square-root upper bound (#126)](../square-root/README.md) gives the bounded bias and centered variance controls we use. Neither note proves an environmental fluctuation law.
* [Dimbinaina Ralaivaosaona, Matas Šileikis, and Stephan Wagner, *A Central Limit Theorem for Almost Local Additive Tree Functionals*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-019-00622-4), [arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00467), is the main probabilistic tool. Its Theorem 1 and Proposition 7 supply the Gaussian limit for descendant-local tolls of controlled root-degree growth; the normalized variance convergence follows from the finite-support comparison in the proof in §5, not merely from weak convergence. Its §6.1, including (44) and the proof of Theorem 10, supplies the stabilization and fringe-replacement precedents. Their independent-set statistic is the log **count**, not the conditional **mean size** or the modal index. We instead approximate that mean in centered $L^2$ using full balls and convert each truncated score into a descendant-local toll. The explicit pair of matched gadgets proves nondegeneracy for this statistic.
* [Fang, Lu, Nevo, Yao, and Zheng](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20961v1), Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 5.1: sufficiently large forests are unimodal, and the size of a hard-core sample on a **fixed** forest satisfies a uniform CLT with variance linear in its order. Their results imply every mode differs from that forest's own mean by $o(\sqrt n)$. This deduction is detailed in §4 of the proof. It transfers the new environmental theorem without identifying the two sources of randomness. [Li, Vallier, and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07943) give all-forest unimodality.
* [Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*](https://www2.math.uu.se/~svantejs/papers/sj264.pdf), Theorem 7.12(ii) and Example 10.2, provides the fringe-frequency law and the Poisson tree representation of uniform labeled trees. [Andriantiana, Misanantenaina, and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08290) study the mean independent-set size as a deterministic invariant. [Isaev, Southwell, and Zhukovskii](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548321000523) prove a general CLT for stable functionals on uniform labeled trees, including pattern counts. These are methodological precedents. Results for the size of a maximum or greedily grown independent set, or the mode after averaging coefficients across trees, concern different statistics.
