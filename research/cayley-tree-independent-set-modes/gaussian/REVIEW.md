## 1. Claim and starting estimates

The randomness in the theorem is **the uniform labeled tree**, rooted temporarily at fixed label 1. The statistic is its hard-core mean at activity 1, A = E[|I| given the tree], *not* one sample of I. The claimed limiting variance is positive but not identified exactly. The same variance and centered limit hold for any chosen mode including extra-random tie-breaking. No local limit theorem, convergence rate or O(1) bias for the modes follows.

I checked the full-ball comparison used in the preceding [square-root manuscript](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/126). The one- and two-root Kesten masses have boundary factors b_i; contracting disjoint prescribed balls yields the product b_i times the powered total number of allowed boundary endpoints, including for adjacent boundaries. For total T small relative to sqrt(n), the ratio of Cayley to independent Kesten shape probabilities differs from 1 by O(T²/n). The error for larger balls absorbs polynomial moments; colliding roots give an additional factor 1/n by fixing both labels in the enumeration of a marked bounded-radius connecting subtree. Its skeleton after suppressing unmarked degree-two vertices has bounded combinatorial complexity depending on the number of marks, not on radius. Thus the estimates apply uniformly to *signed* tests bounded by ball size, with only polynomial dependence on radius; the covariance follows by comparing both the joint moment and the single-root means. In particular sd(sum f) is bounded by sqrt(n) times a polynomial in h with constants independent of f.

In the coordinates y=log(1+odds), the derivative of the parent message in the sum of child coordinates has magnitude at most 1/2. The occupation is 1-Lipschitz in the root coordinate. The change in passing between successive empty-boundary balls is at most (log 2)2^{1-h} times ball size. On each finite tree the expansion stabilizes. The preceding covariance bound applied to normalized increments and Minkowski in **centered** L² give the summable uniform tail. On truncating H_h at size K, the discarded score times K is bounded by ball size, so the cost in the same norm is C poly(h)/K, not a per-site independence estimate. The earlier note gives EA=μn+O(1); it does not supply the CLT or positive limiting variance here.

## 2. Localizing and applying the primary tree CLT

Root and order the conditioned Poisson(1) tree, equivalently use a uniform Cayley tree rooted at 1 and random order among siblings, then forget labels. A vertex v of depth ≥h has one hth ancestor a; any path exiting the subtree at a passes distance h+1, not h. Thus the full h-ball is preserved, including boundary membership. Its contribution to the descendant sum is counted once; omitted near-root vertices contribute at most |B_{h-1}(1)|, bounded in L² for fixed h. The anchored toll needs no children beyond depth 2h in the rooted tree. For each first child of a, consider only path prefixes extendible to eligible endpoints. The endpoint ball then contains *all* neighbors of each internal prefix vertex, so at most K branches continue there. One obtains deg(a) K^{h-1} endpoints, including h=1 and the zero-degree root. This estimate for every finite tree avoids a hard bound on the arbitrary root degree.

[Ralaivaosaona–Šileikis–Wagner, Theorem 1, Proposition 7 and §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00467), [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-019-00622-4): critical positive finite variance and E ξ^{2α+1}<∞ hold for Poisson(1) with α=1. Their root-degree bound is the one just proved. In (3), at M≥2h the toll of the deeper truncation equals that of the M-truncation and is measurable there. Below 2h, the error is bounded in mean by a constant times the finite expected Kesten root degree, uniformly in N≥M, using conditional Jensen. In (4) take M_n=2h+ceil(3 log(n+1)); the conditioned finite-tree truncation error is zero. The sequence p_M constant before 2h and exponentially decreasing afterwards satisfies (5): a_n=O_{h,K}((1+log²(n+1))/sqrt(n)) and sum a_n/n<∞. Proposition 7 permits exact expectation centering. Their §5 proof supplies the crucial *variance* statement rather than relying merely on weak convergence: finite-support approximating variables have variances converging to γ_N², these converge to γ², and the variance estimate following Lemma 8 shows the centered normalized L² errors vanish uniformly. The bounded root error vanishes in normalized L² for fixed h.

Now take K_h large enough, and fix h before taking n→∞. If e_h→0 bounds the uniform centered L² distance to the full statistic, the limiting standard deviations of two approximants differ by ≤e_h+e_j. Therefore their limit exists, and the standard deviations of the full normalized variables converge to it by the same argument. Characteristic functions differ by ≤|t| e_h; passing to the two limits proves the Gaussian law. No uniformity in the RSW constants as h grows is presupposed.

## 3. Positive variance, shape conditioning, independent check

Independent enumeration by root bit gives absent/present (count, first derivative) S=((130,395),(40,140)), R=((117,336),(36,120)); the numbers of root-preserving automorphisms by explicit permutation are 2 and 4. The same positive factor 10/9 multiplies *both* root-bit masses, and both conditional means increase by exactly 1/6. For each allowed exterior and root-bit assignment this preserves its normalized probability while adding 1/6 to its conditional size. The conclusion survives arbitrary exterior structure, including the presence of other gadgets.

Distinct descendant subtrees of the same size cannot overlap. Once all selected subtrees are collapsed to marked slots and each labeled size-ten set and designated root retained, every internal filling by either eligible shape is admissible. The proper subtrees inside a slot have size <10, and proper ancestors have size >10, unchanged by refilling, so there are no newly selected or lost untouched slots. The number of fillings per slot is 9!/Aut independently, giving p=2/3 for S and conditional distribution a translate of Bin(N,2/3)/6. In particular no assumption that the two shapes occur equally frequently is needed.

There is also a direct check of the only frequency needed for the variance lower bound, independent of the general fringe theorem. For n>10 and a specified rooted shape U, there are (n−1)_{10}/Aut(U) labeled choices excluding root 1 for the marked fringe. Choose the tree on the remaining n−10 vertices and the outside vertex adjacent to the designated fringe root: (n−10)^{n−11} completions (one if n=11). Divide the product by n^{n−2} to obtain E N_U; in particular (E N_U)/n→e^{-10}/Aut(U). The total-variance bound hence gives σ²≥(3e^{-10}/4)(2/9)/36 = e^{-10}/216. For the optional uniform interval anti-concentration one needs more than this expectation: the conditioned-tree fringe frequency law does give N/n→3e^{-10}/4 in probability, since these unordered shapes are finite unions of plane patterns. Then each conditional translated lattice contributes at most 1+6L sites to an interval of length L; its largest conditional atom is O(1/sqrt(N)). This is not an assertion about local limit asymptotics.

## 4. Why the random mode has the same law

[Fang–Lu–Nevo–Yao–Zheng, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20961v1) concern a *random independent set conditional on a deterministic forest*, with uniform CLT and c n ≤ Var ≤ C n at activity 1, and unimodality for sufficiently large forests. [Li–Vallier–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07943) separately establish all-forest unimodality. Neither is an environmental CLT. Fix ε>0. If some mode is ≥A+ε sqrt(n) along a sequence then for d=ε/(upper standard-deviation constant), place two adjacent equal-length integer blocks below the mode, with limiting standardized endpoints d/4,d/2,3d/4. Their masses, by monotonicity up to this mode, satisfy earlier≤later even in case of ties; by conditional CLT the limits satisfy the strict opposite inequality on the positive side of a standard Gaussian. Finite lattice rounding costs vanish since sd≥c sqrt(n). Reflection rules out a too-small mode. A violating sequence argument gives deterministic uniformity over forests and all maximizers. The normalized mode-minus-mean difference thus tends uniformly to zero, including in L² for any tree-dependent or randomized tie choice. This transfers both the CLT and the variance limit, but does not establish that the modal expectations differ from μn by O(1).
