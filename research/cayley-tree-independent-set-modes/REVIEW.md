## 1. Which problem is being answered?

[Heilman, JGT 109 (2025), Question 8.2](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225) considers a uniform unrooted labeled tree and asks about the **greatest index** attaining the largest independent-set coefficient. It does not prescribe a fluctuation scale; §1.1 separately discusses whether the center is close to half the asymptotic independence number. The inclusion of the constant coefficient in the candidate cannot create an extra tie for n≥2, as the linear coefficient is n>1. For each fixed positive epsilon, the conclusion bounds the probability that **any** global maximizing index lies outside the interval of radius epsilon n about the deterministic center. It entails a first-order law of large numbers but does not assert a Gaussian or sqrt(n) law for the random center/modes.

## 2. Analytic checks

1. Give the Cayley tree a fixed root label and independent uniform child order. Each fixed ordered shape has (n−1)! bijections respecting the root label. Each resulting labeled ordered tree has probability proportional to the product of reciprocal child factorials, exactly the Poisson(1) tree weight conditioned on size n. All tests are invariant to the auxiliary orders. The finite fringe distribution convergence in [Janson, *Probability Surveys*, Thm. 7.12(ii), with Example 10.2](https://www2.math.uu.se/~svantejs/papers/sj264.pdf) extends to bounded tolls on the countable shape space by retaining a finite set of limiting probability >1−eta. The limiting unconditioned Poisson tree is finite a.s. Thm. 7.1 concerns a *different* local limit when looking from the conditioned tree's root. The example number 10.2 is that of the published text, not the original 2011 arXiv v1.
2. With in/out odds R and occupation fraction q=R/(1+R), the root-deletion partition identity factors Z as the product of all (1+R_v). Write t=log activity. On any compact t-interval q≤a<1. Differentiating log odds gives an alternating sum over descendant paths of products of the q's along them, so its absolute value is bounded by the discounted population sum, including the root. This bound has finite expectation 1/(1−a), since the ordinary critical Poisson process has mean generation size 1. Thus differentiation under the fringe expectation is justified (also for nearby nonzero tilts). The derivative of the toll is q times the derivative of **log odds**, not just the fringe occupancy.
3. Conditioning the n−1 independent parent labels to have all forward paths end at 1 has probability exactly 1/n. Conditional on this event orientations toward 1 give a bijection with labeled trees. Outside the conditioning, permit self-loops and identify duplicate edges. Deleting the tail i of the one altered parent arrow always yields the same induced graph. Partition sums in either configuration lie in the same interval [B,(1+exp(t))B], by injection into sets not containing i. This includes all loops and cycles; the log cost is one log(1+exp(t)), not twice that. McDiarmid concerns the **unconditional** center, not the conditional mean. Paying the factor n for conditioning leaves exponential concentration at every fixed positive scale. The independent fringe convergence pins the deterministic unconditional center to the same p(t): two events within u of the two centers both occur with high conditional probability, so their distance is at most 2u eventually. One could instead appeal directly to the known uniform-spanning-tree concentration theorem in §4.
4. At ±h, differentiability supplies one-sided slopes with slack epsilon/2. Pressure errors h epsilon/8 at each of the two arguments contribute at most h epsilon/4 to log moment differences. Both conditional size-tail probabilities are then at most exp(−nh epsilon/4). No exponential number of events is being union bounded: just three tree pressures. Since some size has conditional probability ≥1/(n+1) and all modes have the same mass, every maximizing atom is excluded from either tail. The same event gives, by boundedness, convergence of the joint-experiment mean size/n. This average is **over uniform trees first**, then their uniform independent sets.
5. Label exchangeability identifies that mean with root-1 occupancy. At any fixed depth the actual messages in a finite tree are bounded between the two parity-alternating extreme recursions with boundary odds 0 and 1. Under the conditioned-tree local limit, expectation of each bounded finite-depth endpoint converges (countably many shapes, all finite locally, truncate the countable law if desired). The Kesten root has one spine child plus an independent Poisson(1) number of ordinary children: biasing degree by d gives the law of 1+Poisson(1). Its ordering is immaterial to the symmetric product. A finite h gives a genuine interval for the limiting occupation; there is no truncation heuristic that assumes h→∞.

## 3. Independent arithmetic and optional separation

The original algorithms were run under python -O and reproduced. Their degree truncation retains less than the true mass e^(−1)/d! in every component and sends all missing mass to the appropriate extremum. Rounding a division value outwards and rounding individual atom masses down before completing them at the extremum are stochastically valid. At a spine node the ordinary product is newly computed at the current level and independent of the *previous* spine level, with the latter's order reversed in division. The diagnostic internal stop condition of the baseline author code tests a coarser interval than its printed one; the exact printed endpoints and the independent enclosure below certify the advertised numbers. For release, hard-coding the asserted tight endpoints is an optional regression improvement.

My `independent.py` computes whole CDFs of the rounded quotient **without** scattering input probabilities into individual output bins. For lower output and index r<K,
  
  Pr(floor(i K/(K+j)) ≤ r | j) = CDF_X(min(K, ((r+1)(K+j)−1) div K)).
  
For upper output it uses CDF_X(min(K, r(K+j) div K)). In probability-grid units round the full CDF **up** for a lower law, **down** for an upper law. This preserves stochastic order and gives legitimate nondecreasing integer CDFs. A separate upper Taylor-geometric bound on e provides the Poisson mass lower bounds; allocate all missing degree mass at a stochastic extremum. The alternative parameters K=512, M=2^43, D=11, height 14 give [0.305007,0.307248], lying in the primary advertised [0.304684,0.307543]. Exhaustive finite mapping enumeration for n=2..6 checked 8,476 parent maps, precisely 1,441 admissible trees, 4,323 exact partition/log-derivative/root-occupation comparisons and 307,521 coordinate/positive-activity comparisons including inadmissible maps.

**Separate expected-coefficient corollary: PASS.** For a fixed k-set, matrix tree on K_n with its induced clique edges deleted has the two displayed nontrivial eigenvalues; dividing tree counts gives (1−k/n)^(k−1). This matches [Heilman, Lemma A.1, after Bedrosian](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225). Use the lemma, not the inexact exponent in the display labelled (15) in that version's body. For 1≤k<n the two entropy bounds differ by at most log(n+1)/n plus log n/n; g(0)=0, g→−∞ at 1, and g has strictly decreasing derivative from +∞ to −∞, so every maximizing normalized index converges to its unique interior maximizer. This annealed characterization alone follows easily from the old formula and is not the new point.

The optional author's [0.305300,0.306955] follows independently via `annealed_independent.py` (same different CDF engine, K=960), which even gives [0.305611,0.306643]. Without using an artanh logarithm series, the code proves at x=0.3074 that (1−x)^2/x > exp(x/(1−x)) by bounding the exponential power-series tail as a geometric series. It also exhausts Prüfer codes and all subset sizes for n=2..7, checking the original mean formula. Thus x_*−mu>0.000445 by the stated author parameters; the two eventual margins 0.00002 cost a total 0.00004, leaving strictly more than 0.0004 n for every realized global mode except with exponentially small tree probability. An actual sampled mode must not be confused with the deterministic maximizer of an expected coefficient; the latter need not be the expectation of a sampled mode.

## 4. Provenance and scope boundaries

* [Basit–Galvin, EJC 28(3) (2021), Theorems 4 and 7](https://www3.nd.edu/~dgalvin1/pdf/journal/random_trees_J.pdf) prove typical monotonicity on initial/terminal intervals. Their 0.307 bound near Problem 8 is for **independent domination**, not the most frequent size. Heilman proves further monotonicity bounds and specifically raises the random-mode question. The maximum independent-set fraction rho is an older Meir–Moon/Pittel result (the latter as in Heilman's Thm. 2.10); rho<0.6 follows already from the rational second-order exponential lower bound in the draft. Thus the primary enclosure separates the mode from rho/2.
* [Ralaivaosaona–Šileikis–Wagner, §6.1, eqs. (34)–(44)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00467) already use the unweighted in/out recursion, the additive log-partition toll and an exponential level-width local approximation to obtain the unit-activity **log-total-count** CLT; this is not a limiting mode theorem. Janson already supplies both local limits. [Dembo–Montanari–Sun, Ann. Probab. 41 (2013), §1.2.3 Thm. 1.12 and §2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.4821) treat general hard-core pressures on locally tree-like graphs and antitone boundary recursions. The finite-tree identities, boundary method, and general pressure/marginal principle are not new.
* [Pemantle–Peres, CPC 23 (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548313000345) prove subgaussian tails for edge-Lipschitz functions of uniform spanning trees. Toggling one edge of an arbitrary graph changes log Z by at most log(1+lambda) by the same endpoint-deletion argument. Taking K_n, their result already provides exponential tails at each fixed positive pressure scale about the true tree expectation (whose normalized limit also follows from bounded convergence in probability). The candidate gives an elementary product-map alternative; do **not** present concentration of the tree pressure by itself as a new general phenomenon.
* [Fang–Lu–Nevo–Yao–Zheng, September 2026, arXiv:2609.20961, Thm. 1.1 and Prop. 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20961) establish sufficient-size unimodality for every forest, and a conditional hard-core size CLT and O(n) variance uniformly over forests at unit fugacity. This already places every mode of a large fixed forest within O(sqrt(n)) of **its own** conditional mean. Indeed, by Chebyshev a central interval of length O(sqrt(n)) has ≥3/4 mass and contains an atom of mass at least c/sqrt(n); every atom between that atom and any global mode has at least its mass by unimodality, bounding their distance. This is stronger on the conditional scale and does not produce an ensemble-specific deterministic center, its strict separation from the expected-coefficient center, or an environment tail around that center. In particular, no new result on general forest unimodality should be inferred.
* Direct website, article and repository keyword checks (mode, peak, independent set sizes, Cayley, Galton–Watson, quenched/annealed) located no earlier explicit statement of this deterministic Cayley-mode concentration or numerical separation. The supplied source/archive scans at main `eb118772b16792709d6f6bc5a42cac5bca2f7351` read 14,929 distinct main blobs, another 277 tag-only blobs, and inspected archives, PDFs and supplemental release assets. Inspected hits notably include a split-graph **dominating** sequence and hub graph-cover results; neither is the present theorem.
