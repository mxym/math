# Independent internal audit: square-root window for Cayley-tree modal indices

**Verdict: PASS** for the claims and model of the frozen SQRT manuscript, SHA-256 `8f4de98e5edd9dce78f214155e134c92c11c5b3376ccda46fcf6b694c2159c16`. I independently derived the one- and two-root comparison and its application; I found no gap. This is AI-assisted internal review, not external validation, an optimal-scale statement, a central limit theorem, or a priority claim.

**Exact disjoint-ball count.** First prescribe the actual labeled vertex sets, roots and internal ball edges. A vertex at distance less than the given radius cannot carry a further edge. Contract the balls; each new supervertex has weight equal to its boundary size $b_i$, since each incident edge chooses its attachment independently. Every quotient tree with such choices expands uniquely, and no external path can shorten distances inside the balls (the expanded graph is a tree). This is still true for an edge between two boundary vertices of different balls. The ordinary multivariate Prüfer identity gives $\prod b_i(n-U)^{n-T+l-2}$ extensions of a *labeled* prescription. For unlabeled shapes, order all their nonroot abstract positions; the number of embeddings modulo independent rooted automorphisms is $(n-l)_{T-l}/\prod |\mathrm{Aut}(B_i)|$, with the roots fixed and distinct. In the regime used, every boundary is nonempty. A zero boundary in a ball means that the connected full tree is captured, excluded on the small-ball domain. Each independent critical PGW shape has probability $e^{-u_i}/|\mathrm{Aut}(B_i)|$ by canceling factorial multiplicities in the multiset of offspring. Summing all distinguished terminal-spine choices gives the Kesten factor $b_i$ without an extra factorial. The powers of $n$ cancel exactly when dividing by the uniform count $n^{n-2}$ and the product Kesten mass, giving (S1). The log of each normalized factor on $T\le\sqrt n/2$ has absolute value $O(T^2/n)$ including the exponent shift; exponentiation is uniform since the last bound is $O(1)$.

**Moment bounds and overlap.** Minimal connecting subtrees for a fixed number of marks (including any coincidences) have at most $O_d(1)$ marked or branching vertices after suppressing unmarked degree-two vertices. This includes the roots even if one lies internally on a path or is itself a mark. All leaves of that minimal tree are marked/fixed. Every contributing tree has $O_d(r+1)$ edges, so the finite set of reduced labeled-mark shapes has polynomially many choices of positive path lengths. At most $n^{v-a}$ embeddings are possible for each expanded shape with $a$ fixed roots. The inclusion probability of a fully specified $v$-vertex subtree is $v/n^{v-1}$ (contraction with unrestricted $v$ attachment points, including $v=n$ directly). Multiplying gives a polynomial times 1 for a one-root moment, and times $1/n$ for two fixed roots whose distance is $\le2r$. These are upper bounds, so overcounting by automorphisms does not invalidate them. Passing to Kesten moments uses bounded truncations at each fixed $r$ then monotone convergence; the bound is polynomial with constants uniform in $r$. Incidentally, in this exchangeable setting (S2) also follows by deterministic double counting: for $\mathrm{dist}(1,2)\le2r$, each radius-$r$ ball at either root lies in $B_{3r}(1)$. Average over label 2 conditional on the unlabelled tree rooted at 1, obtaining an extra factor $|B_{2r}(1)|/(n-1)$. Single-root moments then suffice.

**Full weighted test-function comparison.** For signed $|f_i|\le t_i$ integrate the ratio error on disjoint small balls against the product Kesten law. Both exceptional large-size expectations cost at most $4/n$ times the corresponding mixed moment of $T^2\prod t_i$. Cauchy–Schwarz or the sum bound by a single higher moment gives the required polynomial uniformity; no independence is assumed between the two Cayley balls. Their overlap is included in the event covered by (S2). At $r=0$ the boundary is the root with no prescribed children, as required. These steps justify (S3) at unequal radii, including bounds uniform over signed functions and all radii (large radii simply give a loose right side).

**Stabilization and covariance.** In coordinates $y=\log(1+R)$ each input coordinate of $y'=\log(1+e^{-\sum y})$ has partial derivative of magnitude at most $1/2$. A change at depth $h-1$ therefore propagates along the unique path with factor at most $2^{-(h-1)}$; summing the per-vertex changes pays the level width and bounding that by the ball size is valid. The root occupancy map adds no factor bigger than 1. The radius-0 approximant has odds zero, not one; leaves above the boundary retain the actual recursion. Once the radius exceeds the tree's eccentricity the exact occupancy is recovered. The normalized increments are bounded by ball size, so polynomial moments are dominated by the geometric amplitude singly and in the two-index series. This licenses Tonelli for absolute values and (S3) termwise, proving $\mathbb E p_1=\mu+O(1/n)$ and $\mathbb E p_1p_2=\mu^2+O(1/n)$. Here the centers match the separately proved linear-scale theorem, and the latter product is calculated on distinct roots. Exchangeability now yields $\mathbb EA=\mu n+O(1)$ and $\mathrm{Var}(A)=O(n)$. These are tree-environment moments of the conditional mean $A$, not the within-tree hard-core size variance.

**Mode transfer and status of prior work.** [Fang, Lu, Nevo, Yao, and Zheng](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20961v1), Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 5.1 prove sufficiently-large-forest unimodality and the uniform order-$n$ variance at unit activity. Their results immediately give the elementary mode-mean bound used here by Chebyshev, mass pigeonholing and the monotone interval to a mode, for every deterministic forest in scope. Together with their uniform CLT they in fact imply $o(\sqrt n)$ about the individual forest's mean: along any sequence with a standardized mode at least a fixed positive amount from zero, two adjacent positive equal-width intervals strictly between zero and that mode must have nondereasing probability masses up to a one-lattice-step alignment, contradicting the strictly decreasing positive-side normal density (reflect if the mode is below zero). One may choose blocks of exactly the same integer length to avoid endpoint issues. Neither argument establishes tightness about the *deterministic* $\mu n$ without control of the conditional mean over the random-tree law. The frozen second-moment bound gives the claimed squared maximal deviation as well, by $(x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2$. There is no proved lower fluctuation bound or Gaussian fluctuation assertion in the reviewed manuscript.

Closest source for the stabilization and moments is [Ralaivaosaona, Šileikis and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00467), Lemma 3 and §6.1 equation (44); they already have a $2^{-h}$ times level-width bound for the root hard-core log message and a conditioned-tree almost-local framework. Their Theorem 10 concerns the logarithm of the *count* of independent sets, not the tree-dependent *mean size* of such a set. Do not imply a new contraction principle or that differentiating a scalar CLT is automatic. [Andriantiana, Misanantenaina and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08290) study the average-size invariant and deterministic graph comparisons; [Heilman, Q. 8.2](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225) distinguishes the modal coefficient on a typical Cayley tree from extremal or averaged coefficients. Searches of these works and for quantitative local limits, stabilizing tree parameters, random Cayley uniform-independent-set mean, quenched occupancy, and mode fluctuations found no same $O(1)$ bias and deterministic-center $L^2$ modal-window theorem. This is a limited prior search, not a claim that another general theorem cannot be adapted.

**Independent finite checks.** Without using Prüfer words, enumerate undirected acyclic edge subsets on $n=2,\ldots,6$. All 1,441 labeled trees are included. Direct counts verify 677 fully labeled one-root prescriptions and 511 ordered disjoint two-root prescriptions, including some zero-boundary and full-cover edge cases not needed in (S1). Occupations from exhaustive independent vertex subsets match the stabilized root values; 12,384 depth changes respect the rational enlarged $0.7\cdot2^{-(h-1)}$ width bound. See `independent.py` and `CHECKS.txt`. The author's separate `sqrt_check.py` also ran successfully with `python -B -O`; it counts *unlabeled ball shapes* after grouping Prüfer-word realizations. These enumerations corroborate finite identities; they are not the asymptotic proof.
