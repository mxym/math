# Sources, scope and bounded overlap search

- **Exact problem.** J.-F. Marckert and L. Morin, [*The Sylvester question in* $\mathbb R^d$: *convex sets with a flat floor*, arXiv:2411.08456v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.08456v2), version dated August 31, 2026. Definitions 1.1–1.3 use the entire intersection with the supporting hyperplane, distinguish the subclass under a vertical prism, and normalize area and floor length. Conjecture 1.8 quantifies over the larger planar class at every $n\ge3$. The counterexamples here already lie in the smaller class. The lower theorem allows even a proper marked subsegment, in which case equality cannot occur. [Journal record: *Electronic Journal of Probability* **31** (2026), 1–27](https://doi.org/10.1214/26-EJP1563). The numbered statements above were checked in the latest accessible author version, not independently collated against the publisher PDF.
- **Existing results used or distinguished.** Marckert–Morin Theorems 1.5 and 1.7 prove the two-sample optimum over the smaller and larger classes, respectively; the larger-class supremum is then 1. Their Theorem 3.1 gives the decomposition over roof-adapted caps; Theorem 3.3 recalls the triangle value, Proposition 3.4 computes the square value via two triangular caps, and Theorem 3.5 computes the probability for a parabolic roof. We explicitly use these benchmarks and triangular decomposition. Theorem 4.1 deals with mountains in dimension three, a different statement. The classical triangle-chain probability is due to [Bárány, Rote, Steiger and Zhang](https://page.mi.fu-berlin.de/rote/Papers/pdf/A%2Bcentral%2Blimit%2Btheorem%2Bfor%2Bconvex%2Bchains%2Bin%2Bthe%2Bsquare.pdf); see also [Buchta's exact number-of-vertices distribution in a triangle](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300000127).
- **Near neighbors.** The same authors' [arXiv:2510.26330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26330) considers a random chain conditioned on its number of vertices in a triangle and asymptotic shape. [Morin's regular-polygon work](https://doi.org/10.1017/apr.2024.63) treats all-random samples and large sample size. Neither gives the finite, all-bodies extremal comparison here. Convex-chain decompositions by pushing a boundary until points are hit are established tools, also discussed in the original paper's §1.1. We make no claim of introducing the decomposition principle or a new general integral-inequality theory.
