# The square fails for every number of flat-floor samples greater than two

Let the floor be the entire unit bottom edge of an area-one convex body in the strip $0\le x\le1, y\ge0$. Write $Q_n$ for the probability that $n$ independent uniform points and the two floor endpoints are in convex position. Marckert and Morin, *The Sylvester question in ℝ^d: convex sets with a flat floor*, arXiv:2411.08456v2, Conjecture 1.8, propose the square value as an upper bound for every $n\ge3$, even in the broader class permitting overhang. We show that it fails without any overhang: the unit square is not a local maximizer for any $n\ge3$. This result is about the model with two fixed endpoints, not the ordinary all-random Sylvester model or their proved two-sample bound.

Set $h(x)=4\min(x,1-x)$. Add the strip $1<y\le 1+e h(x)$ to the square and then divide all heights by $1+e$. This gives area-one convex pentagons $K_e$ converging to the square; vertical rescaling does not affect the event. We shall prove $Q_n(K_e)>q_n=Q_n([0,1]^2)$ for all sufficiently small $e>0$, with an optional rational choice below.

**Square with a fixed boundary point.** Write $m=n-1$. Fix in addition $P=(x,1)$, $0<x<1$, and let $s(x)$ be the probability of convex position with $m$ uniform square points. The triangle under the broken line from the left floor endpoint through $P$ to the right floor endpoint must contain no other point. The remaining regions are triangles of areas $x/2,(1-x)/2$. In each, the points must form a convex chain with the endpoints of its sloped side. These two requirements suffice: each accepted chain lies above its sloped side, and, at $P$, the last slope on the left is nonnegative and the first on the right nonpositive (all heights are at most 1). Collinearities and boundary coincidences have probability zero. The classical probability of such a chain in a triangle with $j$ samples and endpoints of one side fixed is $t_j=2^j/[j!(j+1)!]$, $t_0=1$ (Bárány–Rote–Steiger–Zhang; Marckert–Morin Theorem 3.3). Binomially allocate the labeled points as in the same authors' Proposition 3.4:
$$
 s(x)=2^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^m{m\choose j}x^j(1-x)^{m-j} c_j,\qquad c_j=t_j t_{m-j}.
$$
Its integral equals $q_n$: conditioning on the highest of $n$ uniform square points, its abscissa is uniform, independent of the maximum height and of the index. Given these, the other points, after the vertical rescaling sending the maximum to 1, are iid uniform in the square. Equivalently the known value is $q_n={2n\choose n}/[n!(n+1)!]$.

For $m\ge2$, $s(1-x)=s(x)$ and $s$ is strictly increasing for $0<x<1/2$. Indeed $c_{m-j}=c_j$, and $c_{j+1}>c_j$ for $j<(m-1)/2$, since $t_{j+1}/t_j=2/[(j+1)(j+2)]$ strictly decreases. The Bernstein basis derivative writes $s'$ as $m2^{-m}$ times the degree-$(m-1)$ Bernstein expansion with coefficients $c_{j+1}-c_j$. Pair the terms indexed by $j$ and $m-1-j$; the first basis value exceeds the second on $0<x<1/2$ for each positive difference. At least the pair starting at zero contributes strictly.

**Variation.** Put $D=\int_0^1 h(x)s(x)\,dx-q_n$. This rational number is strictly positive: both symmetric functions are strictly increasing on $[0,1/2]$, and $\int h=1$. In detail, under the uniform law on this half-interval the covariance is one half the expectation of $(h(X)-h(Y))(s(X)-s(Y))$ for iid $X,Y$, positive off the diagonal.

For $y\ge1$, let $p(x,y)$ replace the extra point by $(x,y)$, with the other $m$ points still uniform in the square. Scaling all heights by $1/y$, the latter become uniform in a subrectangle of area $1/y$. Conditioning independent square points to be in this subrectangle costs total variation $1-y^{-m}\le m(y-1)$, so $|p(x,y)-s(x)|\le m(y-1)$, uniformly in $x$. The unnormalized integral $B_e=(1+e)^n Q_n(K_e)$ is the sum of contributions with zero, one, or more points in the added strip. The first is $q_n$, the second is $n\int_0^1\int_1^{1+e h(x)}p(x,y)\,dy\,dx$, and the last is nonnegative and at most $2^n e^2$ for $0\le e\le1$. Thus
$$
 Q_n(K_e)=q_n+neD+O_n(e^2).
$$
Explicitly $B_e-q_n(1+e)^n\ge neD-(2nm+2^n)e^2$, using $h\le2$, $q_n\le1$, and the binomial expansion. The rational $e=nD/[2(2nm+2^n)]\in(0,1)$ therefore works at every $n\ge3$.

**One exact small pentagon.** At $n=3$ the convex body with roof $G(x)=(4+h(x))/5$ has probability $317/2250=5/36+1/500$. Here is a direct short calculation using the Marckert–Morin maximal-relative-height split (Theorem 3.1). Given two samples in a cap of width $w$, roof clearance $H$ over its floor line and area $A=\int H$, the unnormalized convex event has mass $A^2-(w/2)\int H^2$. Indeed there are two choices for a nonvertex, each contributing the integral of the area of the triangle formed by the other point and the cap floor. In the three-point split the apex abscissa has density $G(t)$: a uniform point has abscissa density $G$, independent of its relative height $Y/G(X)$, itself uniform; normalize by the largest such height. Left and right caps have total area $1-G(t)/2$, with chord floors meeting $(t,G(t))$ and the respective floor endpoints at height zero. A split with one point in each cap is always accepted. For this split the automatic join at the common apex follows from the concavity of the roof: the two adjacent slopes bracket any supergradient at the apex. By reflection symmetry we obtain
$$
 Q_3=\int_0^1 G(t)(1-G(t)/2)^2dt-\int_0^1tG(t)J(t)dt,\qquad
 J(t)=\int_0^t [G(x)-xG(t)/t]^2dx.
$$
For $0<t\le1/2$, $J(t)=16t/75$; for $1/2\le t<1$, integrating the two linear pieces gives $J(t)=8(8t^2-5t+1)/(75t)$. The first integral in the formula for $Q_3$ is $37/150$; the two contributions to the subtracted integral are $11/1125$ and $12/125$. This proves the claimed rational value. No identification of the optimal upper shape is asserted.
