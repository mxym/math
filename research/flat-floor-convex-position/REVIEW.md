I directly checked Marckert–Morin latest arXiv v2 (31 August 2026) Conjecture 1.8, classical triangle calculation, and published-square formula in Proposition 3.4. The exact target is planar with the floor endpoints fixed and all n other points uniform, not the ordinary all-random Sylvester problem.

**Upper.** The first variation stays within the full-bottom-strip subclass, so the counterexample does not depend on whether overhang is allowed. Anchoring (x,1) in the square cuts the admissible points into two triangular caps; at height 1 their local convex chains meet with the right turn as all other heights are lower. Conditioning on the highest sample gives the integral of the boundary probability equal to the square value. The symmetric Bernstein coefficients are strictly increasing toward the center, with paired derivative terms of one sign on the left half; hence strict positive covariance with the triangular perturbation. The uniform vertical total variation bound and multiple-bump bound give the stated rational epsilon. The explicit n=3 pentagon probability was independently reproduced: over three samples, inclusion–exclusion with hull areas gives Q3=1−3 E A_2+3 E[A_1²]. For a general symmetric trapezoidal roof G_t=1−t+4t min(x,1−x), the sorted-abscissa hull integrations give Q3=5/36+(t−4t²+t³)/24, equal to 317/2250 at t=1/5. This is independent of the maximal-relative-height calculation in the main proof.

**Lower.** Adding the entire bottom face to a smaller floor marking only shrinks the event. Normalize area and height to one. For each highest pivot P and full top-of-truncation slice I(v), the two side triangles along the floor-to-pivot chords are subsets by convexity, disjoint up to boundaries. An accepted chain in the left triangle is the convex left endpoint as a function of height from its unique low endpoint A to pivot P; likewise the right chain gives a concave right endpoint. The two chords are horizontally ordered at every intermediate height, so these chains form the two boundary graphs of a convex region. Every local chain vertex survives as a global one, including with overhang. The pivot factor n and its integration measure w(v) ds dv give the stated nonnegative integral lower bound. For a triangle exactly every successful sample lies in the two side triangles and passes their chain tests, so calibration against the classical triangle value is exact.

The scalar inequality follows by the strict supporting-line inequality of x^n at w0=2(1−v), multiplying by v^(n−1). Its linear remainder has the symmetric weight [v(1−v)]^(n−1), so we can replace w with its symmetric concave average and w0 with constant 1. On [0,1/2] both that average and the weight are nondecreasing and the average has mean 1. The covariance identity with independent uniform X,Y on this interval gives nonnegative remainder. Since the supporting line is strict at distinct arguments for n≥2 and the integration weight is positive on (0,1), equality forces w=w0 everywhere by continuity. Affine widths then force affine endpoints and thus a triangle. The proper marked subsegment case is strictly worse than equality because the explicit parabola configuration and its neighborhood are accepted only with the shorter floor, as verified by supporting tangents and strictly increasing (x−s)²/x.

Both arguments are analytic. This confirms the lower conjectured bound for every n≥3 (and reproduces known n=2) with equality, and disproves the square upper for every n≥3.
