# A sharp triangle minimum and a square counterexample for planar flat-floor sampling

Fix a supporting segment of a planar convex body and sample $n$ independent uniform points. What is the probability that every sampled point is a vertex after the segment's endpoints are added to the hull? [Marckert–Morin, Conjecture 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.08456v2#S1.SS1) proposed comparing this probability to the triangle and square values, respectively, from below and above. The two comparisons have different answers.

- **Lower: true for every $n\ge3$.** The minimum is $2^n/[n!(n+1)!]$, attained exactly by triangles with one entire side as the floor. The [proof](LOWER-SHORT.md) allows overhanging bodies. It keeps two triangular subregions next to the highest sampled point, then bounds the resulting horizontal-width integral by a tangent inequality and a covariance comparison. The two-sample case was already established.
- **Upper: false for every $n\ge3$.** A sufficiently small symmetric triangular bump on the square's roof raises the probability. The [proof](UPPER.md) gives a rational bump parameter for each $n$, and the pentagon need not overhang. For three samples one explicit pentagon gives $317/2250$, exceeding the square's $5/36$ by $1/500$. The true maximizer is not identified.

These are analytic arguments for the **marked-floor** problem, not a solution of the ordinary Sylvester problem with all points random, nor a higher-dimensional assertion.
