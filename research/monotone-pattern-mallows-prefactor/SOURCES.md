# Historical and current comparisons

**Earlier results on the same permutations.** [Hoffman–Rizzolo–Slivken, §2.1 and Theorem 2.1, v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04982v4) define the two axis words from canonical increasing layers and obtain the unweighted traceless Brownian-bridge matrix eigenvalue limit; their encoding is used here. [Regev](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(81)90012-8) gives the sharper-than-exponential **untilted** bounded-strip asymptotic via tableaux, with a different power of $n$. [Canfield–Janson–Zeilberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2089) discuss MacMahon's classical inversion distribution on multiset words: it is this unrestricted enumerator, not the finite canonical count, which is a Gaussian multinomial.

[Budzinski–Dubach–Féray–Kammoun–Maïda](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23751v1), [AofA 2026 publication](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.AofA.2026.18), treat the identical positive $q=e^{\beta/n}$ scaling for 321: full preprint Theorem 7 and Proposition 8(6) give the limit shape and the exponential free energy $\lim n^{-1}\log Z$. Formula (6) does not state the leading equivalent. The direct repository antecedent [#243](../monotone-pattern-permuton-deviations/README.md) treats all fixed $d$ at exponential order, including the signed surrogate, its fixed-content squared hyperbolic-sine product, concentration of the layer proportions and the logistic bulk profiles. Its transfer from surrogate to true counts bounds the conditioning cost by $e^{o(n)}$, not by a particular constant. The present result evaluates this cost, gives the full polynomial and constant, and transfers the fluctuation law of the canonical layer counts.

**Exactly the same chamber determinant, not the same transition.** [Denisov–FitzGerald, ALEA 20 (2023), pp. 1212–13, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2(i)](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v20/20-45.pdf) prove a determinant for nonexit of exponential random walks with unequal ordered rates. After reversing coordinates and rates, the determinant there, including its normalization, is exactly $h$ of TRANSMISSION.md. In particular its zero-start Vandermonde evaluation is not a new determinant identity. Their chain updates *all* coordinates at every integer time by independent exponential steps and tests ordering at the integer times. The jump chain induced by the two word streams here updates only one coordinate at a time, selecting it with probability inversely proportional to its exponential jump rate. The direct kernel check in TRANSMISSION.md is for this latter process, not an assumption that synchronous and asynchronous survival agree generally. [O’Connell–Yor](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/EJP-ECP/article/view/1042/0.html) relate ordered Poisson **counting** processes with different intensities to queues; that is a unit-jump model. [Denisov–FitzGerald–Zhang, October 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10959-026-01550-0) address zero-mean walks in Weyl chambers of types C and D, not the present outward-drifting type A kernel. No priority is claimed for a general chamber/queue theorem.

**Width-two finite polynomials and area models.** [Cheng–Elizalde–Kasraoui–Sagan](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2013.07.026) study the very same 321 inversion polynomials, using recursions, two-coloured Motzkin paths and polyominoes. The width-two transfer in the checker is likewise not a new enumerator. [Cheng–Eu–Fu, Area of Catalan Paths on a Checkerboard, Proposition 4.3](https://garsia.math.yorku.ca/fpsac06/papers/20.pdf) matches inversions of 321 avoiders with area of *shortened* polyominoes. [Prellberg–Owczarek, Theorem 2.2](https://www.aleksowczarek.com/publications/pub-042-48.pdf) give leading finite-perimeter asymptotics for inflated staircase polygons and several other polygons at each **fixed** $q>1$, with error parameters depending on $q$, and examine the approach to one of the resulting dominant expression. This is not a uniform remainder for the joint scaling here. [Mitra–Menon–Rajesh](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.1509) examine area/shape under pressure for convex and column-convex polygons. Width two is therefore a particularly well-studied case; no claim of priority for all representations or special cases is made.

**Search date and bounds.** Retrospective and 1 July–19 November 2026 primary-source searches covered bounded decreasing subsequences, conditioned Mallows normalizers, inversion/$q$-Catalan polynomial leading terms, tilted excursions/staircase polygons, unequal-drift exponential walks, queued and compound-Poisson particles, and Weyl-chamber nonexit. In the checked versions (HRS revised June 2026; BDFKM April full preprint and July proceedings; [Borga–Féray–Winkler, November 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12104)), no theorem was located giving the full fixed-positive-bias prefactor and Gaussian layer-size law for every fixed $d$ in this finite-permutation model. This is a bounded comparison, not a global priority guarantee. Third-party work retains its own rights.
