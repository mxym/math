# The strict positive weak Mallows tilt: a leading factor for bounded decreasing subsequences

Let $d$ be fixed, $\beta>0$ fixed, and put $q=e^{\beta/n}$. Write $Z$ for the sum of $q^{\mathrm{inv}}$ over *uncoloured* permutations of $n$ with no decreasing subsequence of length $d+1$. Set $p=1/d$,
$$a(x)=2\sinh(\beta x/2),\quad G(x)=\int_0^x\log a(y)\,dy,\quad H=G''(p)=(\beta/2)\coth(\beta p/2),\quad F=2[G(1)-dG(p)].$$
Then
$$ Z\sim \gamma^2\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\right)^{d-1}\left(\frac{\pi}{H}\right)^{(d-1)/2}
\frac{a(1)}{\sqrt{d}\,a(p)^d}\ n^{-(d-1)/2} e^{nF}.\tag{1}$$
Here $\gamma=\gamma_d(\beta)>0$ is an effective one-end transmission probability specified below; $\gamma_1=1$ and $\gamma_2=1-e^{-\beta/2}$. This is for fixed positive tilt only. At zero tilt the power is different for $d\ge2$ (Regev). No critical crossover is asserted.

## 1. Exact source of the transmission factor

Use the canonical decreasing-subsequence height decomposition. Match the $j$th occurrences of each colour in the position word $U$ and value word $V$. Each colour is an increasing chain; any two words of common content give a permutation of width at most $d$. They give the *canonical* colouring iff every point of colour $s>1$ has a point of colour $r=s-1$ strictly northwest. Indeed, iterating such witnesses yields decreasing chains of length $s$; if a colour $t\ge s$ were northwest of a colour $s$ point, iterating down to $s$ (or immediately if $t=s$) would contradict that $s$ is a chain. Thus labels increase strictly along decreasing chains. Define $B_W(r,s;j)$ to be the number of $r$ strictly before the $j$th $s$ in a word $W$. The witness property is exactly
$$ B_U(r,s;j)>B_V(r,s;j)\quad (s=r+1,\ 1\le j\le m_s).\tag{2}$$
For $A(W)=\#\{i<j:W_i<W_j\}$ one has $\mathrm{inv}=A(U)-A(V)$ on canonical pairs (all inversions point from lesser to greater colour). Let $S$ be the unrestricted sum of $q^{A(U)-A(V)}$ over equal-content pairs, normalized to define a surrogate probability law. Then $Z/S=\mathbb P_S((2))$ exactly. Conditional on content $\mathbf m$, $U,V$ are independent tilted multiset words of parameters $q,q^{-1}$. Reversal at fixed content interchanges these two parameters. Reversing and swapping both words gives the same test at the opposite end, since the two reversed pre-counts at ordinal $m_s-j+1$ are $m_r-B_V(r,s;j)$ and $m_r-B_U(r,s;j)$.

## 2. Saddle point for the unrestricted sum

The common-content weight by the Gaussian multinomial formula is $[P_n(n)/\prod_iP_n(m_i)]^2$ where $P_n(m)=\prod_{j=1}^m a(j/n)$. Uniformly for $u=m/n$ in any compact subset of $(0,1]$,
$$P_n(m)=(1+O(n^{-1}))\sqrt{(2\pi n/\beta) a(u)}\,e^{nG(u)}.$$
Split $\log a(x)=\log(\beta x)+b(x)$ with $b$ smooth on $[0,1]$, $b(0)=0$. Stirling treats the first partial product and the trapezoid correction for the second log sum is $b(u)/2+O(n^{-1})$. Also $\log P_n(m)=nG(m/n)+O(\log(n+1))$ uniformly including $m=0$. Strict convexity of $G$ implies exponential negligibility outside any fixed neighborhood of the uniform vector, since there are at most $(n+1)^d$ terms. Inside a fixed small neighborhood where $G''$ is bounded below positively, normalized summands are dominated by a constant times $e^{-c\sum x_i^2}$ after removing the common $n^{1-d} e^{nF}$, with $x_i=(m_i-np)/\sqrt n$. On bounded such $x$ the exponent relative to $nF$ is $-H\sum_i x_i^2+o(1)$. Since $x_d=-\sum_{i<d} x_i$, the Gaussian integral in the first $d-1$ coordinates is $(\pi/H)^{(d-1)/2}/\sqrt d$. Riemann sums with mesh $n^{-1/2}$, any lattice shift, and the Gaussian bound on the tails now give precisely (1) without $\gamma^2$ for $S$. This also proves the centred content CLT under $S$, of covariance $(2H)^{-1}(I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/d)$ in the $d$ original coordinates. The degenerate $d=1$ case is immediate below and above.

## 3. Finite windows at opposite ends

For all content sequences with $m_i/n\to p$, the first $K$ symbols of $U,V,\mathrm{rev}(V),\mathrm{rev}(U)$ converge jointly, for every fixed $K$, to four mutually independent length-$K$ iid words of respective letter laws $\alpha,\bar\alpha,\alpha,\bar\alpha$. Here
$$t=e^{\beta/d},\qquad \alpha_i=\frac{(t-1)t^{d-i}}{t^d-1},\qquad \bar\alpha_i=\alpha_{d+1-i}.$$
Indeed the next-letter $i$ probability in an ascending-pair word with remaining counts $h_i$ of total $h$ is $q^{\sum_{j>i}h_j}(q^{h_i}-1)/(q^h-1)$ by the Gaussian multinomial formula; after that the remaining word has the same parameter on its remaining multiset. The corresponding reversed formula switches to $q^{-1}$. One can expose any fixed number of letters at either end in succession, because a fixed prefix or suffix contributes interactions with the middle depending only on its counts, not its ordering. Each conditional letter probability then has the indicated limit, independent of the preceding bounded history.

Define $\gamma$ by requiring (2) for **all** $j$ in two independent infinite iid words of laws $\alpha,\bar\alpha$. For fixed $M$, checking just the first $M$ tests for each adjacent pair at each of the two finite-word ends hence has probability tending to $\gamma_M^2$, the square of the analogous one-end truncated probability. To justify the random stopping positions, truncate the four windows at $K$: the limiting probability of fewer than $M$ occurrences of some required letter goes to zero as $K\to\infty$ by the binomial law. Then $\gamma_M\downarrow\gamma$. The remaining issue is a bound *uniform in the distance from the boundary and in the finite content*, not a formal extension of finite-window convergence.

## 4. Uniform exclusion of middle failures

Fix an adjacent pair $r,s=r+1$, $a_0=m_r$, $b_0=m_s$, $m=a_0+b_0$. Deleting all other letters from $U,V$ yields two independent binary multiset words with the same $q,q^{-1}$. Indeed condition on the interleaving of the contiguous alphabet interval $\{r,s\}$ among other letters and their order; the number of cross-interval ascending pairs is independent of the internal ordering, since every external label is below both or above both. Let $D_\ell$ be the first projected word's low-colour ($r$) count through $\ell$ minus that of the second. Failure at $j$ implies that at $\ell=$ the position of the $j$th high colour $s$ in the first word, that word has at least as many high colours as the second, hence $D_\ell\le0$; also $\ell\ge j$, $m-\ell\ge b_0-j$. The corresponding difference for the reverse-swapped pair at $m-\ell$ equals $D_\ell$.

Choose a fixed small neighborhood of the uniform content vector. During the first $\epsilon n$ steps for sufficiently small fixed $\epsilon>0$, the probabilities of a low letter given the respective histories (with their possibly different residual low/high counts $a',b'$) are
$$p_+(a',b')=q^{b'}\frac{q^{a'}-1}{q^{a'+b'}-1},\qquad
p_-(a',b')=\frac{q^{a'}-1}{q^{a'+b'}-1}.$$
These are continuous functions of the scaled counts, with a strictly positive difference at $(p,p)$; hence uniformly over the two histories their difference is at least a fixed $c>0$ after shrinking the neighborhood and $\epsilon$. The martingale of centred successive increments of $D$ has increments bounded by 2. Consequently $\mathbb P(D_\ell\le0)\le e^{-c^2\ell/8}$ for $1\le\ell\le\epsilon n$; the same bound applies from the opposite end.

For $\epsilon n\le\ell\le m-\epsilon n$, the low-letter *positions* in the first binary word are an independent Bernoulli set with odds $z e^{-\beta h/n}$, $1\le h\le m$, conditioned to have cardinality $a_0$, where $z>0$ is chosen so its unconditional mean is $a_0$. The other word uses its reflection. This representation follows since, with fixed count, the number of ascending low/high pairs is a constant minus the sum of low positions. Put $L=m/n$, $u=a_0/n$. The expectations of normalized prefix counts converge uniformly to the integrals over $[0,x]$ of $f(y)= z_*e^{-\beta y}/(1+z_* e^{-\beta y})$ and $f(L-y)$, respectively, where $\int_0^L f=u$; this is uniform also in the scaled counts in our fixed compact interior neighborhood. Indeed $z$ stays in a compact subset of $(0,\infty)$ and Riemann sums converge uniformly there; strict monotonicity gives the unique limit for every converging parameter subsequence. Since $f$ is strictly decreasing, $\int_0^x(f(y)-f(L-y))dy>0$ for $0<x<L$ (increase until $L/2$ then decrease to zero). Compactness gives a uniform positive gap on the restricted interior.

Size conditioning costs at most $e^{O(\sqrt n+\log n)}$, uniformly: Chebyshev gives some size $k$ within $2\sqrt n$ of $a_0$ of mass $\gg n^{-1/2}$. The ratio of adjacent size masses from $k'$ to $k'+1$ is between $(m-k')\min w_h/(k'+1)$ and $(m-k')\max w_h/(k'+1)$ for odds $w_h$, by counting insertions in weighted subsets. In the window these ratios are bounded away from zero and infinity. Apply this to both words, followed by Hoeffding before conditioning and a union bound over prefixes; failure of strict positivity anywhere on the restricted interior has probability $O(e^{-c_1n})$. Combining with the two endpoint martingale estimates gives
$$\mathbb P(\text{some test fails with }M<j\le b_0-M\text{, some adjacent pair}\mid\mathbf m)
\ \le\ C e^{-c_2 M}+C e^{-c_1 n}.$$
Constants depend only on fixed $d,\beta$ (and our fixed small neighborhood). The endpoint localization thus applies even when $M$ is large compared to one but tiny compared to $n$.

By §§3–4, $\mathbb P((2)\mid\mathbf m)\to\gamma^2$ along *every* sequence $\mathbf m/n\to(p,\ldots,p)$, first taking $n\to\infty$ then $M\to\infty$. As the content under $S$ converges there in probability, its random conditional success probability converges in probability and $L^1$ to the same number (subsequence criterion). Equation (1) follows. Once positivity is established below, the true and surrogate *content marginals* even differ by $o(1)$ in total variation: their likelihood ratio is this conditional probability divided by its expectation. Thus the same Gaussian content CLT holds for the actual canonical layer sizes.

## 5. Nonvanishing, effective accuracy and the binary evaluation

In the two independent one-end iid words, $B_U(r,s;j)/j\to \alpha_r/\alpha_s=t$ and $B_V(r,s;j)/j\to t^{-1}$ almost surely. Thus with positive probability all tests after some fixed $M$ succeed; for large enough deterministic $K$ this event together with at least $M$ copies of every label in the first $K$ of both words still has positive probability. Within those finite prefixes sort the first word ascending, the second descending. This can only improve all tests (within-prefix the first pre-count is maximized, the second minimized; outside unchanged), and now forces the first $M$ tests of each adjacent pair. The joint law after this finite modification is absolutely continuous with respect to the original product law since all finite words have positive probability and the tails were not altered. This proves $\gamma>0$; if $d\ge2$, failure has positive probability already at the first test.

For explicit error control put $u=t/(1+t)$, $v=1/(1+t)$, $\lambda=4uv<1$. In every adjacent binary projection the two independent infinite sequences have low-letter probabilities $u,v$. For their prefix-count difference, the one-step negative exponential moment at $s=\log(u/v)$ is $(1-u+ue^{-s})(1-v+ve^s)=4uv$. Thus
$$0\le\gamma_M-\gamma\le(d-1)\,\lambda^{M+1}/(1-\lambda),$$
by the bad-prefix implication from §4. The length-$K$ two-word sum checking tests through $M$ and rejecting windows with too few required $s$ letters in either word differs from $\gamma_M$ by at most $2(d-1)\mathbb P(\mathrm{Bin}(K,\min_i\alpha_i)<M)$. This defines an effective constant, despite potential inefficiency.

For $d=2$, consider successive high-letter epochs in each iid word. Low-letter numbers between successive high letters are independent geometric counts $X,Y$, with $\mathbb P(X=j)=(1-u)u^j$, $\mathbb P(Y=j)=(1-v)v^j$. Condition (2) is strict positivity of the walk with increments $D=X-Y$ at every positive integer time. The increment masses are $c u^k$ at $k\ge0$ and $c v^k$ at $-k$, $k\ge0$ (count zero once), where $c=(1-u)(1-v)/(1-uv)$. For $h=v/u$ one has $\mathbb E h^D=1$. From positive start $i$, stop the martingale $h^{S_n}$ at $\tau\wedge N$ for first entry into $(-\infty,0]$. At a killing step the overshoot has mass $(1-v)v^k$ at depth $k\ge0$, independent of the pre-step height conditionally on killing, by the geometric tail. Thus the expected martingale at that step conditional on killing is $(1-v)/(1-u)$. On $\{\tau>N\}$ the martingale is bounded by 1 and tends to zero on indefinite survival by positive drift; hence $\mathbb P_i(\tau<\infty)=h^i(1-u)/(1-v)$. Summing over a strictly positive first step from zero yields
$$\gamma_2=\sum_{i\ge1}c u^i\left(1-(v/u)^i\frac{1-u}{1-v}\right)=\frac{u-v}{1-v}=1-t^{-1}.$$
