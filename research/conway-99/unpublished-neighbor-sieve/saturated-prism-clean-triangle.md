# A saturated axis has a triangle-free graph on its six other axes at rank 36

In a hypothetical Conway 99 graph of mod-seven Seidel rank 36, take any vertex o. Call the seven mate pairs in its neighborhood its axes. Join axes a,b when their two pairs, o, and their four other common-neighbor sites form a literal rook grid (a clean cell). If e is incident with all six clean edges, then no three of the other six axes are pairwise joined. In particular two saturated axes force exactly eleven grids through o: there are no additional edges among the remaining five. This conclusion does not require any global or local wing-size assumption. It does not exclude rank 36.

**Inputs.** The prism diagram, notation, and 60 times residual pairing below are from [the six-prism projection](saturated-prism-isotropic.md). Its wing span is nondegenerate of rank 31, hence its complementary space has dimension 5 at rank 36. The four residuals of each clean square off e sum to zero and span three dimensions by [the clean-square theorem](saturated-prism-clean-square.md); consequently its Gram has rank at least 2 (a 3-space inside a nondegenerate 5-space has radical of dimension at most 2, and a rank-one symmetric matrix with zero diagonal over an odd field is zero). In particular discard a rank-zero square: otherwise the span would be totally isotropic of dimension three, impossible. These are necessary conditions on an actual graph, not assumptions about a generic labeling.

Suppose a,b,c contradict the claim. On the six prism components of the e triangle, numbered in order a,b,c,3,4,5, write a far vertex as a word with letters 2w+s, w=0,1,2 for the wings and s=0,1 for the two prism lines. Use w=0 for o. Each word has each wing twice. Let X,Y,Z be the four words on cells ab,ac,bc respectively. Within each cell its two named slots take the four pairs of 0,1 (wing zero), in lexicographic sign order; each other slot runs once through 2,3,4,5. This last fact is a matching to the four nonzero-wing sites of the corresponding prism, by the one-neighbor-outside-grid rule in both directions. Within a cell two rows are adjacent exactly when their two signs differ in one place. Every pair of cells here shares a named axis. Its rows are pairwise NONadjacent across the two cells: viewed from the other cell's nine-grid the unique neighbor of any row is already its signed parent on this common axis.

For words u,v let n count matching letters, k matching wings, h differing wings with equal sign. The diagonal of the scaled mod-7 residual Gram is zero and each offdiagonal is 43−18n−4k−6h−60A(u,v). In two distinct prisms, a literal pair of near sites has exactly 2−[same wing] far common neighbors. Thus two rows from distinct cells cannot match in more than two slots, and cannot match twice in slots of one wing. More importantly, the aggregate twelve rows may never hit one fixed symbol pair more than its quota. We test this last restriction at the end.

**Naming and exact scan.** First generate all 75 clean first-square diagrams with the four other prism slots interchangeable as in the clean-square theorem. Distinguish c among those four. Quotient only by renaming the last three slots, exchanging the two other whole wings, flipping prism-line bits independently, exchanging the two named axes a,b, and changing which of their two bits is called first. This gives 54 representative X (no global graph symmetry is posited). Swapping a,b interchanges Y,Z roles but both are freely filled in the newly chosen naming. After X is fixed no independent bit normalizations are imposed on Y,Z. For either, the four unprescribed slots are permutations of 2,3,4,5 with each row wing-balanced; this gives 23,040 labeled options for each.

Keep the first three rows of each square; the fourth is their negative sum. The cross pairings of X and Y must have zero row and column sums. After the two-square common-neighbor and self-rank tests, the six-row Gram G of X,Y has rank 3,4,5,6 in respectively 28,1072,26709,144619 cases. Rank 6 already fails. For each retained G of rank r choose a nonsingular principal G_II of order r (the actual result is checked, and every retained base gets one). For any prospective Z row its vector w of six known pairings is fixed by its word because all cross adjacencies are absent. If r=5, require w=G_:I G_II^{-1}w_I and zero scaled square w_I^T G_II^{-1} w_I. If r=4, impose only the first relation: failure would add two to the rank upon adjoining the row and column, contradicting the bound five. If r=3, impose neither. For all surviving quadruples Z check the entire symmetric 9-by-9 Gram on first three rows of each square has rank at most five. Omitting further constraints never excludes any actual rows.

Exactly 0,6,14 diagrams survive the full test over the respective base ranks 3,4,5. All have final rank 5. On EACH of the twenty the twelve rows hit one same pair of specified near symbols in distinct prisms **three** times, exceeding even the weaker uniform limit of two. The checker prints first/second/third chart indices, both ranks and an explicit collision for all twenty; populations here count the indicated labeled charts, never graphs. Only twelve far vertices have been constrained. Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 saturated_prism_clean_triangle.py` (NumPy; integer arithmetic only, under twenty seconds in the checked environment).

**Scoped corollary.** In rank 36, a vertex on thirteen grids cannot have every component in its seven triangle wings of size at most 18. In the [previous rank-free 13-grid/no-large-component classification](triangle-wing-thirteen-grid-bound.md) the dirty-axis graph is a K4 with two opposite edges subdivided, either via the same or distinct peripheral axes. Write those subdivisions as A-x-B, C-y-D. An unused peripheral e is saturated. When x≠y the three axes A,B,y are pairwise clean; when x=y take the second unused peripheral z and use A,B,z. Both violate the theorem. Hence any such 13-grid vertex forces some incident triangle to have a component of size at least 24; no existence of a 13-grid vertex is asserted.

Two saturated axes also force some incident triangle with a component of size at least 24. Indeed their eleven clean edges leave each of the other five axes exactly two clean pairs and four dirty pairs. If every split degree were at most three, those four dirty pairs could only split as (2+2) (dirty groups have size at least two), not (3+1). Then none of the seven axes would have a cubic group, contrary to the [four-distinct theorem](triangle-wing-four-distinct.md).
