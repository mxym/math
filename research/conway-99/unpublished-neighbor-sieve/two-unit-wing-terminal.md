# Two seven-unit triangle components: at most two adjacency candidates, at most one graph

Fix a triangle whose 36-site wing graph W has precisely two connected components, of orders \(s,36-s\), with spanning-tree numbers \(\tau_1,\tau_2\) both nonzero modulo seven. Suppose the full Seidel rank is 36. Given the actual component graphs **and the sixty labeled far contact words**, there is a finite, explicit reduction to at most **two** candidate 60-by-60 exterior adjacency matrices after enforcing the elementary necessary tests below. Of those, **at most one** can give a strongly regular graph. No additional far adjacency row is needed for this uniqueness or for this two-candidate verification. No contact-word assignment is constructed, and no two-component or rank branch is excluded. This applies to 6+30, 12+24, 18+18 when both tree numbers are seven-units. The earlier real projection method in [triangle-complement.md](triangle-complement.md) obtains its final rank-one step after prescribing exterior basis rows; the present modulo-seven step needs only the contact words.

**Rank-one terminal block.** Over \(\mathbf F_7\) use \(M=3I-A+J/9\), equal in rank to the Seidel matrix, with zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries 3 (edge), 4 (nonedge). On W this is \(L_W+J/9\). Keep all s vertices of the first component and omit one vertex of the other; the resulting 35-site principal Gram G has
\[
\det G=(s^2/9)\tau_1\tau_2 .
\]
Indeed the unrepaired Laplacian block is the direct sum of the first full Laplacian, whose adjugate is \(\tau_1 J\), and the invertible reduced second Laplacian with determinant \(\tau_2\). Apply the rank-one determinant identity to \(J/9\). The component sizes here are multiples of six and neither is divisible by seven. The omitted mark is in the span of the others by the balanced component-constant relation (the exact component contact numbers on far sites are the component size divided by six); the three base triangle marks are in the same span by the \(3I_3+J_3\) neighbour equations. See [triangle-complement.md](triangle-complement.md). Consequently the far Schur complement has rank exactly one. With \(p_b\) the known wing pairings of far site b, put \(g_{bd}=p_b^tG^{-1}p_d\). There is \(\lambda\ne0\) with full exterior entries \(g_{bd}+\lambda v_bv_d\). The full 36-dimensional discriminant is nonsquare, as in [the rank-36 sign calculation](triangle-wing-tree-rank36.md); hence \(\lambda\) is square exactly when \(\tau_1\tau_2\) is nonsquare. Choose 1 or 3 accordingly.

Define \(e_b=-g_{bb}\). Require \(e_b=\lambda v_b^2\). Zero e fixes \(v_b=0\); all pairs incident to a zero must already have permitted g. For the nonzero sites choose a square root \(r_b\) once for each occurring diagonal type and write \(v_b=\sigma_b r_b\) with \(\sigma_b=\pm1\). For a distinct pair retain the sign products giving \(g_{bd}+\lambda r_br_d\sigma_b\sigma_d\in\{3,4\}\). No choice rejects the input; one choice supplies a signed edge of a forced-relation graph F. Check sign consistency along paths. Two choices occur only when \(g_{bd}=0,\ e_be_d=2\): the two distinct permissible values are mutual negatives and have square 2. If F has k components there are initially at most \(2^{k-1}\) different candidate matrices. Across disconnected components all pairs are ambiguous.

If \(\lambda=1\), the only ambiguous diagonal pairs are (1,2),(4,4); if \(\lambda=3\) they are (5,6),(3,3). Unless all nonzero diagonals have the same distinguished self-pair value \(e\in\{3,4\}\), there are at most two F components: each of the two non-distinguished types is a forced clique, and any distinguished site is connected to every non-distinguished site. In these ordinary cases the at-most-two conclusion is already modular; the common global sign does not affect a matrix.

**The all-distinguished case.** Here choose the same \(r\) on every nonzero site, so \(\lambda r^2=e\). Let n count nonzero sites. From \(M\mathbf1=0=M^2\), a factorization \(M=U^tBU\) with B invertible and U full row rank gives \(U\mathbf1=0\) and \(UU^t=0\). Use coordinates adapted to G and the last rank-one line: v is a row of U up to a nonzero scalar, vanishes on W and the triangle, and the other exterior projections give
\[
 \sum_b v_b=\sum_b v_b^2=0,\qquad \sum_b p_bv_b=0,\qquad A_Xv=3v
 \pmod7 .
\]
The last follows from \(Mv=0\), extending v by zero. Thus n and each separate sign population are multiples of seven.

For nonzero b,d a forced pair has \(g_{bd}=\pm1\), and an ambiguous pair has \(g_{bd}=0\). On every forced pair conjugation by \(\sigma_b,\sigma_d\) gives \(\sigma_b\sigma_dg_{bd}=-2e\): check the two admissible entries 3,4. The conjugated diagonal is \(-e\). Pair \(\sum p_bv_b=0\) with each projection \(G^{-1}p_d\); after conjugation this gives \(e(1+2\deg_F d)=0\), hence every forced degree is \(3\bmod7\). In particular every F component has at least four sites. This alone bounds k by 14 since \(n\le56\), but the *literal* adjacency/common-neighbour rules sharpen the candidate count as follows.

* \(e=3\): sites of the same sign in distinct components are completely joined. Thus one site per positive-bearing component gives a clique, so there are at most three such components (no graph \(K_4\)). If three occur, any adjacent representatives in two have all positives in the third as common neighbours. Each would have just one positive, contradicting that the positive count is a multiple of seven. With two components and a positive total at least seven, the larger one has at least four positives; two positives in the other would share all of those, impossible. Hence, if positives exist, all but at most one are in a single component A. Apply the same argument to negatives to obtain B if needed. At most two sites are outside A and B, yet every further component requires four. A chart with three or more F components is therefore impossible. Otherwise there are at most two matrices.
* \(e=4\): opposite signs in distinct components are completely joined. If both signs occur each population is at least seven. Some component A must have at least two positives: otherwise there are at least seven positive-bearing components each with at least three negatives; two positives in distinct components then have at least fifteen common negatives outside both. Two positives in A force at most two negatives outside A, and therefore at least five negatives in A. These negatives force at most two positives outside A. At most four vertices are outside A, leaving room for at most one more component. Thus if a chart has at least three F components, the sole possible case has **uniform site signs**. This permits at most one matrix (or none if the fixed signed relations forbid it). With at most two components there are at most two matrices as before. Optionally, in the uniform case the induced nonzero-site graph is F and is cubic: degree \(3\bmod7\) and far degree eight force degree three. Each nonzero has five zero-site neighbours and each zero has 0 or 7 nonzero neighbours by \(A_Xv=3v\); hence \(5n\le7(60-n)\), so \(n\le35\). No cubic component can be \(K_4\).

These exceptional assertions are **not** a claim that the sign equations alone have at most two solutions. They first reject any putative solution inconsistent with the stated clique/common-neighbour restrictions; the two-candidate bound is for this pruned, directly testable list. Check its integer degrees and \(A^2=12I-A+2J\).

**Why at most one candidate can be an actual graph.** Two putative graphs on the same contact chart have integer adjacency difference E supported entirely on far pairs, with entries 0,1,−1. Modulo seven this difference of exterior Gram blocks (up to sign) is the difference of two rank-one Schur complements; their computed projection blocks are identical. Hence \(\operatorname{rank}_7 E\le2\). Each integer three-by-three minor has absolute value at most \(3^{3/2}<7\) by Hadamard and is divisible by seven; thus every minor is literally zero. A nonzero symmetric zero-diagonal E has rank at least two, so its real rank is exactly two. The [rank-two difference lemma](triangle-complement.md#a-rank-two-difference-is-a-transposition) would force a transposition changing twelve old neighbours at each of two vertices. All changed adjacency here is between far sites, which have only eight old far neighbours. This contradiction establishes uniqueness with no prescribed exterior adjacency row.
