# Five-site wing bonds force a unit field with a positive edge

The [unbalanced three-cut construction](three-cut-original-fields.md) can be sharpened for a five-site shore if original Fano marks are absent, as they are at [Seidel rank 36](rank36-no-original-fano.md). In that case each nontrivial three-edge wing bond with a five-site shore emits an original signed-unit charge-one field of squared norm 23 with one positive edge and two disjoint negative edges. A field is emitted by at most four such bonds over all triangles. Thus C five-site bonds force at least ceil(C/4) distinct original fields of this type. The result does not assert that any five-site bond occurs.

For the proof, write the five-site shore \(V\) in the three physical wings \(A,B,C\). The published unbalanced-cut parity rule forces counts \(1,2,2\). Its two-site pairs are mates in \(B,C\), whose inter-wing matching is complete on \(V\). These four sites induce a square. The site \(u\) in \(A\) sees one site in each of the other wings. They must be a matched pair \(v,w\), since otherwise \(u\) is a third common neighbor of opposite square corners. Write \(v',w'\) for their mates, also matched across the wings. Thus \(V\) is a square and a triangle sharing edge \(vw\). Its three boundary edges leave \(u,v',w'\) for sites in wing \(A\), respectively the mate of \(u\), the \(A\)-match of \(v'\), and the \(A\)-match of \(w'\).

Let \(T\) be the base triangle, \(p\) its root for wing \(A\), and \(S=T\cup V\). The emitted raw field is
\[
 b=(A-3I)1_S-\mathbf1 .
\]
This is the original mark for coefficient \(-1_S\) and literal charge one. The eight-site set \(S\) has 14 edges, so the strongly regular identity gives \(\|b\|^2=21\cdot8-14\cdot14+2\cdot8^2-77=23\). Inside \(S\) its only negatives are \(p,u,v',w'\), each of value \(-1\); elsewhere its value is \(|N(x)\cap S|-1\), never below \(-1\).

If \(b_x=2\), subtract the ordinary mark \(d_x=(3I-A)e_x\). Since \(b\cdot d_x=7b_x+2\) and \(\|d_x\|^2=23\), this gives an original neutral norm-14 field, which is excluded by hypothesis. If \(b_x=3\), then \(b=d_x\), by the same norm identity. Such an \(x\) cannot be far from \(T\): the only possible far co-contact pairs in \(V\) are the three pairs among \(u,v',w'\), as checked below. A \(+3\) must therefore absorb all three boundary edges in wing \(A\). It is then a singleton on the other shore of the bond, excluded by the nontrivial hypothesis. Higher positive entries would make \(\|b-d_x\|^2<0\). Hence \(b\) is signed unit. In particular the three boundary edges have distinct outside endpoints.

A far site cannot take two vertices of the upper triangle \(u,v,w\), whose edges already have their caps in that triangle. It cannot take \(v,v'\) or \(w,w'\), whose edges already have the corresponding root cap. Nor can it take the two opposite corners \(v,w'\) or \(w,v'\) of the square, whose common-neighbor quotas are full in \(V\). Of the three remaining pairs, \(u,v'\) and \(u,w'\) have one common neighbor already in \(V\). They cannot have one outside \(V\) in the wing, since their boundary endpoints are distinct. The adjacent pair \(v',w'\) can only have a common wing neighbor in \(A\), where its two boundary endpoints are also distinct. Thus each remaining pair has exactly one far witness. These are three different sites, since a triple far contact would give \(+2\). The 50 far contacts of the five wing sites now give the exact histogram \(13,44,3\) at contact sizes \(0,1,2\). Besides the three positive boundary sites, the three far witnesses complete the six positives. The four negative edge endpoints \(p,u,v',w'\) meet them exactly as the vertex–edge incidence of a \(K_4\): the three boundary sites correspond to the pairs involving \(p\), and the three far sites to the other pairs.

For any signed-unit field of charge one and norm 23 the sign sizes are six positive and seventeen negative. Write \(e_P,e_N\) for the two induced edge counts, \(Q_P,Q_N\) for the sums of the internal \(\binom{\deg}{2}\). The equation \(Ab=-4b-2\mathbf1\) gives \(e_N=e_P+1\) by counting crossing edges. Counting common-neighbor slots on the positives gives
\[
30-e_P\ \ge\ Q_P +17+4e_N+Q_N .
\]
Thus \(5e_P+Q_P+Q_N\le9\), hence \(e_P\le1\). The five-site construction already has the two disjoint negative edges \(pu,v'w'\), so \(e_P=1,e_N=2\) and these are the only negative edges.

For a fixed field \(b\), the exceptional root \(p\) must be one of the four negative edge endpoints. Its unique negative neighbor is \(u\), and the other negative edge identifies \(v',w'\). For the pair \(p,v'\), the two common neighbors are the root of wing \(B\) and the positive \(A\)-match of \(v'\). Thus the unique **zero** common neighbor recovers the second root. The same argument with \(w'\) recovers the third. Once \(T\) is known, the three boundary edges are the known within-wing mate and two known inter-wing matching edges at the negative darts. Removing them recovers the actual bond, counted once independently of unrelated components. This proves the four-emitter cap.
