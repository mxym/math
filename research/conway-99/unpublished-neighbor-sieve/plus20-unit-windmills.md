# Entire complementary norm-20 shell is signed-unit

Let G be a hypothetical SRG(99,14,1,2), and use the [complementary +3 sector notation](../complementary-rank56/README.md): F^+(a,t) counts integral fields b of charge a, with Ab=3b+a·1, sum b=9a and squared coordinate norm t. Put N_t^+=F^+(0,t), counting both signs. The following results are rank-free.

* Every neutral N_20^+ vector is signed-unit (all entries in {−1,0,1}).
* Every F^+(2,22) field is signed-unit, and its two negative sites form an edge anticomplete to the twenty positive sites. The positives induce degree five.
* Every F^+(1,17) field is signed-unit, and its four negative sites induce a four-cycle anticomplete to the thirteen positive sites. The positives induce degree four.

Consequently at scaled complementary dual exponent ten there is the graph-resolved identity

    x_10^+ = N_20^+ + 2 F^+(2,22) + 4 F^+(1,17),

with the exact literal support restrictions above. It does not assume any of these populations is nonempty.

## A windmill shadow has a unique center

Call a windmill s=2e_i+1_Z, where Z consists of three distinct mate-pairs in N(i) (the fourteen neighbors of i induce a matching of seven edges). Let U=N(i)∖Z. The support is connected. Write h_s=(A−3I)s. On the support h_s=0, and h_s=2 on U. Every far vertex outside {i}∪N(i) has exactly two near contacts, in different mate-pairs; label it of type UU, UZ or ZZ according to these two contacts. Its height h_s is respectively 0,1 or2. Thus h_s=0 exactly on {i}∪Z∪UU, and h_s=2 exactly on U∪ZZ.

This is the same height pattern used for the cubic tail in the [prior complementary tail-budget proof](../complementary-rank56/tail-budget.md). No cubic tail is required for the center-recovery argument itself. Any alternative windmill center i' producing this h must have height zero and eight height-two neighbors inducing a four-edge matching. A site z∈Z has only four neighbors of height two: the ZZ far vertices labeled by z paired with a nonmate in Z. A ZZ site has exactly its two Z anchors in N(i) by the common-neighbor rule with i, so it has no neighbors in U. A far y∈UU has its two U contacts among the height-two neighbors; they are nonmated since their other common neighbor is i. They have no edge to any other height-two neighbor of y because there are no U-to-ZZ edges and y has no other U contacts. Hence these two sites are isolated in the height-two neighborhood of y. Only the original i can be the center. Once i is found, its height-two neighbor set is U, recovering Z and s uniquely. In particular there are not two different windmills with equal h_s.

## No exceptional neutral norm-20 vector

Let d_i=(A+4I)e_i, of charge two and norm 30. For a field of charge a, b·d_i=7b_i+a. If a neutral norm-20 field has b_i≤−3, shifting by +d_i gives charge two and norm at most 20+30−42=8, less than its absolute mass 18. Reversing signs proves max |b_i|≤2.

If k sites are doubled, the weighted mass on each sign is m=10−k, because 20=2m+2k. A double requires own-sign neighbor mass at least six and so m−2≥6, giving k≤2. If there is a sole double, orient it negative. There are seven other negative unit sites on that side. The double sees six plus the number of its positive neighbors among these seven. If it sees six negatives, the sole far negative would have at most two same-sign neighbors (all would be common neighbors with the pivot), insufficient for its required signed sum. If it sees all seven negatives, each of those seven, already seeing the doubled pivot, needs an additional negative neighbor; the seven lie in the matching on the pivot neighborhood and cannot all be paired. Thus k=1 is impossible.

If both doubles are on one sign, both must connect to every other site on that sign to reach their required neighbor mass of six, leaving four common unit neighbors. This is impossible. The remaining possibility has one double on each sign. Each such pivot sees all six unit sites on its side and no opposite site, because its available same-sign mass is exactly six. Each adjacent unit must see its own mate among those six and no opposite site: its signed magnitude-three neighbor sum consists of weight two at its center, at most a single same-side mate edge, and a nonpositive contribution from the opposite side. Thus the two weighted shores are windmills s,s', disjoint and anticomplete. The actual neutral field would be s−s', with h_s=h_s' by the eigenvector equation. The recovery just proved forbids this, so k=0.

## The two neighboring charged shells

For F^+(2,22), the excess sum p_i(p_i−1)=4 has two contributions of size two, each +2 or −1. A configuration with one negative is impossible because a site of value −1 needs signed neighbor sum −1. If there were two doubles, subtract d_i from the field at either doubled pivot; this gives neutral norm 22+30−2(14+2)=20 with an entry −2, already excluded. Hence the two exceptional entries are both −1. They must be adjacent and anticomplete to the positive support by their neighbor equation. This implies the positive internal degree five.

For F^+(1,17), excess eight is either six plus two or four contributions of two. A −2 could occur only with at most one other −1 and cannot obtain neighbor sum −5. A +3 with only positive entries cannot be adjacent to a zero (whose required sum is one), so it would need fourteen nonzero neighbors despite the mass nine. If one negative accompanied +3 it would be alone and impossible. In the four-contribution case, zero negatives likewise makes any double impossible, by the same zero-neighbor exclusion; one or two negatives cannot supply the required neighbor sum −2 to each. If there are three negatives, they must form a triangle anticomplete to all positives, and the lone +2 would require all fourteen neighbors positive (no negative neighbor and no zero neighbor). There are only ten other unit positives then, impossible. Hence there are exactly four −1 entries and thirteen +1 entries. The induced negative graph has minimum degree two, so it is a four-cycle: the only denser possibilities on four vertices are a diamond or K4, both forbidden by adjacent common-neighbor counts. Each negative vertex has internal degree two and is therefore anticomplete to the positives. This forces positive internal degree four.

Finally the [binary dual dictionary](../complementary-rank56/README.md#2-dual-label-dictionary) at j=10 gives t=20,6 at a=0 with multiplicities one and two; t=17 at |a|=1 with total multiplicity four; and t=22 at |a|=2 with total multiplicity two. To verify directly, the binary numerator 6p²−2pq+2q² ≤ (220+9a²−99|a|)/7 restricts |a| ≤3; for a≥0 the compatibility a≡−p+2q mod11 gives respectively (0,0), ±(2,1); (1,1),(-1,0); (0,1); and none. The neutral norm-six term vanishes by the complementary minimum twelve.

## One exact old-shell overlap with a weighted support

A windmill's positive mass is eight and its internal weighted quadratic form for A−3I is zero, so w(−s) has old squared norm 64/9, charge +1 and raw cost 51, in the zero original seven-class. It is distinct from the 105336 obligatory ≤5-site old marks of that cost. Indeed w(−s)=w(c) for c=1_P−1_Q, sum c=1 and |Q|≤2 would make s+c a charge-one mass-nine +3 field. It has at most two negative unit locations and thus no negatives (each would need two negative neighbors). It is then a 9-grid indicator, because any coefficient ≥2 would have to have all fourteen neighbors nonzero despite mass nine. The doubled center must be decreased by one of the two negative inputs, but at most one of its six unit neighbors can also be removed. It would have grid degree at least five, impossible.

Consequently the old excess C_old(1,51)−105336 has buckets containing either or both induced cubic eight-site supports and a uniquely recovered windmill. All members have the same h once w(−s) is fixed; both inputs have mass eight. Cubic eight-sets miss the same obligatory old marks by the [existing cubic-tail argument](../complementary-rank56/tail-budget.md), which only uses cubicity. Two different connected supports of equal h are disjoint and anticomplete: an overlap would separate the set difference from the common sites, because h is zero on one support and on every anti-neighbor and its other input has strictly positive entries on its support. Thus per old bucket a windmill is unique and there are at most three cubic eight-sets (four would form a disallowed 8+24 anticomplete split).

A bucket with a windmill s=2e_i+1_Z and a cubic eight-set C yields exactly one F^+(2,20) field d_i+1_C−s, since their shadow difference is a true +3 eigenvector. Conversely the [literal F^+(2,20) classification](../complementary-rank56/tail-budget.md) gives a unique doubled pivot i, a cubic eight-set C at distance two, and exactly three mate pairs Z of its neighbors at zero. It recovers exactly this pair and equal h. Thus the extension of the prior tail budget to **all** cubic eight-sets shares the same old E_8 bucket count, with M=F^+(2,20) exactly the cubic eight-sets in windmill buckets. This last description introduces no graph-realizability claim.
