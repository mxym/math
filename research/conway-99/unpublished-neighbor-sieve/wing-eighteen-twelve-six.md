# Rank 36 and the dimension-three 18-component: the 18+12+6 split

In the notation of the [preceding 18-wing results](wing-eighteen-three-two.md), if rank is 36 and a triangle has the special 18-component D3, the other components must be the 12-component B (tree number 7140, centered radical dimension one) and a triangular prism. Moreover, at most 39 of the 99 oriented current-comparison charts in this last case can occur. This does not assert any of them is realizable; it is a statement about a specific rooted component, not all rank-36 triangles.

The earlier 18+18 and 18+6+6+6 exclusions leave only 18+12+6. The unit-tree 12-components were already removed by the rank bound. The two remaining 12-components are A (tree number 7350, centered dimension two) and B, with centered evaluations visible on the exterior. In both cases rank forces their evaluation space to lie in the D3 space: here ell=34−3−d for the three components, and increasing s from 3 to ≥4 gives ell+2s≥37. For d=1 or 2 this containment is a full-row-rank matrix M over F7 (size d-by-3) expressing EVERY pair evaluation on the twelve-component as M times the simultaneous triple evaluation on D3. The D3 template realizes every permutation of the wings; we may therefore fix the twelve-component's relative naming, as may the single prism (label its three pairs by wing and its two triangle lines by bit).

The D3 template is the same flat three-wing chart as before. For the 12-component take the mates (1,0,3,2), (2,3,0,1), (1,0,3,2), the first two cross ties as identities, and the final return permutation as (0,2,1,3) for A or (0,3,2,1) for B. The rank and tree-number inventory in [the twelve-current inventory](triangle-wing-twelve-currents.md) identifies exactly these two classes. By component balance each of the sixty exterior vertices hits D3 in a triple, the 12-component in a pair, and the prism in one point. Enumerate all 612 eligible D3 triples from the preceding proof, all positive-quota pairs of the 12-component, then both possible prism vertices in the remaining wing (discard if combined contacts of D3 and the 12-component already exceed two in some wing). Keep only columns satisfying the displayed current comparison. A legitimate mark set must sum to exactly two contacts from each wing.

As before the pair coordinate for distinct i,j has required count m_ij=2−[i adjacent j]−|N_W(i) intersect N_W(j)|−[same wing]. Coordinates of zero quota are forbidden; all listed six-tuples use positive coordinates only. The 15 ones in the column correspond to its 15 distinct site pairs. If these sixty vertices existed, their columns would sum to m with total mass 60. The script constructs an integral y in each excluded orientation and checks the strict inequality m·y < 60 min_b(b·y) over every eligible column b. This is an exact obstruction even for a real convex combination with total mass 60. The bounded int64 dot-product check is precisely the one in the previous (D3,D2) note; merely failing to find a witness rejects nothing.

The comparison charts can be identified only by simultaneous actual wing-template relabelings, with no assumptions on symmetries of the full graph. For the 12-component and D3, enumerate all wing-aware automorphisms with the same wing permutation (same procedure: first wing determines the remaining sites by cross matchings). This pair acts by U M V^-1. For A this gives 24 simultaneous pairs on 114,912 maps, with 9726 distinct indexed orbit representatives. All 9726 have exact strict separators (in at most 32 steps). For B the eight simultaneous pairs give 99 representatives of 342 maps; 60 have separators, 39 are retained and explicitly printed. Orbit sizes are counted directly, not presumed equal. The checker does not test exterior-to-exterior adjacency or enumerate 99-vertex graphs.
