# Mod-five visibility of elementary cycles in a saturated wing

Let T be any saturated triangle, so that its twelve transversal partner triangles partition the 36 wing sites. Contract the triangles to a cubic loopless K on twelve tails with edges in three physical colors, as in [saturated-five-currents.md](saturated-five-currents.md); parallel edges are allowed. An oriented simple cycle (including a parallel 2-cycle) defines an integral unit circulation h on the darts, with +1 on each forward dart, −1 on its reverse, and zero elsewhere. Such a current is **always visible modulo five unless the cycle has length ten**, independently of Seidel rank and of the other components. In the exceptional case it must satisfy the precise structural requirements below. This is individual visibility of elementary circuits, not joint linear independence of an entire cycle basis or exclusion of a ten-cycle.

Use B for the 60-by-36 contact matrix, D for the wing adjacency, H for the 8-regular far adjacency. Since Dh=−2h, the full SRG block identity gives HBh=Bh. On integral currents B^tBh=10h: indeed write L for the matrix of adjacencies from triangle roots into the three physical wings. The wing block of A²+A=12I+2J gives B^tB=12I−D−D²+2J−L^tL; Lh=0 and Jh=0. If the cycle of length l is invisible modulo five, z=Bh/5 is an integral H-eigenfield of eigenvalue 1 and ∑z=0. Its squared norm is (4/5)l. Every far site takes only six darts, so z has entries in {−1,0,1}; a nonzero entry takes five cycle darts of the corresponding sign and one off-cycle dart. Thus l=5 or 10 (a simple cycle uses at most twelve tails). At l=5 there would be two positive sites, both taking all five forward darts, contradicting the two-common-neighbor cap.

**Four sites of each sign for l=10.** There are four positive and four negative far extrema of z. The equation B^tz=2h implies each forward dart occurs on exactly two positive extrema, and similarly in reverse. On the four positive sites, label each of the ten forward darts by its pair of extreme sites. Pair multiplicities are at most two and the weighted degrees are five; they are therefore two on four pairs and one on the two pairs of a perfect matching. A pair of multiplicity two cannot be adjacent because it already has two common near neighbors. Since Hz=z each positive extreme must see at least one positive neighbor, so its sole possible positive mate is an actual neighbor and it has no negative neighbor. The negative sites obey the same argument. No other far vertex can neighbor two positive extrema: a twice-covered pair already has its two common near neighbors, while each matched adjacent pair already has its unique common near site. By Hz=z, the 28 distinct outward far neighbors of the positives are precisely those of the negatives, each seeing one of each. Every positive-negative pair is nonadjacent; their 16 pairs require 32 common neighbors in all. Exactly 28 are far. Cross-sign extrema can share only their single off-cycle letter. At any off-cycle letter B^tz=0 makes the positive and negative counts equal, k. The four such remaining incidences per sign yield ∑k=4 and the common-neighbor count gives ∑k²=4. Thus the four off-cycle letters are distinct and match positive extrema bijectively to negative ones.

**Tight color and word profile.** Write p_i for the forward dart at successive cycle tail i, and n_i for its reverse at i+1. The distinct p_i are independent in the wing graph. Exactly the ten consecutive pairs share a near wing neighbor, their intervening reverse dart. To see this directly, the three wing neighbors of p_i are n_i, n_(i−1), and the off-cycle third dart of its own tail; the last cannot neighbor another forward dart. Two positive darts of the same physical color have their triangle root as an additional common site. If k_w is the cycle's number of edges of color w, the total number of far sites witnessing pairs of forward darts (with multiplicity) is therefore 2·C(10,2)−10−∑_w C(k_w,2). Four positive extrema each witness ten pairs; negatives witness none. On the 52 zero sites set r_x=(B1_forward)_x=(B1_reverse)_x. Their sum is 100−20=80. Consequently
       
       28 ≤ ∑_(z_x=0) C(r_x,2) = 40−∑_w C(k_w,2) ≤ 28 .
       
The first inequality is C(r,2)≥r−1; the last by convexity and ∑k_w=10 over three colors. Equality forces the color multiplicities 4,3,3 and the zero words to have r=1 at 24 sites, r=2 at 28 sites. In particular every far word meets the directed cycle and every zero word meets it in two or four darts.

Finally the full graph field f=(3I−A)h/5 is integral, equal to zero on T, equal to h on the 36 wing sites and equal to −z on the far sites. It is a genuine neutral minus-four eigenfield of squared norm 28 (twenty wing units and eight far units). Each signed shore induces just two disjoint edges and ten isolated vertices: the same-sign wing darts are independent, and by opposite far sign there is no same-sign wing/far incidence. The two perfect matchings among far extrema supply those two edges. No graph realizing this witness is known or implied.
