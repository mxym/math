# No norm-14 Fano field at Conway Seidel rank 36

The exact verification below excludes every norm-14 field at Seidel rank 36. Use the definitions of P,N,Z,H from [rank36-no-original-fano.md](rank36-no-original-fano.md). No assumption whatsoever is made that the Fano field is original, or that any nonidentity graph automorphism exists.

Fix p=1, q=2 in the seven positive sites, labeled by binary integers 1..7 with nonzero dot product determining adjacency to the negative covectors. The 29 sites P,N,Z and p's ten external neighbors Yp give the true induced adjacency A. Every H site contacts one vertex of P, one of N, and three vertices of Z. In Yp there are four singleton caps and three double fibers. The actual internal three-edge matching is fixed after numbering the two sites of each double fiber: the matching of three duplicate pairs and the matching of their mates alternate around one six-cycle. The 15 Z labels are exactly the edges of a simple cubic graph D on the ten sites of Yp. Indeed every z contacts precisely two of them, and any given two Yp sites already share p. No true duplicate pair or true mate pair may share a Z point, because the duplicate pair shares p,n and a mate edge already has its cap p.

For the nine optional edges among six double sites, enumerate all masks in [fano-six-contact-determinant.md](fano-six-contact-determinant.md) for the **ordinary nonsquare four-dimensional residual**. The matrix H−LᵀD₆L has H₀₀=0 in the ordinary case (not 4), other diagonal5, off3 among the three double differences, and zero elsewhere. Rank4 requires nonsquare determinant; rank≤3 is initially retained for the source enumeration. There are 281 masks. If m of their nine edges are present, cubic degrees force m−3 edges among the four caps. Choose those, then fill the bipartite remainder by exact degree deficits. Reduce only by the 24 Fano incidence label permutations fixing p and their unique double-fiber relabeling preserving the above mate matching. This produces 20,907 whole cubic diagrams: 1504 / 9842 / 6619 / 2861 / 77 / 3 / 1 by m=3 through 9. A coordinate change need not be a graph automorphism: each resulting diagram would still have its specified positive site q.

There is **no Fano-root row** in our mod-seven Gram G=3I−A+4J on the 39 actual sites. Take the pivot set I of a row reduction, put R=(G_II)⁻¹, and let its determinant be d. For a symmetric Gram the principal submatrix on these indices is nonsingular, also checked directly. The dimensions occurring are 35 or 36. At rank36 its nondegenerate section spans the full ambient space; at rank35 its perpendicular is a nonsingular line of type δ=3/d mod7 because the 36-space has nonsquare determinant. All other existing marks have zero projection to this line, their residual norm being zero, so they satisfy exactly the pairing relations on I. In the rank36 case verify the full determinant type and take δ=0 with residual coordinate h=0 only.

For the new star Yq enumerate every possible contact row C: its fixed sites q and n, an arbitrary Z triple, and one old Yp member when n is adjacent to p, two otherwise. This last count accounts for every common neighbor of p and the new site. In the two-member case they are nonneighbors and have exactly one old common neighbor p; all 24 eligible pairs are retained. The necessary tests C A + C ≤ 2 and C_i C_j (A²+A)_{ij}<2 whenever C_i=C_j=1 and i<j impose the actual pair quotas, including that adding a common neighbor cannot exceed an old pair's budget. For w=4·1−C check w_I R G_{I,*}=w; attach each h with w_I R w_Iᵀ+δh²=0. Two distinct vertices must have projected pairing w_I R w'_Iᵀ+δhh' equal to 3 on an edge or 4 on a nonedge, respectively with at most one or two common neighbors already present in the old 39. The self-pairing is zero, so even equal contact rows do not permit reusing the same lifted candidate. Two distinct lifts of one contact row have many common neighbors and cannot coexist.

The 10 selected candidates must allocate 2−A(q,n) for each of seven negative labels, two at each of 15 Z points, and 2−A(q,n_x) at each of ten old Yp sites x: the only possible common neighbor of x and q outside Yq is n_x. The four cap candidates have no mate in Yq (their mate there is their adjacent n). The six double candidates induce three mate edges, no edge on the same negative label since that would share q and n. The induced loopless multigraph on three two-site negative labels has degree two everywhere, hence precisely one edge between each two labels. For any two chosen labels the program tries **all** pairs of candidates for their unique edge, considering the pair only if its number of common old neighbors and its modular pairing allow adjacency. Every other member of Yq has to be a nonneighbor of both of these endpoints, since the actual graph on N(q) is a matching. After that we impose only *necessary* pair compatibility, not the full second-matching prescription.

On the remaining rows choose a needed column of least number of candidate choices; branch on each row that can be the earliest chosen member in that column. Earlier members of the same column are dropped, not rows outside that column. After consuming each selected row delete full columns and incompatible rows; if fewer than one or two survivors cover any positive remaining quota, reject. This exact search allows arbitrary compatible residual graphs and contains every actual completion of this second star. A surviving local diagram would not itself construct a graph. There is no early numerical or depth cutoff: fixed ten tags select exactly ten vertices.

**Arithmetic.** The rank/candidate code uses integer reduction mod7, with dot-product accumulators on 39 coordinates bounded below 32768 in absolute value. The Node part splits each modular pairing into an integer heading contribution (including line lift) and Z-triple contribution. Its table is just sparse evaluation of w_I=4−C_I against Rw'_I. The 32 quota columns 7+15+10 are represented by exact 32-bit Boolean masks (the sign of bit31 is immaterial). Counts per column fit Uint16: there are at most two norm-equation lifts, at most 24·455·2 rows at a single negative label, 112·91·2 at a Z point, and (4+3·9)·455·2 at each old Yp site. The exact required common-neighbor count is the population count of the two 39-bit contact masks, implemented as a 32-bit mask plus its remaining seven bits. No floating-point linear algebra, numerical infeasibility criterion, optimizer, or symmetry assumption enters. The full code takes optional half-open integer interval arguments [START END]; omitting them checks the whole collection.

A rejection on all 20,907 inputs proves N14=0 at Seidel rank 36, applying equally to displaced and original roots. In particular no two Hoffman 22-cocliques at this rank meet in fifteen sites, and every maximal determinant-49 extension has zero norm-two population. This does not exclude ranks 38, 40, 42, or rank 36 itself.
