# The clean graph off a saturated axis is a matching at rank 36

Suppose o has a saturated axis e in a hypothetical graph of mod-seven Seidel rank 36. Among the six other axes **no two clean pairs have an axis in common**. In particular there are at most three additional grids through o beyond the six on e, hence at most nine total. A second saturated axis is impossible. If every component in the seven wings at o has size at most 18, the stronger upper bound is **seven** grids. This does not exclude an arbitrary saturated triangle, the rank-36 branch, or the whole graph. The independent three-square path argument is not needed for this result.

**Known local setup.** Use [the six-prism contact/Schur formula](saturated-prism-isotropic.md) and [the clean-square span theorem](saturated-prism-clean-square.md). The sixty far sites are six-letter words with one 2w+s symbol from each prism, two of each wing w. Wing zero supplies the signed parents at o. The scaled mod-seven residuals live in a nondegenerate five-space; their norms are zero. For distinct far sites x,y define \(b(x,y)=43-18n-4k-6h\), counting exact matches n, same wings k, and unlike wings with same bit h. Then their actual scaled residual pairing is \(b(x,y)-4A_{xy}\) mod seven. Any two near symbols from distinct prisms have exactly \(2-[\mathrm{same\ wing}]\) far common neighbors. In particular two distinct far rows cannot agree in more than two letters or twice in one wing.

Suppose the clean squares are X=01 and Y=02, sharing their center axis 0. They are anticomplete, because a signed parent already gives the unique contact to the other grid. Each four-row word diagram takes the four possible parent-bit pairs at its own two slots in lexicographic order; each of its other four columns permutes 2,3,4,5. Its row sum vanishes in the residual, and its self-Gram has rank at least two by the cited clean-square theorem. Normalize only X: assign component 2 (the other named axis of Y) its place, sort the remaining three components, allow independent prism-line bit name changes and simultaneous interchange of wings 1,2. Do **not** exchange the roles of 0 and 1. Equivalently, from the 75 basic one-square diagrams distinguish the 2-slot, minimize the two parent sign choices (which reorder rows) and the stated wing/slot renamings. This gives 108 first diagrams. For Y use all 23,040 wing-balanced possibilities. Reject excess near-match overlaps and require the cross-Gram row and column sums zero (the square sums). Let G be the 6-by-6 Gram on three rows per square. The sum conditions retain the fourth rows without adding unknown adjacencies or assuming linear independence between the squares.

**Twelve compulsory signed pairs, six simultaneous words.** For each sign \(s\) of axis 0 and each sign \(t\) of each of d=3,4,5 there is exactly one far site with these signed \(0_s,d_t\) graph neighbours: the two parents are nonadjacent, share o, and have exactly one other common site. This site has no far neighbour in X or Y: in either grid its \(0_s\) parent is already the unique contact. Every candidate is therefore one of the \(12\cdot96\) individually balanced six-letter words with zero-wing slots 0 and d. None of these cells is assumed clean. Test each against the eight known sites for the elementary two-word match restrictions above, and do not let it use a near-symbol pair already exhausted by those eight.

All twelve signed-pair classes must have at least one possibility in the same ambient rank chart. Moreover the six words for \(s=0\) must **simultaneously** fill the exact near-symbol marginals. Indeed the ten far neighbours of \(0_0\) contain the two such X rows, two Y rows, and the six unknowns. Against *each* other prism the ten counts are 1,1,2,2,2,2. Subtract the four known contributions. Require one word per remaining \(d,t\) and the prescribed 30 residual entries, disallowing impossible common-neighbour overlaps between every pair selected.

The six unknown sites for \(s=0\) must induce a perfect matching. The whole neighbourhood of \(0_0\) induces seven disjoint edges. Its four near neighbours are already matched: o with \(0_1\), and the other two same-line vertices in the 0-prism with each other. The two known X sites touching \(0_0\) are mates inside X, as are the two Y sites touching it. Thus the remaining six induce three disjoint edges. The checker only needs this further test when the base has rank five.

**Complete treatment of singular two-square spans.** Choose a nonsingular principal sub-Gram \(G_{II}\) of order \(h=\operatorname{rank}G\); the algorithm asserts that a choice exists in every retained case. For a candidate far site z the six pairings w with the six chosen base rows are all prescribed by b (no edges). Set
\[
 p=G_{II}^{-1} w_I,\qquad \Delta=w-G_{:,I}p,\qquad \nu=-w_I^t p.
\]
These are the remaining pairings to the base and the required norm of z's component in the orthogonal complement of I. Reject \(h>5\). The enumeration verifies that every base otherwise has \(h=3,4,5\).

* \(h=5\): require \(\Delta=0,\nu=0\). Each eligible z now has completely known geometric coordinates, so for two candidate words z,z' its adjacency must equal \(2(b(z,z')-w_I^tG_{II}^{-1}w'_I)\pmod7\) since \(4^{-1}=2\). Require this value be 0 or 1, the literal common-neighbour quota \(n+A\le2\), and the perfect matching on the six chosen words.
* \(h=4\): the leftover dimension is one, nondegenerate. Each base vector's leftover component has square zero, so is zero there; require \(\Delta=0\). Under one common choice of line square class, every \(\nu\) must be a square or zero after multiplication by that representative. Both square classes 1 and 3 are tested; we do not pick a sign by an unproved ambient determinant.
* \(h=3\): the leftover dimension is two. All six base residual fragments in it have mutual Gram zero. They vanish, or span one isotropic line \(\ell\). In the first case allow every \(\Delta=0\) candidate without any norm filter. In the second their pairings to every candidate have the form \(\Delta=\alpha u\) along a single common projective direction \(\alpha\in{\bf F}_7^6\), with u the pairing to \(\ell\). If \(u=0\), the new vector is orthogonal to \(\ell\) in the plane, hence on \(\ell\), forcing \(\nu=0\). The checker tests every nonzero projective direction actually occurring among the candidate columns as well as the zero-only option. If no true required word has nonzero \(\Delta\), the latter option covers it even if the base line exists. We do not identify formal Gram kernels with actual relations without these dimension arguments.

Exactly 52,144 / 1,945 / 42 base diagrams remain at ranks 5/4/3. Requiring every one of the twelve signed-pair classes to have some quota-compatible word in the same rank chart leaves respectively 156 / 4 / 0 chart instances (rank four tested separately for each square class). Not one admits the \(s=0\) six-word fan under the stated tests. The companion NumPy-only checker runs in about 37 seconds on one core (36.4 seconds in the independent replay), using integer arithmetic, direct rank, and a depth-six enumeration on the plucked local chart only. A surviving isolated rank-five set of four or six site marks is **not** asserted impossible by itself. Related earlier work on the six-prism rank-four/five extension approach, and the known issue with any class-only rejection, is summarized on the [ProofAtlas research map](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/conways-99-graph-conjecture/); that map is not a proof dependency.

**Corollaries and scope.** With \(k\) matching edges among the six other axes there are \(6+k\) grids through o, hence at most nine. For a root with all wing components of size at most eighteen, [at least four distinct incident triangles](triangle-wing-four-distinct.md) have an eighteen-component. The saturated triangle has none. Each of the \(2k\) matched peripheral axes has precisely two incident grids; after its two prisms the remaining size 24 can only be \(12+12\), as \(18+6\) would supply a third prism. At least four unmatched axes are therefore needed, so \(k\le1\) and o is on at most seven grids.

In particular the six-cubic twelve-grid/no-large-component rank36 case is excluded: nine dirty edges, six cubic groups and no degree-two group force at least one saturated axis (at most six distinct original axes across the six groups). This contradicts the nine-grid bound without requiring a classification of the dirty graph. Together with the independent [four-prism split theorem](wing-twelve-four-prism-split.md) and preceding rank-free four-cubic reductions, a rank36 twelve-grid root has an incident wing component of size at least 24; the analogous thirteen-grid case was already proved. This is a conditional root theorem and does not assert that any such root occurs.
