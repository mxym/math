# Universal bound on invisible five-currents at a saturated triangle

For any saturated triangle in a hypothetical Conway graph, use its properly 3-edge-colored cubic contraction K on 12 tails and the [mod-five far current map](saturated-five-currents.md). Write c for the number of components and e for the kernel dimension. Then **e≤4** unconditionally, and **e≤3** when K is connected. Therefore at least c+2 of its 6+c current dimensions are visible, and only 1+2e≤9 nondegenerate dimensions remain after splitting off the visible hyperbolic pairs in the mod-five wing complement. This is not full injectivity or a saturation exclusion.

Each component has an even tail size n and circulation dimension n/2+1. Use [injectivity up to nine edges](saturated-nine-edge-five-flow.md), and [kernel dimension ≤1 on any union totaling eight tails](eight-tail-five-code.md). The one-component case is covered by the [connected calculation](connected-saturated-five-rank.md). If the partition of 12 includes a 4 and is disconnected, the remaining eight tails carry at most one invisible dimension and the four-tail target has dimension three, giving e≤4 by projection to that target. This also covers partitions with several fours. A component of size six leaves six tails (nine edges) with injective marking, so projection to this six-tail component bounds e by four. Six components of size two are already covered by the [stronger six-prism theorem](six-prism-flow-plane.md). This accounts for every partition except (10,2) and (8,2,2).

For (8,2,2), the pair of two-tail prisms has injective marking. The projection from the invisible code onto the eight-tail flow space V₈ is injective, so e≤5. If equality held it would give the graph of a linear map L:V₈→V₂⊕V₂. Its kernel has dimension at most one by the eight-tail result; it must therefore be surjective. The invisible code is totally isotropic in the direct-sum edge metric. Thus the form on V₈ is exactly the negative pullback by L of the nonsingular form on the two prisms. Each prism's circulation form is the zero-sum plane in three standard coordinates, determinant 3; the direct sum has square determinant. Consequently the radical of V₈ must be one-dimensional and its four-dimensional quotient must have square determinant (the total sign change has positive determinant). There are 19 connected eight-tail contractions, with radical-dimension counts 14,4,1 at b=0,1,2. In every one of the four b=1 types the quotient instead has nonsquare determinant. This suffices, with no contact chart for (8,2,2).

For (10,2) the full flow space has dimension eight. Since the prism metric is nonsingular, its radical dimension is that of the connected ten-tail constituent. A totally isotropic five-plane requires radical dimension ≥2 by the Witt-index bound. Among all 81 connected ten-tail contractions there are respectively 59,21,1 with radical 0,1,2. In the last type the normalized diagonal of the ten-tail circulation form over F5 is (1,1,1,2,0,0). A putative global five-plane projects to only two dimensions in the prism, so contains a three-plane of invisible currents wholly inside the ten-tail component. There are just twelve occurrences of isotropic three-planes of minimum support at least ten in this six-dimensional constituent, using the same maximal-extension/pivot test as for connected K. Each far word selects exactly five darts from these ten tails. Keep the five-dart evaluations zero on that three-plane with distinct tails, ≤2 per physical wing and no forbidden dart pair. The prescribed 30-dart pair-multiplicity vector is outside the binary span of the allowed five-dart pair columns in each of the twelve cases, by the same necessary integer-to-F2 test as for eight tails. Thus e=5 is impossible here also.

The small [universal-cap checker](universal_saturated_five_rank.py) imports the two previous native checkers and reruns the two exceptional component catalogs, square classes, and twelve binary contradictions. The arguments require neither a chosen global automorphism nor an exhaustive search for complete graphs.
