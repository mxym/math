# At rank 36 an 18 | 18 wing bond excludes eight transversal triangles

Let T be a triangle and let D be its 36-site cubic wing. The transversal cycles use only edges between different physical wings; a transversal triangle is a prism mate of T. Without any rank hypothesis, a two-edge cut precludes nine or more transversal triangles. If the mod-seven Seidel rank is 36 and removal of a two-edge cut leaves two connected 18-site shores, T has at most **seven** transversal triangles. This does not exclude 18 | 18 in general, or narrower cuts under the eight-triangle hypothesis.

## The short marked cycles and half graphs

By the [thin-cut theorem](arbitrary-wing-thin-cuts.md), both edges of the cut belong to the same inter-wing matching, AB say. They must lie on the same transversal cycle: a sole deleted edge on a cycle has an intact bypass. The two paths left in that cycle are on opposite shores. A segment with one site of each physical wing would join its two same-sign Fano endpoints via the third-wing site, impossible since all common neighbors of those two signed sites lie on the opposite Fano sign, not at that third-wing zero. The cut cycle thus has at least twelve sites, at least six on each side. This proves the universal nine-triangle assertion.

With eight transversal triangles, the cut cycle is exactly length twelve. In the 18 | 18 case each side therefore contains four complete triangles plus one path on six sites. Restore the missing AB edge between the two endpoints in each half. The two repaired cubic graphs are both 3-edge-connected. Indeed, a bridge other than the restored edge would already have been a bridge in the full graph (the restored edge has an internal bypass). A cut-pair separating the repaired half either separates both terminals from a nonempty part by two old edges, which also cut the full graph, or consists of the restored edge and a separating old edge. In the latter case that old edge and the original crossover leaving its terminal component give a different cut in the full graph. Both alternatives contradict the [six-middle theorem](thin-cut-six-middle.md): a genuine 18 | 18 bond has no second wing cutset at its triangle. No realization of the *repaired* graph as an actual triangle wing is assumed.

Inside one half index sites of A,B,C by 0 through 5, arranging the AC and BC matchings to connect equal indices. The repaired AB permutation is a transposition with the marked A endpoint at index 0, therefore the transposition (0 1) after relabeling. In the unrepaired graph p=A0 and q=B1 each have two wing neighbors on the half; pq is missing. The within-wing mates are three independent perfect matchings on six indexed sites (15 options each). Take minimal triples under the permutations fixing 0 and commuting with (0 1), i.e. arbitrary permutations on the other four indices. No ambient graph automorphism is used. Require the ordinary pair upper bounds on the actual half together with the three roots; require p and q to have no common neighbor there (both are positive Fano sites); require the repaired graph to be 3-edge-connected. Within the remaining single half shape the direct census gives exactly 52 marked half charts. Complementary shores can be indexed separately because the only two crossing edges are from p to the other's q and from q to the other's p. Reversing the orientation of the cut exchanges the shores and Fano signs. Hence all cases are covered by 52·53/2=1378 unordered pairs.

## The compulsory ten signed far words

Write f for the actual norm-14 field emitted by the cut, positive on p,q of the first shore and negative on the two opposite terminals of the second. On the 60 far sites, f(x)=3−|N(x)∩U|. Both signs have seven sites, so there are precisely five further positive sites and five further negative sites, all far. The sign-side labels are the nonzero vectors/covectors of binary three-space, adjacency given by pairing 1 (the complementary Fano design). Give the two positive wing terminals p,q labels 1,2 in binary coordinates; the two negative terminals in the physical A,B wings have labels 2,1: they see the opposite physical terminal, not their own. This normalization loses nothing: the two distinguished covectors have independent restrictions to the plane of the two distinguished vectors; extend by their unique common nonzero annihilator to get the required basis. Label each sign's five far sites 3 through 7.

For a far sign +, enumerate a word with two contacts in the positive half and four in the negative half; interchange those sizes for sign −. The small two-contact set misses the terminals of its own sign and all their actual wing neighbors there: two positive sites have all common neighbors on the negative Fano side and that half contains no negative site. The large four-contact set contains exactly the opposite terminals prescribed by the binary pairing. Each physical wing contributes exactly two sites in the full word. On each partial word one may reject a pair exceeding its available common-neighbor capacity on the half and triangle, or a point having too many common neighbors with the proposed new exterior site there.

For ten chosen full words, two distinct far sites may never use the same unordered contact pair in a physical wing: the pair is nonmate and already has its root in common. Two same-sign sites have no common contact among unsigned wing vertices. For any two words their shared wing contacts are at most two minus their prescribed adjacency; this adjacency is zero on like signs and the fixed binary pairing on opposite signs. Finally check the signed eigen-equation at every wing site: Df_W plus the sum of signed far-contact columns equals −4f_W. Roots of T are zero.

## Exact mod-seven check including deficient wings

In the nondegenerate mod-seven quotient of all the site marks at rank 36 the ambient dimension is 36 and the determinant class is nonsquare, as in the [tree obstruction](triangle-wing-tree-rank36.md). On this wing use G=3I−D+4J over F7. A far word w (a binary 36-vector) has pairing vector h=4·1−w and diagonal zero, and the required mutual pairing for two different far sites is 4 minus adjacency.

Take any full-rank column index set I of G. Since G is symmetric the corresponding principal matrix G_II is nonsingular (the selected coordinate vectors are a basis modulo the radical). Of the 1378 pairs, 1189 have rank 36 and 189 have rank 35; no larger deficiency occurs. At full rank, 547 determinants are square and impossible at once. For the other 642, a word must have h_IᵀG_II⁻¹h_I=0, and the pair projections must match the actual pairings.

At rank 35 the orthogonal complement of the nondegenerate I-subspace is an anisotropic line: the remaining formal wing coordinate, after subtracting its I-projection, has zero norm and hence is zero. Every h must therefore satisfy the one omitted pairing equation h_j=G_jI G_II⁻¹h_I. Let d=3 if det(G_II) is square, d=1 otherwise. Then d represents the residual line's norm up to squares (the ambient determinant is nonsquare). Retain **every** y in F7 with h_IᵀG_II⁻¹h_I+d y²=0, including both signs when nonzero. For each selected pair add the true residual product d y y′ to its I-projected pairing. This imposes no unsupported assumption of full rank or unique signs.

Every one of the 642 full-rank nonsquare and 189 corank-one charts is empty. All calculations are exact integer or finite field operations.
