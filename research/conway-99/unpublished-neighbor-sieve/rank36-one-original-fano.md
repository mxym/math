# At rank 36 there is at most one original Fano direction

This is a graph-wide statement, not assuming that any small cut or automorphism exists. Call a signed norm-14 neutral Fano vector f **original** if f/√7 is in the original seven-local graph lattice. At Seidel rank 36 there is at most one such direction up to sign. Consequently no triangle can have two different thin two-edge cuts (up to complementary shores), at least 168 of the 231 triangles are two-cut-free and at least 42 vertices lie on no triangle with a two-edge cut. Nothing here forbids a single original Fano or an arbitrary displaced Fano.

For distinct Fano directions the absolute intersection dot is at most four (three agreements on the same signed seven-set determine the entire Fano configuration); two original directions have inner product f·g/7 seven-integral, so are orthogonal. Use the [two-Fano core](two-fano-core.md) and its labeling. Write Z_F,Z_G for the 15 vertices missing the respective Fano. These are independent, disjoint, and avoid the other signed support. Every vertex not signed or in Z_F meets exactly one sign of each kind for F and exactly three members of Z_F, and vice versa. For the latter count: each such vertex has 2 neighbors on F and has exactly four neighbors in the 22-coclique made of Z_F and a sign side, of which one is the sign neighbor (the Hoffman bound is tight). In particular every member of Z_G sees exactly three members of Z_F.

Use the [rank-36 unit graph-mark space](triangle-wing-tree-rank36.md) over F7: it has dimension 36 and nonsquare determinant. The site-pairing matrix is 3I−A+4J; its diagonal is zero. An original normalized Fano root has integral pairings f_i with these marks, norm two, and the two assumed original roots pair to zero. Take H on the 24 signed sites and these two roots. It has rank 24, nonsquare determinant on its image, so a principal nondegenerate 24-space has a square-type 12-dimensional orthogonal complement.

For an element of Z_F, its 24-core contact pair is precisely a G-exclusive + and a G-exclusive −. Each of ten G exclusives sees three such vertices. Thus multiplicities form a 5-by-5 nonnegative integer matrix with margins three and entry upper bound 2−A_ij−|N_core(i)∩N_core(j)|. Give repeated rows deterministic labels: relabeling the 15 as necessary is allowed; no global graph symmetry is assumed. Exhaustive row generation produces 4,987 matrices. If U lists the resulting 15 mixed pairing rows with H (4 at all core positions except 3 on the two contacts, zero on roots), their residual Gram after projecting off a nonsingular principal index set I is
K=3I_15+4J_15−U_I H_II^{-1} U_I^t.
Only 22 matrices have rank at most 12 and correct determinant in rank 12; these all have rank exactly 12. Directly recomputing the full 41-by-41 Gram for each yields rank 36, nonsquare type.

In the 22 full-span cases every individual Z_G vertex is uniquely specified in the modular space by its pairings with a nonsingular 36-mark principal basis. Enumerate its contact with F's two exclusive signs (23 pairs with residual capacity), and its three Z_F contacts (all C(15,3) labeled triples). Its root pairings are zero. Test the complete mixed row against all 41 marks, and its self-pairing against zero. The surviving candidates number 27, 35, 36, 37 or 39. Distinct physical Z_G sites cannot reuse the same modular vector, since they have mutual pairing 4 but self-pairing 0. In the finite graph on remaining candidates with edge when the mutual pairing equals 4, all 15 actual Z_G sites must form a clique. The exhaustive maximum-clique sizes are only three or four. This proves the assertion.

The [native verifier](rank36_one_original_fano.py) uses Python and NumPy integer arrays, no search solver. It imports the adjacent published two_fano_core.py. Pivot elimination and Gram reconstruction are exactly modulo seven. The clique calculation branches on each possible next vertex and prunes only when the entire remaining set could not improve the incumbent. Its output checks every stage, including all 22 final charts.

For the corollaries use [thin-cut emission and the 63-emitter bound](arbitrary-wing-thin-cuts.md): distinct cuts in one triangle produce distinct original directions; one direction can be emitted by at most 63 different triangles. The 14 signed sites and 28 distinct Fano-edge caps of the sole potential emitter cannot lie on its emitting triangle. If none is emitted, all 99 roots and all 231 triangles are clean.
