# The complementary norm-22 nonunit shell and literal 12-site wing unions

This is a rank-free necessary identity for a hypothetical Conway graph, not a rank exclusion. All norms below called raw are ordinary squared coordinate norms. They belong to the **+3** eigenspace, not the minus-four sector. Use the [complementary rank-56 notation](../complementary-rank56/README.md): (F^+(a,t)) counts integer fields (Ab=3b+a1) with (Σ b_i=9a) and (Σ b_i^2=t), with positive charge counted in just that orientation, and (N^+_t=F^+(0,t)) counts both signs. Put (d_i=(4I+A)e_i), of charge 2 and norm 30. For any field (b) of charge (a) one has (b · d_i=7b_i+a).

For an actual triangle (T), its [36-site wing](triangle-complement.md) is cubic, each of its components has size divisible by 6, and a size-six component is equivalent to an actual rook grid on (T). Write (c_k(T)) for its number of components of size (k). Let (L_{12}=Σ_T c_{12}(T)) and (n_3) be the number of unordered grid pairs intersecting in a triangle. Let (Q_j) count the charge-two norm-24 fields with (j) entries equal to +2. The only possible (j) are 0, 1, and 3. Then
```
 Q_3=L_{12}+n_3,   N^{+,nonunit}_{22}=2Q_1+6Q_3.
```
In particular the scaled complementary theta coefficient satisfies
```
 x^+_{11}=N^{+,unit}_{22}+4Q_1+8(L_{12}+n_3)+2Q_0+4F^+(1,19).
```
The unit and nonunit terms here partition the true integral +3 eigenvectors; no −4 shell is used.

## 1. Charge-two norm-24 fields

The nonnegative integer excess (Σ_i p_i(p_i-1)=24-18=6) is either one contribution six or three contributions two. A single +3 with all other entries unit or zero is impossible: a zero site adjacent to +3 would see too much positive mass, so all fourteen neighbours of +3 would have value +1, but its neighbour sum is 11. A single −2 is impossible, since it would be the sole negative site and must have neighbour sum −4. For three contributions two, each is +2 or −1. A lone −1 is impossible because it requires neighbour sum −1. Thus the possible cases are:
* (j=3): three doubles and twelve positive units, with all other sites zero.
* (j=1): one double, eighteen positive units, and two negative units. The two negative sites require neighbour sum −1 and hence form an edge anticomplete to the positive support.
* (j=0): twenty-one positives and three negatives. The negative subgraph has minimum degree one, so it is a path with the center having exactly one positive neighbour and the ends having none, or a triangle whose three sites each have one positive neighbour.

For (j=3), write (D) for the three double sites, (U) for the twelve positive units and (e=e(D)). A double must receive neighbour mass 8, so the number of (D)-to-(U) edges is (24-4e). Each unit can have at most two double neighbours (its neighbour sum is 5). Thus unit common neighbours of pairs of double sites use at least (24-4e-12=12-4e) pair slots. Only (6-e) slots are available by the exact strongly regular common-neighbour rule. For (e=0,1) this is impossible; for (e=2), one of the four available slots is already used by the middle double of the path, so it too is impossible. Hence (D=T) is a triangle.

Every unit has at most one double neighbour since a pair of doubles already has the third double as its unique common neighbour. The (12) edges from (T) to (U) then force one per unit. A unit's residual neighbour mass 3 then provides exactly three unit neighbours within the wing, so (U) is a union of entire cubic wing components. Conversely choosing any union (U) of size 12 yields a charge-two field (2,1_T+1_U): each triangle site has four neighbours in (U); an exterior site beyond (T) and its wing has exactly two neighbours in (U) by component balance, while a wing site outside (U) sees one double and no unit. Alternatively, ((A-3I)1_U) has values (4,0,2) on (T,W,F), and ((A-3I)1_T) has values (-1,1,0) there. The three doubles identify (T) uniquely. Hence (Q_3=Σ_T(c_{12}(T)+choose(c_6(T),2))=L_{12}+n_3).

## 2. All nonunit neutral norm-22 fields

A neutral field (b) with an exceptional entry can be oriented with (b_i≤−2). A value at most −3 would make (b+d_i) charge two with norm at most (22+30-42=10<18), contradicting its coordinate mass. Therefore every coefficient has absolute value at most 2. If there are (k) doubled sites, each sign has total mass (m=11-k), since (22=2m+2k). A doubled site requires own-shore neighbour mass at least 6, hence (m−2≥6) and (k≤3). If (k=3), some shore contains at least two doubles. Both must see all other sites of that shore, since its available mass is exactly 6. They are adjacent and would have at least (8-3-2=3) common neighbours from that shore, contradicting (λ=1). If (k=2) with both doubles on one shore, there are five units on that shore. Each doubled site must see at least four of them and hence the two doubles have at least three common neighbours, impossible even for a nonedge.

If (k=2) with opposite doubles, the two doubles are not adjacent: shifting by (d_i) at the negative one would otherwise produce a +2 and a +3, already excess (8>6) in a charge-two norm-24 field. If that pivot sees any opposite-sign unit, the shifted charge-two field would have at least three doubles, including the two nonadjacent original doubles. This contradicts the triangle classification. By symmetry neither doubled site sees an opposing unit. Each therefore sees exactly six out of seven own-shore units. The remaining far unit has no same-sign neighbours other than those six; at most two of them can be neighbours, by the common-neighbour bound with its own doubled site. Its equation requires at least three same-sign neighbours, a contradiction. Thus (k=1).

Orient so the sole double is −2, with eight other negative sites and ten positive sites. Let (h) be the number of positives adjacent to the pivot. Then it sees (6+h) of its eight negative unit sites, so (h≤2). If (h=1), its sole far negative unit could find at most two same-sign neighbours among the seven near units, again impossible. If (h=0), the two far negative units must be adjacent, see exactly two near negative units each, and have no positive neighbours. Now (p=b+d_i) is a charge-two norm-24 field. Its number of doubles is (1+h), so it belongs to (Q_1) or (Q_3). Conversely, in a field counted by (Q_1), subtract (d_i) at its unique double. The original two negatives are anticomplete to the pivot, and exactly eight of the 14 neighbours of its double were unit positives. This gives one neutral norm-22 field with unique negative double and (h=0). In a field counted by (Q_3), subtract (d_i) at any of its three doubles: the other two become positive units (adjacency inside (T)); the unused eight points of the pivot wing are negative units, and no other exceptional entry occurs. This gives three fields with unique negative double and (h=2). The pivot is recovered from the sole negative double and (p=b+d_i). Including the opposite orientations gives (N^{+,nonunit}_{22}=2Q_1+6Q_3).

## 3. Theta normalization

The [dual-label dictionary](../complementary-rank56/README.md#2-dual-label-dictionary) gives (2j=t+(7(6p^2-2pq+2q^2)-9a^2)/11) with (a ≡ −p+2q mod 11), (|a|≤5), (t≥9|a|) and (t ≡  a mod 2). At (j=11) the possibilities have (a=0,t=22) with multiplicity one; (a=0,t=8) with multiplicity two; (|a|=1,t=19) with total multiplicity four including signs; and (|a|=2,t=24) with total multiplicity two. A brute-force check of this tiny arithmetic step is not necessary: the nonnegative numerator (6p^2-2pq+2q^2≥5p^2+q^2) is bounded by (34,21,11,3) for (|a|=0,1,2,3) respectively, and no (|a|≥4) is possible. The congruence gives, for (a≥0), respectively ((0,0),±(2,1)); ((1,1),(-1,0)); ((0,1)); and none. The norm-eight neutral term vanishes by the already proved minimum 12. Substituting the nonunit decomposition into (x_{11}^{+}=N_{22}^{+}+2(Q_0+Q_1+Q_3)+4F^+(1,19)) gives the displayed identity.

## An independent wing-to-shell injection

More generally, for a fixed (k>0), two disjoint unions (U,V) of components of the same triangle wing with (|U|=|V|=6k) yield the true neutral +3 field (1_U-1_V) of norm (12k). This map on ordered pairs, over all triangles, is injective: the original triangle consists exactly of the three sites with (2k) neighbours in each of (U,V). A remaining wing site has neighbours in at most one side, and a far site sees (k) per side by component balance. In particular each triangle of wing type (18+18) supplies two distinct **oriented** norm-36 fields; two disjoint unions of size 12 supply two distinct oriented norm-24 fields. This injection by itself does not bound these shell populations from above.
