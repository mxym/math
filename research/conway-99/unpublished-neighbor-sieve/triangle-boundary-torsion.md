# Integral order of the exterior torsion

Use the arbitrary-triangle notation D,H,B of [the Smith comparison](triangle-boundary-smith.md). For a symmetric integer matrix M let T(M) be the order of the torsion subgroup of its integral cokernel, taken as 1 if trivial. Put d=dim_Q ker(D+2I), Λ=ker_Z(D+2I), and let e_p be the binary/five-ary nullity of B on Λ/pΛ (the actual reductions of the integral lattice, not necessarily the full modular eigenspace). Then at **any** triangle
    T(H−I) = 7·2^16·5^7 · T(D+2I) · (2^(e₂) 5^(e₅))² / 10^d.

For a saturated triangle, with c contraction components of sizes n_i=2k_i in tails, d=6+c and Λ is the ordinary graph cycle lattice represented by its two opposite darts per edge. In particular e₅ is the usual invisible-flow dimension, and
    T(H−I) = 35 · 2^(10−c+2e₂) · 5^(2e₅) · Π_i k_i.
Thus the seven-primary torsion is exactly Z/7, there is no prime divisor above seven, and the five-primary torsion is elementary of rank 1+2e₅+#{i:5|k_i}. The last assertion uses the [outer five-rank](saturated-outer-five-rank.md) to check that the valuation equals the number of nonzero Smith factors divisible by five: the rational rank of H−I is 54−c. The formula permits unknown binary and five-ary invisible dimensions; it does not determine them.

**Real eigenvalue product.** The kernel of B on the real wing space consists precisely of the c−1 weighted-zero component means and the two physical-wing contrasts of total zero. The means have D eigenvalue 3 and map to zero by [component balance](triangle-complement.md); the contrasts have D eigenvalue 0 and map to zero since every far word takes two letters in every physical wing. In their orthogonal complement and perpendicular to constants, L and J vanish and BᵗB=(3I−D)(4I+D) is positive definite, as a real cubic adjacency has spectrum in [−3,3] and all component-constant directions have been removed. The remaining global constant maps to a nonzero constant. Therefore dim col(B)=35−c. In that column space, the far constant contributes H−I=7, and the other eigenvalues are minus those of D+2I after removing all c component means (eigenvalue 5) and the two physical contrasts (eigenvalue 2). Its kernel at H−I is exactly B ker(D+2I).

In the 25+c dimensional complement ker(Bᵗ), a far vector extended by zero on the near sites is a true graph eigenvector whenever it is an H eigenvector. The constant is orthogonal to this complement; the block equation shows the only possible eigenvalues are +3 and −4. The space of global minus-four vectors zero on the near 39 sites has dimension 7+c, by the real near mark rank 37−c, and is exactly the −4 part under this extension. Thus the +3 multiplicity there is 18. Writing pdet for the absolute product of nonzero eigenvalues, we obtain
    pdet(H−I) = 7·2^18·5^(7+c) · pdet(D+2I)/(5^c·2²).
The multiplicity of the eigenvalue +1 of H is d.

**Integral saturation.** On ker(D+2I) the exact integral identity BᵗBh=10h holds. The image BΛ sits in Λ_H=ker_Z(H−I) with finite index I₀. Its quotient exponent divides ten, since Bᵗ y=10h when y=Bh is integral. Thus I₀=2^(e₂)5^(e₅): the p-part of Λ_H/BΛ is elementary, with rank exactly the number of linearly independent reductions of Λ sent to zero by B. The ordinary squared covolumes satisfy det(Λ_H)=10^d det(Λ)/I₀². For any symmetric integer matrix M the identity pdet(M)=T(M) det(ker_Z M) follows by restricting M to the orthogonal complement of its primitive kernel: the image lattice has index T(M) in its saturation, the perpendicular lattice has the same determinant as the primitive kernel in standard unimodular space, and the projection of Z^n to that perpendicular space is the perpendicular lattice's dual. Combining these identities gives the arbitrary-triangle formula.

**Saturated simplification.** The wing D is the line graph of the barycentric subdivision of K. The (unsigned) incidence matrix of the bipartite subdivision has edge Gram D+2I; sign changes on one vertex partition turn its vertex Gram into the ordinary Laplacian. Their nonzero eigenproducts coincide. Matrix-tree therefore gives pdet(D+2I)=Π_i(5 k_i ·2^(k_i+1) τ(K_i)): the subdivided component has 5k_i vertices, and every original spanning tree contributes 2 choices on the halves of each nontree edge (k_i+1 such edges). The kernel lattice consists of signed flows, and its squared covolume is 2^d Π_i τ(K_i), because every edge appears on two darts and the usual integral flow lattice has determinant τ (by Cauchy–Binet on a fundamental cycle basis, whose complementary spanning-tree minors have absolute determinant 1). Taking the ratio gives T(D+2I)=5^c Π_i k_i, and substitution yields the claimed saturated expression.
