# The universal rooted lattice before dyadic saturation

Assume only the SRG parameters (99,14,1,2). Let o be any vertex. Its 14 neighbors Y are seven mate pairs with permutation matrix M; its 84 distance-two vertices X correspond bijectively to unordered nonmate pairs in Y. Let D have those pair indicators as columns. The [rooted rank-32 construction](rooted-rank32.md) gives an integral even positive rank-30 mark lattice L with Gram
```
Q=24I−8A_X+2J−3D^tD−D^t M D,  Q²=56Q,  Q_bb=20.
```
Choose an original maximal isotropic extension H at seven, extended over the unimodular Y-star summand. Let F denote the resulting lattice with **no modification at two**; also use F_j for its number of vectors of squared norm 2j. If x belongs to F, its evaluation y on the 84 residual marks, extended by zero on Y and o, is an integral neutral minus-four eigenfield, Dy=0, with y²=56x². The isotropic extension at seven does not change what its evaluations can be modulo eight.

## Dyadic elementary divisors

Since Q²=56Q, the nonzero Smith factors have 2-adic valuations at most three (56 annihilates the discriminant). Modulo eight, Q equals a fixed matrix independent of all unknown edges. Unit-pivot elimination successively modulo 8,4,2 yields cumulative ranks 6,7,8 (the provided exact Python verifier). Consequently the 30 dyadic factors have valuations 0 six times, 1 once, 2 once and 3 twenty-two times. Equivalently the fixed mod-eight form is the pullback on D Z^84={t in Z^14: sum t even} of (sum t)(sum u)/2−3t·u−t^t M u. The displayed image lattice uses pair differences obtained by choosing a common third-axis point, and one column of sum two.

Thus det F=2^69·49 after the seven-plane extension, and det(sqrt(8) F^{*2})=2^21·49 for the partial dual at two. The ordinary level divides 56: the graph evaluations of F* are integral neutral, hence their squared norms are even and equal to 56x². In particular theta(sqrt(56) F*) counts exactly the H-eligible ordinary neutral fields vanishing on the closed root-star; theta(sqrt(8) F^{*2}) is its subseries after filtering exponents divisible by seven and rescaling by seven, since the remaining 7-discriminant plane is anisotropic. Do not apply the dyadic valuation of det F to a lattice already saturated at two.

## A complete tiny low-cusp reduction

At two F is generated by the frame marks. Write t=Dc and λ=(sum t)/2 for integral mark coefficients c. In evaluation coordinates, for the site indexed by the nonmate pair ij,
```
y_ij ≡ l_i+l_j mod 8,  l_i=λ−3t_i−t_mate(i).
```
These are necessary even after choosing H because 7 is a unit mod 8. In fact they are sufficient locally: if an integral zero-star field matches Qc mod 8 then y−Qc=8b, Qb=56b, so y−Qc=Q(b/7) over the dyadic integers. At any other prime except seven the lattice is unimodular. 

The verifier classifies these residue shapes without assuming graph symmetry. For each of the eight λ classes, the possible ordered inputs on a mate pair can be reduced mod 16; record its unordered l-pair and s=t_i+t_mate mod 16. Choose seven unordered l-pairs and test sum s=2λ mod16 by seven steps. Quotient by formal permutations of the axes and points in each axis, negation, and adding 4 to every l. The last change keeps every y_ij unchanged; it is the entire kernel of the pair-sum map because any two distinct vertices can be compared using a common third axis. The 230 resulting orbits account for exactly 2^21 different evaluation residues, matching the independent Smith count. Using the formal permutations only classifies residue shapes, not graphs, actual fields, or automorphism orbits.

For an orbit, bound y² from below as follows. At each i minimize the sum of twelve squares with the indicated residues and row sum zero, add the 14 minima, and divide by two. For each row begin at nearest residues and move by signed steps of eight until its sum vanishes, using the cheapest available increment each time. This is the exact one-row minimum by separable convexity. Only six shapes have that bound at most 336:
```
(00)^7: 0;              (00)^6(26): 192;
(00)^5(13)(57): 320;    (00)^5(26)^2: 320;
(02)^4(77)^3: 336;      (15)^7: 336.
```
An axis xy denotes the residue pair x,y, not a wing label in the actual graph. For the second, third and fourth shapes, exact lower bounds are respectively 288,432,448: the verifier supplies small integer multipliers a_i and adds the minima of y_ij²−(a_i+a_j)y_ij, using Dy=0. In the second shape the norm is congruent to 96 modulo 32, excluding 336 as well. For the zero signature y² is divisible by 64. Therefore **F1=F2=0** at every root, every remaining Seidel rank and every H. Only the last two shapes survive at F3.

## The norm-six primal shell

For (15)^7 all 84 entries lie in residues 2 or 6. Norm 336 forces the nearest lifts throughout. Equivalently y_ij=±2σ_i σ_j with σ=±1 and opposite signs on mate pairs. Call these wing checkerboards. The simultaneous global reversal of all seven sign choices gives the same product. Distinct choices modulo that reversal cannot give antipodal products: no assignment τ on seven axes can have τ_aτ_b=−1 for every pair of different axes. Thus there are initially at most 64 checkerboards modulo sign, before the actual adjacency eigen-equations.

For (02)^4(77)^3 use integers l=(0,2)^4,(−1,−1)^3. Since the norm equals the rowwise lower bound, each row must be a one-row minimizer. Equivalently use multipliers zero on the first eight points and 2 on the other six: this gives lower bound 336 (also directly by taking the indicated minima). Equality forces the unique nearest entry except for two kinds: (2,2) edges have ±4, and (−1,−1) edges have −2 or +6. On each of the four points l=2, the three other 2-points must all give −4 to offset the sum 12 of its other incident entries. On each of the six late points, exactly one of its four allowed late neighbors must give +6. Thus the +6 sites form a perfect matching of that six-point three-axis graph. Every other value is l_i+l_j.

Set l_o=−2 and d_i=(3I−A)e_i in the full 99 coordinates. Given this signed representative, the ordinary graph field
```
b=(y+sum_(i in Y) l_i d_i−2d_o)/8
```
is integral and neutral: its charge is (sum l_i−2)/8=0. It has −1 at o and at the six pairs of the four l=2 points, +1 at those four neighboring points and the three matching pairs, and zero elsewhere. Thus it is an actual oriented N14 (a Fano field) through o. At seven it is H-compatible: the original marks have zero seven-class, eight is a unit, and the root-star summand is unimodular there. A fixed signed field b can have at most one such y, since its four neighboring positive sites identify the four oriented (02) axes and the remaining entries fix the matching. The negative representative gives the opposite b. The (02)^4(77)^3 shape, its negative, and their global +4 shifts have distinct unordered label multisets; hence its orientation is unambiguous in this injection. No converse is needed.

In fact there are at most 22 checkerboards modulo sign. Two actual products differing in the choice on a single axis up to global reversal would give, after taking the difference of the two chosen unit products and dividing by two, a nonzero neutral minus-four field supported wholly on the 24 vertices meeting that axis. Those 24 induce maximum degree two: each twelve-site wing has its matching from the root-point neighborhood, while the two wings are joined by a matching since every point of one wing already shares its wing root with the opposite root and needs just one further common neighbor. Such an induced graph has spectral radius at most two, a contradiction. Use the unique even Hamming-weight representative of a seven-bit choice modulo complement and delete the seventh parity bit. Distances five and six between the resulting six-bit words both lift to distance six, equivalent to a forbidden one-axis flip. This is a binary anticode of diameter at most four in dimension six, so [Kleitman's diameter theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9800(66)80027-3) gives at most 1+6+15=22.

Therefore, writing f_o for the number of oriented H-compatible N14 fields nonzero at o,
```
F3(o) ≤ 44 + f_o,                 sum_o F3(o) ≤ 4356 + 14 N14(H).
```
For the common H at all roots, the last population counts each oriented Fano once; it has fourteen nonzero sites. On any no-Fano branch, each root has F3≤44. These a priori cusp constraints do not exclude any complete Seidel rank.
