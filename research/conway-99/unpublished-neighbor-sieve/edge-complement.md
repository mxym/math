# A finite rank-19 complement for all four ranks

Fix an oriented edge of a hypothetical Conway graph. There are eleven possible induced neighborhoods consisting of that edge, its third triangle vertex, and the other neighbors of its two endpoints. For each type the original existence question is equivalent, with that edge type prescribed, to placing 72 labeled vectors in an explicit finite list of positive definite rank-19 lattices with specified lengths and pairings. The candidate lattices are orthogonal complements of one specified rank-five lattice in the 23 rooted Niemeier lattices. Embeddings and the simultaneous placement of the 72 vectors require further search.

## The neighborhood

Write the edge as $pq$ and its third triangle vertex as $x$. The other neighbors of $p$ form a set $P$ of size 12, and those of $q$ a disjoint set $Q$ of the same size. Each wing has a mate matching, and the cross edges give a bijection between wings. This is the two-wing restriction of the [triangle neighborhood](../triangle-rank/README.md). After identifying the two wings along the bijection, the two mate matchings give alternating cycles on twelve labels. Their half-lengths form a partition $\lambda$ of six, with a doubled pair counted as a part of size one. No global symmetry is required.

There are 72 exterior vertices. Twelve of them meet $x$ and touch one vertex of each wing. Sixty miss $x$ and touch a nonmate pair in each wing. Every nonmate pair of a wing occurs once in the latter group, and every single wing vertex occurs once in the former. These statements follow by counting common neighbors with $p,q,x$, and of two sites in a wing. No exterior vertex meets $p$ or $q$.

One can restrict the search for some graph to the **nine partitions with at most three parts of size one**. Each part of size one gives a triangular prism in which $pq$ is a matching edge between the two disjoint triangles. Conversely any prism with $pq$ as such an edge gives one part of size one. There are 693 edges and each prism has three matching edges. The [global prism bound](../rank-34-short-support/README.md) gives at most 851 prisms, and $3\cdot851<4\cdot693$. A graph therefore has some edge with at most three parts of size one. This observation does not rule out either omitted type at another edge of that graph.

## The 25-dimensional span and its 19-dimensional complement

Use the [determinant-49 lattice](../maximal-marked-lattice/README.md) $U$ and the centered marks with Gram matrix $3I-A+J/9$. The span $W$ of the 27 known marks has dimension 25. Its integral sublattice $R$ is generated by $3u_p$ and $u_i-u_p$ for the other shell sites. Write $s=3u_p$. At $p,q$ the vector equations $4u_v+\sum_{j\sim v}u_j=0$ express one difference from each wing as an integer combination of the remaining generators (the total mark coefficient in each equation is 18). Let the basis consist of $s$ and the 24 remaining differences. Its Gram matrix is
$$G_{00}=28,\qquad G_{0i}=-9-3A_{pi},\qquad
G_{ij}=3(1+\delta_{ij})-A_{ij}+A_{pi}+A_{pj}.$$
The checker verifies positivity and reconstruction of the full 27-site Gram in each case.

Let $M=U\cap W^\perp$ and write $v_y=u_y-u_p=a_y+h_y$ for each exterior vertex, with $a_y\in W$, $h_y\in W^\perp$. Then $a_y\in R^*$, $h_y\in M^*$. If $t_i$ records the neighbors of $y$ in the shell, its column of pairings with the displayed basis is
$$b_{y,0}=-9,\qquad b_{y,i}=3+A_{pi}-t_i.$$
Thus $a_y$ has basis coordinates $G^{-1}b_y$. For $y\ne z$ choose an edge indicator $\epsilon_{yz}\in\{0,1\}$. The residual requirements are
$$
h_y^2=6-b_y^tG^{-1}b_y,\qquad
(h_y,h_z)=3-\epsilon_{yz}-b_y^tG^{-1}b_z .
$$
When a contact pattern is selected, require its two omitted shell pairings to match the specified contacts as well, using the reconstructed shell marks. As another necessary check, the tight frame projects to $\sum_y h_yh_y^t=7I_{19}$, so $\sum_y h_y^2=133$. Each $h_y$ is the orthogonal projection of $u_y$, giving $h_y^2\le28/9$.

Let $\ell$ be the level of $R$, the smallest positive integer making $\ell G^{-1}$ integral with even diagonal. For the scale $d$ in the table, $\ell\mid d^2$. All vectors $d h_y$ pair integrally with $M$. They have integral pairings with each other and even norms because $v_y\in U$, $a_y\in R^*$ and
$$(d h_y,d h_z)=d^2\big((v_y,v_z)-(a_y,a_z)\big).$$
They can therefore be adjoined to $M$ simultaneously. Take a maximal even extension $M'$ containing the resulting lattice. It has rank 19.

For an even lattice $L$ write $D(L)=L^*/L$ with quadratic form $v^2/2$ modulo one. An even extension along an isotropic subgroup $H$ replaces $D(L)$ by $H^\perp/H$. For a nonsingular finite quadratic group $F$, let $a(F)=|F|/h^2$ with $h$ the largest order of an isotropic subgroup. An isotropic reduction preserves $a$. Indeed, if $I$ has largest order and $T$ is any isotropic subgroup, the subgroup $T+(I\cap T^\perp)$ is isotropic and has order at least $|I|$. This follows since the pairing map from $I$ to the characters of $T$ annihilates $I\cap T$ on the $T$ side, so its image has order at most $|T|/|I\cap T|$. Thus a maximum subgroup containing $T$ exists, which proves the assertion in the quotient. In particular an inclusion-maximal isotropic subgroup has maximum order.

Put $J_0=B(2,1,4)\perp B(2,1,4)$. Its discriminant form is the anisotropic quadratic plane over the field of seven elements and agrees with $D(U)$. The full-rank inclusion $R\oplus M\subseteq U$ gives an isotropic reduction. Apply this reduction after adding $-D(R)$, or first remove $D(R)\oplus-D(R)$ using the diagonal. This gives $a(D(M))=a(D(J_0)\oplus-D(R))$. The same identity holds with $D(K)$ appended to both sides.

The [exact checker](edge_complement.py) verifies three assertions in each row. The maximal isotropic reduction of $D(J_0)\oplus-D(R)$ has order $\det K$. The group $D(K)$ has no nonzero isotropic element. The further sum of the reduced group with $D(K)$ admits a reduction to the trivial group. To perform each elementary reduction at $p$, a nonzero vector $v\bmod p$ must satisfy $Gv\equiv0\bmod p$ and $v^tGv\equiv0\bmod 2p^2$ in the current integral Gram. The script normalizes one coordinate to one, replaces that basis vector by $v/p$, and checks the index and integrality. It accepts a negative line test only after covering all projective lines in the kernel. An isotropic subgroup, if nontrivial, contains a line of prime order. A later extension at another prime preserves the primary part already tested.

It follows that $D(M')$ and $D(K)$ are both anisotropic of the same order and that $D(M')\oplus D(K)$ has an isotropic subgroup of that order. Both projections are bijective, giving an anti-isometry. Gluing its graph produces a positive even unimodular lattice in dimension 24 with $M'$ and $K$ primitive orthogonal complements. Every $K$ in the table has a root. The ambient lattice therefore belongs to the 23 rooted types in the [Niemeier tables](https://gaetan.chenevier.perso.math.cnrs.fr/niemeier/niemeier.html). In the $321$ row an $A_4$ root subsystem is required, excluding $A_1^{24},A_2^{12},A_3^8,D_4^6$.

## Reconstruction and the remaining search

Take each primitive embedding of the specified $K$ in a rooted Niemeier lattice. In its orthogonal complement seek the 72 vectors $d h_y$ with the contact patterns and Gram values above (multiply residual Gram entries by $d^2$). Each search is finite because all prescribed embedding generators and all sought vectors have bounded norm in positive definite lattices. Residual vectors with different labels are allowed to coincide whenever their prescribed Gram permits it. For reconstruction, use $u_p+a_y+h_y$ for each new mark, in $W\oplus (K^\perp\otimes\mathbb R)$. The conditions give 99 marks of norm $28/9$ and off-diagonal products $1/9$ or $-8/9$. Multiplication by three and the [tight-frame equality](../maximal-marked-lattice/README.md) recover all strongly regular graph parameters. This proves sufficiency without any extra assumed symmetry or compatibility of discriminant gluings on the reverse direction.

The table enumerates Gram and discriminant data, not solutions of the simultaneous placement problem. A positive result needs a verified placement of 72 vertices in one target. Exclusion via the edge search needs a certificate covering every admissible placement in the nine sufficient partition types.
