# Three-edge wing cuts emit short original charged fields

At any graph triangle T consider the cubic 36-site wing D, its within-wing mate matchings and its three between-wing perfect matchings. A bond here is a cut with connected shores in one component; unrelated whole components may be omitted. The conclusions also hold for general shores, but any counting of distinct cuts below refers to actual oriented boundary edges, not to adding/removing whole components. Use G=3I−A+J/9, d_i=(3I−A)e_i and let the centered mark of a literal triangle R be w_R=G1_R. Its graph-coordinate raw charge-three field is w_R−1/3·1=(3I−A)1_R, with its three positive sites exactly the triangle R. There are 231 different ordinary triangle fields of this type. In general Ga with integral a is an original mark, its charge being Σa modulo nine; its raw field of charge j is Ga−j/9·1 and satisfies Ab=−4b−2j·1 and Σb=−11j.

**Balanced bonds.** If a shore U meets each physical wing k times, use a=1_U+(k/2)1_T. As in [the thin-cut lemma](arbitrary-wing-thin-cuts.md), f=Ga is zero on T, equals (3I−D)1_U on W, equals k/2−|N(x)∩U| on a far site x, and f²=7|δ_D U|. For a three-edge cut, k is odd and each of the three within-wing matchings has exactly one crossing edge; no cross-wing edge is cut. Consequently f on W consists of three positive and three negative unit darts, one of each in every physical wing. Its far coordinates are odd halves. Their total squared norm is 21−6=15, exactly the smallest possible for 60 such coordinates, so all 60 have magnitude one half. Their sum is zero and each sign occurs thirty times.

Choose the integral coefficient vector a'=1_U+((k−1)/2)1_T, whose sum is 3k+3(k−1)/2≡3 mod9. Then
    b=Ga'−(1/3)1 = f−w_T/2−(1/3)1
is a true original raw charge-three field of norm 39: all three roots have value −1, the wing values are the six signed boundary darts, and X has thirty −1s, thirty zeros. Thus its centered norm is four. Choosing the complementary shore changes f to −f and gives a second field. Indeed for full W, k=12 and G(1_W+6·1_T)=0 by the same zero-boundary norm identity. Its three positive sites recover the inside boundary darts individually; their three physical mates recover the oriented cut. So all distinct oriented cuts of this kind contribute distinct original fields at this fixed T.

Contract all cross-wing cycles (length 3l), weighted by l; this can be done at an arbitrary triangle, not just a saturated one. The balanced bonds above are exactly the size-three bonds in this contraction. Their three positive darts form a triangle only if they constitute a literal transversal triangle (they are in different physical wings). In that event all three outgoing mate edges of this transversal triangle are used by the boundary. If the negative darts likewise form a triangle, then the six boundary sites make a whole prism component. Thus on a face where the original charge-three norm-39 fields are all the ordinary triangle marks (in particular, when the original norm-four coefficient y₂=462), **every non-prism component of the transversal contraction has no cut of size three**. It also has no bridge or two-cut by the thin-cut lemma. A contracted component with only one vertex has no nontrivial cut, regardless of any loops.

When T is saturated, the contracted components are cubic in ordinary unweighted tails. For any *nontrivial* size-three bond (neither shore is a singleton), the crossing edges must form a matching on each side: if a tail has two crossing edges, deleting it from its side gives a two-edge cut; if it has three, it forms an entire isolated shore. The three positive darts therefore are independent, not just nontriangular, and the same is true of their three negatives. If there are C nontrivial unordered three-edge bonds, this supplies 2C original, independent-positive-triple G(3,39) fields, beyond all ordinary triangles, coclique shifts with a positive double, and [charge-one negative-edge switches](shared-norm-18/coclique-sidon-cut.md) with a positive edge. No multiplication by the number of triangle roots is asserted without a collision argument.

**Unbalanced bonds.** If the three shore counts are unequal, the two-edge magnitude debt 2(max k_i−min k_i) and mate parity force two equal even counts and one exceptional odd count differing by one. Orient the cut so the exceptional count is k+1 and the other two are k, even. There is a single within-wing mate crossing in that exceptional wing, and one crossing towards each of the other wings, both from a selected exceptional-wing site to an unselected site of the other wing. For a=1_U+(k/2)1_T the sum is 9k/2+1. Then b=Ga−1/9·1 is a genuine original charge-one field: −1 at the exceptional root, zero at the other roots, (3I−D)1_U on W and k/2−|N(x)∩U| in X. Its total centered squared norm is 7(3+1/9) by a·Ga=3−k/2+(9k/2+1)/9, so b²=23. The three units of outgoing positive wing mass all lie in the exceptional wing; repeats are allowed. The three incoming negative wing units lie one in each wing. If b is an ordinary d_i then its unique +3 must be at a wing site carrying all three boundary edges; in a bond this is a singleton inside shore. A nontrivial unbalanced bond therefore produces a nonordinary original (1,23) field. This does not assume it is a unit field.
