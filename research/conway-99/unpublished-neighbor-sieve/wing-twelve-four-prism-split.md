# A four-prism 12-component splitter cannot occur at rank 36

Fix a graph vertex o and a triangle through it with wing `W` of four prism components and one connected 12-site component D. Denote the four axes of the prism wing pairs at o by 0,1,2,3. The four sites in D on the o-wing are two mate pairs, called B and C. Every other D site has a unique neighbour in that wing; call its strip B or C accordingly (four sites per strip). Suppose cells X=01, Y=02, Z=12 are all clean. At mod-seven Seidel rank 36 it is **impossible** that every site of X and Y has one contact into each of the two strips. We impose **no split on Z**. No other assumption about global symmetry, large wings, or exterior adjacencies is involved.

**Local diagram.** Use [triangle-complement.md](triangle-complement.md) and [twelve-sites.md](twelve-sites.md). The sixty far sites of this triangle have one neighbour in each prism, two in D, and exactly two in each wing. Mark a prism neighbour as 2w+s (wing w, line bit s), with wing 0 at o, numbering the four prisms by 0,1,2,3. In a square on two of the named axes the own two prism slots give rows (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1); in either remaining prism the column is a permutation of 2,3,4,5. The latter follows from the two one-neighbour-outside-grid rules, as in [the six-prism case](saturated-prism-clean-square.md). In D contacts are confined to its nonzero wings, since both zero-wing positions are occupied by the named parents. Conversely each of the eight D sites there sees the square exactly once: it is outside its grid and sees none of its signed parents (different prism components) or o. Thus the four contacts in D are an ordered perfect matching of the eight sites into pairs with strictly positive true quota
$$
m_{ij}=2-A^W_{ij}-((A^W)^2)_{ij}-[i,j\text{ in the same wing}]\quad(i\ne j).
$$
For X,Y only, each matching edge straddles the two strips. All rowwise wing-balanced assignments of the two remaining prism permutations and these D matching edges are enumerated. Sites of different squares are anticomplete because the shared axis already provides the other grid's unique neighbour. Within a square the adjacency flips one of the two signs.

**Coverage and quotas.** The connected twelve-component matchings and return map are regenerated as in the prior twelve-site enumeration, verifying its two strict exclusions. For each retained template keep the parent wing distinguished: the only allowed wing relabelings at this stage fix 0 and allow exchange of wings 1 and 2. There are 21 such forms on the 13 unordered survivors. Within each such form enumerate all three square diagrams. For a single square with contact incidence E (four by 36, rows are the six neighbours) and square adjacency A4, require entrywise \(E(I+A^W)+A_4E\le2\), by the common-neighbour upper bound between a square site and a W site. For X only, identify by explicit automorphisms of D preserving wing zero as a set, coupled to the same wing permutation on all four prisms, and by independent choices of the four initial prism bits. Changes of the two named sign conventions permute the four X rows. These operations preserve the asserted split even when they exchange B and C. No naming normalization is made on Y or Z once X is chosen. Numbers of first representatives in the printed template order are
```
180,144,196,204,320,204,176,280,280,156,156,176,168,120,168,264,84,152,84,300,312.
```
Across two rows in different squares require at most two common W sites; if two, the pair must have quota two. Whenever X and Y have already used that same pair twice, no Z row may also hit it. These are literal occupancy requirements before any 7-arithmetic.

**Bordered rank test, including singular charts.** All ranks here are over F7. The W marking Gram is (U=3I-A^W+4J), where (4=1/9) in F7. Split a principal nonsingular block indexed by I (symmetric elimination allowing 2-by-2 pivots); (r=|I|) is 32, 31 or 30. Every candidate contact row e of length 36 has true W pairings (v_e=4·1−e). Compute the leftover W pairings
$$
L_{e,j}=v_{e,j}-v_{e,I}U_{II}^{-1}U_{I,j}\quad(j\notin I).
$$
After this elimination the leftover W-by-W block is zero. Retain a basis of the columns of L across the whole candidate-word dictionary; the checker verifies its dimension (d=32-r) on *each* template. This reduction is exact even if some remaining wing marks are radical but do not vanish in the ambient graph space: their genuine pairings (L_{e,j}) are retained, not set equal to zero. For chosen actual far sites, with graph adjacency A, the residual graph-mark matrix after the nondegenerate principal part has the form
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}0_{d\times d}&L^t\\L&Q\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q_{ef}=3[e=f]-A_{ef}+4-v_{e,I}U_{II}^{-1}v_{f,I}^t.
$$
Every principal such matrix has rank at most (36-r=4+d). The checker uses three rows per square for rank, all four for graph quotas. Using only a principal submatrix is sufficient, independently of any square dependence.

For every first pair of squares, reject if the corresponding ((d+6))-row base has rank above (k=4+d). If its rank is (h=k) or (k-1), an additional row with prescribed base pairings w must have w in its column space (else the bordered rank rises by two); for (h=k) its diagonal is also prescribed by a nonsingular principal block inverse. At smaller h there is **no** single-row rank rejection. All surviving third diagrams go through the full bordered ((d+9))-row rank check. Every inspected base rank is covered explicitly by some nonsingular principal minor; the script asserts coverage. Result: zero across every one of 21 templates, including template 6 at d=2 and templates 9,17 at d=1. The companion standalone Python/NumPy checker takes about 1.5 minutes on one core and uses bounded integer arithmetic only. The independent replay reproduced all 21 representative counts and zero completions in about 91 seconds. Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 wing_twelve_four_prism_split.py`. It does not assign any remaining far row or construct the graph.

**The twelve-grid application.** In the surviving all-peripheral four-cubic subdivision at a 12-grid root with no wing component of size at least 24, name the cubic axes a,b,c,d, with direct spokes ab,ac,ad and dirty paths b-x-c,c-y-d,d-z-b. By [the split-axis defect equations](big-triangle-wing.md), orient the three path fields p,q,r so their ends are p,−p; q,−q; r,−r in that order. The three spoke columns are r−p,p−q,q−r, respectively. Along the x-triangle, B,C are exactly the two dirty cells bx,cx on the two strips of its 12-component; other axes a,y,z,d give its four prisms. At any site of the clean cells ay or az all three spoke defects vanish (their cells share a with this clean cell), so p=q=r there. On ay the y-path q vanishes, and on az the z-path r vanishes, again by the shared-axis clean rule. Thus p=0 on these two squares. The disjoint-axis defect formula there reads (p=d_{bx}-1) and ( -p=d_{cx}-1), where these degrees count neighbours in the four respective sites. Both are exactly one. The third cell yz is clean with no asserted strip split. Applying the theorem with 0=a,1=y,2=z excludes this all-peripheral layout at rank 36. Consequently the earlier four-cubic 12-grid/no-large-component reduction cannot occur at rank 36. This note alone does not exclude the distinct six-cubic case or all 12-grid vertices.

For six cubic groups at a rank36 12-grid root with no large component there are nine dirty edges and no degree-two group. Repeated original axes would leave at least two saturated axes, impossible by [the saturated-axis triangle theorem and its 11-grid corollary](saturated-prism-clean-triangle.md). Hence six distinct axes carry the six cubic degrees, and the remaining one is saturated. The dirty simple cubic graph on six has complement 2-regular, hence either two triangles (forbidden off that saturated axis) or a 6-cycle. Only the latter, equivalently dirty triangular prism, remains in this six-cubic case; its existence is not asserted.
