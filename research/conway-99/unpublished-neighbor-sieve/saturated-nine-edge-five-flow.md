# Nine-edge visibility via a mixed-pair quota

Strengthen the [eight-edge theorem](saturated-eight-edge-five-flow.md): a nonzero invisible five-current at any saturated triangle has at least **ten** nonzero contraction edges. No graph rank restriction. We use its notation for the signed representatives h, multiplicities n₁,n₂, and 60-row field z=Bh/5. Its total net aligned j-incidences, weighted by |z|, is 4jn_j.

Suppose support nine. Since n₁≡n₂ mod5, rescale to n₁=7,n₂=2. Now |Bh|≤8 (a word has just six darts, with at most two double ones), and ‖z‖²=12: there are twelve unit extrema. The net aligned 2-dart count is sixteen. With k the number of 2-darts aligned to each extremum, Σ C(k,2)≥Σ(k−1)≥4. But the one pair per sign occurs in at most two far rows, giving the reverse bound. Equality forces k=1 or 2 on every extremum, no opposed 2-dart there, and both like-signed pairs occur twice in the far graph, with no near common neighbor or adjacency. Equivalently the pair-budget formula of the eight-edge theorem gives two distinct physical colors and disjoint endpoints for the two magnitude-2 edges. At all four endpoints modular conservation forces outgoing units of signs opposite to the local 2-dart. All remaining active tails are bivalent with two unit edges of opposite signs. Thus the unit edges form a 2-regular multigraph on seven tails, including the four trivalent tails. Denote by C the physical color missing from the two magnitude-2 edges; at most three unit edges have that color by proper coloring on seven tails.

The number of far occurrences of *same-sign mixed* pairs (one unit, one double) is
  4·7·2 − 2(7−n₁,C) − 16 ≤32.
The root cost subtracts one per like-signed dart pair with the same color. At the four trivalent tails each of the two unit edges shares that tail with the double edge. The two local signs differ, so each oriented mixed pair of the same sign has one near common neighbor (the intervening reverse); total 2 per edge pair, including both endpoints if parallel. No other near costs occur. Alternatively using the result as an upper bound suffices.

There are four extrema taking both aligned double darts and eight taking one; they need respective *net* aligned unit counts one and three to reach five. They use at least 4·2·1+8·1·3=32 mixed-pair slots. Thus equality holds: the extrema have exactly (2 doubles,1 unit) or (1 double,3 units), all aligned. In particular no zero row contains any like-signed mixed pair. A zero row taking two doubles must have opposite signs (all like-signed double pairs were exhausted by extrema); it therefore has no units. There are exactly four such rows: the two opposite-sign, *different-edge* double pairings have two far witnesses apiece because their physical colors and endpoint sets are disjoint. A zero row taking one double must take exactly two opposing units. Out of 40 double-dart incidences, extrema use 16 and four double-zero rows use eight; thus 16 zero rows take one double. The other 28 zero rows have r positive and r negative units each. There are 140 unit-dart incidences; extrema used 28 and single-double zero rows use 32, leaving Σr=40 across 28 rows.

Count like-signed unit pairs in all far rows. There are at least 8·C(3,2)+16+2(40−28)=64, using twice C(r,2)≥2(r−1) on the 28 balanced rows. But the exact like-signed unit-pair budget is 4·C(7,2)−2·7−2Σ_w binom(n_w,2)≤60, with n_w the number of unit edges of color w. The seven degree-two unit tails cost two each, independently of relative signs, and the color-pair sum is at least 5 because the seven unit edges are split among three colors. This contradiction eliminates arbitrary support nine, not just simple cycles. Ten and larger remain open.
