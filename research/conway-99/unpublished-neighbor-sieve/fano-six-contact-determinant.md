# The local six-contact determinant at an actual Fano

**Theorem.** For any signed norm-14 Fano configuration in a rank-36 Conway graph, consider any one of its fourteen signed sites. The six noncap contacts defined below have just nine candidate additional edges in their graph of shared zero-site neighbors. Of 466 six-site masks extendible to the requisite abstract cubic ten-site graph, at most 281 survive the ordinary mod-seven site-mark condition. If the Fano lies in the **original marked lattice at seven** (in particular if it is emitted by any triangle-wing two-cut), only **32** masks survive, at **each** of its fourteen signed sites. The number of edges of the surviving six-site graph is 4,5,6 or 9, with respectively 12,18,1,1 labeled masks. This is a necessary local condition, not a Fano or rank exclusion. In the original case all 21 duplicate-contact difference directions described below belong to a common nonsquare 3-dimensional quadratic space over F7.

## The simple cubic graph and its nine bits

Write P,N for the two seven-site Fano sign shores and Z for the fifteen anticomplete sites. Use the [Fano endpoint and contact counts](../four-root-exclusion/README.md): each of the other 70 contact sites meets one p, one n, and three sites of Z; Z is independent. There is one such contact for each adjacent sign pair (its edge cap) and two for each nonadjacent sign pair. Fix p in P, reversing the Fano sign if necessary. Each z in Z has exactly two common neighbors with p, both among its ten contact neighbors Yp. Join the two vertices of Yp for each z. This graph Q is simple: a repeated pair would share two z in addition to p. It is cubic since each contact sees three Z.

Among Yp, four caps are paired in the actual graph neighborhood of p to their respective signed N endpoints. The six remaining actual neighbors form a perfect matching; they come in three duplicate-label pairs, one pair of contacts to each of the three nonneighbors n of p. Neither an actual mate pair nor a duplicate-label pair can meet in Q: in the former case they already share p as their edge cap; in the latter they already share p and n. A duplicate pair is not an actual mate. Hence the duplicate and mate matchings together form an alternating 6-cycle. Label the six vertices 0,…,5 with duplicate pairs 01,23,45 and actual mates 12,34,50. Their induced graph H in Q is any subgraph of the complement of this 6-cycle, a nine-edge prism. A mask can be discarded if its degree deficits do not permit a simple cubic completion by four cap vertices. There are 466 extendible masks among 512.

## One common residue space

Work in the nonsquare nondegenerate mod-seven quotient of the minus-four site-mark Gram 3I−A+4J at rank 36, as in the [triangle tree test](triangle-wing-tree-rank36.md). Within the 29 sites E=P∪N∪Z the Gram has rank 22 and nonsquare nonsingular determinant class: this tiny fixed computation is in the [checker](fano_six_contact_determinant.py). Their actual span has dimension 27. Indeed, a relation in the full quotient has constant coefficients on Z by its pairings to Z. Subtract a multiple of a Hoffman coclique indicator 1_(Z∪P), a relation since every exterior site sees four of its points. With coefficients zero on Z, the Z equations give total coefficient sum zero. Every signed pair has a contact, giving a_p+a_n=0 for all p,n. These are just multiples of the Fano signed difference, itself a relation by the minus-four equation. Thus there are exactly two independent relations.

Choose a nondegenerate 22-dimensional summand E0 of the E-span. Its orthogonal complement in that span is a five-dimensional totally isotropic radical R. Each actual Yp sees all seven negative labels; collectively its pairings separate R. To check this, represent an R vector on signed sites only as above. Its signed coefficients sum to zero. If it pairs to zero with all Yp then all a_n=−a_p. Orthogonality to every positive site implies 3a_i=4a_n, so all positive coefficients agree and the vector is the zero Fano relation. Thus the 10 Yp provide five partners for R, forming, after choosing E0-orthogonal representatives, a nonsingular ten-space of determinant (−1)^5 times a square. Removing E0 and these five hyperbolic planes leaves a nonsquare four-space W; intrinsically W=(R perpendicular in E0 perpendicular)/R. This quotient is common to all fourteen sites.

Let L be the 6-by-3 matrix with columns e0−e1,e2−e3,e4−e5, and A_H the ordinary adjacency matrix of H. In the field F7 define
```
M_11 = 9 I_3 + 3 J_3 − Lᵀ A_H L,
M_14 = Lᵀ A_H 1,
M_44 = −2 |E(H)|.
```
The symmetric 4-by-4 matrix M is three times the Gram on W of the three corrected duplicate differences and one corrected cap sum. Here is an explicit verification independent of which completion Q was chosen. Write u_i for a site mark. For a duplicate difference Δ on Yp, its Z evaluations are −d_z, where d_z is the sum of its signed coefficients at the two endpoints of edge z in Q. Its signed-site evaluations vanish. Since Σ d_z=0, correct it by adding (1/3)Σ d_z u_z to make it orthogonal to E. For duplicates Δ,Δ′ the corrected pairing is the Yp pairing minus (1/3)Σ d_z d′_z. The unsigned incidence Gram of Q is 3I+A_Q. On the three oriented differences the actual mate matching has off-diagonal contributions −1 and zero diagonals. This gives M_11.

For the fourth vector start with v0=Σ_caps u_y−u_p−3Σ_Z u_z. It pairs to zero with the signed sites and to 12−e_z with z, where e_z is the number of cap endpoints of the Q-edge z. The 15 Z evaluations sum to 168=0 mod 7, so subtract (1/3)Σ_z(12−e_z)u_z. The cross entry of three times the corrected Gram is −Σ_z e_z d_z, the negative cap-degree difference in Q. Cubicity identifies this with the H degree difference, giving M_14. The norm of v0 is 3 mod 7. With m cap-to-cap edges in Q, Σ e_z=12 and Σ e_z²=12+2m. Thus three times the corrected norm is 9−Σ(12−e_z)²=1−2m mod 7. Since m=|E(H)|−3 by cubical degree sums, this gives M_44. The three differences, the cap sum and the all-neighbor sum are five linearly independent coefficient combinations on Yp annihilating R. The last is already in E by the eigen-equation at p. Thus these four residual vectors account for its entire remaining Gram, including singularities.

## The original Fano supplies one more direction

If v=f/√7 is an **original** seven-local mark, its true norm is 2 and its pairings are +1 on P, −1 on N, zero elsewhere. The correction of v off E0 is orthogonal to R; it has norm 1 in W. This involves only the fixed 29-site Gram T: for any nonsingular principal basis I and g=(1_P,−1_N,0_Z), the checker verifies g_Iᵀ T_II⁻¹ T_I,*=gᵀ and 2−g_Iᵀ T_II⁻¹g_I=1. Its pairing with each corrected duplicate is zero and with the corrected cap sum is −1 (only the −u_p term contributes). Subtracting this one direction therefore replaces M by M′=M−3 e4 e4ᵀ, in a true nonsquare **three-space**. For rank-three M′ its nonsingular determinant must be square: the ambient ternary form is nonsquare and the factor 3 on all three coordinates is also nonsquare. Rank four is impossible. In fact the 466 masks give ranks four (422) or three (44); of the latter only 32 have the correct determinant. Repeating at every signed site uses the same W and the same original-Fano orthogonal complement. In particular the corrected duplicate attached to a nonedge p,n can be oriented once for both of its endpoints; its correction depends only on its two contacts and their Z incidence.

For an arbitrary Fano without the additional original mark, the four-space W still rules out the 185 masks of full rank with square determinant (scaling by 3 preserves the four-dimensional determinant class). The 217 full-rank nonsquare masks and 64 corank-one masks remain by this test.

The direct integer verifier takes about two seconds with Python and NumPy. For each of the 466 masks it also constructs an actual *abstract cubic completion only*, reproduces the full 39- and 40-row local Gram ranks, and checks the determinant type against the formulas. The analytic calculation proves the test independent of the unspecified cap completion; the checker does not assert those completions extend to SRGs.
