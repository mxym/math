# A two-edge triangle-wing cut cannot isolate six sites

In a hypothetical SRG(99,14,1,2), every connected triangle-wing component of size 6, 12 or 18 is three-edge-connected. More generally both shores of a two-edge cut inside a connected triangle-wing component have at least twelve sites, so any emitting triangle has a component of size at least 24. The statement is independent of mod-seven rank, symmetry and saturation.

By the [triangle-wing thin-cut theorem](arbitrary-wing-thin-cuts.md), there are no bridges. A two-edge cut has equal even shore sizes in all three physical wings, and its two edges cross in opposite directions within just one inter-wing matching, say between physical wings A,B. Its raw original neutral norm-fourteen field is an actual signed Fano field. In particular its two positive boundary endpoints on one shore have all their common neighbours on the negative Fano side, so cannot have a common graph neighbour with field value zero.

If one shore U consists of six sites, it has exactly two in each physical wing. Its internal graph contains each of the three within-wing mate edges and the two other complete inter-wing matchings. The A–B matching loses precisely the missing edge uv between the positive boundary endpoints (the two actual cut edges leave them in opposite directions). Restore uv as a formal edge within U. This makes a simple cubic graph on six sites, necessarily a prism or K_(3,3): its complement is a 2-regular simple graph on six sites, either a six-cycle or two triangles. The K_(3,3) option is impossible even after removing uv, since the two untouched vertices on either bipartite side still have three common neighbours already within U.

In the restored prism each physical-wing mate edge lies in no triangle. Its endpoints are the only two sites of that physical wing, and their cross matches into either other wing are distinct, so no third site can be adjacent to both. The three mate edges are therefore exactly the three rungs of the restored prism. In particular the restored inter-wing edge uv lies in a triangular face. Its third site w already has both edges to u,v in the actual wing (only uv was restored), and is not a cut endpoint, so its Fano field value is zero. This is the forbidden zero common neighbour of two positive sites, a contradiction.

A two-edge cut can split just one connected component, since each split component needs at least two boundary edges in a graph with no bridges. Its two nontrivial shores in that component are connected: otherwise two connected pieces on one side each contribute at least two boundary edges. The balanced-even argument applies to those actual shores, so their sizes are positive multiples of six; six is impossible on either side. This proves all asserted sizes. At size 12 specifically, the old chart already ruled out the same cut-bearing types; the direct Fano proof also covers the entire 18-size regime without local enumeration.
