# The four-cubic connected core at twelve grids

In the [split-axis chart](big-triangle-wing.md) at a root, suppose there are exactly twelve rooted grids, no split group of size four or more, and exactly four cubic split groups on four different original axes. Then the suppressed dirty core is a **simple \(K_4\), with exactly the three edges in one triangle subdivided once**. This residual subdivision is not excluded here. In particular the result is not an exclusion of twelve grids, does not treat six cubic groups, and makes no rank or short-field assumption.

## Conventions and preliminary reductions

An axis cell \(C_e\) has four sites and is clean if its root plus the four sites and the four parents is a rook grid. Its defect is \(v_e=(A-3I)\mathbf1_{S_e}-\mathbf1\), where \(S_e\) is that nine-site set. Defects vanish at the root and all parents, are centered \(-4\) eigenvectors, and for a dirty cell with \(m\) own edges their squared norm is \(14(4-m)\), with \(m\in\{0,1,2\}\). At an own-cell site the value is \(d_{C_e}-2\). At an off-cell site it is the contact count into \(C_e\) if the cells share an axis, and the contact count minus one if the axes are disjoint. The shared-axis Gram for distinct cells is minus seven times their cross-edge count. In particular equal nonzero columns cannot share an original axis, and different split groups on one axis yield orthogonal columns and no contacts. A clean cell is anticomplete to every other cell sharing an axis. At a degree-two split group the two defects are opposite. A length-\(\ell\) path after suppressing degree-two groups therefore has end columns in ratio \((-1)^{\ell-1}\).

There are nine dirty cells, hence six total degree contributions left after the four cubic vertices; there are exactly three degree-two vertices. The suppressed cubic multigraph has no loop: two end columns in the same group cannot be equal nonzero, or be opposite since then the third column would be zero. By the [two-theta theorem](triangle-wing-two-theta-even.md), if the cubic vertices occupy two components then both are \(222\), which would require twelve dirty cells. When the cubic core is connected, a separate degree-two cycle would require four degree-two vertices by the [cycle theorem](triangle-wing-theta-no-cycles.md), more than the three available. Thus all nine cells are in the connected cubic component. A connected loopless cubic multigraph on four vertices is either the simple \(K_4\), or has two opposite doubled links and two other singleton links forming a cross matching: if \(AB\) is doubled then its other two links must go to different remaining vertices \(C,D\), since sending both to \(C\) would leave the final vertex unable to attain degree three without a loop or a further link to \(A,B\). The two remaining missing degrees on each of \(C,D\) give the opposite double. A triple link isolates its two endpoints and is impossible in the connected case.

## Excluding the opposite-doubled \(K_4\)

Write \(AB,CD\) for the doubles and \(AC,BD\) for the singletons, and use lowercase for the original axes. A double needs one subdivision, since its two direct paths would repeat an original cell. If the extra third subdivision lies on a double, one double has link lengths \(1,3\) or \(2,2\) (equal parity), and the other is \(1,2\) (mixed parity). At a double with equal parity, the two singleton-end columns are proportional, by summing the two local equations for its endpoints with the same or the opposite signs. At a mixed double, the two double-link fields \(p,q\) satisfy \(p\cdot q\le0\) and \(p\cdot(-q)\le0\), so \(p\cdot q=0\). Its singleton columns \(-p-q,-p+q\) each have norm squared at most 56, while \(p^2,q^2\ge28\). Thus \(p^2=q^2=28\) and the two singleton fields are orthogonal and nonzero. This contradicts proportionality at the other double, since each singleton transports its field up to sign.

It follows that both doubles have lengths \(1,2\), and one singleton, say \(AC\), has length two, while \(BD\) is direct. At \(A\) let the \(AB\) direct column be \(p\) and the subdivided \(AB\) start \(q\). At \(B\) they are \(p,-q\). The preceding calculation gives \(p\perp q\), both squared norms 28, and
\[
R=-p-q,\qquad S=-p+q
\]
as the \(AC\) start and \(BD\) fields. Both squared norms are 56. At \(C\) and \(D\), the \(AC\), \(BD\) columns are respectively \(-R,S\). The two zero sums then give \(CD\) direct column \(-q\) and the subdivided \(CD\) columns \(-p,p\), in the \(C,D\) order.

The two cells carrying \(p\) with the same sign (direct \(AB\) and the subdivided \(CD\) end) are disjoint in axes; hence the \(CD\) midpoint is not \(a,b\), and cannot be its own endpoints \(c,d\). Likewise the cells carrying \(-q\) with the same sign (direct \(CD\) and the subdivided \(AB\) end) show that the \(AB\) midpoint is an axis outside \(a,b,c,d\). Each such repeated same-sign column is at least \(-1\) everywhere, because it is off at least one of its two own cells at each site. Therefore those repeated cells are matchings: they have two edges and no isolate. In each of \(p,q\), entries of absolute value two are possible only on its single opposite-sign own cell. In fact for any column, an off-cell value is at least \(-1\): this is immediate for disjoint axes, and for shared axes the value is nonnegative.

Both cells on \(AC\) and the cell \(BD\) are empty because their column norm is 56. On \(AC\)'s start the values are \(p=q=1\) and on its end they are \(p=q=-1\), giving eight positive contributions to \(p\cdot q\). On \(BD\) they are \(p=1,q=-1\), giving four negative contributions. On the other six dirty cells their product is zero: at either end of a double, the two fields' shared-axis Gram is zero; at its two adjacent cells the field of the direct link has the same zero Gram with the end columns of the subdivided link, one at each corresponding endpoint. Each of these zero cross-contact statements yields zero value on the other field's own cell by the shared-axis formula.

On a clean cell touching any of \(a,b,c,d\), both fields vanish because both have a representative column at that cubic axis. A clean cell touching either double midpoint has at least one of the two fields zero because a midpoint is a degree-two split group. Thus if the two midpoints are distinct, the four cubic axes and both midpoints exclude all clean cells from the dot product, contradicting \(p\cdot q=0\). If they are the same, the only possible remaining contributions come from the clean cell \(F\) on the two unused axes (if that cell is dirty there are no remaining contributions). Orthogonality forces \(p,q\) to be opposite nonzero units on all four of its sites. Moreover \(p\cdot v_F=0\) implies \(-7\sum_{x\in F}p_x=0\), using \(A p=-4p\). Thus some site of \(F\) has \(p=1,q=-1\), giving \(S=-2\) off its own \(BD\) cell, impossible by the lower bound of \(-1\) for an off-cell column value.

## The simple \(K_4\): triangle parity and the star

On the simple core attach to each link the sign comparing its end columns. The signs of the four triangle products multiply to \(+1\). If precisely two triangle products are negative, switch the cubic-vertex equations by signs to leave exactly one negative link, the edge common to those two triangles. Indeed the ratio of the original labelling to this single-negative labelling has all triangle products positive, and is a vertex switching: fix one vertex, take the three signs to its neighbours, and use the triangles through it to recover the other edges. In a one-negative chart \(AB\), summing the two equations at \(A,B\) cancels the \(AB\) field and makes the sum of the four cross terms zero. The equations at \(C,D\) now give \(2v_{CD}=0\), impossible for a dirty link. Hence all four triangle products agree.

Three subdivisions may lie on a single link or use two links as \(2+1\) interior vertices; both give just one subdivided link of even length, hence just one negative sign and two negative triangles. The alternative is to subdivide three distinct links once each. The three negative signs then form a star, a triangle, or a three-edge path: the last again gives two negative triangles. Only the star and triangle survive parity.

In the star case let \(A\) be the center and \(B_1,B_2,B_3\) the other cubic vertices. Write \(i_j\) for the original midpoint of \(A B_j\), and \(p_j\) for its start column. Then \(\sum_j p_j=0\), and the two end columns are \(p_j,-p_j\). The direct leaf link opposite \(B_j\) has column \(-p_j\): this assignment solves the three leaf zero equations and the solution is unique over the reals (the three direct-column sums at the leaf vertices have invertible coefficient matrix). The cells with repeated column \(-p_j\) have disjoint axes, so the midpoint cannot be either of the other two cubic leaf axes. It is also distinct from its own endpoints. The three midpoints are distinct, because otherwise an original cell at \(A\) repeats. They are therefore exactly the three noncubic original axes. The two repeated same-sign cells are matchings and \(p_j^2=28\). The only possibly nonunit position of \(p_j\) is a \(-2\) at an isolate in its start cell \(C_{a i_j}\). This happens only if its two own edges are a length-two path plus one isolate.

All \(p_j\) vanish on the twelve clean cells. A clean cell touching any cubic original axis shares that axis with a representative column of each \(p_j\); its contact count into each is zero. A clean cell on two midpoints has the two corresponding fields zero by the degree-two rule and the third zero by the sum. Thus all support is within the thirty-six dirty sites. On unit or zero sites the total squared contribution of the three fields is at most two, by their zero sum. At each exceptional isolate the list is \((-2,1,1)\), contributing six. The center of the corresponding two-edge path has \(p_j=0\); the other two values there are nonnegative by the shared axis at \(A\) and sum to zero, so the full squared contribution is zero. These isolate-center pairs are disjoint. If there are \(n\le3\) of them, the total squared norm is at most \(72+2n\le78\), instead of \(3\cdot28=84\). The star is excluded, leaving only a triangle of subdivided links.

**Eleven-grid companion.** The [four-cubic eleven-grid reduction](eleven-grid-four-cubic-core.md) leaves one simple and two opposite-doubled length patterns when all incident components have at most eighteen sites. It does not classify the separate six-cubic case.
