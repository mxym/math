# Binary rank at least six for every nontrivial fixed-contact trade

Suppose two labeled Conway graphs agree on the full neighborhood of a triangle and all sixty labeled far contact words. If their signed adjacency difference F is nonzero, its mod-two rank is **at least six**. This includes no assumption on either Seidel rank or symmetries of either graph. In conjunction with [the odd-prime theorem](far-odd-trade.md), the first five Smith invariants of the nonzero integer F are all 1; its sixth invariant is a power of 7 (possibly 1). This is a restriction on differing actual completions, not existence, exclusion, or unrestricted uniqueness.

**Antipodes are forced before any finite search.** Two disjoint nonempty families of k distinct far words with equal integer incidence sums must have k≥3: squaring their common sum gives 6k≤2k², since different six-site words meet in at most two sites. In any signed trade row the two color neighborhoods P_i,N_i have their near sums equal. If two distinct rows have equal *unsigned* support U, then P_i and N_j have equal sums, each half the word sum on U. Removing their intersection leaves t=|P_i∩P_j| words on each side. The actual common-neighbor bound gives t≤2, forcing t=0. Thus the rows are opposite signed rows. In particular the multiplicity of any nonzero binary row is at most two.

The mod-two difference is alternating. Its active sites have nonzero images spanning a nondegenerate symplectic quotient, the pairing being their unsigned discrepancy. In rank two there are only three nonzero types, and each sees at most four sites in the other two types, versus minimum unsigned changed degree six. Rank four admits a useful literal model: the even subspace of F2^6 modulo the all-one word, with ordinary dot form. It is symplectic, nondegenerate, has dimension four, and its fifteen nonzero elements are represented uniquely by pairs of coordinates. Distinct types pair to 1 exactly when these edges of an abstract K6 share an endpoint. Consequently any binary-rank-four trade is determined by a multigraph of occupied K6 edges of multiplicity one or two, and signs on the discrepancies. Two copies of the same edge have actual opposite rows, not independent signs. All active sets have at most thirty sites.

**Unsigned restriction.** Mod out the six abstract coordinate permutations: enumerate the 156 simple support graph types (including empty), and for each enumerate subsets of doubled edges under its stabilizer. Keep spanning binary Gram rank four, and require every unsigned degree 2k even between six and sixteen. There are 1,294 such multiplicity types. If types a,b are distinct and a is doubled, sum the old and new changed-neighbor intersection sizes for (a+,b) and (a−,b). The sum is precisely the unsigned common-neighbor count of a,b. Since the two actual pairs each have combined cap 3 when ab is a changed edge, and at most 4 otherwise, the unsigned count is at most 6, respectively 8. The bound also holds with b doubled. This leaves 720 types.

**Signed local rows.** For each occupied type choose a representative. A candidate positive neighborhood S at a has size k and contains exactly one site from each adjacent doubled type; the other neighbor sites are its negative set Z. Swapping the representatives within any doubled type is a relabeling. Starting from all singleton types, attach unvisited double types along a forest of support edges (start another tree if necessary). On each forest edge require the two representatives' entry positive: choose the orientation of each new doubled endpoint when it is attached. This fixes only permissible representative labels, not sign switches of actual graphs.

For a positive-neighborhood matrix P, a negative-neighborhood matrix N and p≠q, set d_pq=max((P²+P)_pq,(N²+N)_pq). The actual near contacts of p,q number at most 2−d_pq. Therefore each full row must satisfy the integer debt bound
     
    ∑_(p∈S,q∈Z) d_pq ≤ 2k²−6k.
     
Even before other rows are known, d_pq is at least the unsigned adjacency between p,q. Keep only row candidates satisfying this weaker bound. For two distinct type representatives, agreement of their common entry is necessary, as is d_pq≤2 for all pairs of actual sites from the two types, including either or both antipodes when present. These are tests on just two possible signed rows. Remove a row option if some other type has no compatible option; repeat. Exhaustively branch only where needed.

The 720 unsigned types split as follows: 578 have an initially empty row domain, 96 have a pair of domains with no compatible pair, 26 collapse on the iterated deletion rule, and the last 20 yield 13,551 finite tree nodes total (largest tree 6,221). There are 2,080 complete signed charts before the exact debt bound; 184 pass the bound, and 56 also have tr(F³)=0. For those use the [common-edge equation](far-rank-four-trade.md). Let K consist of the actual entries common to both adjacencies within the active set. Only F_ij=0 pairs are eligible; adding any individually eligible pair to P,N must still satisfy (G+G²)_ab≤2 off the diagonal in each. Subtracting the SRG equations gives KF+FK=−F−P²+N². No contributions involving inactive sites differ. For all 56 cases even the reduction of its right side modulo two lies outside the column span contributed by the individually eligible edges. No signs or graphs survive. The [NumPy verifier](far_binary_trade.py) uses exact integer operations and no stopping limit. No 99-vertex completions are searched.

**Sixth invariant.** In every characteristic p≠7, the rank of F must be even independently of the small-chart arguments. If p≠3,7, use the symmetric rational idempotent (3I−A+J/9)/7, which is defined integrally at p. At p=3 use instead (A+4I−2J/11)/7. These are the two nonprincipal spectral projections, of respective ranks 44 and 54. Each reduction has the same rank as over the rationals, since the idempotent splits the local free lattice into direct summands. For self-adjoint idempotents P,Q, the kernel of P−Q is the direct sum of the common images and common kernels: if Px=Qx=y, decompose x=y+(x−y). The common kernel is the orthogonal complement of the sum of their images. Thus rank(P−Q)=rank(P)+rank(Q)−2dim(im(P)∩im(Q)), which is even for two Conway adjacency projections of the same type. Their difference is a unit multiple of F. The odd-prime gap, together with this parity and the binary gap, gives rank≥6 at every prime other than seven, and rank≥5 at seven. The Smith assertions follow. No even-rank claim at seven is needed.
