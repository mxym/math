# Eliminating the mixed even pair of separate theta components

Continue the [two-theta theorem](triangle-wing-two-theta-odd.md): four cubic split groups on four distinct original axes, no larger dirty split groups, and two separate theta components. Its surviving necessary charts had pairs \(222+222\) and \(222+422\) (up to order). These can be tightened: **both components must be \(222\), and no separate dirty cycle is possible.** There still are 19 ordered axis charts after this test; none is asserted to extend to a graph. Connected cubic cores and profiles with more cubic vertices are outside the scope.

## A universal central-overlap obstruction in a \(422\) component

Consider a \(422\) component, on original endpoint axes \(a,b\), with long path \(a-i-j-k-b\) and two short paths. This argument is internal to the component and applies regardless of other components or their sizes. Suppose one short path has middle axis \(j\), the center of the long path. Let \(p\) be the unit long field at \(a\), \(q\) the short field through \(j\), and \(r=-p-q\) the other short field. The two groups at \(j\) are distinct, so \(p\cdot q=0\) by the shared-axis Gram and the degree-two zero rule. Their norms are each at least 28, and \(r^2\le56\), so \(p^2=q^2=28\), \(r^2=56\). Both cells of the \(r\)-path are empty.

The other short middle is one of the two axes unused by the long path: it cannot be \(i,k\) because the corresponding start or end pair would repeat, and cannot be \(j\) because the two short middles are distinct. Call it \(g\), and call the other unused axis \(d\). On the \(r\)-cells \(C_{ag},C_{bg}\), both \(p\) and \(q\) are unit or zero. This is automatic for \(p\); for \(q\) on \(C_{ag}\), its \(aj\) column gives a nonnegative value by the shared-axis formula, while its opposite column at \(bj\) gives an upper bound of one from the disjoint-axis formula. At \(C_{bg}\) use the reverse signs. Since \(r=\mp2\) on these sites, \(p=q=\pm1\) at each, giving **eight positive contributions** to \(p\cdot q\).

The short field \(q\) vanishes on the two long interior cells by the other split group at the axis \(j\), and on both long end cells by its zero Gram with their columns at the relevant cubic endpoint. Likewise \(p=0\) on the two cells of the \(q\)-path by the degree-two rule at \(j\). The four own long cells use sixteen signed sites of \(p\), and the two \(r\)-cells use eight. As in the [long-path support argument](triangle-wing-theta-paths.md#a-single-length-four-path-and-two-short-paths), all other cells on a long-path axis have zero \(p\); hence the four remaining nonzero unit sites are exactly the entire \(C_{dg}\). Here \(r=0\), because it touches its short middle axis \(g\) in another split group. Thus \(q=-p\) on all four sites there. The full dot product is therefore \(8-4=4\), not zero. This excludes the central overlap; a possible \(422\) component must use the two unused long axes as its two short middles.

Adding this check to the [previous finite axis checker](triangle-wing-two-theta-odd.md#exact-finite-coverage) eliminates all its 24 ordered \(422+222\) charts and all 24 reverses. The other zero counts do not change.

## An additional outer-budget rule excludes the pure cycles

For the remaining case \(222+222\), there cannot be a complementary triple of a pure four-cycle containing one clean cell and two cells from the **same** theta. The four nonisolated sites of its outer twelve-site graph are already used by the clean square, so both theta cells must be empty and mutually anticomplete. Their shared axis is either their short middle, in which case they are the two cells of the same degree-two group and empty cells require two contacts per site across, or a cubic endpoint, in which case they are two incident cells of the same cubic group. If both are empty their two defect norms are 56, and the third column has squared norm \(14(4-m)\le56\). Their shared-axis cross-edge count is \((56+56-14(4-m))/14=4+m>0\), again impossible. Thus those two cells are forbidden in the outer triple regardless of other cells or adjacency choices.

The [updated exact checker](triangle_wing_two_theta_even.py) is the previous fixed-endpoint enumeration with only these two additions. It uses no adjacency realization or numerical arithmetic. Its only remaining before-parity rows are \(133+133\) with 228, \(133+222\) and \(222+133\) with 84 each, and \(222+222\) with 19, all without separate cycles. The already-proved far-degree parity test deletes the odd rows, leaving 19 ordered \(222+222\) labeled diagrams. This also deletes all 42 prior candidate pure-cycle placements in the one remaining even pair. In the surviving diagrams one theta's three short middles are the three noncubic original axes: if each theta used an endpoint of the other as a short middle, one original cross pair would be repeated. Accordingly, up to exchanging components and axes, the other theta uses zero, one, or two of the first theta's endpoints as middles. These are three necessary axis shapes, not three graph configurations.
