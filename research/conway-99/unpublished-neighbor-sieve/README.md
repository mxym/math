# Conway 99-graph: lattice bounds and local reconstruction

The existence of a strongly regular graph with parameters $(99,14,1,2)$ remains open. After the [rank-34 exclusion](../rank-34-exclusion/README.md), the four possible Seidel ranks over the field of seven elements are 36, 38, 40 and 42. These working notes give necessary conditions for each of them. Continuing the [rank-48 reduction](../unimodular-four-shell/README.md), the lattice argument below also rules out every even 2-neighbor of a decomposable rank-48 even unimodular lattice as the canonical target.

The [rooted rank-32 lattice](rooted-rank32.md) works at any vertex, with no coclique or short-type assumption. Its dual graph part vanishes on the closed neighborhood. It gives a lower-dimensional modular-form route at every remaining rank, not an exclusion.

A [triangle-wing criterion](triangle-wing-tree-rank36.md) leaves a rank-eight residual when the 36 wing vertices induce a connected graph and makes rank 36 impossible if its number of spanning trees is a nonzero square modulo seven. Existence of a triangle meeting that test is not established.

A [triangle-wing determinant bound](triangle-wing-short-field.md) forces a nonzero integral −4 field of squared norm at most 76 vanishing on each closed triangle neighborhood at rank 36. The analogous bounds are 124, 202, and 328 at ranks 38, 40, and 42 respectively.

The [axis-splitting argument](big-triangle-wing.md) shows that every vertex lies on a triangle whose wing graph has a component of at least 18 vertices, so there are at least 33 such triangles. It does not assume a Seidel rank or force a connected wing graph. A [weighted incidence bound](triangle-wing-parity-count.md) shows that the number of triangles with largest wing component exactly 18 plus twice the number with a component of at least 24 is at least 66. On the Fano-free face, [each doubly prism-covered square outside a grid](doubly-covered-square-short-mark.md) gives a distinct unit short field in the original marked coset.

The [affine witness identity](affine-witness.md) applies directly to all four remaining ranks, including rootless lattices. Together with the [norm-sixteen packing bound](norm16-packing.md), it forces at least 62,258 distinct vertex subsets of sizes 24, 25 or 28 on the same graph at rank 36. The packing bound also limits the number of oriented norm-sixteen vectors to 6,930 in every rank and to 1,154 in rank 42.

The [rank-48 second moments and rooted counting argument](short-shell-moments.md) require at least five different short labeling types at every remaining rank. Eleven types together account for at least 426,986, 61,151, 8,889 and 1,423 integer labelings at ranks 36, 38, 40 and 42, respectively. At rank 42 there are at most 24,640 oriented eigenvectors of squared norm 18. The [certified overlap and norm-16 switch](shared-norm-18/exclusion.md), the [higher-degree shell identity](shared-norm-18/higher-moments.md) and the [three-label exclusion](shared-norm-18/panel-heights.md#the-three-label-exclusion-and-the-rank-38-contradiction) show that among twelve selected unit types, ranks 36 and 38 need at least three. The only possible two-type support at ranks 40 and 42 would consist of norm-18 and norm-24 eigenvectors. It is excluded by the [rank-40 tangent congruence](shared-norm-18/rank40-two-type-tangent-deck.md) and the [rank-42 centroid contradiction](shared-norm-18/rank42-two-type-closure.md). Thus at least three selected unit types occur at every remaining rank; at rank 36 at least four occur. The [rank-independent support sieve](shared-norm-18/common-six-family.md) leaves at most eighteen exactly-three-type supports, before further rank-specific restrictions. This selected-unit list is different from the original eighteen-type table used for the five-type assertion. Whenever norms 16 and 20 are absent, $N_{18}\le16,166$. For all four ranks the [exterior of a norm-18 marking](shared-norm-18/height-three.md) has at most one height-three site.

The [signed support census](signed-supports.md) shows that the seven-positive and twelve-positive labelings are uniquely determined by their positive sets. For the three-positive labeling, it leaves at most 11,125 local candidates across thirteen neighborhood distributions. This includes 24 candidates in which a pair of positives is adjacent, with the positive names fixed as described there.

The [triangle and edge counts](edge-switches.md) retain the rank-36 lower bound after discarding the norm-24 supports with four or more same-sign edges whose triangle has an unmarked third site. They also give a reversible reduction of the positive-edge three-positive labelings to 132 twenty-site shapes up to reversing all signs.

The [edge-complement calculation](edge-complement.md) reduces all four ranks to 72 residual vectors in explicit rank-19 complements in rooted Niemeier lattices. The [triangle-complement calculation](triangle-complement.md) guarantees a 39-site neighborhood for which at most ten exterior adjacency rows determine the whole graph once the wing contacts are known. The [contact-transitivity census](contact-transitivity.md) rules out a transitive wing action even on the partial contact design. The simultaneous placements remain open.

The [one-wing reconstruction](one-wing.md) determines all completions of a labeled exterior using only its adjacency matrix. A [count over the two ends of a nonedge](two-star-cover.md) permits choosing the first exterior row from a list of 3,467 of the 6,727 normalized starting types. At every nonedge the [twenty ordinary sites are connected](two-star-connectivity.md) and the full two-star has an 18-dimensional complement. The [square reconstruction](square-exterior.md) takes just a graph on 51 exterior sites in arbitrary vertex order and enumerates all completions. A base square may be chosen from [six exterior degree distributions](square-profiles.md). If uncovered squares exist, [at least one tenth](four-cell-choice.md) have at most one mark and no four-cap exterior site. An [eleven-site set spanning 22 edges](eleven-sites.md) forces one of two 33-site graphs spanning 32 marked dimensions. Independently, if all squares are covered by prisms, [35 triangle neighborhoods](covered-square.md) suffice, and the [grid-free covered branch](covered-spread.md) forces a spread of 33 saturated triangles. [Full square coverage forces a saturated triangle](saturated-choice.md), so a triangle partition also follows when grids are present. The [twelve-site certificates](twelve-sites.md) rule out two types of connected triangle-neighborhood component.

## Integer coordinates at scale two

Write the marks as $z_i=u_i+c$, where $c\perp\operatorname{span}(u_i)$ and $\|c\|^2=8/9$. Suppose $\sqrt2 z_i$ have integral coordinates in some Euclidean space. For a coordinate write its 99 entries as $t_i$ and its mean as $\alpha$. Then $A(t-\alpha\mathbf1)=-4(t-\alpha\mathbf1)$ and $18\alpha$ is integral. Since $99\alpha$ is integral as well, $9\alpha$ is an integer. The centered square sum is at most 14 by the tight frame identity.

Rounding the centered entries to their two nearest levels gives $\sum(t_i-\alpha)^2\ge 11k(9-k)/9$ if $9\alpha\equiv k\pmod9$. The only nonintegral possibilities are $k=1,8$. After a translation by an integer and a possible change of sign, the integer entries $n_i$ then sum to $-11$, satisfy $An=-4n-2\mathbf1$, and have $\sum n_i^2\le14+11/9$. Thus $\sum n_i(n_i+1)\le4$. There are at most two positive units and an entry at most $-2$ would require neighbor sum at least six. Each negative unit requires two positive neighbors, so at least eleven sites would share the same two neighbors. This proves that every coordinate mean is integral. Their squared sum should be $2\|c\|^2=16/9$, impossible. The proof does not assume equality between the ambient dimension and 48.

## A cut-rank bound

For a partition $S,\bar S$ of the vertices, let $C$ be its crossing adjacency matrix and $m=|S|$. The positive restricted eigenvalue 3 yields $e\ge 11m(99-m)/99$ crossing edges. Each diagonal entry of $CC^t$ is at most 14 and each off-diagonal entry is at most two. The sum of its diagonal is $e$, and its off-diagonal sum is at most $13e$ by counting pairs in each column. Hence $\operatorname{tr}((CC^t)^2)\le40e$ and $\operatorname{rank}C\ge e/40$.

Suppose all marks lie in two subspaces of dimensions at most 25 and their cross Gram matrix has rank at most two. Assign any mark belonging to both spaces to either part. Each part has at least twenty marks since the entire mark span has dimension 45. The cross adjacency $J-G_{S,\bar S}$ has rank at most three. Yet $e\ge 11\cdot20\cdot79/99>120$, a contradiction.

## Possible summands before taking a neighbor

Write a putative decomposable even unimodular parent as an orthogonal sum. An index-two kernel preserves roots whose pairing with its normal is even. A rank-eight or rank-sixteen even unimodular summand has root system $E_8$, $E_8^2$, or $D_{16}$ (in the last case the lattice is $D_{16}^+$). Each contains four orthogonal $A_2$ subsystems. From each $A_2$, at least one root pair remains in a binary hyperplane since its three positive roots reduce to $a,b,a+b$. This would give four orthogonal root pairs in the target, already excluded. The summands must therefore both have rank 24.

The Niemeier root systems show that a rank-24 summand is either rootless, of type $A_1^{24}$, or contains four pairwise orthogonal $A_2$ subsystems. This last assertion can also be read off component by component: $A_n$ contains $\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor$ such subsystems on disjoint coordinate triples, $D_n$ contains $\lfloor n/3\rfloor$, and $E_6,E_7,E_8$ each contain at least two. In the Niemeier list all components have the same Coxeter number. In particular no $A_1$ component mixes with a larger component. Each listed larger component contributes at least one sixth of its rank in orthogonal $A_2$'s. Thus only the rootless and $A_1^{24}$ cases remain.

A 2-neighbor of a parent $N$ has common kernel $N_0$. Its new coset consists of $h/2+N_0$ after choosing $h\in N$ of squared norm divisible by eight. The normal to $N_0$ is $h\bmod2N$. In particular a new vector pairs half-integrally with each root of $N$ omitted from $N_0$.

* If both factors have an orthogonal root basis over the reals, at least 45 of their 48 root pairs are omitted. Each new vector has squared norm at least $45/8>4$, by orthogonal projection onto these root lines. Every norm-four point is inherited from $N$ and has integral coordinates after scaling by $\sqrt2$ in the orthonormal basis of normalized roots. This was ruled out above.
* If only one factor has roots, it must have type $A_1^{24}$ and omit at least 21 pairs. The squared norm of the root-factor projection of any new vector is at least $21/8$. For a new norm-four vector, twice its projection to the rootless factor is in a fixed class modulo twice that lattice and has squared norm at most $11/2$. It is zero or a vector of squared norm four. In that class there is at most one antipodal norm-four pair: for two different pairs both half sum and half difference would be nonzero and have squared norm at least four, contrary to the parallelogram identity. Every new norm-four point therefore lies in the rooted space plus one fixed line. Inherited norm-four points lie in just one of the two factors since the other factor has minimum four. The cut-rank bound applies.
* If both factors are rootless, write $d_1,d_2$ for the minimum squared norms of the two residue classes of $h$. A new norm-four vector is half of a pair of representatives whose squared norms add to sixteen. If a class has positive minimum $d<8$, its shortest representative is unique up to sign, and every other representative has squared norm at least $16-d$, by the same half-sum and half-difference argument. If both minima are positive and below eight, at least one projection of a mixed mark must lie on the corresponding fixed short line. If exactly one minimum is below eight, every mixed mark has its corresponding projection on that line. A zero class produces a neighbor in one factor and an orthogonal direct sum. If both minima are at least eight, only the case $d_1=d_2=8$ can have mixed norm-four points. All the fixed-line cases put the marked points in the union of a 24-space plus a line and its counterpart with the two factors interchanged. The two cross inner products factor through the two fixed lines, so the cut-rank bound applies.

In a rootless even unimodular rank-24 lattice, the theta series is $E_4^3-720\Delta$. The numbers of vectors of squared norms 4, 6 and 8 are 196560, 16773120 and 398034000. The classes containing vectors of norm 4 or 6 have a unique such antipodal pair and contain no norm-eight representative, by half sums and differences. In any remaining class distinct nonopposite norm-eight vectors are orthogonal, so at most 48 can occur. The count
$$398034000=48(2^{24}-1-196560/2-16773120/2)$$
forces every remaining class to contain a full 24-frame of norm-eight vectors.

For $d_1=d_2=8$ choose these frames in the two factors. An inherited norm-four vector lies in one factor and pairs evenly with each frame vector, since the frame vectors have the same residue there as $h$. A new norm-four point has both projections equal to halves of frame vectors, and also pairs evenly with the frame. In these normalized frame coordinates every marked point becomes integral after scaling by $\sqrt2$. This eliminates the last case.

## An obstruction at the prime three

The target cannot be the even 3-neighbor of a minimum-six even unimodular parent $N$ along a class containing a vector $r\in N$ of squared norm twelve. For any norm-four point $z$ in the neighbor choose its sign so $z=r/3+v$ for some $v\in N$. Then $v\ne0$. Put $v^2=2j$, so $j\ge3$ and $(r,v)=4-3j$. Now $(r+v)^2=20-4j$. The vector $r+v$ is nonzero, since $v=-r$ would give $z^2=16/3$. The parental minimum implies $j=3$. Consequently $|(r,z)|=1$ for every norm-four point.

For the 99 marks set $t_i=(r,z_i)$ and $\alpha=\sum t_i/99$. The same neighbor equation as above gives $9\alpha=k\in\mathbb Z$. Change the sign of $r$ to arrange $k\ge0$. The sum of 99 signed units is odd, so $k$ is odd and $k\le9$. The centered square sum is $99(1-\alpha^2)\le 7\cdot12$, hence $k=5,7,9$. When $k=7$, precisely eleven signs are negative. Their indicator $c$ obeys $Ac=2\mathbf1-4c$, impossible on its support.

Write $r_K$ for the projection onto the four-space of the construction. For $k=5,9$, tightness requires
$$r_K^2=12-99(1-k^2/81)/7=20/9,\ 12,$$
respectively. But its products with the first three reduced basis vectors $s_1,s_2,s_3$ of $K_0$ are signed units, and its product with the common projection $c=e/3$ is $k/9$. Those four directions span the space. The eight sign choices give projection norms in $\{50,86,122,230\}/63$ when $k=5$ and $\{10,22,26,46\}/7$ when $k=9$. Neither contains the required value.

In addition any even 3-neighbor of a minimum-six parent can have at most one orthogonal root pair: two distinct orthogonal roots both have nonzero image in the index-three quotient, so their sum or difference lies in the common sublattice and has squared norm four.

## Three orthogonal Fano configurations cannot coexist

In each of the four ranks the marked integral extension has at most **two antipodal root pairs**. In the rank-42 case this gives at most four oriented integral norm-14 eigenvectors. This upper bound permits zero root pairs.

Consider three pairwise orthogonal signed Fano eigenvectors. By the [four-pair argument](../four-root-exclusion/README.md), the independent fifteen-sets of these configurations are pairwise disjoint and miss every signed side. Each of the four signed intersections for any two configurations is a single site. A site on a signed side sees four opposite sites and none on its own side. A site in its fifteen-set sees neither sign. Every remaining site sees one vertex of each sign and three of the fifteen-set.

A site in all three supports is described by three signs. Two such sites cannot agree in two positions. Up to relabeling the groups and reversing signs, the possibilities for the set of triples are the empty set, one triple, two triples differing in two or three positions, three triples, or four triples. Each unused signed pair gives one double site. Single sites fill each side to seven. Delete the three fifteen-sets to obtain a 54-vertex set. A site belonging to exactly $s$ signs has $5+3s$ neighbors there.

Within each signed configuration, label each side by the seven nonzero binary three-vectors. Two opposite sites $v,w$ are adjacent precisely when $v\cdot w=1$ in the binary field: the nonneighbor triples give the Fano plane. The checker assigns the already named multiple sites to these labels, up to invertible linear substitutions and independent reorderings of the still unnamed singletons. It takes all triples of configurations agreeing on their shared sites. Every pair of multiple sites belongs to at least one common configuration, so their adjacency is fully determined. Known degrees and common-neighbor bounds give the following counts.

| Triple sites | Fano labelings by group | Compatible partial graphs passing the first bounds | Passing the support-neighborhood checks |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| None | 174, 174, 174 | 2 | 0 |
| One | 34, 34, 34 | 16 | 0 |
| Two at distance two | 6, 6, 5 | 13 | 0 |
| Two at distance three | 7, 7, 7 | 41 | 2 |
| Three | 1, 1, 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Four | 0, 0, 0 | 0 | 0 |

For those checks, enumerate each vertex's possible neighbors in the union of the three supports. Within its own configurations the edges are already known. Each outstanding signed-side requirement is at most one, so complete it by choosing sites covering the outstanding signs exactly once. Reject a new common neighbor if the pair already has its quota. For two proposed neighborhoods of named vertices, adjacency must be symmetric and the visible common-neighbor count cannot exceed the graph-wide bound. Repeatedly delete choices with no compatible choice at another named vertex. These deletions use only necessary conditions.

The two surviving cases have 32 support sites, including two triple sites $a,b$ of opposite signs, six doubles, and 24 singletons. There are 22 remaining sites $W_0$ outside the supports and the fifteen-sets.

* If $a,b$ are nonadjacent, each has two $W_0$ neighbors. The neighborhood lists show that every such neighbor sees both $a,b$, sees no doubles, and needs three neighbors within $W_0$. The two cannot be adjacent since they already share $a,b$. Of the other twenty sites each sees either one double and no $W_0$ neighbor, or two doubles and one $W_0$ neighbor. The six doubles each require four $W_0$ neighbors. Thus exactly four of the twenty sites have one available incidence. The first two sites require six.
* If $a,b$ are adjacent, every support neighborhood is fixed. Both triples require four outside neighbors, all in $W_0$. For each triple there are five candidate neighborhoods. Testing four choices with multiplicities against its required common-neighbor counts gives a unique list. The two lists share one point, the common neighbor of $a,b$. Each of the six other points needs exactly one neighbor in $W_0$. Each needs a second common neighbor with the opposite triple, and the shared point already forms a triangle with $a,b$. Thus these six points match across the two lists. Of the six possible matchings, five exceed a common-neighbor bound on the 39 known sites. In the last matching the checker finds a fourth vector $t$, with seven positive and seven negative entries, orthogonal to the first three. Its adjacency quadratic form on these sites is $-56$. Extending it by zero gives Rayleigh quotient $-4$, the least eigenvalue, so this is a fourth global signed Fano eigenvector. The [four-pair exclusion](../four-root-exclusion/README.md) rules it out.
