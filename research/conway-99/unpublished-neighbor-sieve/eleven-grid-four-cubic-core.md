# Four cubic groups at an eleven-grid root

Suppose a root of a hypothetical Conway graph is on exactly eleven rook grids, every incident triangle wing component has at most 18 sites, and there are exactly four cubic split groups. Their original axes are distinct by the [four-axis theorem](triangle-wing-four-distinct.md). Then there is no separate dirty cycle and the four cubic vertices are in one dirty component. Upon suppressing its degree-two vertices, only the following length patterns survive this proof:
* a simple \(K_4\), two opposite edges having length \(2\), a third edge having length \(3\), and the other three length \(1\);
* the opposite-doubled \(K_4\), with both singleton links of length \(1\), and the two double pairs both of type \((1,3)\) or both of type \((2,2)\).

These are strict necessary profiles only, not graph realizations. The case of six cubic groups at an eleven-grid root, and any larger wing, are not addressed.

Use the [split chart and defect facts](triangle-wing-theta-paths.md#defects-and-short-chains). The end signs of a chain of length \(\ell\) have ratio \((-1)^{\ell-1}\); its length is at most four, a three-edge chain is all matching and unit by the [universal three-edge lemma](triangle-wing-three-distinct.md), and a four-edge chain also has matching cells and a unit norm-28 defect. Such a field cannot use three own matching cells of one sign, since a [unit norm-28 field has at most five same-sign edges on either side](big-triangle-wing.md#a-degree-contradiction-for-the-norm-28-cycle-field).

There are ten dirty cells, hence four degree-two as well as four degree-three groups. An attached loop in the suppressed core is impossible by the [normal zero-sum and shared-axis Gram argument](triangle-wing-twelve-grid-core.md). If the cubic groups occupy two components, the [two-theta theorem](triangle-wing-two-theta-even.md) requires two \(222\)'s and six degree-two vertices. If there is a separate degree-two cycle, it is a four-cycle consuming all four subdivisions. The cubic core is then unsubdivided, hence the simple \(K_4\) (parallel direct links would repeat an original cell). Its opposite columns agree and all six cells are matchings. The [outer budget of a separate four-cycle](triangle-wing-theta-no-cycles.md#the-outer-cell-budget-of-any-separate-four-cycle) allows no matching cell among the three cells on its complementary original axes. Consequently the pure cycle uses at least three cubic original axes: two cubic axes in its complement would already include a K4 matching cell there. But a four-cycle through at least three cubic axes must have an edge between cubic axes, repeating a K4 edge. This is impossible. The entire core is connected and, as in the [twelve-grid classification](triangle-wing-twelve-grid-core.md), it is a simple \(K_4\) or has two opposite double links and two singletons.

## Simple core

The [K4 sign-switching argument](triangle-wing-twelve-grid-core.md) requires either all four triangle sign products \(+1\) or all four \(-1\). With four total subdivisions the number of negative links is even. All-positive triangle products mean the negatives form a cut, so their number is zero or four; all-negative products mean the negatives are the complement of a cut, so their number can only be two (six exceeds the four subdivisions). Two negative links are necessarily opposite.

If there are no negative links, each path has even subdivision count. The only possibility is two length-three paths, since a length-five path is impossible. With unchanged signs the cubic equations force opposite core fields equal. A length-three path already uses two same-sign matching cells at its ends, and its opposite link supplies a third same-sign matching cell, whether or not that link is also long. This exceeds the same-sign edge bound.

If there are four negative links, they are the four edges of a \(2|2\) cut, each subdivided once. Name the sides \(a,b\) and \(c,d\). Switching the equations at \(c,d\) makes all links positive, so each opposite pair of crossing paths uses equal columns at its starts on \(a,b\), and equal opposite columns at its ends on \(c,d\). A midpoint of one such path cannot be an original cubic axis: either it would use its own endpoint, or it would share an original axis with one of the equal-column cells on the opposite path, where the off-cell column is nonnegative and its own value negative somewhere. The four midpoint axes are pairwise distinct, since paths with a common end cannot repeat their original endpoint cell, and opposite paths cannot share a midpoint by the same equal-column rule. There are only three noncubic original axes, a contradiction.

With two negative links, the only length distributions are one length-four path opposite one length-two path, or two opposite length-two paths and one other length-three path. To rule out the former, name opposite paths \(a-i-j-k-b,\ c-x-d\), with start fields \(p,t\). The four direct columns \(ac,ad,bc,bd\) are
\[
 u,\quad -p-u,\quad -t-u,\quad p+t+u .
\]
The long \(p\) is unit of norm 28 and its four matching chain cells use sixteen nonzero sites. At each of \(a,b\), the long end cell has one parent, one own matching edge and one remaining cubic-wing contact per vertex, into the two direct cells at that end. Thus those direct cells contain four positive \(p\)-sites at \(a\) and four negative sites at \(b\). Outside the chain and these endpoint halos, \(p=0\) on any cell meeting the long path's five original axes: at an internal axis this is the degree-two zero rule, and at an end the other cells are in different groups. The remaining four units therefore fill the unique cell \(F\) on the two unused original axes.

The cell \(ab\) is clean: neither chain can use that original pair, nor is it a direct link. All four direct fields vanish on it by the shared-axis clean rule, so both \(p,t=0\) there. Each site of \(ab\) sees one neighbour in each interior chain cell \(ij,jk\), carrying opposite signs, and no contacts into the long ends or halos because these share an axis. The cell \(F\) is either clean or one of the short path cells (it cannot involve the long axes or a long endpoint). Its column therefore vanishes on \(ab\), giving exactly one contact per \(ab\) site by the disjoint-axis formula. This contributes an unmatched nonzero \(p\)-value, contrary to \(Ap=0\) at \(ab\). The only simple-core length pattern left is the stated opposite \(2,2\) and one \(3\).

## Opposite-doubled core

Let \(AB,CD\) be the doubles and \(AC,BD\) the singleton links. Denote by \(d_1,d_2\) the total subdivision counts on the double pairs. They are each at least one. As in the [mixed-parity calculation at twelve grids](triangle-wing-twelve-grid-core.md), a double of equal parity has proportional singleton-start columns, while a double of mixed parity makes the two singleton fields orthogonal and nonzero. Specifically, if its even and odd end directions are respectively \(q\) and \(p\), then at its two cubic ends the double entries are \((p,q),(p,-q)\); nonpositive shared-axis products force \(p\perp q\), and the singleton norms force \(p^2=q^2=28\), with both singleton norms 56. The two double pairs must therefore each have equal parity or each have mixed parity, irrespective of sign changes on the singletons.

If both have mixed parity, their subdivision totals are odd. In the \((3,1)\) case up to order, both singletons are direct. At the high double take plus direction \(p\) and minus direction \(q\). The two transported singleton columns \(-p-q,-p+q\) give plus direction \(p\) and minus direction \(q\) also at the low double, from adding and subtracting the cubic equations. A high pair of lengths \(2,3\) makes \(p\) the length-three direction, supplying two same-sign matching cells and the low double's direct column as a third. A high pair of lengths \(1,4\) makes \(q\) the length-four direction, supplying two matching cells of each sign and the low double's short path as a third of each. Both are impossible by the same-sign edge bound.

The other mixed possibility is \((d_1,d_2)=(1,1)\), leaving two subdivisions on singletons. A length-three singleton would be norm 28 instead of 56, so both have length two. Start the fields on the low double \(AB\) as above, so the singleton starts are \(R=-p-q,\ S=-p+q\). Their ends at \(C,D\) are \(-R,-S\), hence the \(CD\) direct direction is \(-p\) and the short direction is \(-q\) at \(C\), \(q\) at \(D\). Each sign of \(q\) now occurs on two cells, so these four own cells are matchings and \(q\) is unit of norm 28. On each of the four singleton cells the own field is \(\pm(p+q)\) or \(\pm(p-q)\), of norm 56, so the cell is empty and the field equals \(-2\) there. Both \(p,-p\) are off their own direct cells, so \(|p|\le1\) there and necessarily \(|q|=1\). The sixteen singleton sites together with the sixteen own \(q\)-sites already require norm at least 32. This excludes mixed parity.

For equal parity, \(d_1,d_2\) are positive even integers, so both equal two and the singletons are direct. Each double has list \((1,3)\) or \((2,2)\). Its two outgoing singleton fields agree when the double has odd lengths, and are opposite when it has even lengths, by the cubic sums at both ends. Since the singletons are direct and nonzero, the two doubles must use the same length parity. This gives exactly the two stated remaining opposite-doubled patterns.

**Further cuts.** The [axis and moment reduction](eleven-grid-odd-doubled-exclusion.md) excludes the entire doubled-odd length pattern. It leaves one simple axis shape with three moment menus and two doubled-even axis shapes. There is still no exclusion of all four-cubic or eleven-grid roots.
