# A spanning set of short rectangle fields at every closed vertex

At every vertex o of a putative srg(99,14,1,2), the real 30-dimensional space of minus-four eigenfields zero on the closed neighborhood N[o] is spanned by **actual integral fields of squared norms 56, 70 and 84**. At least 2,394 distinct oriented such fields occur at each o, including at least 588 of squared norm 56 or 70. No graph symmetry, modular-form estimate, determinant rank assumption or computer search is involved.

All these vectors belong to the original seven-adic class zero. They are not asserted to lie in any one chosen maximally saturated *dyadic* dual: in fact those of norm 70 cannot. The independent [chosen-dual bound 136](rooted-rank32-norm136.md) concerns that more restrictive family.

## The 84 labels and the rectangle fields

The 14 neighbors N=N(o) are seven disjoint mate pairs, by lambda=1. Each x outside N[o] sees exactly two N vertices, forming a nonmate pair. Each nonmate pair occurs once because it already has common neighbor o and requires exactly one other. Thus X=V minus N[o] consists of the 84 edges ij of the complete seven-partite graph with parts of size two on N. Let D be the ordinary unsigned 14-by-84 vertex-edge incidence of that label graph. For i in N, the twelve labels ij incident to i in X form six edges of the real graph, since they are the remainder of the matching on N(i) after o and its mate at i. Write mu_i for the resulting fixed-point-free matching on the twelve indices j.

A rectangle has distinct rows i,l and columns j,k with all four cross labels valid. The vector z supported on X has signs + on ij,lk and - on ik,lj. Call it conflict-free if no actual graph edge joins the two signs. All potentially conflicting pairs share an N endpoint, so this is exactly the exclusion of four mate conflicts, one at each of i,l,j,k. By construction Dz=0 and sum z=0. Extend z by zero to the whole graph. Then t=(3I-A)z vanishes on N[o] and satisfies At=-4t since A²=12I-A+2J. Its squared norm is 7 z^t(3I-A)z, hence 84-14e for a conflict-free rectangle, with e=0,1,2 the number of actual edges within the two like-sign pairs.

Different signed rectangle vectors give different fields. If t=t' then h=z-z' has sum zero with positive and negative masses at most four and Ah=3h. A positive entry at least two would need at least six positive neighbor mass, impossible. If h≠0, its positive side therefore consists of at most four unit sites each needing at least three positive neighbors. They would form a K4, impossible by lambda=1. This also handles both orientations of every rectangle.

## Abundance

There are 2,121 unsigned rectangles before tests: choose an unordered row pair (seven mate pairs with 12 common columns, or 84 other pairs with 10 common columns), then a pair of common columns, and divide by two for exchanging the row and column pairs. This gives (7 binom(12,2)+84 binom(10,2))/2. Let M be the number of actual shared-endpoint X edges ij,ik for which j and k are N mates; there are exactly 84 shared-endpoint X edges altogether, six per i. A shared-endpoint edge is a potential conflict in 11 rectangles for mate j,k and nine otherwise (the other row is any other common neighbor in the label graph). Thus at most 756+2M≤924 rectangles have any conflict. At least 1197 remain, giving the claimed 2394 different orientations of norm at most 84.

For the norm-70 threshold, select a *marked adjacent positive diagonal*: an unordered actual edge A,B whose two label pairs in N are disjoint. There are 420 choices: the X-subgraph is 12-regular and has 504 edges, and exactly 84 share an N endpoint. Each has two possible complementary pairings C,D, one per way to join the two pairs. At most one of the pairings is invalid: crossed N-mate links, if there are two, themselves constitute one matching. The number of diagonals with an invalid pairing is at most 84. Indeed for each endpoint of the label A, its N-mate is nonadjacent to A and already has that endpoint as one common neighbor with A. There is at most one further common neighbor B, hence at most two mate-crossing incidences per A; each invalid diagonal is counted from both A and B. At least 840-84=756 marked possibilities remain.

If one has a conflict, say actual A adjacent to C with labels A=ij and C=ik, the other positive site B must touch k and be adjacent to A. Since k is nonadjacent to A and C already is one common neighbor, there is at most one other B. Once B is fixed, the fourth site D is determined by the unmatched endpoints. Thus every oriented shared-endpoint edge (first site positive, second negative) participates in at most one marked bad possibility. Removing at most 2 times 84 more leaves at least 588 distinct signed z. Both directions are counted when both diagonals are actual edges; there is no need to assume they occur equally often. The norm is 56 if the complementary diagonal is also an actual edge, and 70 otherwise.

## Why conflict-free rectangles already span the entire kernel

Let g_ij=g_ji be any real label weights orthogonal to all conflict-free signed rectangles. For distinct j,k let b_jk be the number of common admissible rows i where mu_i(j)=k. For a fixed j, sum_k b_jk=12: each of the twelve admissible i gives exactly one mate for j. Call j,k light if b_jk≤2, and heavy otherwise. Each j has at most four heavy partners.

For a light j,k remove the at most two bad rows from the at least ten common rows. On the remaining at least eight, form a graph on rows joining i to l unless mu_j(i)=l or mu_k(i)=l. It is connected: its complement has maximum degree two, whereas a disconnected graph on five or more vertices cannot have that property. Every joined pair now gives a conflict-free rectangle, so g_ij-g_ik has a constant value C_jk on those good rows; orient it by C_kj=-C_jk.

For a triple j,k,s whose three pairs are light there is a common row good on all three: exclude at most six indices (the three and their N mates) and at most six bad rows, leaving at least two. At that row C_jk+C_ks=C_js. Now take a bad common row i for a light j,k, so mu_i(j)=k. Choose s different from j,k, admissible at i and light to both j,k. Exclude at most four labels i, its mate, j,k, and at most eight heavy neighbors of j,k. Since mu_i(j)=k the row i is good on both pairs j,s and s,k, giving g_ij-g_ik=C_js+C_sk=C_jk here too.

For a heavy j,k and any two admissible common rows i,l, choose s different from j,k, admissible at both rows, and light to both j,k. Exclude at most six labels j,k,i,its mate,l,its mate, and at most three *other* heavy partners each for j and k (since j,k are heavy to each other). The two light differences through s already hold on all their admissible rows, so the difference between columns j,k agrees on i,l. This defines C_jk on common rows for every pair, heavy or light.

Any triple has an admissible common row, so the same telescoping identity now holds for all triples. There are potentials a_j with C_jk=a_j-a_k. Within row i the difference g_ij-a_j is therefore independent of j, equal to some d_i. Symmetry g_ij=g_ji implies d_i-a_i=d_j-a_j on every admissible edge; the label graph is connected, so this is one constant, absorbed by adding half of it to each a. Thus g_ij is a sum of the two endpoint potentials. Conversely all such weights annihilate the rectangles. Taking orthogonal complements proves that conflict-free rectangles span ker D.

The map 3I-A takes that kernel (on X extended by zero) into the asserted space. Conversely every such field h satisfies Dh=0 from its eigen-equation at N, and (3I-A)h=7h, so the image is exactly the entire real space. Its dimension 30 follows independently from the [rooted projected-frame construction](rooted-rank32.md): the 14 N marks have nonsingular Gram and include the root mark in their span, inside the 44-dimensional minus-four mark space.

Finally the vertex-frame normalization there gives t=Qz/8, so its dual vector is x=sum_{b in X} z_b m_b/8. This is in the original marked lattice locally at seven, since eight is a unit; no seven-discriminant choice is needed. At two the distinction is genuine: the maximal T-dual allows n=28(x,x)=|t|²/2 only in residues 0,2,4,5,6,7 mod 8. Norm 70 gives n=35≡3, always excluded by that saturation. The present spanning theorem is for actual fields, not a claim about the span obeying an imposed dyadic class restriction.
