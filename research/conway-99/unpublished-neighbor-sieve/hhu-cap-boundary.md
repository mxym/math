# Two hexagonal-prism twelve-wings exclude the whole unit 12+12+12 companion family at rank 36

Let H denote the wing template with three internal mate permutations (01)(23), the first two cross-wing matchings the identity and cross-return (02)(13). As an uncoloured graph this is the hexagonal prism. At mod-seven Seidel rank 36, no triangle has the three-component wing split H+H+U for any of the eleven seven-unit twelve-types in the [catalogue](triangle-wing-twelve-currents.md). This excludes eleven of the 286 all-unit unordered multisets, including H+H+H, but not the entire split or rank.

**The forced boundary.** In the chosen anchor component select a cross-wing edge ij not lying on a transversal triangle. It has no cap in the 36 wing vertices; its cap cannot be one of the original triangle vertices either. Such an edge exists in H, so the anchor is always available. Since λ=1 there is a unique far cap y. The ten far neighbors of i and ten of j have only y in common. Their remaining two nine-site sets are disjoint and all eighteen vertices are nonadjacent to y, since the edges iy and jy are already capped. Write D for the wing adjacency matrix, and w for a far vertex's binary six-contact word, with two sites per component and two per physical wing, as in [the twelve-component contact inventory](triangle-wing-twelve-currents.md). A distinct near pair a,b occurs together in exactly
`c_ab=2−D_ab−(D²)_ab−[a,b in the same physical wing]`
far words. Thus all eighteen boundary vertices have prescribed contact pairs in the anchor, with repetitions c_ik or c_jk for k different from i,j. Such a vertex x in the i-half, with anchor contacts i,k, must have exactly `1−D_ik` neighbors in its own nine-set and `1−D_jk` in the other nine-set. In the first count λ(x,i)=1 and k is the only possible known common neighbor; in the second μ(x,j)=2 with i already common and k the only other possible known one. In both counts y contributes zero. Exchange i and j for the other half. The two same-half graphs and the bipartite graph are therefore partial matchings, with individual degrees known once the pairs are known.

**Rank-two calibration.** The 36-by-36 marked Gram block `G=3I−D+4J (mod 7)` has rank 34 (also checked directly). Select 34 principal pivot sites I, and set p_z=(4−w_z)|I, g_zt=p_zᵀG_I⁻¹p_t. By the [rank-36 discriminant calculation](triangle-wing-twelve-currents.md) the full marking space has nonsquare determinant, so the nondegenerate complement is the binary form diag(1,ε), with ε=1 for nonsquare det G_I and ε=3 otherwise. A true far word z has residual ρ_z of square −g_zz. Far adjacency for two distinct words is 4−g_zt−ρ_z·ρ_t in F7, required to be 0 or 1. Enumerate all possible words of y carrying its required anchor pair. For its residual choose one representative of each plane isometry orbit of its prescribed norm: one orbit for each nonzero norm, and at norm zero the zero vector *and*, in the split plane, the nonzero null-vector orbit. Fixing a zero residual requires g_yz=4 for every boundary z, with no restriction lost. The other two components can be relabeled independently preserving each physical wing and exchanged only when identically labeled ordered templates. Minimizing adjacency codes over all 24 first-wing orders shows every physical orientation of H has the same ordered type; the propagated cross matchings determine the remaining labels. First orient U to its canonical wing labeling by a global permutation and then both H to their unique physical ordered type. If U has an uncapped cross-edge, it is the anchor and the other two components are H,H. Only for the flat U, use H as anchor and retain the ordered H,U distinction. In that fallback the actual U has a single physical orientation fixed by the initial global permutation; no nontrivial wing relabeling is used after fixing the anchor. For each fixed anchor edge, take the first representative of each locally isomorphic seed: canonicalize each of the other two contact pairs under its wing-preserving graph automorphisms, sort only for equal labeled components, and retain the norm and zero/nonzero status of the residual. For example, for H+H+H these 544 raw choices reduce to 21 representatives. No automorphism of an unknown whole graph is assumed.

For each seed and each boundary pair tag, enumerate all allowable words z and all residuals ρ_z with the required norm and product 4−g_yz. Test the exact forced adjacency, matching degrees, and the following common-neighbor upper bounds for every chosen pair of vertices. Their literal near overlap is ≤2−A_zt; a near pair with capacity 1 cannot occur twice; and if z,t are adjacent then each near contact a of t must have positive residual common-neighbor capacity `2−w_z(a)−(Dw_z)(a)` with z, and conversely. For a repeated boundary tag the raw word indices can be increasing: distinct far vertices cannot share six near neighbors. The auxiliary source checks with y are the nonedge versions. Every putative graph gives a simultaneous choice in all 18 classes. The [standalone Python/NumPy checker](hhu_cap_boundary.py) rules them all out by exhaustive integer branching.
