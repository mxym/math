# A two-candidate finish for a six-prism chart with all currents visible

At a six-prism triangle, fix the sixty labeled far contact words. Assume all twelve mod-five circulation currents are visible. From the words choose twelve far sites whose pairing matrix with these currents is invertible, and specify the actual far adjacency rows at those twelve sites. Then the remaining adjacency has at most **two** matrices that meet the residual pairing test, and at most **one** of them is a genuine graph. There is no claim that such a word chart, a choice of rows, or a six-prism graph exists.

## The projected Gram leaves a square line

Use the [circulation section](saturated-five-currents.md) and its [far-rank consequence](six-prism-far-five-rank.md). Work with the marking Gram \(M=3I-A+J/9\) over the five-element field. Split off a nonsingular 19-dimensional part of the known six-prism wing Gram. In its orthogonal complement the twelve actual currents are isotropic and independent. All their pairings are known from the specified words. The twelve chosen far sites give dual partners modulo this wing part: their pairings with the currents are an invertible matrix. Their complete rows specify their pairings with the remaining vertices. The resulting 24-dimensional block, of shape \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&U\\U^t&V\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(U\) invertible, is nonsingular. The total 43-dimensional Gram projection \(g\) is therefore known on all far sites.

The full marking determinant has square class over this field: the marking lattice has determinant \(7^{44-r}/9\) at even mod-seven Seidel rank \(r\). The centered nondegenerate wing form is the orthogonal sum of the identical centered prism forms, six copies, and the remaining constant direction has square norm \(36^2/9\). The determinant of the 24-dimensional block is \((-1)^{12}\det(U)^2\). Thus the one-dimensional orthogonal complement of the known projection has square class.

## Resolve relative signs instead of guessing edges

Write the full far Gram as \(g+vv^t\). The diagonal determines \(v_i^2=2-g_{ii}\); a nonsquare rejects the chart and a zero fixes the coordinate. For a nonzero coordinate the two possible squared norms are 1 and 4. Choose one square root at each such vertex. For any pair, the two signs of the product give the two possible Gram entries \(g_{ij}\pm v_i v_j\); only entries 3 and 4 are allowed. Both product signs can be permitted only when their midpoint is 1 modulo five and their squared half-difference is 4. In particular \(v_i^2 v_j^2=4\), so the sites then have different squared norms. Therefore within each of the two squared-norm types, every pair fixes a relative sign (or the chart rejects). The graph of forced signs has at most two nonempty connected components. Propagate each component's signs, check cross-type constraints, and quotient the common sign. At most two different Gram matrices remain, including the cases when only one type or no nonzero coordinates occur. Off-diagonal Gram entries determine the far adjacency.

If two matrices were actual completions, their signed integer adjacency difference would have rank at most two modulo five. Each of its \(3\times3\) minors has absolute value at most four. When a row has at most two nonzero entries the Hadamard bound is \(\sqrt{18}<5\); otherwise all entries are signed units, and the determinant is divisible by four and has absolute value at most \(3^{3/2}<6\). As every such minor is zero modulo five, it must be zero over the integers. A nonzero difference of real rank at most two would now be a [single vertex transposition](triangle-complement.md#a-rank-two-difference-is-a-transposition), losing twelve old neighbors at a transposed site. Changes are confined to the 8-regular far set, so this is impossible.

This result needs the fixed twelve partner rows and does not cover the case of one invisible current.
