# Thin cuts at every triangle and a lower bound of 105 fully two-cut-free wings

Let T be any triangle in the graph. Write D for its 36-site cubic wing and X for the 60 far sites, as in [triangle-complement.md](triangle-complement.md). No saturation, Seidel rank, or graph symmetry is needed. A cut means two shores in one component, or any wing subset, with exactly the stated number of edges leaving it; components elsewhere may be included in either side.

**Forcing in every graph.** Every such D has no bridge. Its only possible two-edge cut uses two oppositely crossing edges of **one inter-wing matching**. Every such cut emits an antipodal norm-14 Fano line in the original marks. At most two distinct such lines occur over all triangles. At most **126 of the 231 triangles** can have a two-edge cut anywhere in their wings. Thus at least **105** triangles have every connected wing component 3-edge-connected. If the original primal norm-two coefficient is zero, every triangle has this stronger property. For the contraction of the transversal, cross-wing cycles only (of arbitrary lengths 3l), no two-edge cut is possible at *any* triangle because only within-wing mates remain after contraction.

**Shore balance.** Write k_i for the three physical-wing counts of a shore U. Each inter-wing perfect matching i–j contributes at least |k_i−k_j| edges to its boundary, and the number of boundary edges in the within-wing mate matching of physical wing i is congruent to k_i mod 2. The first three lower bounds sum to 2(max k_i−min k_i). If k_i differ and the entire cut is at most two, they differ by only one, the matching differences exhaust the entire budget of two, and at least one of the k_i is odd, an additional boundary edge. Thus all k_i=k. If k is odd, all three within-wing matchings contribute, impossible. For even k, each within-wing matching has an even number of boundary edges. Likewise each inter-wing matching has an even crossing count when its two shore sizes agree (as many from U_i as from U_j). This rules out a bridge and shows that a size-two cut must lie in precisely one of the six matchings.

**Raw boundary field.** Let a=1_U+(k/2)1_T and G=3I−A+J/9. Its coefficient sum is 9k/2. The evaluation f=Ga vanishes on T, equals (3I−D)1_U on the wing, and equals k/2−|N(x)∩U| in X. Hence f is integral, neutral and in the minus-four eigenspace, with f·f=7a·f=7|δ_D U|. In particular for a two-edge cut it is a norm-14 Fano field, and its coefficient vector is integral with charge zero modulo nine, giving the original-class assertion. Its two positive wing sites are the inside boundary endpoints, the two negatives the opposite endpoints; they are all distinct because they belong to a single matching. Any two same-sign sites have their two common neighbors on the opposite Fano side by [the Fano characterization](../short-support-packing/README.md). Thus no zero site can see both. If both cut edges were within one physical wing the root of that wing would see both positives, a contradiction. This also proves the transversal contraction assertion since each contracted cycle has exactly l sites per physical wing, and no cut in the contracted graph crosses the inter-wing cycle edges. The [saturated simple-cubic result](saturated-two-cuts.md) is the case of transversal triangles.

**Bounded reuse of the actual fields.** For fields f=Ga and g=Gb emitted at any two roots, f·g=7a·g is a multiple of seven. Different antipodal Fano lines have absolute inner product at most four by [Fano difference packing](shared-norm-18/fano-difference-packing.md). Hence all distinct emitted lines are pairwise orthogonal. The [three-orthogonal-Fano exclusion, as used here](shared-norm-18/rank36-four-orthogonal-cut.md), forbids a third: it is a theorem about the actual compatible Fano fields, applicable in particular in the original class. Consequently there are at most two lines graph-wide, not two per triangle.

Fix one such line, with signed shores P,N of size seven. They are independent, and the cross graph is 4-regular. Each exterior contact site y sees precisely one p∈P and one n∈N: at most one of either by the saturated same-shore pair quota, and the numbers agree by Af=−4f. There are 70 contact sites U_f (seven positives each have ten neighbors outside the two shores), and the other 15 form Z_f, seeing no support. Among U_f, precisely 42 contact a *nonadjacent* pair (p,n). Indeed the 49 cross pairs include 28 edges and 21 nonedges. Each nonedge needs two common neighbors, both outside P∪N because the shores are independent. These count 42 distinct contact sites. At every y∈U_f use (A²1_P)_y=14−1=13 (here y∉P and |P|=7). The unique N-neighbor of y contributes four, its P-neighbor none, all U_f-neighbors one apiece, and all Z_f-neighbors zero. Hence y has 9 U_f-neighbors and 14−2−9=3 Z_f-neighbors.

At a triangle whose wing emits this Fano line, its two participating physical roots lie in U_f, each contacting the unique positive and negative wing endpoints in its physical wing. Those two sites are in opposite shores of the W cut and cannot be adjacent: a mate edge between them would be an additional cut edge outside the one inter-wing matching. Thus both participating roots are among the 42 nonedge-contact sites. The third root belongs to Z_f. Of the 42·3 ordered adjacent pairs (nonedge-contact y, zero-contact z), each lies in exactly one triangle (λ=1). Each emitting triangle uses two different such pairs. Therefore ≤63 triangles emit this Fano line, and ≤126 triangles emit any of the at most two. This argument counts a triangle only as a possible emitter, without assuming that a field or all possible cuts are realized.
