# Weighted ten-mass supports give a mandatory original norm-79 population

Use C_old(a,t) for original zero-seven-class old −4 labels and the usual complementary +3 field convention. In a hypothetical Conway graph let A_10 count the actual (unweighted) ten-vertex cubic supports, and let W_10 count the weighted supports described below. Then

    W_10 ≥ 99792,
    A_10+W_10 ≤ 3 [C_old(1,79)−29360331].

In particular C_old(1,79)≥29393595, unconditionally. All marks counted here are additional to the [five-site mandatory reference population](cubic-ten-old-mark-budget.md). No lower bound A_10>0 is asserted.

## Weighted support construction and count

Fix a graph vertex i. Its fourteen near vertices form seven mate pairs, and every far vertex (outside {i}∪N(i)) has exactly two anchors in N(i), in distinct pairs. There are 84 far sites, each has twelve far neighbours, hence 504 far edges. There are exactly 84 far edges whose endpoints have some common near anchor: at each of the fourteen near sites u, its twelve neighbours other than i and its mate form six edges (the other six pairs in N(u)), and no far edge has two common near anchors by λ=1.

For any near mate pair u,v, let E_u,E_v be their respective twelve far-neighbour sets. They are disjoint because the edge uv has its unique cap at i. Each a∈E_u is nonadjacent to v. It shares u and exactly one other neighbour with v; that other neighbour lies in E_v, since a is not adjacent to i. Thus precisely twelve far edges have one endpoint with anchor u and the other with anchor v. Counting seven mate pairs bounds the total number of such bad edges by 84 (repetitions can only lower the number). Consequently at least 504−84−84=336 far edges a−b have four near anchors in four different mate pairs.

For each of those edges choose one of the other three near mate pairs. The weighted vector s has weight two at i and weight one at the four anchors, the chosen mate pair, and a,b (eight units). At each site of the support, (As)_v=3s_v: the center sees six units; an anchor sees the center and its own far endpoint; either chosen mate sees the center and its mate; and each far endpoint sees its two anchors and its far mate. The exact two-anchor condition and matching in N(i) exclude any extra such support edge. The unweighted support has nine vertices, twelve internal edges, and one site of degree six (its doubled center); the six near units have degree two, and the far pair have degree three. Thus each support uniquely recovers the center, far edge and selected mate pair; there are at least 99·336·3=99792 distinct weighted supports.

## A new old mark, not a previous small mark

Put h=(A−3I)s. It is zero on the support and nonnegative elsewhere. The old mark w(s) has squared norm 100/9 (its internal weighted A−3I quadratic form is zero and its input mass is ten), hence raw squared cost (700+11)/9=79 at normalized charge one. It is in the original seven-class zero, since w(s)−u_0 is a sum of differences of vertex marks plus 9u_0.

Suppose w(s)=w(c) for a mandatory at-most-five-site input c=1_X−1_Y of input sum one. Then k=s−1_X+1_Y is a charge-one +3 field of mass nine. If k has any negative sites, it must have exactly three, forming a triangle anticomplete to positives: at such a site the required sum is −2, from at most two other negative units. Thus all three X sites are outside the s support, and there are no X−Y edges. But the five-site cost 79 requires signed-edge excess e(X)+e(Y)−e(X,Y)=2, which is impossible for a triangle X. Therefore k is nonnegative.

A nonnegative mass-nine field cannot have a double, since a neighbour of a double cannot be zero in charge one; all fourteen neighbours nonzero would exceed the mass. Thus k is an actual rook-grid indicator. To reduce the doubled center i it must include i in X, and at least two of the six original near neighbours must also be in X to yield degree four at i. This is necessarily a five-site input, with X consisting of i and exactly two original near units; Y lies off the support of s to avoid a double and neither Y site is adjacent to i (its grid degree is already four). Each removed near unit outside the grid sees i in the grid and so cannot see either Y site by unique exterior grid contact. Thus e(X,Y)=0 and cost 79 forces e(X)=2 and e(Y)=0. The two removed near units were nonmated and both had degree two inside the s support. Removing them leaves a seven-set with 12−4=8 edges. But the final grid minus the two nonadjacent Y sites must have 18−8=10 edges. This contradiction excludes the small input. The ordinary cubic ten-support marks avoid the same reference population by the already proved separate argument.

## One bucket for three adjacent complementary shells

Every ordinary cubic ten-set is connected: each cubic component has even order at least six, since a four-site cubic component would be a K4. Group the weighted supports and ordinary cubic ten-sets by their shared original old mark w(s). All have mass ten and equal marks exactly when their h shadows agree. On any one support the common h is zero and all support weights are positive. If two distinct connected supports overlapped, any site of one outside the other would have no neighbour in the other (by h=0), separating it from their common sites. Strict containment is excluded by the same argument with the roles reversed. Thus the supports are either equal or disjoint and anticomplete. Equal supports cannot have different weights here: ordinary supports have ten sites, while weighted supports have nine with unique degree-six center.

The common h has first moment 110 and second moment 200: sum (A−3I)s=11·10 and use (A−3I)^2=−7(A−3I)+2J with s·h=0 for the second. For nonnegative integer h, 2·1_{h>0}≥3h−h², so at least 65 graph sites have positive h and hence lie off every member support. A class's support volume is at most 34. Each member uses at least nine graph sites, hence there are at most three members in each bucket. All buckets avoid the mandatory 29360331 old marks, proving the two inequalities and the fixed floor.

If a bucket contains a ordinary ten-supports and b weighted ten-mass supports, it contributes exactly a(a−1) oriented anticomplete unit +3 norm-20 differences of the ordinary kind, 2ab oriented anticomplete norm-22 differences of the mixed kind, and b(b−1) oriented anticomplete norm-24 differences of the weighted kind. The norms are 10+10, 10+12, 12+12 because the supports are disjoint; their differences are actual +3 eigenvectors by equality of h. In each counted subtype the literal signs recover the ordered supports. The mixed differences, oriented with negative doubled pivot, give distinct fields of F^+(2,24) with one double via the [complementary norm-22 dictionary](plus22-twelve-wing-unions.md); this is specifically the anticomplete subtype, not a formula for that entire affine population. All three contributions compete for the same original norm-79 extra buckets; they are not separate counts assumed independently saturable.
