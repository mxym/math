# An active charge-two square determines a full 84-site root row

Use the [c=0 positive-pair classification](u226-positive-pair.md). All assertions below apply at every rank to the actual c=0 subtype; having the other subtypes in the graph does not change them.

Fix a positive root p of b. Its 14 neighbors L are seven mate pairs: write bar(i) for i's mate. Label the 84 remaining sites y_ij by unordered nonmate pairs in L, using their second common neighbor besides p. This is a bijection: every site outside the closed neighborhood of p meets L twice; mates already share p on their edge. Suppose q=y_e is b's other positive and e={z,w} the two common zeros at p and q. The six zeros S in L are independent, comprising one member of six mate pairs. Write U=S minus e, S'=bar(S), and O=L minus (S union S'), the one omitted mate pair.

**Full field and actual graph row.** The negative sites of b in L are exactly the eight outside S. Its 16 far negative labels y_ij are the disjoint edges of three graphs on L:

1. the eight pairs e times U;
2. a two-edge perfect matching on U;
3. a perfect matching from S to S', with no pair of mates.

All eight sites of (1) are the isolated negative sites not touching p or q; all eight sites of (2),(3) are neighbors of q. Furthermore, the complete set of twelve far neighbors of q consists of (2),(3) and four extra pairs: each member of O is paired to two members of bar(U), and all four members of bar(U) occur once. Thus one of just six two-plus-two assignments completes q's actual graph row once b's matchings are given. Nothing claims an arbitrary choice of these matchings extends.

To see this, the c=0 classification identifies the isolated eight with e times U. The unique negative-negative mate edge at q uses two of its eight exclusive negative neighbors; each of these two has both p contacts in U (its only negative neighbor is already its mate, and a common zero is mated at q to a different, non-internally-mated vertex). These two p-pairs are disjoint: if the adjacent vertices shared a p-neighbor, their edge would already have a second common neighbor besides q. Hence they form (2). To verify (3), the p-neighbor counts are exact. At a zero i in S, the equations demand 5+[i in e] negative neighbors, including its negative p-mate, so the far-negative degree of i is 4+[i in e]. At each member of S' the far-negative degree is one, at each member of O it is zero (its p-mate is already negative). Within S the first two sets (1),(2) consume four incidences per endpoint of e and three per member of U, leaving one per member of S. The remaining six far negatives are exclusive neighbors of q with their unique negative neighbor also a p-neighbor. Therefore they each touch S' and fill the indicated matching; an alternative counting proof is that the two internally mated B sites and all eight isolated sites exhaust all far negatives with both p contacts zero, whereas the other six B sites have precisely one negative p contact. P-mate pairs have no far label.

For each i in L the number of q's far neighbors containing i is exactly 2−[i or bar(i) belongs to e]: apply the common-neighbor equation to q,i and subtract the potential p-mate of i that already sees q. After (2),(3) the residual degrees are zero on S and bar(e), one on bar(U), and two on each member of O. O is a mate pair, so its two members cannot be joined. This forces the four extra pairs as stated.

**Matching on fixed root zeros.** Holding p and S fixed, the active e's form a matching on S. Suppose e={z,a}, e'={z,a'} overlap. Since a' lies in U, q'=y_e' is a negative isolated vertex for b, and q is similarly isolated and negative for b'. Thus q,q' are nonadjacent. On S, the two far-negative graphs of degrees (1),(2) share at least six distinct pairs: their cuts share the three pairs from z to the untouched three vertices and {a,a'}, and the U-matching partner of a' for b and that of a for b' give two further pairs in the other cut. At p and its eight negative neighbors the fields agree. Their only opposing signs are q and q'. Thus b dot b' >= 9+6−2=13, so the neutral difference t has norm at most 26 and has t_q=2,t_q'=−2. For d_i=(3I−A)e_i we have (d_q−d_q')²=42 since the sites are nonadjacent, and t dot (d_q−d_q')=28. Therefore t−d_q+d_q' has squared norm at most 12, below the neutral minimum 14, but has entry −1 at q. Contradiction.

**Two ends of a shared edge.** Fix a true edge pz with cap a. Denote its twelve left-wing sites N(p) minus {z,a} by w_i and index its twelve right-wing sites in N(z) minus {p,a} by r_i=y_{z,w_i}; w_i to r_i is a perfect matching. Let l and r be the mate involutions on those left and right indices. The twelve vertices in N(a) minus {p,z} are matched both to the left and to the right wings: define tau(i) to be the left mate-index reached by following the unique edge from r_i to this third wing and then its edge to the left. These cross-wing bijections follow from the two-common-neighbor axiom.

For the square indexed by i, take the two positive diagonals in turn, using b and b'=D_Q−b (Q={p,z,r_i,w_i}). Then S at p consists of z and five left vertices indexed by X_i; the zero-neighbor set at z of b' consists of p and the same five right indices. Indeed for j in X_i minus {i}, r_j is an isolated negative of b touching z and no other corner, so b'(r_j)=0. Also b'(r_i)=0. These five together with p already exhaust the six slots. Hence X_i is independent for both involutions l and r. The matching from S to S' above pairs z to the other p-index of the unique far neighbor of q=r_i containing z, namely r_{r(i)}; so l(r(i)) is in X_i. Similarly r(l(i)) belongs to X_i. The unique far-neighbor pair containing a=bar(z) has other endpoint in S by (3); this index is tau(i). By reversal tau^{-1}(i) also belongs to X_i. In all
```
 { i, l(r(i)), r(l(i)), tau(i), tau^{-1}(i) } ⊆ X_i,
 |X_i|=5,  X_i independent in both left- and right-wing mate matchings.
```
For different active squares on this pz edge, the X_i are distinct by the preceding matching theorem. Thus the [rank-38 three-active-square pressure](u226-rank38-forcing.md) implies three distinct such simultaneous five-sets for one true edge if the selected types are confined to (0,18),(0,24),(2,26). This does not exclude that support.
