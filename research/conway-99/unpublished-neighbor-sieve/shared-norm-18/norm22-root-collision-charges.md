# How many repeated roots can be charged to one positive diagonal?

Under the support and conventions of [the norm-22 root capacity](norm18-norm22-root-capacity.md), call a collision an unordered pair of distinct seeds at the same (r,o), with r oriented. Consider just the m=2 collisions. Their total is at most **6G226**.

The earlier proof gives b of type (2,26) with positives o,x, common zero pair Z and four p-exclusive zero neighbors U at o. Write Q=Z∪J, Q′=Z∪(U\J), |J|=2. The common sum z=d_o+d_x−b has norm 32 and is doubled positive at o,x. At either seed Q its three fields mark each of the six available pair labels exactly twice, so the two t,u positive at x (the pair label from Z) are determined. They have dot seven. Their partners at Q′ have to be z−t,z−u. Thus at a fixed (b,o) and partition of U there is at most one collision up to r↔−r.

At x these same four fields are also positive. At either hub, each pair t,z−t has both sources negative on Z, and disjoint negative subsets of size two partitioning the four x-exclusive zero neighbors V; this is forced by the values of z on N(x) (−2 on Z, −1 on V, zero elsewhere; b has its known c=0 shape). The fields t,u have no common negatives except Q (their root is a degree-three all-seven seed); x, the far-site label of Z at o, meets Q only at Z. Thus t and u use disjoint two-subsets K and V\K of V. The same four sources form a two-by-two array at the two hubs: t and z−u share the x-seed Z∪K, u and z−t the complementary x-seed. Their common dot is 16−7=9, since t·z=u·z=16. Each occupied x-seed therefore has exactly two sources: a third would violate the all-seven theorem. In particular this partition of V cannot itself give a collision with x as the triple root. Nor can a second partition of U map to it: its four labels would have to repeat the two sources at each of these exact x-seeds, which determine their original partition at o. Thus the number of collision partitions at o plus the number at x is at most three. Restoring both orientations gives the claimed uniform bound.

There is also a cancellation independent of this bound. Write H_h for the total number of (r′,o) over oriented N22 with exterior height h. The exact zero-height count is Σ_r n0(r)=11N22−Σ_{h≥3} binom(h−1,2) H_h. In an m=0 collision the secondary marking r′=t_Q−t_Q′=u_Q−u_Q′ is unit N22 and at o has height four. Its orientation recovers the two ordered seeds from its negative and positive sites in N(o). For this r′ and ordered seed pair, translations by r′ match at most three of the first triple's sources with the second triple. Any two matched edges account for one unordered collision up to r-sign; there are at most three choices. Counting both orientations of r′ and r, summing cancels every m=0 collision against 3H4. In detail, for each antipodal r′ choose the orientation that gives a fixed ordering of Q,Q′; the three unordered choices give at most six signed r, equally covered by both r′ orientations with weight three each.

Let T be the count of degree-three seeds and C1 the total number of collisions at intersection size one. If there are k qualifying seeds at (r,o), then k ≤ 1 + k(k−1)/2. Sum over the zero-height roots, cancel the intersection-zero collisions against the height-four debit, and apply the bound for intersection size two. This gives `6T ≤ 11N22 + C1 + 6G226`. It does not yet handle C1 or imply the desired upper 15 per marking.
