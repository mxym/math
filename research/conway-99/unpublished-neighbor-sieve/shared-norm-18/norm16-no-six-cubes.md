# No six matched cubes; at most four good rooted norm-16 extensions

> **Stronger cap:** [The good–bad overlap argument](norm16-four-good-bad.md) improves the graph-wide bound to **N16 ≤ 5,196** whenever N20=0, without a rank assumption.

All statements concern a hypothetical SRG(99,14,1,2), at any Seidel rank. Use the definition of a good field at a rooted independent triple \((o,T)\), and the unconditional common-cube construction in [Packing good norm-16 triples by their common cube](norm16-good-triple-cube.md). The absence of norm 20 is needed only for the **last population consequence**, not for either geometric exclusion below.

**Theorem.** There cannot be seven disjoint induced 3-cubes with their bipartite signs oriented so that every pair of cubes is joined by a sign-preserving bijective matching, with no other cross edges. If five or more norm-16 fields are good at a fixed rooted triple, they yield that forbidden configuration. Thus at most four fields are good at any rooted triple. In particular, when \(N_{20}=0\), the graph-wide oriented count is \(N_{16}\le 6062\).

## Seven matched cubes are impossible

A site outside an induced cube has at most one neighbour of each cube sign, because two same-sign sites in the cube already have two common neighbours in that cube. Fix one site \(p\) of the proposed seven-cube configuration and let \(p'\) be its opposite-sign antipode within its cube.

There are nine neighbours of \(p\) in the cube union: three own-cube sites of opposite sign and one same-sign site in each of the six other cubes. The three own-cube edges through \(p\) have no caps within the cube union: none within the cube itself, and no other cube can supply two contacts to the fixed cube. Each therefore has its unique cap outside all seven cubes, and these three caps are distinct because an outside site has at most one neighbour of the opposite sign in the fixed cube. Likewise the nonadjacent pair \(p,p'\) has no common neighbour inside the union, and both of its two common witnesses are outside. These two sites are distinct from the three edge caps by the same opposite-sign contact rule. They are not adjacent to each other, since they share both \(p\) and \(p'\); adjacent vertices have only one common neighbour. This accounts for all five neighbours of \(p\) outside the union.

The fourteen neighbours of any vertex induce a matching of seven edges. In the matching at \(p\), its three own-cube neighbours pair with their three caps. The two remaining exterior neighbours cannot pair each other, so each pairs with one of the six same-sign neighbours. The remaining four same-sign neighbours form two pairs. Thus every vertex in the 28-site positive shore lies in precisely two triangles entirely within that shore. Counting vertex–triangle incidences there gives 56, which is not divisible by three. This is a contradiction.

## Five good fields force a seventh cube

If \(g\ge4\) fields are good at \((o,T)\), the [common-cube argument](norm16-good-triple-cube.md#four-good-fields-force-a-cube) produces one base cube \(Q_0\) and \(g\) pairwise disjoint complementary cubes \(Q_i\), all disjoint from \(Q_0\). Each field consists exactly of \(Q_0,Q_i\) with their two shores oriented so that the induced matching between them reverses signs. Let \(z_0\) be the sign vector of the base cube, and orient each \(z_i\) for \(i>0\) with the opposite of its sign in the corresponding field. Thus \(z_0-z_i\) is a neutral \(-4\) field, and the base-complementary matchings preserve the signs \(z_i\).

For \(i,j>0,\ i\ne j\), a point \(p\in Q_j\) sees exactly one base site of the same oriented sign. The eigen-equation for \(z_0-z_i\), which is zero at \(p\), gives \((Az_i)_p=(Az_0)_p\). There is at most one neighbour of each sign in \(Q_i\); since this signed sum is \(\pm1\), there is exactly one neighbour and it has the sign of \(p\). This proves the sign-preserving bijective matching for every pair of cubes. If six fields are good, the first part already contradicts the resulting seven cubes.

It suffices to take five of the good fields. Put \(z=\sum_{i=0}^5 z_i\). At any point \(x\) outside these six cubes the signed contact sums \((Az_i)_x\) are all equal; denote this common value by \(r_x\). Define \(r=0\) on the six cubes. A nonzero \(r_x\) is precisely a singleton contact into each cube, with the same sign for all contacts, because any outside site can see at most one of each sign. Each cube site has eleven noncube contacts, of which precisely five go to exterior double-contact sites (three edge caps, two antipode witnesses), as in the first part. Of the six singleton contacts, five go to the other five cubes. Therefore \(r\) has exactly eight nonzero entries: each base cube site has a unique singleton contact to a distinct point outside the six cubes, and conversely. Four of these entries are positive and four negative. Moreover their support \(R\) is perfectly matched sign-preservingly to every cube.

For every \(i\), direct evaluation inside each cube and at exterior points gives \((A+4I)z_i=z+r\). Since \(z_i\) has sum zero, the strong regularity identity \((A-3I)(A+4I)=2J\) makes \(z+r\) a \(+3\) eigenvector. Summing \((A+4I)z_i=z+r\) yields \(Az=2z+6r\), and then \(Ar=z-3r\). Thus at every point of \(R\), the number of opposite-sign neighbours inside \(R\) is three more than the number of same-sign neighbours there. If there were a same-sign edge, both its ends would see all four sites of the other sign inside \(R\), contrary to the unique common neighbour of an edge. Hence the induced graph on \(R\) is 3-regular bipartite, four sites per side. It is the complement in \(K_{4,4}\) of a perfect matching, hence exactly a 3-cube. The forbidden seven-cube configuration now results.

The argument used only the common-cube construction, the strong regularity identities, and actual sign supports, not \(N_{20}=0\).

## Oriented population consequence

Assume \(N_{20}=0\). Keep the notation \(m\) for the number of extensions of a rooted triple and \(g\) for its good count. The [original rooted bound](norm16-good-triple-cube.md#bad-fields-cannot-fill-the-remaining-choices) says \(m\le8\) always and \(m\le6\) if \(g\ge4\), under this shell absence. With \(g\le4\) just proved, it follows that \(m+2g\le14\): if \(g\le3\) use \(m\le8\), and otherwise \(g=4,m\le6\). Each oriented norm-16 field contributes 32 to the sum of \(m\) and at least 16 to the sum of \(g\), over the 27,720 rooted independent triples. Thus \(64N_{16}\le14\cdot27720=388080\). The count \(N_{16}\) is even by negation, so \(N_{16}\le6062\). This improves the earlier 6496; it does not assume the twelve-shell support has any specified form.
