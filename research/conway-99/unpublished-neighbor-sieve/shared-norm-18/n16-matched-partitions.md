# Every signed norm-sixteen support is a matched pair of cubes

Let \(t\) be an integral neutral \(-4\) field of norm sixteen in a hypothetical Conway graph. Its support is bipartite \(4\)-regular, with two independent shores of eight each. Use the exact list of five canonical cross matrices from [the signed norm-sixteen support classification](n16-five-shore-cores.md).

**Theorem.** The support of any \(t\) admits a partition into two induced 3-cubes whose cross edges form a perfect matching. In the canonical deck order \(8,\ 6+2,\ 4+4,\ 4+2+2,\ 2+2+2+2\), the numbers of unordered such partitions are \(1,1,2,2,4\), respectively. No additional shell-absence or Seidel-rank hypothesis is used.

In the five fixed matrices, take any four positive and any four negative sites for the first cube. It suffices to use the half containing the first positive site. Require every chosen site to have exactly three neighbours in the chosen half and every unchosen site to have exactly three neighbours in the complementary half. Each induced half is then a cubic bipartite graph on \(4+4\), hence the complement of a matching in \(K_{4,4}\), which is \(Q_3\). The \(4\)-regular full support makes the cross edges a perfect matching. Conversely any partition of the claimed kind has a half in this list, as either cube uses precisely four sites of each shore. Reading the canonical column masks as eight-byte rows for the positive-shore incidence, the [standard-library checker](n16-matched-partitions.py) tests at most \(\binom73\binom84=2450\) choices per case. It outputs the four-site positive and negative bitmasks of the half containing the first positive.

## Genuine cube shadows

For any induced 3-cube \(Q\) write \(z_Q\) for one of its two oriented bipartite sign vectors, and \(h_Q=(A+4I)z_Q\). The shadow is an integral \(+3\) field. Its restriction to \(Q\) is \(z_Q\). Outside \(Q\) it is \(\pm1\) at singleton-contact sites, and zero otherwise: there is at most one contact per cube sign. The cube's twelve edges have twelve distinct exterior caps, and its four opposite-sign antipodal pairs have eight distinct exterior witnesses. These twenty double-contact sites consume forty of the \(8(14-3)=88\) exterior contacts. There are therefore exactly 48 singleton-contact sites and \(h_Q^2=56\).

If two different signed cubes have the same shadow, their signs agree on the overlap (since \(h_Q=z_Q\) on the cube). Their difference is a neutral eigenfield of norm \(16-2|Q\cap Q'|\). A nonempty overlap thus has size one, as the minimum is 14. It would give a Fano difference whose common site sees three vertices of each sign, impossible by the Fano exterior rule. Thus equal-shadow cubes are disjoint. Their cross edges form a bijective matching preserving the shadow signs: at any site of one cube, the signed contact sum into the other cube is the fixed shadow value \(\pm1\); at most one of each parity is possible, so there is exactly one contact of that sign. The [six-matched-cube exclusion](norm16-no-six-cubes.md) implies that a common oriented shadow has at most five cubes. Conversely, every matched partition of a norm-sixteen support produces such an equal-shadow pair by using on one cube the field signs and on the other the negative field signs. Their difference is the original neutral field.

## One balanced overlap type in a norm-eighteen pair

Let \(t,u\) be oriented norm-eighteen fields with \(t-u=f\) of norm sixteen. They have no opposite signs at a common support site, because \(f\) is a unit field. The size of their total shared support is ten. Denote the common signed part by \(c\), and the private eight-site supports by \(X=\operatorname{supp}(t-c)\) and \(Y=\operatorname{supp}(u-c)\), which partition \(\operatorname{supp}(f)\). Suppose the shared support has five sites of each sign, and some positive root \(o\) and some negative root \(o'\) in it both have zero exterior \(f\)-height. Suppose also that the four common negative neighbours \(Q\) of \(o\) have total \(f\)-height four. This is a conditional classification of actual pairs.

At a vertex \(x\in X\), all its common-shore neighbours have sign opposite its nonzero field sign, since the norm-eighteen support is bipartite. Its \(-4\) row gives \(d_{\operatorname{supp}(c)}(x)=4-d_X(x)=d_Y(x)\); similarly for \(Y\). Summing over all private vertices counts twice the size of the cut. The same sum over common vertices is twice their total \(f\)-height; the heights on the two common shores sum to the same total by comparing the edge count of the common bipartite graph from both shores (each common vertex has internal degree \(4-h_f\)). Since \(Q\) is all of the common negative shore except \(o'\), this gives exactly eight cut edges.

An \(8|8\) cut of size eight in any of the matched-cube supports must itself split into two cubes. To see this, use any one matched decomposition and let \(a\le4\) denote the smaller intersection size of one chosen half with either old cube; its intersection with the other cube is \(8-a\). The minimum internal-cut sizes in a cube at sizes \(a=0,1,2,3,4\) are \(0,3,4,5,4\), respectively, from the maximum induced edge counts \(0,0,1,2,4\). The matching contributes at least \(8-2a\) edges. Thus \(a=1,2,3\) would already give at least twelve edges in total. At \(a=0\) it is the existing decomposition. At \(a=4\) both chosen parts are square faces (four edges each), the unchosen parts opposite faces, and the old matching has no crossings between halves, so the matching within each new half joins two squares and gives a new 3-cube.

If \(o\) is isolated among the zero-height sites of \(f\), then the common-support graph off \(o,o'\) is a single 8-cycle. In that case all four members of \(Q\) have height one (there are no zero-height neighbours at \(o\) and the heights sum to four). Each has internal common degree three, one edge of which goes to \(o\); similarly \(o'\) is adjacent to all four other common positives (there can be no edge to \(o\), whose four common neighbours are already \(Q\)). Each nonroot common positive must see exactly two members of \(Q\): it sees at most two by the common-neighbour cap with \(o\), and the four \(Q\)-sites require eight such incidences. Thus the induced graph on the remaining eight sites is 2-regular bipartite, and cannot contain a 4-cycle because any two of its negative vertices already share \(o\). It is therefore an 8-cycle. No claim about that precise cycle shape is made when \(o\) is nonisolated among the zeros.

Write \(z_X=t|_X\) and \(z_Y=u|_Y\), extended by zero. The difference \(z_X-z_Y=f\) shows they have the same cube shadow. If a third cube \(Q_j\) lies in that shadow class, the field \(z_{Q_j}+c\) is again a neutral integral eigenfield. On an overlap of \(Q_j\) with \(c\) the signs cancel: at a common site \(x\), being in \(Q_j\) means the shared shadow there is \(\pm1\) and \(x\) has a singleton contact into \(X\) of the same shadow sign. This unique contact is opposite to \(c_x\) by bipartiteness of the \(t\) support. Consequently the new field has norm \(18-2|Q_j\cap\operatorname{supp}(c)|\). It is nonzero because \(c\) has ten sites and the cube only eight. At rank 36, where [the Fano shell is empty](../rank36-no-fano.md), overlap size is thus at most one, and overlap one gives an actual norm-sixteen field. This last restriction does not assert that any such overlap or balanced overlap type is forced.
