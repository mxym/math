# At a four-good norm-16 triple, every bad companion forces norm 20

All statements are graph-wide and independent of the Seidel rank. Let \(m\) count the oriented norm-16 fields on a fixed rooted independent triple \((o,T)\) and let \(g\) be their good count (three prescribed far pair centers positive), with the convention of [Packing good norm-16 triples by their common cube](norm16-good-triple-cube.md). Use the preceding [matched-cube exclusion](norm16-no-six-cubes.md), which gives \(g\le4\).

**Theorem.** If \(g=4\), each bad companion \(b\) has dot product six against at least one of the four good fields, and their difference is a unit norm-20 field. Together with the three-good counting argument below, when \(N_{20}=0\) the graph-wide oriented count satisfies \(N_{16}\le5196\). This supersedes 6062, and requires no other shell absence.

**Proof.** The four good fields give five disjoint cubes \(Q_0,Q_1,\ldots,Q_4\), with \(Q_0\) the shared cube, by the unconditional common-cube argument. The good field for \(Q_i\) is \(t_i=z_0-z_i\), where \(z_i\) is the binary parity sign vector supported on its cube, and all pairwise matchings preserve these displayed signs. As in the matched-cube proof, \(h=(A+4I)z_0\) is a \(+3\) field and equals \(z_0+\cdots+z_4+r\). Here \(r\) is zero on the cubes and elsewhere is the common signed contact sum into each cube, in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Its nonzero values occur exactly where the exterior site has one neighbour in each cube, of the displayed sign.

The bad field \(b\) has exactly three positive entries in \(Q_0\), the root \(o\) and two of the three prescribed pair centers. It cannot use all four cube-negative entries, since their required four positive neighbours each would give 16 incidences. Exactly nine of those incidences would come from the three positive cube sites, since every cube site has internal degree three. Its only other five positive sites can contribute at most five in total, since each exterior site sees at most one cube-negative. Thus \(b\) uses exactly three base-cube negatives, namely \(T\).

The [rooted overlap calculation](norm16-good-triple-cube.md#triple-geometry) proves that \(b\) has no opposite-sign conflicts with any good field and has dot product at least six. The [norm-16 overlap theorem](../norm16-packing.md#a-bound-on-the-overlap-of-two-norm-sixteen-vectors) says that any pair with one same-sign intersection at least four has both exactly four, and excludes dot seven. Thus the only options are 6 and 8. Suppose they are all eight. Since the shared cube contributes exactly three sites of each sign, \(b\) uses precisely one site of each sign in \(Q_i\) for every \(i=1,\ldots,4\), and these signs are opposite to \(z_i\) (they agree with \(t_i\)). Therefore the inner product of \(b\) with \(h\) over the five cubes is \(6-8=-2\). It has two remaining nonzero sites off the cubes. Since \(b\) is a \(-4\) eigenvector and \(h\) is a \(+3\) eigenvector, the total inner product vanishes; those two sites must both give \(b_xr_x=1\).

One of those remaining sites is the fourth negative neighbour \(x\) of \(o\) in \(b\). The fourth neighbour lies outside \(Q_0\), whose only neighbours of \(o\) are \(T\). Nor can it belong to any of the four complementary cubes: each has only its unique matching neighbour of \(o\), the fourth parent of the corresponding good field, and [rooted four-neighbour uniqueness](../norm16-packing.md#one-vector-per-four-neighbors) forbids sharing it. If \(r_x\ne0\) then \(x\) has a singleton contact to \(Q_0\), which must be \(o\), of positive sign. Thus \(b_xr_x=-1\), contrary to the conclusion that both remaining sites contribute \(+1\). At least one dot product is therefore six; its difference gives a signed-unit norm 20 because the supports have no conflicts.

## Three-good counting needs no shared negative cube vertex

Assume \(N_{20}=0\). Fix the positive root and its three negative parents \(T\). Let \(F\) be the root and the three pair centers. For a good field \(i\), let \(U_i\) be its four positive sites outside \(F\), and let \(x_i\) be its fourth negative parent. The \(U_i\)'s are pairwise disjoint: two proper good fields already share \(F\) and the norm-16 overlap theorem forces precisely four common positives. Fourth parents are pairwise distinct by rooted four-neighbor uniqueness.

Each proper good field has exactly three distinct members of \(U_i\) meeting \(T\). In fact each parent of \(T\) already sees, in \(F\), the root and two pair centers, and must have one further positive neighbour; another far site sees at most one parent of \(T\), because all three double-contact sites of those parents are the prescribed pair centers. At least two of these three further neighbours have far pair labels \(\{a,x_i\}\) for \(a\in T\): each field uses at least five of the six distinct centers indexed by the pairs of its four negative parents, including here all three pairs in \(T\). Thus \(U_i\) supplies at most one member labeled \(\{a,x\}\), where \(x\) is the fourth negative parent of **any** bad field.

A bad field has exactly five positive sites outside \(F\). Because the bad and each good have dot product eight when \(N_{20}=0\), they have precisely four common positives. The bad therefore selects exactly one site from each \(U_i\), besides its fixed three sites in \(F\). It includes all three distinctly labeled sites \(\{a,x\}\), \(a\in T\), since it uses at least five of its six pair centers but misses a pair center internal to \(T\). At least \(g-2\) of those three labeled sites therefore belong to \(\bigcup_i U_i\). Distinct bad fourth parents give disjoint sets of such labels, so if \(b=m-g\) is the bad count, then \(b(g-2)\le g\). This proof works for \(g=3\) as well as \(g\ge4\), without assuming a shared negative cube vertex. In particular \(m\le6\) if \(g=3\).

When \(g=4\), the preceding four-good theorem gives \(m=g=4\). When \(g\le2\), rooted four-neighbor uniqueness gives \(m\le8\). Thus in all cases \(m+2g\le12\). Each oriented norm-16 field contributes 32 to \(\sum m\) and at least 16 to \(\sum g\), over 27,720 rooted independent triples. Therefore \(64N_{16}\le332640\). Negation makes the count even, giving \(N_{16}\le5196\).
