# A two-edge positive support has a unique complementary pair

In any SRG(99,14,1,2), write D=3I−A and d_v=D e_v. A positive-charge signed-unit (2,30) field b has four positive sites P, 26 negative sites N and (A+4I)b=−4·1. Assume P spans two disjoint edges, hence its entire induced graph is the matching. Then **either no field has these four positive sites, or exactly two fields do**, interchanged by b↦D1_P−b. This statement does not require a Seidel rank, any absent shell, or any automorphism.

Write h(v)=|N(v)∩P|. Each p∈P sees nine negatives. For n∈N, the number of its negative neighbors is h(n). The total number of pair witnesses for pairs within P is 2·6−2=10, none internal to P. Since Σ_N h=36, the identity binom(h,2)−h+1=(h−1)(h−2)/2≥0 gives Σ_N binom(h,2)≥10. Equality exhausts all pair witnesses. Thus every member of N sees exactly one or two P-sites; no outside site sees more than two; and the ten sites T seeing two are forced negatives. For p∈P, its unique pair witness with its partner and its two pair witnesses with each of the other two positives give five distinct T-neighbors. There are therefore eight further *exclusive* neighbors meeting P just in p; exactly four are negative. The four such eight-site sets are disjoint. Every vertex outside P, T and the four exclusive sets is zero.

The vector D1_P is an actual charge-four mark and equals 2 on P, −2 on T, −1 on those 32 exclusive sites, zero elsewhere. Subtract b: the result b' is charge two and signed-unit with the same positive and double-contact sites, while its negative choices in each exclusive octad are complementary. It has the same mass and norm as b, and is distinct.

For uniqueness suppose there were a further solution c. Replace c by its complement if necessary; the nonzero neutral signed-unit field z=b−c then has support size m≤16, all in the 32 exclusive sites. The standard neutral minimum is 14 and z is balanced, so m is 14 or 16. For m=14 the two Fano signs are independent and every same-sign pair has both common-neighbor slots already occupied by opposite-sign sites; equivalently count 2·binom(7,2)=7·binom(4,2). Any outside zero therefore sees at most one site of each sign. The four (zero) hubs P together see at most eight sites of supp z, but every one of the 14 sees its exclusive hub, contradiction. For m=16, every outside zero of norm16 has height at most two per sign. Therefore all four hubs must have exactly two per sign: 16 exclusive incidences use the full capacity 4·4. But the four hubs span two edges, contradicting the [unconditional norm-16 height-two edge bound](norm16-height-two-edge.md). This proves uniqueness.

There are additional necessary checks without choosing which of the complementary solutions is first. For v∈T put k=|N(v)∩T|. Both true solutions give v exactly two negative neighbors, so k≤2 and v has exactly 2−k neighbors in *each* of the two complementary 16-sets in the exclusive octads. For an exclusive site s let k=|N(s)∩T|. In the field where it is negative, it has one negative neighbor; in the complementary field where it is zero, it has five. Hence k≤1 and s sees 1−k sites of its own sign class and 5−k of the opposite class within the 32-set. In particular the 32 exclusive sites carry an actual neutral norm-32 field, vanishing elsewhere. Counting edges from T to these 32 sites gives 40−4e(T)≤32, hence e(T)≥2. The two caps of the edges of P cannot touch the other T sites which share one of their two hubs: each edge between a hub and its cap already has its unique triangle. These are necessary tests for the marked support, not proofs of existence or of nonexistence.
