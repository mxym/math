# A fully unit source triple or a large charge-three family

On the rank-42 two-type face, either a single norm-22 center has three decompositions all of whose pair differences are signed-unit norm-28 fields, or the following actual charge-three populations are necessary:
```
S                     2     5     8     11
L=N22/2               ≥174  ≥170  ≥164  ≥160
G(3,33)               ≥292  ≥276  ≥256  ≥240
G(3,35)               ≤16   ≤68   ≤128  ≤180.
```
In the latter alternative, the total number of exposed negative edges across all oriented norm-24 fields is at most 2G(3,35). An exposed negative edge has both endpoints in the negative sign of a fixed field and the unique triangle cap at a zero site of that same field. This counts incidence pairs (field, edge), not distinct graph edges. Counting both signs doubles that number by antipodal symmetry, so at most 4G(3,35) oriented norm-24 fields can have any exposed same-sign edge. For S=2, this is at most 64 fields among at least 1856.

Proof. Use the [norm-22 Johnson graph](norm22-johnson-double-graph.md) and the [nonzero-centroid refinement](nonzero-centroids-two-sided-wedge.md) D+L≥K=291,330,365,404. Without a fully unit source triple, the unit-pair graph on the m≤6 source indices at each q is triangle-free, hence has at most floor(m²/4) edges by Mantel's inequality (equivalently average d_u+d_v≤m over edges and apply Cauchy). If h_q denotes the number of nonunit incident pairs there, then h_q≥C(m,2)−floor(m²/4), which gives C(m,2)≤1+5h_q/2 for m=0,...,6. Every unoriented double line contributes at both endpoints and at most 6S such lines are nonunit, so 2D≤L+30S. Thus L≥2(K−15S)/3; round up to an even integer by the actual q11 parity. The true scalar equations G(3,33)=2L−8(19+S)/3 and G(3,35)=8W335=4(172+3S−L) give the table. G(3,·) counts all actual fields of positive affine charge +3, not including the negated charge −3. No uniformity inside an individual residue class is assumed for these two counts.

For the incidence bound, if T is the graph triangle consisting of an exposed negative edge and its cap in an oriented norm-24 field c, shift to b=c+D_T, with D_T=Σ_{i∈T}(3I−A)e_i. On T the entries of D_T are +1, its squared norm is 39, and 2c·D_T=14Σ_{i∈T}c_i=−28. Thus b is (3,35). Every field of this type has exactly one positive, with value +1, and 34 negatives, all −1: Σ b_i=−33 and Σb_i²=35 allow just this or one −2; adding d_i at a −2 site in the latter case would give a charge-four field of cost 35+23−28+12=42<44. The positive site p of b is exactly the cap of the starting edge; the two edge endpoints become zeros. Conversely, every triangle through p and two b-zeros reconstructs a unique (oriented norm-24 field, marked exposed negative edge) by b−D_T. Calculation: b·D_T=7Σ_T b+6a=7+18=25, so the reconstructed squared norm is 35+39−50=24, with true edge endpoints −1 and cap zero. The positive site p has 10 negative neighbors by Ab=−4b−6·1 and the other four neighbors are zero. Neighbor pairs around p are a matching, so there are at most two zero-zero mate pairs. This proves the exact inverse and upper bound. For S=2 the [scalar two-type equation](higher-moments.md#a-quantitative-target-for-the-remaining-pair) W024−W335=(13/2)n−93=232 and N24=8W024 gives N24≥1856. None of these alternatives alone excludes a tuple.
