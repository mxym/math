# Two matchings from the two extra (1,23) fields

Assume the rank-42 N18/N24 two-type face, S=2, and that there is no source triangle with three unit double differences. Use the [Johnson graph of double norm-28 lines](norm22-johnson-double-graph.md), on L=N22/2 vertices. Its number D of edges [satisfies D+L≥291](nonzero-centroids-two-sided-wedge.md), its local degree is binom(m,2), and L≤178 is even. Both extra signed-unit (1,23) marks u,v have independent six-site positive sides, by the [preceding two-extra-marks result](two-extra-marks-intersecting.md). No rank exclusion is claimed.

Each nonunit norm-28 direction is uniquely indexed by an extra mark u and a positive site p of u: its orientation with +2 at p is c_p=d_p−u, where d_p=(3I−A)e_p. It has no other doubled site: another −2 requires a positive neighbor of p in u, impossible. There are consequently at most twelve nonunit Johnson edges; denote their number by N. For p,r distinct positives of the SAME mark, c_p·c_r=2−9−9+23=7, using that p,r are not adjacent and u·d_p=7u_p+2. At a fixed source q write q=a_i+b_i with all a_i in one signed norm-18 class. The binom(m,2) incident double directions are a_i−a_j. Any two such directions have product, up to sign, 0 or 14 according as their index pairs are disjoint or intersect. Thus the double edges belonging to either one mark form a matching in the Johnson graph. In particular each center has at most two nonunit incident edges (write h for their number). As the remaining index edges cannot contain a unit triangle, m≤4, and at m=4 the two nonunit edges are a disjoint-index matching, one of each mark.

A nonunit edge cannot join two m=4 centers. Indeed, fix its two centers q=a_2+b_2=a_3+b_3 and s=a_2+a_3−q. All additional decompositions at q and s can be written q=a+b and s=a'−b' in the two fixed signed classes. The anchor equations give q·a'=s·a=4+4−11=−3 and q·s=0. Hence b·b'=(q−a)·(a'−s)=−6−a·a'. The products in either signed class for distinct vectors are 4 or −3, and equality of the two vectors would give 18. The only possibility is a·a'=b·b'=−3. This calculation applies to every attempted cross-pair of additional indices; it does not require the other differences to be unit. If both centers had m=4, the second nonunit edge incident at each would be supported on the two additional indices there. The two resulting differences have product zero, by the cross-pair calculation. Yet these two directions are both indexed by the same OTHER extra mark, giving absolute product 7 if distinct and 28 if equal. Contradiction.

Let t be the number of m=4 centers. Consequently 2t≤N. The local inequality binom(m,2)≤1+2h+[m=4] is valid in all cases (at m=3 need h≥1); equality occurs only at (m,h)=(2,0),(3,1),(4,2). Summing gives 2D≤L+4N+t≤L+9N/2≤L+54. Thus 291≤D+L≤3L/2+27 forces L≥176. The only options are L=176,178 and N≥11 (use D≥113 and L≤178).

At L=176 all bounds are equalities: D=115, N=12, t=6. There are six 2-edge paths with valencies m=3,4,3 at their vertices, and 158 additional m=2 centers. These are the nonunit subgraph paths, not components of the whole Johnson graph. All 1733 lines of the norm-28 shell are represented, since at least 1848 pairs occur in the four signed classes and exactly D repeats are possible. Each signed class has its minimal 462 close pairs, and its centroid has norm18. The [scalar bridge](norm22-johnson-positive-mass.md) gives G(3,35)=8. At each of the six middle centers the two nonunit differences with +2 sites p,r of u,v have disjoint index pairs; all other a-indices vanish at each doubled site (a pair cannot agree nontrivially off supp(q), since a_i+a_j−q is unit). Thus c_p(r)=0 and c'_r(p)=0 for c_p=d_p−u, c'_r=d_r−v. The sites p and r are nonadjacent: some a_i+a_j−q takes +1 at both, using one index from each pair. It follows that u_r=v_p=0 and u·v=2. All twelve index lines occur once at an m=4 center. Consequently all six positive sites of either mark are zeros of the other; the actual signed supports of u and v intersect in exactly TWO sites, negative for both. The six center pairs give a bijection between their positive sets by nonadjacent pairs; no assertion is made about the other cross adjacencies.

At L=178, 113≤D≤116. At most D−113 of the 1735 norm-28 lines can be unrepresented. The number of the four centroids attaining norm18 is at least 4−(D−113), since each excess dot-4 pair in a class raises its centroid norm squared by 14. If u·v≠2 one recovers the preceding stronger equality D=113, N=12 and matching structure.

Finally use the actual unique Hoffman coclique C (c=−1_C). Every actual norm-18 field is orthogonal to 1_C, hence so is each represented c_p. Since 1_C·d_p=7[p∈C]−4, the incidence net sum 1_C·u determines whether p lies in C at ALL represented positives of u. There are at least five such sites per mark because N≥11 and each mark has at most six. If they were in C, we would have 1_C·u=3 with at least five C positives, hence at least two C negatives. Each such negative needs at least two positive neighbors by Au=−4u−2·1; yet C is independent and at most one positive lies outside C. Thus each of the two marks has c·u=4, at most one positive on C, and its number of C negatives is four plus its number of C positives. At L=176, or at L=178 if N=12, each has no C positives and exactly four C negatives.
