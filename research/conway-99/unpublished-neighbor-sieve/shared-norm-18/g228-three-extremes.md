# A unit charge-two cost-28 field is extreme at at most three compatible Hoffman cocliques

Let \(\mathcal C\) be a family of actual 22-cocliques in an SRG(99,14,1,2) such that distinct members meet in 1 or 8 points. Let b be any actual charge-two cost-28 field. Then **at most three** C satisfy \(b\cdot c_C=15\), where \(c_C=-1_C\). If some such C contains a positive of b, it is the **only** one. In particular the theorem applies to the full original-zero family at Seidel rank 36, because the seven-pairing gives intersection 1 modulo seven and intersection 15 would produce a norm-14 difference, excluded at that rank. The graph result itself uses no rank assumption. For the unweighted cap of three, intersections of size fifteen are also allowed, as explained below.

Such a field is unit: a positive entry at least two yields a charge-one field of cost ≤15 by subtracting its d_x; a negative entry at most −2 yields a charge-three field of cost ≤31 upon adding d_x. Both are below minima. The three positive sites P and 25 negative sites N obey d_N(v)=d_P(v) on N and d_N(p)=8+d_P(p) on P, by Ab=−4b−4. In particular 2e(N)=24+2e(P)≥24, and Σ_N binom(d_N(v),2)≤6 by counting common neighbors for the three positive pairs. At least 18 of the negatives are nonisolated in G[N]. Also e(P)≤1 for **every** such field: subtracting the sum of the three fields d_x over P would give charge −1 and cost 43−14e(P), impossible for e(P)≥2.

Two extreme C,D must meet in eight: b−c_C−c_D has charge −2 and cost 12+2|C∩D|. At overlap one this violates the absolute mass floor 22. At overlap eight the reflection b'=c_C+c_D−b is again charge two and cost 28 and hence unit. Since b+b' is −2 on C∩D and −1 on the symmetric difference, every site of C∩D is negative for both b,b', and the entire union contains no positive of either. Both C,D therefore have exactly 15 negatives and 7 zeros of b, and their zero subsets are disjoint. The three positives of b' are exactly N\(C∪D). This proves the positive-bearing singleton statement without computation.

## The four bounded cases

Suppose four extremes exist. All six pairs have intersection eight; their four triple intersections have size three or four by the [rank-independent three-coclique Venn theorem](hoffman-triple-eight.md). Let s be the number equal to four and q the quadruple intersection. Every point occurring at least twice is negative. For negative v put k_v=number of these four sets containing it, and n_j=#negative sites of multiplicity j. Then
\[
\sum k_v=60,\qquad \sum\binom{k_v}2=6\cdot8=48,\qquad 3n_0+n_1+n_4=3.
\]
The last identity is the consecutive-integer quadratic tangent at 2 and 3. If n₀>0 then n₀=1 and n₁=n₄=0. Sum and mass then give twelve multiplicity-three negatives. Each can have an internal negative neighbor only at the unique multiplicity-zero site, whose internal degree is at most three. At least nine negatives would be isolated, impossible. Thus N is wholly covered. Inclusion-exclusion on its four 15-point subsets gives 25=60−48+(12+s)−q; hence q=s−1 and 1≤s≤4. In particular there are only four possible labeled Venn decks up to permuting the four sets. Choose the first s omitted index values to indicate triple size four.

Each nonempty membership mask of size at least two is entirely negative. Every singleton-membership cell consists of exactly seven zeros and the remaining negatives; the seven zero sets are disjoint. Outside the union there are three positives and all remaining sites zero. All these cardinalities are fixed. The proof uses the original positive triple and also the six reflected positive triples N\(Cᵢ∪Cⱼ). Each has at most one induced edge by the unit charge-two lemma above.

## Exact local constraints, and their certificates

Index the resulting nonempty cells by their four-set mask and sign of b. For a root in cell u record the counts y_i of its neighbors in the cells. Include every nonnegative feasible y with total 14, bounded by the individual cell sizes less the root, and with total contacts to each of the four C equal to 0 or 4 according as the root belongs to it or not. Omit only types failing the exact charge eigenvalue equation Σ sign(i)y_i=−4sign(u)−4, or having more than one neighbor in a known positive triple containing the root. The 14 neighbor masks must also admit a perfect matching in which the masks at the two ends of each edge are disjoint: otherwise their edge lies in one of the four independent C. This is only a necessary matching test. The verifier enumerates macro-mask counts first, then all possible signed-cell splits with exact atom capacities. In particular positive and zero outside sites are not conflated in the graph equations.

Write n_i for the literal cell sizes. For root-type multiplicities x_(u,y), require:
* mass n_i in each cell;
* one edge count in each direction between every two distinct cells;
* for every unordered pair i,j (including i=j), total Σ x_(u,y)[2y_i y_j+1_(u=i)y_j+1_(u=j)y_i] = 2[2n_i n_j+12n_i 1_(i=j)]. This is A²+A=12I+2J with both directed cross-edge corrections; the formula is valid on the diagonal;
* total count of positive-edge endpoints over the seven indicated triples ≤14. If some reflected positive sets repeat, summing the seven separate bounds remains valid. Equivalently add a nonnegative slack.

The [standard-library exact checker](g228-three-extremes.py) supplies integer combinations of these rows. For compactness the equality-row weights are orbit sums under permutations preserving the particular four triple sizes; signed edge rows transform with their induced orientation. The test evaluates the resulting coefficient on **every literal integer star** in every cell, without quotienting the roots or star vectors and without assuming any graph symmetry. The weight of the optional inequality slack is nonnegative. The coefficient of every possible multiplicity is nonnegative, but the four weighted right sides are −2382, −5184, −86454, and −57144 for (s,q)=(1,0),(2,1),(3,2),(4,3). Therefore four extreme cocliques cannot occur. This uses only exact bounded Python integer operations in the verifier. The four cases check 9,914, 8,946, 5,716, and 3,038 signed stars respectively. A separate enumeration with the signed cells as direct variables gives the same counts and exact minimum column coefficients 0, 0, 2, 0.

## Consequences for the whole original class

The unweighted cap of three also holds in any family with all intersections in \(\{1,8,15\}\), including the full original zero class at any rank. If two extreme cocliques intersect in fifteen, every third compatible coclique meets each of them in just one, by the [Fano endpoint intersection table](../../marked-coset-shells/README.md). But overlap one is impossible for any two extreme cocliques, by the charge-minus-two mass bound above. Thus such an extreme pair is the entire extreme family. Otherwise all extreme pairs meet in eight and the four-case certificate applies. The positive-bearing singleton conclusion above is only asserted for families with no fifteen-overlap.

For uniform averaging over M>0 original cocliques the same H eligibility and same folded normalization give M·K020(−7)=M·K228(15)≤3W228, by the literal bijection t=b−c_C (charge zero, cost20, dot −7 with c_C). Conversely b=t+c_C. In the no-fifteen-overlap case, if K⁺ is the subset of the extreme incidence in which C contains a positive of b, also M[K228(15)+2K⁺]≤3W228. Neither an angular count for a single selected C nor one conditionally sampled along a high-overlap edge can replace the uniform count in this inequality. The three-extreme relaxation alone has not been proved impossible, nor has a graph realizing three been constructed.
