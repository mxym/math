# A higher-degree shell identity for all four ranks

Among the twelve [short unit types](../short-shell-moments.md#when-the-nonzero-labels-must-all-be-signed-units), omit the norm-18 eigenvectors and the type $(1,25)$. The remaining ten account for at least $2072p/475$ oriented graph labelings, where $p=7^{(44-r)/2}+1$. In particular **the norm-18 and $(1,25)$ types cannot be the only two**. Together with the [norm-16 switch](exclusion.md), the only two-type possibility at ranks 38, 40 and 42 is the pair of norm-18 and norm-24 eigenvectors. At rank 36 at least three types must occur. The subsequent [three-label exclusion](panel-heights.md#the-three-label-exclusion-and-the-rank-38-contradiction) eliminates this final pair at rank 38. Configurations with three or more of these twelve types still require investigation at all four ranks.

Fix the rank-44 marked extension, the four-space $K$ and the three shifts of the [second-moment construction](../short-shell-moments.md#four-unimodular-lattices-from-the-same-extension). Use its folded populations $W_{a,t}$. Replace the three whole-shell counts at $(1,25),(1,27),(0,26)$ by unit counts plus the known nonunit contributions, using the four substitutions in the cited calculation (two enter the same column). Write $V$ for the populations in these new coordinates. In particular $V_{2,34},V_{3,35},V_{4,44}$ are whole-shell counts. Adding the rows of the following table gives the exact equation $\sum c_{a,t}V_{a,t}=0$.

| Charge | Squared norms in order | Coefficients in order |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | 0, 14, 16, 20, 24, 26 | 31725, 245, −3372, 2375, 180, 11 |
| 1 | 21, 23, 27 | 1769, −435, 290 |
| 2 | 26, 28, 30, 34 | −270, −825, 650, −46 |
| 3 | 35, 37 | −180, −11 |
| 4 | 44 | −738 |

Subtract the relation from 2375 times the sum of the ten types. The coefficient of $V_{1,23}$ is 435, those of $V_{0,0},V_{0,14}$ are $-31725,-245$, and every other coefficient is nonnegative. Every extension has $V_{1,23}\ge99$, $V_{0,0}=1$, $V_{0,14}\le4$. Thus the sum is at least $(435\cdot99-31725-245\cdot4)/2375=2072/475$. On all ten selected types the expectation over extensions equals the graph count divided by $p$.

## Deriving the relation

The original quadratic identities hold in each of the four gluings. Add the trace harmonic $12\|h\|^2-\|z\|^2$ where $h$ is the four-space projection of the full vector $z$. Its Laplacian vanishes since $12\cdot4=48$. Its values on squared-norm-two vectors in the four-space and the perpendicular space are respectively 22 and $-2$. The latter occur $W_{0,14}$ times.

For the remaining equations only the first shift $e=(-2,0,0,0)$ is needed. The theta series weighted by an even homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree $d>0$ is a cusp form of weight $24+d$. For $d=4,6,8$, these spaces have dimension two. Their bases may be taken as $\Delta E_{16},\Delta^2 E_4$ at weight 28, $\Delta E_{18},\Delta^2 E_6$ at weight 30 and $\Delta E_{20},\Delta^2 E_8$ at weight 32. In this statement one may use any normalized Eisenstein monomial of the indicated weight as $E_k$. Only the relation between the first three coefficients matters. Expanding $E_4=1+240q+2160q^2+\cdots$, $E_6=1-504q-16632q^2+\cdots$, $\Delta=q-24q^2+252q^3+\cdots$ gives
$$S_6=c_1 S_2+c_2 S_4,$$
where $S_j$ is the weighted sum at squared norm $j$. In order of degrees $4,6,8$ the pairs are $(151740,192),(-99180,-552),(50220,432)$. For degree two use $S_4=-48S_2$. [Weighted theta modularity and the level-one graded ring](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/papers/venklat.pdf)

Write the form of $K$ as $B\oplus B$ with $B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. For $u=(a,b)$ and $v=c(a+4b,-2a-b)$ we have $u^t Bv=0$. Hence $u+v,u-v$ have equal squared lengths. Use the real part of the $d$th power of a complex linear form with $u+v$ on the first plane and $i(u-v)$ on the orthogonal second plane. Its coefficient vector has complex bilinear square zero, which implies harmonicity. The checker uses twelve integer triples $(a,b,c)$. For squared norm $2n$ enumerate all projected coordinates with $h^2\le2n$ and use $t=7(2n-h^2)+11a^2/9$. The bounds $|w_i|\le s\sqrt{2n K_{ii}}$ follow from Cauchy–Schwarz, with denominator $s=1$ for the unshifted lattice and $s=3$ for the neighbors. The same charge and gluing congruences as at norm four apply. Count opposite positive charges by simultaneous negation and average over all anti-isometries at seven as before.

The [exact checker](higher_moments.py) regenerates the rows, makes the unit substitutions and verifies that adjoining the displayed identity leaves the rational rank unchanged. It also checks the coefficient signs of the lower-bound argument. Run it with Python and python-flint.

## A quantitative target for the remaining pair

Under the hypothesis that the only two of the twelve unit types present are norm 18 and norm 24, the same exact row-span test also gives
$$
6V_{0,18}=93+2V_{0,14}+2V_{1,23}+5V_{2,22},\qquad
6V_{0,24}=45+V_{0,14}+13V_{1,23}+34V_{2,22}+6V_{3,35}.
$$
Thus $N_{18}\ge97p/2$ and $N_{24}\ge222p$. Norm-18 eigenvectors come in opposite pairs, so the first bound at rank 40 rounds to 2,426.

## A forced overlap at rank 38

Suppose only the two types above occur. In particular $N_{16}=N_{20}=0$. At fixed $o,Q$ write $D_t$ for the positive subset of the six pair labels. Distinct norm-18 vectors at this root and these parents have dot product seven or nine. Indeed the [rooted inner-product bound](../short-shell-moments.md#a-bound-using-only-the-root-and-its-four-negative-neighbors) leaves seven to ten, and eight and ten would give the forbidden differences. All entries of a difference of norm at most 22 are signed units or zero, so there are no sign clashes.

Let $M$ be the number of oriented vectors at this $o,Q$. Delete one of the two vectors from each still-present dot-nine pair until none remain, giving a family of size at most $C$. Here $C$ is the largest size allowed for label sets with pairwise intersections of size at most two, since the five root and parent sites already agree. If two sets survive, both have size four and their two-element complements are disjoint. There are at most three such sets. A singleton of size five or six contains an independent four-subset and is covered by the same capacity. Write $j,k$ for the two kinds of label edges as in the [window count](exclusion.md#counting-windows). On all admissible six-label graphs a direct check gives $8C+2j+3k\le24$. The [checker](packing.py) enumerates all disjoint complements in these six-element sets.

Consequently the number of unordered dot-nine pairs with the root distinguished is at least
$$
9N_{18}-\sum_{o,Q} C
\ \ge\ 9N_{18}-
\frac{24\cdot99\cdot560-16\cdot99\cdot84-3\cdot99\cdot336+78P}{8},
$$
where $P\le851$ is the [triangular-prism bound](../../rank-34-short-support/README.md). For $N_{18}\ge16684$ this gives at least **4,645** rooted occurrences. The common panel has between three and five positive sites because it contains four parents and at least two shared pair labels besides the root. With four or five positives there are at most five negative sites, so two distinct eligible positive roots would share at least three of their four negative neighbors. With three positives there are at most three eligible roots. Thus even after forgetting the root there are at least **1,549 distinct unordered pairs of oriented vectors**.

Each such pair has exactly nine common sites. The four-positive-label overlap theorem leaves two or three shared pair labels. Color the three panels as in the [overlap proof](exclusion.md#nine-shared-sites-force-a-tenth). Every panel is the set of nine exterior sites of the third norm-18 support inside the union. At a vertex $x$ of that panel, its height with respect to the third support is exactly $4-d(x)$, where $d(x)$ is its internal panel degree. Indeed it has that many neighbors in each other panel, one group of each sign in the third vector. The height restrictions proved in the [short-labeling consequences](exclusion.md#consequences-for-short-labelings) therefore give minimum internal degree one, at most one vertex of degree one and no edge from degree one to degree two. Also, a panel vertex of internal degree four cannot have four other panel vertices sharing two of its parents, since orienting the two supports through it would give an excluded four-label window.

These tests leave **24 possible rooted common panels** from one-support charts, counted before adjoining the second support. Two of the following calculations exclude entire panel types. To regenerate them, take the 26 bipartite nine-by-nine supports of degree four satisfying the pair quotas, try each of their 18 vertices as a positive root, keep its four neighbors, and choose the remaining four common sites on either side. Apply the panel tests to the two resulting nine-sets. Canonicalize only the common panel, distinguishing the root, its parents and the two sign sides. The counts grouped by (number of common positives, number of shared pair labels) are 2 at $(3,2)$, 11 at $(4,2)$, 8 at $(4,3)$ and 3 at $(5,3)$. These shapes are necessary candidates. The census does not assert that all embed even into a 27-site union. Run the [nine-site census](forced_pair.py) with `python forced_pair.py --genbg /path/to/genbg` and nauty 2.8.9.

## One common panel is excluded

In one of the eight $(4,3)$ cases, the common positives are $o,z_{ab},z_{ac},z_{bc}$. The five common negatives are $a,b,c,d,e$. The root sees $a,b,c,d$. Each of the other positives sees its two parents and $e$. With the negatives ordered as above its four positive contact masks are $15,19,21,22$.

The [panel replay](one_panel.py) generates this case by taking a root and all orderings of its four negative neighbors in each of the 26 one-support graphs. The three prescribed labels are forced and the last negative must see all three. Apply the preceding panel tests to both halves, sort the two exclusive sign groups by their common contacts, and try every permutation within ties. This gives 72 distinct one-support charts with the common vertices named. Gluing these along the common panel with cross matrices of sizes four and five gives 7,776 gluings and 2,592 distinct colored cores. All 2,592 are excluded under the two-type hypothesis.

For the exterior test use the degree and common-neighbor columns from the [unconditional overlap theorem](exclusion.md). For each of the three markings, the complementary panel already has zero or one height-three site. Disallow exterior contacts of height over three, a second height-three site, or a proposed edge from a height-three site to another exterior site of height at least two in the known panel. For the 72 outside sites in aggregate, impose the remaining height-three budget as an upper bound for each marking. These restrictions are necessary by the norm-thirty argument above. The [archive](conditional-panel.json.gz) contains integer separating inequalities for 2,404 cores. For an upper-bound row the separator coefficient must be nonnegative. In the other 188 cases the integer multiplicities, 72-site limit and triple-contact quotas have independently checked DRAT refutations. This excludes the panel with masks $15,19,21,22$.

## Another panel forces a six-contact exterior site

Consider the $(3,2)$ panel with positive masks $15,51,60$, using the four parents first and the two extra negative sites last. All three first-color sites have degree four. The other two panels must have the same property because a first-color site in either has no cross edge to the first-color sites of the common panel, so its internal degree is four. In each panel there are twelve incidences from three sites into the six opposite sites. Their three pairs have at most six common-neighbor incidences, while convexity at mean two requires at least six. Thus each of the six sites has internal degree two and every pair on the three-side has exactly two common neighbors. The 27-site core has nine sites of degree four and eighteen of degree six.

The same named-chart procedure, now using the two pair labels with disjoint parents and ordering their two shared extra negatives, gives 88 charts and 99 distinct 27-site cores. The [exact enumeration](one_panel.py) checks on each core that possible exterior contact sizes are $0,2,3,4,6$. Write their populations as $n_s$. At an exterior site let $u_i,v_i$ count contacts with the three first and six second sites in panel $i$. Each $u_i\le1$ by the saturated pair quotas, and $u_i-v_i$ is constant in the three panels. If that constant is $w$, contact size is $s=2\sum_i u_i-3w$. The three heights with respect to the three norm-18 supports are $u_i+u_j-w$ for the three pairs $i,j$. The number of heights equal to two is zero for sizes zero, two and three, one for size four, and three for size six. All heights are at most two. These statements follow by trying $u_i\in\{0,1\}$ and nonnegative $v_i$ with the given total.

For each marking the exterior of its support supplies eighteen double sites because the 36 positive pairs each need two common neighbors and the nine opposite sites supply $9\binom42=54$ incidences. Its complementary panel already contains six such sites. Hence $n_4+3n_6=3(18-6)=36$. The exterior supplies 234 core incidences and 306 missing core-pair coincidences, by the core degrees and pair quotas. Solving these equations together with $\sum n_s=72$ gives
$$(n_0,n_2,n_3,n_4,n_6)=(6-k,3k,30,36-3k,k).$$
In fact $k\ge2$. Let $Z$ be the $z=6-k$ zero-contact sites. For each exterior site $y$ let $a_y$ be its number of neighbors in $Z$. Each $x\in Z$ has fourteen exterior neighbors supplying exactly 54 contacts with the core, since $x$ has two common neighbors with each of its 27 sites. Subtracting three contacts per neighbor shows that the four- and six-contact sites together satisfy $\sum_{s_y=4}a_y+3\sum_{s_y=6}a_y\ge12z$. Among the four-contact sites use $\binom a2\ge a-1$, and among the six-contact sites use $\binom a2\ge3a-6$ for nonnegative integers $a$. The common-neighbor cap on pairs in $Z$ gives
$$z(z-1)\ge\sum_y\binom{a_y}{2}\ge12z-n_4-6k=36-15k.$$
This requires $k^2+4k-6\ge0$, excluding $k=0,1$. **Every completion of this common panel contains between two and six exterior sites with six core contacts.** These sites can be chosen before attempting the other exterior adjacencies.

The [99-site contact table](exterior-contact-example.json) exhibits the obstacle on another panel, of type $(3,2)$. It has two six-contact exterior sites. Its 27 core sites satisfy all three local eigenvector equations. Its 72 exterior contact words meet the exact core degrees, all core-pair common-neighbor quotas, the height-three budgets and the triple-contact bounds. To join the exterior sites to each other, exterior site 9 and core site 25 would require two exterior common neighbors. Only exterior site 56 can supply one, since every other candidate already shares two core contacts with site 9 or would exceed a filled common-neighbor quota there. Indices within each group start at zero. This excludes the particular assignment. The next exhaustive check covers alternative assignments to the same core as well.

Run `python one_panel.py --genbg /path/to/genbg --drat-trim /path/to/drat-trim` to reconstruct the catalogue, check the integer separators, regenerate and verify the 188 DRAT proofs, and check the contact table and its obstruction.

## The six-contact site excludes the whole second panel

Choose one exterior site with six contacts, whose existence was just proved. Append it to the 27-site core. The 99 cores produce **1,128 different 28-site inputs** up to isomorphism permitting permutation of the three panels. The coloring distinguishes the nine first-color sites, the eighteen second-color sites, and the new site. Inside the original 27 sites the first-to-second graph has exactly three connected components, the panels. This coloring therefore preserves the three norm-18 supports and their signs up to permuting the supports and reversing their orientations.

The [exact search](high_contact_search.py) refutes all 1,128 inputs. At each new site extend every previous possible exterior contact word with either value of its new adjacency bit. Discard it if it hits a saturated degree or pair quota, or meets a closed core neighborhood more than twice. All contacts of an actual completion survive this filter. Use the degree, pair and exterior-site bounds and the at-most-one quota on every triple of contacts, with integer multiplicities. When the integer system is infeasible the verifier produces a DRAT proof and checks it independently. If the system remains feasible, choose a core degree or core pair with positive missing quota and fewest possible supplying words, then try adding each such word as the next site. Every completion would appear in at least one child. Two full replays refuted all 1,128 inputs. Their search trees used respectively nine and eleven branch nodes, with 1,138 and 1,141 independently verified infeasible leaves. The two independently generated catalogues agreed as complete sets of colored 27-site graphs.

Thus the panel with masks $15,51,60$ is excluded too. **At most 22 of the original 24 common-panel types remain** in the rank-38 two-type alternative. Run `python high_contact_search.py --genbg /path/to/genbg --drat-trim /path/to/drat-trim` from this directory to check the second exclusion. The search constructs its inputs directly and uses no numerical linear-programming statuses.

## Continuing on the remaining panels

For each remaining core $B$ and $27\times72$ binary contact matrix $D$, the next unknown is a symmetric zero-diagonal binary $72\times72$ matrix $H$. In addition to degree 14, the exact continuation equations are
$$
B^2+DD^t=12I-B+2J,\qquad BD+DH=2J-D,\qquad
D^tD+H^2=12I-H+2J.
$$
A completed triple $(B,D,H)$ gives a direct 99-vertex adjacency-matrix test. The first equation concerns core pairs, the second concerns one core and one exterior site, and the third concerns exterior pairs. The concrete table fails already in the second equation with the common-neighbor cap from the third. Enforcing these two conditions together during the selection of $D$ avoids regenerating assignments with that defect. Finishing this enumeration would address the rank-38 two-type alternative. The three-or-more-type alternatives and the other ranks have their own existence conditions.

The other $(3,2)$ panel has positive masks $15,19,60$. It has eleven internal edges. The cross-edge count on the three first-color sites forces eleven internal edges in each of the other panels as well. Their three first-color internal degrees are necessarily $4,4,3$. In each panel the six second-color degrees must be $2,2,2,2,2,1$ because their sum is eleven, their minimum is one and at most one site has degree one. The three degree-three first-color sites, one in each panel, have exactly one edge to each other panel and must form a triangle. Within a panel the three pairs of first-color sites have five common-neighbor incidences in total, each at most two. Thus exactly one pair shares just one internal neighbor. No site of another panel can neighbor two first-color sites of this panel, as only the distinguished first-color site has any cross edge. In a full graph each panel forces a specific pair to have its missing common neighbor among the 72 exterior sites. In fact the three required sites are distinct. The two degree-four first-color sites already have two common neighbors among the six second-color sites, by their sizes. Consequently the exceptional pair always contains the degree-three first-color site. Its counterparts in the other panels form the distinguished triangle. A site outside that triangle cannot see two of its vertices, since their edge has already used its unique common neighbor.

There are only two contact profiles for each of the three forced exterior sites. The degree-one site in each complementary panel uses the unique height-three allowance for the opposite support. Therefore all 72 exterior sites have height at most two with respect to every marking. With $u_i,v_i,w$ as above, the site realizing the exceptional pair has $u_i=2$ on its own panel (it cannot contact all three first-color sites since some pairs are saturated). For $j\ne i$ one has $2+u_j-w\le2$ and $u_j-w=v_j\ge0$. Hence $u_j=w$, $v_j=0$, and $v_i=2-w$. The choice $w=2$ would use the exceptional pair of the other panels and is excluded by the triangle. At $w=0$ the site has four contacts, all in its own panel. At $w=1$ it has five contacts, exactly one of them a first-color site in each other panel. This supplies three distinguished exterior vertices for the next completion test.

For a fixed 27-site core this gives at most **26 contact choices for each distinguished exterior site**. Its exceptional pair consists of first-color sites of degrees three and four. Their one common internal neighbor cannot contact the new site, since the resulting edge would have two common neighbors. The five remaining second-color sites split into two neighbors of the degree-three site and three of the degree-four site. In the four-contact case exactly one must be chosen from each group since two from the same group give two common neighbors on an edge incident with the new site. This gives six choices. In the five-contact case one of the five is chosen, and the first-color choice in each other panel must avoid the distinguished triangle vertex. This gives at most twenty more choices.

The [change-of-root and height counts](panel-heights.md) subsequently exclude this three-positive panel and reduce the original catalogue to six rooted shapes and exclude the rank-38 two-type alternative.
