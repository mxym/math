# A soft cap on norm 18 at seeds occupied by norm 16

Assume just **N20=0 and raw G026=0**. The latter is equivalent to U026=U121=0 by G026=U026+8U121. Allow actual norm-16 fields and all other selected types. At every rank the actual oriented counts obey
```
9k N18 ≤ 55440 k(k+1) + [385−6(k−1)(k−2)] N16       (integer k≥2).
```
In particular **N18≤64,742**. Here and throughout, a seed is a positive root o and the four negative neighbors Q, independent in its neighborhood matching.

## Coexistence at one norm-16 seed

Fix a unit norm-16 field f positive at o and negative on Q. Its signs each have eight vertices and induce a 4-regular bipartite support. For a norm-18 field t at that seed the signs each have nine with the same degree property. Write d_f,d_t for their positive pair-label counts among the six second common neighbors of parent pairs. The other positives in either field receive twelve total incidences from Q and at most two each, so d_f≥5, d_t≥4, with respectively d_f−5 and d_t−4 positives missing Q. Any opposite overlap must be one of these missed-parent positives. At least 5+d_f+d_t−6 signed sites agree. Thus f·t≥8. Their difference has norm at most 18 and therefore is signed-unit (as is every nonzero neutral field through norm 24). No opposite overlap occurs.

The minimum neutral norm 14 bounds f·t≤10. Dot 9 gives a second norm-16 field f−t of dot 7 with f, impossible by the [norm-16 overlap theorem](../norm16-packing.md#a-bound-on-the-overlap-of-two-norm-sixteen-vectors). At dot 10 the difference would be Fano. Let p,n be the common positive and negative sizes; they are ≥4, with p+n=10. The f-private sides have 8−p,8−n vertices, and each common site is outside the Fano, hence sees at most one private vertex of each sign. At (p,n)=(4,6) the four private positives would send ≥16−6=10 edges to two private negatives, impossible; the case (6,4) is symmetric. At (5,5) the three private negatives require ≥12−4=8 edges to the three private positives: o meets no private negative, leaving only four common positives available. Two full rows in a three-by-three bipartite graph with at least eight edges violate the pair common-neighbor cap. So **f·t=8**.

Consequently the common support is exactly o, Q and three positive pair labels. This forces d_f=5,d_t=4, with t also positive on L, the sole missing label of f. If d_f=6 the seed is empty of norm-18 fields. The site L has f-height two since it sees two f-negative parents. Its two f-positive neighbors cannot be among the t/f-common positives.

Use the [two shore multigraphs](../n16-zero-star-forest.md#the-five-allowed-component-shapes): each site of f-height two forms an edge, with multiplicity, between its two contacts on each f shore. Each multigraph is 2-regular with only even-size components. For d_f=5 the two f-positive sites other than o and the five pair labels each see one member of Q; they are precisely the two distinct neighbors of o in the positive shore multigraph. Indeed for each positive pair the multigraph multiplicity is two minus its number of common f-negative neighbors. These two neighbors cannot be joined by a shore edge, since that would give a 3-cycle component. Since L marks a shore edge not through o, at least one endpoint of its edge is among the five f-labels. This bars at least one of those labels from every t. Thus the three common f-labels of any t are a three-subset of a **fixed set of at most four sites**.

For two different t,u at this f-seed, their shrunk label sets share L and at least two of their common f-labels. By the [three-label panel theorem](panel-heights.md#the-three-label-exclusion-and-the-rank-38-contradiction), t·u=8 or 10. Dot 8 would give the absent norm 20, so t·u=10.

Each t contains a negative exterior site z of f-height two joined to two of the three common f-labels. Indeed each common label needs two negative neighbors beyond Q in the support of t. Six incidences hit its five private negatives; one private site is used twice. Such a z is an edge on the **same fixed set of at most four** f-positive sites. The number of candidate sites is at most four, counting parallel edges, since the shore has degree two. If two markings use the same z, then q=t+u−f has squared norm 40 from the three dot products 10,8,8. Its entries at L,z are +2,−2. If L,z are nonadjacent then d_L−d_z has norm 42 and q·(d_L−d_z)=28. Hence q−d_L+d_z is a neutral field of squared norm 26, forbidden by the raw-shell hypothesis. We conclude that whenever L has no neighbor of f-height two, each of the ≤4 candidates z is used by at most one marking. Its seed occupancy is therefore at most four.

There is at most one actual graph edge among the eight height-two sites, by [the height-two/zero-star theorem](../n16-zero-star-forest.md). For this fixed oriented f, a given missing label L occurs at at most one positive root o: its two negative neighbors have exactly two common vertices, L and o. Thus at most two of the eight f-seeds can escape the occupancy-four bound; roots with d_f=6 were already empty. The [norm-16 seed packing](../norm16-packing.md#one-vector-per-four-neighbors) makes the resulting **at least 6N16 low-occupancy seeds** genuinely distinct.

## From seed occupancies to the actual population

For seed multiplicity m the [triangle-free dot-7/9 theorem](norm18-triple-remaining-bridges.md) and Mantel give ≥g(m)=floor((m−1)²/4) unordered bad pairs (g(0)=0), now all at dot 10. Their differences have norm 16. Recall the exact global capacity: fix one oriented norm-16 difference r and common positive root o. A second decomposition of r at the same root satisfies t·u′=10−||t−t′||²/2≤2 by the minimum distance 16 between distinct norm-18 markings. For h common negative parents at o, counting equal double labels and subtracting possible opposite sites missing all those parents gives t·u′≥7h−15−binom(h,2), at least 3 for h=3,4 ([derivation](norm18-triple-remaining-bridges.md#unconditional-capacity-and-scalar-pressure)). Thus distinct decompositions use four-sets meeting in at most two. There are 280 independent triples in N(o), four per four-set, hence at most 70 decompositions. All such roots have r-height zero; r has exactly eleven zero-height sites. Summing both orientations and dividing by two bounds total unordered bad pairs by 385 N16.

For all m, g(m)≥k m−k(k+1); for m≤4 and k≥2 add the slack (k−1)(k−2), obtained at m=4 (direct subtraction at m=0,1,2,3 suffices). Summing over the 55,440 seeds, using Σm=9N18 and at least 6N16 distinguished low-occupancy seeds, proves the displayed inequality. At k=9 the coefficient of N16 is 49. Since N20=0 gives [N16≤5,196](norm16-four-good-bad.md), it follows that 81 N18≤5,244,204, hence N18≤64,742 by antipodality.

## Rank-36 consequence

Under those hypotheses, U125=U337=0 requires **both** U226>0 and U230>0 at rank 36. Two exact nonnegative [native scalar identities](norm18_rank36_mixed_soft.py) compare the *same* oriented populations. With U230 additionally absent the first yields 12N18+14N16≥1,008,840, forcing N18≥78,008. With U226 instead absent the second gives 10N18+18N16≥797,464, forcing N18≥70,394. In each case N16≤5,196 was used and the lower bound exceeds 64,742. No Fano-absence substitution is needed in these two scalar comparisons. Taken separately they exclude two complete six-type families, including the four-type candidate {016,018,024,127}; they do not exclude rank 36 altogether.

After these two exclusions, three four-type supports remain under the [accumulated necessary tests](norm18-rank36-star-sharp.md): `{018,020,024,026}`, `{018,020,026,125}`, and `{016,018,125,127}`. Higher-cardinality supports may also pass the tests; there is still no conclusion of existence or nonexistence.
