# A rank-36 induced four-cube has a matching on its eleven height-zero vertices

Use the notation and all standing lemmas of the published two-star certificate, including its two explicit P,N,H cube portraits, the 11,33,19 zero-incidence orbits, 2693,785,43 labeled zero-forest placements and the rank36 nonsquare determinant theorem. Here we exclude **exactly the one-degree-two placements**: a three-vertex path plus three isolated edges and two isolated vertices on the eleven Z sites. The already-proved two-degree-two exclusion then forces the entire Z graph of any rank36 cube to be five independent edges plus an isolate. This particular certificate does not eliminate cubes of that shape, the norm16 shell, or rank36. The [subsequent unified proof](q4-excluded-r36.md) handles the remaining five-edge matching case.

**Exact relabeling.** A base is a sorted multiset b of eleven cyclic H-neighborhood masks, together with a labeled zero-forest e on those rows. For each dihedral relabeling h↦r+d h (d=±1) preserving the multiset b, consider *every* vertex-label bijection g that matches the transformed masks to their original values, allowing equal masks to permute. This sends e to g(e). On a cube portrait with shift s and period ℓ=2 or 4 (cube-to-dihedral stabilizers in the previous proof), the image shift is ds−r modulo ℓ: comparing C[h+s] with C[(r+dh)+(ds−r)]=C[d(h+s)] uses its admissible reflection. Thus keeping the smallest (forest index,s) among all such pairs preserves every global completion up to relabeling, without assuming any graph symmetry. This gives 10,880, 3,230, 168 aligned physical charts for 0,1,2 degree-two sites. The middle 3,230, original forest indices [2693,3478), are the only ones asserted here.

**Root checks.** Use the published h-star six-word cover and the two conflict-neighbor demands at h±1, which are necessary because neither conflict neighbor can have common Y with h. R=12I+2J−U−U² is always the exact missing pair-incidence matrix on any literal old adjacency U. In addition to the full rank-35/36 check when applicable, we use exactly these weaker or stronger projections. For an invertible principal pivot I of the 35-site Gram, checking the complete six-label extension on the full old radical means appending the residual columns 4−W_J−(4−W_I)G_I⁻¹G_IJ to the Schur residual of the six new labels; the old radical has zero Gram. Reject too large a rank or a full-rank wrong square class. For an additional row that meets h', projecting first to I and then to a nonsingular principal minor of the six-label Schur residual is another valid rank/minor screen: if at most one nondegenerate dimension is left, all remaining six-label residual columns which are radical after this choice must have zero image. Therefore its 0/1 adjacency vector to the six first-row labels is fixed by its bits on those pivot labels; enumerate them all, and check square norm when the remaining dimension is zero or one. The h' row must satisfy the pair quota against each of the 41 known labels. For each demanded column sum, the sum of its largest achievable entry in each distinct physical P tag is an upper bound. This same necessary test on a subset of rows before final 41-column evaluation is allowed; failing it is exact and passing it makes no claim.

**One exceptional 41-site refinement.** Exactly two lifts of the aligned base (2843,0,0), root h=6, survive these checks. Their 41-site Gram has rank 34, and determinant square class 3, so the ambient residual plane has determinant class 1. Over F7 this is anisotropic (−1 is a nonsquare); every projected old radical vector must actually vanish. Thus every remaining Y-neighbor word satisfies ALL 41 evaluations from its entries on the invertible minor. Test each 35-site Y word and 64 choices of its adjacency to the first six vertices; apply these linear identities, the exact old common-neighbor upper bounds, and reject a word simultaneously meeting an old pair whose remaining R_ij=0. These candidates are distinct literal row patterns: two distinct Y meeting the same pattern would already share three sites (its p,n and at least one Z), impossible. For each cell (p,n) exactly R_pn remaining vertices are required, and every remaining Y lies in exactly one such cell. Let X_pn be its list of legal candidate rows. If |X_pn|<R_pn the test fails. For any old pair (i,j) its contribution from this cell is at most min(R_pn, (X_pn^T X_pn)_ij). A contradiction follows when the sum over cells is below R_ij. No assignment or LP sufficiency is presumed. The two surviving pivots both fail. The other once-slow base 3199 is verified at h=2; the six alignments have 0,8,0,0,8,4 first-rank-compatible lifts, all fail the two-star test.

**Replay.** [q4-p3.py](q4-p3.py) requires Python3 and NumPy. Run OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 q4-p3.py (or append a half-open interval within 2693..3478). No time limits or floating-point rejection; every uncompleted root list or survivor raises an error. The orbit and scope checks are executable. Independent replay can be parallelized by disjoint intervals. The formulas use integer entries and Gaussian elimination modulo 7. This is a theorem about full rank36 graphs, not a search for symmetric graphs.
