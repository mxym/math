# The complementary rank-33 Hoffman residual has a determinant-two saturation

Assume a Conway SRG(99,14,1,2) has an actual Hoffman coclique C of size 22. No Seidel rank, graph automorphism, grid, or sparse-type condition is assumed. There is a maximal even lattice T of rank 33 and determinant **2** containing its positive-eigenspace residual marks. Define A,R,B to be its oriented dual shell counts at squared norms 1/2, 2, 5/2; the norm-two shell belongs to T itself. All three count actual integral +3 eigenfields vanishing on C of respective ordinary squared norms 14,56,70, subject to membership in this saturation. Then
```
A is 0 or 2,       B = 60 + 2R - 32A.
```
Thus either there is a true zero-on-C norm-14 field, or at least 60 oriented genuine zero-on-C norm-70 fields. This is a structural alternative, not a graph nonexistence or full-shell equality outside T*. In particular, a grid's nonintegral-seven projected norm 20/7 is **not** a T* vector.

## Residual and integral fields
Use the [complementary +3 mark lattice](../../complementary-rank56/README.md), with marks v_i of Gram 4I+A-2J/11 and frame constant 7. On C the Gram is 4(I-J/22), rank 21, with the single relation that their sum is zero. Denote by P orthogonal projection off this span in the 54-dimensional eigenspace. This is the [positive Hoffman residual](hoffman-positive-residual.md). If D is the C-to-complement incidence, its columns are blocks of size four with DD^T=12I+2J. For b among the 77 complement sites, set m_b=2Pv_b. Since the removed projection's Gram is one fourth the Euclidean Gram of the centered design columns, these marks have Gram
```
Q = 16I+4A_B-D^T D,    Q^2=28Q,   diag Q=12.
```
They span an even integral rank-33 lattice L. Its dual maps injectively via x→t_b=(x,m_b) to honest integral +3 eigenfields extended by zero on C: the vector 2x has precisely these graph evaluations and zero on C, and all graph evaluations belong to the +3 eigenspace. Conversely any such field has this support and arises by the frame inverse. Its squared norm is 28(x,x). In particular 28x=Σ t_b m_b, so the discriminant of L has exponent dividing 28.

## Saturation at seven
At seven, the known rank-56 complementary lattice Λ is the orthogonal direct sum of the positive graph-mark lattice and its binary norm-11-determinant supplement, since its glue has index 11. The former is generated there by all v_i, since 11v_i=Σ_{j∼i}(v_i−v_j). Any 21 of the C marks have determinant 4^21/22, a seven-unit, and the remaining mark is their negative sum. Thus their span splits off unimodularly. Its orthogonal complement is generated by Pv_b: project the graph-mark generators, keeping p-integral coefficients since the split summand is unimodular. Since 2 is a unit, this is exactly L locally. Consequently its seven-primary discriminant form coincides with that of Λ, an elementary space of dimension 54-r for Seidel rank r (the dimension is even). The only prime in det Λ is seven. By Milgram's Gauss-sum theorem its normalized Gauss sum is exp(2πi·56/8)=1. A nonsplit finite quadratic space of positive even dimension over F7 has Gauss sum −1, whereas the split one has +1 (split off hyperbolic planes; on an anisotropic plane the norm has one zero and eight vectors above each nonzero element). Thus this space is split, and adjoining any maximal totally isotropic subgroup eliminates the entire seven-part.

## Saturation at two
After the seven extension, take a maximal even overlattice at two, which corresponds to quotienting by a maximal isotropic subgroup. The remaining finite quadratic form F, with q(x)=(x,x)/2 modulo 1, is nonsingular and anisotropic, has exponent dividing 4, and has Brown invariant 33≡1 modulo 8 by Milgram. Its order has odd two-valuation: the rational determinant of L is 2 modulo rational squares. For that last fact, work over the rationals. The rank-56 lattice Λ has square determinant 7^(54−r) and is rationally an orthogonal sum of the positive graph-mark space and the binary space of determinant 11. The graph-mark space has determinant square class 11. The C-mark span has determinant 4^21/22, of square class 22. Consequently its orthogonal complement has determinant square class 11/22, which is 2 modulo rational squares. Scaling the projected marks by 2 does not change this square class.

For clarity there is a short elementary way to finish. An element x of order 4 has q(x) an odd eighth, since q(2x) must not vanish. The cyclic four-group splits off nonsingularly. A second order-four element y in its orthogonal complement would make 2x+2y a nonzero isotropic element. The orthogonal complement has exponent two and no element of q-value 1/2 (add 2x); consequently it has dimension at most one. Indeed two independent quarter-valued elements have a sum of integral or half-integral q-value. Odd two-valuation therefore forces the group Z/4⊕Z/2. An odd eighth on the first summand has **odd** Brown invariant, by summing its four terms; a nondegenerate order-two line has invariant ±1. The total Brown invariant is even, a contradiction.

If F has exponent two, split off nonsingular lines whenever there is an element of q-value ±1/4. The remaining alternating space is either zero or a single anisotropic plane (a nonzero anisotropic alternating space has all nonzero values 1/2; a symplectic space of dimension ≥4 has distinct orthogonal nonzero vectors). Its Brown invariant is 4. The quarter-lines all have the same sign, since opposite lines give an isotropic sum. There are at most three, since a sum of four equal-sign lines is isotropic. If the plane is present at most one line is possible, since two quarter-lines sum to half and a plane element supplies the other half. Using Brown invariant 1 and odd order valuation leaves just one positive quarter-line. Thus |F|=2. Patching the two primary extensions gives an even maximal T of determinant 2, with every projection mark still in it.

## A forced graph shell from E7
Any T* vector in its nonzero coset has squared norm 1/2 modulo 2. Glue T and E7 along their opposite determinant-two forms: the E7 dual coset has squared norm 3/2 modulo 2. This gives an even unimodular U of rank 40. The E7 shells at 2,4 have 126,756 members, and its nonzero dual coset at 3/2,7/2 has 56,576 members, respectively. The [stdlib checker](hoffman_plus_det2.py) obtains these from E8 by projecting orthogonally off a root. In each shell the quadratic pairing tensor on E7 is scalar by irreducibility of its Weyl representation; divide the product of population and norm by 7 to obtain the scalar.

Write n2,n4 for the two U populations. The dimension-two space of scalar weight-20 forms gives
```
theta_U=E4^5+(n2-1200) Delta E4^2,   n4=39600+456n2.
```
Degree-two harmonic theta has weight 22, with cusp space spanned by Delta E10 and q²/q coefficient −288. Thus on any unit direction the shell-four squared pairing moment plus 288 times the shell-two squared pairing moment equals (4n4+576n2)/40. In an E7 direction, counting both lattice glue layers gives n2=126+R+56A, and the two moments are 432+36R+288A+12B and 36+12A. Substitution gives the displayed identity B=60+2R−32A.

If T* contains x of norm 1/2, then 2x in T has norm 2 and pairs evenly with every T vector. Splitting this A1 summand orthogonally leaves an even unimodular rank-32 lattice. Its positive minimum is at least 2, so ±x are all the dual vectors of norm 1/2. If there is no such x, B≥60. The resulting fields are genuine graph fields; membership in T* is a local restriction, not a claim that every integral field or every grid is counted.

The modular ingredients are the standard full-level dimensions and Fourier expansions (e.g. Diamond–Shurman, *A First Course in Modular Forms*) and Milgram's formula for finite discriminant quadratic forms.
