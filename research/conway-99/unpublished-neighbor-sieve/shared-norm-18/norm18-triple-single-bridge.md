# A shared norm-18 triple needs at least two leaf bridges

Under N16=N20=0, every triple with a common positive root and the same four negative neighbors has all pairwise inner products seven. Between its private four-leaf sets the [bridge graph](norm18-triple-bridge-graph.md) is a nonempty simple graph on three groups. **It cannot have just one edge**: the possible shapes are reduced to a path with two edges and a triangle. No restriction on the affine shells or the Seidel rank is required. This is not a proof that the two remaining shapes extend.

If only groups i,j are bridged, every group is balanced by the preceding result (an unbalanced group needs bridges to both others). The leaves of each group are thus partitioned into two pairs, one attached to each omitted center. The two leaves attached to one center have distinct Q parents: they already share the center and a private-negative neighbor. So the underlying data, with the bridge edge omitted, are exactly among the [34 positive leaf charts](norm18-triple-core.md#exact-34-chart-check). In each chart select one of the three unordered pairs of groups and one of the sixteen choices of a leaf u in i and v in j, then add their edge. These 48 refinements per chart are exhaustive regardless of stabilizers or automorphisms of a graph.

The same two leaves are the only private positives meeting a private negative of the other group. Thus there is exactly one extra contact from v to N_i, and one from u to N_j. For each group take the earlier [private-negative table generator](norm18_triple_core.py), whose five rows choose four of eight possible positives in the field (four own leaves, four used centers), with column sums 3,3,3,3,2,2,2,2. All these conditions including the old unbridged positive-core common-neighbor upper bounds remain necessary: adding a bridge cannot decrease a codegree. For each of the two bridged groups assign the one additional foreign leaf to any of its five rows. The other group has no foreign leaf contact. The rows are already distinguished by their original four contacts: equal four-sets would give four common neighbors, so sorting them loses no extra-attachment choices.

After any two such groups are assigned, the five-by-five binary matrix of edges between their private negatives has forced margins. At x in N_i the field t_j vanishes, so its #N_j contacts equals its already prescribed net positive sum in t_j. This fixes both sets of margins, allowing zero entries and multiple matrices. Enumerate all binary matrices with those margins. Three pair-interface choices determine the full induced 38-vertex adjacency. Reject actual codegree excesses (including those through the third group), and reject cores where either pair of omitted centers leaves two or more common neighbors outside the support. The latter rejection is valid even though (3,37) fields may occur: the [two exterior negative pivots](norm18-triple-exterior.md#partial-cores-and-the-missing-neighbors) would leave a nonunit (3,37), impossible.

There is one further necessity on every partial or full core. Write A for its adjacency, T for the three-by-m matrix of the restricted markings, and s for the column-sum vector. The three full fields have Gram matrix 11I+7J with inverse I/11−7J/352. Subtract the orthogonal projector onto their joint span from the graph's minus-four projector before restricting to the selected vertices. Together with the ordinary plus-three projector this gives
```
9504I−3168A+352J−2016 TᵀT+441ssᵀ ⪰ 0,
44I+11A−2J ⪰ 0.
```
These are genuine necessary conditions for the three actual full fields, regardless of their partial support. The projector formulas are those in the [one-nine test](norm18-triple-one-nine.md#exact-marked-spectral-test-then-five-site-completion).

Run [norm18_triple_single_bridge.py](norm18_triple_single_bridge.py) alongside [norm18_triple_core.py](norm18_triple_core.py). Python 3.10+ and NumPy suffice; there is no eigensolver or floating-point test. Each candidate is first tested on the 23-site positive chart. For either Gram let B be this 23-site block. The verifier uses fraction-free symmetric elimination to find a positive definite principal block on indices I of size rank(B); a negative pivot or a zero pivot with nonzero remaining row refutes B. Exact Gauss-Jordan gives a positive common denominator and the integer numerator of B[I,I] inverse. For a private negative vertex its signature is its group and its 23-bit contact mask, which determine its full column b into the 23-site Gram. The cross column must satisfy b=B[:,I]·B[I,I]^{-1}b_I, not only the restricted equation on I. The remaining necessary PSD matrix on any group(s) has entries given by the corresponding original Gram minus b_Iᵀ B[I,I]^{-1} c_I; correcting a proposed negative-negative edge adds the appropriate coefficient −3168 or +11. Positive scaling clears the common denominator. Thus the one-, two- and three-group checks have orders only five, ten and fifteen. Every symmetric elimination is on arbitrary-width integers with exact divisions asserted. Fixed-width arithmetic is confined to the original small binary graph counts and the displayed 23-site integer Gram entries (absolute value <16384).

The complete independent loop has 1,632 positive refinements (48 in each of 34 charts); 856 pass the first codegree and projection checks. After the group and pair Gram checks and the full codegree and omitted-center checks, the only complete matrices needing the final projection test occur at chart 7 (2), 21 (10), 22 (36), 27 (2), 30 (4), 31 (20). All 74 fail. The source checks the exact per-chart transcript. Optional chart numbers select deterministic shards. Native all-chart replay: 45 seconds.

**Count consequence.** Each surviving triple now supplies at least four oriented edged differences (two unordered bridge pairs). Every such oriented field has one internal positive edge, not two, by the [height argument](norm18-triple-bridge-graph.md). If H2 counts all graph-wide oriented norm-22 fields with two positive edges, the number with one edge is B1+2B2−2H2 by the [positive-edge bijection](norm18-u230-edge-pressure.md#charging-all-seven-triples-through-edged-differences). Each one-edge difference has at most six height-zero roots, each with at most 5+C/2 seed choices, and the summed colored-collision count is at most 6n1. Hence the number T of shared triples satisfies
```
9N18−110880 ≤ T ≤ min(44G444, (15/2)(B1+2B2−2H2)+(3/4)n1).
```
All symbols retain their actual graph-wide orientation conventions. Neither remaining bridge shape, nor the graph itself, is excluded by this bound alone.
