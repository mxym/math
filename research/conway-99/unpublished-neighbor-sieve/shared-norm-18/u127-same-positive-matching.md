# Same-positive unit (1,27) pairs: the norm-16 and norm-24 matching

Let A be the adjacency matrix of an SRG(99,14,1,2), and d_i=3e_i−Ae_i. A field of charge a satisfies Ab=−4b−2a·1. Write U(1,27) for the unit type b=1_P−1_N of charge 1 and squared norm 27; |P|=8 and |N|=19. We use the published [neutral nonzero squared minimum 14](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/conway-99/short-support-packing/README.md) and [charge-one squared minimum 21](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/conway-99/unpublished-neighbor-sieve/short-shell-moments.md) (the affine norms have the parity of the charge).

## Negative girth and isolates

On a negative vertex the positive neighbor count is 2 plus its negative degree; on a positive vertex the negative count is 6 plus its positive degree. Summing gives e_N=e_P+5. The 28 positive pairs have 56−e_P common-neighbor slots; at a negative vertex of negative degree d, the positive-pair contribution is choose(2+d,2)≥1+2d. Thus 56−e_P≥19+4e_N and e_P≤3.

For a negative k-set S with e internal edges, b+∑_{i∈S}d_i has charge 1+k and squared norm 27+11k+2k²−14e. For k≥4 through 8, applying charge folding by adding 1 and negating changes this to charge 8−k and squared norm 104−11k+2k²−14e. A negative triangle maps to (4,36), a negative four-cycle to (4,36) or smaller, and a negative five-cycle to (3,29) or smaller; all violate t≥11a. In particular the negative graph always has girth at least six. With ≥5 edges it has ≥6 nonisolated vertices, since a graph with at most five nonisolated vertices and girth≥6 is an acyclic graph with at most four edges. It has at most 13 isolates.

## Distance 16 and its converse

Two fields with the same positive set differ by t=1_X−1_Y, where X and Y are equal-sized differences of their negative sets. Put d=|X|=|Y|. Their internal edge counts are equal, say e: the eigenvector equation A t=−4t gives each vertex of one sign 4 plus its internal degree on the other sign. Write r_z for either number of sign contacts on a zero site. The positive-pair quota for X, after subtracting its witnesses in X and Y, gives
`∑_{z∉X∪Y} choose(r_z,2)=d(d−7)−7e−(∑_{X∪Y} deg_internal²)/2.`
The 8 common positive sites together have at least 2d+2e contacts into X, because each x∈X is negative in one field and sees 2 plus its internal negative degree into P. At d=7 this is impossible. At d=8, the sum on P alone consumes at least 8 slots; if e>0 then at least 12 would be required while the global budget is at most 0. Hence e=0, all p∈P have exactly 2 contacts in either sign, and every other zero site has height ≤1. The common negative set has no contacts into X∪Y because the 2d quota into P is already exhausted. The precisely 8 height-two sites and 11 height-zero sites give P and the common N; all moving negatives are isolated in their respective negative sets.

Conversely, at squared norm 16 every neutral vector is unit and its signs are independent. For independence, the neutral norm-16 affine lift below has squared norm 27−14e, always ≥21, forcing e=0. The same-sign deficiency matrix D_X=2I+2J−KKᵗ (K the bipartite incidence matrix) is a 2-regular multigraph. For any component C, Kᵗ1_C has sum 4|C| and norm squared 2|C|², so equality in Cauchy–Schwarz on 8 coordinates gives entries |C|/2. Every component has even size. An exterior vertex seeing 3 sign-support neighbors would consume all the deficiency in a triangle component, impossible; ≥4 exceeds degree 2 already. Thus the exterior has exactly 8 sites of height two and 11 of height zero, by the first two moments. This is also stated in the public [norm-16 switch](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/conway-99/unpublished-neighbor-sieve/shared-norm-18/exclusion.md#every-oriented-norm-16-vector-gives-a-different-unit-labeling-of-type-127). The two fields are b=A1_X−3·1_Y−1 and b'=A1_Y−3·1_X−1; the A² relation gives charge 1, and their values are −1 on X (respectively Y) and the height-zero sites, and +1 on the same eight height-two sites.

Consequently each oriented norm-16 vector gives exactly one ordered same-positive pair of U(1,27) at distance 16, and every such pair is obtained. Orient t consistently with the ordered difference b'−b.

## No path of length two at distance 16

Suppose b0 has two distinct same-positive distance-16 mates b1,b2. Write A_i for the sites negative only in b_i, and B_i for those negative in both other b's but not b_i; let C be the common negatives. Distance equations give sizes |A1|=|A2|=a, |B1|=|B2|=b, |A0|=8−b, |B0|=8−a. The moving sites in each distance-16 pair are isolated in the relevant negative set. Every A_i and B_i is then isolated in any negative set to which it belongs: for example B1 is known isolated in N0, while in N2 it cannot touch A2 or B0 (these move in (0,2) and are isolated in N2), nor C or B1 (also in N0). B2 is symmetric. The two 8-site moving subsets inside N0 meet in A0, and lie in the ≤13 isolates of N0, so |A0|≥3 and b≤5.

In pair (0,1), each B1 vertex has 4 neighbors on the opposite side A1∪B0, all in A1 since B0 and B1 share membership in N2. Conversely any vertex of A1 is outside the moving support of pair (0,2), and is not in P, so has at most one neighbor in A0∪B2; it has none in B2 as they belong to N1. In (0,1) it needs 4 neighbors in A0∪B1; hence it has ≥3 in B1. The groups A1,B1 are both nonempty: if A1 were empty and B1 nonempty the four-neighbor requirement fails; if both empty then b1=b2; and if B1 empty and A1 nonempty the ≥3 requirement fails. Thus b≥3 and 3a≤4b imply a≤6. Now |B0|≥2 and |A0|≤5. In pair (0,1) each B0 vertex has all four opposite neighbors in A0, since B0 and B1 share N2. Two such vertices would have ≥8−|A0|≥3 common neighbors, impossible.

The distance-16 graph on each fixed positive set is a matching: its vertex count from nonisolated members is exactly N16 when summed over P. This reproduces the known U(1,27)≥N16 but gives a pair characterization. It does not control collisions at larger distances.


## A convolution-type gap in the *literal* same-positive distance spectrum

For a same-positive pair of fields and the same d,e,r notation above, let H_j count zero sites of the difference of height j, let Q=∑deg_internal² on the moving support, and write T=∑_{j≥3} choose(j−1,2)H_j and D=∑_{j≥3}(j−2)H_j. We have T≥D, with equality only when all heights are at most three. The ordinary height sum is 10d−4e and the exterior size is 99−2d; use the already proved pair quota to deduce
`H_0=99−19d+d²−3e−Q/2−T.`
Indeed ∑_{j≥1}(j−1)H_j=(10d−4e)−(99−2d−H_0), and this is ∑_j choose(j,2)H_j−T. The common negative set C has size 19−d. Thus its total height E=∑_{x∈C}r_x is at least 19−d−H_0. Counting contacts from a moving negative side into P gives exactly 2d+2e+E: each such negative has 2 plus its degree within its full negative set, with other negative neighbors either same moving side or C. Eight sites of P account for at most 16+D, since r≤2+(r−2)_+ there and all exterior high-site excess is included in D. Substitution gives the rank-free condition
`5e+Q/2+T−D ≤ (d−8)(d−12).`
It excludes d=9,10,11, that is, squared distances 18,20,22. Squared distance 14 was already excluded, so a nonidentical pair has squared distance 16 or at least 24.

At d=12 every term on the left vanishes and the inequalities used to derive it are equalities. Thus e=0, no height is above 3, all height-3 sites lie in P and every P site has height ≥2. In particular H3≤8. Moreover H0=15−H3 and E=|C|−H0=H3−8≥0. Thus there are exactly H3=8, H0=7, H1=24, H2=36; P=H3, C=H0 and E=0. Both moving sides are independent and have no contacts to C; all their sites are isolated in the relevant negative set. For a choice of signed difference, s=A1_X−3·1_Y−1 is an actual charge −3 field, with values −1 on X and H0, zero on Y and H1, +1 on H2 and +2 on H3. Therefore b−s=−1_{H2∪H3} is an actual charge-4 norm-44 field. Conversely, given a unit norm24 vector with e=0 and the above height census, if H2∪H3 supports this charge-4 field then s−1_{H2∪H3} and its switched sign companion are unit G127 fields with the same P and squared distance 24. This is a bijection of oriented norm24 vectors with that exact graph marking to ordered distance24 pairs.

## The union of distances 16 and 24 is a matching

Suppose b0 has two mates b1,b2 with the same P and the relevant distance from b0 equal to 16 or 24. Use precisely the prior definitions of A_i and B_i; moving site isolation for both distance types implies again that every A_i/B_i vertex is isolated in each negative set whose membership it has. In particular, an A1 vertex has no neighbors in B2 (common membership in N1), and B0 and B1 share membership in N2 and cannot be adjacent. The two moving subsets in N0 lie in its ≤13 isolates and intersect exactly at A0.

For two distance24 mates, |A0|≥11, |B1|=|B2|≤1. Any vertex of A1 is exterior to the moving support of pair (0,2), and not in P, so it sees at most two sites in A0∪B2. These are all in A0 by the isolation just noted. In pair (0,1) the vertex needs four opposite neighbors in A0∪B1, impossible. Thus A1 is empty, and any B1 vertex would then need four neighbors inside empty A1 (its opposite side in (0,1) is A1∪B0, with B0 unavailable). Hence B1 is empty and the size equations also make A2, B2 empty, giving b1=b2.

For a distance24 mate b1 and a distance16 mate b2, |A0|≥7 and |B2|=8−|A0|≤1, |B1|=12−|A0|≤5. Outside the distance16 moving support, A1 sees at most one vertex in A0∪B2, all in A0. In pair (0,1) it needs four neighbors in A0∪B1 and therefore ≥3 in B1. B1 has its four opposite neighbors in A1, so 3|A1|≤4|B1|≤20 and |A1|≤6. The endpoint moving half-size is |A1|+|B2|≤7, impossible for distinct same-positive fields. The pure distance16 pair was proved above. Thus the union of both pair types is a matching. In particular U127≥N16 plus the number of oriented N24 fields satisfying the exact charge4/height predicate; this is not a global bound on all N24.

## The common negative graph in the distance-24 case

Write P=H3, C=H0. Since the 44-set S=H2∪H3 is genuine, every outside vertex sees eight S vertices and every inside vertex sees four. Each x in either moving sign set X or Y sees exactly two P vertices: it is isolated in one of the two negative sets and the field equation at x gives 2 positive contacts. The 24 moving vertices therefore supply 24 positive-pair witnesses.

All negative edges occur in C. Thus e_C=e_P+5. At c∈C, its P degree is 2+δ_c, where δ_c is its degree in C. Its positive-pair contribution is choose(2+δ_c,2)=1+2δ_c+choose(δ_c,2). The sum over seven c is at least 7+4e_C+(2e_C−7)=6e_C, using choose(δ,2)≥δ−1 for all δ≥0. There are 56−e_P total common-neighbor slots of P pairs. If e_P≥1, at most 31 remain after the 24 moving witnesses, whereas C contributes at least 36. Thus P is independent and e_C=5. The girth≥6 argument makes C a forest on seven vertices with two tree components. The sharper exact C contribution is 22+∑δ_c²/2 and must be ≤32, so ∑δ_c²≤20. At any p∈P, its N-degree is six, exactly three of which lie in its moving negative side, so p has three C contacts. Its six moving neighbors each contribute one P-pair incidence through p; its three C neighbors contribute ∑_{c∼p}(1+δ_c). Since altogether p may have only 14 P-pair incidences, ∑_{c∼p}δ_c≤5.

The [standard-library companion checker](u127_same_positive_matching.py) exhausts the seven unlabeled two-tree forests meeting this bound, using Prüfer codes for components and canonical rooted tree encodings. For each it enumerates the distinct 3-subsets specifying the neighborhoods of the eight independent P vertices in C. It imposes the just-derived degree sums, the C–P pair common-neighbor caps from the induced C graph, each C vertex's precise 2+δ_c P degree, and every C–C common-neighbor quota. Distinct P rows are forced, since identical triples would give at least three common neighbors. It quotients the remaining eight triples by automorphisms of C, which are tested over all seven-vertex permutations. Exactly 32 colored cores survive, all with C=P3⊔P4. This is a necessary local projection, not a realization or an exclusion of distance24.
