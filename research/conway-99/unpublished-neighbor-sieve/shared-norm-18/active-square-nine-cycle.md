# Nine active squares exclude one alternating 12-cycle at a graph edge

This is rank-independent. At a fixed true edge `pz`, call a square active if it is realized by a charge-two cost-26 field of subtype c=0, as in [the full root row](u226-full-root-row.md). If the union of the two wing mate matchings at `pz` is an alternating 12-cycle, at most eight of its twelve squares are active. There is **no** assumption on the third-wing return permutation, an automorphism, or another shell. In particular any edge forced to have nine active squares must have at least two alternating components; this does not exclude other component partitions.

Use the notation of the full-root-row result: `w_i` and `q_i=y_{z,w_i}` are corresponding left/right sites, `a` is the cap of the fixed edge, `l,r` are the wing involutions. For any active `i` orient the two positive vertices as `p,q_i` using the square switch if necessary. Set `S_i={z}∪X_i`, where `X_i` consists of five left indices, `U_i=X_i−{i}`, and let the bar denote the mate at p (bar(z)=a and bar(j)=l(j)). The prior result gives:
* `X_i` contains `i,l(r(i)),r(l(i))`; it is independent in both wing involutions.
* The full far-neighbor row `R_i` of `q_i` consists of a mate-free perfect matching `H_i:S_i→bar(S_i)`, a perfect matching on `U_i`, and four pairs from the omitted mate pair to `bar(U_i)` (each omitted member has degree two). The partner of z in `H_i` is the right mate index `r(i)`. The partner of a is `tau(i)`. Both `tau(i)` and `tau^-1(i)` lie in `X_i`.
* The negative far support `F_i` is the union of `H_i`, the matching on `U_i`, and the eight pairs `{z,i}×U_i`. The only positive far site of this field is `q_i`. At p's neighbors, z and `X_i` are zero and all others are negative.

**Pair equations.** If `i,j` are distinct active indices, put `e=[r(i)=j]`. The two right sites have z as a common neighbor, no other common p-neighbor, and are adjacent precisely for e=1. Hence `|R_i∩R_j|=1−e`. Moreover
```
|R_i∩F_j| = 0  if i∈X_j or e=1,
            3  otherwise.
```
Indeed `q_i` has just z and `w_i` as its near neighbors at p; summing the j-field over the far row and using `Ab_j=−4b_j−4·1` gives `4+4b_j(q_i)+b_j(w_i)+e` for this intersection. Its value is the displayed dichotomy since `q_i` is a negative of `b_j` exactly when `i∈X_j−{j}` or `e=1`, and `b_j(w_i)=−[i∉X_j]`. Apply in both orders. The checker also enforces injectivity of `tau` on chosen rows and reciprocity of `tau(i)=j⇒i∈X_j`, but does not require every partial assignment to extend to a permutation. Thus every true graph is covered.

**Exact small exhaustion.** Label the 12-cycle by `Z/12`, with l pairing (0,1),(2,3),… and r pairing (11,0),(1,2),…. The 12 relabelings `g_j(i)=j+(−1)^j i` preserve both colors and act regularly. The root neighbors fixed by these relabelings are z and a. The 84 far labels are precisely the nonmate pairs of the fourteen near vertices. At i=0 enumerate all five-subsets and the three matchings specified above, with z's partner fixed. This gives 5,724 distinct rows (the positive index is implicit). Push these by `g_i` to each i; composition `g_i^-1 g_j = g_{g_i^-1(j)}` ensures each precomputed ordered compatibility matrix at index 0 is reused with its actual row indexing unchanged. This is a relabeling of the case space, not an assumed graph symmetry.

Delete a row if <8 other indices have any surviving compatible row; every row in a real nine-tuple survives throughout. Successive domain sizes are 5724,2057,1561,1470,1418,1407,1407. For each nine-subset now delete rows without a witness at any selected other index until a domain is empty or stable. All 220 nine-subsets are covered by 19 representatives under the actual 12 relabelings, and all 19 yield an empty domain without branching. Numeric bits are exact 64-bit words holding the 84 far labels in two parts; cardinalities never exceed 84. The finite test uses no solver, geometry tolerance, support exclusion, or time limit. Run `python active_square_nine_cycle.py` with Python 3 and NumPy ≥2.0 for bitwise population counts.
