# Rank-43 complements and two actual rank-48 gluings from a 33-set

Work on the rank42 no-Fano two-type face. Let \(S\) be any one of its forced \(G(3,33)\) sets, so \(b=-1_S\), \(A1_S=6\mathbf1-4 1_S\), and \(y=b+\mathbf1/3\) has ordinary graph norm 22. Use the standard even rank-44 graph lattice \(U\) of Gram determinant 49. Its marks \((u_i,u_j)=3I-A+J/9\) span at every prime away from three, and \(3u_i\), \(u_i-u_j\) belong to and generate the original lattice. All marks in this note are actual marks of the hypothetical graph. The graph metric is seven times the original physical mark metric on evaluations.

Put \(m=\sum_{i\in S}u_i\). It belongs to \(U\) because \(33u_0\) belongs and all differences \(u_i-u_0\) do. Pairings of \(m\) with \(u_i\) are \(14/3\) on \(S\) and \(-7/3\) off \(S\); hence \(m^2=154\) and the pairing ideal with \(U\) is exactly \(7\mathbb Z\). The squarefree squared norm makes \(m\) primitive. If \(K=U\cap m^\perp\), the projection of \(U\) onto \(\mathbb R m\) is \(\mathbb Zm/22\), since \(154/7=22\). Thus \([U:K\oplus\mathbb Zm]=22\), and
\[
\operatorname{GramDet}K
=22^2\operatorname{GramDet}U/154
=154.
\]
The dual graph vector associated to \(y\) is \(-m/7\). In particular \(m\) is orthogonal over \(\mathbb Z_7\) to a rank-43 complement whose one discriminant line is perpendicular to this marked seven-residue line.

## True norm-four marks in \(K\)

Every actual triangle \(T\) gives \(u_T=\sum_{i\in T}u_i\in U\) of squared norm four. Its pairing with \(m\) is \(7(|T\cap S|-1)\). Thus the triangles meeting \(S\) once give norm-four vectors in \(K\). In graph evaluations these vectors have the form \(D_T+\mathbf1/3\), where \(D_T=\sum_{i\in T}d_i\) is \(+1\) precisely on \(T\), \(-1\) on the 36 wing sites, zero elsewhere. Distinct triangles therefore give distinct vectors; their negatives have the other charge-three sign and cannot equal these positive-charge vectors, since \(D_T+D_{T'}=-2\mathbf1/3\) would not be integral.

Let \(\tau\) be the number of triangle components of \(S\), and let \(t_i\) count actual graph triangles meeting \(S\) in \(i\) sites. Then \(t_3=\tau\), \(t_2+3t_3=33\) counts the 33 induced edges of \(S\), and \(t_1+2t_2+3t_3=33\cdot7\) counts site-triangle incidences. Hence \(t_1=165+3\tau\). In particular \(K\) has at least \(330+6\tau\) oriented ordinary norm-four vectors.

## Two explicit positive even rank-five complements

The discriminant of the codimension-one complement \(K\) has order 154, squarefree. At prime two and eleven, \(U\) is unimodular and its primitive rank-one line \(\mathbb Z_p m\) has discriminant opposite to \(K\). At seven, \(m\) splits off: all pairings are multiples of seven and its length has valuation one. Its norm-\(11/7\) discriminant line has square numerator in the anisotropic plane of square determinant, so the remaining discriminant line of \(K\) also has square numerator; the opposite line has nonsquare numerator. **In the statements below the quadratic form is the squared norm divided by two modulo one.** Consequently any candidate complement must have the correct type at seven as well as at two and eleven.

Let \(P_A\) be the even positive rank-five lattice generated by a standard \(A_4\) basis and one vector \(v\) with \(v^2=32\), pairing one with the second simple root and zero with the other three. Let \(P_B\) be generated by \(A_2\oplus A_1\oplus A_1\) and a vector \(w\) with \(w^2=14\), pairing one with a simple \(A_2\) root and with the first \(A_1\) root, zero with the remaining roots. Their Schur complements to the four root coordinates are \(154/5\), \(154/12\), respectively; both determinants are 154.

**Normalization precaution.** At seven, the form on \(K\) is in the original graph lattice \(U\). For these two proposed complements to glue, one must compare their seven-parts with \(-q_K\) in that normalization, not with an auxiliary form after a nonsquare similarity. In the rank-four standard auxiliary lattice with Gram \(B_0\oplus B_0\), \(B_0=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&4\end{smallmatrix}\right]\), its coordinate-one discriminant generator has \(q=1/7\), and the \(q\)-form is the nonsplit sum of two such lines. The line paired with \(m/7\) in this auxiliary space has \(q=-11/7=3/7\) modulo one. Its auxiliary perpendicular line therefore has nonsquare numerator. This is the exact complementary discriminant of \(K\): the original seven-plane of \(U\) is the *negative* of this auxiliary plane up to the chosen anti-isometry, so the original marked line \(11/7\equiv4/7\) is **square**, and its original perpendicular line also has square numerator. Thus \(-q_K\) has nonsquare numerator, agreeing with both candidate complements.

At seven the two candidate discriminant numerators are respectively \(11/5=5\) and \(11/12=5\) in \(\mathbb F_7\), nonsquare. At eleven their rank-one ratios to \(\langle154\rangle\) are \(1/5\) and \(1/12\), both squares modulo eleven. At two, \(P_A\)'s Schur rank-one form differs from \(\langle154\rangle\) by the unit \(1/5\equiv1\bmod4\), so has the same quadratic value \(1/4\) on its order-two discriminant. For \(P_B\) the second \(A_1\) is an orthogonal direct summand and carries the entire two-part, giving again \(1/4\). Hence each \(P_A,P_B\) has discriminant anti-isometric to that of \(K\). Gluing along either anti-isometry produces an even unimodular rank-48 lattice \(J_A,J_B\).

## Their entire root shells are in the auxiliary summand

Every \(x\in K^*\) lifts to \(z=x+a m/154\in U^*\) with \(a=(z,m)\in\mathbb Z\): extend the pairing functional on \(K\) to \(U\), since \(K\) is primitive. The different lifts differ by \(\mathbb Z m/7\), so \(a\) may be chosen with \(|a|\le11\). For a nonzero \(x\) its lift \(z\) is also nonzero. If \(z^2<22/7\), the two-type graph shell minima force \(z\) to be a true neutral \(N_{18}\) vector of squared norm \(18/7\). Then \(|a|\le9\), by the already checked mixed angle against \(y\). Thus \(x^2\ge18/7-81/154=45/22>2\) in this case. Otherwise \(z^2\ge22/7\) gives \(x^2\ge22/7-121/154=33/14>2\). This proves \(\min(K^*)>2\).

No unimodular gluing root can have a nonzero \(K^*\) projection. If its projection vanishes, its auxiliary vector belongs to \(P_A\) or \(P_B\) itself. Their positive Schur complements exceed two, so their roots are exactly the \(A_4\) system (20 oriented roots) or the \(A_2+2A_1\) system (10). The degree-two rank-48 harmonic identity now yields, for either gluing, an actual norm-four shell sum. With \(R=20\) or 10 the number of roots, \(Y_2\) the number of squared-norm-four vectors, and \(P_K\) the rank43 orthogonal projection, the harmonic polynomial \(\|P_K v\|^2-(43/48)\|v\|^2\) gives
\[
\sum_{v^2=4}\|P_K v\|^2=\frac{43}{12}Y_2+86R.
\]
Indeed the root sum is \(-43R/24\), and weight 26 on the unimodular rank48 cusp has second Fourier coefficient \(-48\) times the first. The same principle applies to projection onto the one auxiliary direction orthogonal to the four roots' span, where the norm-four projection sum is \(Y_2/12+2R\). Both are actual necessary identities, not a numerical exclusion.
