# A 308-direction tight frame in every Hoffman residual

Let C be any 22-coclique in a putative SRG(99,14,1,2). Let X be its 77 exterior vertices, D their 22-by-77 C-block incidence, and A_X the exterior adjacency. Write Q=9I−3A_X+J−D^t D, so Q²=21Q. For an integer coefficient vector v on X write z(v)=Qv, a true C-zero integral minus-four field with ordinary squared norm 21 v^tQv. In the residual mark lattice L0 the corresponding vector has squared norm v^tQv; it lies in L0 before any local extension at three. The seven extension is arbitrary. The 22-site fiber residue of a single outside mark is 1−D_b (on the 22 points), up to addition of any word of K=ker(D^t mod 3). For z=0 the fiber residue belongs to K; see the [fiber construction](hoffman-22site-fibers.md). Except when discussing lifted residues, no hypothesis on a sparse short-shell palette is required.

Each outside vertex lies on four exterior edges capped in C, one per point of its C-block, and on three triangles entirely in X. There are 154 such capped edges and 77 such triangles. Use the following 308 representatives with the indicated orientations, together with their antipodes:
- 77 singletons, v=e_b;
- 154 capped exterior edges ij, v=e_i+e_j;
- 77 exterior triangles T, v=sum_{i in T} e_i.

The diagonal of Q is six. For distinct points its entries are 1−3A_{ij}−|D_i∩D_j|, between −3 and 1. On a capped edge Q_{ij}=−3. The three C-blocks of an exterior triangle are pairwise disjoint since each triangle edge has its unique cap inside that triangle. On its three edges Q_{ij}=−2. Thus in all three families v^tQv=6 and z(v)^2=126. With the listed orientation their residues have signed compositions (18,0), (6,15), (0,12), respectively. The residue of a triangle specifically equals −1_{U_T}, where U_T is its twelve-point union.

**No repetitions up to sign.** Singletons cannot coincide up to sign since |Q_{ij}|≤3<6. A capped edge has value 3 at its two endpoints. Equality with +Qe_b would require a distinct-row entry 3, impossible; equality with −Qe_b would require b adjacent to both endpoints, impossible since their unique common vertex is the cap in C. Distinct capped edges do not give equal z: with four distinct endpoints the norm v^tQv of their signed difference is at least 24+2(−6−4)=4, and any shared endpoint cancels leaving a difference of two distinct singletons. For opposite z, shared endpoints already contradict the values 3. If the edges are disjoint, at an endpoint b of the second edge the first gives 2−3a−s=−3, where a≤1 counts adjacencies to the first edge and s≤4 sums the two C-block intersections. Thus a=1, s=2. Symmetrically all four endpoints form a square. Every diagonal already shares both opposite endpoints as neighbors and has no C-block intersection. The sole adjacent rung then accounts for s by itself, but two adjacent vertices have C-intersection at most one.

For a triangle T, z_T equals 2 at its three vertices and equals 3−3a−h elsewhere, where a≤1 counts adjacencies to T and h=|D_b∩U_T|. No singleton ±Qe_b can equal z_T: at three vertices this would require 2, which is impossible off diagonal, or −2, which requires adjacency (nonadjacent block intersections are at most two). No vertex can be adjacent to all three vertices of T by the unique-cap condition.

Two distinct triangles sharing a vertex cannot give opposite vectors by value 2 at that vertex, or equal ones since each remaining vertex of one has an adjacency to the other and thus takes value ≤0 there. For disjoint triangles T,T', equality would force h=1,a=0 at all three sites of T', so |U_T∩U_T'|=3. The zero difference would give a K-word −1_{U_T}+1_{U_T'} of balanced weight 18 (nine of each sign). Opposite vectors would force h=2,a=1 at all three, so the intersection is six and the sum gives a K-word of weight 18 (six versus twelve). Neither exists. For completeness, if a K-word has signed support sizes p,q then every block has the same number of plus as minus mod 3. Pair counts give (p−q)(15−p−q)=0: sum (i−j) over blocks is 14(p−q); sum (i−j)(i+j−1) is 2(p−q)(p+q−1); for allowed block types (0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(3,0),(0,3) the latter equals twice the former. Thus weight18 would be balanced. For p=q=9, the equations for point and pair slots demand 36 (2,2), 18 (1,1), and 12 each of (3,0),(0,3), total 78 blocks >77.

Finally, equality z_T=w_ij for a capped edge requires both endpoints have value 3 in z_T, hence both D_i,D_j miss U_T. The residue r(ij)−r(T) then has six + and three −, impossible by the signed K identity. Equality to −w_ij requires value −3 for both endpoints, i.e. both have a=1,h=3. Their contacts in T are distinct since ij already has its cap in C. At the uncontacted remaining vertex t of T, w_ij(t)=−2, so exactly four C-pair intersections with i,j are needed, two with each. Then t and i share those two C neighbors and also the T-vertex adjacent to i, contradicting μ=2.

**Angular sum.** For any actual C-zero field z with A_X z=−4z,
```
sum_{ij capped} (z_i+z_j)^2 + sum_{T exterior} (sum_{i in T} z_i)^2 = 3 ||z||^2.
```
The first incidence Gram is 4I+A_cap. The second is 3I+A_ext, since every remaining edge is in one exterior triangle. Their sum is 7I+A_X. Thus the 308 representatives, including the singletons, give total angular energy 4||z||². In Euclidean residual-mark units their frame operator is 84I (the nonzero eigenvalue of Q is 21). In particular [q^3]theta(L0)≥616, from 308 distinct antipodal pairs. Each representative contributes 3^k distinct pairs (z,r), k=dim K; the union of the three families and their antipodes contributes 616·3^k to the n=126 composition mass.

This is an unconditional-on-palette statement for actual 22-cocliques, not a proof that a general putative graph has such a coclique and not a rank or sparse-face exclusion.
