# At most six norm-18 decompositions of one norm-22 field

Assume the two occurring types on the twelve-unit-type face are neutral norms 18 and 24. Fix a maximal determinant-49 extension H of the graph lattice. All fields below are eligible in this same H, and neutral fields have the ordinary Euclidean inner product on their 99 coordinates (so the squared norm of an integer field is the sum of its coordinate squares, not the squared norm of the associated rank-44 vector). Then every oriented neutral norm-22 field c is the sum of at most **six unordered pairs** of oriented norm-18 fields. A norm-24 field admits at most one such unordered decomposition.

We use the [unit-shell substitution identities](../short-shell-moments.md#when-the-nonzero-labels-must-all-be-signed-units). In particular raw G(1,25) is absent: its unit type is absent and the only nonunit source is neutral norm 16, also absent. Raw G(1,27) consists exactly of t+d_j for norm-18 t at a negative site j; the other sources (unit (1,27) and (2,26)) are absent. Here d_i=3e_i−Ae_i, charge one, squared norm 23, with d_i·t=7t_i for any neutral field t. A translate by d_i preserves seven-compatibility. We use also the established absence of Fano/N14 on this two-type face. Independent arguments for the two raw substitutions are in the short-shell tests.

All eligible norm-22 and norm-30 fields are unit (nonzero coordinates ±1): a coordinate of absolute value at least two would give via a signed site translation a charge-one field of norm at most 17 or 25 respectively. At norm 30 the only possible exceptional magnitude is two by the charge-one minimum of 21, and G(1,25) is absent. All norm-18 fields have independent positive and negative 9-sites and degree four across these signs.

One further site fact is needed and does *not* require a Hoffman coclique. If an eligible neutral norm-32 field v is nonunit, it has exactly one +2_p and one −2_q, and t'=d_p−d_q−v is an eligible norm-18 field. No magnitude three is possible, by the charge-one minimum. If the exceptional site is initially positive of value two, b=d_p−v is raw (1,27) with b_p=1. Write b=t'+d_q, where q is negative in t'. There is exactly one +2 in b, at q; all other positive sites are the positive sites of t' missing q, an independent set missing q. There is no −2 since the negative sites of t' are independent. Thus p is a positive site of t' missing q. In v=d_p−b the only possible further magnitude-two entry is −2 at q: any other would require a positive neighbor of p in b. Conversely a negative exception in v is handled by negation, so both occur. This applies to all eligible neutral norm-32 fields on the face, not just the zero-on-coclique part.

In a decomposition c=t+u the two norm-18 vectors have prescribed nonzero norm class in the residual two-dimensional anisotropic quadratic space over F7. Their classes sum to a nonzero class of c. On that affine line of equal norms, the norm equation has at most two solutions (a nonzero vector of an anisotropic plane cannot give a degenerate quadratic coefficient). The solutions are interchanged by β↔[c]−β. A coincident class would give ||c||²≡4·18 mod7, different from 22 and 24, so there are exactly two distinct ordered classes when a decomposition exists. Orient all decompositions (t_i,u_i) by putting class β first. For i≠j one must have t_i·t_j=4 or −3: the raw dot product is congruent to 18 mod 7. Beyond these integers, a distinct same-class pair with dot 11 or −10 would give neutral norm 14 or 16, respectively; dot −17 is below the universal minimum on summing, and dot 18 means identical.

For ||c||²=24 one has t_i·c=12. A second decomposition would give t_i·u_j=12−t_i·t_j in {8,15}; subtracting these two norm-18 fields gives forbidden norm 20 or 6. Thus the claimed uniqueness holds. For ||c||²=22, t_i·c=11, and t_i·u_j=11−t_i·t_j cannot be 14 because the difference would have norm 8. Hence all t_i·t_j=4. Put g_i=2t_i−c=t_i−u_i. These integral neutral fields are perpendicular to c; their Gram diagonal is 50 and every off-diagonal is −6. They equal ±1 at each of the 22 nonzero sites of c because exactly one of t_i,u_i is nonzero there; off that support their values are 0 or ±2. For any even subset I the half-sum z_I=(1/2)∑_{i∈I}g_i is an *integer H-eligible neutral field*, since it equals ∑t_i−(|I|/2)c, with squared norm |I|(56−6|I|)/4.

Suppose there were seven distinct decompositions, discarding any extra ones. For |I|=2, the preceding norm is 22 and z_I is unit, so at any exterior site there is at most one positive and at most one negative g_i. In particular no four-element z_J can be exceptional outside the support of c. For |I|=6 the norm is 30 and z_I is unit. On each c-site the seven signs must therefore split three against four: if at least five have the same sign, choose six containing five of them to get |z_I|≥2. Every c-site now belongs to exactly one monochromatic four-subset J. Here |J|=4 gives norm 32. For each J with any monochromatic site, z_J has exactly the two exceptional sites +2_p,−2_q by the norm-32 fact, both on c. These pairs over different J are disjoint and cover the 22 sites of c.

Moreover c_p=c_q in every pair. Indeed the reconstructed t'=d_p−d_q−z_J has t'·c=7(c_p−c_q) since z_J⊥c. Opposite c-signs would give dot of absolute value 14 and produce a nonzero integer neutral norm-12 field by adding or subtracting c and t'. This contradicts the universal minimum 14. The partition would consequently pair the eleven positive sites of c among themselves, impossible. This proves the upper bound six.

For the overlap graph whose vertices are the unoriented H-eligible norm-18 vectors, an exceptional pair of absolute dot 6 or 7 chooses uniquely the sign of the sum up to global negation. It therefore corresponds to one decomposition counted on each of two opposite oriented norm-24 or norm-22 resultants. If E6,E7 count these exceptional unoriented pairs and N24,N22 count the eligible oriented neutral fields, then
```
2E6 ≤ N24,              2E7 ≤ 6N22.
```
These inequalities do not by themselves contradict the remaining arithmetic profiles.
