# Eight distinct 44-sets in the last cost-21 population profile

In the rank-42 \(\{018,121,125\}\) portfolio consider the [sole surviving aggregate](cost21-four-coclique-walk.md), with \(G_{121}=80,\ G_{335}=0,\ G_{444}=8\) and ten \(121\) fields in every signed residue. The raw G335 zero is certified from `3G335=−3168−172r−256f+184M−12S−10Q`, with `f=N14/2`, `Q=G444`, by the same original-site chart. No graph witness follows. Each of the eight nonzero signed residues on the \(444\) circle contains exactly **one** \(444\) field. Its negative-indicator support \(X\) is a 44-set with \(A1_X=8\mathbf1-4\,1_X\). More generally the \(125,129,232,333,444\) populations agree in each pair of opposite signed classes.

For each norm-four auxiliary quadratic row of [the unsigned proof](rank42_u121_residues.py), subtract the row for the negative gluing. This kills all isotropic contributions, the pure-auxiliary root correction, and all neutral fields by charge-zero negation. It replaces each remaining opposite-pair population in positive charge by its *difference*. The physical \(121\) differences vanish (the edge-reflection interchanges opposite signed classes), the \(127\) differences vanish because each signed \(127\) comes from translating one of nine same-sign sites of an \(018\) field and the neutral population has equal opposite counts, and \(335\) is empty here. The resulting matrix on the five remaining anisotropic positive-charge shells has 20 columns and exact rank 20 as checked [here](cost21_eight_signed_44.py). Hence their skews all vanish. The published even chart makes \(444\) equally distributed on all four projective classes, since \(121\) is uniform. The total eight now proves the assertion. The equitable property uses only \(\sum b=-44,\ \sum b^2=44,\ Ab=-4b-8\).

For either of the sorted \(018\) populations before the [26-point split cut](cost21-26-tight-frames.md) \((33,26)\) or \((30,29)\) the signed *count* constraints do not by themselves contradict the profile. For example, take unsigned \(333\) deviations all zero. In lexicographic representative order the signed \(018\) counts are \(a,b,b,a\), \(125\) counts \(79+d,79-d,79-d,79+d\), \(022\) counts \(45.5-d/6,45.5+d/6,45.5+d/6,45.5-d/6\), where \(d=-63\) or \(-9\) respectively. The \(333\) count is 33 in each class. These are not graph constructions.

**A graph consequence.** Every original Hoffman 22-coclique \(C\) meets any one of these \(X\) in 9 or 16 vertices. Isotropy of \(C\) forces \(k=|X\cap C|\equiv2\bmod7\); at \(k=2\) the field \(\mathbf1-1_X-1_C\) has charge \(-3\) and squared cost \(33+2k=37\), forbidden. (The same conclusion applies to any actual \(X\) on this face.) For one fixed \(X\), call \(C\) high if \(k=16\). Let \(C,D\) be two high cocliques meeting only at \(s\). Then \(f=1_C+1_D-1_X\) is a neutral cost-26 field. If \(s\) were in \(X\), this field would be signed-unit, forbidden. Thus \(s\notin X\), \(f_s=2\), and \(u=d_s-f\) is a \(121\) field: its charge is one and its squared cost is \(23+26-2\cdot14=21\). For every original coclique \(E\), using the previously proved \(u\cdot(-1_E)=4\) yields
\[
 |X\cap E|=|C\cap E|+|D\cap E|-7[\,s\in E\,].
\]
Consequently every third high coclique meets both in eight, and no third coclique can meet both in one. The one-overlap graph induced on the high cocliques is a matching. These deductions make no transitivity assumption.
