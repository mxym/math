# How repeated triple differences project to a 14-site root graph

Assume the rank-38 support is contained in N18,N24,U226. Work throughout with actual graph markings, not graph orbits. Write T for the number of seeds with three markings. By [the all-seven triple theorem](rooted-norm18-triples.md), T≥9N18−110880 and N18≥13772. Each of its six **ordered** pairs yields an oriented neutral norm-22 field r=t−u, zero on the closed neighborhood of its positive root. Let m(r) count all such incidences. Then Σ_r m(r)=6T; both red/blue sign orientations of r are included in the sum.

On this support, in each fixed maximal determinant-49 extension H the exact harmonic row is
```
4W022 = 12V018 −420−14W014−2W335−S,      S=W123−99−7W014.
```
The [exact verifier](norm18_norm22_root_capacity.py) eliminates just the nine absent **unit** coordinates in the native higher moment matrix. All subtracted nonconstant populations are nonnegative. The two neutral nonisotropic oriented shells both average with factor 344. Consequently N22≤3N18−36120≤12378, using N18≤16166. Combining these bounds shows that **some oriented N22 has m(r)≥16**: otherwise 6(9N18−110880)≤15(3N18−36120), or N18≤13720.

Each r has independent sign sides of size 11. Indeed the field is signed-unit by the usual translation at a doubled site, and translating down at an adjacent positive pair would yield the absent raw (2,30) up to sign; similarly on the negative side. Its support is thus 4-regular bipartite. For x outside it put h(x)=|N(x)∩r⁺|=|N(x)∩r⁻|. Then Σh=110 and Σ binom(h,2)=44: the 11 positives require 110 pair witnesses, 66 being supplied by the 11 negatives. Hence n0≤77−110+44=11 by summing (h−1)(h−2)/2. All actual roots contributing to m(r) are among these at most 11 sites.

Fix such a zero-height root o. Every site outside its closed neighborhood is identified uniquely by the two distinct, nonmated vertices in N(o) that neighbor it. Mark the eleven r-positive parent pairs red, the eleven r-negatives blue. Their simple union K on the 14 sites has every red and blue degree equal to h. Write M for the matching on N(o) and also its adjacency matrix. For any contributing seed Q, the four vertices of Q have no mate edge among them, and precisely four of the six pairs internal to Q are colored (two each color). The uncolored internal pairs are common positive sites of t,u; together with o these are all common positives. Q are the only common negatives. Among the red sites, those meeting Q are t-positive and those disjoint from Q are u-negative; among blue sites, those meeting Q are u-positive and those disjoint are t-negative. This follows from the triple shape: the four pair labels and the four singleton positives of each marking all meet Q, and an extra negative cannot. Thus the ordered pair (t,u) is uniquely determined by r,o,Q.

In particular a necessary small kernel condition is
```
 (A_K−I−M)(1−2·1_Q)=0.
```
At q in Q the incidence to colored pairs entirely inside Q is h(q), since the three t-positive nonroot neighbors of q consist of its h(q) red contacts and the internal uncolored pairs touching q. At w outside Q, put a_+,a_− for its red and blue neighbors in Q. The sum of t on its incident far-site pairs is a_++a_−−h(w), which must be zero when its mate is in Q and −1 otherwise by At=−4t. Thus in general A_K 1_Q=h−1+1_Q+M1_Q, and A_K1=2h. Nullity one over the rationals suffices for at most one Q: all candidate sign vectors have sum six.

**An exact two-seed distance.** Let Q,Q′ be distinct contributing seeds for the same ordered r,o. Write m=|Q∩Q′|, k=4−m, and j for the number of actual mate edges between X=Q\Q′ and Y=Q′\Q. Let O=Q∩Q′, Z be the remaining root neighbors. Write e_U for edges of K internal to U and e_UV for its U-to-V edges. At X the kernel equation summed and subtracted over the two seeds gives 2e_X−e_XY=k−j; the same holds at Y, so e_X=e_Y. Also e_OX=e_OY=4−e_O−e_X, and e_XZ=e_YZ=e_OX+k−j, since h sums on X to e_OX+2e_X. Across the 22 r-sites the site values of t_Q and t_Q′ differ exactly where one projected pair meets exactly one of Q,Q′: these are 2(e_X+e_XZ)=2(4−e_O+k−j) changed sites. Off the r-sites only the two four-sets of common negatives and three-sets of common positives matter. The latter share o and ell=binom(m,2)−e_O uncolored internal pairs. Opposite signs are impossible there, since w∈N(o) hosts all common negatives and the two extra common positives are far sites. It follows that
```
 d=||t_Q−t_Q′||²=2(4−e_O+k−j)+2k+2(2−ell)=28−m(m+3)−2j.
```
Now z=t_Q+u_Q′=t_Q′+u_Q has norm 50−d (use t_Q·r=t_Q′·r=11). Its positive doubled sites are precisely o and the ell shared uncolored pairs. Its negative doubled sites are the m common parents. At all r-sites the possible sums are 0,±1. Thus when ell=0 the actual field w=d_o−z of charge one is unit, with norm 45−d. Its number of positive neighbors at o is m, and an ordinary d_i or Fano-site nonunit translate cannot be such a mark. The [norm-18 distance bound](../short-shell-moments.md#bounding-the-norm-18-alternative) gives d≥16.

* m=3 is impossible by d≤10. For m=2, d=18−2j and N16=0 force j=0. If ell=0 this gives the forbidden unit (1,27). Hence the unique pair in O is uncolored; let x be its far site. Then w=d_o−z has value −2 at x; b=w+d_x has positive charge two, norm 27+23+2(−14+2)=26, and is unit. Its positive locations are exactly o,x (which are nonadjacent), and at the two common parents its value is zero. Thus these two Q intersect at the common-zero pair of a real (2,26) marking. At o the exclusive four sites of the two Q are exactly the other four b-zero neighbors, so for fixed (b,o) there are at most three unordered candidate partitions into Q,Q′.
* m≤1 has ell=0. For m=0, d=28−2j with 0≤j≤4; for m=1, d=24−2j with 0≤j≤3. The signed-unit w must have norm ≥21 and cannot have costs 21,25,27 on this support. Also N16 and N20 are absent. Thus only (m,j)=(0,3),(1,1) are possible. Both yield d=22 and a genuine extra signed-unit (1,23) mark w with a positive root o and exactly m positive neighbors there.

This is a necessary structural restriction and a forcing of a 16-incidence norm-22 marking. It is not a proof of a 15-incidence upper bound.
