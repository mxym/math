# Three-parent saturation and recurrent norm-18 supports

Use the rank-38 hypothesis and the exact lower bound N≥11,576 of [the four-family window](norm18-rank38-four-window.md), with N the actual oriented norm-18 population.

Fix a true positive root o and three independent neighbors. There are exactly eight possible fourth negative parents, the two vertices on each of the four other mate axes. Each four-parent seed has at most two markings by the [triangle-free root theorem](norm18-triple-remaining-bridges.md), since neither norm 16 nor norm 20 occurs. Call a three-parent choice *saturated* if it carries sixteen markings, two for every extension. Each marking supplies 9·4 three-parent choices. There are 99·C(7,3)·8=27,720 choices. If B are saturated then 36N≤15·27,720+B; hence **B≥936**. The arithmetic threshold is tight enough that eliminating just these full three-parent choices, not all marking pairs, would already refute this four-family window. No such elimination is assumed.

There is a second constraint on the literal 9-by-9 incidence graphs of the two signs. At any incident pair of markings at a fixed four-parent seed, the two positive pair-center sets each have exactly four sites: they each have at least four, and their intersection has at most two by [the projective-root theorem](panel-heights.md). Each marking therefore has no other positive missing all four parents (the degree-two count at the parents is four plus the degree-zero count). In particular if two positive sites of one marking have disjoint negative neighborhoods within its 4-regular support, their positive four-parent seeds are both singletons.

Within either sign there are 36 unordered pairs, with 54 total opposite-side common-neighbor incidences. Each pair has zero, one, or two common neighbors. Consequently the number of twice-covered pairs is 18 plus the number of disjoint pairs. Twice-covered pair counts for the two signs agree because both count all internal four-cycles of the support. Thus if disjoint pairs occur in either orientation, they occur in both. Let E count oriented markings with no disjoint pairs in either sign. Every one of the other N−E oriented fields then has at least two singleton positive seeds. The 9N marking-seed incidences plus the singleton deficit are at most 2·55,440, so
```
2E≥11N−110,880,    E≥8,228.
```
There are at least 4,114 distinct actual exceptional 18-site supports, since the oriented signing on a support is unique up to sign (the normalized sums and differences of two different signings on that support would be integral eigenfields of total squared norm 18, below 14+14). In the existing [26-shape support catalogue](panel-heights.md), the exceptional criterion selects nine oriented isomorphism types. The [standalone integer catalogue replay](norm18_three_parent_supports.py) verifies the split. The counting argument does not need the catalogue or assume that any listed pattern can occur in a true graph.

In fact at least **2,440 distinct literal supports have at most one singleton among all eighteen signed roots**. Each singleton positive seed is owned by a unique oriented marking, so the total over the N/2 antipodal supports and their eighteen roots is at most 110,880−9N. If H supports have at most one, the total is at least 2(N/2−H). Thus 2H≥10N−110,880. Every such support is exceptional by the disjoint-pair argument. For a choice of orientation, a partner at a negative root is reoriented back to agree with the chosen field. The resulting at least seventeen companions of any one such field are distinct, even up to sign. Dot seven gives three common positives and four common negatives at a positive seed, and dot nine gives four and five by the rooted panel theorem. Two positive roots of the same compatible pair would therefore have four negative neighbors each among at most five common negatives and so at least three common neighbors. Nor can the same pair share an oppositely signed root, because the common-positive/common-negative counts would have to reverse. No pair with opposite orientations can evade this: a compatible pair has no opposite-sign overlap, whereas each partner shares sites with the given field.
