# A rank-22 lattice and theta cuts at an actual Hoffman coclique

Assume the only occurring types among the twelve selected unit types in the rank-44 shell test are neutral norms 18 and 24. Fix a maximal seven-extension H of the graph lattice, and one actual compatible Hoffman coclique C, of size 22. Such a C exists on this face by the [two-type residue balance](two-type-residue-balance.md). We use an *additional* choice of maximal isotropic extension at three, defined below. Every choice yields exact residual theta identities, and in that residual there are at most **32 oriented zero-on-C norm-18 fields and 74 oriented zero-on-C norm-24 fields**. The counts need not equal the full compatible counts unless the local ternary condition is automatic. No existence or whole-rank conclusion is claimed.

## Construction and graph dictionary

Write B for the 77 other vertices, D for the 22-by-77 incidence matrix from C to B, and A_B for adjacency within B. The 77 four-subsets form a 2-(22,4,2) design, so DDᵀ=12I+2J. Use the usual tight graph marks u_i in 44-space: their Gram is 3I−A+J/9 and their frame operator is 7I. Thus a compatible neutral field t is realized by y=(1/7)∑t_i u_i, with t_i=(y,u_i) and 7y²=∑t_i². Here a field always counts with its orientation. Oblique charge offsets are not part of this zero-on-C neutral lattice.

The 22 marks on C have Gram 3I+J/9, hence span a 22-space. Orthogonally project onto the complementary 22-space by P. For each block b take m_b=√3 P u_b. Indeed the removed projection of u_b is (1/21)∑_{i∈C}u_i−(1/3)∑_{i∈b}u_i (test its pairings with the 22 C marks). This gives the 77-by-77 Gram and frame
```
Q = 9I−3A_B+J−DᵀD,        Q²=21Q,     diag Q=6.
```
The latter follows since ∑ m_b⊗m_b=21I in the complementary space. The lattice L generated by these 77 marks is therefore even integral of rank 22. Its dual vectors correspond *bijectively* by x↦((x,m_b)) to the integer neutral −4 eigenfields that vanish on C, extended by zero there: the corresponding graph vector is y=√3 x. In particular ∑t_b²=21x². The frame gives 21x=∑(x,m_b)m_b for x∈L*, so the discriminant exponent divides 21. These statements require only the existence of C, not the two-type condition.

At three write k=dim ker Dᵀ over F3. For a lift z in this kernel, DDᵀ yields 3|∑z_i and 3|∑z_i², so the kernel K is self-orthogonal and lies in 1⊥. As each column of D has sum 4, Q=Dᵀ(J−I)D mod 3. The column space U=K⊥ contains 1, which has norm 22≡1. On U the form J−I has radical ⟨1⟩⊕K: split off 1 and use the ordinary dot form on its orthogonal complement. Consequently rank(Q mod 3)=21−2k; the surjection ℤ^77→L is split as abelian groups, so the same rank is obtained from an integral basis Gram. The discriminant at three is elementary of dimension 1+2k. Take any maximal isotropic subgroup there, of dimension k (odd-dimensional nondegenerate quadratic space over F3), so the new residual is a nondegenerate line.

At seven take specifically the rank-44 integral extension U_H with determinant 49 and anisotropic residual plane. All graph marks belong to U_H locally at seven (the charge-index extension has only powers of three). Put v=−∑_{i∈C}u_i/7. This is exactly the graph vector for the charge-two field −1_C, since its mark pairings are −1+2/9 on C, 2/9 off C. It lies in U_H at seven: compatibility gives dual membership and the squared length 22/9 has integral seven-valuation, forcing its seven-class to be zero in the residual anisotropic plane. The integral span of the C marks locally has determinant 3^19·49 (from 3I+J/9). Adding v gives an integral, index-7 overlattice that is unimodular locally at seven. Therefore the orthogonal summand perpendicular to the C span in U_H has residual discriminant the same anisotropic plane. Take this summand with form scaled by 3 as the seven-extension of L. It contains each P u_b by unimodular splitting; multiplication of the form by a seven-adic unit leaves anisotropy and order unchanged. This is a rational similarity description, not an assumption that √3 exists in ℚ_7. At other primes L is already unimodular.

Patching these local finite-index overlattices (equivalently taking the direct sum of the isotropic subgroups in L*/L) gives an even rank-22 lattice T, containing L, determinant 3·7²=147. At seven a residual dual vector x defines the original compatible y=√3 x. In rational coordinates where the residual form is scaled by 3, this map is x↦3x, a unit at seven. At three the maximal isotropic subgroup was a choice: T* is a sublattice of L*, so every dual label is genuine, but not every genuine label has to occur in one specified T*. At the other primes there is no new local restriction. In particular, for k=0 any zero-on-C compatible norm-18 or norm-24 field occurs. For k>0 and such a field, its three-class in L*/L is isotropic (the norm divided by 21 is integral at three). It occurs for every maximal subgroup containing its class; a nonzero isotropic line has inclusion fraction 1/(3^k+1). This follows by counting incidences of isotropic lines with maximal subgroups in the parabolic (2k+1)-space: there are (3^{2k}−1)/2 lines and each maximal contains (3^k−1)/2. In an odd residual of dimension 1 a zero-norm class perpendicular to the subgroup belongs to that subgroup. The zero class occurs always.

## Exact coefficient conditions

Take F=θ_T and D(q)=θ_{√21 T*}, with exponent half the squared length (distinct from the incidence matrix D above). Write D_j=[q^j]D(q); these count graph fields of squared norm 2j in this particular residual dual. Both lattices are even of level 21. The finite quadratic form at three has q-value +1/3 on either nonzero class: the Milgram normalized Gauss sum is exp(2πi·22/8)=−i and the anisotropic seven-plane has Gauss sum −1, so the three-line has sum +i. At odd primes the even level equals the exponent here. Thus D_j=0 when j≡2 mod 3: multiplying the discriminant norm j/21 by seven gives j/3 and kills the seven-part. Also F_j=D_{21j}: an integral norm q-value in T*/T must vanish separately in each of the two anisotropic primary spaces, so the vector belongs to T.

The universal minimum of a nonzero neutral graph field is 14, and at 14 it is supported on a bipartite degree-four pair of 7-sets, each independent. The 7·binom(4,2)=42 incidences inside the opposite sign exhaust the 2·binom(7,2)=42 common-neighbor slots of each sign. A coclique C disjoint from the support could therefore see at most 22 incidences from the positive sign, whereas those seven sites send 28 edges to C. Thus even a globally present Fano vector would not give D7. The two-type face excludes neutral norms 16 and 20 outright and neutral norm 26 including its nonunit fields (the latter require (1,21), absent). Hence
```
F0=D0=1;    D1,…,D8=D10=D13=0.
```
All these cuts pertain to scalar, unweighted populations.

For the modular-form calculation, the theta theorem gives weight 11, level 21 and character χ_{−3} with the square factors in (−147/·) removed at units. We include a dimension argument to certify the span. The modular curve X0(21) has index 32, four cusps, no elliptic points of order two and two of order three, hence genus one by the genus formula. At either order-three point choose an order-three stabilizer of trace −1; its lower-right entry is 1 modulo three since c=0 modulo 3 and d²+d+1=0 there. Thus χ(d)=1. With λ=cτ+d a primitive cube root and a local zero of order m the transformation gives λ^{−2m}=λ^{11}, hence m≡2 mod3. Valence 11·32/12=88/3 leaves degree at most 28 after removing the two forced contributions 2/3. More explicitly divide all forms by a fixed nonzero form of this character; the resulting functions on the compact genus-one curve have poles bounded by the divisor of that fixed form after removing the forced zeros. At cusps the character is trivial on the stabilizers (squarefree 21 implies d≡1 mod 21 for positive parabolics at its cusps); in any event additional forced vanishing could only reduce the degree. Riemann–Roch gives dimension at most 28 for this positive degree. The verifier exhibits 28 independent q-series through q^27, so they span.

Take the binary thetas a=∑q^{x²+xy+y²}, b=∑q^{x²+xy+2y²}; they have discriminants 3,7, weights one and the corresponding quadratic characters. The basis consists of explicitly listed monomials of total weight 11 from a(z),a(7z),b(z),b(3z),E4(dz),E6(dz) for d|21, with odd a-degree and even b-degree. Under full Fricke inversion the binary factors swap within each pair: each binary form is isometric to its appropriately rescaled dual (the adjugate of a binary Gram is integrally equivalent to it). Eisenstein factors of weight w swap d with 21/d. With the normalization taking θ_T to θ_{√21T*}, each product acquires √(147/δ)·(−1)^{#E6}, where δ multiplies the formal scaled determinants 3,147,7,63 for the binary factors and d^{2w} for E_w(dz). This follows by factoring out the common power (21z/i)^{11}; ordinary Eisenstein inversion then contributes i^w, giving precisely the negative signs at weight six. This sign and normalization matter.

The [standard-library verifier](hoffman_residual_theta.py) uses exact coefficient arithmetic through q^70. On the 28-dimensional Fourier coefficient vector the homogeneous conditions have rank 23; the remaining parameters may be chosen as D0,D9,D12,D15,D16. The following are **equalities**, not floating-point bounds:
```
394D9+16D16+D19+2D27+D33 = 13056,
6277D12+658D16+43D24+224D27+38D30 = 472520,
F1 = −8+2D9+3D12+D15+D16,
D18 = 8+D9+2D12+D15+D16.
```
The first two follow in the checker by appending coefficient rows without changing exact rank. Nonnegativity and antipodal parity of nonzero shells now give D9≤32 and D12≤74. In particular D18=F1+16−D9−D12 is the number of oriented norm-36 labels in this residual dual.

For connection with fields not vanishing on C, let t be an H-eligible neutral field and let z be its coordinates on C, extended by zero. Suppose ∑z_i=0. Then w=3t−(3I−A)z vanishes on C and ∑w_i²=9∑t_i²−21∑z_i² by the tight frame or the eigenvalue equation. Its residual vector is x=(1/7)∑_{b∈B}t_b m_b=√3 P y, and is in T* regardless of the maximal subgroup at three: it lies in L locally at three by the denominator seven, and is compatible at seven by the chosen orthogonal splitting. (Graph y=√3x associated to w is 3P y_t.) For norm 18, C hits kappa of each sign; when kappa=3 one obtains a norm-36 field in D18. The [ternary kernel argument](hoffman-ternary-rank.md) bounds this map by three-to-one at kappa=3. Thus there are at most 3D18 such oriented t for fixed H,C. This is a link between the residual count and the full graph, not a lower bound on D9.

The modular and lattice background is classical; see, e.g., [Diamond and Shurman](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-27226-9) for valence and modular curves, and [Böcherer and Nebe's account of theta series of maximal lattices](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/papers/MAX.pdf) for full/partial dual transforms. The graph-specific construction and the four numerical cuts above are separate from those general facts.

## Improvement from the actual coclique height switch

The [literal height switch](hoffman-kappa-literal.md#no-norm-18-field-can-avoid-the-actual-compatible-coclique) shows that no eligible norm-18 field vanishes on C. Thus on this two-type face D9=0 in every prescribed maximal ternary extension. The same exact coefficient calculation above gives a fifth row, valid before imposing this extra vanishing:

```
82D12+3D15+D27+D30 = 5256+67D9.
```

With D9=0, nonnegativity and antipodal parity improve the norm-24 cap to **D12≤64**. This is still a bound only in the specified residual dual, not a contradiction.
