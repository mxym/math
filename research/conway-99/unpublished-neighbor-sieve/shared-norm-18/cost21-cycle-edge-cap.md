# What the cost-21 reflection gives on the next shells

Use the notation of [the cost-21 reflection](cost21-hoffman-edge.md). All counts below are literal in one fixed eligible H; no division by eight is necessary. Let e=pq be the unique positive edge of u. Its edge reflection u′ has the same endpoints. The other three positives P0 of u and the other three positives P1 of u′ form the two shores of an induced six-cycle.

Indeed each endpoint has 7 neighbors in N=N(u). Their common cap r is negative: r positive would give extra positive edges, r=0 would give u′_r=−2, and (1,21) is unit. Exactly 13 negatives are thus adjacent to an endpoint, and the remaining three are P1 by the formula for u′. Every point in P1 has exactly two neighbors in P0. Every point in P0 has six N-neighbors and at most four of them near p,q by the two common-neighbor bounds μ=2. Hence it has exactly two P1-neighbors. Both triples are independent, so their cross graph is K3,3 minus a perfect matching. If G337(H)=0, then for each eligible D neither endpoint lies in D, and both u,u′ have angle 4. If D contains h points from P0, it contains h+4 from N. These each require two neighbors among the other 5−h positive sites, so h≥3 would put at least seven common neighbors at one pair (or leave fewer than two sites). Thus h≤2, as for P1. Since the only independent triples of a six-cycle are its two shores, |D∩(P0∪P1)|≤2.

**Neutral transport.** Assume G337(H)=0 and that the *unit* cost-26 neutral population vanishes. A cost-26 neutral t has an entry +2 or −2, and no entry of greater magnitude (translate at that entry by a site mark). A negative double at i gives u=t+d_i of type (1,21) with positive pivot i; conversely each such u and i gives t=u−d_i. Its cost-26 angle is 4−(4−7·1_D(i)), hence 0 or 7, and can be 7 only when i lies in D, necessarily outside e. In that case t has a unique −2: at any other negative site u−d_i is ≥−1 (u is unit and d_i is 0 or −1). Additionally it has no +2: a +2 would have to occur at another positive adjacent to i, contrary to its isolation. If t had only a positive double, −t would have a negative double, hence could not have angle −7. These statements give the precise per-D identity
```
# {eligible t neutral cost 26 : t·c_D=7} = sum_{u of type 121} |D∩P0(u)| ≤ G121(H).
```
The inequality groups the u's into fixed-point-free reflection pairs, at most two contributions each. In the usual notation both sides have the same nonisotropic /8 normalization. If unit 026 is present, the equality still holds for the nonunit part alone.

**A rank-42 residual-plane sparsity bound at a physical edge.** Assume rank 42, so every Hoffman coclique is original and available; the argument also applies in any H when N14(H)=0 or every eligible Fano has two eligible endpoints. For a general H endpoint eligibility is not automatic. Suppose raw N20,N24,G337 vanish in H. Each e supports at most four eligible (1,21) fields. For each such u put z=u−E/2 with E=d_p+d_q. Then z²=12, and the two reflected fields give antipodal z. The centered projection of z has the same squared norm (its sum is zero), and z is integral locally at 7: E is an original graph mark, so the terminal residue of E/2 is zero. Write β=[z], with β²=5 in the terminal anisotropic plane in the ordinary integral-field normalization. For distinct nonantipodal directions z,w at e, z−w and z+w are actual nonzero eligible neutral fields: they are u−v and u−v′ respectively. Their squared norms 24±2z·w are at least 14. Neither may be 14 because all of u,v,v′ have the same dot 4 with all eligible Hoffman cocliques, whereas at rank 42 a Fano is a difference of its two (automatically eligible) Hoffman endpoint fields. Neither may be 20,24 by assumption. Consequently |z·w|≤4. If β=±[w], the pairing is congruent to ±5, so the only remaining magnitude is 2, yielding forbidden norm20. If β is perpendicular to [w], the pairing is zero, yielding forbidden norm24. There are four antipodal directions of norm five in the plane, paired by perpendicularity (each line contains exactly two opposite vectors of this norm, and a 90-degree isometry takes it to its distinct perpendicular line). At most two directions can therefore occur at e, each supporting just one reflected pair, as claimed. For two surviving lines the possible residual pairings are ±3 (can be checked by transporting the norm-five circle to the unit circle {(±1,0),(0,±1),(±2,±2)} over F7). The alternative integral magnitude 4 would give N16, so if N16 also vanishes the pairing has magnitude exactly 3, producing a true N18 field. In particular the raw U121 population divided by four bounds below the number of distinct physical edges whose endpoints lie in no eligible Hoffman coclique.

On the rank-42 support contained in `{018,121,125}`, the [eight-class balance theorem](rank42-u121-residues.md) and the physical-edge cap force at least 18 distinct positive edges whose endpoints both miss all Hoffman cocliques.
