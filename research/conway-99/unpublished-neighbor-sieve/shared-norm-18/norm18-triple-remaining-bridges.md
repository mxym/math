# Rooted norm-18 markings: a triangle-free relation in every graph

Fix an actual positive root o and an independent four-subset Q of its neighbors. Consider **all** oriented graph norm-18 markings that are negative on Q and positive at o. Join two distinct such markings when their dot product is seven or nine. **This graph is triangle-free**, without any rank or shell hypothesis. In particular, when N16=N20=0 there are at most two markings at each seed, so the total of oriented norm-18 markings satisfies **N18≤12,320**. Neither (2,30) nor any other affine type is assumed absent.

## Why the argument applies to a single proposed triangle

The [rooted inner-product bound](../short-shell-moments.md#a-bound-using-only-the-root-and-its-four-negative-neighbors) is unconditional: two distinct markings at the seed have dot 7,8,9 or 10. Their difference is unit (norm 16 to 22). For a good pair at dot seven the two common positive pair labels already use the two available shared slots beyond o,Q; for a good pair at nine the at-most-two shared label conclusion is the final **unconditional** [panel exclusion](panel-heights.md). Thus for a proposed good triangle all three pairs have at most two shared labels. Each individual marking uses at least four of six labels, so their complements are three disjoint pairs L_i partitioning the six. The local [two-nine](norm18-triple-exterior.md#partial-cores-and-the-missing-neighbors), [all-nine](norm18-triple-all-nine.md) and [one-nine](norm18-triple-one-nine.md) contradictions apply to these three pairs. No absence of a short vector *elsewhere* is used there: the required two-field differences of dot-nine pairs are themselves norm 18; the norm-22 differences of dot-seven pairs are always unit. Those tests allow all the (2,30),(3,37),(1,27) fields. The only remaining possibility is three dots seven.

Use the [actual bridge description](norm18-triple-bridge-graph.md). Group i has four private positive leaves P_i and five private negatives N_i. Each leaf has one parent in Q and three neighbors in N_i; its parent multiset is the multiset of four endpoints of L_i. Each omitted center has exactly two leaf neighbors and misses its own N_i. A group is balanced if both center-neighbor pairs are disjoint, otherwise they intersect at B leaving an uncovered leaf E. Every pair i,j has zero or one bridge joining P_i,P_j, and exactly the same number of cross edges from P_i to N_j (and from P_j to N_i). In the balanced case a bridged pair uses the same leaf for these two functions. In the unbalanced case both bridges must be present: E receives the other positive and B the other negative. All these statements are from the eigen-equations, pair quotas and the unconditional norm-22 height budget. A bridge graph with no edges is exactly the [independent-difference 38-site case](norm18-triple-exterior.md): its earlier (2,30) absence served *only* to deduce independent difference signs, which now follow from no bridges. Similarly the [single-bridge refutation](norm18-triple-single-bridge.md) then used only the determined local pattern and unconditional projectors. We exclude the only remaining graphs, a two-edge path and the triangle.

## A common two-endpoint parametrization

In a path take the two endpoints as 0,1. They have no mutual bridge and are both balanced, since an unbalanced group requires two bridges. In a triangle take two groups of the same balance status, which exist by pigeonhole. They have a mutual bridge, and in either case each endpoint has exactly one bridge to the remaining group 2. No assumption on group 2's balance is made.

Start with 19 sites: o (0); Q (1..4); L_0,L_1,L_2 (5..10 in successive pairs); and P_0,P_1 (11..18 in successive fours). The center at pair {a,b} sees just a,b among Q. A leaf of group i meets only its prescribed parent and its specified subset of L_i among o,Q and the six centers. It misses the other positive leaves of its own group. For balanced endpoints choose two leaves at each omitted center, with distinct parents: they share that center and at least one private negative among their two triples on five sites. For an unbalanced endpoint its three leaves B,C,D meeting a center have distinct parents. Indeed B and C (or D) already share the center and a private negative; writing the three private neighbors of B as a triple, C and D must both use both remaining negatives, so also cannot share a parent. The fourth leaf E has no omitted-center contact. A mutual bridge, if required, chooses one endpoint leaf in each group: that leaf also receives the far negative in a balanced group; for an unbalanced group the positive/negative contact sites are E/B. These prescriptions include every edge on the 19 sites.

Quotient only by the 24 permutations of the four named parents and the ordering of the two chosen groups; normalize leaves by their parent and center incidence (and their marked roles if a bridge is present). Different labels within a two-leaf center group are distinguishable by parent. The two omitted parent pairs and then the remaining pair are determined from the records. This leaves 26 charts for the path, 258 for balanced triangle endpoints, 199 for unbalanced triangle endpoints; the latter may duplicate a triangle with three equally typed groups, harmlessly.

For each endpoint group, take five distinct four-subsets of its eight possible cross positives, namely P_i and the four centers outside L_i. Their column totals are 3,3,3,3,2,2,2,2. No member can see o or a parent in Q, both N_i's own sign and the four neighbors at o being fixed. Two negative rows already sharing three sites are impossible; any four-subset used twice is therefore impossible. Sort the five rows to name them. At a bridged endpoint add the specified foreign leaf to any one of the five rows. Two tables and a binary five-by-five matrix give all 29 sites of the two complete markings. On N_i the other endpoint field vanishes; hence its signed sum over the first 19 neighbors equals the number of edges still owed to N_j. This fixes both row and column margins of the five-by-five matrix. Try all binary matrices with those margins and test genuine pair capacities. For the path only, the 29-stage also removes equivalent tables under the parent permutations stabilizing the endpoint diagram and possible endpoint interchange. Each group of five old negatives is sorted by its 19-bit contact word, which is distinct by the preceding four-subset argument. The retained key encodes both sets of five words and all twenty-five cross entries; it identifies only relabelings of the full marked adjacency.

## From 29 sites to 33

A new satellite leaf y has exactly one parent q; its parents across four leaves are prescribed by L_2. Let l be its number of contacts to L_2 and f_i its number in P_i. Then f_i is zero or one; over all four y there is exactly one such positive contact per endpoint. At y the endpoint field t_i vanishes. Its positive neighbors are l+f_i and its negative neighbors are its unique Q parent together with its contacts in N_i. Thus exactly l+f_i−1 in {0,1} of N_i must be selected. Again the total over y is exactly one for each i. No other old contact is possible. Require two contacts to each L_2 center across the four leaves. An unbalanced endpoint can receive the single positive contact only at E. Two distinct y already have a common satellite negative among their private triples, so their 29-bit words intersect at most once. Leaves with a common parent may be sorted by word (the word has at least two contacts, and so cannot repeat). Enforce every ordinary common-neighbor cap throughout, and at most one unresolved exterior common neighbor of the two L_2 centers: the five missing N_2 sites miss L_2, and two remaining exterior witnesses would produce an impossible nonunit (3,37) by the [two-pivot argument](norm18-triple-exterior.md#partial-cores-and-the-missing-neighbors).

For each wholly included norm-18 field, an off-support site's common positive/negative height is at most three, with **at most one height-three site in the entire graph**, independently of the rank and other fields. Both facts are proved in [height-three.md](height-three.md). Thus if a height three is already present in the 29 sites for t_0 or t_1, every new y must have height ≤2 for that field.

Use the two [marked projector Grams](norm18-triple-single-bridge.md) on every proposed induced set, with the three marked rows restricted from complete fields having Gram 11I+7J. There is also a small true-incidence Gram at 29 sites. Write A for their induced adjacency, t for the restricted t_2, and C for their unseen binary incidence on the 70 remaining vertices. Then
```
F=CCᵀ=12I+2J−A−A²,     h=(A+4I)t,    d=14·1−A1−h.
```
The 70 sites consist of four missing positive leaves, five missing negatives and 61 unmarked sites. Thus h=C u with u equal to −1,+1,0 on these three blocks; d=C v with v equal to 2,0,1. Their 2×2 Gram is [[9,−8],[−8,77]]. Appending h,d to F with that corner is a necessary 31×31 Gram. A true Y-word w is the image under C of its own coordinate axis; appending it requires cross column (w,−1,2), diagonal one and cross zero between different leaves.

## The last five rows and the integer certificate

Write A for a surviving 33-site matrix. The five missing private negative words sum to H=(A+4I)t. Each chooses exactly four of the eight satellite positives P_2 and L_0∪L_1, and none of o,Q or other sites of nonzero satellite weight. The other selected coordinates must have H_j>0. On both original endpoint markings it is a zero site, so the two sign-hit counts match and are at most three. Enumerate every subset respecting those conditions, each true pair capacity, and (A+I)w≤2. Exact-cover the vector H by five distinct such words, pairwise overlapping at most twice (the five new sites are independent). A recursive pivot picks the unresolved coordinate with the fewest ways to choose all its still-needed words, tries each such subset, then discards the other words containing that coordinate. This enumerates every feasible cover regardless of order. For each L_0,L_1 pair require its remaining common-neighbor quota outside all 38 sites to be zero or one, by the same two-pivot argument. Recheck the full pair capacities and marked Gram necessities. Finally F_38−ddᵀ/61 is positive semidefinite in any graph, where F_38 is the remaining incidence Gram and d=14·1−A_381; it comes from subtracting the projection onto the constant vector on the 61 remaining sites.

The [Python verifier](norm18_triple_remaining_bridges.py) requires NumPy and [norm18_triple_core.py](norm18_triple_core.py). **Every rejection is by exact integer arithmetic.** To accelerate the principal Gram tests the checker may *propose* a short integer vector from a numerical eigendirection. It discards a case only after checking xᵀGx<0 exactly, with a strict integer overflow bound; a failure to find such a witness admits the case. For the larger family of four-leaf choices, let G denote the fixed 29- or 31-dimensional block, B the proposed cross rows, and K the proposed tail Gram. For each possible row choose **any** integer vector W_i and positive integer scale D_i (here proposed by rounding). The restriction to columns (−W_i,D_i e_i) must have positive semidefinite Gram
```
WGWᵀ − WBᵀD − DBWᵀ + DKD.
```
Its diagonals, two-by-two principal minors and the exact four-by-four Bareiss test on each eligible quartet are necessary. No claimed inverse, nullspace dimension, tolerance or floating sign is trusted. Every fixed-width quadratic computation is guarded by a strict entrywise absolute-sum overflow bound; the final minors use arbitrary-width integers. A different valid proposer could make the test slower or weaker but cannot remove a true core.

Run `python norm18_triple_remaining_bridges.py`, or pass `path`, `balanced`, or `unbalanced`, optionally followed by zero-based chart IDs. The old 34-chart empty- and single-bridge tables use separate checkers linked above.

## Unconditional capacity and scalar pressure

Let m be the number of markings at a seed. Its triangle-free good-pair graph has at most floor(m²/4) edges by Mantel. Hence it contains at least floor((m−1)²/4) **bad unordered pairs**, i.e. dot 8 or 10, at every seed (take zero when m=0). The norm of the corresponding oriented difference is respectively 20 or 16. In particular N16=N20=0 implies m≤2, as claimed. There are 99·560 choices of o,Q and each oriented norm-18 marking is counted at its nine positive sites.

For completeness, this also gives a graph-wide inequality without any absence. Fix an orientation r of a bad difference, and a common positive root o. For another decomposition t′−u′=r, distinct from t−u=r, the [unconditional norm-18 distance minimum](../short-shell-moments.md#bounding-the-norm-18-alternative) gives
```
t·u′ = 18−r²/2−||t−t′||²/2 ≤2 .
```
On the other hand any norm-18 markings t,u′ at o sharing m negative parents have dot at least 7m−15−binom(m,2). To see this, their other eight positives each receive at most two contacts from these m parents and receive 3m in total. If d_t meet two parents then 8−3m+d_t meet none, and any positive of t negative in u′ must miss all these parents. Each double contact uses one of the binom(m,2) actual second common-neighbor labels. Count the shared o and m parents, at least d_t+d_u′−binom(m,2) shared double contacts, and subtract the two upper bounds for sign conflicts. For m=3,4 the lower bound is 3,7, contradicting the preceding upper bound. Therefore the Q's of distinct decompositions at r,o intersect in at most two (and cannot coincide). Each four-set thus uses four distinct independent triples of the root matching, among only 8·binom(7,3)=280. There are at most 70 decompositions for that particular r,o.

Such o has no r-support neighbor, so has zero r-height. For a unit neutral r with sides of size k, write e for the common number of internal edges per side. At an exterior site x let h(x) count its neighbors of either sign. Standard pair counting gives Σ h=10k−4e and Σ binom(h,2)=k(k−7)−9e−J_+−J_−, where J is the sum of binom(internal degree,2) over that side. Thus the number of exterior height-zero sites is at most Σ(h−1)(h−2)/2 ≤ k²−19k+99, which is 11 at k=8 and 9 at k=10. Summing over oriented r and dividing by two to forget orientation proves
```
Σ_(seeds) floor((m−1)²/4) ≤ 385 N16 + 315 N20,
9 N18 ≤ 110880 + 385 N16 + 315 N20.
```
The second inequality uses floor((m−1)²/4)≥m−2 and Σm=9N18. No classes or unoriented shells have been substituted for these actual graph counts.

Finally at **rank 38, under just N16=N20=0**, the [native scalar identity](norm18-u226-graph-cap.md) and [disjoint positive-path and isolated-edge transfers](u226-disjoint-transfers.md) now give
```
3 U026 + 81 U121 + 26 U127 + 10 U230
  ≥ 66,696 + 2 U125 + 19 U228 + 3 U337 .
```
This keeps every other selected unit coordinate free. In particular if the support is contained in {018,024,226,230} then U230≥6,670. The inequality itself does not eliminate this enlarged support, the whole rank, or the graph.
