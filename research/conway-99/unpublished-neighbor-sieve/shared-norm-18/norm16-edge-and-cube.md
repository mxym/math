# An edge at height two forces an isolated cap and two missed zero sites

Let an actual integral neutral norm-16 marking have signed support shores P,N, eight height-two sites H, eleven height-zero sites Z and 64 height-one sites Y. The [zero-star forest proof](../n16-zero-star-forest.md) gives `d_Z(g)=4+d_H(g)` on H, `d_H(z)=2+d_Z(z)` on Z and at most one edge on H. Each signed support site meets precisely two H sites. Write W_g for the four signed neighbors of g, two on each shore. No rank or symmetry assumption.

**Theorem.** If H has an edge ab, its unique common neighbor z is in Z and is isolated there. Exactly two Z vertices m,m' miss both a,b. The other six H vertices g meet the eight signed sites W_a union W_b in exactly 2,2,1,1,1,1 sites. The two meeting two are the entire common neighborhood of m,m'; their own two signed contact words are disjoint. Each of the four others touches exactly one of m,m'. The Z-degrees of m,m' sum to four. No height-one site adjacent to a or b is adjacent to another H site.

For completeness, the cap belongs to Z: height one is excluded by the [forest theorem](../n16-zero-star-forest.md#the-cap-of-the-unique-height-two-edge). If p in P caps ab, the two N-contact pairs of a,b are disjoint and miss N(p). They are therefore the four N sites outside N(p). Let x be a's other positive neighbor. It is nonadjacent to b and shares at most one negative with p because they already share a. Consequently x meets at least three of those four negatives. But it can meet at most one negative at a (one common neighbor with a), and at most one at b (it already shares a with b). Contradiction; reverse the signs for a negative cap.

Put A=N_Z(a), B=N_Z(b): both size five, intersection exactly {z}, hence precisely two missed sites M={m,m'}. The endpoint signed words are disjoint. For every other g in H write x_g=|W_g intersect (W_a union W_b)|. Every endpoint signed neighbor appears once more, so the six x_g sum to eight. Let C=N_Z(g), of size four. The nonadjacent pairs ga,gb have two common neighbors each, of which x_g total are already signed. Thus
```
|C intersect A|+|C intersect B| <= 4-x_g,
|C intersect M| >= x_g + [z in C].
```
In particular x_g<=2, forcing at least two twos. Each two supplies a common neighbor of m,m', so there are exactly two. The other four x_g are all one. The two common-neighbor slots are exhausted, forcing equality |C intersect M|=x_g for all six g and z not in any C. It follows that d_H(z)=2, hence d_Z(z)=0. Also m,m' are nonadjacent, their two H witnesses have no common signed site, and d_H(m)+d_H(m')=8 yields the Z-degree sum four. Both endpoint common-neighbor counts ga,gb are exact on support and Z; the endpoints share z only, proving the final assertion.

## The four-double-edge case has only two portraits

Assume each of the four components of each shore's deficiency multigraph is a double edge. The 16-site signed support is the four-dimensional cube Q4. Indeed each 2-by-2 crossing block of the paired shores is a perfect matching (each vertex meets half of each opposite-shore pair). Encode a block by a sign selecting straight or crossed matching. Distinct rows of this 4-by-4 sign matrix are orthogonal since two positive vertices in different pairs share exactly two negative sites. Swapping the two vertices within any shore pair negates its row or column. Normalize first row and column positive. The remaining three rows are the three distinct balanced sign-rows starting positive, different from the constant one. Thus the normalized table is unique up to row/column permutations, and the antipodal pairs in the even and odd parts of Q4 realize it. Conversely any induced Q4 in a graph with the Conway parameters gives a norm-16 marking: the opposite bipartition signs supported on the cube attain the global minimum Rayleigh quotient −4, hence are a full eigenvector.

Each H site specifies one antipodal pair on each shore. No such ordered pair repeats as two H sites cannot share four common neighbors. These eight cells therefore form a simple 2-regular bipartite graph on the four pairs from each shore. Its edge conflict graph on H (two cells conflict if they have a common paired shore) is C8 or C4+C4; a conflicting pair shares two signed sites, so its Z-neighborhoods are disjoint. A C4 component is impossible even if H is edgeless: its four vertices send at least 16 incidences to the 11 sites of Z. No Z site can take more than two from a C4. At least five Z sites must take two. Only two opposite H pairs can do so, each with at most two common-neighbor slots.

Finally an edge ab in H is impossible. Each endpoint has two conflict neighbors, other than a,b. No g can conflict with both because that makes x_g=4, while each of these four distinct neighbors must have x_g=2. The theorem above allows exactly two. Hence every induced Q4 has edgeless H and conflict graph C8.

The optional standard-library [portrait verifier](norm16-cube-portraits.py) enumerates the 90 possible 2-regular cell placements and quotients the explicit 384 coordinate permutations/translations of Q4, with an optional edge of H on two disjoint cells. This gives 4 unmarked portraits and 20 one-edge portraits; the analytic cuts leave precisely two unmarked C8 portraits. These are necessary local portraits, not known graph completions or an exclusion of Q4.
