# The positive-eigenspace residual at a Hoffman coclique

Suppose a strongly regular graph with parameters (99,14,1,2) has an independent set C of size 22. Write B for the 77 vertices outside C, D for the 22-by-77 incidence matrix, and A_B for adjacency on B. Each outside vertex is incident with four points of C. The **+3** eigenspace, rather than the [−4 residual](hoffman-residual-theta.md), gives an even integral lattice of rank 33. Every actual 3-by-3 rook grid maps injectively into its dual shell of squared norm 20/7. This is not a claim that the shell consists only of grids, or that any grid exists.

## The lattice

The [usual +3 site vectors](../../complementary-rank56/README.md) are v_i=√7 E_3 e_i, with Gram 4I+A−2J/11. They form a tight frame with frame operator 7I. Those on C have Gram 4I−2J/11 of rank 21. Let P be orthogonal projection in the +3 eigenspace off their span. For each outside vertex b, put m_b=2P v_b. To see the new Gram, the projection removed from v_b has evaluation on C equal to D_{·b}−(2/11)1_C. This is sum zero, so inverse Gram on these evaluations is multiplication by 1/4. Subtract its contribution and multiply by four. The constants cancel, giving
```
Q = 16I + 4A_B − DᵀD,       diag Q=12,       Q²=28Q.
```
The latter identity follows from the inherited frame operator 28I on a space of dimension 54−21=33. The lattice generated by the m_b is therefore even integral. If x belongs to its dual then 28x=Σ_b(x,m_b)m_b lies in the lattice, so its discriminant exponent divides 28.

## Literal grids and the block design

For a rook grid G the centered indicator 1_G−(1/11)1 lies in the +3 eigenspace: each outside vertex sees exactly one grid vertex, by saturation of the internal common-neighbor quotas. The centered C indicator lies in the −4 eigenspace, and their orthogonality gives |G∩C|=2. Let e=G∩C. The two points of e are nonadjacent and determine the grid, by the [grid intersection rule](../../complementary-rank56/tail-budget.md) that any two distinct grids intersect in zero points, one point, or a common triangle.

Let y_G be the vector whose v_i evaluations equal the centered grid indicator, and put x_G=2P y_G. Projecting off the C-mark span subtracts the term computed with the inverse Gram 1/4, as above, giving
```
(x_G,m_b) = 4·1_G(b) − |N(b)∩e|.
```
These integers put x_G in the dual. Among the seven grid vertices off C, two see both points of e, four see precisely one, and one sees neither. Thus the seven positive evaluations are 2,2,3,3,3,3,4 and recover the grid off C; the full map is injective because an intersection of seven points already determines the grid. Every point of C outside e has just one grid neighbor, so the seven outside blocks for G, after deleting e, partition the other twenty points of C in sizes 2,2,3,3,3,3,4. The two blocks containing e exhaust its common-neighbor slots. Off the grid the evaluations are therefore twenty copies of −1 and fifty zeros. Their total squared sum, including the seven positive values, is 80, hence x_G²=80/28=20/7.

## The exact intersection Gram

For two actual grids G,G′ let i=|G∩G′| and s=|e∩e′|. The unprojected product (y_G,y_G′) is (i−9/11)/7. The removed product is (s−2/11)/4, by the inverse C Gram. After scaling,
```
7 (x_G,x_G′) = 4i − 2 − 7s .
```
Its diagonal is 20, and this entire Gram matrix has rank at most 33. In particular this supplies a trace constraint. Let g be the number of grids. Let D0 and D1 count unordered pairs meeting in a triangle respectively disjoint from or meeting C, and let E_C=Σ_{c∈C} binom(g_c,2), where g_c counts grids containing c. Then
```
25g + (1/2)binom(g,2) + 12D0 − 2D1 + (21/8)E_C  ≥  25g²/33.
```
Indeed for the Gram above divided by four the diagonal is 5. The default unordered off-diagonal square is 1/4; add 6 at (i,s)=(3,0), 21/16 at (1,1), and 5/16 at (3,1). No other cases occur. Sum the diagonal and twice the unordered squares and apply the trace–rank inequality. It gives a necessary graph count, not a contradiction on its own.
