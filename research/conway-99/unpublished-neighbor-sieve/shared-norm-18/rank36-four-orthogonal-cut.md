# Orthogonal Fano pairs in the unit charge-one shell, and one more exact four-type exclusion

At Seidel rank 36 the four-type palette \(\{U_{0,20},U_{0,26},U_{2,28},U_{2,30}\}\) is impossible. This is a fourth independent exclusion to accompany the three in [the preceding three exact palette cuts](rank36-four-fano-palette-cut.md), not an exclusion of rank 36. It uses the same original rank-44 scalar and rank-48 low harmonic equations; no rank-52 or higher-prefix positivity assumption enters this certificate.

## Rank-free orthogonal-pair injection

Let \(m_k\) count unordered pairs of actual antipodal Fano lines with absolute raw dot product \(k\). Let \(S_{\rm graph}\) count all unit charge-one square-23 labels on the actual graph. Then
\[
4m_0\le S_{\rm graph}.
\]

For orthogonal Fano vectors \(f,g\), each of the four intersections of their signed sides consists of one site. This is the published marked-coset orthogonal-pair theorem: the zero sets are disjoint and each avoids the other support. Alternatively the compatible root version applies because the two isotropic seven-classes with zero mutual bilinear product span a totally isotropic subspace, which is contained in a maximal one. The published three-orthogonal-Fanos theorem excludes any third line orthogonal to both.

For any oriented sum \(s=\pm f\pm g\), orient \(f,g\) to realize it as \(f+g\). It has norm 28 with exactly one \(+2\) site \(p\) and one \(-2\) site \(q\). If \(p,q\) were nonadjacent, then \(t=s+d_q-d_p\) would be neutral square \(28+42-56=14\), using \(d_i=3e_i-Ae_i\); its dot with \(f\) and with \(g\) would be \(14-7-7=0\). It would be a third orthogonal Fano, impossible. So \(p,q\) are adjacent. The label \(h=s+d_q\) has charge one square \(28+23-28=23\). It is unit: \(p\) turns to \(+1\), and no other original negative site can be adjacent to \(q\) because it belongs to at least one of the two negative sides that contain \(q\). The two positive sites \(p,q\) form an isolated positive edge of \(h\): \(p\) was positive in both summands and any other \(s\)-positive site was in at least one of their positive sides, which miss \(p\); any other positive neighbor of \(q\) would drop to zero.

A unit charge-one square-23 label has six positives and seventeen negatives. Write \(e_P,e_N\) for their internal edges. Counting cross edges gives \(e_N=e_P+1\). Every negative has two more positive than negative neighbors, so its marked positive-pair contributions total at least \(17+4e_N\le30-e_P\). Hence \(e_P\le1\). Any two origins of the same \(h\) must therefore be \(s_1=h-d_q=f+g\) and \(s_2=h-d_p=u+v\) for the endpoints of that unique edge; two distinct decompositions from the same \(s_i\) are ruled out by global uniqueness of the sums of distinct Fano lines (or by the common \(+2\) site and the \(6+6\) positive partition triple-closure argument).

Suppose both origins are present. The summands \(u,v\) have the opposite signs to \(f,g\) at both \(p,q\). No cross dot among these four pairs is positive. Indeed a positive dot with at least two opposite-sign intersections requires at least three same-sign agreements, including a same-sign pair; that forces exactly two agreements in each sign and no opposite signs, as in the proved Fano shared-square lemma. But \(f\cdot s_2=f\cdot s_1+f\cdot(d_q-d_p)=14-7-7=0\), and likewise for \(g\). Consequently both cross dots of \(u\) with \(f,g\) are zero, contradicting the three-orthogonal-Fanos theorem. The source of \(h\) is unique. Each unordered orthogonal line pair yields four oriented sums, proving the bound.

## Exact rank-36 verification

The \((1,23)\) fields all have zero quadratic value at seven. The nonunit subpopulation consists of 99 original marks plus seven class-preserving translates per oriented Fano. Thus \(S=W_{1,23}-99-7W_{0,14}\) is the mean of the unit counts in the actual maximal extensions. A unit field in the original zero class is retained in every extension; any other isotropic class has retention \(1/2402\). Therefore \(S_{\rm graph}\le2402 S\). With \(N=N_{14}\), count all pairs of the \(N/2\) lines; no absolute dot three is possible. The necessary inequality
\[
2402S+4(m_1+m_2+m_4)\ \ge\ 4\binom{N/2}{2}=\frac{N(N-2)}2
\]
has the exact affine lower slack \(2402S+4(m_1+m_2+m_4)-399N+80000\ge0\), the tangent at 400. Combined with the same baseline scalar/harmonic and local graph equations as the previous three exclusions, the companion [direct checker](rank36_four_orthogonal_cut.py) verifies a positive integer combination of 19 such lower slacks equal to \(-170270270\), without optimization. It imports the published baseline rows of the preceding three analyses and replays the fourth certificate.

## Sharpening the separate dot-four transport

In the prior \(s=f+g\), \(b=s+d_{Q_0}\) construction from a shared positive pair, write \(Q_0=\{q_1,q_2\}\) for the shared negative diagonal, and \(P_0\) for the shared positive diagonal. Both \(P_0\) sites are zero in \(b\) because \(s\) shows \(+2\) there and each is adjacent to both \(q_i\). Each exclusive positive of one summand sees at most one of \(Q_0\) (their two common neighbors are just \(P_0\)); of the five exclusive positives exactly four see one and a unique one sees neither (each \(q_i\) has two of its four Fano neighbors outside \(P_0\)). Thus the other two positives of \(b\) are just these two exclusive positives; they are nonadjacent to either member of \(Q_0\). The root choice \(Q_0\) is therefore a pair of isolated positives with no negative common neighbor in \(b\).

If \(b\) has a positive edge, at most one root choice is possible. If it has none, the total of positive neighbors over its 26 negatives is 32; the sum of their pair counts is at least \(32-26=6\). There are at most two negative witnesses for each positive pair, so at least three pairs are not root choices. This gives at most three sources per target and \(2 m_4\le3G(2,30)\).

For an oriented difference \(t=f-g\) of norm 20, the two sign sides each have the same number \(e\le1\) of internal edges, by the exterior height budget \(9-5e-\sum_i\binom{d_{\rm own}(i)}2\ge0\). If \(e=0\), the two unique exterior positives \(x,y\) of the two summands (the ones missing \(Q_0\)) have no edge to one another. To see this, for \(x\), all four of its original Fano negative neighbors belong to the five first-summand exclusive negatives, and as a \(t\)-positive site it can have only four \(t\)-negative neighbors in total. It therefore misses \(y\). In those five exclusive negatives, the fifth vertex not adjacent to \(x\) is also not adjacent to \(Q_0\) (same original negative sign) and not adjacent to \(y\) (that would be internal on the negative side of \(t\)). It thus gives a far negative in \(b\); the symmetric argument gives a second. For such \(b\) the number of positive-pair marked witnesses is at least \(32-24=8\), and there are at most two possible root choices. If instead \(e=1\), the unique negative edge on each oriented \(t\) shifts to a positive edge of a unit \(G(2,28)\) label. This map is injective because such a label has at most one positive edge: subtracting the mark of a positive edge gives unit \(N_{20}\), so a third positive touching its endpoints is impossible. Therefore \(2m_{4,e=1}\le G(2,28)\).

Taking each source with \(e=0\) at weight \(1/2\) and \(e=1\) at weight \(1/3\) gives total weight at most one at any \(G(2,30)\) target (if it has any \(e=0\) source it has at most two total sources). Since every line pair contributes two alignments, this proves \(m_{4,e=0}+\tfrac23m_{4,e=1}\le G(2,30)\), and thus \(6m_4\le6G(2,30)+G(2,28)\). If \(G(2,28)=0\) it sharpens to \(m_4\le G(2,30)\). These refinements are not needed by the four exact certificates.
