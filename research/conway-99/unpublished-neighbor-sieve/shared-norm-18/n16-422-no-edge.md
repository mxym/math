# The 4+2+2 norm-sixteen deficiency deck has no height-two edge

At **every** Seidel rank, if a norm-sixteen field has shore deficiency deck 4+2+2, its eight height-two sites H form an independent set. Consequently its eleven zero sites Z span five edges, not six. Together with the known four-double case this covers two of the five decks. No emptiness of the underlying norm-sixteen shell is asserted.

Suppose ab is the edge. Use the [published height-two edge theorem](norm16-edge-and-cube.md), the [zero-star forest](../n16-zero-star-forest.md) and the [actual shore catalogue](n16-five-shore-cores.md). Its central cap z is an isolate of Z, and Z splits as z, four vertices A adjacent to a, four B adjacent to b, and the two missed vertices M. Write W_i for the four signed neighbors of i in H. For g in the six other H, put s_g=|W_g∩W_a|, t_g=|W_g∩W_b| and u_g=s_g+t_g. Exactly two g have u_g=2, their signed words disjoint; the remaining four have u_g=1. The sharp incidence proof for the cited edge theorem gives precisely 2−s_g neighbors in A, 2−t_g in B and u_g in M. Both M sites see both heavy words, and each light word hits one of them. Since d_Z=d_H−2 on Z and each such degree is at most three, each M mask has three through five H neighbors; the lower bound three follows because the two M degrees sum to four.

Begin with each of the 26 signed 4+2+2 overlays, not the smaller connectedness refinement. Check every candidate ab against the just-stated necessary intersection restrictions. Also for each signed site p, the number of Y(a) adjacent to p must be nonnegative: this is 2−[p in W_a]−[p in W_b]−|N(p)∩W_a|. These checks give 34 possible marked physical placements. Reorder the two endpoints then the other six H points by the s,t pair with all tie permutations; record the least pairwise overlap-multiplicity tuple. Only six abstract signatures occur. Each physical portrait is carried along with one explicit relabeling attaining this key; no actual graph automorphism is assumed.

For the four A sites enumerate their six-bit columns: row g has 2−s_g ones. Independently do the same for B, then sort columns in each block to remove only interchangeable labels. In either block each column must have 1..4 selected g's because its H degree is 1 plus this cardinality. Distinct columns can share at most one g since their two sites already share the endpoint. Sort the two M masks. Check every pair of Z vertices already has at most two common H (including its compulsory endpoint where applicable). For every pair of the six g's the total number of shared signed and Z contacts is at most two. Finally the zero forest has at most four wedges since it has six edges; all its degrees are already known from these H masks. Exactly **one** of the six signatures admits such marginals; it has two distinct A/B/M tables up to the stated relabelings, and two physical lifts. In 8-bit H numbering (edge at 0,1) the two ordered lists of Z masks are
```

 (3,13,21,41,49,70,98,138,146,212,232),quad
 (3,13,21,41,49,70,98,138,146,204,240).
```

For each incidence table construct every true Z graph by assigning edges in increasing endpoint order at the prescribed degrees d_H−2. Discard any violation of the H,Z common-neighbor quotas or of the prior forest component theorem (order≤4, no cycle). Exactly 89 labeled forests survive for each, before reattaching the signed words. Thus the final input is just 2·89·2=356 literal 35-site adjacency charts.

**Endpoint test.** Each endpoint h sees four signed sites, its other H endpoint and five Z sites; it needs exactly four Y neighbors. None meets a further H, by the published edge theorem; each therefore meets exactly two Z sites by d_Z(y)=1+d_H(y), together with its one P and one N site. For the old adjacency A₀, R=12I+2J−A₀−A₀² gives exact old pair demands to be filled by Y. Enumerate every 35-bit candidate with support (p,n,h,z₁,z₂). Its entries cannot exceed R_h; all its chosen old pairs need positive residual; its known common-neighbor count with every old j is at most 2−w_j. Cover R_h exactly by four distinct such rows. Two cannot have the same word or meet on three old sites. Their pair incidences across the old points cannot exceed R. Any chosen Y with known mate in N(h) uses it already; the other four minus these matched rows must pair exactly among themselves. Try all such pairings, requiring a paired Y,Y' to share only h among old neighbors and updating the old-to-new common-neighbor ceilings with its mate. All 356 cases fail at a or b. Positive partial assignments would not prove realizability, but a failed necessary endpoint test is conclusive.
