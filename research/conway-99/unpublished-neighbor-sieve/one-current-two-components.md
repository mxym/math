# One visible harmonic current in a two-component wing

Fix a triangle with two wing components at mod-seven rank 36. Assume the 36 near marks have Gram rank 34 and their centered Laplacian radical has a single current h, **visible** on far contact words. The balanced component-constant formal kernel gives no true current. With the entire 60-word contact chart specified, if the nonzero h-evaluations have two inequivalent absolute values modulo seven, the whole adjacency is determined directly without guessing any exterior entries. In the concrete B12 plus seven-unit 24-component branch, only **46 specified labeled full wing-contact words** are needed to give a list of at most eight full graph candidates. This is a conditional reconstruction theorem, neither a uniqueness claim on those 46 words nor an exclusion of the case.

**Split-plane coordinates.** Over \(\mathbf F_7\) use \(M=3I-A+J/9\), whose actual off-diagonal entries are 3,4. Choose 34 wing marks giving a nondegenerate principal Gram G. They span a nondegenerate subspace; its orthogonal complement in the full rank-36 marking space is a nondegenerate plane. The nonzero actual harmonic mark \(u=\sum_{i\in W}h_i u_i\) is isotropic in that plane. Choose the isotropic dual f with \((u,f)=1\) (subtract a multiple of u from any dual vector to make its square zero). For each far site b let \(p_b\) be its known pairings to the 34 marks and let \(g_{bc}=p_b^tG^{-1}p_c\). The residual is \(a_b f+d_b u\), where \(a_b=(u,u_b)=-\sum_{\mathrm{contacts}}h_i\); the minus sign comes from the oriented near pairing, with h centered. Its required square is
\[
 2a_b d_b=-g_{bb}.
\]
For \(a_b\ne0\) this determines \(d_b\) from this *one word*. Consequently all mutual adjacencies of any specified collection of nonzero-current words are already fixed (or impossible): their full entries must be \(g_{bc}+a_bd_c+d_ba_c\in\{3,4\}\). For \(a_b=0\), require \(g_{bb}=0\) and
\[
 d_b\in\bigcap_{a_c\ne0}(\{3,4\}-g_{bc})/a_c .
\]
Two different elements in a factor of this intersection differ by \(\pm1/a_c\). Two distinct absolute values of \(a_c\) therefore give at most one choice of \(d_b\). All pairs of zero-current sites have their entries fixed by \(g_{bc}\), independently of \(d_b,d_c\). None of this calculation assumes the far adjacency is provided.

Even if all nonzero \(a_c\) have the same absolute value, a two-element intersection for b gives complementary adjacency to *every* nonzero-current site; if there are more than sixteen of them, both alternatives cannot have far degree eight, so at most one survives. In arbitrary full fixed-word charts under the rank/visibility assumptions, any two exterior completions differ mod seven by \(a\,\delta d^t+\delta d\,a^t\), of rank at most two. Every integer 3-by-3 minor of a signed adjacency difference has magnitude \(<7\) by Hadamard, so its real rank is at most two. The [rank-two transposition argument](triangle-complement.md#a-rank-two-difference-is-a-transposition) rules out two actual graphs differing only among their eight-regular far vertices. This last uniqueness is for a **full fixed contact chart**, not for 46 partial words.

**Actual B12 + seven-unit 24 example.** The [12-component inventory and sign restriction](triangle-wing-twelve-currents.md) identifies the B-type with tree number 7140 and a one-dimensional visible centered radical. The seven-unit larger tree must have nonsquare class for rank 36. Take the published representative with mate permutations \((10)(23),(02)(13),(10)(23)\), writing \((10)(23)\) for the transpositions \(0\leftrightarrow1,\,2\leftrightarrow3\); first two cross maps are the identity and return map is the tuple \((0,3,2,1)\). Label wing sites \(0,\dots,11\) in the standard consecutive groups of four. A current is
\[
 h=(2,1,5,6,\ 0,0,0,0,\ 5,6,2,1).
\]
For each unordered contact pair \(i,j\) its multiplicity is \(2-[i\sim j]-|N_D(i)\cap N_D(j)|-[i,j\text{ same wing}]\). Summing these fixed multiplicities gives the histogram \(10,12,9,4,4,9,12\) of \(a=0,1,\dots,6\); it does not assume an exterior contact assignment. All three absolute amplitudes occur and fifty far vertices have nonzero current. All ten zero-current pair tags have multiplicity one. As edges of a graph on the twelve sites, they are the isolated edges \(02\) and \((8,10)\) plus the cycles \((1,3,11,9)\) and \((4,5,6,7)\).

In addition to those ten, leave unknown the four words tagged by pairs \((0,3),(0,5),(0,6),(1,8)\). Each has multiplicity one and nonzero current. Their 12-dimensional unoriented edge-incidence columns augment the above ten columns from rank 8 to rank 12 over the rationals. The [small exact checker](one_current_two_components.py) regenerates all counts, labels and ranks without a solver. Thus the 14 remaining sites are distinctly labeled by B12 tags and the incidence matrix \(H\) of their B12 contacts has kernel precisely two-dimensional, spanned by the two alternating cycle vectors (zero on the four extra tags).

**Recovery from 46 words.** Specify the two component graphs and the full 36-site contact word of every *other* far vertex, 46 labeled words in all. They have nonzero current, so the preceding calculation gives their mutual adjacency and hence the entire known 85-site induced graph P (the triangle, 36 wings, 46 far). Let Z be the 85-by-14 binary incidence with the fourteen missing sites. The common-neighbour equation gives
\[
 ZZ^t=12I-P+2J-P^2 .
\]
Its twelve B12 rows are H. For each known vertex i the products of its row \(z_i\) with the rows of H are fixed. Thus its projection onto \(\operatorname{row}(H)\) is fixed. There are at most **four** possible binary rows with that projection: any two differ only by a linear combination of the two disjoint four-cycle alternators; on each cycle its four binary edge values are fixed by the vertex sums up to at most two choices. All other coordinates, including the four added nonzero tags, are unchanged. Equivalently the cycle can be ambiguous only when its four vertex sums are all one, before accounting for the four already pinned extra coordinates.

Subtracting the known projection Gram from \(ZZ^t\) gives the rational Gram of residuals in a fixed Euclidean plane. If its rank is zero, all rows are determined; for rank one choose a nonzero residual row in at most four ways, and inner products against it fix the others. For rank two choose a first nonzero row (at most four ways), then an independent one. Its inner product with the first and its length give at most two positions by intersecting a line and a circle; every further residual is fixed by its two inner products. Ranks greater than two, nonpositive Gram, or incompatible candidate rows reject. Thus at most eight explicit matrices Z remain. Each supplies the unknown W contacts of the fourteen far sites. The fixed-word formulas determine their mutual edges (the retained 46 supply all three absolute amplitudes), and all proposed incidences and the complete integer SRG equation must then be checked. In particular the computation does not assert existence from a feasible plane, or identify distinct B12 tags by a presumed graph symmetry.
