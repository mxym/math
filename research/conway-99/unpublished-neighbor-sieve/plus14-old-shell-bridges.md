# Complementary norm 14 and two original small-shell budgets

In a hypothetical SRG(99,14,1,2), write N14+ for the oriented neutral +3 norm-14 fields, and C(a,t) only for original zero-seven-class −4 labels. Put E8=C(1,51)−105336. Then

    N14+ ≤ 6[C(2,36)−693],
    N14+ ≤ 24(E8−3465).

Consequently C(1,51)≥108801+ceil(N14+/24), and the complementary coefficient x7+=2+N14+. This is rank-free and does not establish N14+>0. The equal-mark seven-support dictionary and class cap were known in the complementary construction's working proof; the explicit unit-mark characterization, canonical-edge subtraction and disjoint (1,51) bridge below are the refinements.

## The literal norm-14 buckets

The norm-14 fields are signed-unit: adding (A+4I)e_i to an entry ≤−2 would give a charge-two field of squared norm ≤16 but mass 18, a contradiction; reverse signs. The seven-site sign shores P,N have internal degrees three plus opposite degrees. An induced seven-set of minimum degree three has no degree D≥5, since the D neighbouring sites lie in a matching and each needs at least one more contact, but the remaining 6−D sites can supply at most 2(6−D) contacts by the common-neighbour quota with the root. Thus the cross graph is a matching, of odd size E. Set h=(A−3I)1_P=(A−3I)1_N. Its sums are 77 and 98−7E, and h is one at cross endpoints and zero elsewhere on the shores. External h(h−1) sums to 21−7E, so E=1 or3. If E=3, each shore has three highs of degree four. A high has 8 pair slots with the other six shore vertices, and the internal neighbours already consume 8+d_H of these, where d_H is its number of other high neighbours. So all highs are independent and each sees all four lows, contradicting the pair bound.

For E=1 each sign shore is a min-degree-three 7-set with 11 edges and one high x of degree four. If U is its four-site neighbourhood inside the shore and a,b the remaining pair, each U site needs at least one a/b link and a,b can each supply at most two, so all U sites are mated in two U edges and each has exactly one a/b link. Thus a,b are adjacent; each U mate pair splits between a,b (otherwise its edge would get a second triangle cap beyond x). These facts apply to any actual 7-set of this degree type. Its residual pair slots after internal common witnesses are: one at ab; one each at the four a/b–U attachment edges; and one at the two nonmate U-pairs attached on opposite sides. All other pairs are full: x sees the U mates and the two low neighbours of each a/b; a nonattached a/b–U pair shares the opposite a/b site and its U mate; and a nonmate U-pair on the same side shares x and its attachment site. The residual graph is ab plus two length-three paths, hence triangle-free. No exterior site has three contacts, and exactly seven have two contacts. The original old evaluation b=h−1 of −w(1_P) is therefore unit, charge +2 and norm 36 (its old mark squared norm is 40/9, input sum −7 folded to 2). Its original seven-class vanishes by shifting input sum by −9. It misses all 693 canonical edge marks D_i+D_j (i~j), where D_i=(3I−A)e_i: those labels have +2 at both endpoints, which also distinguishes all 693.

Two distinct 7-sets of this degree type in one old bucket have equal h and a neutral unit difference of norm 14−2|I| where I is their overlap. The complementary minimum twelve limits |I|≤1. If they share a site, its at least three neighbours on either set's exclusive side would all need positive h to touch the other set, while each support has only one positive h. Thus they are disjoint. Between them the only edge is between their highs (shared h=1), so high vertices in one bucket form a clique, of size m≤3. Ordered pairs recover and exhaust N14+, each bucket contributing m(m−1). This proves the first bound.

## Mapping each nontrivial bucket to an extra (1,51) mark

For m=2, let T be the two highs and their unique triangle cap. The cap is outside both supports, and has h=1: a second contact on either side would spend an additional pair slot involving the high, whose budget is already full inside. For m=3, T is the high triangle itself and h=1 on T. In either case T is unique. Fix a member P with high x∈T; each other T vertex has its only P contact at x. Set D_T=(3I−A)1_T and g=D_T−b. The old inner product b·D_T=Σ_{i∈T}(7b_i+4)=12, giving g charge one and norm 39+36−24=51. It is the old mark of input c=1_T+1_P, of mass ten, sole double at x. It is original zero-seven-class because its input sum differs from 1 by 9. The set T∪P has nine sites and fourteen edges (the 11 P edges plus the three T edges).

Suppose g equals one of the 105336 mandatory (1,51) marks represented by 1_X−1_Y with input sum 1 and support≤5, as in the complementary [tail budget](../complementary-rank56/tail-budget.md). Then k=c−1_X+1_Y has mass nine and (A−3I)k=1. If k has any negative entries, all three possible X sites are actual negatives and form a triangle anticomplete to positive sites (each needs neighbour sum −2). At x the input c gives neighbour mass six and Y adds at most two. If x∈Y, its coefficient three requires sum ten, impossible. Thus x is doubled, has required neighbour mass seven and no negative neighbours. It has at least seven zero neighbours, each requiring a negative contact to offset weight two. But x is nonadjacent to the three negatives and can have at most six incidences of common neighbours with them in all. Contradiction.

If k is nonnegative, it has no double (any double would need all fourteen neighbours positive), so is a rook grid. Here X must contain x and remove two of its six neighbours to make degree four, requiring a five-site input. Cost 51 for a five-site input demands X a triangle and Y an anticomplete edge (the signed-edge excess e(X)+e(Y)−e(X,Y) is 4). Thus the surviving grid removes from T∪P one of the three mate pairs at x, either T∖{x} or a U mate pair, and adds the two Y sites (disjoint from T∪P to avoid doubling). In the first case removing the Y edge from the alleged grid leaves P, impossible because the third cap of that edge would have degree two in P. In the second case deleting the two adjacent degree-three U sites from T∪P leaves seven sites with 14−5=9 edges, whereas a grid minus an adjacent pair has eleven. Thus g is extra.

Moreover g cannot be the old mark of a negative windmill −s from [the norm-20 unit proof](plus20-unit-windmills.md). It has value +1 on T and on the two outer lows a,b of P (anticomplete to T), and zero on U, so g≥0 throughout T∪P. A windmill mark g=(A−3I)s−1 has g=−1 throughout its support. Thus that s and c would be disjoint. Their old cancellation would give c+s, a nonnegative +3 charge-two field with mass 18 and norm 22 having two doubles, already excluded. The windmill marks themselves number 99·choose(7,3)=3465, all distinct by the proven unique height recovery and all outside the 105336 short marks.

## At most four buckets map to a given g

Any representing triangle T is an isolated positive triangle of g: its three entries are +1 and g=−h≤0 on the entire T wing. Distinct such triangles are disjoint and anticomplete. For fixed T the bucket b=D_T−g is unique. Because g=D_T+1−h with h≤2, its entries lie in {−2,−1,0,1}; every negative double touches every representing T. Write d for the number of negative doubles. The raw norm 51 and mass −11 give 31−3d negative units and 48+3d zeros. Each T sends total negative neighbour mass 24 (two positive neighbours internally per site and required signed sum −6), so touches 24−2d negative units, all d doubles, and 12+d zeros. Each exterior site touches at most one per T.

If five triangles represented g, each of their ten pairs would have exactly 18 common exterior vertices (nine nonadjacent pairs and two common neighbours each). Counting incidences instead over the negative-unit vertices, zero vertices and doubled negatives gives at least

    3(120−10d)−6(31−3d) + (60+5d)−(48+3d) + 10d = 186,

using choose(k,2)≥3k−6 on units and ≥k−1 on zeros, if k counts represented triangles touched. This exceeds the 180 allowed incidences. Thus each image covers at most four buckets and each bucket contributes at most six oriented fields. The E8−3465 available images give the second bound.
