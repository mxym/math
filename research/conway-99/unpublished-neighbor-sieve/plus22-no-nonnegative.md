# No nonnegative complementary charge-two field of cost 22

In a hypothetical Conway graph with adjacency A, a complementary charge-two field is an integer vector k with (A−3I)k=2·1. It has coordinate sum 18. We prove it cannot be nonnegative and have squared cost 22, without a rank or shell assumption.

### Nonadjacent doubles

No nonnegative complementary mass-18 field can have exactly two nonadjacent doubles. For if \(x,y\) were such doubles, the other 14 support sites would be singles. Each double has eight single neighbors, and any exterior vertex meeting a double meets no other support site, by the coefficient sum 2 there. All two common neighbors of \(x,y\) are therefore singles, and their two eight-element single-neighbor sets \(U,V\) exhaust the fourteen singles. A single in \(U\setminus V\) has exactly three single neighbors by the coefficient equation, and exactly one in \(U\) and two in \(V\) by the common-neighbor equations with \(x,y\); no exterior point can occur in those common counts. Since \(U\cup V\) is all the singles, these counts cannot overlap. Thus no \(U\setminus V\) site can see \(U\cap V\), and analogously neither can \(V\setminus U\). Each of the two intersection sites, which sees both doubles, has to have exactly one single neighbor. They would have to be adjacent to each other, impossible since they already share two common neighbors \(x,y\).

### General adjacent-double extension

A nonnegative integral \(k\) of mass 18 satisfying \((A-3I)k=2\mathbf1\) cannot have exactly two doubled sites and all others single or zero, **even if the doubles are adjacent**. The nonadjacent proof is above. For adjacent doubles \(x,y\), their single-neighbor sets \(U,V\) each have size six. The unique cap \(c\) of \(xy\) is a single in their intersection: a zero cannot meet two doubles. For \(u\in U\setminus\{c\}\), the unique common neighbor of \(u,x\) must be a single in \(U\), since \(u\) is not adjacent to \(y\) and no zero adjacent to \(x\) can also see a single. It is not \(c\), since \(x,c\) are adjacent and already share the cap \(y\). Thus the induced graph on the five vertices \(U\setminus\{c\}\) is 1-regular, impossible.

Consequently there is no **nonnegative** complementary charge-two cost-22 field at all: the excess \(\sum_i k_i(k_i-1)=22-18=4\) permits only two doubles in the nonnegative case. In an unrestricted integral field of that mass and cost, the two exceptional coordinates each lie in \(\{-1,2\}\). One negative together with one double is impossible, because a negative vertex requires neighbor coefficient sum \(-1\). The sole remaining possibility is two negative units; they must form an edge, and they are anticomplete to the twenty positive units, which induce a 5-regular graph. This does not claim exclusion of that signed case.
