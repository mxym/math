# Three separate theta components cannot use three doubled cubic axes

Use the [triangle-wing split chart](big-triangle-wing.md). Suppose all split degrees at a root are at most three and exactly three original axes \(A,B,C\) carry cubic groups, two each. Suppress the degree-two chains in components with cubic groups. Their cubic core **cannot be three separate theta components**. This does not exclude other arrangements of the six cubic groups.

There are no chain loops at a cubic group: equal end columns violate their nonpositive shared-axis Gram and opposite end columns annihilate the third column. There are no chains between two groups of the same original axis, by absence of cross contacts between such groups and equality up to sign of the end fields. All six groups are therefore joined only across the three original parts. There are six path incidences at each of \(A,B,C\); solving the three degree equations gives three paths between each original pair. If the core is three theta components, there is consequently one theta per pair \(AB,BC,CA\). Each of the original cells \(AB,BC,CA\) is a direct edge of its theta. Since all three doubled axes have only cubic groups, none of these axes is internal to any chain.

Each theta has odd path lengths \(1,3,3\): it contains its direct edge, all path parities agree, and the degree-two chains have length at most four. By the [universal three-edge matching lemma](triangle-wing-three-distinct.md#two-local-facts-used-beyond-the-preceding-two-cubic-case), every cell on its long paths is a matching cell and their fields are unit. The internal pairs of a theta cannot be disjoint, by the [disjoint-overlap identity](triangle-wing-theta-disjoint.md#the-exact-overlap-identity-when-the-internal-axes-are-disjoint): on its own long middles both corrections are zero, while \(m+u>0\). That identity still applies here: if the internal pairs were disjoint, outside the theta every cell meets either one of its endpoints (a different split group there) or an internal axis of one long path, so the two long fields have no common support there. Thus each theta is crossed, with one shared internal original axis. Their six middle cells give a partition of all six pairs of the four noncubic original axes. In particular all 21 original cells belong to the three theta components, with no separate cycle.

## Forced peripheral stars and endpoint orientation

In each crossed theta its two long middle edges are a two-edge star on the four noncubic axes. Its two leaves differ by distinct middle edges, and its center is the common internal axis. The centers of the three stars are distinct, as using one center twice would require four of its three available incident edges. Call the centers \(x,y,z\) for the thetas \(AB,BC,CA\), and call the fourth axis \(g\). The three edges at \(g\) must appear once each, so every star has \(g\) as one leaf and uses one edge among the centers as its other leaf. Assigning each of the three center-to-center edges to the star which owns it gives a directed triangle, with each center one outgoing edge. By relabeling the original axes if necessary write the outgoing edges \(x\to y\to z\to x\).

At either cubic endpoint of a theta the center of its peripheral star is used as one of the two long endpoint-neighbour axes. Its other such axis is one of the two leaves, and the two cubic endpoints use different leaves. At \(A\) the two theta centers \(x,z\) both occur. The \(CA\) star cannot also use its leaf \(x\) at \(A\), because the original pair \(Ax\) would repeat. It therefore uses the leaf \(g\) at \(A\), and \(AB\) uses \(y\) there. Similarly at \(B\), \(AB\) is forced to use \(g\) and \(BC\) uses \(z\); at \(C\), \(BC\) uses \(g\) and \(CA\) uses \(x\). This specifies the six long paths completely:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
AB & A-x-g-B & A-y-x-B\\
BC & B-y-g-C & B-z-y-C\\
CA & C-z-g-A & C-x-z-A.
\end{array}
\]
Each row also has the indicated direct path.

## A signed-sum contradiction in one far cell

For theta \(AB\), let \(p,q\) be the start fields of the long paths in table order. They are perpendicular, since \(p\) is zero on the \(q\)-end cell at the shared internal axis \(x\), by the degree-two zero rule. The shared-axis Gram at that endpoint then gives zero pairing. Their direct column is \(-p-q\), of norm 56 and hence from an empty direct cell. Thus \(p=q=1\) on all four \(AB\)-sites. Each long field uses its eight negative end-cell sites, four positive middle-cell sites, and four positive direct-cell sites. It vanishes on the other long path's middle because of the shared internal axis, and on its end cells by zero intercell contact from orthogonality. It also vanishes on all other cells touching one of its own axes, either by the degree-two zero rule or through different split groups at the endpoints. Thus its remaining twelve units (six of each sign) fill the three cells on the complement of its four original axes.

The three far cells for \(p\) are \(C_{Cy},C_{yz},C_{Cz}\); for \(q\) they are \(C_{Cg},C_{gz},C_{Cz}\). They share the last cell \(F=C_{Cz}\) only. The four direct agreements and \(p\cdot q=0\) force \(q=-p\) on all four sites of \(F\). For \(v\in F\), the disjoint-axis formula using the \(p\)-end columns gives \(1+p_v\) neighbours in each \(p\)-end cell, of sign \(-1\); its middle column gives \(1-p_v\) neighbours in its positive middle cell. The direct \(AB\) column vanishes at \(v\) because \(q=-p\), so gives one neighbour in \(AB\), of \(p\)-value \(+1\). These main-cell contributions total \(-3p_v\). Hence its neighbours within the three \(p\)-far cells must contribute \(-p_v\), by \(Ap=-4p\). Exactly the same argument holds for \(q\).

But \(F\) is the first matching cell of the \(CA\) path \(C-z-g-A\). At the original axis \(C\), its cubic group uses the other cells \(CA,Cx\). It is therefore anticomplete to the \(p\)-far cell \(Cy\) and the \(q\)-far cell \(Cg\), which use the other split group at \(C\). At the original axis \(z\), its degree-two group pairs it with \(gz\), not \(yz\); so it is anticomplete to the \(p\)-far cell \(yz\). Its own mate must alone supply \(-p_v\) in the \(p\)-far sum, and this mate then also supplies exactly \(-q_v\) in the \(q\)-far sum since \(q=-p\) on \(F\). Yet \(v\) has exactly one contact into \(C_{gz}\): its wing degree three at the \(z\)-split has one first-layer parent and one own-cell mate, leaving one edge into that partner cell. The \(q\)-value of this extra neighbour is nonzero. This contradicts the required \(q\)-far sum. Thus the three-theta core is impossible.
