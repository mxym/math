# A rare reciprocal coefficient counts true (2,34) labels, not arbitrary shapes

On the rank-42 two-type face of [shared-norm-18](shared-norm-18/two-type-true-hoffman.md), at each true root o use F and A=sqrt(7) F^{*7} of the rooted-unsaturated-dyadic note. Let A27(o) count oriented q^27 vectors of the ordinary theta of A. Then
[
  \sum_o A27(o)\le 2G(2,34).
]
Here G is the entire raw positive-charge field population, NOT an averaged W. In the four surviving cases G=8 times 129,102,75,48, respectively. The stronger statement below has separate multiplicities for two actual types. This is an upper bound, not an obstruction by itself.

Use the [preceding lift formula and congruence](rooted-dyadic-reciprocal-gap.md#two-universal-omitted-coefficients-and-a-sparse-face-four-term-gap). Every reciprocal q^27 vector gives a distinct 84-site field y on the exterior of the root, zero on its closed neighborhood, with y.y=432 and 14 row sums zero. The integral residues have the form y_ij≡l_i+l_j mod 8. As before the unordered neighbor-mate axis types are sorted only for notational normalization, NOT by assuming graph automorphisms. Up to reordering axes, swapping axis endpoints, negating the field and a simultaneous potential shift by four, there are 230 initial shapes. The exact row minimum and mod128 test in the predecessor leave seven. For each remaining shape, any integer coefficient tuple c_o,c_1,...,c_14 at the root and its neighbors with the correct residues defines the genuine raw charge a field b=(y+c_o d_o+sum c_i d_i)/8. The normalization is harmless: orient y and identify the genuine sites with ordered indices making the chosen residue shape apply. The graph and integer/charge identities, and graph norm calculation, are proved in the predecessor.

Five cases admit one of the forbidden charge/norm pairs (0,26),(±1,21),(±3,37); in fact (0,26) and (3,37) suffice. Two cases survive:
* (02)^2(15)^3(46)^2: charge −2, c_o=−2 and c=(0,2,0,2,1,−3,1,−3,1,−3,−4,−2,−4,−2), OR c=(−4,−2,−4,−2,−3,1,−3,1,−3,1,0,2,0,2). In both cases t=34 and on negating to make the charge positive, b_o=−1 and precisely five of its neighbors have value +1. The two vectors are distinct even on the 14 labeled sites.
* (11)(37)^6: charge +2, c_o=2, c=(1,1,3,−1,3,−1,3,−1,3,−1,3,−1,3,−1). Here t=34, b_o=−1 and six neighbors have value +1.
The supplied self-contained stdlib replay regenerates all seven and verifies actual signed root and neighbor evaluations. Its short bounded search over c is sufficient: only existence of displayed lifts matters, not absence of other lifts.

All raw G(2,34) fields on this face are unit. One way: if b_i≥2 or b_i≤−2, subtract or add d_i. Norm t′=34+23±2(7b_i+4) is at most 21 at charge1, or at most37 at charge3, respectively. The only possibilities at these cutoffs are the forbidden (1,21),(3,37); larger absolute values fall below minimum. Thus b has exactly six positives and 28 negative sites by its sum −22. No two actual roots can both see five of its positives: their neighbor sets would share at least four sites, but common-neighbor counts in the graph are 1 or 2. At its unique root, the signed projected field y is determined up to sign by b. Indeed y is in the neutral star-zero space, hence orthogonal to d_o and d_i by (y,d_i)=7y_i, and y/8 is the ordinary orthogonal projection of ±b onto that space. Raw charge does not affect this since the space is also orthogonal to the all-ones vector. Thus the sets of positive-charge labels assigned to different pairs (o,±y) are disjoint. If X,Y are the global numbers of antipodal pairs of the first and second type, respectively, we have 2X+Y≤G(2,34), while sum_o A27(o)=2(X+Y). In particular the stated bound follows, without assuming root transitivity.
