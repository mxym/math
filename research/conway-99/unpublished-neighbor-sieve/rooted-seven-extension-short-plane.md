# Three independent short true fields in every seven-extension at every vertex

Fix any vertex o and **any** maximal isotropic original seven-subspace H. There are at least **three linearly independent** integral minus-four fields zero on N[o], with original class in H and squared norm in {14,28,42,56}. In fact there are at least seven distinct antipodal pairs of such fields. These assertions concern the original seven extension and actual integrality; they impose no further maximal dyadic saturation.

At Seidel rank 40 or 42 there is even at least one such field of *original seven-class zero* at each vertex. The argument need not give three independent zero-class fields at rank 40. None of the four listed norms is eliminated here.

## The small determinant without imposing a dyadic extension

Use the local field lattice E and the [integral rectangle kernel](rooted-integral-rectangle-kernel.md) J. Equip J with the actual field inner product divided by seven and denote the resulting lattice by B. It is even integral: for a field (3I-A)z with Dz=0 the new norm is 3|z|²-z^tAz, even since sum z=0. Its graph-mark representative is sum z_i u_i in the original mark inner product.

We claim det B=2^21·7^(44-r), with determinants meaning Gram determinants. At seven this follows from the previous kernel theorem or directly by splitting off the unimodular 14 neighbor marks. The residual unscaled seven lattice has elementary discriminant of dimension 44-r. At odd primes other than seven there is no contribution: in the [rooted projected frame](rooted-rank32.md) E is realized as the full dual of the span of m_b, whose discriminant exponent divides 56. Its graph inner product is 56 times the dual inner product, the new scale is thus eight, and [E:J] is a power of seven.

For clarity, the two-part can be computed directly. The original rank-44 mark lattice is unimodular at two and generated locally by the graph marks. Put U_N=span of the 14 neighbor marks, of raw Gram determinant 2^25/9. If sum a_i u_i for i in N is in the local lattice, its inner product with the exterior mark for each nonmate pair ij is -a_i-a_j+(sum a)/9 in Z_2. Differences using a common index show that all a_i have the same residue alpha mod Z_2. Then the displayed condition is -4alpha/9=0 modulo Z_2. Conversely sum_N u_i/4=-u_o belongs to the lattice. Thus the primitive saturation has index exactly four in the raw N-span and 2-determinant valuation 21. The orthogonal section in the unimodular lattice has the same determinant valuation (extend a primitive basis and apply the complementary dual Gram minor). Under the original graph marks a vector in this section evaluates to an integral field zero on N[o], and all such dyadic fields come from the section because the original mark lattice is self-dual at two and the frame scale seven is a unit. Finally J and E coincide locally at two because 7E is contained in J. This proves the claimed valuation for B.

Take B_H, the preimage of H in E in this divided-by-seven metric. It is an even integral overlattice of B: at seven H is totally singular, and nothing has changed at two or any other prime. Its determinant is 2^21·49 since the nonsplit original seven-discriminant leaves an anisotropic plane. Every vector still gives an integral field at the chosen root. The ordinary Blichfeldt bound gamma_30 ≤ (2/pi)(16!)^(1/15) now shows its minimum squared norm is below ten (indeed below 9.10), hence at most eight and even. This already gives the one-vector assertion for each H, without any numerical lattice search.

## Why the short fields cannot be confined to a plane

Use the published [Cohn–Elkies sphere-packing bound, Table 3](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v157-n2-p09.pdf), whose upper bound for **center density** in dimension 30 is 12.30390, in particular below 13. A rank-30 lattice of Gram determinant D and minimum at least ten gives center density at least (5/2)^15/sqrt(D).

Every positive even integral lattice K of rank at most two has a sublattice of index at most seven with squared minimum at least ten. Rank zero already satisfies this vacuously. For rank one multiply a generator by three. For rank two of minimum at least four, use 2K of index four. Otherwise take a reduced basis u,v with |u|²=2, (u,v)=b, |b|≤1, |v|²=c even. For c≥4, the sublattice generated by 3u,2v has index six; vectors with nonzero v-coefficient have norm at least 4(c-b²/2)≥14 and multiples of 3u have norm at least 18. If c=2 and b=0, identify K with the Gaussian integers with norm twice the ordinary norm; multiplication by 2+i has index five and minimum ten. If c=2 and |b|=1, identify K with the Eisenstein integers in the same scaling; multiplication by 3+omega for omega²+omega+1=0 has index seven and minimum fourteen.

If the B_H-vectors of squared norm at most eight spanned a space of dimension at most two, intersect B_H with this space to get a primitive section K, and choose an integral group complement. Thin K as above, leaving the group complement unchanged. The resulting lattice of index at most seven has no nonzero vector of norm below ten: any such vector would already lie in K. Its center density would be at least (5/2)^15/(7 sqrt(2^21·49))>13; the last inequality can be checked as 5^30 > 13²·2^51·7^4. This contradicts Cohn–Elkies. Therefore at least three independent vectors are short. The same density argument without thinning gives one nonzero short vector in B itself when r=40 or 42, since its determinant is at most 2^21·7^4.

For the count let k be the number of antipodal pairs of nonzero B_H-vectors of norm below ten. On any large finite patch in the lattice, order the vertices by a generic linear functional and join pairs whose difference is one of those short vectors. Each vertex has at most k earlier neighbors, so the greedy coloring uses at most k+1 colors. One color gives a subset of the patch with mutual squared distances at least ten and density fraction at least 1/(k+1). To apply the packing bound rigorously, take lattice boxes of growing side, and periodically repeat the selected color in translated boxes separated by a fixed sufficiently wide empty boundary. The lost fraction tends to zero. Thus center density at least (5/2)^15/((k+1)sqrt(2^21·49)) is achievable in the limit. If k≤6 this exceeds 13 by the same exact inequality, a contradiction.
