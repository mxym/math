# Two candidate matrices and uniqueness from the 46-word B12 chart

In the [rank-36 B12 plus seven-unit 24-component reconstruction](one-current-two-components.md), **at most two complete candidates** need be checked, and **at most one actual graph** realizes the specified 46 full words. The component labels and the distinct B12 tags for withheld sites are fixed as in that note.

First the four extra nonzero tags among the fourteen withheld words are determined without any choice. In the 85-by-14 cross-incidence matrix, the projection of every row onto the 12-dimensional B12 row span H was already fixed by its H pairings. Both missing dimensions are supported on the ten zero-current tags, so the four nonzero-tag coordinates of each row are fixed. In particular their full W contacts are known; the split-plane formula determines their mutual adjacencies. Include them in the known induced block P, now of order 89. Denote the ten remaining sites by their distinct B12 tags and the 89-by-10 incidence by Z. The matrix of their B12 incidences is the unoriented pair-incidence of two isolated edges and two disjoint four-cycles; its row space has dimension eight and its orthogonal plane is spanned by the alternating edge vectors of those cycles. Call their disjoint four-site supports S and T. These are sets of graph vertices indexed by pair tags, not assertions of graph-theoretic 4-cycles among the ten far vertices.

**A forced anchored row.** For a subset S of four graph vertices write \(d_S(x)=|N(x)\cap S|\). Across all 99 vertices \(\sum d_S=56\) and \(\sum\binom{d_S}2\le2\binom42=12\). Since \(1_{d=1}\ge d-2\binom d2\), at least 32 vertices have exactly one neighbour in S. At most ten of these are outside the known block, and at most twelve have two or more neighbours in T. Consequently at least ten known vertices have precisely one S-neighbour and at most one T-neighbour. For such a row, the B12 endpoint sums determine both of its cycle patterns uniquely: zero or one incidence on a cycle of distinct edges is determined by the endpoint degrees, and the two isolated-edge bits are already pinned. Both cycle totals can be read off without knowing Z. The component of this row along the normalized S-alternator is \(\pm1/2\), so this yields a fixed nonzero vector in the residual two-plane. Test for such a row and reject if none exists. A second independent residual has at most two choices from its norm and inner product with the anchor; every other row is fixed. If all residuals are collinear, the anchor already fixes them. Thus at most two candidate incidence matrices Z remain before checking the actual graph equations.

**There cannot be two actual completions.** Write the full graph in blocks as \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}P&Z\\Z^t&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). In any true solution Z has full column rank. Indeed \(\ker Z\) is contained in the plane of the two cycle alternators, every vector of which is centered and has at most four positive and at most four negative entries. The top-right equation \(PZ+ZB=2J-Z\) shows that B preserves \(\ker Z\). A nonzero kernel would have a real eigenvector t of B which, extended by zero on P, is a centered eigenvector of the whole adjacency with eigenvalue 3 or −4. Change sign if needed so its largest absolute entry is positive M. At eigenvalue 3 at most three other positives can contribute, so all must equal M, all must be neighbours, and no negative can be a neighbour; applying the same observation to each gives a forbidden \(K_4\). At eigenvalue −4 it must see all four negatives, each equal to −M; their own equations force every one to see all four positives, producing \(K_{4,4}\), contradicting the two-common-neighbour ceiling. Thus \(\ker Z=0\).

For two true candidates \(Z,Z'\), equality of their prescribed real Grams gives \(Z'=ZO\) with O orthogonal on ten coordinates. The twelve B12 rows are fixed, as is the additional anchored row above. Thus O is the identity or a reflection across a hyperplane; in the nontrivial case its normal lies in the alternating cycle plane. The all-one ten-vector is in the span of the B12 rows (sum them and divide by two), so O fixes it. Applying the top-right equation and the full column rank yields \(B'=O^tBO\). The two full adjacencies would therefore be conjugate by a single Householder reflection, extended by identity on the 89 old sites.

If \(w\) is its unit normal, supported on the ten sites, the adjacency difference equals \(wz^t+zw^t\) for an appropriate z, by expanding the reflection. Its zero diagonal gives \(z_i=0\) on the support of w. If the difference is not zero, its real rank is two and its support graph is complete bipartite on \(\operatorname{supp}w\) and \(\operatorname{supp}z\). The [rank-two SRG difference lemma](triangle-complement.md#a-rank-two-difference-is-a-transposition) gives a transposition and support graph \(K_{2,24}\): the two switched sites each lose twelve neighbours and gain twelve. A nonzero vector in the span of the two disjoint four-site alternators has support exactly four or eight, never 2 or 24. This contradiction proves at most one full labeled graph (and leaves open that there is none).
