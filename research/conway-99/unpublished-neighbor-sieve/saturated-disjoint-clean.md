# At most one further grid through a saturated-axis vertex at rank 36

In the notation of the [saturated-prism Schur complement](saturated-prism-isotropic.md), let \(e\) be a saturated axis at \(o\) at Seidel rank 36 modulo seven. Among the other six axes **two disjoint pairs cannot both be clean**. Combined with the [clean-matching theorem](saturated-prism-clean-matching.md) excluding incident pairs, the number of grids at such \(o\) is at most seven (six on \(e\), at most one other). There is no component-size hypothesis. This does not exclude the existence of a saturated triangle, the nonsaturated roots, or the whole rank.

We give a bounded two-square proof. Label the two disjoint pairs of axes 01 and 23, and put \(X=C_{01}\), \(Y=C_{23}\), each consisting of the four far vertices of a clean square. Both nine-site extensions through \(o\) are rook grids. Every outside vertex has exactly one neighbor in a grid, by its eigenvector equation. An \(X\) vertex misses \(o\) and the four parents of \(Y\), hence sees precisely one site of \(Y\), and conversely. Enumerate all 24 perfect matchings. Each pair of signed nonmate parents on axes \(i\in\{0,1\},j\in\{2,3\}\) shares \(o\) and one far vertex. This gives **16 distinct compulsory sites**, four independent sign choices for each of four crossed pairs. Each has no contact with any row of \(X,Y\): it already touches one signed parent of each grid.

**Word diagrams.** Use the six-site words, scaled mod-seven pairing \(b(u,v)-4A_{uv}\), and actual dimension-three span / sum zero of the four square residuals as in the saturated-prism clean-square and matching notes. Here \(b=43-18n-4k-6h\) with the same exact-match, wing-match and unlike-wing-same-bit counts. The parent bits occupy the corresponding two zero-wing slots in every square word. Its other four columns permute \(2,3,4,5\); every row is wing-balanced. For \(X\), fix two of those four slots to name the future axes 2,3; gauge parent sign/axis exchanges, the name exchange of wings 1,2, independent bit exchanges in the four new slots, and sort independently within slots 2,3 and 4,5. These are only changes of description of this six-prism wing, not symmetries of the unknown graph. Direct enumeration leaves 83 first diagrams. For \(Y\) use every one of the 23,040 balanced diagrams. The checker treats all 24 relative perfect matchings, checks off-diagonal row and column sums of the residual cross-Gram are zero, and rejects any pair \(x,y\) already sharing too many near or within-square neighbors for its proposed adjacency. More explicitly the bound on the number of common near symbols is the corresponding entry of \(2J-E-A_XE-EA_Y\), where \(E\) is the matching adjacency.

On three rows per square set \(G\) to the resulting joint \(6\)-by-\(6\) Gram. Its rank \(h\) is at least two since either square's self-Gram has rank at least two; \(h>5\) is impossible. Each of the 16 crossed signed-parent classes contains at most 96 wing-balanced candidates. For each candidate \(z\) all six base pairings \(w\) are prescribed by \(b\) (no base edges). For a nonsingular principal \(h\)-block \(I\) set \(p=G_{II}^{-1}w_I,\ \Delta=w-G_{:,I}p,\ \nu=-w_I^tp\). The remaining ambient space is nondegenerate of dimension \(5-h\); the residuals of the six base vectors there are mutually orthogonal isotropic vectors. Near-symbol pairs in distinct prism slots have capacity \(2-[\text{same wing}]\); subtract usage by \(X,Y\). Two sites cannot agree twice in a wing, or more than \(2-A\) times overall.

**Lift all actual possibilities, including singular ones.**
* \(h=5\): require \(\Delta=0,\nu=0\); full pairing of two candidates is \(w_I^tp'\).
* \(h=4\): the remaining line is nondegenerate, so the six base residuals vanish and \(\Delta=0\). Allow both forms \(q(t)=t^2,3t^2\) and every solution \(q(t)=\nu\) in the common form.
* \(h=3\): in the remaining plane, if the base residuals vanish, \(\Delta=0\) and both plane forms \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1),\operatorname{diag}(1,3)\) are tested. Otherwise the plane is split. Write each base residual as \(\alpha_j u\) on a single isotropic line and an extra mark as \(x u+y f\), with \(u\cdot f=1\). Then \(\Delta=\alpha y,\ 2xy=\nu\).
* \(h=2\): the three-dimensional remaining space contains at most one isotropic line of mutually orthogonal base residuals. It must contain one, because the three-dimensional actual span of the first clean square cannot fit in the nonsingular 2-space \(I\). Use \(2xy+\epsilon z^2=\nu\), with \(\epsilon=1,3\) and \(\Delta=\alpha y\).
* In either case, if at least one compulsory word has \(\Delta\ne0\), its \(\Delta\) gives the common projective direction of \(\alpha\); normalize the first nonzero coordinate to one. Every such direction occurring in the broad initial word pool is tested. If instead **all** 16 have \(\Delta=0\), the \(h=3\) plane enumeration with no base residual gives a valid superset even if a nonzero base radical exists; for \(h=2\) test all \((x,0,z)\). Thus neither a singular Gram nor an invisible tested contact is treated as an actual vector relation.

In any fixed lift chart, candidates \(v,w\) in different classes have prescribed mutual residual pairing \(s=p_v^tw_I+f_v^tH f_w\) (the first term uses the other word's \(w_I\); \(H\) is the remaining-space form). Their proposed adjacency \(2(b(v,w)-s)\) modulo 7 must be 0 or 1. Check the common-symbol restrictions above and disallow any near-symbol pair used by both words when its remaining capacity is less than two. Iteratively remove any candidate lacking a compatible member in any other **required** class. No solution of all 16 classes can be removed by this necessary filter; no assumption of its converse is needed. Every chart loses a whole class. Counts:

The [standalone replay](saturated_disjoint_clean.py) uses Python 3 + NumPy, exact integer/mod-seven arithmetic only; run with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`. A full pass typically takes about two minutes.
