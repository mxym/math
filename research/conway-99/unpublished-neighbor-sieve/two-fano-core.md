# The unique 24-site core of two orthogonal Fano lines

**Theorem.** Two distinct orthogonal actual Fano lines have a unique possible induced graph on their 24 signed sites with the normalized labeling below. Within it exactly eight sites belong to a sign side or cap an edge of the configuration, for **both** Fano lines. Two further distinct such sites necessarily lie outside the core. Consequently, in any Conway graph at least **ten vertices** have all seven of their incident triangle wings two-cut-free. This does not exclude two Fano lines or establish a graph completion.

For an orthogonal Fano pair (F,G), cite the [orthogonal endpoint intersection and exterior counts](../four-root-exclusion/README.md) and the rank-free [opposite doubles are adjacent](shared-norm-18/rank36-four-orthogonal-cut.md). Their four signed intersections are singleton sites 0,1,2,3 of respective signs ++,+−,−+,−−, and edges 03,12 exist. The only other twenty sites in the union form four ordered five-sets (exclusive positive and negative of F, then those of G). Every exclusive site sees exactly one of each sign in the other Fano, since the other fifteen-set misses its support.

Within each Fano label its positive and negative sites by the nonzero vectors and covectors of the binary three-space, with adjacency given by nonzero pairing: the nonneighbors of a positive form a Fano-plane line. The two shared positive sites can be any ordered independent vectors, chosen as the first two basis vectors. The two shared covectors have prescribed evaluations (0,1),(1,0) there. They are independent; extending the two basis vectors by the nonzero element of their common kernel makes the shared covector labels 2,1 (in binary integer notation). This works independently for both Fanos, consistently fixing the same physical 0,1,2,3. Label the remaining five elements of each side by 3,4,5,6,7. No graph automorphisms are assumed.

Only four opposite-configuration exclusive cross graphs remain unspecified, one for each signed choice. For a fixed choice, the five vertices on one side all need one neighbor of the specified other sign; a prescribed shared site already supplies one to three of the five (its fourth same-configuration neighbor is shared). The two still missing a neighbor must match the corresponding two on the other side. Therefore the four binary swaps exhaust the 16 candidate graphs. [The direct standard-library checker](two_fano_core.py) rejects 15 because some pair in the core has more than (2-A_{ij}) common neighbors there. On the survivor its eight signed-or-edge-capped sites for both are explicitly 0,1,2,3,5,10,15,20. Edges 03 and 12 have no cap inside the union and hence have actual caps outside. These two caps are distinct: a single vertex cannot see both 0 and 1 of F’s positive side outside that Fano support. They too cap a true Fano edge in both lines.

By the [thin-cut emission theorem](arbitrary-wing-thin-cuts.md), at most two different Fanos can be emitted by cuts, and a triangle emitting a given Fano cannot contain a vertex on that Fano's support or a contact whose two signs are adjacent. The ten sites above are therefore simultaneous clean roots if two lines are emitted. With one emitter its fourteen support sites and 28 edge caps already give 42, and with none every root is clean.
