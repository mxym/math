# No nontrivial far adjacency trade has rank at most four in odd characteristic

Suppose two labeled Conway graphs have identical neighborhoods of a triangle, including all sixty labeled six-site far contact words. For their adjacency difference F, any odd prime p satisfies

    F ≠ 0  ⇒  rank_Fp(F) ≥ 5.

The [earlier real-rank and mod-seven trade obstruction](far-rank-four-trade.md) proves this at seven and over the reals; the proof here covers in particular three and five, where direct lifting of six-minors does not suffice. There is no assumption on the full Seidel rank, that a triangle is saturated, or that any far rows are specified. No existence or rank branch is excluded.

**Common tests.** Use the active principal difference matrix, again F, with P and N its positive and negative adjacencies. As proved in [the earlier trade note](far-rank-four-trade.md), F is symmetric, hollow, signed ternary, every row has equally many positive and negative entries k with 3≤k≤8, no two rows coincide, and tr(F³)=0. The latter also follows from the paired spectrum of the real difference of the two equal-rank projections. For distinct i,j the number of common wing sites of their two words is at most C_ij=2−max((P+P²)_ij,(N+N²)_ij); in particular this must be nonnegative. The six-site word sums on the positive and negative positions of each row coincide. Squaring their common sum gives ∑_(p∈P_i,n∈N_i) C_pn ≥ 6k_i. These are necessary actual integer tests, not modular replacements.

For each odd p a rank-d reduction of F represents its nonzero rows by distinct nonzero isotropic vectors spanning a nondegenerate d-space, with their pairings equal to the original signed entries modulo p. Distinctness holds because actual signed ternary entries are reconstructed uniquely in odd characteristic. Dimension one has no nonzero isotropic points. Whenever the complete signed matrix is reconstructed, the earlier terminal check applies: in each remaining case P²=N² and the only possible additional common adjacency between active sites is on disjoint antipodal-row pairs. The support contains a triangle. For the matrix K of additional common edges, subtraction of the two SRG equations gives KF+FK=−F. The antipodal matching then demands alternating binary choices around that triangle, impossible.

**At five.** Choose a nonsingular principal d-by-d block: one exists by choosing a basis among the vectors of the nondegenerate quotient. Enumerate all symmetric hollow signed blocks for d=2,3,4, identifying only basis permutations and simultaneous changes of basis signs. Basis changes are not asserted to be graph automorphisms. A site's signed pairing word b against this basis is in {0,±1}^d and satisfies b^tH^(-1)b=0 mod5. Its pairing with another vertex must be 0 or ±1 mod5, which determines its actual integer entry in F. Enumerate the maximal sets of pairwise compatible antipodal signature lines containing the basis lines. There are 1, 2, 68 such sets across the respective basis types; at d=4, the sizes are 4 twice, 8 fifty-eight times, and 9 eight times. For each set, try every subset of its signed representatives, keeping at least one orientation of every basis line and checking the exact row constraints. The integer sum of the occupied signed signatures is zero, because the signed basis columns each have zero sum. At ranks 2,3,4 the exact near-incidence and common-neighbor tests leave 0,0,8 cases respectively, all eight failing the terminal check above.

**At three.** A nonsingular d-dimensional symmetric form in odd finite characteristic has a diagonal representative diag(1,…,1,c), c=1 or 2, over F3. This also follows directly by diagonalization and the congruence diag(2,2)≅diag(1,1). Enumerate all nonzero isotropic lines L0, orienting each once; at d=4 there are 16 or 10. For each subset L spanning the ambient form, let B be its Gram matrix reduced entrywise to {0,±1}. The actual occupation of a line is one of its orientations, or both. Write m_i=+1,−1,0 respectively. Then exact row sums require Bm=0 over the integers. Fix L up to sign changes of ambient coordinates or permutations of equal entries of the diagonal form; these are actual form isometries and do not impose graph symmetry. To enumerate m efficiently, reduce B at auxiliary prime 1,000,003 and try every assignment in {0,±1} on the free columns. Every integer solution occurs; check all pivot coordinates, and check the whole exact integer Bm=0. At d=4 the two forms leave 502/47 spanning occupancy-mask representatives, 2378/57 possible signed occupations and finally 1/0 cases after the actual word test. The sole candidate fails the terminal check. Lower dimensions leave none.

**At larger odd primes.** By the paired real spectrum, if the real rank were at least six some principal six-minor would be nonzero. Mod-p rank at most four forces all six-minors divisible by p². For p≥13 this is impossible by Hadamard's 5³ bound for six-by-six hollow signed matrices, and for p=7 or 11 the 4562 sign-normalized six-site graphs in the earlier checker have no nonzero determinant divisible by 49 or 121. Thus the earlier real-rank-four exclusion applies. The [standalone Python/NumPy replay](far_odd_trade.py) checks both small-field atlases and the 7/11 finite determinant assertion in approximately two seconds. No LP solver or floating-point arithmetic is used.

**Known-row corollary, including singular pivots.** Over any field let two symmetric matrices G,G' have rank ≤r and agree on rows I of full-row rank h. Their column spaces share the h-dimensional span of those same columns. Their sum has dimension ≤2r−h, and coordinate restriction to I on this sum has rank at least h, because the rows G[I,*] have rank h. The image of G−G' is in the kernel of this restriction on the sum, so rank(G−G')≤2(r−h). This does not need a nonsingular principal block on I. Thus if the actual signed far adjacency differences are also the (up to a unit) differences of these two symmetric markings, knowing h≥r−2 forces uniqueness, for every odd p. One may append common auxiliary marks with known exact pairings without changing the argument.

For six prism wings, mod seven the full known near rows have rank 31; at respective Seidel ranks 36,38,40,42 there exist 3,5,7,9 additional far rows bringing h to r−2 and therefore guaranteeing at most one actual graph. This is a uniqueness bound for labeled words and sufficiently independent specified rows, not a claim that any supplied rows work, that words are feasible, or that there are at most two abstract terminal candidates. At five the known near rows have rank 31−e, with e as in [the 60-site five-rank theorem](six-prism-far-five-rank.md). In the e=1 case lift a nonzero invisible flow h to an integral circulation and append the auxiliary mark (∑h_i u_i)/5. It belongs to the unimodular 5-local marked lattice because all graph pairings are integral; all of its evaluations, and its squared norm, are determined by the near words. On the wing it evaluates to h itself (since Lh=5h); hence it pairs nontrivially with some previous radical current: the current-space metric is twice the ordinary A2^6 form, nondegenerate at five. Every other linear combination of the old wing marks pairing with that current is zero mod5. Thus the appended row genuinely raises h by one. In either case the fully known rows have rank at least 31; with r=44 the same fiverank trade argument gives at most one completion after eleven suitably independent far rows, without conditioning on visibility.
