# At a connected saturated triangle, at most three independent five-currents are invisible

Use the [mod-five current map](saturated-five-currents.md). Suppose the 12 transversal triangles of a saturated triangle T contract to a **connected** properly three-edge-colored cubic multigraph K. Its 18 edges allow parallels but not loops. Orient each undirected edge and write V=ker(incidence of K over F5). This is a seven-dimensional space. The far contacts evaluate its flows on all 60 rows. The kernel E has dimension **at most three**. Equivalently the flow marks have rank at least four in their actual mod-five wing complement, and after removing the hyperbolic pairs contributed by the flow marks the remaining nondegenerate dimension is at most seven (precisely 1+2 dim E). No Seidel rank or global symmetry is assumed. Disconnected contractions are not covered by this theorem.

The result follows from an exact finite proof, not a search for the 99-vertex graph. Its inputs are the must-have contacts from a single triangle and the proved [ten-edge minimum](saturated-nine-edge-five-flow.md). The [checker](connected_saturated_five_rank.py) requires Python 3 and NumPy and uses only integer arithmetic. Here are the coverage and rejection arguments.

**Graph catalogue.** The first two physical colors are perfect matchings whose union is a disjoint collection of alternating even cycles, including parallel digons. Their sizes divided by two give a partition of 6. Put each possible partition in its linear consecutive-vertex representative; the third color ranges through all 11!! perfect matchings. Drop a disconnected union. To remove relabeling, traverse a connected edge-colored graph from a chosen tail with neighbors always visited in physical-color order (breadth first); write the three visited indices per successive visited tail and minimize this code over roots and permutations of the three colors. Identical codes give an actual isomorphism by their traversals. This only relabels input coordinates; it never posits an automorphism of a hypothetical graph. There are 580 resulting types.

**Four-plane coverage.** E is totally isotropic for the ordinary edge metric on V: expanding from one edge to two opposite darts scales it by two, and the true circulation norm identity gives 5 | h·h for invisible h, hence all pairings vanish by polarization. Every nonzero vector of E has at least ten nonzero edges. Assume dim E≥4 and choose an arbitrary four-plane E₀≤E. The radical of V has dimension b, with counts 352/196/24/8 for b=0/1/2/3 in the catalogue. For clarity, the four-plane enumeration does not assume it contains that radical. Diagonalize the edge metric, splitting off the radical; a nondegenerate F5 form is congruent to diag(1,…,1,ε), ε=1 or 2. Its Witt index in dimension n is floor(n/2), except for n even and ε=2 when it is n/2−1 (−1 is a square). Every totally isotropic subspace is contained in a maximal one. Every maximal one contains the radical, so it is the complete inverse image of a maximal isotropic subspace in the nondegenerate quotient. Enumerate that quotient's maximal subspaces by all reduced row-echelon pivot sequences and every freely variable coordinate, keeping exactly zero mutual pairings including the diagonal. Restore the entire radical. Retain candidate four-planes if their full projective codeword lists have no support ≤9. When the maximal lift has dimension five, **all** its hyperplanes are tested: their projective normal must pair nontrivially with every short vector of the lift, not merely with the radical. The occurrence counts can include harmless repetition due to extension to more than one maximal plane. At most five occurs by the preceding radical census. There remain 24,487 occurrences on 134 K types.

**Coverage of actual far rows.** A dart is tagged by its evaluation vector in F5⁴ on a basis of E₀; the reverse dart has the opposite vector. Index the 12 sites of each physical wing by its six mate edges and orientation. There are precisely 60 unordered choices of two nonmate sites per wing. Every such tag occurs in exactly one actual far word: its two sites are nonadjacent, have their physical triangle root as a common neighbor, and have no common neighbor in the wing (the underlying same-color edges are disjoint). Conversely every word takes one tag in each wing. The 60 words therefore form a permutation of the 60 tags in each physical wing separately. Their six darts must have distinct tails (an edge of a transversal triangle already has its cap there), and their vector sum must vanish in F5⁴. For every two distinct darts d,e the exact required number of words containing them is
  M(d,e)=2−D(d,e)−(D²)(d,e)−[d,e have the same physical color],
where the wing adjacency D joins the darts at a common tail and each reverse pair. This subtracts all common neighbors on the triangle or its wing and uses λ=1, μ=2.

For speed, reject any candidate as soon as any of the following shortages appears.
1. A tag in one wing cannot pair with a disjoint-tail tag in a second wing whose negative sum belongs to the third wing's tag sumset. Cycle the wings to test all.
2. Some tag has no zero-sum triple with all six tails distinct.
3. Remove any triple containing a dart pair with M=0. A required dart pair appears in fewer than M remaining triples. No two real far words can be identical, since even one physical tag determines its word.
4. For a required dart pair, in one physical wing fewer than M **distinct tags** occur among all remaining triples containing the pair. These cannot represent M distinct real far sites because the tag map is injective in each wing.

The four successive rejections are **20,581; 3,756; 142; 8**, with none left. Thus no E₀ can occur. The rejected abstract planes were only hypothetical simultaneous dependencies, not actual graph classes.
