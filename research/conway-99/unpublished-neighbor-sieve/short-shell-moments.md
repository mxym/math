# Five short shell types are necessary at every remaining rank

Let $A$ be a hypothetical Conway adjacency matrix. Write $G_{a,t}$ for the number of integer labelings $b$ of the 99 fixed vertices with
$$Ab=-4b-2a\mathbf1,\qquad b^tb=t.$$
At least **five** different entries in the following table must have positive counts. The assertion also holds inside each maximal determinant-49 extension, counting the vectors that lift to its dual marked sublattice as specified below.

| $a$ | Tested values of $t$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26 |
| 1 | 21, 25, 27, 29 |
| 2 | 26, 28, 30, 32 |
| 3 | 33, 35, 37 |
| 4 | 44 |

Three disjoint subfamilies must each contribute. Let $p=7^{(44-r)/2}+1$ for Seidel rank $r$. In each row sum the indicated $G_{a,t}$ once per entry.

| Types $(a,t)$ | Lower bound on the sum |
|---|---:|
| $(0,24),(1,25),(2,28)$ | $49p$ |
| $(0,20),(1,21),(1,27),(2,30)$ | $21p/4$ |
| $(0,16),(0,18),(0,26),(3,37)$ | $329p/16$ |

The sum over all eleven types is at least $45685p/257$. Rounding up gives **426,986**, **61,151**, **8,889**, and **1,423** at ranks 36, 38, 40, and 42 respectively. These are counts of integer labelings. Distinct labelings can have the same support, and translating a lattice vector may generate other labelings. The five-type assertion refers to the eighteen entries of the first table. The bounds leave possible populations at every rank.

## Four unimodular lattices from the same extension

Use the [rank-44 reduction](../maximal-marked-lattice/README.md), its [rank-48 gluing](../unimodular-four-shell/README.md), and the [charge dictionary and averaging proof](affine-witness.md). Fix a maximal extension $U$ and its marked sublattice $D=\{x\in U:(x,3u_0)\equiv0\pmod3\}$. Its discriminant consists of a cyclic group of order nine generated by $[u_0]$ and an anisotropic plane over the field of seven elements. A dual vector $y$ of charge $a$ evaluates on $u_i$ as $b_i+a/9$. The labeling satisfies the displayed graph equation, and tightness gives
$$7y^2=t-11a^2/9.$$
The component at seven is zero exactly when $7\mid9t-11a^2$, since the plane is anisotropic. Write $W_{a,t}$ for the number of these dual vectors of charge $a$, divided by eight if the component at seven is nonzero. Eight is the number of vectors of any specified nonzero quadratic value in the plane. Count just charge $+a$ when $a>0$.

Take $K$ with Gram matrix $B\oplus B$, where $B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, in coordinates $a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2$. Glue $U$ and $K$ along an anti-isometry of their discriminant planes to obtain the even unimodular lattice $T$. Also use three neighbors of $T$, taking respectively
$$e=(-2,0,0,0),\quad (-2,1,0,0),\quad (-1,1,-1,1).$$
Each has squared norm eight. With $v=3u_0+e$, take the index-three kernel of pairing with $v$ modulo three and adjoin $v/3$. The kernel has index three by a pairing in $K$ (in the second case use $b_1$), and $v^2=36$ makes its extension even unimodular. Its intersection with the 44-space is $D$ and with the four-space is $K_e=\{k\in K:(k,e)\equiv0\pmod3\}$. The two discriminant groups have order 441. At three the graph of the gluing is generated by $([u_0],[e/3])$. Both have order nine and the two quadratic values sum to an integer. At seven the original anti-isometry specifies the gluing, since the change has index a power of three.

In the unshifted lattice the roots project either to zero or to $\pm a_1,\pm a_2$. Any nonzero projection in the dual of $U$ has squared norm at least two. In the neighbors the roots from $U$ have zero projection, and exactly the signed $a_i$ satisfying $(a_i,e)\equiv0\pmod3$ survive in $K_e$. A new root outside the common kernel is impossible since the squared norm of its 44-dimensional projection is at least $22/9$ by the [dual shifted-coset bound](../unimodular-four-shell/README.md). The same projection bound excludes additional pure four-space vectors from the new cosets.

## The second-moment equations

For a homogeneous harmonic quadratic polynomial $P$ on a rank-48 even unimodular lattice, its weighted theta series is a cusp form of weight 26. This space is spanned by $\Delta E_4^2E_6=q-48q^2+\cdots$. Consequently
$$\sum_{z^2=4}P(z)+48\sum_{z^2=2}P(z)=0.$$
The modularity statement and the graded ring used for the one-dimensional cusp-space calculation are given in [Nebe, Boris Venkov's Theory of Lattices and Spherical Designs](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/papers/venklat.pdf).

Choose polynomials depending only on the four-space projection $h$. Let $K_{ij}$ denote its basis Gram entries and $w_i$ the pairings of $h$ with that basis, indexed from zero. Use $2w_iw_j-K_{ij}w_0^2$ for $0\le i\le j<4$. The Laplacian vanishes because $K_{00}=2$. Sum the equations over all anti-isometries and divide by their number. For each projection the expected multiplicity is $W_{a,t}$: at nonzero quadratic value the orthogonal group is transitive on eight vectors. At zero there is only the zero class. The two charges $a,-a$ contribute equally to these even polynomials after negating both projections.

In $T$ use all $w\in\mathbb Z^4$ with $h^2=w^tK^{-1}w\le4$. Possible signed charges have representatives $0,\pm3$ because $y\in U^*$. In a neighbor use $g=3w$. Since $K_e^*=K^*+\mathbb Z(e/3)$ this integral tuple satisfies $g\equiv jKe\pmod3$ for $j=-1,0,1$, where $Ke$ is the vector of pairings. Conversely these congruences characterize the projected dual. Pairing with $u_0+e/3$ requires $a\equiv-e^tg\pmod9$, and this determines the full three-component. Test $h^2\le4$ and $t=7(4-h^2)+11a^2/9$. The bounds $|g_i|\le3\sqrt{4K_{ii}}$ make the enumeration finite. The checker uses the polynomial on $g$ for this lattice, multiplying the equation by nine. In both models the only graph eliminations needed are the integer constraints on the sum and squared sum, the nonzero minimum fourteen at charge zero, and the established impossibilities $t\le19$ at charge one and $t=24$ at charge two.

Since all roots with nonzero four-space projection are known, these rows involve just 23 populations including $W_{0,0}=1$. The seven independent equations are regenerated with integers. The population $W_{1,23}\ge99$ includes every original mark, and $W_{0,14}\le8$ follows already from the [four-pair root bound](../marked-coset-shells/README.md). The three single-polynomial rows for the displayed family bounds are, in the checker's ordering of lattices, $(2,2)$ in the second shift, $(0,2)$ in the third shift, and $(1,1)$ in the unshifted lattice. The checker verifies their signs and the stated constants. It also verifies a single explicit row combination for the joint bound by equality of exact ranks.

To test four types, keep four of the eighteen nonzero-value populations. The remaining free populations of zero value are $W_{0,14},W_{1,23},W_{2,22},W_{4,46}$. Subtract 99 from the second and include a nonnegative slack for the root cap eight. For each of the 3,060 choices the checker tests the resulting system over the nonnegative rationals. If a nonnegative real combination of columns gives the target, dependence among columns of positive coefficient allows at least one to be removed. It therefore suffices to solve on independent column sets, extending such a set to a basis by zero coefficients. Rational elimination excludes all choices. Fewer than four surviving types are covered by padding a choice with zero coefficients.

Finally average over maximal extensions. A fixed graph solution with nonzero quadratic value lifts to $D^*$ with probability $8/p$, by the finite quadratic-space count in the [affine identity](affine-witness.md). Hence $\mathbb E W_{a,t}=G_{a,t}/p$ at all eighteen types. This converts the three inequalities and their joint bound into the graph counts above.

## When the nonzero labels must all be signed units

Take the eleven types in the family table and add $(2,26)$. There are at least two of these twelve types with a labeling in $\{0,1,-1\}^{99}$. If exactly two occur, one is a norm-18 eigenvector. The other is a norm-16 eigenvector, a norm-24 eigenvector, or a unit labeling of type $(1,25)$. At rank 36 the norm-16 companion alone is insufficient, by the previously established lower bound on the sum of the norm-24 and the two affine support counts. In particular, if a graph lacks a norm-18 eigenvector, it has at least three of these twelve unit-labeling types. The squared norms 26 for $(0,26)$ and $(2,26)$ coincide, although their signed sums differ.

Here are the subtractions needed to count just the unit labelings. Write $N_t=G_{0,t}$. Translating by $d_i=3e_i-Ae_i$ sends charge $a$ to $a+1$ and changes $t$ by $23+2(7b_i+2a)$. Subtraction has the opposite cross term. The class at seven is preserved: $d_i$ is the charge-one evaluation of $u_i$, with trivial component at seven. Negation preserves all counts after grouping the two opposite classes. Thus the following subtractions hold for the folded populations as well.

* Norms 16, 18, 20, and 24 at charge zero are all unit profiles: orient a nonunit entry to be at most $-2$ and add $d_i$ to get charge one with norm at most 19. At charge one, $t=21$ also has only units. An entry at least two gives charge zero of norm at most twelve, with zero impossible by $t'=40-14b_i$. An entry at most $-2$ gives charge two of norm at most twenty.
* The types $(2,26),(2,28),(2,30)$ have only unit entries or zeros. Subtracting at an entry at least two would give charge one of norm at most 17. Adding at an entry at most $-2$ would give charge three of norm at most 33. Equality at 33 would require exclusively nonpositive entries while the distinguished entry has become one. The same test at $(3,37)$ gives charge two of norm at most 20, or charge four of norm at most 44. At equality the same positive distinguished entry is impossible.
* For $(1,25)$ the nonunit count is $8N_{16}$, as in the [affine identity](affine-witness.md). For $(1,27)$ it is $9N_{18}+2G_{2,26}$. An entry two yields a norm-18 vector on subtraction. Conversely, start at any of the nine negative sites of a norm-18 vector and add $d_i$. Its negative side is independent, so the result has a single exceptional positive entry and no exceptional negative entry. An entry $-2$ instead yields $(2,26)$ on addition. The two positives there are nonadjacent. Otherwise subtracting the labeling from $d_i+d_j$ at that edge would give a norm-18 eigenvector with adjacent positive sites, which the [bipartite support theorem](../short-support-packing/README.md) excludes. Either positive can be chosen for the inverse translation, giving a single exceptional negative entry. Larger exceptional entries would force a norm below the corresponding minimum.
* For $N_{26}$ the nonunit count is $8G_{1,21}$. Each $(1,21)$ labeling has five positives and sixteen negatives. If $e$ is the number of positive edges, its number of negative edges is $e-1$ by counting the cross edges from either side. Every negative vertex with $d$ negative neighbors has $2+d$ positive neighbors. Counting common neighbors of positive pairs therefore gives $20-e\ge16+4(e-1)$, so $e=1$. Translating at the unique negative entry $-2$ of a norm-26 vector by $+d_i$ gives type $(1,21)$. More precisely, the inverse at any of its five positive sites creates exactly one $-2$. It creates a $+2$ also precisely when the chosen site is one of the two positive-edge endpoints. Entries of magnitude three or more give an impossible smaller charge-one vector. Inclusion-exclusion for the two orientations gives $(5+5-2)G_{1,21}$.

Replace the four whole-shell populations by their unit populations plus these nonunit contributions. Keep the other moment variables free and allow only two of the twelve specified unit populations to be nonzero. The exact cone check leaves just the three stated pairs among the 66 possible choices. A singleton would extend to eleven feasible pairs and is therefore excluded.

## Bounding the norm-18 alternative

Two distinct norm-18 vectors have squared distance at least sixteen. Suppose their difference $f=t-u$ has norm fourteen. Each site on its Fano support occurs in exactly one of $t,u$, while off that support they agree. Equal norms give seven of the fourteen sites to $t$. Write $k,7-k$ for the numbers there of its two signs. Let $E$ count the edges between those selected sign sets. Each positive has four negative neighbors in $t$. Each of the $9-(7-k)$ negative sites of $t$ outside $f$ can see at most one selected positive, by saturation of pairs on the Fano side. Thus $E\ge3k-2$ and likewise $E\ge3(7-k)-2$. A smaller side of size at most two allows only eight edges. For sizes three and four at least ten edges are needed; their incidences on the four sites give at least eight pair coincidences, while the three sites have at most six. This excludes distance fourteen.

Use this minimum distance in the [rank-34 seed computation](../rank-34-exclusion/README.md). The four capacities when all six labels are independent become $2,3,2,2$, and the four capacities using just the positive labels become $4,5,3,2$. The three seed bounds are now $47,44,43$. Consequently **$N_{18}\le 289520$** at all four ranks.

At rank 42 one also has **$N_{18}\le184800$**, improving to **$92400$ if $N_{16}=0$**. Fix a positive root, its four negative neighbors, and any four of their positive pair labels. The parent/positive projection energy in the cited computation is at least $231/20$ even with only those four labels specified. For two vectors with this data the dot product is at least $2(231/20)-18>5$. Their difference then has squared norm at most 24 and hence has only signed units as nonzero entries. There are no conflicting signs. The common root, the four negative neighbors and the four positive labels imply a dot product at least nine. By the new distance bound it is either nine or ten for distinct vectors.

At rank 42 the bilinear dot products modulo seven come from the [anisotropic discriminant plane](norm16-packing.md). Two norm-18 classes would have Gram determinant $4^2-2^2=5\pmod7$ at dot product nine. The determinant of the plane is a square, whereas five is nonsquare. Thus only ten is possible, giving a norm-16 difference. The sum of the two classes is then isotropic since $36+2\cdot10=56$, so they are negatives of one another. Three vectors cannot have every pair of nonzero classes opposite. Each choice of four labels therefore occurs at most twice, or at most once when $N_{16}=0$. There are fifteen choices of four among the six labels and every vector has at least four positive labels. Multiply by $99\cdot560/9$ to obtain the bounds.

## A bound using only the root and its four negative neighbors

At rank 42 the preceding upper bound improves to **24,640**. It improves further to **18,480** in the absence of norm-16 vectors and to **12,320** in the absence of both norm-16 and norm-20 vectors.

Fix the positive root and the four negative neighbors as above. Let $D_t$ be the positive pair labels of a norm-18 vector $t$ and put $d_t=|D_t|$. The eight other positives have twelve incidences with these neighbors. Of those eight, exactly $d_t$ see two, $12-2d_t$ see one, and $d_t-4$ see none. If a positive of $t$ is negative in another vector $u$ with the same root and four neighbors, it sees none of the four, since the negatives of $u$ are independent. Thus at most $d_t+d_u-8$ sites have opposite signs. At least $5+|D_t\cap D_u|\ge 5+d_t+d_u-6$ sites have equal nonzero signs. Subtraction gives $t\cdot u\ge7$. For distinct vectors the minimum squared distance sixteen also gives $t\cdot u\le10$.

At rank 42 the dot products modulo seven come from the anisotropic discriminant plane. In a basis starting with a norm-18 class its diagonal form is $(4,4)$ up to rescaling the second basis vector. Indeed anisotropy over $\mathbb F_7$ implies that the second diagonal coefficient is a square. Scaling both coordinates gives the standard dot product since $4=2^2$. The eight points of square four then represent every possible class. Distinct vectors in the rooted family have different classes, since none of $7,8,9,10$ is congruent to four. The checker enumerates the 256 subsets of these eight points and retains those for which every pair has one of the permitted dot products. At most four points survive together. Omitting squared difference sixteen reduces the maximum to three, and omitting twenty as well reduces it to two. There are $99\cdot560$ root choices, and each oriented vector is counted at nine positive vertices, giving the three bounds.
