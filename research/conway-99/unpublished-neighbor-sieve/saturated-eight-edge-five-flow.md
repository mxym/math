# Eight-edge visibility of any mod-five current

Let T be a saturated triangle: twelve transversal triangles cover its 36 wing sites. As in [saturated-five-currents.md](saturated-five-currents.md), contract these to a cubic loopless multigraph K with three physical edge colors, one of each at every vertex. The wing sites are the oriented darts of its 18 edges, adjacent precisely within a common tail or across reversal. B is the incidence from the 60 far vertices to the 36 darts. Each far row takes six darts, never both reversals (a mate edge already has its unique triangle root as cap) and at most one in any transversal triangle (each of its edges already has its cap). Columns have ten ones. No rank or symmetry assumption.

**Theorem.** A nonzero circulation of K modulo five that vanishes on all actual far rows has at least nine nonzero undirected edges. In particular the marks of all flows in any fixed subgraph of at most eight edges are jointly independent (i.e. the marking map restricted to its circulation space is injective).

**Centered representatives.** Let h take values 0, ±1, ±2 on darts, with reversal negating h, and let n_j count magnitude-j undirected edges. Write σ_i for the signed integer sum at tail i; u_i=σ_i/5 is integral for a mod-five flow. For e:i→j, (Dh)_e=σ_i−2h_e, (D²h)_e=σ_j+4h_e, where D is the wing adjacency. Both the all-ones block and the physical-wing root incidence kill h by reversal. The wing block of A²+A=12I+2J now gives (BᵗBh)_e=10h_e−σ_i−σ_j. Thus z=Bh/5 is integral, sums to zero, has squared norm 4(n₁+4n₂)/5, and (Bᵗz)_e=2h_e−u_i−u_j. In particular n₁≡n₂ mod5, and subtracting the two mate equations yields 4j on every positively oriented magnitude-j edge. Accordingly the sum over rows of |z| times (number of magnitude-j contacts matching the sign of z, minus the number with opposite sign) is 4j n_j. This applies anew after multiplying the mod-five current by two and recentering, which interchanges its magnitude classes.

**Up to seven edges.** For support ≤6, up to scaling the nonempty multiplicity pairs (n₁,n₂) are (1,1),(2,2),(3,3),(5,0). For (1,1) no row can reach magnitude 5. For (2,2) all four positive z rows would have to take both +2 darts, exceeding two common neighbors. For (5,0) its two positive rows would share five darts. For (3,3), z has twelve unit extrema. Their total net magnitude-2 count is 24. If k counts aligned 2-darts at each extremum, summing C(k,2) gives at least 24−12=12, yet both signs' six pairs together have at most twelve far common neighbors with multiplicity. Hence each sign's pairs all occur twice, and cannot be adjacent or have any common near neighbor. The same holds after doubling and recentering. A trivalent active tail has repeated same-signed darts of one of the two magnitudes (since ±1,±2 are the only nonzero residues and the three sum to zero). The preceding bound in one scaling forbids this. Thus the support consists of cycles, homogeneous in magnitude. Each three-edge magnitude class must be a directed triangle (K has no loops). Two consecutive forward darts share the intervening reverse dart as a near neighbor, impossible.

For support seven normalize to (6,1). The eight extrema of z are units. The magnitude-2 edge contributes net eight, so all eight rows take its aligned dart. Each of the six unit edges' darts can then be aligned in at most two extrema of its sign, by common-neighbor capacity with the prescribed 2-dart. Across both signs this gives 24 slots, exactly the required net unit count 24. Thus every like-signed (1,2) pair has two far common sites. At the head of the positive 2-dart, since that edge alone has magnitude 2, balance forces two outgoing +1 darts. Either shares its local −2 mate as a near common neighbor with the +2 dart, impossible.

**Support eight.** Necessarily (n₁,n₂)=(4,4). Each positive-magnitude class graph has degree at most two: three darts of the same magnitude cannot sum to zero mod five. A degree-two tail of the overall support has both edges of one magnitude; a degree-three tail has a 2+1 magnitude split.

Fix the magnitude-2 graph, v its number of degree-two vertices, and n_w its physical-color counts. The total number T of far occurrences of pairs of two +2 darts or two −2 darts, counted with multiplicity, is
  T=24−2Σ_w C(n_w,2)−2v.
Indeed the homogeneous-sign pairs start with two common neighbors each, minus one for adjacent pairs (λ=1), minus the actual near common neighbors. The triangle roots subtract 2Σ_w C(n_w,2). At any tail of magnitude-degree two: if the two darts point with the same sign, their adjacency and their common third dart subtract two for that sign; if opposite, the two forward darts of their directed path share the local reverse, and so do the two backward darts, subtracting one for each sign. These exhaust the near contributions: a length-two path in the dart graph between sites on distinct undirected edges must use their common tail. This remains true with parallel edges, when both endpoint contributions must be counted.

Here ‖z‖²=16. Let t=# |z|=2 extrema; larger magnitude is impossible from six contacts. Every double takes four aligned 2-darts plus two aligned units. There are b=16−4t unit extrema. They have required total net 2-dart count 32−8t=2b. Hence they consume at least b aligned pairs by C(k,2)≥k−1, and doubles another 6t. Thus Σ C(n_w,2)+v+t≤4. Each magnitude class has a repeated color. Apply the same argument to the rescaled flow to see that the total number of active tails, v₁+v₂, is at most six. Equality is necessary since 16 edge ends need ≥6 cubic tails. It follows that each v_j=3, each color split is (2,1,1), there are four trivalent and two bivalent tails, both double counts vanish, and the pair bounds are tight. All sixteen nonzero z have exactly two aligned and no opposed 2-darts: equality forces Σk=2b and k∈{1,2}. All same-sign far pair occurrences have been exhausted in their signed aligned extrema.

Take one +2 dart e:i→j, let d_i,d_j be the two local magnitude-2 degrees, and r the number of other magnitude-2 edges of its physical color. Its same-sign pair occurrences therefore equal its positive z contacts, hence 4−u_i−u_j. Compute these occurrences instead by the pair budget with the other three +2 darts. Starting with six, the root costs r. A second 2-edge at i costs two if it has the same outgoing sign at i and one if opposite; a second at j costs zero if its local outgoing sign is the same as the −2 reversal and one if opposite. At i the repeated same sign makes u_i=1, and at j the repeated negative makes u_j=−1; all other endpoint cases have u=0. Thus the combined local cost is d_i−1+d_j−1+u_i+u_j. Equating yields r+d_i+d_j=4.

Consequently the two edges of the repeated color each join degrees (1,2), and the two other edges join (2,2). These four edges form a five-vertex path: the two edges among the three degree-two vertices must be a path rather than a parallel pair, as the remaining internal vertex otherwise takes both terminal edges of the same color. Their consecutive colors on the full path are (a,b,c,a), all a,b,c distinct. The same is true of the magnitude-1 path by rescaling. Its two terminal vertices must be distinct internal vertices of the magnitude-2 path, since each requires two edges of the other magnitude. These two terminations have the same physical color. But the three internal vertices of (a,b,c,a) have three distinct missing physical colors, so at most one can receive it. Contradiction. No assertion about larger arbitrary supports or nonexistence of saturated triangles follows.

**Nine-edge strengthening.** The [mixed-pair quota](saturated-nine-edge-five-flow.md) rules out support nine too: an invisible current uses at least ten contraction edges. This still does not imply injectivity on the full circulation space.
