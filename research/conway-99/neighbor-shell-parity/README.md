# Projective shell parity from determinant-49 neighbors

**Claim.** For a positive definite even lattice L of rank 44, level 7, determinant 49, and no scaled-dual vectors of squared lengths 2 through 12, put x_j=|{v∈L*: 7(v,v)=2j}|. The discriminant form is an anisotropic F₇-plane in the application here. Unconditionally x12≡x10 (mod 4) and x13≡0 (mod 4), as well as the additional table below. These are necessary for any even rank-44 lattice of level seven with determinant 49, anisotropic discriminant plane, and no scaled-dual vectors in indices 1…6. The graph hypotheses supply that minimum. No graph automorphisms are used. At seven-ranks 36,38,40 apply the congruences on every maximal isotropic neighbor; see below for an actual support bound, not merely a finite list of congruences.

**Two scalar order-four torus characters.** In coordinates A=F49=F7[i], i²=−1, choose normalized q(a,b)=3(a²+b²) modulo 7; this is isometric to any anisotropic plane by scaling, since norm F49*→F7* is surjective. Write z_j(v)=number of actual vectors in the precise discriminant class v, not combined with −v. For nonisotropic classes z_j(v)=z_j(−v). Multiplication by i is a norm-one order-four motion and the difference d_j(v)=z_j(v)−z_j(iv) isolates the order-four characters of H=ker Norm; an immaterial factor two distinguishes d from an idempotent projection. On even functions of classes, taking the difference I−i annihilates the torus-invariants and its order-two character, leaving exactly the two character spaces χ(g)=±i with g=2+2i, g²=i. This is a symmetry of the finite discriminant representation, not a graph symmetry.

The two order-four character spaces are irreducibles of dimension six. To see this, use the usual theta transformation for the Weil representation, which factors through SL₂(F₇) for an even-signature level-seven discriminant module (see [Bruinier–Stein, §2](https://fbim.fh-regensburg.de/~sto39575/Publikationen/bru_st_hecke_op.pdf)). On a torus-character space there is one basis vector per nonzero norm circle. The T operator is diagonal in these bases with six distinct entries. For S the matrix entries, up to nonzero factors, are sums Σ_(l=0)^7 i^l ζ₇^{B(x,g^l y)} or their conjugates. The companion checker enumerates their seven grouped coefficients in Z[i]: for each of the 36 ordered norm-circle pairs, they are not all equal. This proves nonvanishing since the minimal polynomial of ζ₇ over Q(i) is 1+X+⋯+X⁶. Any nonzero invariant subspace contains one T eigenvector and S with nonzero entries reaches all six; this proves irreducibility. A scalar weight-22 theta vector in them vanishing through j=11 is zero. Each of its Γ(7) components vanishes to order at least 12 at *every* cusp, because the slash-translates are linear combinations of the same components. The torsion-free X(7) has index 168, 24 cusps of width seven and genus 3. Its weight-one line bundle has degree 14, since Δ is nonvanishing in the upper half plane and has order seven at each cusp. Thus the full space of sections of weight 22 with this cusp vanishing has degree 22·14−12·24=20, hence dimension 18 by Riemann–Roch. It injects via leading cusp terms into Ind_U^(PSL₂(7)) ξ, since at order ≥13 the degree is −4. The cusp stabilizer U has order seven, and ξ is a nontrivial character. The ordinary [PSL₂(7) character table](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/lin/L32/) gives 3+6+7+8, multiplicity one, for that induction. The 18D submodule excludes 6 because the only way to remove dimension 6 is that constituent. Component versus dual conventions are immaterial; the 6D irreducible is self-dual. Graph shells at j=1…6 are empty and j=7 only has class zero, so the entire d-sequence is determined by its two independent coordinates at each j=8,9,10,11. Explicit reconstruction needs example modular forms that realize these eight independent numbers; it does *not* assume all formal theta series arise from lattices.

**Exact test modular forms.** Take T=A₆⊕A₆. Its discriminant has exactly the same quadratic form: class r of A₆ consists of x=m−r·1/7, m∈Z⁷, Σm_i=r, and 7x²/2=(7Σm_i²−r²)/2. Under the integer indexing its normalized discriminant q is 3r². The degree-two harmonic ||x||²−||y||² on two equal six-dimensional summands yields a vector-valued theta of weight 8 and is odd under interchanging the two class axes by i. Rotating the finite classes by H acts equivariantly, so gives further modular forms of the very same Weil type. Apply iterated Serre derivative D_k=q d/dq−k E₂/12, and multiply by the full-level E4,E6 in all ways giving weight 22. The derived forms need not themselves be theta series: their exact levels and transformation laws suffice. T and L have ranks 12 and 44, equal modulo eight, so there is no phase twist of their Weil actions. In the checker q₇=e^(2πiτ/7), so the derivative on a coefficient multiplies by j/7. The raw integer polynomial 2i−j used in convolution is (7/2) times the specified norm difference.

**Maximal isotropic lift.** At seven-rank r=36,38,40 let d=44−r=2t+2, t=3,2,1 respectively. The discriminant quadratic space V is minus type of Witt index t. Each maximal totally isotropic H yields the even overlattice L_H with (L_H)*/L_H = H⊥/H an anisotropic plane. Its scaled dual is a sublattice of the original scaled dual, hence has the same minimum bound through j6. Therefore all preceding shell congruences apply to the actual neighbor counts x_j^H, obtained by counting vectors with original class in H⊥. They are not congruences on averaged rational coefficients.

For x12 vs x10, the two residues 5 and 3 differ by the square 4 in F7; thus their supporting nonisotropic projective points are the same norm type. On each such point P let f(P)∈F₂ be the parity of the difference of its two counts of antipodal lines (equivalently one precise opposite-class representative per separate shell). Each H gives Σ_{P⊂H⊥}f(P)=0 mod2. For the zero-output x13 relation take just the parity of the one-shell counts instead. This also applies to the other pairs in the table (their index ratio is a square). If f is nonzero its support has at least 1+(7^t+1)/2 points: fix P in its support. There are ∏_{j=1}^t(7^j+1) maximal H perpendicular to P, since P⊥ is odd nondegenerate of dimension 2t+1. Every such H must be perpendicular to a second support point R. The space (P+R)⊥ has dimension 2t; if nondegenerate split it contains 2∏_{j=1}^{t−1}(7^j+1) such H, if minus then none. If P+R is degenerate its radical dimension is one, the intersection orthogonal space has the same radical dimension, and every eligible H includes it, yielding ∏_{j=1}^{t−1}(7^j+1) through the nondegenerate odd quotient. These standard products also follow recursively by counting isotropic points and incident maximal isotropic subspaces with the orthogonal quotient until rank zero. Dividing gives the stated covering bound. Hence the lower support thresholds are 173,26,5 at ranks 36,38,40. If x10+x12<7^t+3, parity on EACH projective point must match. If x13<7^t+3, every projective-point population there is even. In particular x10=0,x12=2 is impossible in all four still-live ranks, without hypotheses on x8,x9 or x11. The lower bound five at r40 is sharp for the incidence parity code itself: in q=x₁x₂+x₃²+x₄², the five nonsquare projective points (1,5,2,6),(1,6,4,5),(0,0,1,2),(0,0,1,3),(1,3,1,3) cover each isotropic point an even number of times by polar planes. **Status.** This cuts actual candidate scalar/individual-class profiles. It does not eliminate any entire rank or all unrestricted graphs. In particular the earlier high-multiplicity sample tables need not violate the projective parity bound. 
**No automatic global promotion at ranks below 42.** The demonstrated five-point odd codeword at r40 lifts to odd-weight codewords at r38 and r36. Fix an isotropic projective point R in a minus space of dimension 2t+2, reduce R⊥/R to dimension 2t, and replace each support point in the quotient by all seven of its projective lifts in R⊥. For H containing R, each selected quotient point perpendicular to H/R contributes all seven (odd). For H not containing R, H cannot be perpendicular to R (maximality); its intersection with R⊥ projects to a maximal (t−1)-dimensional totally isotropic subspace in the quotient. Exactly one lift of each perpendicular quotient point satisfies the remaining H orthogonality condition. Thus the parity test holds in both cases. The resulting odd weights 35 and 245 show the all-ones functional is not a linear combination of the incident-neighbor tests at ranks 38 and 36 either. This is a code construction, not a realization by graph shells. The checker explicitly tests all 36 finite Fourier entries in Q(i).

**Quantitative sparsity/concentration bound, all ranks.** Let k∈{8,9,11}, and let s,h be the other two indices from this set. On an actual maximal neighbor the two differences at j12 are the sum of four 2-vectors. For indices 8,9,11 their coefficient matrices are signed Hadamard matrices (ℓ¹ length of each output block = twice the ℓ∞ length of its two independent differences); for j10 the matrix is −I. Each input pair of difference entries has ℓ¹ norm at most x_j^H/2. The triangle inequality and x12^H/2≥||d12||_1 therefore give
x12^H+2x_s^H+2x_h^H+x10^H≥4||d_k^H||_∞.
For fixed k, assign m_P≥0, the original class population counted up to opposite signs on projective point P, so Σ m_P=x_k/2. In any H, qualifying P⊂H⊥ map to nonzero quotient points of the appropriate norm circle. Each m_P contributes with sign to one of the two differences. Even if other points map to the same or competing coordinates, the largest individual m gives ||d||∞≥2m−Σm≥Σm−2Σ_{pairs}min. Summing over all H and dividing by the number L of H perpendicular to any one nonisotropic point therefore yields
x12+2x_s+2x_h+x10 ≥ 2x_k − 8 Σ_{P<R} (λ(P,R)/L) min(m_P,m_R)
                            ≥ 2x_k − [16/(7^t+1)] Σ_{P<R} min(m_P,m_R).
Here λ(P,R) counts H perpendicular to both original points. At r42 take the sole H=0, t=0, so λ/L=1; at the other ranks the earlier product formulas bound this ratio. This is unconditional on the ranks, independent of any automorphism or presumed uniformity. In particular if k=11 is carried by one projective point and x8,x9,x10 vanish then x12≥2x11, including x11=2 ⇒ x12≥4 in all ranks. A large correction can make the inequality vacuous on dense profiles; no full-rank exclusion is inferred.
