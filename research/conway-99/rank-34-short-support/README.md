# Rank 34 forces signed configurations on 16 or 18 vertices

**Theorem.** Suppose a strongly regular graph with parameters $(99,14,1,2)$ exists and $2A-J+I$ has rank 34 over $\mathbb F_7$. Let $N_m$ count the vectors $t\in\{0,1,-1\}^{99}$ with $At=-4t$ and exactly $m$ nonzero entries, counting both signs. Then
$$407N_{16}+43N_{18}\ge 1,439,102,\qquad N_{16}+N_{18}\ge3536.$$
If the graph contains no triangular prism, the weighted lower bound increases to 16,920,274. These are necessary conditions. The existence of a rank-34 example remains open.

## Two lattices and a coefficient identity

Use the graph vectors with Gram matrix $C=3I-A+J/9$, put $w(c)=\sum_i c_i u_i$, and write $s=\sum_i c_i$. The lattice $M$ is generated by $u_i-u_j$. Every $u_i$ represents the same order-nine class modulo $M$. Thus $w(c)$ lies in $L=M+\mathbb Z3u_0$ when $3\mid s$, and its class modulo $M$ determines $s\bmod 9$. Its squared norm is
$$3\sum_i c_i^2-2\sum_{i<j} A_{ij}c_ic_j+s^2/9.$$
At rank 34, $L$ has rank 44, determinant $7^{10}$ and level seven. Write $x_j,y_j$ for the numbers of vectors of squared norm $2j$ in $K=\sqrt7 L^*$ and $L$, respectively. Then $x_0=y_0=1$, $x_1=\cdots=x_6=0$, and $(x_7,x_8,x_9)=(N_{14},N_{16},N_{18})$. The lattice transfer and the signed-vector dictionary are established in [Wave 66](https://github.com/YesterdaysLemon/conway-99-research/tree/main/attempts/wave66-spherical-code-shift) and [Wave 71](https://github.com/YesterdaysLemon/conway-99-research/tree/main/attempts/wave71-modular-theta-extension).

The two theta series satisfy
$$2387x_7+407x_8+43x_9=-13,804,112+x_{11}+22,827y_1+2737y_2+196y_3+7y_4.$$
To check this identity use $y=-7^6(x|W_7)$ in weight 22. The script evaluates both sides on the Eisenstein products of odd weights $a,22-a$ with $a=3,5,7,9,11$ and either character placement. For a general product replace the constant on the right by $81242y_0+\sum_{j=0}^6 c_j x_j$, with $c=(-13885354,-4954081,-1685907,-542542,-163198,-45122,-11193)$. The checked products have coefficient rank 15 through exponent 14. The Sturm bound 14 makes these products a spanning set. See the [Fricke transformations](https://github.com/YesterdaysLemon/conway-99-research/tree/main/attempts/wave86-level7-exact) and the [determinant scaling](https://github.com/YesterdaysLemon/conway-99-research/tree/main/attempts/wave101-all-rank-level7-lp).

## Counting graph marks

A mark of type $(a,b)$ has $a$ distinct positive sites and $b$ distinct negative sites, all disjoint, with coefficients $+1,-1$. Count marks whose images have the indicated squared norms. Reversing signs gives a second family when $a\ne b$. Write $P$ for the number of induced triangular prisms.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $(1,1),(2,2),\pm(3,0),\pm(4,1)$ together | 462 | 24,948 | 1,398,474 |
| $(3,3)$ | | $7392-2P$ | $765072+18P$ |
| $(6,0)$, one sign | | $20790-9P$ | $831600+36P$ |

For $(4,1)$ only count marks whose negative site misses all four positive sites. The counts in the first row are distinct images, as shown below.

Here is a reproduction of the subgraph count behind the table. Compute the number of homomorphisms of each labelled simple graph $H$ through six vertices. Removing a site of degree zero or one contributes the factor 99 or 14. For a degree-two site with neighbors $i,j$, use $A^2=12I+2J-A$. The three terms identify $i,j$, delete the site, or add the edge $ij$ after deletion. A quotient with a loop has zero maps. To recover injections subtract the injections of all proper partitions into fibers. For a graph of minimum degree at least three only the prism can embed, with $12P$ injections.

Indeed an embedded vertex of degree at least five has neighbors inducing a matching and cannot occur on at most six sites of minimum degree three. For degree four its four neighbors would all have to meet the only remaining site, exceeding the common-neighbor bound. At maximum degree three we need a cubic graph. On four sites this would be a forbidden clique. On six sites the cubic possibilities are the prism and the forbidden $K_{3,3}$, as seen by taking complements. Finally invert inclusion-exclusion on the possible extra edges to count induced labelled subgraphs. Evaluate the norm formula on each adjacency pattern and divide by $a!b!$. All recurrences are affine in $P$, so evaluations at the formal inputs zero and one certify the whole table.

## Equal images with equal coordinate sums

If $w(c)=w(d)$ and $c,d$ have the same sum, then $t=c-d$ satisfies $At=3t$. Let $m$ be the positive mass of $t$, equal to its negative mass. For $0<m\le6$, a coefficient of magnitude at least two would require at least six neighbor mass of the same sign, yet at most four other units of that sign exist. Thus every nonzero coefficient is a unit. Each sign set has minimum degree three. The preceding classification forces $m=6$ and two prisms with no edges between them, since their internal degrees already equal three. Conversely the indicator difference of two disjoint anticomplete prisms belongs to the 3-eigenspace. It attains Rayleigh quotient three on $\mathbf1^\perp$, whose two eigenvalues are three and minus four.

Prisms with equal images therefore form classes of disjoint prisms. Members share $h=(A-3I)\mathbf1_T$. On a prism this vector vanishes, and outside it takes values at most two because a point can see at most one vertex of each constituent triangle. The identities $\sum h=66$ and $\sum h^2=72$ give exactly 63 nonzero coordinates. All prisms of the class lie in the remaining 36 sites, so every class has size at most six. The total number of unordered pairs within classes is at most $D=\lfloor5P/2\rfloor$.

Within the first row of the table, distinct marks of the same coordinate sum have difference mass at most five. The other sums in that row are distinct modulo nine. Comparing a $(3,3)$ mark with a smaller zero-sum mark also gives mass at most five. Two distinct equal-image $(3,3)$ marks determine one unordered pair of disjoint anticomplete prisms. Order those prisms once and label the two marks so their difference is positive on the first prism. The first mark picks three positive sites in that prism and three negative sites in the second. Its squared norm is $18-2(e_1+e_2)$, where $e_i$ are the internal edge counts of the chosen triples. In a prism exactly two triples span three edges and twelve span two. This costs at most $4D$ unordered collisions at norm six and $48D$ at norm eight. Subtracting the number of equal pairs bounds the loss even for images occurring more than twice. Among positive six-sets a collision requires two prisms, both of norm four.

## Equal images across coordinate sums

A positive six-set $X$ can meet a previously counted image only in the charge $-3$ family. For such a collision $w(c)=0$ where $c=\mathbf1_X+\mathbf1_T$ for a triple $T$, or $c=\mathbf1_X+\mathbf1_Q-e_v$ for four sites $Q$ with $v$ missing $Q$. Here $Cc=0$ gives $Ac=3c+\mathbf1$. A negative coordinate would be the sole negative entry and would have nonnegative neighbor sum, contradicting that equation. If an entry were at least two, every one of its fourteen neighbors would need to be in the support, since a zero coordinate has neighbor sum one. The total mass is nine. Thus $c$ indicates a nine-site 4-regular set, and every outsider has one neighbor on it. Such a set is a three-by-three rook grid. [Nine-site equality case](../../conway-99-involution/grid-density-gap/README.md)

In the triple case $T$ is contained in the grid and determines $X$. A grid has 36 two-edge triples of norm six and 36 one-edge triples of norm eight. In the four-site case $Q$ lies in the grid, $v$ is outside it and $X$ equals the grid minus $Q$, together with $v$. A grid has no four-set with five edges and has 45 four-sets with four edges. For each such four-set there are 50 eligible $v$, since each grid vertex has ten private outside neighbors. Thus the one-sign losses per grid are at most 36 and $36+45\cdot50=2286$ for the two norms. The same bounds apply after reversing all signs. There are at most $g=\lfloor P/6\rfloor$ grids. Each contains six prisms, and the unique triangle-completing vertices of a prism's three matching edges determine the remaining three grid sites.

## Combining the bounds

The prism identity $n_3+3P=4158$ and $n_3\ge1605$ give $0\le P\le851$. The general upper bound on the norm-fourteen shell is $N_{14}\le 2\lfloor(55440-5n_3)/14\rfloor$. [Triangle-pair lower bound](../../conway-99-involution/uncovered-squares/three-grid-shell/Theorem.md) · [Rooted second-moment bound](https://github.com/YesterdaysLemon/conway-99-research/tree/main/attempts/wave100-general-pair-moment)

The estimates give $y_2\ge462$ and
$$y_3\ge24948+\max(0,7392-2P-4D)+2(20790-9P-36g),$$
$$y_4\ge1398474+765072+18P-48D+2(831600+36P-2286g).$$
Substitute these bounds in the modular identity, using $x_{11},y_1\ge0$. Over the 852 integers $P$ the minimum weighted bound is 1,439,102 at $P=851$. Both signed counts are even, giving the stated 3536. For $P=0$ the individual lower bounds are $y_3\ge73920$, $y_4\ge3826746$ and the upper bound is $N_{14}\le4950$.

## Reproduce

Run `python3 count_marks.py` and `python3 verify.py` in this directory. Both use the Python standard library. The first enumerates the induced subgraphs and the second checks the exact rational modular identity, grid and prism multiplicities, and the final inequalities.

The [rank-34 exclusion](../rank-34-exclusion/README.md) combines the marked counts with a separate upper bound on norm-18 supports. The remaining ranks are 36, 38, 40 and 42.
