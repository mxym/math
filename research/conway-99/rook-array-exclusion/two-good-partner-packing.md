# Two good partner pairs leave no third partner at an anticomplete pair

**Theorem.** For two anticomplete graph triangles $A,B$ in an $\operatorname{SRG}(99,14,1,2)$, call $(E,F)$ a partner pair if $A\cup E$ and $B\cup F$ are distinct prisms with equal graph mark. If at least two distinct common good prism mates of $A,B$ occur, the two partner pairs they supply are the **only** partner pairs. In particular
* $A,B$ have at most **two** common good mates;
* if $A,B$ are faces of distinct equal-mark bad prisms, they have at most **one** common good mate.

This improves the common-good-mate bounds three and two in [triangle pair packing](triangle-pair-packing.md). It neither reduces that paper's cap of three on arbitrary partner pairs nor resolves the graph-existence problem.

### Reduction to ten triangle fibers

Use the [triangle-mark identities](triangle-pair-packing.md). Write $a,b$ for the root triangle marks; $(a,b)=1$. For each of three distinct partner pairs $(E_i,F_i)$, $i=3,4,5$, write $a+e_i=b+f_i$. The two equal prisms are disjoint and anticomplete, so $A,F_i$ and $B,E_i$ are anticomplete and all four lines are distinct; $E_i,F_i$ are anticomplete. For $i\ne j$, $E_i$ and $E_j$ are distinct (the mark of $F$ is determined by $E$). Therefore $(e_i,f_j)=(e_i,e_j)-3\ge-2$ forces $(e_i,e_j)=1$ and $(e_i,f_j)=-2$; also $(f_i,f_j)=1$. Thus on six abstract indices label $A=02$, $B=12$, $E_i=1i$, $F_i=0i$. These eight triangle marks have exactly the Gram matrix of $v_{jk}=\sqrt 3(\mathbf1_{\{j,k\}}-\frac13\mathbf1)$: distinct intersecting index pairs have product 1, disjoint index pairs have product $-2$, and norms squared are 4. The 1 and $-2$ products characterize actual distinct anticomplete and perfectly matching disjoint graph triangles, respectively.

A common good prism mate $T$ supplies a partner pair via $a+e_i=b+f_i=-t$. Distinct common good mates give distinct pairs. The corresponding $T$ has the mark assigned to the abstract edge complementary to $\{0,1,2,i\}$ because the three disjoint abstract edge vectors sum to zero. If at least two of the three partner pairs come from good mates, relabel the three leaves to keep those complementary edges $35,45$. The ten resulting fibers are exactly the edges of $K_6$ other than $01,23,24,25,34$, with the prescribed Gram/matching pattern. The finite proof below excludes this ten-fiber pattern.

### Exact root and support census

Complete abstract perfect matchings on the ten indices give four rook grids (three graph triangles matched in pairs, with consistent ternary coordinatizations because the only permitted double contacts of a prism are matched rung endpoints). The core label proof uses the same gauge argument as in [the eleven-fiber verifier](partial-k6-11.md): there are ten fiber nodes and four complete-grid coordinate nodes, connected by 20 individual ternary identifications (12 grid-to-fiber and eight uncompleted matching edges). Normalize a spanning tree, leaving seven independent elements of $S_3$. Three common intermediate fibers on an anticomplete pair cannot all send a given site to the same target. These guards leave 76,336 of $6^7$ assignments, or 13,178 after simultaneous conjugacy. Further relabeling by the independent swaps $0\leftrightarrow1$ and $3\leftrightarrow4$ gives 3,527 orbits (46 of size one, 396 of size two, 3,085 of size four). Each relabeling is applied to the concrete ternary data; the gauge is restored and the checker verifies every orbit by set equality. No symmetry of an ambient graph, or of the contact arrays, is assumed.

An exterior site $x$ contacts at most one vertex per triangular fiber and exactly one of the nine sites of each complete rook grid (by internal pair quotas and 90 edges leaving that grid). Moreover, in the six-index Gram model $a+e_i=b+f_i$ is an identity, so the corresponding actual vector also vanishes after subtraction. On the common 99 vertex directions this implies $(3I-A_\Gamma)(1_{A\cup E_i}-1_{B\cup F_i})=0$. Thus the contact counts of $x$ on the two prism sides are equal. If $x$ touches two disjoint-index fibers its two sites must be rung endpoints: nonrung pairs have their common-neighbor quotas filled inside the prism. Since a vertex neighborhood induces a matching, the support's disjoint-index pairs must therefore also form a matching. Exhausting $2^{10}$ support masks with these three restrictions gives exactly the six ordinary stars and the five exceptional sets of all present indices disjoint from one missing index.

Each ordinary type has 9 vertices, each exceptional type 3. For completeness the program verifies these populations and their uniqueness. Let $C$ be the 10-by-10 core adjacency quotient with diagonal 2 and off-diagonal 1 precisely at disjoint index pairs, and $U_{it}$ the membership matrix of fibers in types. Then
$$
\sum_t U_{it}U_{jt}p_t=3(12I+6J-C-C^2)_{ij},\quad i\le j.
$$
These are the SRG common-neighbor residuals summed over two fibers (for $i=j$, an exterior touches at most one site in that fiber). The indicated vector satisfies every row; the matrix of products $U_{it}U_{jt}$ has full column rank over $\mathbb F_{101}$, by direct pivoting, and therefore over the rationals.

### Necessary contact equation and exterior-row checks

Let $A$ now denote the concrete adjacency matrix of the 30-site core, and let $B=12I+2J-A-A^2$. Enumerate every ternary site selection $z$ in a possible support. Discard selections containing a core pair with $B_{ij}=0$, or with $(zA+z)_i>2$ for some $i$. Any word of size at least three is binary, by the graph's common-neighbor bound. **Both kinds of size-two words can repeat**; include $B_{ij}$ copies for a word on $i,j$. Require the population quotas above, exterior degree $B_{ii}$ at every core site and pair-incidence quota $B_{ij}$ at each distinct core pair. At most one selected word may contain a fixed triple of core sites. Propagate all forced 0/1 choices; on unresolved positive equalities enumerate every subset with the required size. Copies are distinct binary columns, so the census can count a given unlabeled configuration more than once, never fewer times.

For each complete putative contact array and an exterior root word $r$, choose its exterior neighbors only among selected columns $s\ne r$. Their count must be $14-|r|$ and their site marginals must sum to $2\mathbf1-r-rA$. Necessarily $(r,s)$ share at most one core site if adjacent; if they share site $i$, each root's demanded $i$-coordinate is positive. Two selected neighbors of $r$ cannot already share two core sites because $r$ is another common neighbor. Finally, exterior neighbors $s$ disjoint in core contacts from $r$ must pair off inside $N(r)$ with other such exterior neighbors: an edge of this matching already has common neighbor $r$, so its endpoints' core words must be disjoint. These are all necessary conditions. The checker tries both choices for unresolved neighbor slots and all pairings for the final matching. One failed root rejects the contact array; no further global assumption is made.

**Replay.** Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python two_good_partner_packing.py` with Python 3.10+ and NumPy, without `-O`. No LP solver, timeout or branch budget is used. Ten orbits have an empty type, 349 omit a needed incidence row, and the other 3,168 are exhausted in 39,466 integer cover nodes. Only seven of those admit a full exterior contact array: their numbers of binary leaves are 64, 16, 212, 16, 3, 36 and 4, totaling 351. Every one has a failed exterior row. Neither result on the number of common good mates presumes a symmetry or a lattice rank.

The later [eight-fiber exclusion](cube-eight-fibers.md) proves the stronger statement that three partner pairs are impossible even without two common good mates.
