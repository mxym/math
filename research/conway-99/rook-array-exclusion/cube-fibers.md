# Twelve triangle fibers indexed by the complement of a perfect matching

An SRG(99,14,1,2) cannot contain twelve disjoint triangles indexed by the edges of $K_6-\{01,23,45\}$, with triangles at incident indices anticomplete and triangles at disjoint indices joined by a perfect matching. The 36-vertex exclusion holds at every rank and requires no graph automorphism. It implies that the whole graph has **at most 120 nine-vertex rook grids**. All four possible ranks remain open.

## The 63 exterior vertices

Each present perfect matching of $K_6$ supplies three mutually matched triangles. They form a literal rook grid. Indeed, a point of the third triangle meeting the first two must meet corresponding rung endpoints, as unmatched endpoints already have two common neighbors within their prism. Any outsider sees exactly one vertex of a nine-point grid by the [one-contact count](README.md). The eight grids are indexed by abstract perfect matchings. Each fiber is in two grids, and the eight grids joined across these twelve fibers form a cube.

An exterior vertex sees at most one site per fiber. Enumerating the $2^{12}$ subsets meeting each of the eight grid triples exactly once gives nine possible sets of contacted fibers. There are six ordinary types, each hitting the four indices containing a fixed abstract point, and three exceptional types, each hitting the four indices disjoint from a fixed missing pair. All types have four contacts.

The four fibers for a missing pair form two separated triangular prisms. For each prism the three matching edges have distinct caps, the unique common neighbors of their endpoints. They lie outside the twelve fibers because only the missing index could be disjoint from both faces. They have the exceptional type of this missing index. Conversely any such exceptional vertex touches both faces on matching endpoints, so is a cap and the same three caps serve both prisms. Each core vertex therefore sees one cap. Its seven core neighbors leave six ordinary neighbors. If the ordinary populations are $p_a$, each present fiber $ab$ gives $p_a+p_b=18$ by counting these six contacts at its three sites. The connected nonbipartite abstract graph gives $p_a=9$ throughout.

Each individual site of fiber $ab$ sees **three** ordinary vertices of each of the two types $a,b$. To check this, set $C_{fg}=2[f=g]+[f\cap g=\varnothing]$. Each site of $f$ sees $C_{fg}$ core sites in $g$. For a site of $f$, its exterior neighbors must supply $6+12[f=g]-C_{fg}-(C^2)_{fg}$ contacts into $g$ by the SRG equation. Try the seven splits of its ordinary neighbors between $a,b$, adding the known cap. The split $(3,3)$ is the unique one meeting these twelve demands, as checked for each fiber.

## Exhaustion over integer contact counts

From this directory run `python3 -B cube-fibers.py` with Python 3.10 or later and a C++17 compiler. The [Python checker](cube-fibers.py) and [C++ matching enumeration](cube-fibers.cpp) use their standard libraries. The enumeration includes all labelings up to the explicitly stated changes of names.

1. Give each of the eight grids ternary coordinates constant along its three perpendicular lines. The shared fiber between two grids specifies a permutation between their coordinates. After fixing the coordinates along a cube spanning tree, five permutations remain free. The checker partitions all $6^5=7,776$ assignments by the 48 permutations of the six abstract points preserving the missing matching and simultaneous changes of the three labels. It transports the permutations, normalizes again along the tree and checks that the resulting orbits are disjoint and cover the complete list. There are 79 representatives.
2. Six further independent site matchings specify the rungs of the six prisms for missing indices. This gives $79\cdot6^6=3,685,824$ core assignments. On incident abstract indices, every site pair must have at most two common core neighbors. This leaves 31,371. For each missing index, use the rungs of its first prism to name the caps. Try all six bijections to the rungs of its second prism. Any pair of sites attributed to the same cap must have an available common-neighbor slot. Exactly 11,713 core assignments admit cap choices for all three indices.
3. Fix a star of four fibers at point $a$. Two of its sites in different fibers have a known number of common core neighbors and common caps. Only the ordinary class $a$ can provide the remaining common neighbors of the pair. The resulting six $3\times3$ pair-marginal tables entirely specify the necessary pair counts among that star's nine four-site words. Fix a label on the first fiber. Exactly three words use it. For each other coordinate, its multiset on these three words is prescribed by the marginal with the first fiber. Sort the three words by one of those coordinates and enumerate all permutations of the other two coordinate multisets. Join the three slices using the other pair marginals. No two different exterior vertices may share three core neighbors. Also a word $w$ must satisfy $|w\cap N_{\rm core}(i)|+[i\in w]\le2$ for every core site $i$. Only the two cap alignments meeting this star affect its marginals. The six star checks together leave **15 labeled assignments** of core and caps.
4. For each survivor the Python checker independently enumerates each star's four-coordinate words, then all unordered triples at each first-coordinate value. Each of the six stars has a unique table. The checker verifies all 36 core degrees and all $\binom{36}{2}$ common-neighbor counts against the resulting 63 exterior words.

For a cap with word $w$, it remains to choose its ten exterior neighbors. For every core site $i$ their words must supply
$$
q_w(i)=2-[i\in w]-|w\cap N_{\rm core}(i)|.
$$
An exterior neighbor with word $v$ must share at most one core site with $w$. It cannot contain a site where $q_w=0$, and the analogous condition holds with $v,w$ exchanged. Among the available words containing a still-required site $i$ the checker tries every subset of exactly $q_w(i)$, subtracts its contributions and removes the remaining words containing $i$. This enumerates every possible neighborhood. Completing the 36 demands gives ten neighbors since each word has four sites and the demands sum to forty. In every one of the 15 assignments at least one cap has **no possible neighborhood**. Thus none extends to 99 vertices.

## At most one rectangle per equal bad-prism pair

In the [rectangle count](bad-prisms.md#rectangles-of-good-prism-mates), fix the charged equal-mark bad prisms $X,Y$. Suppose two different rectangles charged this pair. Let the two faces of $X$ be $A,E$ and the two faces of $Y$ be $B,F$, with $s=a+e=b+f$ in triangle-mark notation. For each rectangle its common good mate of $A,B$ determines the rectangle uniquely by [triangle-pair packing](triangle-pair-packing.md#rectangles-and-the-grid-count). Denote these mates by $C_1,C_2$. They come from different grids through $A$. In the same grid the other two parallel lines are prism mates, while $B$ is outside that grid, is anticomplete to $A$, and cannot be a prism mate of both those lines by one-domination. Thus $C_1,C_2$ are anticomplete. Each is anticomplete to $E,F$ by the rectangle construction.

Use the model triangle marks $v_{ij}=\sqrt3(1_{ij}-\mathbf1_6/3)$ of the [partial $K_6$ theorem](partial-k6.md). Their squared norm is four and two distinct marks have inner product $1$ or $-2$ according as their indices intersect or are disjoint. Assign $A,E,B,F$ the labels $24,35,25,34$, and $C_1,C_2$ the labels $03,13$. The given face and mate relations verify the Gram matrix on $a,e,b,c_1,c_2$; also $f=a+e-b$. Each rectangle supplies three more original lines, whose marks are $-a-c_i$, $-b-c_i$ and $a+b-s+c_i$. The first two close the grids at $A$ and $B$ and the third closes the grid of $E$ with the second. In the model their labels are respectively $15,14,02$ and $05,04,12$. These are all twelve edges other than $01,23,45$. The verified Gram matrix transports every pairwise inner product. Values $1$ and $-2$ identify disjoint actual graph triangles with zero and three cross edges respectively. This gives precisely the excluded 36-site configuration.

Consequently $R\le H\le B_0$, where $R$ counts the good rectangles, $H$ counts unordered equal-mark pairs of bad prisms and $B_0=P-6G$ counts bad prisms. Here $P$ is the total number of prisms and $G$ the total number of grids. Substituting in the [global path count](triangle-pair-packing.md#rectangles-and-the-grid-count) gives
$$
12(P-B_0)-5544\le3696-P+2H,\qquad
13P\le9240+14B_0,\qquad 84G\le9240+P.
$$
Hence $P\le851$ gives $G\le120$, and at $P=851$ at least 131 prisms are bad. At that endpoint the [norm-four count](bad-prisms.md) gives at least 550 vectors. These constraints apply together with the [pentagon isolation inequality](pentagon-isolation.md) $5N_5+16R\le32B_0$.

For the [norm-six and norm-eight count](triangle-pair-packing.md#consequences-for-the-short-lattice-shells) put $h=B_0-[3\nmid B_0]$. The new upper bound on equal-mark prism pairs is $d=h+\min(15G,\lfloor(3696-P+2h)/4\rfloor)$. The checker obtains uniform minima of **44,636** and **3,737,742** respectively from the earlier shell formulas. One can check both minima directly. The rougher $d\le924+5P/4-9G$ gives $y_3\ge70224-25P-36G$ and $y_4\ge4838526-588P-5004G$. At $(851,120)$ these expressions are 44,629 and 3,737,658. Lowering either integer parameter raises both past the stated minima, and at the corner the precise $d=906$ gives the claimed values.

The [triangle parity refinement](triangle-grid-parity.md) keeps the exact correction in the path inequality and improves the grid cap to 119, and the uniform shell bounds to \(y_3\ge44{,}672\), \(y_4\ge3{,}742{,}746\).

The independent [eight-fiber exclusion](cube-eight-fibers.md) bans a 24-site subarray of this 36-site cube and strengthens the general partner cap.
