# A cube of eight triangle fibers is impossible

**Theorem.** No strongly regular graph with parameters $(99,14,1,2)$ contains eight disjoint triangles $L_i,R_i$ for $i=1,2,3,4$ such that $L_i$ matches $R_j$ in a perfect matching whenever $i\ne j$, and there are no other edges between fibers. The quotient on the eight triangles would be the three-dimensional cube. The conclusion is only a forbidden 24-site subconfiguration.

**Partner consequences.** Two anticomplete triangles have at most **two arbitrary equal-prism partner pairs**, improving the previous cap of three in [triangle pair packing](triangle-pair-packing.md). In particular they have at most two common good prism mates, or at most one if they are faces of distinct equal-mark bad prisms. Also a nonzero oriented difference $a-b$ of marks of two anticomplete triangles has at most **three** ordered representations as a difference of triangle marks.

Here and below the marks of distinct graph triangles have product in $[-2,1]$, with $-2$ precisely for disjoint full prism mates and 1 precisely for disjoint anticomplete triangles. Their common squared norm is four. Suppose $a_i-b_i=d$ for distinct ordered representations of a difference from an anticomplete pair. Its norm is six, so the two triangles in each representation are indeed anticomplete. Since $(a_i,d)=3$ and $b_i=a_i-d$, we have $(a_i,b_j)=(a_i,a_j)-3$ and $(b_i,b_j)=(a_i,a_j)$. For $i\ne j$, the $a_i$ are distinct, and $a_i=b_j$ is impossible since it would imply $(a_i,a_j)=7$. Hence the triangle product bounds force $(a_i,a_j)=(b_i,b_j)=1$ and $(a_i,b_j)=-2$. Four representations give the forbidden cube with $L_i=a_i$, $R_i=b_i$. Conversely, at a fixed first representation $a-b$, every additional representation $f-e=a-b$ is exactly an equal-prism partner pair $(E,F)$ at the roots $(A,B)$. A common good mate contributes a pair by negating its mark to get the common sum of the two prisms; a specified bad partner is not among these good contributions.

This proof uses neither good prisms nor an assumption on graph symmetries or lattice ranks. As a logical check, the owner's earlier [36-site theorem](cube-fibers.md) forbids its own larger array and proves a rectangle multiplicity result. Its abstract indices are $K_6$ minus a perfect matching. Taking one missing pair as the two-element side and the remaining four indices as the other side leaves an intact $K_{2,4}$. Within these eight abstract edges, disjoint indices are precisely the cube adjacency above. Thus the present proof also rules out that particular larger array; the earlier proof is independent.

## Why the exterior contact system is small

The Gram pattern of the eight fibers implies $L_i-R_i=L_j-R_j$ for their **marks**: they have equal squared norms six and mutual product six. Thus $w_{L_i}-w_{R_i}$ is the same vector for all four $i$. For a site $x$ off the 24-site core, its scalar products with these differences give the same contact difference $\delta$ at all four pairs. Indeed the graph-mark Gram is $3I-A+J/9$, and equal-size marks cancel the $J$ term. Each triangle admits at most one contact from $x$. If $\delta$ has magnitude one, $x$ touches all four left fibers or all four right fibers (the two **ordinary four-contact** types). If $\delta=0$, $x$ touches both $L_i,R_i$ for the indices in some subset $J$ and neither for other indices. A point meeting both faces of a prism must meet matched rung endpoints, since an unmatched pair already has its two common neighbors inside the prism. Therefore $|J|\ge3$ would give a contacted core site with at least two neighbors among the other contacted sites, impossible since $N(x)$ induces a matching. This leaves six double-index types $|J|=2$, four single-index types and one empty type, thirteen in all.

* For each $J=\{i,j\}$ there are exactly three vertices. Each is the unique common neighbor (cap) on one rung of $L_i,R_j$ and one rung of $L_j,R_i$. The only freedom is a bijection between their two sets of three rungs: six choices. These choices are made **independently** for all six unordered index pairs before compatibility tests.
* For $J=\{i\}$ there are exactly nine vertices. The nine pairs in $L_i\times R_i$ are nonedges and have no common neighbor in the core. Hence each of the nine site-pairs requires exactly two exterior witnesses. The three double-index groups through $i$ contribute nine witnesses in total. After choosing the six cap bijections, the remaining single-index count at each specific site-pair is **two minus the cap contribution there**, including multiplicity two when it occurs.
* Each all-left or all-right ordinary type has nine sites: a fiber sends $3(14-5)=27$ edges out of the core, of which nine come from its single-index type and nine from the three double-index classes containing its index. Exactly **three** exterior sites are empty, since $99-24-6\cdot3-4\cdot9-2\cdot9=3$. The algorithm includes all three as distinct vertices, rather than assuming a nonempty contact at every vertex.

## Complete finite certificate

The [standalone checker](cube_eight_fibers.py) requires ordinary Python 3.10+ and NumPy 2.0+. There is no SAT/LP oracle, random search, or assumed symmetry of contact tables.

1. The cube has 8 nodes and 12 ternary matching maps. Gauge-normalizing a spanning tree to the identity leaves five $S_3$ maps. Enumerate all $6^5=7{,}776$, identify simultaneous conjugates, and obtain 1,393 assignments. Now permute the four opposite pairs and optionally swap left/right everywhere. These 48 changes act only by isomorphisms of the marked cube. Transport actual matchings, invert if orientation reverses, regauge on the fixed tree (verifying its restored identities), and take the least of all simultaneous conjugates. The checker verifies each resulting orbit as a set, not just a numerical division. There are 79 representatives.
2. For the concrete core adjacency $A$, set $B=12I+2J-A-A^2$. A literal candidate binary contact word $z$ cannot contain two sites with residual $B_{ab}=0$, or have $(zA+z)_a>2$ at a site $a$. Enumerate the six cap bijections above and all their products, discarding negative residuals. Every ordinary left word has four ternary coordinates, one site per fiber. Its pair marginals for any two left fibers equal their $3\times3$ matrix of entries of $B$ minus the unique cap triple touching both. The right side has the same rule. All marginals have row and column sums three, and the two ordinary sides can be enumerated separately at this stage.
3. Fix a value $o$ of the first coordinate of an ordinary side. The three words with this value have their multisets of other coordinates fixed by the first three pair marginals. Sort the second coordinate, try every permutation of the multisets at coordinates three and four, and discard a repeated word or any two words sharing three core sites. Join the three slices at their remaining three pair marginals, again rejecting a pair sharing a triple. The packed addition uses four-bit digits: at most nine words contribute in a cell, so no carry is possible. No invariance of either word set is imposed. Form both sides, the six cap triples, all single-index words with their exact multiplicities, and three empty words. Recheck directly that the resulting 75-by-24 binary matrix $Z$ obeys $Z^\top Z=B$ and that no two distinct rows have more than two common core neighbors. There are exactly **160** labeled contact systems up to the stated small-core normalization. The ten nonempty core indices and counts are: 17:4, 24:39, 37:2, 53:4, 65:4, 66:4, 70:2, 76:50, 77:42, 78:9. These are necessary contact systems, not partial graphs known to extend.
4. For an exterior site $i$ with a four-site word $w_i$, enumerate possible sets $S_i$ of exterior neighbors. They have size ten. Their core marginals are $2\mathbf1-w_i-w_iA$. If $j\in S_i$, words $w_i,w_j$ can share at most one site; any shared site must have positive demand for both rows. For two distinct sites in $S_i$, sharing two core sites already would make $i$ a third common neighbor. Finally the exterior neighbors with words disjoint from $w_i$ must be paired within $S_i$ to complete the matching in $N(i)$. A paired pair must have disjoint core words, as its members are adjacent with $i$ already a common neighbor. All row tests are necessary; the checker branches both ways on unresolved membership and tries all required final pairings.
5. **No incomplete domain is ever used to reject.** When a row is found to have more than $L$ choices, the checker discards that entire row *as a constraint*, including all pair comparisons involving it. Here $L=2{,}500$ for core 17 and $400$ elsewhere. The row's site still appears as a possible neighbor in every retained domain. This deliberate relaxation keeps only fully enumerated domains; a graph would necessarily satisfy them. A retained domain of size zero immediately rejects its contact system. Otherwise choose a row from each retained domain. For each pair of retained sites $i,j$, test membership reciprocity $e=[j\in S_i]=[i\in S_j]$ and the exact common-neighbor quota
$$
 |S_i\cap S_j|=2-e-|w_i\cap w_j|.
$$
The depth-first search branches on every remaining option of the smallest current domain, retaining all compatible options for each other site. Thus it cannot reject any genuine graph. All operations are integer. The 75-bit row masks use exact integer popcounts (split into 64-bit low and 11-bit high blocks in the vectorized pair test).

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python cube_eight_fibers.py`, or replay one normalized core with `--case 17`. The 160 contact systems split into 12 empty-domain rejections and 148 exhaustive pairwise-domain rejections. The checker asserts the full counts for every core, not only that no completed graph was printed.
