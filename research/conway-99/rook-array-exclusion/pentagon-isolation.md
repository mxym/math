# Double-cell isolation and the bad-prism cycle graph

Let \(B,R,N_5\) count actual bad prisms, good-prism rectangles, and unoriented Berge pentagons of good grid blocks as in [the rectangle budget](pentagon-rectangle-budget.md). By [the eleven-fiber theorem](partial-k6-11.md) all five distinct secondary prisms of any such pentagon are bad. Then the following unrestricted tradeoff holds:
\[
                  \boxed{\,5N_5+16R\le32B\,}.
\]
It implies \(N_5\le 32B/5\), and if \(R=2B\) there are no such pentagons. This strengthens a necessary incidence bound, not a rank or full existence decision.

**The cell matrix of a bad prism.** Write \(X=U\cup T\) for its two triangular faces. Rows are parallel-line blocks \(g=(U,A,B_1)\) through \(U\); columns are blocks \(h=(T,C,D)\) through \(T\). We write \(B_1\) for a triangle, distinct from the count \(B\). An entry records all good-prism edges between \(\{A,B_1\}\) and \(\{C,D\}\). It is always a matching. For example \(U\) is anticomplete to \(C,D\): their mark sum is \(-T\), and their inner products with \(U\) must sum to 2, hence are both 1. Similarly \(T\) is anticomplete to \(A,B_1\). A line in one pair cannot have two mates in the other pair by grid one-domination. Equivalently the marks would give dot 4 with the opposite grid's third line, forcing equality. Thus a cell has zero, one, or two recorded edges.

**Isolation.** Suppose that \(h=(T,C,C')\) is a double cell in row \(g=(U,A,B_1)\), with \(B_1C,AC'\) good. If another column \(h'=(T,D,D')\) in that row has a good edge to \(A\), name its endpoint \(D\). Then \(A,B_1,C,T,D\) in this cyclic order form a genuine Berge pentagon. They are five distinct lines: \(A,B_1\) are anticomplete to \(T\), while \(C,D\) are mates in different blocks through \(T\). Its blocks are \(g\), the unique block on \(B_1,C\), \(h\), \(h'\), and the unique block on \(D,A\). The middle two are distinct by assumption; neither of the cross-edge blocks can be \(h\) or \(h'\) since \(A,B_1\) are anticomplete to \(T\). Neither cross-edge block can be \(g\) because \(C,D\) are outside \(g\): they see \(T\) in a good prism, whereas \(U\) has a bad prism with \(T\) and the other \(g\)-lines are anticomplete to \(T\). Finally the two cross-edge blocks cannot coincide since that would require four distinct parallel lines in a block of size three. The secondary prism associated with edge \(C,T\) is \(C'\cup A\); this was good, a contradiction. Exchanging the names \(A,B_1\) covers an edge in the other row position. Interchanging \(U,T\) gives column isolation as well: **a double cell has no other nonempty cell in its row or column**.

Write \(d_X,s_X\) for the counts of double and single cells. The face bound \(g_U,g_T\le4\) for endpoints of a bad prism is in [bad-prisms.md](bad-prisms.md). The double cells use distinct rows and columns, hence
\[
 s_X\le(g_U-d_X)(g_T-d_X)\le(4-d_X)^2\le16-4d_X .
\]
In a row \(g\) with \(s\) single cells, let \(a,b\) be the numbers of these that use \(A,B_1\), respectively. The orientation \(U\) (edge-completing line) to \(T\) can receive at most \(ab\) pentagon charges there: the remaining two cycle vertices must attach respectively to the two row lines and lie in different \(T\)-blocks. A double row receives none by isolation. Since \(a+b=s\le4\), \(ab\le s\). Summing both orientations gives at most \(2s_X\) charges for \(X\).

**Double cells are good rectangles counted at their two bad diagonals.** A double \(g,h\) gives the actual rectangle \(A,B_1,C,C'\), with opposite block completions \(U,T\). Conversely for any good rectangle of four distinct lines, its two pairs of opposite edge blocks have as their completion lines the two actual diagonal prisms, which are bad by [the forbidden-array rectangle argument](bad-prisms.md#rectangles-of-good-prism-mates). For each such bad diagonal, the two opposite edge blocks give exactly one double cell and determine the rectangle. The two bad diagonals are distinct. Therefore \(\sum_Xd_X=2R\). Each pentagon supplies five distinct charges by the eleven-fiber result, so \(5N_5\le2\sum_X s_X\le32B-8\sum_Xd_X=32B-16R\).

**A second graph.** A single at \(X\), say \(B_1C\) good in \(g=(U,A,B_1)\), \(h=(T,C,C')\), has the opposite pair \(Y=A\cup C'\) a bad prism. Indeed \(A\) is anticomplete to \(T\), and \(A\) is anticomplete to \(C\) since \(C\) uses its three contacts to \(g\) matching \(B_1\). Thus all three contacts of \(A\) with the other grid lie on \(C'\), forming a matching. It is bad since the cell is single. Now \(g,h\) give a single at \(Y\) with opposite bad pair \(X\). Given the two bad prisms, the paired faces are uniquely identified by the good edges \(UA,TC'\); the alternative face pairing \(UC',TA\) is anticomplete. The two grids are the unique good-prism completions. Thus singles define a simple undirected graph \(\mathcal K\) on actual bad prisms, with degree \(s_X\). Endpoints \(X,Y\) have marks whose sum is the mark of the third line completing \(B_1,C\). This follows by adding the parallel line relations \(U+A+B_1=0\) and \(T+C+C'=0\) in mark notation.

For a good pentagon \(T_i\) in cyclic order, let \(U_i\) complete \(T_i,T_{i+1}\), and \(X_i=U_i\cup T_{i+3}\) (indices modulo five). It yields a cycle \(X_0,X_2,X_4,X_1,X_3\) in \(\mathcal K\). For example in the \(X_0\)-matrix the \(U_0\)-block on \(T_0,T_1\) and the \(T_3\)-block on \(T_2,T_3\) form a single with good crossing \(T_1T_2\); its opposite bad pair is \(T_0\cup U_2=X_2\). The edge mark sum is \(U_1\). Rotation gives all five links. This is an **induced** cycle in \(\mathcal K\). For the explicit labels \(T=(12,34,15,23,45)\), \(U=(56,26,46,16,36)\) from [the 13-fiber note](partial-k6.md), adjacent abstract indices \(i,i+1\) give only one disjoint-index cross pair between the two faces of \(X_i\) and those of \(X_{i+1}\) (the other three pairs are anticomplete); a single cell requires two good cross pairs, so \(X_i,X_{i+1}\) are not joined in \(\mathcal K\). This map on unoriented pentagons is injective: recover its five completion lines from the five edge mark sums (triangle marks identify triangles uniquely); they select exactly one face of each of the five pairwise disjoint bad prisms. The five remaining faces have their good prism edges in their original unoriented pentagon order, since the nonconsecutive pairs are anticomplete. Not every cycle in \(\mathcal K\) is asserted to come from a pentagon.
