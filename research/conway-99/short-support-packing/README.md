# Packing signed eigenvectors in a Conway graph

Let $N_m$ count the oriented integral $-4$ eigenvectors of squared norm $m$ in a putative strongly regular graph with parameters $(99,14,1,2)$. Write $P$ for the number of induced triangular prisms. The following bounds hold at every rank:
$$N_{14}\le990,\qquad 7N_{14}+4N_{16}\le55440,\qquad 14N_{14}+8N_{16}\le77616+24P.$$
Every norm-18 support induces a 4-regular bipartite graph on nine positive and nine negative vertices. At rank 34 these assertions force **at least 254,844 oriented norm-18 vectors**, increasing to **537,904** if $P=0$. A vector and its negative give the same unlabelled support since the bipartite graph is connected. Indeed each component would require at least five vertices of each sign, as four-by-four degree four violates the common-neighbor bound.

## The Fano configuration is determined by three vertices of one sign

Use the [short-vector classification](https://github.com/YesterdaysLemon/conway-99-research/tree/main/attempts/wave71-modular-theta-extension). Every nonzero integral $-4$ eigenvector has squared norm at least 14. At norms 14, 16 and 18 every entry is a signed unit or zero. The norm-14 support is the incidence graph of complements of lines in the Fano plane. Every two sites on one side have precisely two common neighbors, both in the opposite side. Therefore, from known members of a sign side we can adjoin both common neighbors of each pair to the opposite side. This uses only the ambient graph.

Any three sites on one side generate both full sides by this rule. To check it, represent both sides by the seven nonzero binary triples, with incidence $p\cdot q=1$ over $\mathbb F_2$. The checker starts with each of the 35 triples and finds all fourteen signed sites. Thus two oriented norm-14 supports sharing three positive sites coincide. There are $99\binom73 2^3=27720$ independent unordered triples with a common neighbor in the graph. The neighborhood of each vertex consists of seven mate pairs, and a triple cannot have two common neighbors. Each positive seven-set contains $7\binom43=28$ such triples by its seven negative vertices. No two of these oriented supports use the same positive triple, so $28N_{14}\le27720$.

## Rooting the norm-16 configurations

Fix a positive root $o$. Both at norm 14 and at norm 16 the support induces a 4-regular bipartite graph. Its four negative neighbors form a set $Q$ obtained by choosing one site from each of four mate pairs at $o$. For each pair $a,b\in Q$ let $z_{ab}$ denote their common neighbor other than $o$. The six labels are distinct because a nonneighbor of $o$ has only two common neighbors with $o$. Each label sees its prescribed pair and no other site of $Q$.

At norm 16 let $D$ be the set of pairs whose labels are positive. The seven other positive sites have a total of twelve incidences with $Q$, since every member of $Q$ sees three more positives. A positive can see at most two sites of $Q$. Thus either $|D|=5$ and the two other positives see one member of $Q$ each, or $|D|=6$ and the last positive misses $Q$. When one pair is missing, its two endpoints are exactly the sites that see the two singleton positives. At norm 14 all six labels are positive and exhaust the other positives.

To compare vectors $t,u$ with the same rooted $Q$, count matching nonzero signs $k$ and opposite signs $c$. Their inner product is $k-c$. A positive touching $Q$ cannot be negative in the other vector, by independence of its negative side. Each six-pair norm-16 vector therefore contributes at most one possible opposite sign, and a five-pair vector contributes none on its positive side.

* Norm 14 and norm 16 cannot share the rooted $Q$. They agree at ten or more sites in the five-pair case and eleven in the six-pair case, with at most zero and one opposite signs respectively. Their difference would have squared norm below fourteen.
* Two six-pair norm-16 vectors agree on at least eleven sites and have at most two opposite signs. Their difference would have squared norm at most fourteen. An opposite sign would give a coefficient of magnitude two, impossible at that norm, and with no opposite signs the norm is below fourteen. The same comparison excludes a six-pair vector and a five-pair vector, which agree on at least ten sites and have at most one opposite sign.
* Two five-pair vectors with the same omitted pair agree on ten or more sites without opposite signs. Distinct five-pair vectors must therefore omit different pairs. They agree on at least nine sites, so their difference has norm exactly fourteen and they agree on exactly nine sites. If the omitted pairs share an endpoint $a$, the first vector has both the second vector's omitted label and a singleton positive touching $a$, neither in the second vector. These are two positive sites of the difference, with the common neighbor $a$ outside the difference. This contradicts saturation of same-sign pairs in the Fano support.

Thus a rooted $Q$ allows at most two norm-16 vectors, with disjoint omitted pairs in the double case. It allows at most one norm-14 vector, and its presence excludes both norm-16 slots. Summing over the $99\cdot560$ rooted choices proves the second displayed inequality.

## Counting transitions in the same seed

A transition of $Q$ is an edge between $z_{ab}$ and $z_{ac}$ for three distinct sites $a,b,c\in Q$. Let $j$ count such edges. The three labels at any $a$ belong to its neighborhood, which induces a matching. Hence $j\le4$. All transitions must meet the omitted label of a five-pair vector. That label belongs to two endpoint neighborhoods and can meet at most two transitions. Two vectors with disjoint omitted pairs cannot both block a transition, since the two labels of a transition share an endpoint. Norm-14 and six-pair norm-16 vectors require $j=0$.

Consequently twice the number of rooted norm-14 vectors plus the number of rooted norm-16 vectors is at most $2-j/2$ for every $Q$. For $j=0$ this is the preceding packing lemma, for $j=1,2$ there is at most one five-pair vector, and for $j=3,4$ there is none. At each root there are 84 pairings of residual labels in the fourteen endpoint neighborhoods. Those using three distinct mate groups occur in eight seeds each. The other pairings are in bijection with prisms through the root. In detail, in the neighborhood of $a$ the twelve labels paired at $a$ yield six transitions. A pair $z_{ab},z_{ac}$ with $b,c$ mates closes the prism on the triangles $obc$ and $a z_{ab}z_{ac}$. Conversely the vertex matched to $o$ in a prism recovers $a$ and the labels. Thus $\sum j=8(99\cdot84-6P)$. Summing the seed inequality proves the third bound. ## The remaining norm-18 support is bipartite

The [norm-18 reduction](https://github.com/YesterdaysLemon/conway-99-research/tree/main/attempts/wave74-short-vector-closure) allows zero or one internal edge on each nine-site sign side. Suppose $a,b$ are endpoints of the internal positive edge. Each has five negative neighbors by its eigenvalue equation. Their two five-sets in nine sites meet in exactly one site $w$, since the adjacent $a,b$ have exactly one common neighbor. Their union is the whole negative side. The site $w$ has at least four positive neighbors. Choose a third one, $c$. Its at least four negative neighbors include $w$, so their intersections with the two five-sets have total size at least five. One intersection has size at least three, violating the common-neighbor limit for $c$ and the corresponding endpoint. This rules out the entire one-edge case at every rank.

## Numerical consequence at rank 34

Apply the [rank-34 marked theta inequality](../rank-34-short-support/README.md). At each $0\le P\le851$ use the lower bounds there for $y_2,y_3,y_4$, and maximize $2387N_{14}+407N_{16}$ subject to the packing inequalities and evenness. The worst bound is at $P=851$: $2387N_{14}+407N_{16}+43N_{18}\ge17603866$, whereas the first two terms sum to at most $2387\cdot990+407\cdot10522$. Parity gives $N_{18}\ge254844$. At $P=0$ the corresponding upper pair is $(990,7968)$, giving $N_{18}\ge537904$. These lower bounds leave existence open. Run `python3 verify.py` to replay the Fano closures and all integer bounds.

The [rank-34 exclusion](../rank-34-exclusion/README.md) combines the marked counts with a separate upper bound on norm-18 supports. The remaining ranks are 36, 38, 40 and 42.
