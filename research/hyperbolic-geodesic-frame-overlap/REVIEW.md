### Volume and geometry

Decompose the ambient Lorentz space as the Lorentzian span of the crossing plane and the positive Euclidean orthocomplement of dimension $D-2$. Off the plane each point has the unique coordinates in the proof: the transverse length is $\sinh r$ and the normalized time-oriented projection onto the Lorentzian span is $p$. The derivative with respect to $r$ has squared norm $-\sinh^2 r+\cosh^2 r=1$; the two plane directions scale by $\cosh r$ and the $D-3$ unit sphere directions by $\sinh r$. The resulting ordinary volume density is therefore $\cosh^2r\,\sinh^{D-3}r$, with both normal sides counted for $D=3$. The omitted plane has zero $D$-volume. For $q$ on either central geodesic, $-\langle x,q\rangle_L=\cosh r\cosh d_P(p,q)$; minimizing in $q$ gives the stated simultaneous planar-width test. The intersection is bounded for a fixed interior angle, since its planar radial bound stays finite, and has positive volume near the crossing. Endpoints of the radial integral do not affect strictness.

The three vertices of a nondegenerate crossing triangle span its totally geodesic plane. An extra line outside it has at most one point on that plane, so cannot intersect all three sides, whose complete geodesics have no common point. There is a nonconcurrent triple whenever a family is not concurrent. The anchor-collapse comparison used in [the planar result](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/168) requires an acute-angle cost strictly decreasing and strictly convex, not a planar-volume normalization. Those properties follow from the symmetric, strictly angular-log-convex planar cost by strictly positive mixing and then a positive power. The strict average gain and the distinctness of the collapsed directions consequently remain valid.

### Independent one-integral check of the kernel

The Stieltjes formula for $h_b$ has an integrable density at $z=0$. The substitution $z=b\sin^2 w$ gives $2[(b+x)\int_0^{\pi/2} (x+b\sin^2 w)^{-1}\,dw-\pi/2]/\pi$, equal to $\sqrt{1+b/x}-1$. This verifies its normalization, not just its sign.

Write $A=1+z$, $B=A^2-1$ and $y=1-t=\sin^2\theta$. Instead of the moving-endpoint argument, substitute $w=\cot u$ directly in the angular integral. It becomes four times the integral of $1/((A-1)w^2+A+1)$ between $-\tan(\theta/2)$ and $\cot(\theta/2)$. For $A>1$, set $k=\sqrt{(A-1)/(A+1)}$. The two primitive arctangents have positive arguments with product $k^2<1$; hence their sum is on the positive principal branch. Its tangent is $k(\cot(\theta/2)+\tan(\theta/2))/(1-k^2)=\sqrt{B/y}$. For the integral in the manuscript one thus has
$$
 H_z(t)=4\int_{\sqrt y}^{\infty}\frac{dv}{v^2+B}
       =2\int_0^{\infty}\frac{du}{(y+u+B)\sqrt{y+u}}.
$$
At $B=0$ this also gives $4/\sqrt y$. In the last integral every positive integrand is strictly increasing and strictly log-convex in $t$: the second log derivative is $(y+u+B)^{-2}+\tfrac12(y+u)^{-2}$. Integration and Hölder give strict log convexity of $H_z$ on the entire interval $[0,1)$. The same argument applies to the positive Stieltjes mixture, and then the Fermi mixture. Strictness holds before Hölder on a set of positive measure in each nonzero mixture. The derivative inequalities in the original manuscript independently give the same result. Positive powers preserve strict log convexity and thus imply ordinary strict convexity, including all $0<s<1$.

### Equality and prior scope

With $S=\sum u_i u_i^T$, the trace is $N$, and the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm counts the $N$ unit diagonal terms as well as all ordered off-diagonal squared inner products. Equality in the spectral mean-square bound requires all $D$ eigenvalues equal, so span and tightness are automatic. Strict increase and strict Jensen require respectively tightness and constant off-diagonal square. The resulting lines are distinct for the stated parameters; conversely any such real ETF realizes equality as geodesics through a point. For $N\le D$ no averaging is needed; every summand is minimal exactly at perpendicularity. For $N=D+1$ sign changes of representatives normalize the sole kernel vector of the scaled Gram projection. The resulting Gram has off-diagonal entries $-1/D$, uniquely fixing the configuration up to an orthogonal map, realizable by an ambient isometry. The draft does not assert the existence of ETFs for other arbitrary integers.

The frame rigidity is a classical consequence of the frame potential and Welch inequalities: [Benedetto–Fickus](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1021323312367) and especially [Ehler–Okoudjou, Prop. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.0140) (crediting Oktay) already prove exactly this conclusion for powers of the absolute inner product with exponent greater than two. The integral-geometric kernel and its nonlinear positive powers are not that potential. [Steinerberger, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.03805) asks a broad question in the hyperbolic *plane*; the present theorem in $D\ge3$ is a new formulation, not resolution of an explicit higher-dimensional conjecture of that author. The [spherical overlap paper in this repository](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/167) and the [hyperbolic plane optimizer](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/168) are genuine direct predecessors. The latter supplies the planar angular kernel and anchor-collapse theorem; these parts should not be counted again.
