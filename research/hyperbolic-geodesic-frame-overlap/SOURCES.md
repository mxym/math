# Scope of the question and closest prior work

- [S. Steinerberger, *Well-distributed great circles on S²*, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.03805#page=5) ([journal](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-018-9994-z)) investigates a spherical problem, treats a Euclidean planar analogue, and asks about the **hyperbolic plane**. The present higher-dimensional, ordinary-volume formulation is distinct. It is not a problem stated verbatim in that paper.
- [Earlier repository note, #168](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/168), gives the exact planar tube kernel, its strict angular log convexity, and the anchor-collapse comparison for general strictly decreasing, strictly convex acute-angle costs. These are invoked, not claimed again. [#167](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/167) concerns overlaps for spherical great-circle zones and a projective energy asymptotic, not the finite classification here.
- [L. R. Welch, *Lower bounds on the maximum cross correlation of signals*](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1974.1055219), and [J. J. Benedetto and M. Fickus, *Finite Normalized Tight Frames*](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1021323312367): the moment lower bound and the tightness equality are classical. [M. Ehler and K. A. Okoudjou, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.0140), crediting O. Oktay, already show that a finite $p$-frame potential for $p>2$ attains its sharp lower bound precisely at equiangular unit-norm tight frames when these exist. In that formula the diagonal contributes $N$; here the objective omits the diagonal. The proof for any strictly increasing, strictly convex function of squared off-diagonal inner products uses the same Welch bound plus Jensen. The new kernel check in this note is for the actual hyperbolic tube-intersection volume, including arbitrary positive powers of that volume. We do not discover new ETFs or claim uniqueness for each parameter where ETFs exist.
- [M. Fickus and D. G. Mixon, *Tables of the existence of equiangular tight frames*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00253) explain the existence issue that makes the second range conditional. [D. Bilyk, A. Glazyrin, R. Matzke, J. Park and O. Vlasiuk, *Optimal measures for p-frame energies on spheres*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00885) study inner-product power energies and general classes of projective potentials. These do not directly evaluate two-tube intersections.

## Bounded comparison

Primary literature and citation searches used combinations of hyperbolic tube/cylinder intersection, common radius, crossing geodesics, angular volume, arrangement energy, squared cosine, log convexity and projective/ETF optimization. The classical frame conclusions, the general role of convexity and the plane result were found; no source inspected gave the finite higher-dimensional tube theorem as stated. Database and keyword searches cannot certify novelty or rule out equivalent formulations.

The prior tube-specific repository scan used [main #166](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/af107620b6dbc381974d5f25b9f2dd923c9476d8) and 51 tag trees, covering 15,353 main blobs and 283 additional tag-only blobs and extracting recognized text, including 162 PDF occurrences and 160 archives. We checked the complete tree-path list and read the two direct geometric additions in [#167 and #168](https://github.com/mxym/math/compare/af107620b6dbc381974d5f25b9f2dd923c9476d8...556dc236d098e58fc3bf117c31452faaf079f7b2). [#169](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/169) adds nine unrelated farthest-point Riesz-mass files. Through this baseline, the 51 remote tag SHAs and the inventory of 50 releases with 175 uploaded attachments (identifiers, lengths and digests) matched the earlier scan, including 102 attachments already represented by repository files and 73 inspected separately. This is a bounded search of public, recognized text and extractable PDFs, not a reading of every binary format, unpublished work or all external literature.
