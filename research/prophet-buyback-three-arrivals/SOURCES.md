# Original question and prior comparison

* Farbod Ekbatani, Rad Niazadeh, Pranav Nuti and Jan Vondrák, *Prophet Inequalities with Cancellation Costs*, STOC 2024 ([conference DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3618260.3649786), [open conference manuscript](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/12758/files/Prophet-Inequalities-with-Cancellation-Costs.pdf), [open first long version, §§1.1,4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v1#S4.SS1)). These authors gave the dynamic program, the exact extremizing three-arrival candidate for every 0<f<1, proved the corresponding upper bound on the best possible online guarantee, and conjectured the matching guarantee when there are three variables. Appendix C of the long version gives the exact value for two arrivals. The existence of the candidate, its ratio, and the two-arrival comparison are prior, not contributions of this note.
* Same authors, *Optimal Prophet Inequalities with Buyback*, [EC 2026 program](https://ec26.sigecom.org/program/main-technical-program/), expanded manuscript [April 2025 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v2#S3.SS1) and [October 2026 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v3#S3.SS1). Section 2.4 gives the unrestricted optimal ratio as the solution of a functional differential equation for all positive fees and identifies its closed expression for fees at least 1/3; §3.1, Eq. (10), explicitly matches that part to the three-variable example. The unrestricted lower guarantee for all fees does not automatically establish a *higher*, fixed-horizon worst-case value when f<1/3. Section 2.1, Lemma 2.3, may introduce several independent arrivals to replace a single law; it does not preserve three arrivals. Their unrestricted algorithm is also order-agnostic; our finite-horizon calculation assumes the order and laws are known.
* For small positive fees and **unrestricted** horizon, ENNV [v3, §9](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00527v3#S9) already prove logarithmic loss up to constants. In particular their threshold algorithm (Theorem 9.5) gives the requisite upper bound on the loss with unit natural-log coefficient. Their lower example (Theorem 9.1) proves a bound on the loss with coefficient 1/(2 ln 2). We use their earlier indifference criterion (Lemma 3.2, Eq. (11)) and their method of measuring the latest positive arrival by knots (Eq. (12)); our contribution here is the particular quadratic knot sequence, its harmonic-sum calculation and the resulting two-sided remainder of order f, not a claim that their general hard-instance method is new.
* Distinct models include [Lookback Prophet Inequalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06805), in which a declined value may later be retrieved at reduced value, and [Multi Choice Min Prophet](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.55), which addresses cost minimization and multiple choices rather than a charge proportional to a discarded provisional choice.

**Scope of the search (19 November 2026).** We checked the original conference text, the two subsequent versions, the authors' publications, and recent citing/adjacent work under the combinations of prophet inequality, buyback, cancellation costs, three arrivals/variables, fixed/finite horizon, linear recourse, exact factor and the small-fee logarithmic constant. No inspected primary source established the fixed-horizon residual below 1/3 or the matching small-fee lower loss with unit natural-log coefficient.
