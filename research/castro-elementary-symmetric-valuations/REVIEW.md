# Independent adversarial audit: Castro et al., Conjecture 6.1

**Verdict.** Internal AI mathematical review, not external peer review or formal verification: **PASS on the stated degree-admissible domain**. I read the source PDF (the displayed formula on p. 9, not its flattened text alone), rederived the calculation, and separately checked it computationally. The result is the proposed exact two-adic order for a specific infinite family; it is **not** the general Cusick–Li–Stănică balancedness conjecture.

## Source and logical domain

The original is Castro, González, Medina, Negrón and Rubio, [2017 manuscript](https://ccom.uprrp.edu/~labemmy/Wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Raul-Oscar-paper.pdf), dated January 9, not the three-author covering-method article. Equation (1) defines an elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $k$ *in* $n$ variables; Eqs. (2), (3), (5) are respectively its exponential sum, layer decomposition and $2^n-2W$. In Conjecture 6.1 the exponent is $i\ge 2^{s+1}+s$, not $2s+1+s$, and $a\ge2$, $r=0,1$. The two branches are distinguished by $\ell=0$ or $\ell\ge1$ in the leading-bit expansion $m=2^\ell+q$. Taking $s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, $m$ positive odd and $0\le q<2^\ell$ makes explicit the evident integer/binary conventions; $s=0$ is covered even if one's convention makes naturals positive.

The context's $1\le k\le n$ must be **explicit**, since the printed paragraph does not repeat it. Here $n-k=(2^s-m)2^{a+i-s}+r$, so this is equivalent to $m\le2^s$ in the parameter range. For instance, if one chooses $a=i=2$, $r=s=0$, $m=3$ outside this domain, $k=44>n=12$: the empty sum is the zero polynomial, and the printed right-hand side would even be $1/2$ instead of $v_2(2^{12})=12$. The formula is not valid as a statement about extending $\sigma_{n,k}$ by zero to unrestricted large $k$. On the admissible domain the two exponents match the original after inserting $m=1$ in its first branch.

## Mathematical audit, independent of tests

* Put $P=2^p$, $e=P/2$, $T=N/e\in2\mathbb Z_{>0}$. In $R=\mathbb Z[x]/(x^e+1)$ for $p\ge2$, the leading and constant terms of $(1+x)^e$ cancel. At interior exponent $k$, $\binom{e-1}{k-1}$ is odd by Lucas. Hence $U=(1+x)^e/2$ has coefficient order $t(k)-1$, where $t(k)=\log_2(e)-v_2(k)$. Its sole odd coefficient is at $e/2$. Reduced wrap changes signs but not lower divisibility bounds.
* $t(k)$ is the log of the additive order of $k$ in $\mathbb Z/e$, including $t(0)=0$. Thus $t(k+j)\le\max(t(k),t(j))$. In squaring $U$, cross terms pick up a power of 2 giving order $t(k)+t(j)-1$, sufficient. Diagonal terms have order $2t(k)-2\ge t(2k)$, including $k=e/2$. Coefficient-order lower bounds $t(k)$ form a multiplicative subring; $U^2\equiv1\pmod2$ in $R$, so $U^T$ has odd constant and the required component orders for every *even* $T$.
* $B=1-x+\cdots-x^{e-1}$ obeys $(1+x)B=2$; cancel 2 in the torsion-free additive group of $R$ to deduce the stated formula for $(1+x)^{N-1}$. On reducing $x^kB$, a residue below $k$ is the wrapped term with a minus sign; $e$ is even and every selected residue is even. The signed sum is therefore $(-1)^k(2^f-2C)$, where $C$ counts selected residues strictly less than $k$. Blocks cut at this $k$ have size $2^{v_2(k)}$. If $t=t(k)\le f$, each accepted upper configuration contributes a multiple of $2^{f-t}$ below the cut: at most $t$ freely varying positions lie above or at the cut's scale. Both $2^f$ and $2C$ are divisible by $2^{f-t+1}$ (since $t\ge1$). If $t>f$, $U^T$ alone absorbs $2^{f+1}$. Therefore all nonconstant terms vanish modulo $2^{f+1}$; the constant has exact order $f$, proving $v_2(V)=T-1+f$ with no unproved cancellation. This also handles $f=0$, $t=f$ and $k=e/2$.
* In ordinary/negative half-period reduction, their sum doubles exactly the contribution in residue classes in the lower half modulo $2e$; no double counting arises. Dropping the highest required zero and all the $g-1$ newly uninfluential free high bits gives the same ordinary half-period sum as a shorter-period problem, **not** a sum multiplied by $2^{g-1}$. The next prescribed zero exists because bit 0 was fixed. In induction the shorter-period valuation exceeds the signed one by $(2^g-1)T-g\ge1$; at $g=1,T=2$ the difference is exactly one. The base $P=2$ gives $2^{N-2}$ even when $N=2$, with no negative ring required. This proves the whole free-bit-order gain.
* In the original weight write $D=2^{a+i-s}$, $L=2^s$, $A=2^a$, $n=LD-A+r$. Every admissible $j$ is uniquely $(u-1)D+(D-A)+v$, with $0\le v<A$ and the high bits of $u-1$ containing those of $m-1$. For $h=L-u$, the allowed $h$ are exactly the $s$-bit words disjoint from $m-1$. The reflected indices are $hD+r-v$; at $h=0$ only $v\le r$ survives. This is valid even for the empty $s=0$ word. If $t=hD-w$, $1\le w\le A-1-r$, both $w$ and $A-r-w$ are positive and $<A$, with $h,L-h>0$. Both summands of $t+(n-t)$ therefore have ones in each position $a,\ldots,a+i-s-1$, producing at least $i-s$ carries. This supplies the modulus without assuming any parity of $h$.
* At $t=hD$, telescoping the binomial ratio from row $N-1$ down to row $n$ gives $\prod_{j=1}^{A-r-1}(1-t/(N-j))$ in $\mathbb Q_2$. Each factor is 1 modulo $2^{i-s+1}$ since $v_2(N-j)=v_2(j)\le a-1$. At $r=1$, the adjacent binomial multiplier $(n-t)/(t+1)$ differs from $n$ by $-(n+1)t/(t+1)$ and the denominator is odd. Thus the anchor multiplier is $n+1$ with valuation exactly one ($a\ge2$). The largest anchor pair fits because $(L-1)D+r\le LD-A+r$.
* For $m=1$ the period is $D$ with no free bits and anchor sum order $2L-2$. For odd $m>1$, $1\le\ell\le s-1$, and the largest fixed zero is position $d+\ell$, $d=\log_2D$. Since $w_2(m-1)=w_2(m)-1$, the number of free bits below that fixed high zero is $\ell-w_2(m)+2$, not one more/less. The full period $2^{d+\ell+1}$ divides $N=LD$. The resulting anchor order is $V_0=2^{s-\ell}-w_2(m)+\ell\le2L-2$. Hence $V_0+r\le2L-1<i-s$ at the *inclusive* conjectural threshold. The discarded terms cannot change that order. Last, $V_0+r+1\le2L<n$, giving the displayed valuation of $S$. The $s=0$ extremal case simply has degree $n-r$.

## Prior work and limits

* The [2017 manuscript](https://ccom.uprrp.edu/~labemmy/Wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Raul-Oscar-paper.pdf) Thm. 3.1 already gives the entire $m=1,r=0$ branch for positive $s$ with a weaker gap, and Thm. 4.1 gives $s=3,m=3,r=0$ with a weaker gap. Thms. 4.2 and 4.5 give further overlapping lower bounds, not identical to the full formula. Its Conj. 4.4 concerns a different lower threshold and must not be asserted solved throughout. The [2014 project report](https://ccom.uprrp.edu/~labemmy/Wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Technical-Report-Oscar-y-Raul.pdf) and [student poster](https://ccom.uprrp.edu/~labemmy/Wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/AficheOscarRaul.pdf) are antecedents.
* Even bare nonbalancedness is a prior consequence over a substantial additional subrange. In [Su, Tang and Pott, Thm. 5(ii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.1418), put $t=a-1$, $l=2^i-1$, $d''=0$, $d'=2^{i-s}m-1$. The mask $(l-1)/2=2^{i-1}-1$ contains every smaller word. Thus $m>2^{s-1}$ implies the theorem's noncontainment and strict weight upper bound. The degree-weight condition holds since $i-s\ge2$. This does **not** give the proposed exact order. Do not claim that all individual nonbalancedness consequences are new.
* The [2015 boundary work](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12095-015-0124-y) is a genuine predecessor in exact valuations and recurrences; the five-author manuscript itself cites it and then states its Conj. 6.1. [The 2025 AMS abstract](https://meetings.ams.org/math/fall2025se/meetingapp.cgi/Paper/51079) discusses boundary cases but does not state this complete two-branch theorem. [The original CLS source](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608369) treats a much larger balancedness problem. Recent [certificate-complexity results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15678) concern a different invariant despite closely similar titles.
* [Tollisen–Lengyel 2004](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/INTEGERS/papers/e4/e4.pdf) includes the one-residue case $f=0$ and describes normalized vectors modulo 2; it is insufficient merely to sum $2^f$ odd normalized integers to infer exact order $f$. Their [circulant-matrix paper](https://sites.oxy.edu/lengyel/papers/Intermediate_GT_TL__ArsCombinatoria_revised.PDF) also studies the minimum order over components of a cyclic orbit. A minimum over shifts need not equal the order at the particular anchored cube. For example, at $N=P=16$ and allowed residues $0,2,4,6$, the sum in the unshifted row $15$ is 6476 (order 2), but the shift by 2 gives 12910 (order 1). No claim that the general ring method is original. Subject/title/author/method searches did not identify an earlier complete theorem; that is not an exclusivity or priority guarantee.

## Reproduction

The proof is typeset for this draft; the hypotheses, argument and two valuations are unchanged from the audited text. The independently rerun author script passed (768 exact cyclic cases, 181 original-row cases, 238 large modular cases). Fresh `python -B -O independent.py` passed 1,408 exact lower-half/shorter-period/signed ring cases and 56 degree-admissible Conj. 6.1 checks across 38 fully streamed Pascal rows, through $n=2,097,149$ and both $r$. The fresh code uses straight shift-and-add for the rings and successive rational binomial ratios with separate order/unit for **every** index in each original row, not the author's odd-factorial modular code nor the proof's deletion of non-anchors. Failures explicitly raise; no Python assertion is needed. These tests guard against implementation and sign errors, not substitutes for the argument.
