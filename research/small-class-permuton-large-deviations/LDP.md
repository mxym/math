# A permuton large-deviation principle for small permutation classes and regular word interpretations

**Theorem.** Fix a regular language $L$ over a finite letter alphabet and a pair of MSO formulas with two free position variables in finite ordered labelled words, giving two strict total orders on all positions of every accepted word. Sample a uniform word in $L_n$; the two orders specify its permutation, counting words with multiplicity if the output map is not injective. There exists a positive integer $P$ such that, for each residue $r$ mod $P$, either there are eventually no words, or there is an LDP at speed $n$ for its permuton on that whole residue, with a good rate function given by (5) below. For a length-preserving bijection of such a language onto an **infinite hereditary permutation class**, the LDP in fact holds on the entire sequence with a single rate function. In particular this holds for every small permutation class (upper growth rate strictly below $\kappa\simeq2.20557$). No claim is made for all proper permutation classes or all output domains of MSO transductions.

We work in the compact metric space $X$ of probability measures on the unit square, with $W_1$ for the sup-norm ground metric. Finite permutations may be represented by atoms of mass $1/n$ at their two normalized ranks or by uniform little squares; the distance is at most $1/n$. The space of permutons (both marginals Lebesgue) is closed and contains every limit point of the empirical measures. Logs are natural and $0\log0=0$.

## 1. An endpoint-and-flow path count

Use a single finite directed multigraph $H$; paths count edge multiplicities. Choose $P$ divisible by the periods of all cyclic strongly connected components, taking $P=1$ if there are none. With prescribed endpoints $u,v$ and length $m$ in a fixed residue mod $P$, refine paths by their SCC route, exact crossing edges, and the residues of lengths of internal paths in cyclic components. Acyclic SCCs are singletons without loops and have zero internal length. The entry and exit states are determined. Discard incompatible records: the crossings must link, the length residues must sum, including crossing edges, to the prescribed residue, and in each cyclic SCC the specified residue must be allowed by its endpoint cyclic classes. If an SCC has period $p\mid P$, a pair of vertices admits arbitrarily long walks at **every sufficiently large length** of each allowed residue modulo $P$, by the primitive Perron theorem on its cyclic classes. A compatible record without any cyclic component has only one fixed length and is irrelevant as $m\to\infty$. All other compatible records occur at every sufficiently large allowed $m$.

Let $x_e$ be the number of times an edge is taken divided by $m$. For a record $R$ with cyclic components, the possible limit set $D_R$ is all unit-total nonnegative circulations supported on their internal edges, including zero mass on an entire visited component. Thus $D_R$ is a compact polytope. On it define
$$h(x)=\sum_v x_v\log x_v-\sum_e x_e\log x_e,\qquad x_v=\sum_{e:\operatorname{tail}(e)=v}x_e. \tag{1}$$
This is the entropy per edge, continuous through boundary faces. The following local count bounds suffice. Any convergent sequence of path vectors on this record has limit in $D_R$, and its particular type has at most $\exp[m h(x)]$ paths (use (1) even for the slightly unbalanced finite vector). Conversely for every $x\in D_R$ and every open neighborhood of $x$, the exponential lower rate of the number of record paths with vectors in that neighborhood, along *all* admissible $m$, is at least $h(x)$.

For the first bound, crossing edges occur once; net imbalance at any vertex is at most $1/m$. Given the starting vertex, the ordered lists of successive outgoing edges at all vertices determine the entire path. Thus the count for a type is at most the product of multinomial coefficients for the outgoing lists, bounded by $\exp[m h(x)]$. There are at most $(m+1)^{|E(H)|}$ types. For the lower bound write the nonzero restriction of $x$ to a component as $\theta q$, where $q$ is a unit circulation. Approximate $q$ by positive full-support circulations by mixing it with a tiny part of a stationary edge flow of a full-support irreducible chain on that component. For positive $q$, the transitions with probabilities $q_e/q_v$ produce empirical flow converging to $q$, even from a prescribed entry vertex. On every sufficiently large compatible length $j$ the prescribed exit has probability bounded below, by periodic Perron convergence; hence a positive total probability is on paths near $q$ with that exit. For these paths the log probability divided by $j$ is close to $\sum_e q_e\log(q_e/q_v)=-h(q)$, so their count has lower exponent arbitrarily close to $h(q)$. For a zero-mass component fix any finite allowed entry-exit path of its required residue. For positive-mass components allocate lengths near $\theta m$ in the prescribed residues, absorbing bounded rounding and all fixed crossings/lengths in the last positive component; its remaining length automatically has the prescribed residue and is large. Multiply the independently chosen path counts, and send the full-support mixture and the tolerances to zero. Since (1) is homogeneous in the separate component flows, the limit exponent is $h(x)$. This proves the assertions without assuming that the maximizing flow is ergodic or has connected support.

## 2. A fixed graph recording both sides of a mark

The Büchi–Elgot–Trakhtenbrot theorem gives a common morphism into a finite monoid $M$ of the alphabet with two flags that tests each output order with its two positions flagged. Write $\eta(w)$ for the ordinary unflagged product, and $a^{[b]}$ for the product of a single letter with flag $b$. Fix a deterministic automaton for the input language with states $Q$ and start $q_0$. Construct the **one fixed graph**, independent of any later number of blocks, with state set $Q\times M\times M$ and an edge labelled $a$ from $(q,p,s)$ to $(q',p',s')$ exactly when
$$q'=\delta(q,a),\qquad p'=p\eta(a),\qquad s=\eta(a)s'. \tag{2}$$
Use the period multiple of this graph in §1. In a block with input automaton endpoints $u,v$ and total product $t$, impose the lifted endpoints $(u,1,t)$ and $(v,t,1)$. Each original word in this block satisfying these data has **exactly one** lifted path: $p$ is forced by forward multiplication from $1$ and $s$ by backwards multiplication from $1$. Conversely every such path reads one of these words. The fact that some edges allow several possible future values $s'$ does not introduce any weight. For every position the traversed edge gives its type $(p,a,s')$ with $p$ the product strictly before the position in its block and $s'$ the product strictly after it. If the position carries flag $g$, the exact product of its entire block is $p a^{[g]}s'$. All products are of original letters, not auxiliary labels.

## 3. Approximate the permuton by finitely many block data

Fix $K\ge1$. For total size $n\equiv r\pmod P$, give the first $K-1$ blocks each length $P\lfloor n/(KP)\rfloor$ and the last the remainder. For fixed $K$, all relative lengths tend to $1/K$, the first block lengths have residue $0$, and the last has residue $r$. (For $K=1$ this means just the last block.) Specify each block's product, endpoints in the input DFA, and one of the records of §1 for graph (2) between the imposed lifted endpoints. Match the underlying DFA states across cuts, with the total path starting at $q_0$ and ending in an accepting state. There are finitely many such skeletons at fixed $K,r$, independent of $n$. Retain only compatible cyclic records in all blocks. For large $n$ these suffice and every such skeleton is feasible. Conditional on the skeleton, path counts factor exactly over the blocks by (2) and determinism. Eventual nonemptiness on a residue already follows by taking $K=1$.

Given types $a,b$ in two distinct blocks $i,j$, the relative order of their positions in either interpreted order is now known exactly from the finite skeleton and types. Replace the two affected block products by their flagged products from §2 and concatenate all block products in their original order. This works also when flag 2 is encountered before flag 1. Write $I^{(l)}_{jb,ia}$ for the test that the point from $(j,b)$ precedes the one from $(i,a)$ in order $l$. Values on unachievable type pairs are irrelevant. For each block $i$ let $z_{ia}$ be its normalized frequency of type $a$, a linear projection of its empirical edge vector $x_i$. Define
$$v^{(l)}_{ia}=K^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i,b} z_{jb} I^{(l)}_{jb,ia},\qquad
\nu_K=K^{-1}\sum_{i,a} z_{ia}\,\delta_{(v^{(1)}_{ia},v^{(2)}_{ia})}. \tag{3}$$
This is a continuous function into $X$ of all the edge vectors at a fixed skeleton. If the actual block fractions replace $1/K$, its distance from the empirical permuton of the original word is at most $\max_i |\mathrm{block}_i|/n+1/n$: couple each position to its type-atom and note that only its rank contribution from its own block was omitted. Replacing the fractions back by $1/K$ costs $O_K(P/n)$ in the limit for fixed $K$, by bounding both the weighted coordinate errors and the leftover mass. Hence uniformly over accepted words this approximation has limsup error at most $1/K$, with no assumption of typical local behavior.

## 4. The exponential variational formula

For continuous $F:X\to\mathbb R$ put $Z_n(F)=\sum_{w\in L_n}\exp(nF(\mu_w))$, *not normalized*. For each eventually occupied residue and each $K$ put
$$B_{r,K}(F)=\max_{R_1,\ldots,R_K;\ x_i\in D_{R_i}}
\left(F(\nu_K)+K^{-1}\sum_i h(x_i)\right), \tag{4}$$
with the maximum over the compatible skeletons just specified. It is a finite union of compact feasible sets. For the block approximation $\nu_K$ in place of $\mu_w$, $n^{-1}\log Z_n(F)$ tends on the residue to (4): the upper bound follows from polynomially many type arrays, the multinomial bounds and compactness of possible optimizing vectors; the lower bound follows by choosing open neighborhoods of a maximizing array, the independent exact block counts of §1 and continuity. The fixed $K$ ensures that the subexponential factors remain subexponential. In particular no probability tilting or positive-probability assumption about the maximizing skeleton is made.

By uniform continuity of $F$ and (3), the limsup and liminf for the original $Z_n(F)$ both lie within $\omega_F(1/K+o_K(1))$ of (4), where one may for example use the finite-$n$ bound $2/K$ for each fixed $K$ and large $n$. Thus both agree as $K\to\infty$, and equal $B_r(F)=\lim_{K\to\infty}B_{r,K}(F)$. Write $b_r=B_r(0)$, the exponential word growth on this residue. The compact-space Laplace principle (Bryc's converse to Varadhan's lemma) gives the LDP with good rate
$$ I_r(\mu)=\sup_{F\in C(X)}\{F(\mu)-B_r(F)+b_r\}. \tag{5}$$
This dual formula uses only the finite-dimensional entropy maximizations (4) and the limit over block resolutions. The rate is $+\infty$ away from permutons, since all limiting measures have uniform marginals; equivalently one can state the LDP on the permuton space using the square embeddings.

A direct local entropy version of the same formula is available. Put $S_r(\mu)=\inf_{\epsilon>0}\limsup_{K\to\infty}\sup\{K^{-1}\sum_i h(x_i):W_1(\nu_K,\mu)<\epsilon\}$ with the supremum on the skeletons in (4), and $\sup\varnothing=-\infty$. This function is upper semicontinuous by nesting balls. The entropy scores are bounded between $0$ and $\log(\max(1,|E(H)|))$. Compactness gives $B_r(F)=\max_\mu(F(\mu)+S_r(\mu))$: for the upper bound take a converging subsequence of optimizing $\nu_K$; for the lower bound use successively smaller neighborhoods along subsequences approaching their suprema. Upper semicontinuity gives $I_r=b_r-S_r$ (for instance test increasingly steep negative continuous distance penalties away from a fixed $\mu$). No closed-form elementary evaluation of the rate is asserted.

## 5. Heredity removes all length residues

Suppose the word map bijects onto an infinite hereditary permutation class, length by length. Then every size occurs and $Z_n$ is the unweighted sum over distinct length-$n$ permutations in the class. For fixed $t\ge0$, delete the last $t$ positions in first-coordinate order of every length-$(n+t)$ permutation, standardize both ranks, and remain in the class. Each resulting permutation has at most $(n+t)^t$ preimages: choose the ordered tuple of distinct values at the discarded positions. Matched surviving ranks after normalization move by at most $2t/n$ in either coordinate; their old and new total masses differ by $t/(n+t)$, which can be coupled arbitrarily at cost at most that mass. Thus the two empirical measures are within $O((t+1)/n)$, also for the square convention. For continuous $F$,
$$Z_{n+t}(F)\le (n+t)^t\exp\!\left(t\Vert F\Vert_\infty+n\omega_F(O((t+1)/n))\right) Z_n(F).$$
For any two residues $r,s$ select $0\le t<P$ with $r+t\equiv s\pmod P$. Passing to their residue limits gives $B_s(F)\le B_r(F)$. Reverse their roles (a possibly different nonnegative $t$) for equality. This works for **every** continuous $F$, including $0$, so normalizing and applying (5) gives a single full-sequence LDP, not merely comparable exponential word counts.

For an infinite small permutation class use the fully proved forward free-position order interpretation and size-bijective regular canonical language of Markström and Räty, arXiv:2610.22218v2, Thms 36–37 and §6. For this application no primitive-component hypothesis and no inverse logical interpretation are necessary. Borga, Féray and Winkler, arXiv:2611.12104v1, Problem 6, ask for which permutation classes an LDP holds and to determine its rate when it does. This is a positive family with a block-entropy rate characterization, not a solution for arbitrary classes. At polynomial growth, exponential-speed probabilities with nonempty attainable neighborhoods have trivial entropy cost; the more substantial application is to exponential-growth small classes. For geometric classes a prior repository result already supplies an explicit profile variational principle. No strict global growth threshold for LDP existence follows.
