# Prior work and retrospective / 2026H2 comparison

* **What the question asks.** [Borga–Féray–Winkler, *Permutons*, §4.1 and Problem 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1) (9 November 2026) ask for LDP existence, the speed, and the rate for exponentially growing hereditary permutation classes. Their §4.1 distinguishes a fixed law sampled independently from uniform sampling on a class. The [permuton-sampling and Gibbs results of Borga–Das–Mukherjee–Winkler](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnad096) and the uniform unrestricted permutation LDP predate this note. Our result concerns exponential-cost neighborhoods under fixed-size **uniform class** sampling, not entropy under a given independent sample. [Budzinski, Dubach, Féray, Kammoun and Maïda, arXiv:2604.23751v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23751v1), Propositions 4 and 5, §§4 and 7, and Corollary 20, give explicit speed-$n$ laws for uniform 231/321 avoidance and hence the size-three single-pattern classes by symmetry. Those Catalan classes have growth 4, so the small-class corollary does not contain them. [Madras and Pehlivan](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v23i4p36) treat exponential one-entry location probabilities for monotone avoidance, another observable. No first LDP for pattern avoidance is claimed.
* **Program and earlier repository results.** The [geometrically griddable-class note, #222](../geometrically-griddable-permuton-ldp/README.md), already counts trace profiles and proves a speed-$n$ LDP for every hereditary subclass of a fixed geometric grid class; it resolves overlaps without charging exponential multiplicities. It is a positive antecedent, not new here. [#226](../permutation-class-limit-law-counterexamples/README.md), drawing on [Pantone–Vatter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30369), supplies a proper exponential-growth, convergent-shape hereditary example **without** a speed-$n$ LDP. [#238](../small-class-permuton-limit-shapes/README.md) gives the typical shape under the same kind of word interpretation by boundary buffers and a Dirichlet phase law; its estimates discard atypical words and do not give this rare-event rate. The present argument instead has a deterministic coarse error bound for *every* word, counts both forward and backward finite states without multiplicity, and equates all residue free energies by hereditary deletion. The hypotheses of #222 and of the present general encoding theorem are not synonyms.
* **What is imported for small classes.** [Vatter](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdr017) gives the small-class threshold and structural background. [Markström–Räty, arXiv:2610.22218v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22218v2), Theorems 36–37 and §6, give the forward free-variable order interpretation and canonical regular size-preserving bijection. This application does not need their sketched reverse interpretation or even the primitivity in their Theorem 62. Their Theorem 1 and [Falgas-Ravry et al., arXiv:2610.34328v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34328), address logical limits, counting and observable sentence probabilities, not permuton tail probabilities.
* **Standard techniques.** Finite-word MSO recognition is classical (Büchi–Elgot–Trakhtenbrot). Transition type counting long predates this argument: [Whittle (1955), eq. (8)](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/17/2/235/7026736) gives an exact transition-count law; [Csiszár, Cover and Choi (1987)](https://www-isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/transIT/0788csis.pdf) develop Markov conditional limit results via second-order empirical distributions. We give the elementary upper bound by ordering edges and the full-support approximation lower bound to retain prescribed endpoints, periods, reducible routes and zero-mass components. The compact-space Laplace-to-LDP implication is [Bryc, *Large Deviations by the Asymptotic Value Method*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-0564-4_25); it is not new here. A regular-language conditional growth or a scalar empirical-measure LDP alone need not fix an output permuton's two global ranks.
* **Current scope.** On 19 November 2026, checks covered historical and July–November 2026 results on permuton LDPs for uniform pattern classes, Mallows models, word interpretations and Markov path counts. The arXiv version histories give April v1 for Budzinski et al.; October v2 (19 October) for Markström–Räty, October v1 (25 October) for Falgas-Ravry et al.; and November v1 for the Borga–Féray–Winkler survey. The statements of the directly relevant results were checked, not inferred from their titles. No primary result giving the full all-small LDP or the general position-interpretation block rate was located in this bounded search. This is not a guarantee of priority.
