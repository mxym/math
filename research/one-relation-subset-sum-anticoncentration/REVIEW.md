# Independent review: one rational relation among Bernoulli weight types

**Verdict: PASS.** For $s$ distinct positive absolute magnitudes of arbitrary positive multiplicities, rational rank at least $s-1$ suffices for $K\rho^2\le1$ in the independent fair-bit subset-sum model. The assertion also multiplies across configurations whose rational spans form a direct sum. This is about the dimension of the rational relation space (at most one); it does not prove the general conjecture, even for three commensurable magnitudes. Frozen proof SHA-256: 1a604874c0f54aba4e5ceccad76c244ae24770656c9e4bc572b18ea630a46f0b. Review is independent and internal to the AI-assisted work, not external peer review, a priority determination or a proof-assistant verification.

## The two-dimensional constant

- In the box $[0,n]\times[0,m]$ with step $(\pm b,\pm a)$, reflection of a binomial count $x\mapsto n-x$ preserves its law and interchanging the axes preserves the problem. A coset here really is a translate of $\mathbb Z(b,a)$, *not* the saturated lattice on its real line. Its intersection with the box consists of consecutive integer multiples of that step. This holds without any gcd hypothesis. Counting a first point in each coset gives $K_2=(n+1)(m+1)- (n+1-b)_+(m+1-a)_+$. If the subtracted term vanishes, the two counts identify every coset and their binomial peaks multiply. Otherwise, $K_2=P+Q-ab$ with $P=a(n+1), Q=b(m+1)$. On each coset every $y$ is in a fixed residue mod $a$ and determines at most one $x$; likewise for $x$ mod $b$. Thus the bounds $c_nR_m(a)$ and $c_mR_n(b)$ are valid regardless of primitivity.
- The squared-peak bound $dR_j(d)^2\le1/2$ for $j\ge2d$ was proved in the two-magnitude note. At $d=3$ the strengthened bound $3/8$ follows from $3(22/64)^2<3/8$. Independently, at $d\ge4$ the elementary binomial bound $c_{2d}^2\le1/(3d+1)$ from the earlier review gives $\sqrt d\,c_{2d}<7/12$; adding $2\sqrt d/4^d\le1/64$ gives at most $115/192$, whose square is below $3/8$. This yields the same strengthened estimate without requiring the sharper $\pi$ bound in this step. The sharp binomial comparison $(j+1/2)c_j^2<2/\pi$ used elsewhere follows from the increasing even subsequence and Stirling limit in #95; equality in the limit is not needed.
- If $a\ge2$, $P\rho_2^2,Q\rho_2^2<1/2$ by the one-length residue bounds. If either multiplicity reaches its corresponding double step ($n\ge2b$ or $m\ge2a$), the corresponding one of those bounds tightens to $(3/4)(1/2)=3/8$, giving $K_2\rho_2^2<7/8$. Otherwise $P,Q\le2ab$, so $P+Q-ab\le(3/2)\max(P,Q)$ suffices. For $a=b=1$, sum/difference fibers have a translated $\mathrm{Bin}(n+m,1/2)$ law with full consecutive support, giving $K_2\rho_2^2\le3/4$.
- If $a=1<b$ and $b\le n\le2b-1$, the integer bound $K_2=n+bm+1\le b(m+2)$ gives $K_2\rho_2^2<2/3$ by #95. If $n\ge2b$ and $b\ge3$, split $K_2=(n+1/2)+(bm+1/2)$. The two contributions are bounded by $2/\pi$ and $(2/\pi)(3/8)$, respectively; $11/(4\pi)<8/9$ uses only $\pi>99/32$.
- The remaining step $(2,1)$ deserves care. Each fiber visits distinct indices of each binomial and only one parity of the first. Thus $\sum_\text{fiber} p_n(i)^2\le c_n/2$, using the parity mass $1/2$; also $\sum_\text{fiber}p_m(j)^2\le\binom{2m}m/4^m=c_{2m}$. With $U=n+1/2,\ V=2m+1/2$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\rho_2^2\le c_n c_{2m}/2<1/(3\sqrt{UV})$. The two endpoint estimates $\rho_2\le c_n$ and $\rho_2\le c_m/2$ hold as well. For $U/V\ge3$, $(U+V)c_n^2\le(4/3)Uc_n^2<8/9$. For $U/V\le1$, $(U+V)c_m^2/4\le (m+1/4)c_m^2<2/3$. For $1\le x=U/V\le3$ the Cauchy–Schwarz estimate is $(\sqrt x+1/\sqrt x)/3\le4/(3\sqrt3)<8/9$. This includes $n=2b$ and both ratio boundaries without a seam. All exhaustive cases give the stated $8/9$, not a claim of optimality.

## Higher-dimensional reduction

1. If the rational types span dimension $s-1$, the kernel in $\mathbb Z^s$ equals $\mathbb Zh$ with primitive $h$: scaling a rational generator to coprime integer coordinates gives this by Bézout. If $h_i=0$ its count coordinate is separately determined by the sum fiber, factors from support and peak, and contributes $(n_i+1)c_{n_i}^2\le3/4$. After deleting these inactive coordinates, at least two remain because a single nonzero magnitude cannot have a nontrivial one-coordinate relation. Full rank, a single magnitude, and the empty type family are covered by the product argument.
2. For the active coordinates, let $l_i=|h_i|$. If $n_i<l_i$ anywhere then every coset-meets-box intersection is a singleton. In general a nonempty intersection is a consecutive integer interval in the parameter $t$, so the count is precisely $\prod_i(n_i+1)-\prod_i(n_i+1-l_i)_+$ by counting points with no predecessor. In particular equality $n_i=l_i$ leaves precisely one overlap in that axis, not zero. For multiplicity $n_i=1$, either $l_i>1$ and all full fibers are singletons, or $l_i=1$, in which case $f_i=l_i/(n_i+1)=1/2$ and the binomial factor is $1/2$. No assumption of many copies of each type enters. The same argument applies with any number of such coordinates.
3. Project onto the two greatest $f_i$. The image of an individual global fiber is contained in **one coset of the projected integer step**, not merely one real line: all points of the fiber use the same integer parameter. Projection is injective on the fiber because the projected step is nonzero. Multiplying the largest possible projected coset mass by $\prod_{i\ge3}c_{n_i}$ is a valid upper bound even when the other coordinates truncate that projected coset. Conversely there is no need for the projection to preserve distinctness of *different* fibers.
4. The denominator $D=1-(1-f_1)(1-f_2)$ is positive. For sorted $f_i$, the terms $u_i=f_i\prod_{j<i}(1-f_j)$ form a decreasing nonnegative sequence (indeed strictly decreasing if all are positive). Thus $\sum_{i=1}^s u_i/D\le s/2$ without a claim that the $f_i$ are equal. The ratio of consecutive values of $(s/2)(3/4)^{s-2}$ is $(3/4)(s+1)/s$, greater than one at $s=2$, one at $s=3$, and less than one thereafter. The peak $9/8$ exactly explains the local requirement $8/9$; in particular the $s=3,4$ borderline closes. Rational-direct-sum factors are independent functions of disjoint bits, and the sum map on their supports is injective: equality of sums places a zero-sum relation across the direct-sum subspaces, forcing componentwise equality.

## Numerical check, known scope and limits

The independent integer program walks actual lattice cosets one step at a time, not via the invariant hash in the author's program. It tested 11,520 two-dimensional cases, all 1,512 dimension-three/four boxes with multiplicities in $\{1,2,3\}$ and step magnitudes in $\{1,2\}$, five explicit zero-coordinate, nonsaturated, and boundary cases, and the strengthened cyclic bound at moduli 3 through 500. Among the 1,512 cases precisely 608, 432, 136 and 16 have respectively one, two, three and four multiplicity-one coordinates. These finite checks do not prove the universal assertion.

[Jain–Sah–Sawhney, p. 2, the sentence immediately before Eq. (1.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.07726v2#page=2) and [Li v2, Conjecture 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01529v2), who credits Madiman with communicating the question, give the general conjecture. Their sufficiently separated repeated blocks have an injective count-to-sum map and distinct from one relation that generates actual collisions. The [earlier two-magnitude note, merged as #95](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/1a73fcf02f417ccbf6fc05a8a601426e0eb3c449/research/two-weight-subset-sum-anticoncentration) already gives $K\rho^2\le1$ in the two-type problem and proves the central-binomial and modular estimates used here. Distinguish that prior work from the stronger $8/9$ estimate for arbitrary integer steps, including nonprimitive projections, and its application to any number of types with at most one rational relation. No matching result for this latter scope was found in targeted searches, but no guarantee of priority follows. The full repository/release search through #94 is documented in the earlier audit; the entire increment to #95 was the eight files of that two-type note. No extension to unrestricted rank or other asymptotic regimes was reviewed.
