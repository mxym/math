# Source scope and attribution

1. [AICR c/0059, *The Binary Dual-Distance Bound Never Exceeds the Bound Over a Larger Field*](https://aicr.info/c/0059/), formal statement (the two displayed definitions of \(D_2,D_p\), followed by the universal comparison), and the separate note on the mismatch of ensembles. The page calls its statement “AI-written, not yet formalized”; this note refutes its explicitly stated universal quantification, not an unstated claim restricted to particular parameter families. Its preceding probe was consistent with the bound comparison at its tested inputs; the present proof uses a distinct support-multiplicity mechanism to obtain strict crossings. The projective certificate here is an additional mathematical variant, not the original definition.
2. **Lennart Braun, Geoffroy Couteau, Kelsey Melissaris, Mahshid Riahinia, and Elahe Sadeghi**, [*Fast Pseudorandom Correlation Functions from Sparse LPN*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1644), [ASIACRYPT 2025 proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-5122-4_14). The [fixed archive snapshot stamped 2026-04-28](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1644/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf) has the random ensembles as Definitions 3–4, the binary sum in §4.3 (pp. 24–26), its “Analysis Over a General Field” expectation on printed p. 26, and the sparse prime-field sum with the factor \((p-1)^w\) plus its convolution in §4.5 (pp. 30–32). The official ePrint record lists its latest revision as 2026-01-12; the fixed snapshot includes the correction to the binary table values from the proceedings, which this note does not use. The Section 2 proof counts only four and five independent rows of synthetic matrices, not a concrete setting from the paper.
3. Geoffroy Couteau, Peter Rindal and Srinivasan Raghuraman, [*Silver: Silent VOLE and Oblivious Transfer from Hardness of Decoding Structured LDPC Codes*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/1150), Part III, gives prior arbitrary-field random-code distance analysis. The no-singleton idea and counting by support are standard and are not claimed as new. The result here concerns two *specific vector-count first moments*, not bounds on all random-code ensembles.
4. Majid Khabbazian, [*Linear Distance for Fixed-Row-Weight Expand–Accumulate Codes over Arbitrary Fields*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1753), is a field-uniform distance theorem in the different expand–accumulate ensemble. The 2026 fixed-field analyses [2026/2315](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2315) and [2026/2712](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2712) likewise address expand–accumulate distance, not the two certificate cutoffs above. Hasler and Reisert, [*Pseudorandom Correlation Functions for Multiparty Beaver Triples from Sparse LPN*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2002), is cited in the c/0059 literature note for a binary-parameter calculation; no inference about their models is needed here.
5. The independent verifier uses the elementary additive-character transform of a Hamming sphere (the usual Hamming-scheme/Krawtchouk diagonalization); the other verifier uses the weight-shell recurrence already present in Braun et al. §4.5. Neither counting technique is presented as new.

Searches on 2026-11-27 included the c/0059 title/identifier, the ePrint identifier and the exact phrase “bound obtained through our analysis over F2” together with `counterexample`, `first moment`, `larger field`, `support multiplicity`, `union-bound certificate`, and 2026-11 arXiv/ePrint searches for random fixed-row-weight large-alphabet weight-enumerator comparison. The sources inspected did not reveal a proof or counterexample for these two prescribed cutoffs or for the fixed-\((k,n,m)\), \(p\to\infty\) comparison. This is a bounded search, not an absolute priority claim.
