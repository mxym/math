# Planar signed sums: independent internal review

**Verdict:** PASS for the explicit planar example and both finite-length claims in PROOF.md (reviewed initial hash f28fa98c2440649d11340270488adb5cfab49a2403d5ed0b6c060acd09f173e9). An attribution caveat below matters: the planar outer event realizes exactly the sign pattern of Dvořák–Klein's existing five-term **three-dimensional inner** example, after relabeling and negating some signs. The dimension and the direction of the inequality are the distinction.

**Definition and counts.** [Dvořák–Klein, §7 after Prop. 7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.10005#page=34) uses finite independent fair signs, deterministic Euclidean vectors and trace normalization, with the *closed outer* event. No equal-norm, isotropic-covariance, distinctness, sum-zero or genericity condition. The unnormalized covariance is ((7,−1),(−1,5)) of trace 12 and rank two. Write “expected squared norm is one,” i.e. E||X||²=1; “squared expected norm” is ambiguous. Enumeration by homogeneous coordinates gives the multiplicities 2:6, 10:20, 26:4, 34:2, mean 12 and no ties. Thus 6/32=3/16, proving O₂<7/32 and also O_d≤3/16 for d≥2 by embedding. This is not the value of the unrestricted O₂.

**Finite-length claims.** The squared norm minus its mean is an even linear combination of the pair characters. On either parity layer of a fixed four-coordinate set J, every one of these characters has mean zero by orthogonality (distinct from the empty and the four-set characters). For n=4 the eight antipodal classes divide into two such layers, so at least one class in each layer has nonnegative value. For n=5, given two putative nonnegative classes, choose representatives at mutual distance k≤2 by simultaneous-negation equivalence. If k=1 drop the differing coordinate; if k=2 drop an agreeing coordinate; if k=0 drop any coordinate. This produces a four-set J with equal parity on the representatives, hence on both classes. The opposite nonempty layer would have all values strictly negative and zero mean, impossible. The proof includes ties, degenerate forms, vanishing vectors and arbitrary dimension. Zero padding covers n<4. Thus n=5 has sharp lower bound 3/16 for every fixed ambient d≥2; the lower inequality holds for d=1 as well, not the asserted attainment in d=1. No statement for unbounded n follows from the finite lower bound. The code verifies all 8 one-class and 136 unordered two-class cases allowing repeats.

**Relation to prior examples.** [Dvořák–Klein, §7](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1428212) explicitly distinguished the planar question from one in arbitrary dimension, and supplied a 3-dimensional, 5-sign inner event of mass 3/16. For their signs δ let a=−δ₄, b=δ₅, h=−δ₁+δ₂+δ₃. On each of the 32 patterns their inner event is exactly (h+2b)²+(a−h+b)²≥12. Thus the Boolean excursion pattern is not new; a planar realization of the *outer* event is the relevant feature.

In fact a short perturbation of their numerical matrix gives a second, explicit derivation. The DK centered quadratic form with first coordinate squared s is

    D_s = s(δ₁+δ₂)² + (δ₁−δ₂+δ₃)²/9
          + (δ₁−δ₂−δ₃+δ₄+δ₅)²/25 − (2s+8/15).

DK used s=7/30. Its minimum absolute value on the sign cube is 1/25 (exact direct check). Changing s to 2/9 changes D_s by −δ₁δ₂/45, so none of its signs change. With W equal to (16,0), (−16,0), (−16,0), (−9,15), (−9,−15), dot products or expansion give

    ( ||Σδ_i W_i||² − 1380 ) /3600 = −D_(2/9).

Normalization of the W vectors by √1380 gives a second planar witness. This is an inference from the published DK construction; neither the planar matrix nor this rank-two completion is asserted in that paper. It strengthens the reason to avoid claiming a wholly new extremal family or priority.

**Other sources.** [Hollom–Portier, published text §6](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.70024) proves the scalar O₁=7/32; Theorems 6.2–6.4 identify the dimension-free infimum with an unrestricted matrix infimum A, show A is not attained, and prove 0.0446≤A≤3/16. Since DK already gives a finite-matrix witness of 3/16, A is strictly less than 3/16. None of those statements implies a witness in dimension two. [Yuan 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40305-012-0001-8) used a **negative rank-one matrix** on six signs yielding 7/32 (not below 7/32). [Chasapis–Liu–Tkocz, Thm. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02421) treats genuine rank-two Rademacher vectors, but bounds their tails by Gaussian tails and does not exhibit the desired extremal example. [Hollom et al., reverse Littlewood–Offord](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70539) concerns unit steps and inner balls, a different problem. Searches for O₂, planar, complex-coefficient, rank-two and centered quadratic Rademacher forms did not locate a published prior planar outer example below 7/32. This is not proof of priority.
