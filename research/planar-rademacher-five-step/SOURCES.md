## Primary texts and scope

* V. Dvořák and O. Klein, [Probability Mass of Rademacher Sums Beyond One Standard Deviation](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1428212), [author PDF, §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.10005#page=34), §7. Their Proposition 7.1 uses independent signs and the scalar trace normalization Σ||v_i||²=1; the ensuing text defines O_d with the non-strict upper excursion ≥1, T_d with ≤1. They explicitly ask readers whether examples with O₂<7/32 exist, distinguishing that question from the weaker existence for some dimension. They give T₂≤1/4 and T₃≤3/16, not the two-dimensional O₂ bound. The planar outer sign pattern here is exactly their three-dimensional inner pattern under a signed permutation (see PROOF.md).
* L. Hollom and J. Portier, [Tight Anti-Concentration of Rademacher Sums](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.70024), published August 2025, §6 (Theorems 6.2–6.4). Confirms O₁=7/32, identifies infimum of the matrix sign problem A = lim O_d = lim T_d, proves A is not attained and A≤3/16; this already disproves all-dimension equality O_d=7/32 and even gives A<3/16. The displayed proof via embedding a matrix in some dimension does not establish the planar inequality. Use the published text, not merely the shorter early arXiv preprint, which omits these high-dimensional conclusions.
* Y.-x. Yuan, [A Counter-Example to a Conjecture of Ben-Tal, Nemirovski and Roos](https://lsec.cc.ac.cn/pub/home/yyx/papers/2013-JORSC-1-155-157.pdf): the all-minus-ones order-six matrix (negative semidefinite rank one) yields exactly 7/32; it does not give a strictly smaller probability. No claim to have first disproved the Ben-Tal–Nemirovski–Roos one-quarter conjecture.

The search is specifically for a result under the planar/rank-two positive semidefinite **≥** inequality, not for ≤, the unrestricted matrix infimum, or the classical scalar Tomaszewski theorem. Exact priority not guaranteed.

* G. Chasapis, R. Liu, T. Tkocz, [Rademacher–Gaussian tail comparison for complex coefficients](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02421), Theorem 1, proves a *universal upper bound* by a multiple of the Gaussian tail with the same covariance, including rank-two coefficients. Theorem 2 uses matrix-weighted spherical increments; neither gives the planar example or 3/16 finite-five sharpness.
* Related but distinct: [Double-jump phase transition for the reverse Littlewood–Offord problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24202) assumes equal **unit** planar steps and bounds the probability **inside** a fixed small ball. [Noncentral sections of convex bodies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04200) bounds vector sums with continuous inputs. [Worst-case nonparametric bounds for Student's statistic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13226) concerns scalar sign sums.
