# Why phasewise intersection alone is wrong at polynomial growth

Consider the following regular language on $\{A,B,C,0,1\}$. A word has the form $X0^i1^j$ for $X\in\{A,B,C\}$ and $i,j\ge0$. At **even** total length allow $X=A,B$ when $i=0\pmod4$, and allow $X=C$ when $i=0,1\pmod4$. At **odd** total length allow just $X=A,B$ with $i=0\pmod4$. Both length parity and these conditions define the *given mother language* by a finite automaton; no parity predicate is put in the first-order logic.

At each length the number of available $i$ in a specified congruence class is $n/4+O(1)$, since $i+j=n-1$. Conditional on $X$ on any phase where it occurs, both $i,j$ tend to infinity in probability. Thus each FO sentence has one limiting truth bit on $X0^\omega1^\omega$ for each $X$; the bits are freely selectable by testing the first symbol. The limiting weights for $(A,B,C)$ are $(1/4,1/4,1/2)$ at even sizes and $(1/2,1/2,0)$ at odd sizes. Both phases separately admit the limit $1/2$, but no common FO sentence produces it on both: the odd phase would require exactly one of $A,B$, while the even probability would then be $1/4$ or $3/4$ depending on $C$. In fact equality of the two means is $x_A+x_B=2x_C$, so the full-sequence spectrum is precisely $\{0,1\}$. This illustrates the shared Boolean-vector condition in POLYNOMIAL.
