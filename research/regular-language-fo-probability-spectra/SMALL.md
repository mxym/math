# Application: determine the limiting probabilities throughout the small permutation classes

Let $\kappa$ be the positive real root of $1+2x^2-x^3=0$ (about 2.20557). A class is *small* in the sense of the sources below, with growth rate below $\kappa$. For any such class with exponential growth $r>1$, a uniformly chosen ungridded permutation of size $n$ has first-order TOTO limit-probability spectrum
$$\mathrm{Lim}(\mathcal F)=[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q(r).$$
Here every individual sentence has a limit, and every element of this countable set is the limit of some individual first-order sentence. Combined with the existing polynomial theorem, this determines the spectrum for every infinite small permutation class. No assertion about the boundary $r=\kappa$ or all proper classes is made.

**Proof and precise dependency.** Markström–Räty, [arXiv:2610.22218v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22218v2), Theorem 1(3),(5), give the full count asymptotic $|\mathcal F_n|\sim c n^d r^n$ and convergence for each first-order sentence. The needed inverse is their Theorem 1(2), §7.1, Theorem 65: a *length-preserving* bijection between the class and an alternative regular word language which is an isomorphism in first-order logic. The authors expressly label this two-way version a **sketched** construction: it uses canonical strong substitution intervals rather than left-greedy blocks. We invoke the stated theorem as such, not the one-way result for their other language in Theorem 1(1). Indeed their §7 gives an example showing why simply reversing the original one-way map fails for first-order logic. The length-preserving isomorphism makes the fixed-size uniform probabilities agree, in both directions. The ordinary pure-count special case of WORDS therefore gives exactly the asserted spectrum. We do not claim an independent new proof of their encoding. Separately, the *upper* field inclusion follows already from their rational enumeration of restrictions, Theorem 1(4), using the quotient-at-the-positive-pole argument; the new conclusion is reverse attainment, not convergence or the field upper bound.

Falgas-Ravry, Markström, Räty and Skerman, [arXiv:2610.34328](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34328), §1, describe their broad aim as determining the possible limiting probabilities for small classes. Their Theorem 1.2 gives the exact **finite** spectrum in polynomial growth (with $|\mathcal F_n|=b\binom n{k-1}+O(n^{k-2})$, it is $\{0,1/b,\ldots,1\}$); their Theorem 1.4 gives **density** in the unit interval in exponential growth. We sharpen the latter and leave the former unchanged. The 2025 Eurocomb extended abstract of Markström–Räty already treated convergence; no priority claim for that result.

**Why the earlier geometric field result does not exhaust this case.** For example the layered permutations (direct sums of decreasing blocks) have $2^{n-1}$ elements at size $n\ge1$, by compositions, and are therefore small. They are not contained in any fixed monotone grid, hence not in any fixed geometric grid. Indeed take arbitrarily many direct-sum copies of 21. Each interior horizontal or vertical boundary can split at most one of those distinct adjacent consecutive-rank pairs. Of the unsplit pairs, at most one belongs to any one cell: an increasing cell cannot contain such a pair, and a decreasing cell cannot contain two of them in direct-sum order. Any fixed finite grid is therefore insufficient. Thus the geometric scope and the small scope overlap without the latter being a corollary of geometric containment. Conversely one-row increasing-cell geometric grids can have arbitrarily large growth as noted in the earlier geometric result. Braunfeld–Kukla had already proved a logical limit law for layered permutations; here this example just separates the hypotheses. The included regular-language result is independent of the permutation coding theorem.
