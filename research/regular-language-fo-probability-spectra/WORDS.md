# Hidden-state transfer: exact limiting probabilities on exponentially growing regular languages

Use the vocabulary of a linear order on positions and one unary predicate per letter, **not** predicates for automaton states, edges or congruences of indices. Let $L$ be an infinite regular language on a fixed finite alphabet, sampled uniformly at each nonempty length. Take a trimmed deterministic partial finite automaton for $L$, and let $P$ be a common multiple of the periods of all its cyclic strongly connected components. For each residue $s$ with infinitely many nonempty lengths $Pj+s$, define $r_s$ to be the largest spectral radius of any component on an accepting route feasible at that residue, as made precise below. Assume that each such $r_s>1$. Then the set of numbers attained as **ordinary limits along all nonempty lengths** by individual first-order sentences is exactly
$$[0,1]\ \cap\ \bigcap_s\mathbb Q(r_s^P).$$
This does not assert that every sentence has an ordinary limit. In the useful special case $|L_n|\sim c n^d r^n$ for all $n$, $r>1$, the answer is $[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q(r)$. No particular DFA states need be first-order visible. All language-specific symbols, finite menus, numerical periods and repetition counts used in constructing a sentence are fixed *outside* the formula; the formula never counts unbounded distances modulo $P$.

## 1. Routes at a fixed residue and an upper bound

Every accepted word has just one path starting at the initial state. Fix a route by its successive components, connecting edges, and terminal state. These data fix all entry and exit states. Also fix all residues of the internal component lengths modulo $P$ (and require length zero for an acyclic singleton). Discard infeasible choices; remaining choices have some full realization with total length of the desired residue. The number with specified lengths is the product of the corresponding entries of the adjacency matrix powers, allowing multiplicities of differently labeled edges.

For a cyclic matrix $M$ of radius $r$ and internal entry/exit $x,y$ on an allowed residue, $(M^{t+Pj})_{xy}/r^{t+Pj}$ is bounded and tends to a positive constant. This is primitive Perron–Frobenius on the cyclic classes of $M^P$. If an endpoint residue occurs once, this asymptotic holds for all sufficiently large $j$. Hence for a route with cyclic components, maximal radius $R$ and $h$ occurrences of that maximal radius, the count at $Pj+s$ is asymptotic to a positive constant times $j^{h-1}(R^P)^j$. Indeed after fixing the normalized lengths of the subcritical components (their normalizations by $R$ are summable), one sums bounded factors over weak compositions of $j-O(1)$ into $h$ parts. Every part tends to infinity in probability under the uniform composition law; for $h=1$ this is immediate. Therefore their product converges in bounded mean to a positive constant. Summing subcritical lengths by dominated convergence is legitimate: the composition count is $O((1+j)^{h-1})$ uniformly. This shows also that each critical length tends to infinity in probability on each dominant route/residue choice. Finite routes with no cyclic component do not matter. Consequently residues are eventually empty or eventually occupied, and on an occupied residue only routes of highest $r_s$ and then highest $h$ matter with probability $1-o(1)$. Conditional on lengths and route data, each internal walk is an independent uniform bridge.

First-order word predicates are regular. On residue $s$, both the size enumerator and the predicate enumerator, indexed by $j$, are rational power series over $\mathbb Q$ (matrix powers, blocking or arithmetic-subsequence extraction). Write them $A_s,B_s$. As $z\uparrow r_s^{-P}$ the first diverges by the count asymptotic. For any convergent full-sequence probability $\alpha$, the weighted average $B_s(z)/A_s(z)$ tends to $\alpha$, since all finite initial weights vanish. Cancel the rational quotient and evaluate at $r_s^{-P}$ to obtain $\alpha\in\mathbb Q(r_s^P)$ on each such residue.

## 2. Identifying a component type using only the original letters

For a cyclic component $C$ let $F(C)$ be the factorial language of words labeling some internal path from some state therein. It is irreducible in the sense that any finite list of admissible words can be linked by connecting words on internal paths, and placed on a single closed walk. Group components of equal radius $R>1$ by equality of $F(C)$. The size-$n$ word counts in $F(C)$ have exponential rate $R$: right determinism means each word occurs on at most the fixed number of states' many paths, and the total number of length-$n$ walks has growth rate $R$ by irreducible Perron–Frobenius (row sums are comparable to $R^n$ using the positive Perron vector).

For each group $G$ and every cyclic component $D$ of radius $<R$, choose a word in $F(G)\setminus F(D)$ by the count inequality. Such a word also exists if $D$ has equal radius but different language. Indeed a strict factorial-language containment at equal entropy between *irreducible* such presentations is impossible. If $F(G)\subsetneq F(D)$, take $w\in F(D)\setminus F(G)$. From every state in $D$ there is positive probability to see $w$ in the next bounded number $m$ of steps of its Perron chain. Each individual edge $x\to y$ has probability $u_y/(R u_x)$ for a positive right Perron vector $u$; thus the probabilities of length-$n$ walks are bounded above and below by positive constants times $R^{-n}$. By the Markov property the probability of avoiding $w$ for $n$ steps is $O((1-\epsilon)^{\lfloor n/m\rfloor})$ uniformly in the initial state. This bounds the exponential rate of words avoiding $w$ strictly below $R$, a contradiction. An acyclic singleton reads no nonempty internal word and needs no special exclusion.

For each $C$ in $G$ choose a word of minimum nonzero *internal image size*: the image $S_C(w)$ consists of the possible endpoints after $w$ starting somewhere in $C$. Include all those words, and all exclusion words from the previous paragraph, as factors of the label $b_G$ of a closed walk in one component of $G$. If necessary choose a nonempty closed walk; all powers of $b_G$ are in $F(G)$, and by equality of factorial languages they are admissible in every component of $G$. Since the presentation is deterministic, the image cardinality cannot increase on adding a prefix or suffix to a factor. So $S_C(b_G)$ has minimum nonzero cardinality in **every** member $C$ of $G$.

There are finitely many SCCs in the trim graph, say $m$. To advertise a group use the fixed substring $b_G^{m+2}$; these fixed strings are letter predicates in FO, not variable powers. Any occurrence must have one whole copy of $b_G$ strictly internal to some component, because each connecting edge can spoil at most one copy and there are at most $m-1$ such edges. Consequently its advertised radius never exceeds the maximum radius on the witnessing path. On a route of radius $R=r_s$, consider its first component of radius $R$, of group $G$. No rank-$R$ advertisement can end before entering this component. Suppose one ends before leaving it. Some whole copy must be internal there because none can be internal to an earlier component of lower radius. In fact the *last* copy is fully internal there: after the first internal copy and before this completion there is no transition out of this component. The group advertised must be $G$, because its marker was designed to exclude all different equal-radius component languages. A full $G$ advertisement entirely within this component occurs before any fixed large horizon with probability approaching one as the horizon grows in its infinite Perron chain; its positive chance of occurrence within uniformly bounded blocks from every starting state and the Markov property suffice. Hence on dominant routes with probability $1-o(1)$ the globally highest advertised radius is $R$, and the earliest **completion** at that radius occurs in this first long component with its final $b_G$ entirely internal. Any ties can be broken by a fixed order; in this initial component no different equal-radius type can tie.

Here and below justify infinite-chain experiments on this component as follows. With internal length $j\to\infty$ on its allowed residue and with fixed endpoints, the uniform bridge agrees in the limit on each fixed prefix with the Perron chain. Perron edge products telescope, and the conditioning factor for a prescribed $H$-prefix is the ratio of the remaining $(j-H)$-step endpoint probability from its end to the original $j$-step endpoint probability. These two probabilities tend to the *same* positive number: the irreducible chain of period $p$ converges along compatible residues to $p$ times the stationary mass of that endpoint. Prefix length $H$ has the correct phase automatically. This convergence is uniform over the finitely many prefixes at fixed $H$. Conditional on route/lengths, the earlier history outside the component is independent of the internal bridge; only the bounded string of preceding letters relevant to a crossing advertisement matters. On any such history from this route the earliest advertisement completion can be used as a stopping time in an infinite continuation within this component (none of equal rank had ended earlier). Truncate the first-advertisement/experiment completion event at $H$ internal steps; then let $H$ grow. Finite histories relevant to the initial crossing, finitely many component entry/exit states, almost sure stopping and the uniform bridge comparison justify this double limit. Later components cannot raise the advertised radius above $R$, so there is no conditioning on a future event that would bias the experiment.

## 3. Equal-weight regeneration with hidden states

In a fixed component $C$ of group $G$, abbreviate $b=b_G$, $S=S_C(b)$. For any $w$ obtainable after $b$, the nonempty image $S_C(bw)$ must have at least $|S|$ elements by minimality; hence every state of $S$ reads $w$ internally and these transitions have distinct images. In particular all states in $S$ have exactly the same sets of possible next words of each length. Right determinism identifies their cardinalities with the entries of $M^n\mathbf1$. Consequently these states have equal right Perron coordinates: the limit of $(1-zR)(I-zM)^{-1}\mathbf1$ as $z\uparrow1/R$ is a *positive* multiple of the right Perron vector, by the simple Perron eigenvalue; other peripheral eigenvalues do not give poles at this point. No equality of normalized vectors across different components or aperiodicity is being presumed.

At the end of an internally read occurrence of $b$, the state belongs to $S$. For a specified next word $w$ such that $bw\in F(G)$ and $bw$ ends in $b$, it is readable from every state of $S$, and every terminal state belongs to $S$. Its conditional probability is therefore **exactly $R^{-|w|}$** by telescoping. Admissibility and the suffix test are independent of which component in the group is used. This statement includes $|w|<|b|$, so that the occurrences can overlap. The positive gaps in letters between consecutive completion times of $b$ define first-return strings: admissible $w$ with $bw$ ending in $b$ and with no such completion at an intermediate positive position of $w$. They occur again almost surely by irreducibility. Their distribution, on this countable alphabet, thus consists of the specified probabilities $R^{-|w|}$ regardless of the hidden state in $S$. Repeating at the successive completion times gives i.i.d. return strings by the strong Markov property. There must be at least two distinct possible strings: if there were only one, its probability $R^{-|w|}$ would be strictly less than one despite total mass one.

Two particular distinct return strings $A,B$ therefore suffice to simulate a uniform $q$-die for each fixed integer $q\ge2$. As in the [geometric note](../geometrically-griddable-fo-probabilities/EXACT.md), take the earliest of the $q$ return-alphabet markers $AAB B^i A B^{q+1-i}$, $0\le i<q$. Each has identical composition, and no proper suffix of any marker is a prefix of another: they start with the only $AA$ and end in $B$. Exchanging the earliest complete marker for another, up to a fixed horizon in number of complete return symbols, preserves path probability under the i.i.d. excursion law and cannot create an earlier occurrence crossing the replaced block. An occurrence eventually appears almost surely. Thus the die is uniform. This is FO in the **original letters**: a possible start must be a completion boundary of $b$; compare the following literal concatenated string with a fixed marker. Given the preceding suffix $b$, all intermediate $b$ completions are prescribed by that fixed string, hence correspond exactly to its return-symbol boundaries; prohibit earlier eligible starts after the chosen reset. There is no need to count how many excursions have elapsed. A one-sided input starts after an advertisement, so a preceding $b$ for the reset boundary is always present.

Let $R$ be one of the positive rates $r_s>1$. The number $R^P$ is the Perron root of a primitive integer matrix (a diagonal cyclic-class block in some $M_C^P$), so all its other algebraic conjugates have modulus strictly less than $R^P$. We can, on every selected group of this radius, simulate a common bias of the form $p=m/R^{PK}$. For each group pick a representative $C$ and any $x\in S$; fix $y$ with $y\stackrel{b}{\longrightarrow}x$. The number of paths from $x$ of length $PK$ ending with this indicated $b$ is at least $(M^{PK-|b|})_{xy}\ge c_C R^{PK}$ for all large $K$, by periodic Perron–Frobenius and the edge-phase compatibility forced by $y\stackrel b\longrightarrow x$. They give distinct words by determinism. Every such $w$ satisfies $bw\in F(G)$, since some occurrence of $b$ ends at $x$, and ends with $b$. Thus each is a valid probability-$R^{-PK}$ trial word in every presentation of this group. Choose $s_0>0$ smaller than $1/2$ and half the least $c_C$ for these finitely many groups, then take common large $K$ and $m=\lfloor s_0R^{PK}\rfloor$ strictly above one and above all other conjugate moduli of $(R^P)$ raised to $K$. For each group take any $m$ trial words as its menu. At a completion of $b$, test whether the next $PK$ letters belong to the menu, and reset at the first $b$ completion at or after that trial endpoint. A reset is a recurrent stopping time and returns to $S$ even on failure. Success has probability $p$ from any state in $S$; any fixed number of such trials gives i.i.d. bits. The die after the last reset has its uniform conditional law, independently of the bits. Trial endpoints, first subsequent completion, and a *fixed* number of resets are FO as specified by literal fixed gaps, bounded substrings, and absence of intermediate completions.

## 4. All common field values

For this bias, $\mathbb Q(p)=\mathbb Q(R^P)$, and $p$ is the sole conjugate in $[0,1]$. Indeed any nontrivial embedding of $\mathbb Q(R^P)$ fixing $(R^P)^K$ would send its generator to a different conjugate of equal modulus, impossible. Every other conjugate of $p$ has modulus $>1$ by the choice of $m$. Thus for every $\alpha\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb Q(R^P)$ the construction in the geometric note's EXACT §§4–5 applies. To recall it: start with $f_0\in\mathbb Q[t]$, $f_0(p)=\alpha$. If $h$ is the minimal polynomial of $p$, rational polynomials approximate the continuous extension of $(\alpha-f_0(t))/h(t)$ uniformly on the unit interval. Thus replace $f_0$ modulo $h$ by $f$ strictly between zero and one there. Its degree-elevated Bernstein coefficients converge uniformly to its sampled values, by the standard without/with replacement bound on falling factorials for each monomial. They are therefore rational numbers in $(0,1)$ at a sufficiently large common degree $N$. A fixed number $N$ of $p$-bits and one independent finite die realizes $f(p)=\alpha$ (Goyal–Sigman's fixed-budget Bernstein construction). Endpoints need no randomness.

Take $\alpha$ in the intersection in the theorem. For each group whose radius is some $r_s$, specify the foregoing letter-only experiment and an outcome of limiting conditional probability $\alpha$. A finite FO disjunction selects the globally highest advertised radius and earliest completion of that radius, breaking ties deterministically, and tests the corresponding experiment; prescribe rejection if no applicable group/experiment completes. On every occupied residue, §§1–3 show that with limiting probability one the initial marker and the experiment complete in the first critical component on a dominant route. The conditional limit is $\alpha$ irrespective of route and residue. There are finitely many residues and all other lengths are eventually empty, proving the lower inclusion.

Finally, if $|L_n|\sim c n^d r^n$ on the whole sequence then each occupied residue has $r_s=r$ (and in fact every residue is occupied). Moreover $r$ is strictly larger in modulus than every other algebraic conjugate. The reduced rational count generating function has a pole at $1/r$ of order $d+1$ by the real radial asymptotic, e.g. by weighting the assumed count asymptotic against $z^n$. All conjugates of $1/r$ are also poles of that same order by rationality. No pole has smaller modulus by the radius of convergence. On $z=t e^{i\theta}/r$ as $t\uparrow1$, $\theta\not\equiv0$, the contribution from $c n^d$ after division of counts by $r^n$ is bounded since $\sum n^d w^n$ is rational with sole pole at 1; the $o(n^d)$ errors give $o((1-t)^{-d-1})$ by absolute values. Hence there is no pole of order $d+1$ at a different point of modulus $1/r$. Since the cyclic components also show $r$ to be a positive algebraic integer, it is a Perron number here. The same embedding argument gives $\mathbb Q(r^P)=\mathbb Q(r)$. This proves the asserted special form.
