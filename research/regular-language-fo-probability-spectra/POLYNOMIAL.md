# The finite alternative and the mixed case

Together with WORDS this describes the ordinary first-order probability spectrum of **every infinite regular word language** at its nonempty lengths. It does not promise that every sentence converges on every language.

Use exactly the deterministic automaton, common period $P$, and route/residue patterns of WORDS §1. An occupied residue $s$ is called polynomial if $r_s=1$, exponential if $r_s>1$; an unoccupied residue is eventually empty and never appears in the criterion. Positive directed cycles ensure $r_s\ge1$ for every occupied residue. If there is no polynomial residue, use the field-intersection formula of WORDS. Otherwise the spectrum is the following computable finite set of rational numbers:
$$\{\alpha:\ \text{there is }x\in\{0,1\}^t\text{ with }Wx=\alpha\mathbf1\},$$
where the finite rational matrix $W$ has one row for each polynomial residue. Its columns are **shared across all of them**, as now defined.

No feasible accepting route of polynomial residue contains any component of radius $>1$: an accepting realization fixes all internal lengths modulo $P$, and increasing a length in such a component by multiples of $P$ already gives exponentially many words along that same residue. In an irreducible component of radius one, each state has precisely one outgoing internal edge, counting multiplicity. In fact each has at least one; if any has more, multiply $M\mathbf1\ge \mathbf1$ with at least one strict coordinate by a positive left Perron vector, contradicting $\rho(M)=1$. Thus each cyclic component on such a route is a simple directed cycle of length dividing $P$ (one-loop case allowed).

Fix a polynomial-residue route, including each internal length residue $t_i\in\{0,\ldots,P-1\}$ for its cyclic components. The acyclic components each have zero internal length. For an allowable residue, traversing $t_i$ edges of the cycle from the fixed entry ends at the fixed exit: the required distance is the unique residue modulo the cycle length. Thus the possible internal lengths are exactly $t_i+Pj_i$ for $j_i\ge0$, and each gives just one word from that entry. Collecting the fixed factors and taking, at each cycle, the $P$-letter word read during one extra $P$ steps from its entry, the words have the form
$$u_0v_1^{j_1}u_1\cdots v_h^{j_h}u_h.$$
The $u_i$ can be empty; each $v_i$ is nonempty. Distinct route/residue patterns or different exponent vectors cannot count the same accepted word, because the automaton is deterministic from its fixed starting state, and that path uniquely determines every connecting edge and internal length. At total length $Pj+s$, $\sum_i j_i=j+O(1)$ with a fixed integral offset. The number of exponent vectors is $\binom{j+O(1)+h-1}{h-1}$. Finite acyclic routes disappear. Therefore the dominant patterns (maximal $h\ge1$ for this $s$) all have exactly the same limiting weight, the reciprocal of their number $N_s$. Conditional on one pattern, all its $j_i$ tend to infinity in probability; for $h\ge2$ the count of compositions with any given part bounded is $O(j^{h-2})$, and $h=1$ is immediate.

Associate to each dominant pattern its product term $u_0v_1^\omega u_1\cdots v_h^\omega u_h$. Given a finite aperiodic monoid and an arbitrary assignment of the alphabet to it, evaluate $\omega$ as the eventually stable power. Every FO word sentence is recognized by such a monoid and an accepting subset, and conversely every accepting subset under such a morphism gives a first-order language (Schützenberger; McNaughton–Papert). For a fixed recognizing morphism the value of an actual word on the pattern equals the term value once each exponent is sufficiently large. Thus every FO sentence has a single limiting truth value $0$ or $1$ on each individual pattern, and its limit on each polynomial residue is the uniform weighted sum of those truth values.

Merge two terms from *any* polynomial residues iff they have the same value under every assignment to every finite aperiodic monoid. This equivalence is decidable by McCammond's word problem for $\omega$-terms in finite aperiodic semigroups, also proved by Almeida–Costa–Zeitoun. Omit empty word factors; the terms are nonempty because $h\ge1$. Using semigroups gives the same test as monoids: adjoin an identity if needed, preserving aperiodicity and all these nonempty term evaluations. Equal terms cannot be told apart in the limiting truth values of FO sentences. Unequal terms have a distinguishing assignment and two different elements of a finite aperiodic monoid; a singleton accepting set at one of them distinguishes them by a first-order sentence. As there are finitely many term classes, pairwise separating sentences, complements and finite conjunctions isolate every class, and unions realize every subset *simultaneously* on these residues.

Index the resulting $t$ equivalence classes by $a$. Let $W_{s,a}$ be the number of dominant route/residue patterns of residue $s$ assigned to class $a$, divided by $N_s$. Hence a single FO sentence produces a vector of limits $Wx$ on the polynomial residues for some Boolean vector $x$, and conversely all such vectors occur there. Any full-sequence limit must have these coordinates all the same, proving necessity of the proposed finite set. No assertion that separate phasewise spectra can simply be intersected is made.

For sufficiency take such an $x$ and its FO classifier. If there are exponential residues, combine two branches by the FO guard “some advertisement of radius $>1$ from WORDS §2 occurs.” In a polynomial residue it is impossible: a full marker copy lies internally in some component of the route, while every radius-one component excludes that group's marker. On exponential residues the guard holds with probability tending to one, by WORDS §§1–3 applied to each dominant route there (that argument did not require other residues to grow exponentially). The common value $\alpha$ of $Wx$ is rational, hence the exponential realization can use just the fixed-denominator uniform die of WORDS §3, taking the highest advertised rank then earliest completion as there. It has conditional limiting probability $\alpha$ in each such regime. Use this output when the guard holds and the term classifier otherwise. This gives the same ordinary limit over every occupied residue. If all are polynomial only the term classifier is needed. All graph and term computations defining $W$ are finite and effective.

The polynomial-growth decomposition, weak-composition weights, first-order/aperiodic correspondence, and $\omega$-term word algorithm are not claimed as new tools. This result records their joint phase-consistency criterion, and its combination with an original-letter positive-entropy guard.
