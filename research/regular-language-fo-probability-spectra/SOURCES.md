# Inputs, prior conclusions, and search scope

**The same relative density, before this note.** Jakub Kozik, [*Conditional Densities of Regular Languages*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.entcs.2005.06.023) ([institutional full paper](https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/bitstreams/17535109-6bfb-4865-b052-8b10945aa02d/download)), ENTCS 140 (2005), 67–79, uses exactly the fixed-length uniform-relative probability for a given pair of regular languages. His Example 1.2 already has an irrational golden-ratio density. Sections 3–7 decompose counts into arithmetic progressions with dominating poles, compare the separate ratios, and decide existence/positivity for a given pair. In particular the pole calculation already yields the present **upper field containment** on each exponential phase, which then intersects across phases. Our inverse problem ranges over all observable first-order predicates for one fixed language: we construct every admissible number, using no automaton-state predicates. We claim neither the underlying density notion nor its per-pair computation.

Berthé, Goulet-Ouellet and Perrin, [ICALP 2025](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.143), Theorem 3.6, Corollary 5.1 and §6, prove densities for aperiodic languages and field containment under their symbolic-dynamical measure assumptions, including Markov/sofic cases. This is a direct word-measure precedent; a uniform word conditioned to belong to an arbitrary regular language at each exact length need not be a stationary sample. [Lynch](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/7/ocr-ajc-v7-p145.pdf) already proves logical convergence results for independent letters under the hypotheses there. We do not claim convergence of every predicate in the arbitrary conditioning language.

**Standard ingredients and the role of hidden states.** [Parry's intrinsic Markov chains](https://doi.org/10.2307/1994009) are the source of the Perron transition rule. Resolving presentations, follower equivalence, synchronizing words and the Fischer cover are established in symbolic dynamics: see, for example, the presentation preliminaries of [*Distribution of integers with digit restrictions via Markov chains*](https://doi.org/10.1017/etds.2025.10256), with Fischer and Lind–Marcus references there. [Béal and Perrin's synchronized-automata chapter](https://www-igm.univ-mlv.fr/~perrin/Recherche/Publications/BookChapters/Synchronized.pdf) treats minimum-rank methods. Our automata may be periodic and need not synchronize to a *single* hidden state. WORDS proves explicitly which follower-word counts, Perron weights and marker-return probabilities agree, and ensures a visible repeated marker cannot be spuriously completed entirely in smaller-growth components. It then reuses the marker-die, algebraic bias and rational Bernstein construction of [the repository's geometric-grid proof](../geometrically-griddable-fo-probabilities/EXACT.md). For the underlying string-race, cross-bifix-free-code, unique-conjugate and Bernoulli-simulation precedents (Guibas–Odlyzko; Chee et al.; Akin et al.; Mossel–Peres; Goyal–Sigman), see its [detailed attributions](../geometrically-griddable-fo-probabilities/SOURCES.md). None of these tools is attributed anew to the present note.

**Polynomial phases.** Bounded cyclic-product structure in sparse regular languages is classical; see Szilard, Yu, Zhang and Shallit, [*Characterizing regular languages with polynomial densities*](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-55808-X_48). The FO/aperiodic equivalence is the Schützenberger–McNaughton–Papert theorem (also used in Markström–Räty's Lemma 7). [McCammond, *Normal forms for free aperiodic semigroups*](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~mccammon/papers/normalform.pdf), and [Almeida, Costa and Zeitoun's alternative proof](https://doi.org/10.1112/S1461157014000448) establish the decidable equality test on omega-terms used in POLYNOMIAL. The finite matrix compares the *same* limiting term theories across different residues. Rational weak-composition weights, term normal forms, and polynomial-growth structure are not claimed to be new.

**Permutation classification.** Markström and Räty, [arXiv:2610.22218v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22218v2) (19 October 2026), Theorem 1(2–5) and §7.1/Theorem 65, are indispensable: a length-preserving two-way first-order encoding by an alternative regular language, pure count asymptotics, rational restricted enumeration, and first-order convergence. Their two-way first-order encoding is expressly a **sketch**, distinct from their fully detailed one-way encoding. Their [2025 Eurocomb paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22218v2#bib.bib26) was an earlier convergence announcement. Falgas-Ravry, Markström, Räty and Skerman, [arXiv:2610.34328v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34328) (25 October), §1 and Theorems 1.2/1.4, set out the broad goal of determining all limiting probabilities and give the exact finite polynomial spectrum and dense exponential spectrum, respectively. We answer the remaining **attainment** part for the exponential case, not the already-known convergence question, and do not represent their stated broad aim as a numbered conjecture. Small is the strict growth threshold in those papers, not a synonym for geometrically griddable. [Braunfeld–Kukla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06307) and [Albert–Bouvel–Féray–Noy](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/11751/download) give earlier special-class convergence results; the latter also prove density for 231-avoiders. We make no new claim covering all proper classes or all 231-avoiders.

**Search boundary.** On 19 November 2026 retrospective and July–November searches covered regular conditional and relative densities, limiting values of FO or star-free languages, polynomial bounded languages, aperiodic omega-terms, irreducible sofic and Markov laws, and logical probabilities for small permutation classes. The current arXiv histories of the two direct October papers, including the word “sketched” in the former and the difference between exact polynomial spectrum and dense exponential set in the latter, were checked directly. [Maslic–Shallit, September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08571), solve Bucher's differently defined density problem: existence of an intermediate context-free language under inclusion modulo finite sets, not fixed-size conditional probabilities. The November [permuton survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1) concerns scaling structures; neither that observable nor the [repository's recent counterexample](../permutation-class-limit-law-counterexamples/README.md) for more general classes is this positive spectral classification. We did not locate a primary theorem already giving the full original-letter spectrum across regular languages or the complete exponential small-class field interval. This is a bounded search, not a priority determination.
