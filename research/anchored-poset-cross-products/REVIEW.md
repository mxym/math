Brightwell–Felsner–Trotter, 1995, p. 338 closes §3 by explicitly asking for the displayed all-index inequality anchored at (1,1). It is not their full adjacent-cell conjecture. The two statements are distinct; this review is internal and does not certify external priority.

If z is not minimal in A the filter inequality is automatic; otherwise the first-rank counts are e(A−z), e(H−z). At j=1 both rank-one constraints reduce to Fishburn after removing z. When E⊆D, any b minimal in D and in E has e(D−b)/e(D) ≤ e(E−b)/e(E) by applying the extension-count inequality to E,D−b, whose intersection is E−b and union D. All other b have zero D-probability. This verifies the exact marginal direction.

For the induction on the second rank, first elements b outside A are minimal in the union and leave the intersection unchanged. For common b≠z, q_b>0 because b is minimal in B. The coupling consumes exactly p_b on each common diagonal and assigns each residual q_b−p_b to w outside H, with total required mass equal to its p-mass. When b is one of the original first elements of B, w=b or w outside B is minimal in A−z and has no predecessor z; similarly it has no predecessor in B. Removing w and b therefore leaves filters containing z. Their exact intersection/union are H−b and Y−w, regardless of whether w=b. The positive factors p_w and q_b are the ratios of the corresponding first-rank counts; multiplying by π/(p_w q_b) uses every original B term once and each Y term at most once. The latter minima are in A, disjoint from the previously charged ones outside A. This completes the inductive inequality including empty and zero-count cases.

Finally the original positive-gap counts are unchanged by imposing the specified mark order; impossible order gives all counts zero. Cutting at y gives the up-set lattice of elements incomparable to y, and the fixed earlier/later sets used for each side are appropriate ideals/filters. The paired inequalities put f_j and g_1 on A∪B, and f_1 and g_i on A∩B, so Ahlswede–Daykin gives exactly F(1,1)F(i,j)≤F(1,j)F(i,1). No signed-gap assertion or arbitrary adjacent rectangle is needed.
