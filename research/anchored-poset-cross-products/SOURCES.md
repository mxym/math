# Sources and boundaries

1. **The original target.** G. Brightwell, S. Felsner and W. T. Trotter, [*Balancing pairs and the cross product conjecture*](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/97.pdf), *Order* 12 (1995), 327–349, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01110378). Their full Conjecture 3.1 (p. 335) compares neighboring entries with an arbitrary positive lower-left index. Theorem 3.2 proves that comparison at (1,1). On **p. 338, the last display in §3**, they suggest the distinct all-index rectangle anchored at (1,1). That display is the theorem of PROOF.md. We retain their cut at the middle mark, their filter-lattice indexing, and their four-functions summation. Their proof establishes the needed single-side comparison at second rank 2; our induction treats arbitrary second rank.
2. **Classical ingredients.** P. C. Fishburn, *A correlational inequality for linear extensions of a poset*, *Order* 1 (1984), 127–137. Its factorial-normalized filter inequality implies the unnormalized comparison used here; see BFT Thm 3.4 or [Chan–Pak, Thm 3.1](https://msp.org/pjm/2023/323-2/pjm-v323-n2-p01-p.pdf). R. Ahlswede and D. E. Daykin, [*An inequality for the weights of two families of sets, their unions and intersections*](https://math.bme.hu/~balint/oktatas/perkolacio/percolation_papers/ahlswede_daykin.pdf), 1978, give the four-functions theorem. The elementary monotonicity of endpoint probabilities is also prior; the all-rank filter comparison is not claimed to be a new proof of Fishburn's inequality.
3. **Positive-gap literature.** S. H. Chan, I. Pak and G. Panova proved the full positive-gap conjecture, including positive rectangles, [at width two](https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/8679), *Trans. AMS* 375 (2022), Thms 1.4, 1.6. Their [*Canadian J. Math.* paper](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X24000087) 77 (2025; online 2024), Thm 1.2, gives bounds at arbitrary positive adjacent indices for unrestricted width and an exact zero-condition case. The strong point here is constant one for all **anchored** rectangles at all widths, not the comparison for arbitrary adjacent cells. See also [Chan–Pak's survey](https://ems.press/journals/emss/articles/14298658), §9.7. The [multivariate P-partition cross-product theorem](https://msp.org/pjm/2023/323-2/pjm-v323-n2-p01-p.pdf) concerns order-preserving maps, not the present fixed-gap counts of bijections. Brightwell–Trotter's [2002 correlation paper](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/117.pdf) treats Fishburn, XYZ and other correlation statements.
4. **Recent related work.** J. T. Virtanen's October 2026 [arXiv:2610.25227v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25227v1), Thm 1.2, proves an inclusion coupling of prefixes at consecutive ranks of **one** mark, and a rank-count inequality conjectured by Chan and Pak. It already uses monotonic endpoint probabilities and equal total masses in its coupling. Its filter/ideal coupling is a methodological precedent. Here the distributions in the elementary coupling are first-item laws of two nested filters, and the objective is the three-mark BFT rectangle. We do not claim the one-mark inequality as a consequence in either direction. Chan–Pak's [arXiv:2607.06275v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06275) studies equality in correlation inequalities including Fishburn and four-functions; no such equality classification is asserted here.
5. **Search and repository.** Checked the original §3, these comparisons and their references, plus searches through November 19, 2026 for the exact anchored formula, its endpoints, arbitrary-rank filter inequalities, the classical author names, and CPC developments. No earlier proof in this scope was located; this is not an exhaustive bibliography or a priority guarantee. A repository-content check through [#157](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/157), including the main and tagged sources, formalizations, release manifests, searchable manuscript text, and retrievable public release content, found no earlier copy of the anchored result. The increments through [#158](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/158) and [#159](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/159) concern binary deletion and insertion entropy, not poset ranks. The nearby [#156](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/156) concerns **other** auxiliary numerical assertions at width two; the anchored proof uses neither them nor their counterexamples. [#153](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/153) concerns majority-cycle spectra. Two older draft-release files previously identified in the inventory and unindexed/image-only material were outside the retrieval check.
