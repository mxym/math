# Independent internal audit: real symmetric q-permanent at the right endpoint

18 November 2026. A separate AI-assisted pass audited the qualitative argument and all constants in the finite construction. The two source manuscripts reviewed were subsequently typeset as [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) and [FINITE.md](FINITE.md). No fatal mathematical error was found. This is internal review, not external peer review, proof-assistant certification, or a world-priority determination.

## Check of the qualitative proof

1. For fixed ordered row pair i<j, subtract twice its inverted-image contribution from per(A). Group the remaining signed terms by the unordered pair k<l of image indices to get a 2-by-2 determinant times the complementary cross permanent. This is a sum of inner products of corresponding row wedges and complementary row polynomials. Summing over row pairs yields binom(n,2)per(A)-2P'(1)=||F||² with no extra factor two. The Fischer cross-permanent identity follows by expanding products and counting permutations preserving the coordinate counts; the factorial for exponent vector a is the product of its coordinate factorials. Distinct permutations may have negative scalar weights and nothing here assumes otherwise. The table ordering is fixed throughout.
2. Complex spherical integration of a homogeneous polynomial of degree m gives squared Fischer norm times (d-1)!/(m+d-1)!. The degree discrepancy between n-2 and n gives denominator (n+d-1)(n+d-2) when recovering ||F||²/per(A) from the tilted mean. The complex variable z is an integration argument of polynomials whose coefficients come from entirely real input rows. The identity contracting with z wedge y is *bilinear*, not the standard Hermitian pairing, but its modulus is bounded by the Hermitian norms by Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the conjugated first argument. Also ||z wedge y||²=1-|<z,y>|²≤1. Consequently a large modulus gives a lower bound as required, not an upper bound. Conjugating z and y in the second tube simply conjugates the ratios because the rows are real.
3. Integrating the phases in the correct order gives the mean log max of the two mixture terms; the second may first be conjugated, after which an auxiliary circle integral gives affine terms in t (not in its conjugate). The exact unmodified complex-Cauchy angular/radial average is log hypot(|a|,|b|). Truncating by setting oversized radii to zero only lowers the log-plus mean; independent positive pinning makes the inequality strict when b!=0 and a!=0. If a=0, the pinned term gives minus infinity. The simultaneous-zero case uses log 0=-infinity and can be ignored at a null set of auxiliary phases when testing equality at a finite maximum. The Jensen norm average is exactly 1. Equality requires u2=w2=0 and then u1*w1=0; the two projective points stated in the note are exactly the maximizers. Logarithms are bounded above; clipping from below or applying Tonelli to an upper constant minus the integrand makes the extended-real manipulations legitimate.
4. Marginal density of Re T is 1/(2(1+x²)^(3/2)). For two iid such variables the half-mean absolute difference is π/4. Its change under a specified coupling is no greater than the one-variable mean absolute displacement (triangle inequality for two iid copies). The truncation and pinning loss is at most 2/100+π/200. Ordering is not applied to the Gram matrix *after* the fact: it defines its displayed order. Repeated pin values present no problem because the finite sorted sum is the sum of all unordered absolute differences, including zeros from ties.
5. Compactness of the row support and continuity of the clipped log imply the requisite limsup for every sequence z_n→z, including when the limiting potential is minus infinity. Compactness of the closed complement of **the union containing both** peak circles gives a uniform strictly negative deficit there. Near only one peak the denominator of the tilted measure already has a positive-volume lower bound, uniform in the rows. The two resulting amplitude bounds give concentration after exponentiation. Uniform lower bounds for ratios on both peak neighborhoods follow from nonvanishing denominator at both centers and bounded row norms. There is no need for an interchange of an unbounded expectation or derivative with a limit; restricting the nonnegative integrand to a neighborhood suffices. Take every g<G separately and then g upwards to G. The strictly positive margin of G² over 1/2 yields an actual negative derivative at all sufficiently large indices, not merely a negative limit inferable from a weak inequality.
6. Nonzero centered rows give per(A)>0 by the polynomial norm. Adding a small positive identity gives full real positive definiteness; the negative derivative persists by continuity at the selected fixed order, and rules out a diagonal matrix. Continuity along (symmetric) rational perturbations or rational scalings can then be used. PROOF.md *alone* does not give a concrete finite order or printed permanent.

## Quantitative certificate

1. Multiplicities: F(j/100)=j²/(10000+j²), so the common factor D/100 clears each summand (no coprimality hypothesis). There are exactly n=10^46 times the product of (10000+j²) over 0≤j≤10000 rows. An independent integer calculation (no floating point) found **71,503 decimal digits** for the number n itself. The smaller data identifying any row are rational and its discrete labels break ties unambiguously. Algebraic-point rounding, including on axes and for negative entries, is well-defined. Rank is at most four for the unperturbed PSD Gram only; the final PD matrix has full rank.
2. Root product bounds do not require exact rotational invariance of the rounded points: for s_p^0=e^(πip/M), remove a unit factor and the term involving (s_p^0)² ranges over an entire regular M-gon. Exact product magnitude ≤ |U|^M+|V|^M; analogous formula for the other full roots. The change after rounding is ≤2δM3^(M-1)B^M. With δ=2·10^(-2M) this coefficient is <1 (already ≤4M·10^(-M-1)). This treats both possible signs/parities in the exact formula, as well as zero terms. The half-circle in the first grid is intentional.
3. Quantitative upper potential bound (6) retains the pinning term: the unpinned radial variable is floored/capped here (not the same truncation as the qualitative proof), which only decreases log max(|a|,r|b|). Apply Jensen with its coefficient 0.99; the remaining 0.01 circle mean is log max(|u1|,|w1|). Additive errors total at most 2 log 3/M. This proves the needed uniform bound directly with no finite-grid-to-continuum convergence assumption. On the complement of the two phase tubes the maximum is ≤1-rho0²/2, so the actual upper bound using log(1-x)≤-x is below −4·10^-19. At the tiny denominator tube each individual modulus gives a lower log bound −4·10^-20. Its volume estimate follows directly from the Beta(1,3) law, and the exponent ratio shows the missing mass is <0.01.
4. In the finite Gini comparison the angle quantization displacement is at most 100(2π/M+δ), radial flooring/capping contributes ≤0.01+0.02, and pinning ≤π/200. The resultant loss is <0.046. By 3.14<π the Gini exceeds 0.739. For all rows and both phase tubes, the ratio perturbation is <0.001; the rounded factors s_p cancel exactly in v·y0/v·z0=t. With tilted probability >0.99 the bound 0.73 n² on the contraction yields 0.99(0.73)²=0.527571 before the finite denominator. Even the stronger threshold 0.5(1+3/1000)(1+2/1000) is smaller. Thus the negative derivative at q=1 is strictly certified by inequalities at the single displayed n.
5. With L=100Q², Lv is integer even in the products r_j c_h s_p. Integers B=L² A0 have absolute values <20000 L², and P'_1(B) is a negative integer, hence ≤-1. Telescoping polynomial products controls the effect of ζI with absolute error ≤1/4. On [0,1] the second derivative is bounded termwise by J (differentiate integer powers, dropping zero contributions for exponents below 2). For h=1/(4J), Taylor gives P_(1-h)(A)-P_1(A)≥5h/8>0, with q=1-h genuinely in (0,1). Multiplying A by the positive denominator of ζ multiplies the polynomial by the same positive degree-n factor at both q values. Full rank, rationality and non-diagonality follow without any numerical eigenvalue tests.

## Source and scope audit

- R. B. Bapat's [1992 research problem](https://doi.org/10.1080/03081089208818174) predates [Bapat–Lal (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(94)90497-9). Bapat explicitly attributes the earlier question to the 1992 article in his own [2007 survey, §6, Conjecture 7 and reference 3](https://www.isid.ac.in/~rbb/imsperm.pdf). The 1994 strict formulation concerns non-diagonal PD matrices on [-1,1], not matrices with all entries positive. Real examples here need not have nonnegative entries. The failure P_q(A)>per(A) is stronger than a failure of strictness. It does not refute the distinct known nonnegativity assertion P_q(A)≥0.
- [Mitchell, Theorem 1](https://files.ele-math.com/articles/oam-14-56.pdf) already treats passing between the singular PSD and PD problem, and proves certain low-rank/low-order cases; the diagonal-perturbation principle is not novel. [Kitamura's immutable source](https://github.com/KitaKen1/bapat-lal-q-permanent-lean/tree/4200da4fc1a132d69c23fb877795b5b72089544b) explicitly provides a 144-row complex Gram certificate with a nonreal entry and identifies its endpoint pair-minor and rank-two Fischer/wedge machinery. The general Hermitian conjecture has *already* been disproved. The present proof extends that machinery to real rows with a complex-sphere concentration argument rather than proving that arbitrary complex witnesses realify.
- [Da Fonseca–Goyal, §1, Conjectures 1.1–1.2 and §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14694v1) explicitly distinguish their real counterexamples at q>1 from the real case on [-1,1]. Corollary 4.4 allows a different PD integer matrix for each r>1; this is not a single endpoint witness. Similarly, their small-order minimality statement is for the half-line extension, not for the question here.
- Targeted searches by original title, real symmetric/rank four, endpoint derivative, pointwise permanent upper comparison, q- and μ-permanent, and GitHub repository search did not produce a previous real PD counterexample on [−1,1]. The closely related Schur-power, Bapat–Sunder and subset-preserving conjectures are different. This search is not proof of worldwide priority.

## Independent computation and remaining limitations

`python3 -B -O audit_real_q.py` directly checked the Fischer identity and inversion sign by permutation enumeration and independent coefficient arithmetic in 36 small ordered real examples, including repeated rows and nontrivial reorderings; verified the sorted sum with ties; checked all 10,001 precise multiplicities; checked the order's digit bound and all consequential rational slack estimates. Those small examples test identities and **are not counterexamples**. The enormous matrix, its q-permanent values, and its coefficient expansion were **not generated, calculated or enumerated**; no small or minimal witness is established. The explicit finite result stands on the analytic proof, not on those diagnostics. This review does not establish the minimum counterexample order or a useful practical order bound.
