# Sources and scope audit

1. **Atsuya Hasegawa and Ranitha Mataraarachchi, [New lower bounds for CDS and f-routing, arXiv:2609.24291v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24291v2).** Definition 2.4 says only Alice gets the one-bit secret, Bob does not, each has independent private randomness, the referee knows (x,y), and the hidden shared variable R has finite support with arbitrary probabilities. It uses ℓ1 rather than TV for each law's distance to the simulator. Section 3.1 gives the D(x,y)=TV(T_0,T_1) bounds; §3.2 supplies the posterior representation (4). Theorem 3.6/Eq. (7) bounds deterministic SMP by C q^4 γ^{-2} log₂²(Cq/γ)+1 for γ=1−2ε−δ>0, supplying the constant-gap Ω(log n) term in (5). Appendix A provides a perfectly correct O(log n)-shared-bits equality protocol for constant positive δ. Our robust novelty is the support trace estimate (3) and the resulting uniform dependence on shrinking δ for equality, not the constant-error order. The original §5 asks if robust inner-product CDS with constant errors requires polynomially many shared bits. We do not answer or claim progress on that question.

2. **Benny Applebaum and Prashant Nalini Vasudevan, [Placing Conditional Disclosure of Secrets in the Communication Complexity Universe](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2019.4), [full version ePrint 2018/1169](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/1169.pdf).** The discussion preceding Corollary 2 already gives a pairwise-independent hash for exact one-bit equality CDS and notes its almost-pairwise-independent variant at constant privacy error. Their two-message transcript scheme uses the secret at only one encoder, even though their general model permits both parties to know it. Their full-version §4.2, Lemma 1 gives a general randomness sparsification upper bound from a low-communication CDS. It implies the order of our small-error upper bound, since the bit-equality protocol sends two bits. Our field projection and public-list construction are self-contained implementations of standard ideas, not claims of first upper bounds. Their one-perfect CDS lower bounds concern different measures/functions and do not supply the exact resource formula here.

3. **Yael Gertner, Yuval Ishai, Eyal Kushilevitz and Tal Malkin, [Protecting Data Privacy in Private Information Retrieval Schemes](https://www.freehaven.net/anonbib/cache/SPIR.pdf), §3.2, Lemmas 2–3.** This already gives perfect equality CDS with the secret held by one party and sends ℓ bits per party at shared randomness 2max(n,ℓ) for two parties. The proof uses the field product plus offset and the ℓ-coordinate projection, with one full field element for each of the two common random variables. When n>ℓ, the offset is used only through its projection, so sampling only the ℓ projected bits explains the matching upper here. For ℓ≥n the construction already has the exact upper bound proved here. [Romain Gay, Iordanis Kerenidis and Hoeteck Wee, Communication Complexity of Conditional Disclosure of Secrets and Attribute-Based Encryption](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/665.pdf), Theorem 4 and Appendix C, gives the optimal per-party fixed message-length lower bound for nontrivial predicates and explicitly describes affine equality masks for single or multiple symbols; the appendix's optimality claim refers to communication, not optimizing the seed size over all encoders. We include the short communication proof for the model at hand but claim no novelty for it. The new exact result is the matching general support/entropy converse and the resulting ℓ+max(n,ℓ) optimum.

4. **Vahid Asadi, Eric Culf and Alex May, [Rank Lower Bounds on Non-Local Quantum Computation, ITCS 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol325-itcs2025/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.11/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.11.html).** They already derive asymptotic linear perfectly-private randomness lower bounds for equality via reduction to f-routing and a nondeterministic-rank witness, §4 and §5 (Equality and non-equality). That is prior art for order and the rank connection. It does not give the exact fixed-fair coin count, multi-secret 1+N(M−1) classical support bound, or robust small-error tradeoff in this note. [Allerstorfer et al., Conditional disclosure of secrets with quantum resources, ePrint 2024/630](https://opencryptology.org/2024/630/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf) and [Girish–May–Orshansky–Waddell, arXiv:2505.02939v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02939v4), study quantum CDS and adjacent separations, not the classical exact seed resource optimized here.

5. **Akinori Kawachi and Maki Yoshida, [Randomness Bounds for Private Simultaneous Messages and Conditional Disclosure of Secrets, ePrint 2021/1037](https://opencryptology.org/2021/1037/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf).** §2.2, Definitions 3–4 give all sending parties the secret and count a common uniform string. Theorems 6/14/22 address XOR, AND and inner product, including secret-length-dependent entropic bounds and statistical privacy, not the one-secret-holder equality result here. Similarly [Li and Sun, Conditional Disclosure of Secrets: A Noise and Signal Alignment Approach](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10340081) and [Li, Qin, Zhang, Fan, Chen, and Caire, Graph-Theoretic Characterization of the Noise Capacity of Conditional Disclosure of Secrets, arXiv:2510.22671v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22671v2) study two encoders both knowing the secret, with a distinct rate/capacity setting. The model difference really matters: for N=2, when Bob also knows s, one pad U suffices by giving (A_0,B_0,A_1,B_1)=(s⊕U,U,U,s⊕U), both off-diagonals constant-pair uniform and both diagonals decodable. This violates no bound here because Bob uses the secret on label 1.

6. **Small-error equality in communication complexity.** [Lalonde, Mande and de Wolf, Tight Bounds for the Randomized and Quantum Communication Complexities of Equality with Small Error](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2023.32) concerns communication and error of computing the equality bit, not shared randomness for disclosing a separate secret conditional on equality. It is adjacent to the technique and parameter shape, but neither its model nor the optimized resource is the same.

Targeted source checks on secret-holder models, exact and small-error equality shared randomness, and rank/entropy formulations found the previous results above. This note claims only the new classical converse calculations and their precise consequence in the stated one-secret-holder model, not a general first result or a new hashing technique.
