# Sources and scope

1. **Counting input.** Pavel Hrubeš, [Hard Submatrices for Non-negative Rank and Communication Complexity](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.13), CCC 2024, Appendix A, (4), printed pp. 13:11–13:12 ([author PDF](https://users.math.cas.cz/~hrubes/PDFs/HardSub.pdf); [journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00037-025-00269-4)). This is exactly the bound on the *number of distinct sets of complete ternary sign patterns attainable as the quantified variables vary*, not an ordinary sign count. Hrubeš credits Basu–Pollack–Roy, [Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-33099-2), Theorem 14.16. For background on the single-block elimination and its uniformity see [Saugata Basu, §2.4](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf). No new real-algebraic estimate is claimed.
2. **Known upper constructions.** [Kunjwal, Heunen and Fritz (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5948) realize arbitrary finite structures via direct sums of minimal incompatible subfamilies. They explicitly ask about the minimum Hilbert dimension in the discussion ([full text, p. 5](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/23277790/1311.5948v2.pdf)). [Andrejić and Kunjwal (2020)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00785), Cor. 8 and subsequent discussion, give binary qubit blocks for all Specker scenarios. This optimizes the *individual direct-sum blocks* in that recipe; it is not a proof that the dimensions of arbitrary alternative binary realizations obey a corresponding lower bound. [Kunjwal's 2025 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10063), Defs. 5.1–2 and §5.3, states the finite-family POVM formulation.
3. **Related obstructions.** [Heunen, Fritz and Reyes (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.3698), Thm. 4, rule out fixed universal dimension for **sharp** PVM graphs, a restriction absent here. [Repository #118](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/118) gives positive finite-outcome universality on one qubit with ≤2M+1 outcomes when there are M facets; [#119](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/119) exhibits parity/Hadamard pair graphs impossible for *binary* qubit measurements. These deal respectively with upper bounds and finite witnesses, not a worst-case lower rate with unrestricted n.
4. **Different resource.** [Skrzypczyk, Hoban, Sainz and Linden, The complexity of compatible measurements (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10085) characterize/upper-bound how many outcomes are needed for a *parent* measurement of an already compatible family. Our resource q bounds the outcomes of the n given marginal measurements that encode an entire compatibility structure. Their SDP and its dual are standard antecedents; we give the closed marginal-cone separation proof for the particular unnormalized certificate used here. [Grinko–Uola](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07711) and [Yoshino et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38836) concern analytic joint criteria for particular classes of binary qubit measurements; [McNulty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15954) treats anticommutativity and quantitative robustness.
5. **Other counting background.** Classical semialgebraic graph counting, e.g. [Sauermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11575), concerns the speed of fixed-complexity pair predicates. Applying a fixed-(d,q) pair predicate already suggests qualitative nonuniversality, but by itself gives neither the all-faces quantum encoding nor the uniform dependence on d,q used here. Hrubeš's argument for extended formulations is also a precedent for using joint sign-fiber information as a counting device; our application uses a single family of quantum dual inequalities and vanishing coordinate masks.
6. **Search limits.** External searches using quantum joint-measurability hypergraphs/complexes, minimum Hilbert-space dimension, bounded outcome count, exponential dimension/alphabet lower bounds, incompatible measurement witnesses, parent measurement complexity, quantum realization resources, sign fibers and semialgebraic complexity found no direct prior instance of the quantitative application. This is a scoped search, not evidence of worldwide priority or a new named conjecture. The results are nonconstructive worst-case estimates, not optimal rates or a certificate for any small numerical n.
