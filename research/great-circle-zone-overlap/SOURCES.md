# Sources and comparison

* Stefan Steinerberger, [*Well-Distributed Great Circles on S²*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-018-9994-z), [open author version, 2016, §§1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.03805). The three growth orders, the connection with projective energies, and the limiting kernel when the circles are held **fixed** while the width goes to zero are all prior. The author asks what Riesz theory can tell us about minimizers of the actual overlap; [the March 2026 problem list, §2](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf#page=4), also asks about multiplicity norms. Our comparison is for the optimized, simultaneously shrinking-width problem. Neither a microscopic classification nor equality of actual and bare finite minimizers follows.
* [Chen, Hardin and Saff, *On the Search for Tight Frames of Low Coherence*](https://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/saffeb/texts/280.pdf), §§3–4 and Theorems 4.1 and 6.3: the projector representation, uniqueness of the equilibrium measure on projective space for integrable powers, equidistribution of bare Riesz optimizers and separation for hypersingular bare energies are prior. [Hardin and Saff, *Minimal Riesz energy point configurations for rectifiable d-dimensional manifolds*](https://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/saffeb/texts/199.pdf), (8)–(9), (13) and Theorem 2.4, give the critical and hypersingular **bare** constants and the limiting distribution of asymptotically minimizing arrays. Their one-point comparison and separation method also precedes this note. We apply the manifold theorem only after proving control of the actual saturated kernel at its optimizing separations.
* [Fodor, Vígh and Zarnócz, *Covering the sphere by equal zones*, §2, Lemma 1](https://publicatio.bibl.u-szeged.hu/8607/1/sphere_plank-2015-12-18.pdf) give a two-zone area formula when $2w\le\alpha\le\pi/2$, where $w$ is the half-width, and monotonicity. We use a two-sheet projection with a uniform estimate including nearly coincident poles; a two-circle formula by itself does not optimize $n$ varying circles. [Bezdek, Fodor, Vígh and Zarnócz, Theorem 2 and Corollary 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02172) use random zones and study the maximum overlap count; at widths of order $1/n$ they obtain an $O(\log n/\log\log n)$ existence bound. Our fixed-finite-$p$ order observation does not improve that maximum result or claim that the probabilistic construction is new. [Jiang and Polyanskii](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10550) concern coverings of the whole sphere.
* [Borodachov, Hardin and Saff, *Low complexity methods for discretizing manifolds via Riesz energy minimization*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.6337), including their varying-weight condition and large-distance cutoff, are related comparison theory. The actual/bare pair ratio here need not approach one at distances much *smaller* than the width: its limit at coincidence is zero for each fixed width. [Steinerberger, July 2026, *A set of points on the sphere with small Riesz energy*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08049) instead treats usual chordal energy on the sphere and a second-order upper bound, not area-overlap minimization on unoriented poles.
