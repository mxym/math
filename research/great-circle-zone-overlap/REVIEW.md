Both graph sheets of the sphere give Jacobian 2/sqrt(1-x²-y²) for ordinary area. The squared radius at the far vertex of the strip parallelogram is h²+h²(1+c)²/r²=2h²(1+c)/r², giving (1) when the whole parallelogram lies inside the disk. The lower-bound rectangle in (2) lies in the strips since |cx+ry|≤h/2+rh/[2(h+r)]≤h and its radius is strictly below one; near parallel the single zone suffices for the upper bound. Pair sums are ordered and thus each relocation difference is twice the single-pole difference. Projective cap probability is 1−sqrt(1−b²)≤b², and average replacement on the remaining probability at least 1/2 bounds the single-pole energy by the displayed elementary radial integral. In the soft case this excludes coincidence for s>1 and proves r≫h in all three stated separation ranges. Using a minimizing configuration of each kind in the appropriate direction gives the ratio, with the lower inequality following from rJ≥1 only after separation. It also makes actual overlap minimizers asymptotically minimizing bare configurations at s≥2.

For integrable powers the truncated continuous kernel is bounded by the soft kernel eventually everywhere, including coincident poles; the only removed diagonal adds M/n. Uniform independent poles give limsup via the integrable upper bound C_s r^−s. Chen–Hardin–Saff Thm 4.1(1) gives uniqueness on the projective quotient, not on the signed sphere. For s≥2 the differential of uuᵀ/√2 is an isometry on tangents and the antipodal quotient has area 2π; Hardin–Saff's smooth manifold version and its asymptotically minimizing arrays apply. The ordered sums produce respectively factors (8h²)^s, 8²·π/(2π)=32 at s=2, and 8·(π/2)=4π at s=1. Normalized multiplicity second moment is nh plus A_1/(4π). The finite-p bound is correctly a separate elementary statement, not its sharp constant or an L∞ bound.

Steinerberger's original 2016 pp. 3–4 already connects the fixed-n vanishing-width limit with the projective kernel, and asks further structural questions. This result transfers asymptotic minima and macroscopic equidistribution in the simultaneous n→∞, width=1/n regime; it does not settle microscopic structure, uniqueness, or identical minimizers at fixed n. Do not claim original order estimates or new bare Riesz theorems.
