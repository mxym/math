# Source, version and scope checks (19 November 2026 UTC)

- **Original conjecture.** [J. Jakimiuk, C. Tang, T. Tkocz, Math. Z. 313 (2026), Art. 11, §3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-026-04016-5); [final arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22040v4). The paragraph following (3) in §3 conjectures the unrestricted-length real Rademacher upper comparison with a Gaussian and a quartic coefficient deficit. The meaningful missing exponent range there is $2<p<3$. A *different* paragraph discusses a finite-length comparison and a further paragraph treats lower moments in higher dimensions. The earlier [J. Jakimiuk, Bernoulli 32 (2026), 2524–2542](https://doi.org/10.3150/25-BEJ1965), [Theorem 1 and §5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07001v2), gives Gaussian stability for $p\ge3$, not the subcubic extension. At $p=2$ all normalized second moments equal one, so no positive coefficient is possible.
- **Crucial historical input.** [U. Haagerup, *The best constants in the Khintchine inequality*, Studia Math. 70 (1981), 231–283](https://doi.org/10.4064/sm-70-3-231-283), obtained the sharp qualitative bound. [O. Mordhorst, Colloq. Math. 147 (2017), 203–216, published PDF](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/91999); [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07850). Lemma 2.1 gives the scalarized characteristic-function bound, (9) gives the target integral, §5 proves nonnegativity at $s=\sqrt2$, and Lemma 4.1 supplies the strict negative sublevel difference near zero and the single crossing used in §3 (Nazarov–Podkorytov's method). We extract strictness of the normalized integral above its base and a uniform $1/s$ gain for large $s$, not a new proof of Mordhorst's crossing lemma. The dominant-coefficient interpolation is in the spirit of the original large-coefficient case.
- **Closest 2026 results.** [Gao–Qian, *Fourth-Moment Geometry of Rademacher Sums*](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17802v1), Theorems 1.1–1.4, is the closest by both model and fourth mass: their sharp coefficient theorem is for $p\ge4$, and their third-moment theorem is at $p=3$. It does not give the subcubic result. Its [unversioned record and history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17802), current unversioned HTML, [arXiv's metadata entry](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=2608.17802), and DOI metadata were checked; they identify the 18 August v1, not a later theorem (explicit v2 abstract, HTML and PDF addresses returned HTTP 404). [Chávez–Sheng v2, Theorems 1.2 and 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19313v2), require even integer moments. [Fang–Wang, 1 November revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27908v2), and [Xie, October, Theorems 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07526v1), treat $L_1$ stability about the two-coordinate real-sign extremizer. The comparison in the present range is with a Gaussian.
