# Independent internal review of the universal power symmetric-modulus norm test

For X(A,B)=[[0,A],[B,0]] with A,B Hermitian, X*X and XX* respectively have diagonal blocks (B²,A²) and (A²,B²). Thus the symmetric sum of powers has identical diagonal blocks with |A|^p+|B|^p, and its operator norm after taking the positive pth root gives exactly (1). Taking B=t^(1/p)P, the larger scalar eigenvalue of tP+C equals t+C_22+O(1/t) by rationalizing the 2-by-2 discriminant. In the fixed-data pth root expansion the coefficient of t^(1/p−1) is C_22/p. The strict scalar Jensen gap survives multiplication by the positive normalization and hence gives a midpoint violation at a sufficiently large finite t for each fixed p.

For 1≤p<2 the two signed projections are exactly ±P_±, each absolute power has (2,2) entry s², and their midpoint is the off-diagonal matrix with entries cs. Its absolute power is (cs)^p I, and the ratio to s² tends to infinity as s→0 because p−2<0. For p>2 the diagonal endpoint contributes zero in position (2,2) while the exchange endpoint contributes 1. The positive eigenvalue T of their sum has eigenvector (T,1); its squared weight on coordinate two is 1/(T²+1), yielding the claimed lower bound for the midpoint. It diverges as T→∞ when p>2. These cases cover every finite p other than 2 and use real 4-by-4 inputs. For p=1 the separate finite example has PSD/NSD rank-one signed endpoints. The eigenvalues of the positive blocks after adding B are 24,7 (trace 31, determinant 168), and the midpoint block is diag(4,25), proving the numerical gap exactly. Block padding preserves the operator norm.

For p=2, apply the same symmetric gauge to the n singular values of the rectangular additive stack [X;X*]/√2. The triangle inequality follows from its rectangular unitarily invariant norm. Complex scalar homogeneity of the final function follows from |λX|=|λ||X| and does not require the stack to be complex-linear. This case is already Bourin–Zhang Corollary 6.5. The universally quantified Conjecture 8.7 is the only target. Neither classification of all individual unitarily invariant norms or dimensions below four nor optimal constants in their preceding question follows.
