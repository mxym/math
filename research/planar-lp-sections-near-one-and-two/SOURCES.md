# Prior work and what these two neighborhoods add

* **The question and the known point.** G. Chasapis, P. Nayar and T. Tkocz, [*Slicing ℓₚ-balls reloaded: stability, planar sections in ℓ₁*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05645v2), [Ann. Probab. 50 (2022), 2344–2372](https://doi.org/10.1214/22-AOP1584), Theorem 1, §3.2, Lemmas 6–7, and §8. Their planar minimum at one includes uniqueness up to signed permutations. They credit the proof to Fedor Nazarov. The lower bound used near one is taken from that proof, not claimed as a new polygon inequality. Keeping its length and gap deficits supplies local coercivity; continuity of the area Hessian and global isolation then give persistence under a change in the exponent. Their quantitative stability Theorem 2 is about **hyperplane** sections. Their planar conjecture concerns the entire interval $(0,2)$.
* **Lower dimensions and related questions.** A. Koldobsky, [*An application of the Fourier transform to sections of star bodies*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02773465), Israel J. Math. 106 (1998), 157–164, treats the minimum for real hyperplane sections when $0<p<2$. It covers the planar case in dimension three. In dimension two there is just one plane. See also [Nayar and Tkocz's survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00885v3). Results on [restricted hyperplane sections](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07163), [hyperplane projections](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21787) and [maximal hyperplane sections](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07619v2) concern other extremal problems.
* **Angular energy.** H. Cohn and A. Kumar, [*Universally optimal distribution of points on spheres*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607446v2), [J. Amer. Math. Soc. 20 (2007), 99–148](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-06-00546-7), Theorem 1.2. The regular polygon is sharp, so its universal optimality for the individual Poisson potentials is known. Here those potentials occur as a positive mixture representing the second-order area comparison on equal-weight tight frames. The alternate Fejér calculation in [the audit](REVIEW.md) is a special-case verification, not a new claim of universal optimality.
