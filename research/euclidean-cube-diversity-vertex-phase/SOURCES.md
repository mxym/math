# Sources and boundaries of the cube-vertex result

- [Tom Leinster and Emily Roff, *The Maximum Entropy of a Metric Space*, Quarterly Journal of Mathematics 72 (2021), 1271–1309](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haab003) ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11184)). Section 10(2) asks for the maximum-diversity measure on a Euclidean ball or cube, including its support. The conjectural union of spheres in the next sentence is **specifically about a ball**. Theorem 7.1 and Corollary 7.3 supply the simultaneous maximum principle: solving the reciprocal energy problem (order 2) gives a simultaneous maximizer. Their Example 8.4 gives the interval case, with a nonzero multiple of Lebesgue measure and endpoint atoms for every positive length; our dimension-uniform result consequently starts at d=2. Their work uses an exponential of Euclidean distance, not of its square and not the Manhattan distance on cubes.
- The closest predecessor is [Shilan Anwer M. Salim, *The q-spread dimension and the maximum diversity of square grid metric spaces*, Sheffield PhD thesis, 2021](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/29673/) ([full text](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/29673/1/PhD_thesis.pdf)). Chapter 4 already studied corner phases; this behavior and the four-corner magnitude formula are not new. Section 4.2 details computations on finite grids and critical values for small resolutions. Theorem 4.3.3 compares the corner orbit with the other individual orbits on a fixed n×n grid n>3 for sufficiently small grid spacing t. Theorem 4.3.4 (odd n>3) and Corollary 4.3.5 (even n>2) address the weightings of the union with one further orbit. Together these results imply qualitative, sufficiently-small-scale optimality on each fixed grid in their scope, via one-point first variations on every orbit (see [the derivation in the independent review, §4](REVIEW.md)). Her t is grid spacing; total side length s=(n−1)t. Do not infer a discovery of the corner pattern or priority for finite small-grid evidence. The sharp solid-square threshold and the all-dimension optimal common side-length interval are the incremental statements considered here. The note only asserts a threshold uniform over all grid resolutions, not sharpness separately on any given finite grid.
- [Adam Dajani, *Maximum Diversity and Support Transitions on Disks and Three-Dimensional Balls* (October 2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38031) announces a full phase diagram in Euclidean **balls** in dimensions two and three, including explicit boundary-support thresholds and a central supported region above them. Section 2, equations (4)–(5), already gives the positive-energy identity and necessary-and-sufficient equilibrium-potential test, which we use without any novelty claim. The variational test is common to the problems, but neither domain nor threshold agrees. This manuscript does not claim to settle any remaining ball conjecture and does not give the square measure above its first transition.
- External searches covered the LR article, Salim and subsequent work, Dajani, metric space magnitude/maximum diversity, homogeneous weighting, Euclidean square/cube grids, corner or vertex-supported measures, exponential-distance potential equilibrium, stochastic covariance and optimal design, and variations in dimensionality/scale terminology. No prior paper explicitly giving this sharp continuum square threshold or the full-dimensional uniform statement was located. Searches cannot exclude unindexed work, different terminologies or a theorem in another field implying the same statement; no absolute priority claim.
