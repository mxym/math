* For a fixed pure state the quantile formula is the top-third counterpart of the bottom-third Werner formula; empirical atoms do not inhibit exact selection. The objective \((\mu,\psi,t)\mapsto t+\int \sum_j(p_j-t)_+d\mu\) is jointly continuous by uniform basis-integrand convergence on a compact domain and weak convergence. The probability measure space on \(U(3)\) is compact. The three-dimensional Dirichlet calculation gives Haar average \(11/18\) for the mean of the largest probability in each basis. Saturation forces that mean and the pooled sum to equal \(11/18\) pointwise, as a nonnegative continuous deficit of mean zero is identically zero. Each fixed-state common cutoff works on the entire support: a strictly positive continuous integrand on a support neighborhood could not integrate to zero.
* For any support basis \(A\) flat on \(\psi\), the common cutoff at \(\psi\) equals \(1/3\). Against any other basis with dense pivot column the proposed row can be made to have constant term zero and a nonzero oscillatory coefficient; in quadrature that coefficient has squared modulus proportional to \(|c_i|^2+|d_i|^2\). Strict amplitude persists in an open arc. At both simple zeros in the second phase, each other row must vanish or the sum-zero relation yields a positive row then simultaneous positivity by continuity. A two-point homogeneous interpolation problem has dimension one in the three real harmonics. The complex amplitude vectors are therefore real multiples of one amplitude. The wedge with the conjugate vanishes on an arc and identically by the Laurent polynomial identity. The inverted diagonal pivot shows the two mixed columns are independent and hence the resulting conjugating scalar has modulus one.
* With no modulus-one entry, two zeros cannot share a row/column and cannot be in distinct rows/columns (the corresponding rows would have only one nonzero common term in their inner product). If one entry is zero, a different dense pivot contradicts equality of the moduli of the other two columns. If all entries are nonzero, applying the dense-pivot property to all columns gives a \(3\times3\) complex Hadamard; after dephasing each nontrivial row contains the two primitive cube roots of unity off its first column, in different orders, giving the Fourier matrix. For Fourier and a flat \((1,1,-1)/\sqrt3\) input the output masses are \(1/9,4/9,4/9\), directly violating the hypothesis. Thus each pair shares a ray. Two distinct bases sharing \(r\) have remaining rays in \(r^\perp\); a third basis sharing one other ray with each would have to contain \(r\) as the remaining orthogonal ray. At that common ray the mean maximum is 1, impossible under saturation.
* The minimum is attained on the compact measure space yet cannot equal the Haar lower bound. That proves a uniform positive gap over all lists, not just non-attainment at each fixed finite size. No numerical margin, unrestricted alignments or physical-threshold obstruction is obtained.
