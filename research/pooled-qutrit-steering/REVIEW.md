# Independent internal check of the frozen qutrit Werner argument

Reviewed frozen argument SHA-256 32095f4c398eddd502bac2d12fecc1a08f308251d76f00f0f99ff1bc3de50ce9; only the manuscript status line changed for this draft. PASS as a uniform lower bound, not the optimum. This is internal AI analysis, not external peer review.

* In Yang–Qiao's [version of record](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-026-01204-3) and [author v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20950v2), Eqs. (16), (21), (24), Table 1 and the text immediately following Eq. (25)/Fig. 2(b) together specify the pooled direct-sum qutrit Werner formula. The threshold is \(1-3h\), where \(h\) minimizes the normalized total of the bottom third. The physical threshold is not in question. Eq. (25) alone has the isotropic subscript and is not by itself sufficient for Werner scope.
* The spectral maximum allows all rank-one outcomes subject only to the total selection count. Finite best selection is exactly the smallest \(N\) values; in the weighted extension a quantile with fractional ties attains the cutoff maximum. The measures are on basis triples with one unit of mass per outcome. The resulting finite pure-state minimum exists; no compactness of measure space is needed for the quantitative proof.
* For any cutoff the cost gap from single-minimum selection is termwise the sum of the upper deviation of the minimum plus the lower deviations of the other two. It dominates both \(\alpha-t\) and \(\beta(t-s)\). A convex combination in proportions \(\beta:1\) cancels \(t\), and the restriction \(h\ge s\) legitimizes multiplying by the weaker \(\beta/2\) coefficient. The other case \(h<s\) gives a strictly stronger lower bound on visibility. The Haar simplex density makes each pair event a square of area \(s^2\) with density 2, and the pair events are disjoint under \(s<1/3\). The tail integral for the minimum is \(1/9\). The separate rational checker independently checks the constants and fractional cutoff equivalence on abstract finite probability triples, including ties. No realization assumption is needed for those checks.
* If a true lattice join uses a concave envelope, it only raises the bound. The Werner target is affine on each side of rank \(2N\), so at this bound it cannot cross an enclosing concave curve elsewhere. No assertion about other aggregations, arbitrary alignments, isotropic, or unrestricted measurements.
