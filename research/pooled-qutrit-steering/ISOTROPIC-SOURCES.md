# Isotropic companion: primary sources and search scope

**Question.** Ma-Cheng Yang and Cong-Feng Qiao, [npj Quantum Information 12, 55 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-026-01204-3), [full author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20950v2). The text following Eq. (25) and Fig. 2(a) concerns the optimized pooled isotropic threshold approaching 5/12; Fig. 2(b) treats Werner. Eq. (25) is the isotropic optimization problem, not the entire family of steering tests in the paper. The displayed probabilities in Eq. (20) have a single preferred outcome of mass (1+2w)/3; Eqs. (22), (23), (32) and (33) fix the normalization and the unconstrained pooled selection. Independently, in [the authors' MATLAB code at fixed commit 7e82b6a](https://github.com/yangmacheng/High-Dimensional-Quantum-Steering/blob/7e82b6a7a7f4cc0ad3238ee25ce598111763c802/omegak_batching_optimized_Bloch.m), the program puts all N*d rays in one array, enumerates every size-k subset, and maximizes the norm of the sum of its chosen projectors. It does not require exactly one ray from each basis. This confirms the semantics without relying on the numerical optimizer or reproducing its plots.

**Different kinds of sharpness.** The actual all-projective-measurement steering thresholds come from [Wiseman, Jones and Doherty (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.98.140402), not from saturation of the particular pooled witness. [Nguyen and Gühne (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.125.230402) distinguish what two-outcome measurements and three-outcome measurements can reveal on qutrit systems. Their restriction on the physical measurements is not the pooled selection rule for rank-one three-outcome bases studied here. Yang and Qiao's later [PRL 137, 160201 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/86gq-fyry), [Eq. (20) and Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00595), uses an optimized Tsallis entropic uncertainty bound for fixed measurement configurations, with a visibility example for isotropic states under asymmetric measurements. The greater sensitivity there and the stated generic looseness of majorization-based entropic bounds are not a proof of a uniform lower bound on this distinct top-N pooled threshold over all numbers and choices of bases.

[Designolle, Skrzypczyk, Fröwis and Brunner, Eq. (7)](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.09609v3) optimize a norm by choosing exactly one outcome per measurement. That constrained quantity is not in general the pooled optimum of Eq. (25).

**Random-phase sums.** The short proof of the pair lemma uses three independent random phases (one can fix the common phase). For general spherical sums [König and Rudelson, Prop. 5.1](https://websites.umich.edu/~rudelson/papers/KR-non-central_sections.pdf) prove a uniform probability lower bound at the root-mean-square radius; in the plane their bound is (2√3−3)/5. [Chasapis, Liu and Tkocz, §2.2, Thm. 2](https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/15718) give a general, smaller universal constant for matrix coefficients. Those broader results do not give the strict probability greater than 1/3 needed here for a non-singleton three-term scalar row. We make no claim of a sharp three-term constant.

**Earlier torus geometry.** Hartmut Führ and Ziemowit Rzeszotnik, [Linear Algebra Appl. 484 (2015), §6, Ex. 6.1, Remark 6.2 and Thm. 6.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.06738), already used the regions where individual output coordinates of a unitary acting on a flat torus have modulus at least one. Shared boundary tests, the block/zero-pattern observations, and the existence of a common flat vector are prior. Ole Andersson and Ingemar Bengtsson, [Rep. Math. Phys. 79 (2017), §§5–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.09062), studied pairs of qutrit Clifford tori, generic transverse intersections, exceptional mergers and two-circle intersections on unistochastic edges. Their account explicitly separates analytic examples from numerical exploration. At a transverse common intersection the existence of nearby doubly large coordinates follows already from local openness of the probability map. Our required implication is about *strict interiors* for all non-block matrices, including exceptional ones, rather than the number or transversality of simultaneous unbiased states. Neither finding a biunimodular vector nor a generic transversality statement suffices by itself. We do not claim that the torus setup is new or offer a classification of flat-flat intersections. The uniqueness of the order-three complex Hadamard, relevant to the alternate Laurent proof, is classical; see [Tadej and Życzkowski, §5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0512154), [original printed DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11080-006-8220-2).

**Search limits, 19 November 2026.** Queries combining author, arXiv and journal identifiers with qutrit, isotropic, threshold, gap, optimal settings, direct-sum and conjecture; and separate queries on biunimodular vectors, nontransverse Clifford tori, three-term unit-circle random phases, and the strict two-coordinate condition, did not identify a prior uniform exclusion of 5/12 for this Yang–Qiao witness. This is not a priority guarantee.
