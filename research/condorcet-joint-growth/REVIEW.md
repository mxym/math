# Independent audit: strict Condorcet winners with two growing parameters

**PASS** on the analytic proof. I rederived the probability representation, both changes of measure, the threshold localization, the asymptotic quantifiers and the local corollary. This is internal AI review, not external peer review or a priority decision.

**Main conclusion.** In impartial culture with $m$ independent uniform strict rankings of $n$ candidates, $\log Q(m,n)/\log n\to-1$ along *every* sequence $m,n\to\infty$, including even $m$ with a strict rather than tied pairwise victory required. This is neither a ratio equivalent nor a fixed-$m$ statement. The hypercube theorem counts local winners in full random rankings, not global winners among all cube vertices.

### 1. Conditional law and uniform lower bound

* Generate each voter's ranking by independent continuous uniform scores, larger preferred. Conditional on the designated candidate's vector $u$, different competitors' comparisons depend on independent columns of scores. For one competitor, it receives a strict majority exactly when $S=\sum_i\mathrm{Bernoulli}(u_i)>m/2$. Thus the conditional probability of a designated overall win is $(1-b(u))^{n-1}$, where $b=P(S\le m/2)$. Scaling by $n$ counts a disjoint union, including when some contests tie.
* Put $t=e^a$, $h=t(1-u)+u/t$. There is a direct normalization check: $q(u)=(u/t)/h$ has derivative $1/h^2$ and maps $[0,1]$ onto itself. Hence $\int h^{-2}=1$ for each deterministic $a$. The outer tilt is a product law, not a tilt of Bernoulli outcomes. With $y=-\log h$ it has density $e^y/(t-1/t)$ on $[-a,a]$. The first two derivatives of $\log[2\sinh(ta)/t]$ at $t=1$ (here $t$ is a moment parameter) give exactly $\mu=a\coth a-1$ and $v=1-a^2/\sinh^2a$.
* For $a\le1$, $v\le a^2$ by range and $\mu\ge a^2/(3\cosh1)$ since $\int_0^a x\sinh x\,dx\ge a^3/3$ and $\sinh a\le a\cosh 1$; $3\cosh 1<5$. For $a\ge1$ use $v\le1$, monotonicity of $\mu$, and $\mu(1)>1/5$. Thus variance at most five times the mean is **uniform in $m$ and $a$**. Taking $M=(\sqrt H+10)^2$ as the tilted mean is always possible, with positive $a$. The interval $M\pm2\sqrt{5M}$ lies in $[H,H+25\sqrt H+145]$, and Chebyshev costs only $1/4$. The Bernoulli Markov inequality gives $b\le e^{-X_a}$ at all $u$, so $b\le1/(2d)$ there ($d=n-1$) and $(1-b)^d\ge1-db\ge1/2$. Undo the *outer* tilt using $e^{-2X_a}$ at the upper end of that interval. This yields the stated lower bound for all $m\ge1$.

### 2. Upper bound and both large parameters

* We need only $d$ sufficiently large that $4\log d/d<3/4$. If $\sum_i u_i\le m/2-\sqrt m$, conditional Chebyshev bounds the win probability by $1/4$. For the remaining small-loss environments, the tilted sum of success probabilities starts strictly above $m/2-\sqrt m$, decreases continuously to zero, and has a unique positive tilt attaining this target once $m\ge256$ (endpoints $u_i=0,1$ have probability zero). Conditioning here is different from the outer tilt.
* For a Bernoulli outcome vector with $S$ successes, the old mass divided by the inner-tilted mass is exactly $\prod_i h_i\,t^{2S-m}$. This sign and normalization ensure a lower bound $\tfrac34 e^{-X_a-4a\sqrt m}$ on the original loss mass by considering $|S-(m/2-\sqrt m)|<\sqrt m$ under the new law. Even $m$ and a nonintegral center cause no lattice correction, since this smaller event is contained in the loss event.
* For the random-tilt cutoff, if $a>A$ then $\sum_iq_A(u_i)>m/2-\sqrt m$. In the original iid uniform environment $d\mathbb E q_s/ds=-2\mathbb E q_s(1-q_s)$. Restrict $u$ to $[1/4,3/4]$ to obtain $\mathbb E q_A\le1/2-cA$ for $A\le6$, with the stated positive $c$. Since $cA\ge10\sqrt{L/m}$, centered Hoeffding costs at most $\exp(-mc^2A^2/2)\le d^{-4}$. For $A\ge6$, the threshold is at least $3m/8$; if fewer than $m/6$ coordinates exceed or equal $1/4$, their sum is at most $3m/8$. The one-coordinate probability is $3/(3+e^{2A})$. With integer ceiling, the union bound is at most $2^m(3e^{-2A})^{m/6}\le e^{-mA/6}\le d^{-4}$. This is an environment probability, not a false deterministic bound on the tilt.
* $X'_a=2\sum q_a-m=-2\sqrt m$ at the chosen tilt and $-m\le X''\le0$. A nearest mesh point spaced $1/\sqrt m$ loses at most $2+1/2$. For every **fixed** mesh value, $\Pr(X_j\ge z)\le e^{-2z}$ follows from the normalized outer density. Only then take a finite union. The grid size is at most $2+C(\sqrt L+L/\sqrt m)$; the reverse-tilt cost is $4C(\sqrt L+L/\sqrt m)=o(L)$ for every way $m,L\to\infty$. All exceptions have probability or integrand contribution at most $d^{-4}$. The designated exponent is therefore $-2$, and the union over candidates makes the global exponent $-1$.

### 3. Independent local check

One star has the same probability as the designated global event with just $d$ competitors. Disjoint closed stars depend on disjoint independent score sets. Removing Hamming balls of radius two gives at least $2^d/[1+d+\binom d2]$ mutually disjoint stars. The uniform lower bound for a star is $d^{-2}\exp(-O(\sqrt{\log d}))$; this proves the stated exponential bound on absence. The same bound with ordered covariance pairs gives $\mathrm{Var}(W)/(\mathbb EW)^2\le[1+d+\binom d2]/(2^d\theta)\to0$ uniformly. This is for full rankings, not independent edge directions. Under independent fair orientations, the factorial moment of order $r$ equals $2^{-rd}$ times the number of ordered distinct, pairwise nonadjacent $r$-tuples: adjacent vertices cannot both be sinks; nonadjacent vertices use disjoint edges. For fixed $r$ this tends to 1, since the fraction of tuples with a repeated or adjacent pair is $O_r((d+1)/2^d)$. Thus the comparison probability tends to $1-e^{-1}$, already known from the random-game literature. The 2011 authors' strict existence comparison follows for sufficiently large $d$ uniformly in odd $m$, not at each finite dimension.

### 4. Primary-source boundary

* [May (1971)](https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830160204), as cited in [Hązła et al., §1.3, (1.7)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-020-00994-7), gives the stronger leading equivalent $\sqrt{8\pi\log n}/n$ **after** taking the electorate limit with $n$ fixed. Cite the leading term only; no error term from this source is required.
* [Sauermann, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13713) computes the fixed odd-electorate limit with its leading constant; it gives exponent $-(k-1)/k$ for fixed $m=2k-1$. This cannot be extended to arbitrary growing $m$ solely by exchanging limits. [Caizergues et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06028) keep the number of candidates fixed for their expansion and address broader voting distributions/criteria. Do not confuse impartial culture with the anonymous or random-common-culture models.
* [Pittel v5, Eq. (1.1), Theorem 1.1(a,b)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20613v5#S1.Thmtheorem1): v5 is the current arXiv version at this check. Part (a) states a quantitative approximation at $m\gg n^4$, distinct from the exponent here and not contradicted. Combined with the older May leading term, (a) already yields that leading equivalent when the error is negligible, for example $m\gg n^6/\log n$. Part (b) as displayed claims a super-polynomial upper bound for the electorate-limit $Q_n$. This cannot hold for the very integral (1.1): set $z=\sqrt{2\log n}$. On $-z-1/z\le y\le -z$ the Gaussian tail $G(y)\ge1-1/n$ and $e^{-y^2}\ge e^{-3}n^{-2}$ for $z\ge1$, so $Q_n\ge c/(nz)$. This contradicts $O(n^{-2})$ already, independently of the new proof or statistical computations. The authors' qualitative conclusion of vanishing in their regime is unaffected. No external correspondence or inference about intent.
* [Conitzer, Lang & Xia, §6](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~conitzer/hypercubewiseIJCAI11.pdf) distinguish the uniform-CP-net/fair independent-edge model from full-ranking impartial culture, state the finite-dimensional comparison as a conjecture and report several experiments. The comparison here is asymptotic only; deterministic existence for each profile is not claimed. The independent-edge Poisson limit is prior; see also [Amiet et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10758).

[The only incremental files through #113](https://github.com/mxym/math/compare/0b40a41829ff499f49ab461c7a8090e0585ed8cd...e6ab8d2ee787ae44333aca59fa112ee159bb9d70) concern an unrelated quantum-measurement equality problem. ### 5. Regression evidence

The independent standard-library checker compares two different enumerations for 19 small elections including strict even thresholds, recovers $17/18$ at $(3,3)$ and $1/n$ for two voters, checks 2,032 conditional likelihood identities in rational arithmetic, checks the exact environment Jacobian, and exhaustively verifies two sink-count factorial moments for cubes through dimension three. These tests do not replace the analytic large-parameter estimates.
