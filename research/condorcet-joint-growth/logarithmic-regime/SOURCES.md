# Literature, scope and internal duplication screen

* [Maassen & Bezembinder (2002), Theorem 5](https://www.math.ru.nl/~maassen/preps/condorcet.pdf) gives the integral for iid uniform *linear* rankings. Their Theorem 6 concerns weak rankings, not our model. The polynomial representation is earlier work.
* [May (1971)](https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830160204) and [Hązła et al., Eq. (1.7)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-020-00994-7) record the iterated-limit leading term sqrt(8π log n)/n. The present result concerns a fixed finite voter/log-candidate ratio. Formal consistency of the coefficients when that ratio increases does not prove uniformity in the ratio.
* [Sauermann (2022), Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13713) treats fixed odd electorates and gives an asymptotic with k-dependent constant; this does not treat m proportional to log n. [Caizergues et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06028) give a large-electorate expansion with a fixed number of candidates and a parity-dependent correction; parity effects on strict versus tied majorities are not a new observation.
* [Pittel (2025), v5, Thm 1.1(a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20613v5) controls the absolute error in the Gaussian approximation for m much larger than n⁴. This does not cover the logarithmic regime. The separate issue with the displayed v5(b) estimate has already been discussed in the repository and is not used here.
* [Kim & Shin (Oct. 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29904v1), Thms 1.1 and 1.2, state Kelly's two monotonicity conjectures for odd electorates. As the attribution correction to #114 explains, voter-count monotonicity already implies the odd joint logarithmic exponent when combined with the fixed and iterated results above. Their §2.3 also parametrizes a **common translation of voter log odds**, a standard conditional-Bernoulli device: their reference coordinate is the last log odds, and their Jacobian relates to the tie probability. Our moving centered-slice coordinates select the point on that same odds-translation orbit at which the conditional sum has mean m/2. We do not claim the odds-translation idea or the integral itself as original. Their monotonicity does not furnish these relative equivalents and two explicit limiting constants.
* The repository's [#114](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/114) proves the logarithmic exponent without relative growth restrictions by a direct method. See [its attribution correction](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/116) for prior consequences and the strict-even lower-bound distinction. Neither #114 nor its numerical regressions establish the present equivalent.

* **Equivalent geometric model.** With continuous iid coordinates, a strict winner is precisely a point which no other point exceeds in $k=\lceil m/2\rceil$ of the coordinates: a tie at $m/2$ is a loss for our purpose. Thus $Q$ is also the expected size of the $k$-dominant skyline, which here has size at most one. [Hwang, Tsai & Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.6224) prove vanishing for fixed dimension and general $k<m$; their sharp growing-dimension threshold concerns $k=m-1$, not the majority level here. [Schibler & Suri, Theorem 6](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~suri/psdir/kds.pdf) give cardinality bounds which in this range reduce to $O(1)$, not an explicit chance of existence. This connection is an equivalent formulation, not an extra theorem.
