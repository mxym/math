# Independent internal mathematical audit: strict Condorcet crossover

**PASS** for the theorem in the proof. This is an AI-assisted audit, not external peer review. Scope: iid uniform strict voter rankings, m voters and n candidates both tending to infinity, m/log n→c strictly between zero and infinity, and a candidate must win *strictly* against every rival. The odd/even ratio compares adjacent electorates with the same candidate count. The limit is along every such sequence, not merely an iterated limit or one special rate of approach to c. Neither endpoint c=0,∞ is included.

## 1. The exact integral and its tilt

Generate all utilities independently. Given the distinguished candidate's vector u, comparisons with distinct opponents are independent, with each one-comparison success count distributed as a Poisson-binomial sum. A strict win requires at least floor(m/2)+1. Distinct putative winners cannot both strictly defeat one another, hence Q=n times the distinguished-winner probability with no parity exception. Below the central half-space, Markov gives loss-or-tie probability ≥1/(m+2); that half is exponentially negligible even relative to n⁻²√m.

I checked the change of variables in odds coordinates: logit(u_i)=logit(q_i)+2s, with sum q_i=m/2. Each upper-half u has exactly one positive s by strict monotonicity. In the coordinates q_1,…,q_{m−1},q_m=m/2−Σ_{i<m}q_i, write R=exp(s), h_i=1+(R²−1)q_i, k_i=h_i/R. Then du_i/dq_i=1/k_i², du_i/ds=2u_i(1−u_i). Factoring 1/k_i² out of each row leaves the first columns e_i−e_m and final column 2q_i(1−q_i), hence determinant 2V exp(−2X). The coordinate measure used is the projection onto the first m−1 coordinates, *not* Euclidean surface measure. Its mass is the m-fold uniform density at the center by solving for the last variable in the convolution. There is no factor √m here.

For any fixed bit vector of weight l the original Bernoulli probability divided by its tilted value is R^(2l−m)/product k_i. With l=floor(m/2)−j the exponent is −η−2j, so losses including a tie produce B as written. The chain X'_s=Σ(2u_i−1), X''_s=4Σu_i(1−u_i)≤m confirms the root monotonicity and bounds even for inconsistent individual signs of X'_s's summands. The source equations determine exactly the sufficient statistic for the survival probability; the appearance of V in the original Jacobian is necessary.

## 2. Slice local limit, concentration and the bad-environment bound

Fourier inversion for the centered sum of uniforms gives f_m~sqrt(6/(πm)) from centered variance 1/12 and the actual lattice-free density. Its uniform O(1/√r) upper bound (r sufficiently large suffices) follows by the same absolute inversion. Every half-path marginal under normalized coordinate slice volume has density bounded relative to iid by sup f_{m-k}/f_m≤C; using this on each half is sufficient for Hoeffding concentration, no spurious independence assumption. For D=−Σlog q_i/m, Cauchy–Schwarz across the two halves gives E exp(tD)≤C(1−2t/m)^(-m/2)=exp(O(t+1)) whenever t is positive and o(m), including odd m.

For the local Poisson-binomial CLT, Taylor expansion of each centered factor at zero has uniformly bounded cubic remainder. With V≥δm, rescaling the product gives a total O(m/V^(3/2)) error at bounded arguments, uniform over environments. The exact sin² modulus bound majorizes the whole inversion by an integrable Gaussian after rescaling. The inversion phase has modulus one, hence sup over lattice points is legitimate. Truncating the geometric series *after* using the uniform pointwise O(1/√m) bound leaves a uniform local formula on compact positive s; the parity offset survives fully.

The rare environments are the delicate point: their probability alone, exp(−γm), would not suffice without uniformly controlling I(q) relative to d⁻². I independently reconstructed the envelope:
* at tilted mean m/2, Markov guarantees at least 1/(m+2) left-tail mass, and Hoeffding removes at most (m+2)^−8 beyond 2√(m log(m+2)), regardless of V or coordinates;
* multiplying the window mass by its least lattice weight gives B≥exp(−hs)/[2(m+2)], h=4√(m log(m+2)); the odd half-integer endpoint does not change this bound;
* X≥m(s−D) and X'≥X/s, so survival is ≤exp(−U exp(−(1+h/m)x)), U=d exp(−hD)/[2(m+2)], with x=X. For x<1 the length in s is ≤D+1/m, and its survival is O(U^(-2/(1+h/m))); for x≥1, ds/dx≤1/m+D, and integration over all real x is bounded by the same power times a bounded Gamma factor;
* multiplying by d² incurs only exp(O(h)) since log d=O(m), whereas the D-dependent polynomial times exp(O(hD)) has at most exp(O(h+1)) second moment by the slice mgf. Cauchy–Schwarz against the bad indicator leaves exp(−γm/2+o(m)). This also covers exponentially tiny q_i and vanishing V near degenerate portions of the slice. I found no implicit uniform ellipticity outside the good event.

## 3. The moving saddle and the constant

Uniform concentration at a/2 and 2a and the elementary μ(s)=s coth(s)−1 (strictly increasing) bracket the level x_0=log d−(1/2)log m with linear-in-m slack. Concentration of X' at a/2 bounds the denominator on x≥x_0−εm. On smaller x only a superexponentially small binomial survival contribution remains. For y=x−x_0≤0 in the remaining range, 2V/X' is bounded and the j=0 probability at the central lattice site bounds the normalized integrand by C exp(−2y−c exp(−y)); for y>0, C exp(−2y) is enough. This bound is deterministic on good environments and integrable on the entire line.

For fixed y, bracketing around a by a±ε' gives s→a in probability even if log d−m/c is unbounded but sublinear. Continuity of X'_s/m under a shift in s follows from X''≤m. Consequently V/m→1/6, X'/m→μ'(a), sqrt(m) B→ B₀=sqrt(3/π)exp(−aη)/(1−exp(−2a)). The normalized integral is (3μ'(a))⁻¹ ∫exp(−2y−B₀exp(−y))dy = [3μ'(a)B₀²]⁻¹. Combining with n f_m m/d² gives the stated C_η, including sqrt(6π)/9, not sqrt(6/π)/9. Its factor exp(2aη) makes odd/even winners more frequent by exp(2a) in the adjacent comparison. The separate-parity proofs cover mixed parity sequences since only two residues occur.

## 4. Search, adjacent prior, and independent checks

[May (1971)](https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830160204) and [Hązła et al., Eq. 1.7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-020-00994-7) give the sharp iterated Gaussian limit; [Sauermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13713) treats fixed odd m with growing n. [Caizergues et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06028) treat an expansion with the number of alternatives fixed; the parity of the electorate already matters there, but they do not give this moving-candidate leading-order multiplicative ratio. [Pittel v5, Theorem 1.1(a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20613v5) treats m≫n⁴ with an absolute error; the logarithmic-voter regime is outside its hypothesis. [Repository PR #114](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/114) gives the generalized logarithmic exponent only, and [#116](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/116) is an attribution update, not this ratio asymptotic. Primary-source searches on both scales, parity, prefactor, and saddle point did not locate the same crossover. These checks do not certify worldwide priority.

A separate standard-library checker verifies the Jacobian and full joint binary likelihood on rational data in both parities, the exact central-slice normalization on four larger m, and the parity geometric factor with direct exact binomial coefficients. It tests identities and scales; it cannot replace the proof of the limiting theorem. This is independent internal review, not an external referee report.
