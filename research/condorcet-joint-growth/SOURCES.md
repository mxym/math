# Source and scope checks

* [R. M. May (1971), *Some mathematical remarks on the paradox of voting*](https://doi.org/10.1002/bs.3830160204), and [Hązła et al., §1.3, Eq. (1.7)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-020-00994-7), record the leading equivalent $\sqrt{8\pi\log n}/n$ **after** first taking the electorate limit. This is sharper in its regime, but successively taking two limits alone does not control arbitrary diagonal sequences. The Gaussian orthant representation and results about monotonicity in the limiting model are also discussed by [Krishnamoorthy & Raghavachari](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0511140).
* [L. Sauermann, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13713) obtains the sharp asymptotic and leading constant when the odd number $m=2k-1$ of voters is **fixed**, with global exponent $-(k-1)/k$. May treated the three-voter case. Our designated-candidate conditional integral is prior; the new argument concerns uniform control as the number of voters varies.
* [Caizergues, Durand, Noy, de Panafieu & Ravelomanana](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06028) consider more general preferences and victory thresholds and derive a finite-electorate expansion under impartial culture with the candidate count fixed. Their parity correction is important and is not computed here.
* [B. Pittel, arXiv:2506.20613v5, Theorem 1.1(a)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20613v5) compares $Q(m,n)$ to the infinite-electorate Gaussian integral with absolute error $O(n^2/\sqrt m)$ for $m\gg n^4$ and asks whether the growth restriction can be relaxed. Our conclusion removes the restriction for the logarithmic exponent and for vanishing; it does not extend that specific Gaussian error estimate to arbitrary relative rates. The separate displayed super-polynomial assertion in v5(b) about the iterated-limit integral is not used. A direct check of the displayed integral is included in the [review, §4](REVIEW.md). No inference about other versions or the reason for the discrepancy is needed.
* [V. Conitzer, J. Lang & L. Xia, IJCAI 2011, §§5–6](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~conitzer/hypercubewiseIJCAI11.pdf) contrast two models on binary issues. One draws full, consistent linear orders from impartial culture. The other draws a uniform CP-net without imposing consistency, giving iid fair aggregate edge orientations for odd electorates. They prove a lower bound and give empirical comparisons. We compare existence asymptotically, not at every dimension. In the fair-edge case the Poisson limit is earlier random-game work: [Rinott & Scarsini](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0899825699907753); see also [Amiet et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10758) and [Quattropani & Scarsini](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08518). With two actions for each player and iid continuous payoffs, each edge uses two player-specific payoff entries that are used by no other edge, so the equivalence is exact.
