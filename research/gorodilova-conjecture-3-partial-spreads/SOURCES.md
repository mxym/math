# Sources, definitions and scope

1. A. A. Gorodilova, *A note on the properties of associated Boolean functions of quadratic APN functions*, Prikladnaya Diskretnaya Matematika 47 (2020), pp. 16–21, [DOI 10.17223/20710410/47/2](https://doi.org/10.17223/20710410/47/2), [journal record and full text](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/pdm691), [arXiv:2005.10788v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10788). The exact target is Section 2, Step 3, **Conjecture 3**, on printed journal page 20 and [preprint PDF page 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10788#page=6). Step 2 (journal pp. 19–20; preprint pp. 4–5) gives the defining table for \(N_0^{ij}\). The table ranges over the three nonzero points of each plane, not the common zero. Step 2 first specializes to a zero set of a component of the ortho-derivative. In contrast, the separate Conjecture 3 starts by defining \(M\) as the union of an arbitrary indexed family of 2-spaces, and states only the intersection and family-size hypotheses. It does not repeat the APN restriction. The explicit graph meets every hypothesis of that geometric formulation, not the more restrictive APN construction of Step 2.
2. C. Carlet and J. Thornburgh, *Resolving a conjecture on quadratic APN functions and a new quadratic \((n,n)\)-function associated to crooked functions*, [arXiv:2608.23888v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23888v2), Theorem 4.8 and Corollary 4.9, prove that every nonzero ortho-derivative component has degree \(n-2\) for quadratic APN maps in their stated dimensions. Their term “Gorodilova's conjecture” in that result means **Conjecture 2** of source 1 (their Conjecture 1.1), not the geometric Conjecture 3. Their proof also uses the fixed nonzero polar values of the APN plane families, which are absent in the geometric conjecture. It does not give our nonlinear all-even partial spreads. At \(n\ge5\), their theorem also excludes an APN component with linear zero set in odd dimension.
