# Sources and scope

- Joel J. Wallman and Steven T. Flammia, [*Randomized benchmarking with confidence*, arXiv:1404.6025v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.6025v4), New Journal of Physics 16, 103032 (2014), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/16/10/103032). Section VII, Definition 15 (PDF p. 23) defines \(n\)-contractivity as having at most one characteristic root on the unit circle. The conjecture immediately before Proposition 16 (PDF p. 24) concerns \(n=2\) for a nonunitary CPTP channel and any unitary 2-design; Proposition 16 proves the unital case and the discussion also settles qubits. We use the whole averaged conjugation of the tensor-power channel. It is not the compression onto the subspace fixed by \(U^{\otimes n}\).
- [TrureTuring, *Wallman–Flammia unrestricted two-contractivity*, frozen release identifier e5692c67d463](https://www.trureturing.org/research/wf1404/). This independently available Lean proof already settles the finite exact-design subgroup case at two copies. Its scope inventory explicitly leaves higher tensor powers, approximate designs and infinite/compact designs outside the proved statement. We credit their resolution of the named two-copy case, not a priority claim for solving it.
- Joel Wallman, Christopher Granade, Robin Harper and Steven T. Flammia, [*Estimating the Coherence of Noise*, arXiv:1503.07865v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.07865v4), New Journal of Physics 17, 113020 (2015), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/17/11/113020). Theorem 1 identifies unitarity with the trace-zero block Frobenius norm divided by \(d^2-1\), Proposition 3 computes the \(u\) entry on the two-copy invariant subspace, Proposition 7 is strict \(u<1\) for nonunitaries, and Proposition 9 gives the Choi-purity identity. Their Theorem 5 uses the invariant sector for the unitarity-benchmark fit. These standard ingredients are not new; the invariant-sector computation does not bound the whole higher-copy spectrum.
- Masaki Ogura and Clyde F. Martin, [*A limit formula for joint spectral radius with p-radius of probability distributions*, arXiv:1401.3026](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3026), Linear Algebra and its Applications 458, 605–625 (2014), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2014.06.034). Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 3.4 describe the even-\(p\) spectral-radius identity and the general comparison to \(p\)-radius for stochastic matrix products. The second-moment transfer is classical. Our use of CPTP uniform path bounds, the full trace-adapted tensor decomposition, the exact all-copy subleading radius and linear-in-copy-number mixing estimate are the quantum-channel claims here.
- [*Characterizing quantum channels from local-unitary invariants*, arXiv:2606.03722v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03722v1) uses higher moments of local-unitary invariants to distinguish quantum channels; it does not give an all-copy spectral comparison for an independently iterated channel ensemble.

Bounded checks on 2026-11-27 used the exact Wallman–Flammia title and words “n-contractive”, “two-contractivity”, “tensor powers”, “arbitrary copies”, “twirled tensor channel”, “unitarity”, “spectral radius”, “mean-square stability”, and “p-radius”. We found the overlapping two-copy solution and the standard random-product and unitarity sources above, but no matching whole-spectrum all-copy theorem. No priority claim is made.

The channel dimensions are finite. Arbitrary iid CPTP ensembles are covered by the comparison theorem, and the exact-design formula is a separate specialization. No claim is made for correlated circuits, arbitrary gate-dependent randomized benchmarking sequences, or a channel-independent gap near unitary channels.
