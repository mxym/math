# Independent internal review: soup-kitchen fractional and rare-stockout theorems

Reviewed frozen proof f57df2d33678536e14777ba7863e3af2661348768e66bb93b211c8770cd0c126. PASS for both claims in the explicitly stated scopes. This is not external peer review.

The [original v3 conjecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19292v3) compares up-to-unit independent demand at a given supply with an ordinary Poisson sum. Its soup model defines availability by the strict remaining-supply event, hence stockout includes equality, and admits real supply. The distinct application with true exclusion probability and supply K versus threshold kappa=K−1 is not asserted to be solved by the result here.

For noninteger K>1, writing r=ceil(K), the demand-size K/r is less than one and reaches the stockout threshold at exactly r positive occurrences; the unit Poisson also crosses at count r. Matching binomial and Poisson tails by bracketing means yields convergence of their bounded throughput functions. The rescaled-binomial limit has smaller throughput already at a Poisson count of one, an event with positive probability at every interior stockout target. Raising some nonzero jump sizes to one does not affect the event: at most r−1 such jumps still cannot reach K, while at least r reach it even without upgrades. For the explicit rational three-diner instance, masses 27,27,9,1 over 64 give availability 27/32 and relative throughput 47/128. At lambda=log 2, the Poisson counterparts are (1+log 2)/2 and 1/2−(log 2)/6. The rational log bounds in the proof are strict and make both greater. Monotonicity in lambda means every Poisson benchmark misses at least one desired inequality. No claim for the all-target integer K≥2 question.

The two-jump optimization is valid on all finite nonnegative measures with prescribed first moment. The low part gives no internal products, so for fixed high measure and low first moment one can replace it by a single low atom to approach the corresponding linear supremum. Its zero-cross case is bounded by the high-only measure. For one low atom x, the remaining high atoms can be moved to K/2 and K−x without reducing the number of qualifying ordered pairs or raising cost. Renormalize so cost is one. The payoff is convex in the proportion between these two high marks, at fixed low mass; an endpoint suffices. The remaining scalar maximum is 1/[x(2K−3x)]. The denominator of this reciprocal is concave, so its minimum is at K−1 or K/2, giving exactly the two claimed coefficients. The crossover equation is (1−c)(1−5c)=0. Meester's fixed-mean two-iid-draw result already gives this optimization and crossover; the new scope is the uniform many-variable low-stockout law.

With total mean mu, the bounded-demand product gives T≥1−exp(−mu), so along nearly minimizing sequences with small T the mean must vanish. For all such sequences T=mu+O(mu²), also by the direct product bound. When a fixed eta lies below K−1 and a fixed d<eta/3, the truncated small values' exponential bound gives O(mu^(eta/d)), uniformly in the number and law of terms. Three large jumps have probability O(mu³) by the factorial moment bound. On the complement of those events stockout requires exactly two large jumps with sum at least K−eta. The cross-pair union bound is at most half the product-measure pair count, because diagonal terms are nonnegative. Take mu small at fixed eta,d and then eta down to zero. This is valid at K=2 by left continuity. Existence of finite designs for every sufficiently small target follows from independent common thinning of an attaining mark law, continuity, and the strictly positive second-order coefficient. Keeping N fixed in that derivation and only subsequently taking N arbitrarily large removes the finite distinct-pair penalty. Taking near minimizers then proves the matching lower asymptote without mistakenly inferring vanishing mean just from vanishing stockout.
