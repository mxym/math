# The conjecture, prior results, and search boundaries

* **Exact target.** Aadityan Ganesh, Jason Hartline, Atanu R. Sinha and Matthew vonAllmen, [*Fundamental Limits of Throughput and Availability*, EC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3670865.3673553), [authors' full arXiv version 3 (July 15, 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19292v3). The unnumbered conjecture in the introduction's “Characterization of Throughput versus Availability” concerns independently distributed demands in $[0,1]$ and asks for an ordinary Poisson total demand giving two componentwise lower metrics at the same supply. The preceding discussion identifies the ordinary Poisson distribution with the many-rare **unit**-demand limit. Neither the displayed conjecture nor §2.1 bounds the supply to integers; the neighboring Theorems 2–3 explicitly state real inequalities on the supply. The $\ge$ convention for stockout, $<$ for availability, and capped **total** consumption, not an all-or-nothing bin-packing reward, are specified in §2.1. Figures 1–3 are conceptual/proof diagrams; Figures 4–5 use integer illustrative capacities 100 and 5. Their separate posted-price application (§3.1) considers a divisible good with $K\in\mathbb R_+$ and a different event $\delta=\Pr(\bigcup_i\{D-D_i>K-1\})$; Figure 6 uses $K=101,10$ and effective $\kappa=K-1$. We do not identify that event with stockout, produce a counterexample to their proved concentration inequalities, or assert that their integer figures are wrong. The notes on limitations and open questions in §5 again distinguish the known unit-demand result from the open fractional-demand benchmark. Our Theorem A concerns the ordinary Poisson at noninteger supply. It leaves the full integer-supply version at $K\ge2$ undecided.
* **Unit-demand predecessor.** Shuchi Chawla, Nikhil R. Devanur and Thodoris Lykouris, [*Static pricing for multi-unit prophet inequalities*, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.07990), optimize utilization for Bernoulli (zero/one) demands at a given availability. Ganesh et al. explicitly attribute the unit-demand frontier to this work. A scaled Bernoulli is an admissible *up-to-unit* demand; its total need not be integer. The Poisson approximation in Theorem A is classical.
* **Exact prior for the two-jump variational problem.** Ludolf E. Meester, [*Extremal distributions for tail probabilities of sums of iid random variables on [0,1]*, arXiv:0808.1669 (2008)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.1669), especially §3.3, Eq. (18) and the summary table on printed p.13; [related IWAP 2008 publication record](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/extremal-distributions-for-the-sum-of-two-iid-random-variables-on/). Meester solves the exact **two iid bounded variables of prescribed mean** problem and identifies an additional three-point regime. For the upper tail of $X+X'$, put $Y=1-X$, $t'=2-K$, $m=1-\mathbb EX$. For $\mathbb EX=u\downarrow0$, the two applicable entries in that table are $u^2/[1-(2-K)^2]$ and $u^2/(K/2)^2$, with the threshold $t'=4/5$, i.e., $K=6/5$. Complementing the supports gives $\{0,K-1,1\}$ versus $\{0,K/2\}$. After homogeneity and dilution by an atom at zero, this explicitly implies the two-jump kernel bound in the proof. Neither that kernel nor its transition or the two optimizing supports should be described as discoveries here. The additional step is the **uniform** sparse reduction of arbitrarily many, possibly nonidentical demands to that prior two-draw problem, inversion at a prescribed probability of stockout, and matching finite-input constructions. No other conjecture of Meester is addressed.
* **Related bounds and asymptotics.** The unbounded nonnegative iid mean/tail problem, including the result of Hoeffding–Shrikhande for two observations, is compared in [Łuczak, Mieczkowska and Šileikis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03547). Bounded support matters here. Conditioning on the number of large jumps in a small-parameter limit is classical; compare [Figueroa-López–Houdré, *Small-time expansions for the transition distributions of Lévy processes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.0849). A fixed compound Poisson or Lévy law's expansion is not a uniform extremal theorem over all finite independent arrays. At the endpoint $K=2$, the unit coefficient in the leading tail bound can also be recovered from Ganesh et al.'s own Theorem 3 using the positive-part function $(x-1)_+$, since for $Z\sim{\rm Pois}(T)$ its expectation is $T^2/2+O(T^3)$, with matching rare units. The fractional interior is where the correction is needed.
