# Two basis conditions on each nine-variable cubic orbit

Only the top degree matters. The degree-d part of a Boolean ANF, modulo degrees <d, transforms as an exterior d-form: products of transformed linear forms have as each degree-d coefficient the determinant of the corresponding minor in characteristic two; repeated indices lower degree after squarefree reduction. Translations do not alter the top form. Thus any certified linear basis change suffices for the original function, with its arbitrary lower terms.

For a cubic top form T write T(a,b,c) for its trilinear alternating evaluation. In a basis B=(b_i), the ANF top coefficient on i,j,k is T(b_i,b_j,b_k). A nonzero direction v is fast exactly when its contraction with T vanishes (the quadratic top of the derivative is the contraction). An affine hyperplane given by a nonzero covector h drops the degree precisely when h wedges T to zero: in coordinates h=x_1 this says every term of T contains x_1. The constant defining the affine translate has no effect.

**OP2 in degree 3 (and OP1 in degree 6).** In positive rows of op2-certificates.json, for each i the pair j,k certifies that T(b_j,b_k,b_l) equals the Kronecker delta of i and l. This is exactly a top monomial with private complementary pair relative to i as required by CFS Corollary 8. The covectors obtained form a basis, hence no fast direction exists. Each of the other 32 rows gives a nonzero v with T(v,-,-)=0 instead. These are the zero form and 31 nonzero degenerate orbits. All 317 nondegenerate orbits pass: 308 slice constructions, 4 standard bases and 5 changes found by elementary transvections.

A useful sufficient construction for the cubic side is as follows. If i_s T has radical precisely the line of s, take a,b with T(s,a,b)=1. The covector beta=T(a,b,-) has beta(s)=1 and kills a,b. On W=ker(beta) the symplectic form (i_s T)|W is nondegenerate: V is the direct sum of W and the line of s, and the latter is the whole radical in V. Complete a,b to a symplectic basis of W. The pair (a,b) gives the dual of s; each other dual basis covector is the contraction of s and the corresponding symplectic partner. This explains many certificates but is not needed by their direct verifier.

**OP1 in degree 3 (and OP2 in degree 6).** In positive rows of op1-certificates.json, for every i a four-set Q containing i has exactly one nonzero triple on its faces, Q minus i. Thus the monomial indexed by Q minus i does not contain i and replacing any one of its variables by i gives an absent term. This is CFS Theorem 5; it also makes the coefficient on Q of any h wedge T equal h_i in that basis. The 344 affirmative rows break down as 300 standard and 44 other bases. The remaining five rows (four nonzero and the zero row) are excluded by the provided nonzero h with h wedge T=0.

**Complement and coverage.** In dual coordinate bases the degree-(9-d) complement sends a d-set to its complementary index set. Intrinsically this is the exterior identification using the unique nonzero volume element over F2: a basis change corresponds on the complementary side to its inverse transpose. It is a bijection of GL orbits, not an assertion that identical coordinate matrices work on both sides. Contraction zero for T corresponds to wedge zero on its complementary form and vice versa. In the monomial conditions, the same excluded root/replacement condition becomes the included-root/private-face condition after taking complements. Thus the degree-3 OP2 and OP1 checks give, respectively, degree-6 OP1 and OP2 in n=9. The formal zero orbit is accounted for in the finite input, but is not a degree-3 or degree-6 function.

The 349 representative forms, including zero, are taken as a complete orbit system from Brier and Langevin (see UPSTREAM.md). The rank/basis/monomial tests prove the claim on each orbit for this external input; the verifier does not reprove or silently assume orbit completeness from the mere count. This does not settle other dimensions or all degrees in nine variables.
