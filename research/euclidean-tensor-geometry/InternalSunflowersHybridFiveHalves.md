# Hybrid \(m^{5/2}\) pure-pair congestion for rank-four internal sunflowers

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal mathematical checks; no external peer review claimed.

Let \(H\) be a finite simple four-partite four-uniform family with coordinates \(A,B,C,D\). The problem is the internal-witness version of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). Write \(\tau\) for a projected \(ABD\) triple and \(f_\tau\) for its number of \(C\)-extensions. Classify an occupied \(\tau\) as light if \(f_\tau<3m\) and heavy otherwise, for an integer \(m\ge2\). For a fixed pure pair \(u\) of types \(AB,AD,BD\), define
\[
b(u)=\sum_{\tau\supseteq u:\,f_\tau<3m} f_\tau
+\#\{\tau\supseteq u:f_\tau\ge3m\}.
\]
Suppose \(\max_c\deg_H(c)\le Lm^{5/2}\) and \(\max_u b(u)\le Lm^{5/2}\). Then \(H\) has a subfamily of size \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\) internally witnessing all pairwise intersections, including the empty kernel. No \(C\)-cross cap, no ratio or minimum on the fiber sizes, and no degree assumption on \(A,B,D\) is needed. In particular:
* With arbitrary fiber sizes it suffices that all three pair codegrees in the simple \(ABD\) shadow are \(O(m^{3/2})\).
* If every occupied fiber has at least \(3m\) extensions, the simple shadow can have all three pair codegrees \(O(m^{5/2})\) and arbitrary variations in fiber sizes.
* Ordinary physical pure-pair caps \(O(m^{5/2})\) suffice as a special case. The hybrid cap may instead allow arbitrarily large physical pure-pair multiplicities when the mass lies in wide fibers.

This is not the unrestricted rank-four theorem, nor the theorem with just one bounded side.

## Lemma: weighted linear triples, nonempty kernels

A simple linear tripartite triple system, with arbitrary nonnegative edge weights of total \(W\), has a nonempty-valid subfamily of weight \(\Omega(W/m^{3/2})\). A linear subfamily is nonempty-valid exactly when every positive vertex degree is either 1 or at least \(m\). The exponent is the same as in the [unweighted sharp linear theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/a236b148148a706c0fa5e544bd544666830c84dc/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/LinearTripleInternalSunflowers.md), but is needed here uniformly under arbitrary weights.

First repeatedly delete any positive vertex degree below \(m\), charging each removed edge to the side of the deleting vertex. If the remaining core has weight at least \(W/2\), it suffices. Otherwise choose the heaviest of the three charged pieces; it retains at least \(W/6\) and one side, say \(X\), has degree bounded by \(m\). Each concrete vertex is charged once, no matter how much weight was incident to it initially. Reset \(W\) to the current weight.

Let \(p=1/(8\sqrt m)\) and \(K=\lceil64m/p\rceil\). For each of the other two sides \(Y,Z\), sum the masses of the \(K\) heaviest incident edges per vertex (or all if fewer). If one side's sum exceeds \(W/64\), restrict to its heaviest lists. Now two sides, called leaves, have degrees bounded by \(K\); call the third the center side. We prove a weighted \(\Omega(1/K)\) retention by disjoint stars. If the edges at centers of degree below \(2m\) have at least half the restricted weight, their maximum degree over all three sides is at most \(\max(K,2m)\), so greedy coloring of the edge-conflict graph with \(O(K+m)\) colors gives a matching of weight \(\Omega(W/K)\), absorbing the fixed restriction loss. Otherwise work on the remaining high centers. Activate centers independently at \(q=1/[128(K+m)]\), initially keeping every edge of an active center. Delete each initially kept edge with a competitor at either leaf in the initial sample. A competitor has a different center by linearity, so the conditional deletion probability per edge is at most \(2Kq\). Also delete each entire center left with fewer than \(m\) edges. Given its activation, the expected fraction of lost petals in a center is at most \(2Kq\), and to shrink below \(m\) it must lose at least half its petals, by its original degree. Markov bounds the additional rejection probability by \(4Kq\). Each original edge survives with probability at least \(q(1-6Kq)\), regardless of its weight. The surviving stars are vertex-disjoint, and each has at least \(m\) edges. This proves the concentrated case.

In the diffuse case, independently sample labels on \(Y,Z\) at probability \(p\), keep edges with both labels sampled, and delete every initially kept edge having a competitor at its \(X\) label in the initial sample. By linearity such a rival has both other labels distinct, hence conditional activity probability \(p^2\). The loss is at most \(mp^2=1/64\) conditional on the given edge appearing. Thus expected retained weight before further peeling is at least \((63/64)p^2W\). Repeatedly peel positive \(Y,Z\) degrees below \(m\). For any given label \(v\) of one of these sides, charge this peel against its initial sample. Among the original top \(K\) at \(v\), each edge is initially active with probability \(p^2\). Each other edge weighs at most \(W_v/K\) for total original incident mass \(W_v\), and at most \(m\) such edges can be charged, only when \(v\) was active. Thus expected cost per side is at most
\[
p^2W/64+pmW/K\le p^2W/32.
\]
An outcome retains \(\Omega(p^2W)=\Omega(W/m)\), and the surviving \(X\) labels are private while each remaining \(Y,Z\) label has at least \(m\) incident edges. This proves the lemma in both cases.

## Nonempty lifting with physical light counts and simple heavy counts

The nonempty argument applies to the original host and to the residual after reserving an \(m\)-matching, keeping the original classification in either case. In a residual, an originally heavy \(\tau\) that avoids all deleted pure vertices can lose at most \(m\) of its \(C\)-extensions (they have distinct \(C\) labels), hence has at least \(2m\) unless its entire projection was removed. A surviving original light \(\tau\) still has fewer than \(3m\) extensions. The light physical and heavy simple counts, and every \(C\) degree, only decrease.

Write \(E'\) for the edge count of the host under consideration, and \(r=1/[128 Lm^{5/2}]\), increasing \(L\) to at least 1. Use the light or the heavy piece, choosing whichever has more physical edges.

**Light.** Independently activate individual physical edges at probability \(r\), and delete every initially active edge with an initially active competitor sharing its \(C\) label or any pure pair. Conditional on activation of that edge, the union bound for all four kinds of conflict is at most \(4Lm^{5/2}r\). Thus an outcome of weight \(\Omega_L(E'/m^{5/2})\) survives. The surviving \(C\) labels are private and the pure shadow is simple and linear. Apply the weighted linear lemma (or its unweighted case) and lift by the unique extension in each surviving projection.

**Heavy.** Activate each occupied distinct \(\tau\) independently at probability \(r\), initially keeping its full reservoir of \(C\)-extensions. Drop any activated projection with an initially active competitor sharing a pure pair, an event of conditional probability at most \(3Lm^{5/2}r\). Also remove any physical edge sharing its \(C\) label with another initially activated projection. Given its own projection was active, each competitor in this latter test is in a different projection, as simplicity precludes repeats within a reservoir, so the conditional probability per edge is at most \(Lm^{5/2}r\). Discard an activated projection if its reservoir shrinks below \(m\) because of this \(C\) test; since its reservoir initially had at least \(2m\), given activation Markov bounds this event by \(2Lm^{5/2}r\). All rejection rules may be evaluated against the initial selection, so conditional dependence between them is immaterial. Each physical edge survives with probability at least \(r(1-6Lm^{5/2}r)\). In a good outcome the remaining projections form a linear simple shadow; they have mutually private \(C\) reservoirs each of size at least \(m\). Apply the weighted linear lemma to that shadow, weighting a projection by its surviving reservoir size, then retain every extension of each selected projection. This yields physical size \(\Omega_L(E'/m^4)\). A repeated pure projection has its own \(m\)-petal witness; a singleton pure intersection of two projections lifts a singleton witness by choosing one extension per witnessing projection. There are no other nonempty kernels because the shadow is linear and \(C\) is globally private.

In either piece this proves the nonempty claim. The same proof actually gives nonempty \(\Omega(E'/(m^{3/2}\max\{1,D,B\}))\) if \(D,B\) are arbitrary maximum \(C\) degree and hybrid congestion; no other use of the common \(Lm^{5/2}\) scale is made.

## Empty-kernel restoration

If an \(m\)-matching leaves at least half of \(H\) after deleting its vertices, run the nonempty argument on the residue and restore the reserved matching. The restored edges are mutually disjoint and disjoint from the output found on the residue, so they only introduce empty intersections, supplying their own witness. Original heavy reservoirs that remain occupied have at least \(2m\), as checked above.

Otherwise, if an \(m\)-matching exists, its \(4m\) endpoints meet more than half the original edges, so one vertex has degree at least \(|H|/(8m)\). If no \(m\)-matching exists, the at most \(4(m-1)\) endpoints of a maximal shorter matching cover all edges and yield the same degree lower bound. Apply the [full rank-three theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) to the simple link of that vertex, retaining an \(\Omega(m^{-3})\) fraction of its edges, and lift by the fixed vertex. Every link intersection lifts together with its witnessing sunflower. This is exactly the desired rank-four scale.

## Scope checks

If each pair in the simple projected shadow lies in at most \(M\) projected triples, then \(b(u)\le 3mM\), so \(M=O(m^{3/2})\) gives the arbitrary-fiber corollary. With all projections heavy the factor \(3m\) disappears and \(M=O(m^{5/2})\) suffices. This goes beyond the previous \(O(m^{1/3})\) equal shadow-cap case but does not generally improve its asymmetric parameter criterion. For example, one already gains hosts with all three shadow codegrees about \(m^{5/2}\), \(C\)-cross multiplicities about \(m^2\), and highly variable medium/wide heavy reservoirs: use a complete tripartite shadow on \(n=\lfloor m^{5/2}\rfloor\) labels per side; for each \(A\) label partition its \(B,D\) assignments into batches of at most \(m^2\), giving each batch a distinct private \(C\) reservoir (choose its size per batch, at least \(3m\), with no upper bound). All triples in a batch use that whole reservoir. Those batches attain the simultaneous large congestion scales without violating either hypothesis.
