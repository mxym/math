# Concentrated endpoint locks for rank-four internal sunflowers

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Let \(H\) be a simple 4-partite 4-uniform family with pure sides \(X,Y,Z\) and complementary side \(C\). Each occupied triple \(\tau\) on the pure sides has \(f_\tau\) extensions. Let \(E=|H|\), let each \(C\)-degree be at most \(Lm^2\), and assume each occupied \(XZ,YZ\) bucket has physical mass at least \(Qm^3\) (its mass is the sum of the \(f_\tau\)'s). The \(XY\) mass is unrestricted. Here all lower-bound constants can be doubled when needed below. Each of the following gives a subfamily of size \(\Omega(E/m^4)\) internally witnessing all pairwise intersections by \(m\)-sunflowers, including the empty intersection; the big-Omega constants can depend on the fixed constants in the hypotheses.

1. \(f_\tau\le Um^2\) and each \(X\) and \(Y\) has \(O(m^2)\) distinct \(Z\) neighbors. No \(C\)-cross bound. Alternatively impose the quadratic support cap on \(X\) only and require the total mass of the \(B'm^3\) heaviest \(YZ\) buckets per \(Y\), summed over \(Y\), to be at most \(\varepsilon E\) for sufficiently large \(B'\) and small \(\varepsilon\).
2. \(f_\tau\le Um^2\), each \(X,Y\) has \(O(m^3)\) distinct \(Z\) neighbors, and every \(CZ\) pair has codegree at most \(Lm\). Other \(C\)-cross pairs are unrestricted.
3. \(f_\tau\le Um\), the \(X\to Z\) support cap is \(O(m^2)\) and \(Y\to Z\) is \(O(m^3)\). Require only that each \(CYZ\) triple has codegree at most \(Lm\); no linear bound on a \(C\)-cross pair is needed.

These complement the [diffuse endpoint/representative-first theorem](InternalSunflowersMiddleFibersDenseShadow.md) for [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). A complementary no-C-cross variant with large \(CYZ\) fibers is below. These results do not address arbitrary endpoint concentration or solve unrestricted rank four.

## Quadratic support: hard locks

Activate \(Z\) labels independently at \(p=\alpha/m^2\) with small \(\alpha>0\). Retain a projected triple only if its \(Z\) is the sole initially active neighbor at its capped \(X\) and \(Y\) endpoints. Given its own \(Z\) activation, each lock fails with probability at most \(B\alpha\) for support cap \(Bm^2\). Independently choose one \(C\) representative uniformly from each reservoir and activate the triple within \(Z\) with probability \(\beta f_\tau/m^2\), with \(\beta\) small. Delete every initial \(C\) collision; conditional on a target and its proposed \(C\), a rival uses that same label at probability at most \(\beta/m^2\), even in the same \(Z\). The loss bound is thus \(L\beta\). These tests yield \(\Omega(p\beta E/m^2)\) expected survivors. Peel every positive high bucket below \(m\) projected triples, charging at most \(m\) per bucket and only when its \(Z\) activates. Total expected cost is at most \(2pmE/(Qm^3)\), negligible with large \(Q\). This gives the desired count for nonempty witnesses.

Both \(X,Y\) are tied to unique \(Z\), so no singleton for either can arise. Low \(XY\) cannot repeat among distinct projected triples, because \(X\) is locked. High pairs have \(m\) distinct completions and each positive \(Z\) has a bipartite \(XY\) link of minimum positive degree \(m\), hence an \(m\)-matching. The representatives make these pure witnesses valid in \(H\).

In the one-lock variant, do not lock \(Y\). Instead impose an initial cap of \(4\beta F/m^2\) proposed triples in each \(YZ\) bucket of original mass \(F\), discarding the full bucket if its cap fails. Conditioned on a target proposed, this costs at most \(1/4+1/(4\beta F/m^2)\), by Markov, so a fixed expected fraction still survives. Interleave an extra peel of positive \(Y\) having fewer than \(m\) surviving \(Z\) neighbors. Put \(U'=\lceil B'm^3\rceil\). Each \(Y\) is charged fewer than \(m\) buckets, each of count at most \(4\beta F/m^2\); original top lists activate at probability \(p\) and sum to at most \(\varepsilon E\), and each other bucket contributes mass at most the original \(Y\)-mass divided by \(U'\). The expected cost is at most \((4\beta/m^2)(p\varepsilon E+mE/U')\), negligible for large \(B'\) and small \(\varepsilon\). A positive \(Y\) has \(m\) \(Z\) neighbors and distinct \(X\) representatives because each \(X\) is locked; these give the additional singleton witness.

## Cubic support with a \(CZ\) bound

Use \(p=\alpha/m^3\) and lock both endpoints as before. Inside an active \(Z\), independently activate a triple with probability \(\beta f_\tau/m\) if \(f_\tau<2m\) and \(\beta f_\tau/m^2\) otherwise. In the first case propose one uniform \(C\) label, in the second a uniform \(2m\)-subset; small \(\beta\) ensures all rates are at most one. An individual original label is proposed with probability \(\beta/m\) or \(2\beta/m\) respectively. Conditional on a target and all its proposals, a particular proposed label has a conflicting proposal with probability at most \(2\beta L+2\beta p Lm\), splitting competitors by same \(Z\) (\(CZ\)-cap) or other \(Z\) (\(C\)-degree). Remove all such collisions and discard an entire heavy triple if it loses more than \(m\) proposals. The latter conditional probability is at most twice the per-label loss bound by Markov. Remove failed locks simultaneously on the initial outer sample, yielding expected retained **physical** count \(\Omega(pE/m)\). Peel positive high buckets below \(m\) distinct projected completions. Each charge removes at most \(2m^2\) physical edges and only when its \(Z\) activates; the total expected cost is at most \(4pm^2 E/(Qm^3)\), negligible. The same pure witnesses lift because \(C\) labels are disjoint between different projected triples; any repeated pure triple has at least \(m\) surviving \(C\)'s. Both \(X,Y\) remain locked.

## Staggered locks with only a \(CYZ\) cap

Activate \(Z\) at \(p=\alpha/m^2\). Hard-lock \(X\) as in the quadratic regime. Conditioned on the full active set, at each \(Y\) independently choose at most one active neighboring \(Z\), giving each such neighbor exact marginal \(r=1/(Tm)\) if there are at most \(Tm\) active neighbors, and otherwise choose none. The law with these marginals exists since their sum is at most one. Given target \(Z\) active, the expected active-neighbor count at its \(Y\) is at most \(1+\alpha Bm\) for support cap \(Bm^3\), so the overflow loss is small for sufficiently large \(T\). The \(X\) hard lock costs \(O(\alpha)\), before the \(Y\)-assignment. Independently at each projected triple choose one uniform \(C\) and activate with probability \(\beta f_\tau/m\); this uses \(f_\tau\le Um\).

For initial \(C\)-collision tests, condition on the full \(Z\)-sample and the target's \(Y\)-assignment and proposed label. Rivals with the same \(Y\) in a different \(Z\) cannot occur. Those in its same \(YZ\) bucket and using its \(C\) correspond to at most \(Lm\) occurrences, giving \(O(\beta L)\). Rivals at other \(Y\) have assignment probability at most \(r\), independently of the target's, and use that \(C\) at probability \(\beta/m\), giving conditional loss at most \(r\beta Lm\). Thus the expected surviving count is \(\Omega(pr\beta E/m)\). Peel high buckets below \(m\) cell counts at expected cost \(2pmE/(Qm^3)\), small compared to the expected count when \(Q\) is large relative to \(T/\beta\). Again \(X,Y\) each tie to a unique \(Z\), so the same witnesses work, and \(pr\beta E/m\) is of order \(E/m^4\).

## Empty intersections

In each regime, double the lower threshold \(Q\) in advance and, in the one-lock diffuse variant, halve \(\varepsilon\). If an \(m\)-matching can be reserved whose endpoint deletion leaves at least half the edges, apply the nonempty theorem to that residue. For a surviving high pair, the losses from deleting at most \(m\) labels in its remaining pure class and at most \(m\) \(C\)-labels are bounded by \(Um^3+Lm^3\). Support and codegree caps are hereditary, and top-list masses (as absolute budgets) cannot increase. Restoring the matching adds only empty intersections and their witness. Otherwise the endpoints of any \(m\)-matching cover at least half the edges, or the endpoints of a maximal shorter matching cover all edges. One vertex then has degree at least \(E/(8m)\). The established [rank-three theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) applied to its full link gives \(\Omega(E/m^4)\) after putting that vertex back. The rank-three empty kernel reappears as the singleton fixed vertex, as needed.


## Complementary no-cross variant: keep a common \(C\) inside one \(YZ\)

Retain the asymmetric support assumptions of the staggered regime: \(f_\tau\le Um\), \(X\to Z\) degree at most \(Bm^2\), \(Y\to Z\) degree at most \(Bm^3\), \(C\)-degree at most \(Lm^2\), and all positive \(XZ\) physical masses at least \(Qm^3\). Replace the \(CYZ\) upper cap by a lower bound: each occupied \(CYZ\) fiber has at least \(Km\) distinct \(X\) completions. Assume each occupied \(YZ\) has either at most \(B_0m\) occupied labels of \(C\), or has at most an \(\eta\) fraction of its physical mass in its top \(t_0=\lceil Q' m^2\rceil\) \(CYZ\) fibers. There is no \(C\)-cross upper cap, and no separate \(YZ\) physical-mass minimum in this variant. For suitable constants the full \(\Omega(E/m^4)\) result holds.

In a diffuse \(YZ\) bucket trim those top \(t_0\) fibers once (losing \(\le\eta E\) overall); remaining fibers in a bucket of original mass \(F\) weigh at most \(F/t_0\). Use the same staggered \(Z\)-activation and endpoint assignments with \(p=\alpha/m^2\), \(r=1/(Tm)\). In contrast to proposing individual representatives of \(XYZ\), activate entire \(CYZ\) fibers independently at rate \(u=\beta/m\), selecting all their extensions. For a target already sampled and with its \(Y\) assigned to that \(Z\), eliminate conflicts of its \(C\) with another initially selected \(CYZ\) group. Rivals with its own \(Y\) are impossible (the assignment is unique and each fixed \(CYZ\) is one group), and rivals at other \(Y\) occur with conditional probability at most \(ru\), even in the same \(Z\). There are at most \(Lm^2\) of them. Delete duplicate \(XYZ\) proposals (cost at most \(uUm\)), and in a short \(YZ\) bucket require that exactly one \(C\) group initially activates (cost at most \(uB_0m\) conditional on the target). For \(\beta\) small, these tests and the lock losses leave expected mass \(\Omega(pruE)\). They make \(C\) unique to each retained group, although it can repeat within that group's fiber.

Interleave three peels. A positive \(CYZ\) with fewer than \(m\) \(X\) completions charges fewer than \(m\) physical edges, and is only charged if its \(Z,Y\)-assignment and group activated (probability at most \(pru\)); its expected total cost is at most \(pruE/K\). A positive \(XZ\) with fewer than \(m\) edges costs at most \(pmE/(Qm^3)\), using the original bucket minimum and activation of its \(Z\). A diffuse \(YZ\) with fewer than \(m\) surviving groups costs at most \(prmE/t_0\) in expectation, using the original mass and requiring its \(Y\)-assignment. The three costs are negligible relative to \(pruE\) with sufficiently large \(K,Q,Q'\). There is then an outcome of weight \(\Omega(E/m^4)\).

To witness an \(XZ\) pair use \(m\) distinct \(Y\) labels (duplicate \(XYZ\) is absent), whose \(C\)'s are in distinct groups. A short \(YZ\) uses only one \(C\), so it cannot occur as an intersection without that \(C\); in diffuse \(YZ\), choose \(m\) distinct groups and choose a fresh \(X\) from each group's at least \(m\) completions. A repeated \(CYZ\) has its own \(m\) petals. A singleton \(Z\) uses an \(XY\)-matching of size \(m\) in its projected link: every positive \(X\) has at least \(m\) \(Y\)'s, and every positive \(Y\) has at least \(m\) \(X\)'s from any surviving group. Distinct \(Y\)'s also have distinct \(C\)'s. Since both \(X,Y\) are locked to \(Z\) and \(C\) to \(YZ\), these exhaust nonempty possible intersections.

As before reserve a matching or use the high-degree link. A fixed surviving \(CYZ\) loses at most \(m\) \(X\) completions. A fixed surviving \(XZ\) or \(YZ\) loses at most \((U+L)m^3\) mass. For an originally diffuse \(YZ\), the original mass is at least \(Km t_0\), since it has more than \(t_0\) fibers; its relative top-list bound survives with doubled tolerance after removal of at most \((U+L)m^3\) edges, with \(K,Q'\) sufficiently large and \(\eta\) appropriately halved at the start. Thus matching restoration gives the full result.
