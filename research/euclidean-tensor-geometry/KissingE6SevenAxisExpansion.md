# Seven common axes force D5: the A₃+A₁ rotation atlas

In a six-dimensional kissing configuration, two distinct E₆ root systems whose axes are jointly represented and which share **at least seven** axes must intersect in D₅ (20 axes). The earlier [eight-axis theorem](KissingE6EightAxisExpansion.md) covered all cases except exactly seven.

Independently, two complete D₅ spin layers and at least **27 of the 40 signed equatorial D₅ roots** preclude 73 points. Together these give strict blocker expansion and two vertex-disjoint negative outsider edges throughout the 73-point complete-E₆ branch **p≥7**. None of the statements asserts that p=7 is impossible or that the unrestricted kissing number is 72.

## Which seven-axis subsystem?

A simply-laced common root system of rank at most six with seven axes is A₃+A₁, 2A₂+A₁, or A₂+4A₁. For each of the 360 nonorthogonal pairs among E₆'s 36 axes, the [exact checker](verify-kissing-e6-seven-axis-expansion.js) finds six perpendicular axes with nonorthogonality graph consisting of two triangles, hence a perpendicular 2A₂. Four orthogonal A₁'s are impossible there. In the 2A₂+A₁ case the two A₂'s use disjoint factors, and their perpendicular space contains a third A₂ in each E₆: after choosing the first A₂, the second is one of the two perpendicular A₂ factors. The common A₁ fixes one line of the third A₂ in the plane. An A₂ containing a fixed line in a given plane is unique (the other two unoriented lines are at 60°). There would already be nine common axes. Only A₃+A₁ requires a new calculation.

## Normalize the common flat

Let K=A₃+A₁. The full root subsystem in its four-dimensional span in either E₆ is K itself: the only rank-four ADE determinant dividing det(K)=8 with square quotient is 8, for K. Choose a vector perpendicular to this span but to no other roots. Moving it to the dominant chamber shows K becomes parabolic, generated by four simple roots on an A₃+A₁ subdiagram. Match a chosen *ordered* simple basis of K to the chamber's ordered simple roots using its own Weyl group, allowing both possible orders of the A₃ endpoints.

Use simple nodes 0–1–2–3–4 and 5 attached to 2. All eight ordered parabolic A₃+A₁ subdiagrams (endpoint, middle, endpoint, isolated) lie in the full automorphism orbit of J=(0,1,2,4); the checker explicitly finds all eight in its 51,840-element ordered orbit. Thus we can identify the simple bases of two copies of K **pointwise**, leaving only an orthogonal transformation of their common perpendicular plane.

The plane has perpendicular basis h=(1,2,3,2,1,2), v=(1,2,3,4,2,0)/3 in simple-root coefficients, with squared lengths 2 and 4/3. For a root r, r·h=r₅ and r·v=r₃−r₅. Reflection in h is a root-system symmetry fixing K pointwise; composition eliminates the determinant-minus possibility, and conjugation identifies θ and −θ. It suffices to vary a rotation 0≤θ≤π.

For positive-root representatives r,s of two axes let m=r·s before rotation, and set
```
D=2r₅s₅+3(r₃−r₅)(s₃−s₅),  C=4m−D,  E=r₃s₅−r₅s₃.
```
After rotation, four times the cross product equals C+D cosθ+√6 E sinθ. Substitute y=√6 tan(θ/2), so cosθ=(6−y²)/(6+y²) and √6 sinθ=12y/(6+y²). For t=−8,−4,4,8, the corresponding threshold polynomial has coefficients C−t−D, 12E, 6(C−t+D).

As in the [A₄ atlas](KissingE6A4SpinTen.md), products strictly between 1 and 2 in absolute value force a sign choice, and at absolute value 2 one can choose the same actual representative on the common axis. Propagate these equalities/oppositions on the 36+36 reference roots. All 14 finite nonnegative algebraic endpoints are isolated by rational intervals, with integer sign comparisons also at the endpoints themselves. Every open interval fails and the only consistent finite endpoint is y=0 (identical systems). At y=∞ the graph is consistent with 20 common axes, already covered by the eight-axis theorem. This finishes the mixed assertion.

## A thirteenth omitted D₅ sign

Use the same two 16-point parity layers and the [center classification and chamber estimates](KissingE6A4SpinTen.md). A centered extra at a signed coordinate requires 4+k incident omissions and contributes at most k+2 points (at most eight if k=4). Every noncentered extra, in its signed orthant, has largest absolute coordinate ≤1 and all ten pair sums strictly >1. Let m≤13.

**All extras centered.** If aᵢ∈{0,1,2} signed centers are present at coordinate i, each support {i,j} needs at least max(aᵢ,aⱼ) distinct omissions. Six signed centers therefore require at least 14 omissions (the minimum is the pattern 2,1,1,1,1; the verifier checks all 3⁵ patterns). There are at most r=5 centers. If all k≤3 then Σ(k+2)≤m+binom(r,2)−2r≤m. A k=4 group needs eight missing roots. With one further group of parameter l one needs m≥8+4+l−1; with two, one needs m≥8+4+4−3 plus their free-slot counts. Thus in the last case m=13 and both others have k=0. Four or five centers with a k=4 would require at least 4r+4−binom(r,2)≥14. The group-size bound in all these cases is ≤m.

**There is a noncentered extra w.** Change signs so its omissions are the ten positive pair sums M₀. There are e=m−10≤3 other omissions. Every noncentered extra has positive horizontal coordinates, since changing a nonempty sign set changes at least four of its ten required omissions. Every center is positive since a negative center needs four incident roots outside M₀.

In fact there is **only one noncentered point**. For two such points with positive horizontal parts u,v, each coordinate sum S exceeds 5/2 (sum all ten pair inequalities) and the sum excluding any one coordinate exceeds two (pair off the remaining four). If min(u)≥1/2, then u·v≥S(v)/2>5/4. Otherwise let t=min(u)<1/2 at j; all other u-coordinates exceed 1−t. Hence u·v is bounded below by the convex combination of S(v)/2 and S(v)−vⱼ with coefficients 2t and 1−2t, again exceeding 5/4. Since the three largest horizontal coordinates all exceed 1/2, the previous bound L+√3|z|≤2 gives |z|<1/(2√3). The full product therefore exceeds 5/4−1/12>1, a contradiction.

There are r≤5 positive centers. Their free slots each require a different omitted directed difference eᵢ−eⱼ outside M₀, so Σk≤e. Apart from w the group sizes total at most 2r+e. For r≤4 this suffices. For r=5 we save one point as follows. At least 5−e≥2 centers i are **unaffected**: no difference with source i is missing. Their coordinates are positive and have range at most one. By the positive-orthant chamber estimate their horizontal sums exceed 9/4. The vector w has at most one horizontal coordinate below 1/2; choose an unaffected center i distinct from that index j if there is one. For any point x at i the horizontal product x·w (horizontal parts only) exceeds one: if w has a small coordinate j use the same convex-combination inequality, with S(x)/2>1 and S(x)−xⱼ>1 since i≠j, xᵢ≥1 and all satellites are positive. If no such j exists, use S(x)/2>1 alone. Full compatibility with w requires the height of x to have sign opposite to the height of w (if w has zero height this center cannot occur). Two points at this unaffected center cannot both have the same height sign: their center product is at least one and their four positive satellite products are strictly positive. This center contributes at most one instead of two. The total including w is at most 1+10+e−1=m.

Thus the spin section has at most (40−m)+32+m=72 points. In the projective equality argument described in the [preceding proof](KissingE6EightAxisExpansion.md), p≥7 now forces a D₅ intersection with at least 20+p≥27 actual signed roots; both spin layers are present. The same contradiction gives |B(S)|≥|S|+1 for all nonempty projectively compatible outsider sets and the usual two-disjoint-edge obstruction.
