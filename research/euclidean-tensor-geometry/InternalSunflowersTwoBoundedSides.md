# Full internal extraction with two quadratically bounded coordinate sides

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** Let \(H\) be a finite simple four-partite four-uniform family, with coordinates \(A,B,C,D\), and \(m\ge2\). If every vertex in \(C,D\) has degree at most \(Lm^2\), then there is a subfamily of size \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\) in which every realized intersection is the core of an internal \(m\)-petal sunflower. No pair, triple, A-degree or B-degree restrictions are imposed. This is a new restricted, gap-free case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

An \(AB\) **fiber** below is the simple bipartite graph on \(C,D\) of extensions of a fixed \(AB\) pair. A degree at \(c\) or \(d\) *inside a fiber* is a triple multiplicity, not its total degree. The **congestion** of a C/D vertex is the number of distinct \(AB\) fibers in which it occurs.

## 1. Finite owner decomposition

Choose a fixed \(\Gamma\ge100\). Starting with no bounded fiber sides, peel all positive degrees below \(\Gamma m\) inside any \(AB\) fiber at either as-yet undesignated side \(C,D\). Assign each deleted edge to the side of the vertex whose deletion removes it. If the remaining core has at least half the mass, use it: in all its fibers every positive degree is at least \(\Gamma m\) on the peeled sides. Otherwise take the largest owner piece (at least a quarter of the mass), and designate its owner side **low**, since within each fiber each owner label is charged at most once, when its degree is below \(\Gamma m\). Repeat skipping low sides; the low caps persist. At most two designations and one final core choice are needed. We lose only a fixed factor and each side now has a uniform status: low fiber max degree at most \(\Gamma m\), or high positive fiber min degree at least \(\Gamma m\). These bounds concern the resulting host and not an assumption of an initial gap.

If both sides are low, the [two-triple-cap theorem](./InternalSunflowersGapFreeProjectedCongestion.md#broader-theorem-remove-all-cross-pair-caps) applies directly: indeed it assumes exactly those fiber degree caps and \(O_L(m^2)\) global C/D degrees. It yields the nonempty and full result. We now show the nonempty bound in the other cases.

## 2. A robust local operation for one high side

Suppose \(C\) is high and \(D\) low (swap if necessary). In each fiber independently construct a random local subgraph of expected size \(\Omega(e/m)\), where \(e\) is its size, with pointwise inclusion probability \(O(1/m)\) for every original edge. It is a union of C-centered stars whose D vertices are all distinct, and is either one C-star of size at least \(4m\), or has a *reserve* of \(4m\) disjoint singleton stars (one per C center), with every other retained C-star of size at least \(4m\). Empty outcomes are allowed.

If the fiber has \(q\le16m\) positive C labels, select at most one entire C-star, giving each label probability \(1/(16m)\) (and giving the unused probability to the empty outcome). Its degree is at least \(\Gamma m\). Expected size and pointwise bounds follow.

Otherwise reserve the \(4m\) lightest C labels by degree, and select one edge per such label sequentially, uniformly among edges whose D vertices have not been selected. At each step at least \((\Gamma-4)m\) choices remain, so the pointwise inclusion probability for a reserve edge is at most \(1/((\Gamma-4)m)\). Remove those C and the occupied D labels from the rest of the fiber. The former remove at most \((4m/q)e\) edges; the latter remove at most \(4m\cdot\Gamma m\le(4m/q)e\), because \(e\ge q\Gamma m\). Hence at least \(e/2\) edges remain. Each remaining C-star still has at least \((\Gamma-4)m\) edges. Independently activate each remaining C-star with probability \(\alpha/m\) (\(\alpha\) small in terms of \(\Gamma\)). Delete edges sharing D with another active star, and then discard entire active stars whose residual size is below \(4m\). Conditional on a star's activation, each edge has D conflict probability at most \(\Gamma\alpha\). The probability that this star falls below \(4m\) is at most \(2\Gamma\alpha\) by Markov, because failure deletes at least half of it. Thus each original residual edge is retained with probability at least \((1-3\Gamma\alpha)\alpha/m\). Append the separate reserve. The two promised bounds follow.

We use a basic robustness observation. In a structured local subgraph as just constructed, if some random deletion has probability at most \(\delta\) for every edge (conditional on the whole local graph, and on its activation if applicable), then one can reproduce the structure with thresholds halved at expected cost at most \(5\delta\) times its size. In the reserve case, drop any non-reserve star falling below half the previous minimum and discard the whole fiber if fewer than half the reserve edges survive. Markov bounds each such adverse event by \(2\delta\); there is no cascading interaction because the reserve uses distinct C labels disjoint from the other stars, and all D vertices are distinct. In the single-star case only its star strength must be checked. The same estimates are valid even when all deletion events are arbitrarily dependent.

Now cap the total sampled D degree at \(\beta m\): discard all sampled edges at each D vertex exceeding that threshold in the initial union of local subgraphs. Given the entire local outcome of any one \(AB\) fiber, the contribution at one of its D labels from that fiber is exactly 1, and from all other fibers has expectation \(O_L(m)\), by their independence from that outcome and the \(O(1/m)\) per-edge bound. With \(\beta\) large in terms of \(L\), conditional failure probability of any of its edges is at most a small fixed \(\delta\). The robustness observation gives a deterministic surviving host of mass \(\Omega_L(E/m)\), with matching reserves and star minima at least \(2m\), or a single C-star of size at least \(2m\). It has D congestion \(O_L(m)\). It has C congestion \(O_L(m)\) already in the host before local sampling: each C incidence with a fiber then had degree at least \(\Gamma m\), and total C degree was at most \(Lm^2\). Both congestion promises persist under restriction.

## 3. Global pair activation and soft locks

In the mixed case use the host from Section 2. In the both-high case use the full host, where again both C/D congestions are \(O_L(m)\) by the positive fiber-degree bounds. In either case any restriction to whole \(AB\) fibers preserves its structural properties.

Give every occupied pair a common nominal activation rate \(p=\varepsilon/m\), with \(\varepsilon>0\) small in terms of the congestion constant. Use the weighted soft support test on A and B: for an unlocked side and each of its labels, sum that label's top \(K=\lceil C_0m/p\rceil\) opposite-pair weights (fiber edge counts). If the total for a side exceeds a \(1/C_0\) fraction of the current host, restrict to those pairs, lock that side, and reduce \(p\) to \(1/(C_0K)\). There are at most two locks, each restriction keeps a fixed fraction, and the final \(p=\Omega_L(m^{-3})\). A locked side has support bound \(K\) from its own locking stage; decreasing the rate later preserves \(pK\le1/C_0\). Every remaining unlocked side passes its test at the final rate.

Activate pairs independently at rate \(p\). Prune edges whose C or D label occurs in a different initially active \(AB\) pair; at a locked A/B label also prune entire pairs when at least two initially active pairs use that label. Conditional on a fixed pair being active, each edge is lost with probability at most \(2p\cdot O_L(m)+2/C_0\), small by choice of constants. Distinct surviving \(AB\) fibers now share no C or D labels.

In the mixed case apply the robustness observation once more conditionally on each pair being active, reaching thresholds \(m\) from \(2m\); expected repair cost is small relative to \(p\) times the host mass. The repaired fiber is fully valid internally, including its empty link kernel if realized: in a single C-star all pairs meet in C, and in the multi-C case the reserved singleton stars form a matching of size at least \(m\). Each C-center with repeated edges has at least \(m\) petals, while D labels are injective. In the both-high case instead peel positive C/D fiber degrees below \(m\). For an original fiber of size \(e\), the total original number of C/D labels is at most \(2e/(\Gamma m)\), so this peeling loses at most \(2e/\Gamma\) edges when active. Every nonempty repaired link then has all positive degrees at least \(m\), hence has an \(m\)-matching (otherwise a König vertex cover of size less than \(m\) would leave some positive vertex outside it, contradicting the minimum degree). Thus both single-label and empty link kernels have internal witnesses.

Finally repeatedly peel any unlocked A/B label with fewer than \(m\) positive neighboring pairs, deleting the entire corresponding repaired fibers. Its cost is at most its \(m\) largest initially activated bucket weights, independent of all prior pruning. Across a side, the top-\(K\) weights contribute at most \(pM/C_0\) in expectation, for \(M\) the host mass after locks and before activation. Outside those top buckets the deterministic contribution is at most \(mM/K\le pM/C_0\). So the unlocked peeling costs at most \(4pM/C_0\) in expectation. For sufficiently large \(C_0,\beta\) and small \(\varepsilon\), the expected final mass is \(\Omega(pM)\). This is \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\) in the mixed case (including its \(1/m\) local loss) and \(\Omega_L(E/m^3)\) in the both-high case.

All realized nonempty intersections within one \(AB\) fiber are witnessed by its repaired link. Across distinct fibers, only A/B singletons can occur nontrivially. A locked label is paired uniquely, so cannot be such an intersection. An unlocked label has at least \(m\) neighbors; any one edge from each of \(m\) distinct fibers gives a sunflower since their C/D labels are disjoint. Whole-fiber deletions in this last phase do not disturb finer kernels.

## 4. Empty-kernel completion and scope

If some \(m\)-matching leaves at least half the family disjoint from its endpoints, apply the nonempty theorem to that residue, then restore the reserve. The original hypothesis is hereditary; all fiber degree classifications and robust local steps are performed *anew* inside the residue. If not, a vertex has degree at least \(E/(8m)\): either an existing \(m\)-matching's endpoints touch more than half the family, or the endpoints of a maximal shorter matching cover it. Apply [full rank-three extraction](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) to its simple link and lift. This proves the full theorem.

The new ingredients beyond the linear-triple-capped result are a **pointwise sparse local witness sampler** for the high/low fiber case and **robust prescribed witnesses** that can survive correlated deletions with small conditional marginal failure probabilities. They avoid demanding cross-pair caps or global triple caps. Without quadratic C/D caps the argument does not control projected congestion or the global D cutoff, and the unrestricted question remains open.
