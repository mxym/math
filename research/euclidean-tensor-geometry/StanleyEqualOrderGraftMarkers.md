# Hidden markers on equal-order rooted grafts

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review or priority claim.

Let \(S\) be any tree on \(q\ge2\) vertices. Identify a vertex of \(S\) with the root of a rooted block of common order \(s>h\), one per vertex. Every branch off a block root has order at most \(h\). In the resulting tree \(T\), the heavy edges at cutoff \(h\) are exactly the edges of \(S\). A conservative sufficient tail hypothesis in the results below is \(s-h-1\ge\sum_{i=1}^h i b_i\), where \(b_i\) is the number of rooted tree types of order \(i\). Any sharper hypothesis guaranteeing the same rooted-block identification also works.

One result: if precisely one block is of a different rooted type from all the others, the **ordinary** chromatic symmetric function reconstructs \(T\) among all trees. The mark causes no difference in intact block order. Unlike [the cutoff-two result](StanleyRootMarkerBranchingCore.md#a-sole-rooted-type-as-a-tag-without-any-leaf-role-condition), the off-root branches may be arbitrary trees of bounded order.

A second result gives a complete core cut deck labeled by an additional scalar for arbitrarily many block types. Fix any single rooted branch \(B\) of order \(h\ge2\). At \(v\), use \(b_v\) copies of \(B\) and \(s-1-hb_v\) attached single leaves, with nonnegative counts. Define
\[
D_*=\max_r\left|\left\{\sum_{v\in C}b_v:\ |C|=r\right\}\right|-1 .
\]
If \(s-h-1\ge D_*\) and the rooted tails identify the blocks, every core cut is recovered in the sense of its multiset of labels \((|C|,\sum_{v\in C}b_v)\), with multiplicities. This determines \(T\) among all trees under the marker conditions below, and on path cores without any marker conditions. Two arbitrary same-order outliers on a path also admit a direct two-port recovery argument. In particular \(D_*\le\sum_v b_v\). Unrestricted Stanley reconstruction is not asserted.

## Extract the labeled series by intact mass

The [heavy-edge factorization](StanleyHeavyEdgeCore.md#1-factorization-by-heavy-components) shows that the usual unsigned tree cut polynomial knows the multiset of block orders and root-component tails through deficit \(s-h-1\), also in any rival with the same chromatic symmetric function. The edge count and positivity of the top layer certify the number of roots and block sizes. The [long tail criterion](StanleyHeavyEdgeCore.md#3-from-a-decorated-core-to-the-full-tree) then identifies the entire multiset of rooted block types under the stated assumption.

For a block of size \(s\) let \(F(t)\) give its normalized rooted series, with root-component size \(s-d\) encoded by deficit \(t^d\), and the detached component sizes encoded by \(x_1,\ldots,x_h\). Its constant is 1. Fix labeled core-component cardinalities \(r_1,\ldots,r_\ell\) summing to \(q\) and requested deficits \(0\le d_i\le D\le s-h-1\). In the unsigned polynomial inspect the coefficient of the high product \(\prod_i x_{sr_i-d_i}\), retaining all low variables. Each of these high components has some core root. Its intact mass, the sum of the orders of its contained roots' original blocks, is a multiple of \(s\), no less than its visible mass. Therefore it is at least \(sr_i\), because \(d_i<s\). The prescribed masses sum to \(qs\), the whole order. The actual masses use disjoint root sets, so these bounds are all equalities and no root is outside the specified high components. This avoids any bound on the total simultaneous low mass.

The ordered query counts all ways of labeling the components. Since the intervals for \(sr_i-d_i\) with different \(r_i\) are disjoint, its value is the coefficient of the high product multiplied by the factorial multiplicity of every identical \((r_i,d_i)\) signature. This is exactly the coefficient of \(\prod_i t_i^{d_i}\) in
\[
\sum_{\text{core cuts and compatible labels}}\prod_{i=1}^\ell\ \prod_{v\in C_i} F_v(t_i).
\]
Indeed detached components all remain within individual blocks, so after a core cut the blocks contribute independently. There are separate deficit variables \(t_i\), but the detached-component variables \(x_j\) may be shared. [A direct finite checker](verify-stanley-equal-grafts.js) compares 78 labeled deficit queries, including 144 nonzero coefficients, against independent full enumeration on two 18-vertex instances. The checks test the extraction; rooted-type identification in the theorem uses its own stated precision hypothesis.

## Invert powers of a common series

Work over \(K=\mathbb Q(x_1,\ldots,x_h)\). Suppose for each component the series equals a known unit depending on its cardinality \(r\), times \(H(t)^e\), where \(e\) is an unknown additive count, \(H(0)=1\), and \(H-1\) has finite \(t\)-valuation \(\nu\). At a given \(r\) the possible \(e\)'s are a certified set of \(m_r\) distinct integers from the full multiset. Divide by the known unit. The distinct leading degrees \(0,\nu,\ldots,(m_r-1)\nu\) of powers of \(H-1\) show that precision \((m_r-1)\nu\) gives the binomial moments \(\binom e j\) for \(0\le j<m_r\). Higher powers have larger orders and cannot interfere. These moments determine the distribution of \(e\) because the evaluation matrix of the polynomials \(\binom e j\) on the \(m_r\) distinct values is a Vandermonde matrix multiplied by an invertible triangular change of polynomial basis. Take tensor products of these invertible changes for the variables \(t_i\) separately, then forget the labels. This recovers the full typed cut deck. Knowing a larger feasible set from all subset sums is harmless: unattained values simply receive multiplicity zero.

For two arbitrary same-order types \(A,B\), take the known unit \(F_A^r\), and \(H=F_B/F_A\); \(e\) counts \(B\) blocks. Their identified tails differ at some \(\nu\le s-h-1\), otherwise the tail identification would make them the same. This yields the full two-type deck whenever \((\max_r m_r-1)\nu\le s-h-1\). In particular with precisely one \(B\), \(m_r\le2\), and its first differing coefficient suffices. At every cut mark the unique component containing \(B\), and give all vertices weight one in the resulting rooted cut polynomial. The [weighted rooted factorization](StanleyRootMarkerBranchingCore.md#a-weighted-rooted-polynomial) recovers the core and its mark; reattaching the certified blocks gives \(T\). For an example at \(h=3\), the two blocks can use a three-vertex branch rooted at either its center or an endpoint, padded identically with single leaves; the two leaf deficits differ already in degree one.

For repeated copies of \(B\), a leaf contributes \(A(t)=1+x_1t\) and the attached branch contributes a fixed unit \(P(t)\). The component series is \(A^{(s-1)r}(P/A^h)^e\), with \(e=\sum_{v\in C}b_v\). The coefficient of \(t\) in \(P\) is \(x_1\) times the number of nonroot leaves within the attached branch, which is strictly less than \(h\): its own attachment root has degree at least two after attachment. Consequently the series ratio has valuation one. Its feasible counts are the certified subset-sum sets defining \(D_*\), yielding the claimed precision.

## Mark a component, then reconstruct

Give each core vertex the positive weight \(J+b_v\), with integer \(J>\sum_v b_v\). Any component's pair \((r,e)\) is recovered from its one numerical weight \(Jr+e\); conversely the recovered deck determines this weighted cut polynomial. If all but one \(b_v\) vanish modulo some \(M\ge2\), the exceptional component in each cut is the one with \(e\ne0\bmod M\). The same marking works if some slot in the certified \(b_v\) multiset has disjoint \((|C|,\sum_{v\in C}b_v)\) supports for subsets containing or avoiding the slot. The [rooted weighted factorization](StanleyRootMarkerBranchingCore.md#a-weighted-rooted-polynomial) then recovers the whole decorated core. Likewise the [two-marker reconstruction](StanleyRootMarkerBranchingCore.md#two-markers-in-a-weighted-core) applies to two opposite distinct nonzero residues in the \(b_v\)'s at any distance (choose \(J\) divisible by \(M\)), or two odd exceptional residues on adjacent vertices. Reattach the certified rooted branches to recover the original tree.

[Orellana–Tomba, Theorems 4.10–4.11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18940) treat the equality-preserving and reflecting operation of a fixed uniform graft; here the nonuniform grafts share their order and the distinguishing information is in the normalized cut tail. [Awan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13464v2) and [Zeng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18179) use proper-core weight conditions. Our intact orders alone cannot identify a unique root in these examples.


## A unique arbitrary rooted outlier of unequal order

The equal-order restriction in the first result can also be dropped. Suppose all but one block have rooted type \(A\) of order \(s_A\), with the sole other block \(B\) of order \(s_B\ne s_A\). All branches have order at most \(h\), and require \(\min(s_A,s_B)-h-1\ge T_h=\sum_{i\le h} i b_i\), so both blocks and multiplicities are certified. Then the ordinary chromatic symmetric function still reconstructs the whole tree among all trees, without a prescribed common divisor.

If \(s_A\nmid s_B\), specialize the low variables to zero: every off-core edge must be retained and the result is the weighted core cut deck for the intact orders. A component contains \(B\) exactly when its weight is not a multiple of \(s_A\), so rooted weighted reconstruction applies. If \(s_B=\ell s_A\), then \(\ell\ge2\) and the common divisor \(s_A>T_h\). For an intact component weight \(m_i\) use all high signatures \(m_i-d_i\) with \(0\le d_i\le T_h\); the same mass-sum argument works because all masses are multiples of \(s_A\). Removing \(F_A^{m_i/s_A}\), a component series is \(1\) or \(F_B/F_A^\ell\) according as it lacks or contains the outlier. This ratio differs from 1 through degree \(T_h\). Otherwise the [rational logarithmic derivative argument](StanleyCentroidTailReconstruction.md#2-two-quantitative-sufficient-conditions) would force \(F_B=F_A^\ell\) exactly: all factors are attachment factors of rooted branches of size at most \(h\). The normalized series \(F_A^\ell\) is the rooted series of the tree formed by putting \(\ell\) copies of all the branches of \(A\) on one root. It has order \(1+\ell(s_A-1)\), not \(s_B=\ell s_A\). Equality of full normalized series forces equality of orders, since the highest deficit degree of any rooted tree of order \(n\) is \(n-1\) (cut all edges to isolate the root). This contradiction proves the ratio claim. The same two-value interpolation marks the component, and the weighted rooted deck distinguishes the core. This extends the cutoff-two unequal-order tag to arbitrary bounded rooted grafts.


## All scalar-decorated path cores after deck extraction

For a path core the scalar-labeled deck already determines all the decorations up to reversal. There is no condition on their count, residues, multiplicities, or separation. Thus all path cores in the repeated-branch family above are distinguished **among all trees**, subject only to the stated tail requirements. This also applies to the two arbitrary same-order block types whenever their full typed deck was extracted; for example two outliers of the same type, anywhere on the core path, require precision at most \(2\nu\).

First the path shape is certified even against a branching rival: project the typed deck to component cardinalities. For \(q\ge3\) the one-edge cuts with a singleton side count the leaves, and a tree with exactly two leaves is a path. The case \(q=2\) is immediate. Encode the nonnegative scalar \(b_v\) by a weight \(w_v=J+b_v\) for \(J>\sum_v b_v\); for the two-type case use \(b_v\in\{0,1\}\) to indicate the exceptional type. A component weight \(Jr+e\) encodes both of its labels. All vertex weights belong to \([J,2J)\).

Write the weights in path order as a composition \(w\). Cuts of the path give exactly the unordered partitions obtained by coarsening \(w\) through adjacent sums, with multiplicities. We use [Billera–Thomas–van Willigenburg, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405434): all compositions with the same coarsening multiset are obtained by independently reversing irreducible factors under their composition product \(\circ\). In our range these reversals reduce to just the full reversal. To see this, proceed inductively through the irreducible factorization, writing \(w=\alpha\circ\beta\) with rightmost factor \(\beta\). If \(\beta=(k)\) has one part, the operation scales each entry of \(\alpha\) by \(k\), preserving the strict ratio condition \(\max(w)<2\min(w)\). Reversal of \((k)\) does nothing, so induction applies to \(\alpha\). If \(\beta\) has at least two parts, every entry of \(\beta\) survives in \(w\): first and last survive at the two ends of the product, and its interior entries are unchanged in the copies. Any entry of \(\alpha\) at least two would produce the join \(\beta_{\rm last}+\beta_{\rm first}\) of two consecutive copies, an entry at least \(2\min(w)\). Hence \(\alpha\) is all ones, so \(w\) is just repeats of \(\beta\). The factors of an all-ones composition cannot change it under reversal, since factor reversal preserves its coarsening multiset and that multiset determines the original multiset of entries. Reversing the identical copies of \(\beta\) is just a full reversal of \(w\). The base case of a single irreducible factor is immediate.

Consequently the scalar order along the path is determined, hence so is the rooted type assigned to each vertex and the original tree. The direct path/classification bridge is separate from the extraction of the scalar-labeled deck from an ordinary tree; the latter is where equal orders and sufficient tail precision are used. For comparison, the known weighted-path/ribbon correspondence is also described in [Aliste-Prieto–Crew–Spirkl–Zamora](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00147). This does not distinguish all trees with arbitrary off-path branches or establish that their full multitype decks can always be extracted.


## An arbitrary affine palette of nested branch-count profiles

Let the branch count vector of the block at v belong to an affine integer line, so that it equals n_0+b_v a, with fixed integral vectors n_0,a indexed by rooted types of order ≤h, and with nonnegative integral parameter b_v after shifting the baseline. Assume a≠0 unless all blocks are equal. Equal block order is automatic under Σ_{τ}|τ|a_τ=0. For attachment factors P_τ from StanleyCentroidTailReconstruction.md, put H=∏_τ P_τ^{a_τ}; the branch-factor uniqueness gives H≠1 (or just multiply the equality H=1 by the negative powers to compare multisets of attached branches). Let ν be the valuation of H−1. A component with r roots contributes F_0^r H^e where e=Σ_{v∈C}b_v. Let m_r be the number of distinct possible e over all r-subsets of the certified block multiset. At precision D≥max_r(m_r−1)ν, the existing binomial-moment interpolation in the repository recovers the entire typed deck with component labels (r,e). This includes arbitrary combinations of any two equal-order rooted types, any number of occurrences; it also includes the arbitrary repeated fixed branch plus padding-leaf family for which ν=1. The only novelty claimed here is the nonuniform heavy-graft transfer and the inference below; the composition-classification theorem is external.

The scalar-decorated path argument above now recovers the ordering of the parameters and hence of the rooted types.

## Two arbitrary outliers, with no extra tail length

Suppose precisely two blocks differ in rooted isomorphism type from the background A. Their known types are B and C, and B=C is allowed. For this statement n≥3, and take A to be a background type of the target, identified in that known multiset whenever both compared trees are considered. There are no assumptions on how far apart B,C lie, and they can compensate algebraically (F_B F_C=F_A² is allowed).

For side sizes (r,n−r), extract the normalized labeled two-component series S_r(t,u), dividing the query by F_A(t)^r F_A(u)^(n−r). Define H_B=F_B/F_A, H_C=F_C/F_A, P=H_B−1, Q=H_C−1 in K[t]/(t^{D+1}), with K=Q(x_1,…,x_h). Both P,Q are nonzero by the block-identification guarantee. Choose a K-linear functional L on the series space with L(1)=0 and L(P),L(Q) both nonzero. Such a functional exists because the union of two proper hyperplanes over K cannot exhaust the dual of the positive-degree subspace. The same functional is applied in each component variable separately.

There are two labeled occurrences at each first-side size r: the prefix of r and the suffix of r. For r=n/2 they are the two labelings of the same cut. An occurrence with both outliers on one side contributes H_BH_C(t)·1 or 1·H_BH_C(u), annihilated by L⊗L. An occurrence splitting the two outliers contributes H_B(t)H_C(u) or H_C(t)H_B(u), and in both cases the double functional gives L(P)L(Q). Thus
N_r = (L⊗L)(S_r)/(L(P)L(Q))
is exactly the number of size-r sides of cuts splitting the marks.

Number the path positions 1,…,n, and write a<b for the two positions. Then
N_r = 1_{a≤r≤b−1} + 1_{n+1−b≤r≤n−a}.
The distance d=b−a is (Σ_{r=1}^{n−1}N_r)/2. The smallest r with N_r>0 is min(a,n+1−b), so after a possible global reversal it is a, and then b=a+d. Equal outliers are now fully reconstructed.

If B≠C and the two intervals coincide, the positions are mirror images, so exchanging the two types is a global reversal. Otherwise choose r in the symmetric difference of the intervals. Exactly one labeled occurrence splits the marks at r, so the first-variable marginal S_r(t,0) changes under exchanging the two types by ±(H_B(t)−H_C(t)). This truncated difference is nonzero because the certified known tails distinguish B,C. All other occurrences contain both or neither on the first side, so they are unchanged under the exchange; in particular no assumption on the product profile is required. The ordering is thereby determined. Reattach the blocks to complete reconstruction among all trees.

The precision needed for the two-port itself is only the maximum of the first differing deficits between A,B and A,C, and if B≠C also B,C. D≥T_h suffices both for certifying the types and for the two-port. In particular no extra factor of 2 in tail length is needed. This uses the separate deficit variables at the same time; taking only each first marginal would miss the split count.

## Related results

For earlier path graft families see [proper caterpillars](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2267) and [proper q-caterpillars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02234). The latter use branches that are paths of fixed length, whereas the above nonuniform grafts can use unrelated bounded rooted branches. Neither all nonaffine palettes with more than two outliers nor the full Stanley conjecture are settled here.
