# Eliminating 24 antipodal pairs in the complete E6 branch

**Conditional theorem.** If a 73-point six-dimensional kissing configuration contains one orientation of each of the 36 axes of an E6 root system, it has at most **23** antipodal pairs. This is not an unrestricted kissing-number upper bound.

Use norm-squared two coordinates, so two distinct code vectors have inner product at most one. The [preceding theorem](KissingE6TwentyFivePairs.md) gives at most 24 pairs. All pairs lie on the original axes and an outsider off those axes has at least five signed strict blockers (roots r with x·r>1), occurring only opposite singly represented original axes; see the [covering argument](KissingExceptionalRootCoverBranch.md). Suppose there are 24 pairs, hence twelve available signed blockers and thirteen outsiders. In the negative graph of outsiders, x,y are adjacent if x·y<−1. Their blocker masks are disjoint: otherwise x,y,−r have pairwise products below −1, contradicting nonnegativity of their sum's squared norm. The same argument excludes triangles. An edge has endpoint sizes (5,5), (5,6), (5,7), or (6,6). Across endpoints of **different** disjoint edges, neither mask can contain the other or be equal, by the [five/six geometry](KissingE6TwentyFivePairs.md) and [seven-blocker lemmas](KissingE6SevenBlockers.md).

## An exact finite test for two disjoint edges

This uses outer approximations, so it is enough that their relaxed test finds no allowable configuration. All sign, chamber, and coefficient conventions are the same as the preceding lemmas. [Standalone verifier](verify-kissing-e6-24-pairs.js); run with Node.js, with no dependencies.

1. List all signed masks of cardinalities 5 to 7. Move each outsider to a fundamental chamber. Its positive blockers form an upward ideal of the indicated cardinality; the earlier lemmas eliminate the nonstar five-mask. Up to diagram reversal there are six representatives, denoted 5, 6a, 6b, 7a, 7b, 7c. Enumerating the six simple reflections and diagram symmetry produces 103,680 distinct signed-root permutations. Images of the six simple roots determine these isometries uniquely. The six signed-mask orbits have respectively 432, 8640, 6480, 25920, 2160, 2160 members.
2. For each representative use the rational outer hull of its **chamber** sphere slice certified in the aforementioned lemmas (the special twelve-generator five-hull, and the clipped hulls for six and seven). Put all generator coordinates on a common scale 1152. For masks A and B to support a negative edge they must be axis-disjoint and some pair of their hull generators, in some allowed relative chambers, must have product <−1. Indeed if **every** generator pair has product at least −1, bilinearity gives the same bound for the actual vectors. Here a direct linear comparison at the common scale is essential; comparing only negative cosines would not justify convex-hull propagation.
3. Fix the first representative's chamber, apply every signed-root group element to the second chamber, and record every second mask passing that necessary test. Then saturate the recorded masks by the first mask's stabilizer. This last step permits *all* chambers for points with the first signed mask, not just the one chamber in the calculation. The resulting numbers of ordered second-mask orbits under this stabilizer are:

| First and second types | 5+5 | 5+6a | 5+6b | 5+7a | 5+7b | 5+7c | 6a+6a | 6a+6b | 6b+6b |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Outer-hull candidate orbits | 4 | 13 | 2 | 21 | 4 | 4 | 51 | 5 | 1 |

4. For every representative A,B of one possible edge, range over *all* signed masks C,D for a second edge. Require each to be sign-consistent with A∪B, incomparable by inclusion with both A and B, mutually axis-disjoint, and to have |A∪B∪C∪D|≤12. Test the second edge using the saturated table after carrying C or D (the smaller type) to its canonical mask. Exactly two anchored four-mask patterns survive; in each both edges have type 6a+6a. Enumerating unordered C,D allows the second edge to be found regardless of its order.
5. In both patterns one can pair up the cross-edge endpoints so that each paired set of six-masks shares exactly five signed roots. For each such pair, compare all relative hull orientations again. Every generator product is at least **+1**, with equality only when each generator is on the highest-root ray **of its own chamber**. By bilinearity this gives product strictly greater than one for genuine non-root points: a non-root point cannot be a convex combination supported on just that one ray. Thus the endpoints from two different edges could not all have belonged to the kissing configuration. No matching of size two exists.

All vertex enumeration and Gram construction are performed with exact rational determinants represented using integers. In the group loop the common-scale dot products, including their intermediate partial sums, are exact integers in the binary64 exact range; the verifier checks a conservative magnitude bound before comparison. Hull generators are not rounded floating-point approximations to the sphere.

## Completion of the conditional exclusion

A triangle-free graph with no two disjoint edges has a vertex cover of size at most one. Remove one outsider meeting every edge, or an arbitrary outsider in the edgeless case. The remaining twelve outsiders span twelve distinct lines, because there are no outsider antipodal pairs; their pairwise absolute products are at most one. Together with the 24 original paired axes (whose two signs occur in the code) these give 36 independent projective code lines. By [Anstreicher's optimality and uniqueness theorem](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/), the rescaled signed vectors on those lines form another E6 root system. It has exactly 24 axes, or 48 roots, in common with the original system: none of the twelve outsiders belongs to an original axis.

The common roots are closed under reflections in their own roots and hence form a simply-laced crystallographic root system of rank at most six. Such a system cannot have 48 roots: in rank six the irreducible types have 42, 60 or 72; in lower rank they have at most 40; any reducible type of rank at most six has at most 42. This contradiction excludes exactly 24 pairs and proves the conditional theorem. No step assumes maximality of the pair count among codes.
