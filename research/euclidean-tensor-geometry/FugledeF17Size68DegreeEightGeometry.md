# Size 68: degree-eight four-blocking bridge

Assume the [size-68 moment-seven and no-layer results](FugledeF17Size68MomentSeven.md). Let F be a nonzero homogeneous degree-eight form vanishing on U_B, and suppose a projective line has at most three U_B sites. As in the M7 proof, the seventeen slices of B' are full collinear four-supports and at least six have one axis e. Their quotient locations C along e have cap4. The secant between any pair of such supports produces at least seven transverse D(B') directions, by Cauchy–Davenport, in addition to e.

## The case of no radial factors

Normalize projective coordinates so e=[1:0:0]. If no radial line through e is a factor, each restriction has at most seven finite roots because e is also a zero. Thus each support pair gives exactly seven transverse roots; [Vosper's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-31.2.200) applied to their axial four-sets shows that they are four-term arithmetic progressions with the same nonzero step up to sign. Since every pair has this property, every support in our selected six-share-axis family has the same step σ up to sign. Its transverse root sets are 7-term arithmetic progressions.

Whenever two quotient chords have the same slope, take the same fixed nonzero representative (Y,Z) of that slope on the radial line. Both chords give the full set of seven transverse roots there, since the radial line is not a factor. Equality of these root sets implies equality of the AP steps up to sign: the sum of squared deviations from their common mean is 28 times the squared step, and 28 is nonzero modulo17. The normalized step of each chord equals σ divided by its nonzero quotient scalar on the representative, so the two quotient difference vectors agree up to sign. Thus our chosen six quotient sites obey the equal-parallel-chord constraint; they are not collinear by cap4. The standalone [verify-fuglede-f17-size68-equal-chords.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size68-equal-chords.js) enumerates every normalized six-site set satisfying this constraint after fixing a marked triangle, and shows that it determines at least nine directions. It visits 37118 states and gives 30360 completions, with direction histogram {9:300,10:900,11:1920,13:18120,15:9120}.

Expand
```
F(X,Y,Z)=A(Y,Z)X^7+B(Y,Z)X^6+C(Y,Z)X^5+D(Y,Z)X^4+E(Y,Z)X^3+...
```
where the binary coefficient degrees are 1,2,3,4,5. The first coefficient A is nonzero, since all seven distinct roots occur on some nonfactor radial line. It is nonzero on each of the at least nine quotient directions. At any such direction the mean is μ=−B/(7A). A seven-term AP centered at its mean has elementary symmetric coefficient of order three zero. Translating to the mean, the resulting cubic coefficient relation after clearing denominators is
```
49 A²D −35 ABC +10 B³=0 .
```
Indeed the X^4 coefficient of F(X+μ) is D+5 C μ+15 B μ²+35 A μ³, whose product by 49 A² is the displayed expression. The displayed homogeneous binary degree-six polynomial vanishes at more than six directions, so vanishes identically. Reducing modulo the linear polynomial A forces A|B. Therefore μ is itself a binary linear form and we may carry out the shear X→X+μ globally.

Write C',E' for the new X^5,X^3 coefficients. For the centered AP7, the elementary coefficients of orders two and four are respectively 3 σ_0²,15 σ_0^4, where σ_0 is the step in the chosen radial coordinate (take the second and fourth even power sums of −3..3 to check). Hence E'/A=13(C'/A)² on the same directions. The homogeneous binary polynomial A E'−13(C')² has degree six and vanishes at more than six directions, hence identically. Reducing modulo A gives A|C'. Now Q=C'/A is homogeneous quadratic. Evaluate on the actual unnormalized quotient vector between two support positions, not on the arbitrary representative; in that coordinate the step of the axial difference progression is exactly σ up to sign. Consequently Q=3σ²≠0 on every such vector.

Translate one of the six quotient locations to the origin. The five remaining vectors therefore have Q(v_i)=3σ² and, for all i≠j, Q(v_i−v_j)=3σ². Their Gram matrix for the polar bilinear form of Q is (3σ²/2)(I+J), of rank five, since the characteristic17 does not divide six. But the vectors lie in a two-dimensional space. This contradiction rules out zero radial factors.


## The case of exactly one radial factor

Let L(Y,Z) be the sole radial factor, so F=L H and H has degree seven. Let its planar slope be the exceptional quotient direction. Our six arm positions cannot all lie on one parallel quotient line in that slope, since cap4. On any chord not in the exceptional direction, its seven or more transverse roots of F are roots of H. This implies H(e)≠0 (otherwise the finite degree would be at most six). Scale H monic in X. On such chords the seven roots are again an AP with common width, by Vosper. The graph of these chords is the complete multipartite graph on the groups of arm positions lying on parallel exceptional quotient lines. It is connected, so all six axial arms have a common step up to sign.

The mean of the seven roots, given by the X^6 coefficient, is binary linear. Make the corresponding global X shear to center H. The axial means of the six arms then differ by zero along each chord between distinct groups, hence coincide by connectivity. Translate to make their common centered axial mean zero. The coefficient of X^5 in H is a binary quadratic Q. It equals the same nonzero 3σ² on the actual difference vectors between distinct groups.

We claim Q has rank one and there are exactly two groups. Rank zero is impossible. If there were at least four groups, choose one representative per group and then translate one to zero. The three or more remaining vectors have Gram matrix (3σ²/2)(I+J); already a four-point subset would yield rank three, impossible in dimension two. If Q had rank two, a group of three would force the full quadratic Q(q−w) to have the same value at three distinct points on an exceptional-direction line for any outside representative w. Thus its quadratic and linear coefficients vanish: the direction is isotropic and orthogonal to an outside difference. In a nondegenerate binary form an isotropic vector's orthogonal complement is its own line, so this is impossible. Likewise, two groups of at least two give four equal quadratic values on the cross differences q, q+a v, q−b v, q+(a−b)v with a,b≠0 and v exceptional. Subtracting their second difference gives Q(v)=0, and another subtraction gives orthogonality to the outside difference, again impossible. At most three groups with at most one group size two would contain at most four points. Rank two is therefore impossible. With rank one, representatives of three groups would yield a rank-two Gram matrix, so exactly two groups occur. Since one group has at least three points, the earlier univariate argument now makes the rank-one direction radical exactly the exceptional direction.

Normalize the common step to one, the two quotient group levels to y=0,1, and write the remaining quotient coordinate z. Then Q=3y². The two groups have six points altogether, hence the ordinary chords determine at least five slopes by Cauchy–Davenport applied to their z coordinates on the parallel y-lines. The centered AP7 shows the X^4 coefficient of H vanishes on those slopes, and the X^3 coefficient equals 15y^4 there. Binary degrees three and four, respectively, make both statements identities. Thus the top five coefficients of H(X,y,z) are [1,0,3y²,0,15y^4], independently of z.

Every site (x,y,z) of B' with y≠0,1 has differences to the four points of a selected full arm in each of the two groups. After the shear, a full arm has X coordinates {7,8,9,10}, the centered four-term AP with step one. For a selected arm in group i these differences are transverse roots of H on their common quotient vector (y−i,z−z_i), so all four values x+{7,8,9,10} are roots of a monic degree-seven polynomial with the above top coefficients at y−i. Multiplication of the quartic factor by a monic cubic determines the cubic's three remaining coefficients from the next three leading terms, and checks compatibility at the fourth. The exact 17-field enumeration in [verify-fuglede-f17-size68-degree-eight-radials.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size68-degree-eight-radials.js) finds only seven possible (y,x) pairs obeying both group tests, namely (6,4),(6,13),(9,0),(9,5),(9,12),(12,4),(12,13). They give at most seven affine lines of B' outside the two exceptional parallel planes. Therefore |B'|≤2·16+7·4=60<68, contradiction.

## At least two radial factors

Write k for the radial factor count. A nonfactor through e has at most 1+8−k≤7 U points, so every chord among our six full arms gives a radial factor. Their quotients determine at least five directions, so k≥5. With k≥5 a nonfactor through e has at most four U points, so every affine plane of its direction through a full four-arm consists of that arm alone. The occupancy sum of the full arm's pencil is then at most 72+12k, excluding k=5. For k=8 all radial factors cover every quotient direction; the quotient has ≥17 sites, and at =17 all fibers contain four so its cap4 forces ≥14 directions, while >17 would give all eighteen. This excludes k=8.

For k=6,7 choose the initial six full-arm quotient points. They are a clique for the k factor slopes. In fact every quotient site of B' is compatible with each of the six in those same slopes: otherwise its difference from the four points of the associated full arm gives four transverse directions plus e on a nonfactor, impossible. This would give seventeen or more common compatible points around the six selected quotient points. The exact normalized-triangle verifier in [verify-fuglede-f17-size68-degree-eight-radials.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size68-degree-eight-radials.js) checks all six-point cap4 cliques of up to k indicated slopes whose common compatible-site supply could reach seventeen. The search visits 140 and 4262 states for the 81 and 238 mask orbits, respectively, and finds zero in both cases. So k=6,7 are impossible as well.

These contradictions prove four-blocking under F8. This does not prove moments through eight. In particular a four-site U pencil can correspond to a nonuniform partner occupancy profile: each parallel planar slice then has cap five by the planar four-direction census in the moment-seven reduction, and the exact replay [verify-fuglede-f17-size68-cap-five-profiles.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size68-cap-five-profiles.js) shows that degree≤8 profiles of mass68 with cap5 are exactly the uniform profile and the 34 profiles 4±(t−h)^8. The latter are genuine octics of weight8/17. In the uniform case the pole belongs to the partner U; in the nonuniform case it does not.
