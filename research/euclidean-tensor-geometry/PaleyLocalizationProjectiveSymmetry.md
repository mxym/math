# Projective symmetry of Paley localizations

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review or priority claim.

For \(p\equiv1\bmod4\) prime, let \(P_p\) have an edge whenever a nonzero difference is a square. A clique localization is the induced common-neighbor graph of some fixed clique of anchors. We use the clique upper bound \(\vartheta(\overline H)\) for a localized graph \(H\). The two-localization bound in [Randomstrasse101, Conjecture 27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29571) and the asymptotic improvement over [Hanson–Petridis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09134) remain open here. The reduction below gives smaller exact semidefinite blocks and a distinct Fourier kernel, not an upper bound with a new leading constant. Compare [Kunisky](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16475) for the spectral-localization program and numerical bounds.

## Any projective stabilizer of a clique of anchors acts

On the projective line use representatives \(v_x=(x,1)\) at finite \(x\) and \(v_\infty=(1,0)\). The Paley conference entry is \(C_{xy}=\chi(\det(v_x,v_y))\). At distinct finite points it is the quadratic character of their difference, and at \(\infty\) it is 1 against every other point since \(\chi(-1)=1\). A finite anchor clique together with \(\infty\) gives a set \(K\) on which all distinct entries equal 1. Assume \(|K|\ge3\).

For a projectivity \(g\) preserving \(K\) setwise, choose a matrix for it, and write \(g v_x=\kappa_x v_{gx}\). Then
\[
C_{gx,gy}=\chi(\det g)\chi(\kappa_x)\chi(\kappa_y) C_{xy}.
\]
Multiplying its values on three anchor pairs gives \(\chi(\det g)=1\); it then forces all \(\chi(\kappa_x)\) on \(K\) to be the same. For \(x\) outside \(K\) with \(C_{x,k}=1\) for every \(k\in K\), take \(k=g^{-1}\infty\) in the same identity. Both conference entries equal 1, forcing \(\chi(\kappa_x)\) to be the common anchor value. Thus \(g\) preserves this common-neighbor set and all its internal signs. This gives graph automorphisms not necessarily obtained by restricting affine graph automorphisms of \(P_p\).

## Three blocks for the two-localization SDP

Normalize the two anchors to \(0,1\) and let \(S=\{x:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}\), of order \(n=(p-5)/4\). The Möbius transformations permuting \(0,1,\infty\) give an \(S_3\) action on \(S\), generated by \(x\mapsto1-x\) and \(x\mapsto1/x\). A transposition has one fixed point in \(S\) if \(\chi(2)=1\), and none otherwise (e.g. the possible fixed point \(1/2\) of \(1-x\)). A 3-cycle has the roots of \(x^2-x+1\) as possible fixed points. They both belong to \(S\) when \(p\equiv1\bmod3\), and otherwise do not exist: in the former case each is a primitive sixth root and is a square because \(12\mid p-1\), and \(x-1=x^2\). Write \(e={\bf1}_{\chi(2)=1}\), \(f={\bf1}_{p\equiv1\bmod3}\). The permutation character is \((n,e,2f)\) on the three conjugacy classes. Inner products with the trivial, sign and standard characters give multiplicities
\[
\frac{n+3e+4f}{6},\qquad \frac{n-3e+4f}{6},\qquad \frac{n-2f}{3}.
\]
Equivalently the exceptional orbits have sizes 3 (the \(e\) orbit) and 2 (the \(f\) orbit); other orbits are free. Here \(p=5\) gives the empty action as expected.

Use the primal theta SDP \(\max\langle J,X\rangle\), \(X\succeq0,\operatorname{tr}X=1\), with \(X_{xy}=0\) on the distinct nonedges of \(H=P_p[S]\). Averaging over the action preserves feasibility and objective. Because all the \(S_3\) irreducibles are of real type, an equivariant real symmetric matrix is specified by one real symmetric block per multiplicity space; the standard block is repeated twice. Positivity is exactly positivity of those three blocks. Linear constraints can be imposed once per unordered pair orbit; in block coordinates they can couple the blocks, so one cannot discard the sign and standard blocks. The objective only sees the trivial block.

## Four blocks for any three anchors

Normalize anchors to \(0,1,t\) with \(t\in S\). On the four projective anchors including \(\infty\), the following projectivities form the three nonidentity elements of a Klein four group:
\[
g_1(x)=t/x,\quad
g_2(x)=(x-t)/(x-1),\quad
g_3(x)=t(x-1)/(x-t).
\]
Each swaps two disjoint pairs of anchor points. On the localized vertex set \(S_t=\{x\in S:\chi(x-t)=1\}\), an involution \(g_1,g_2,g_3\) can fix respectively \(\pm\sqrt{t}\), \(1\pm\sqrt{1-t}\), or \(t\pm\sqrt{t(t-1)}\). They split over the field because the radicands are squares. No distinct involutions share a fixed point: the equations for the first two would force \(x=t\) and \(t=t^2\); any common fixed point of another pair would also be fixed by the remaining involution. Hence the other involutions swap each such potential fixed-point pair, and either both or neither lie in \(S_t\). With \(n_t=|S_t|\), let \(f_i\in\{0,2\}\) count their fixed points there. The four real character blocks have sizes \((n_t+\sum_i \xi(g_i)f_i)/4\) for the four real characters \(\xi\). This is an exact reduction for every anchor triple, regardless of whether different triples yield different localizations.

## A factored Seidel kernel on squares

Let \(B\) denote the multiplicative square subgroup of size \(m=(p-1)/2\). The map \(x(z)=(z+2+z^{-1})/4\) from \(B\setminus\{1,-1\}\) onto \(S\) has fibers \(\{z,z^{-1}\}\). If \(x\in S\), take \(z=(\sqrt x+\sqrt{x-1})^2\); the inverse corresponds to changing the sign of one square root, by their difference-of-squares identity. Conversely for \(z=w^2\) the images \(x(z),(x(z)-1)\) are \(((w+w^{-1})/2)^2,((w-w^{-1})/2)^2\) and nonzero.

Put \(f(z)=\chi(1-z)\). The pullback Seidel matrix factors exactly as
\[
\chi(x(z)-x(w))=f(w/z) f(zw).
\]
This follows from \(4(x(z)-x(w))=(z-w)(1-(zw)^{-1})\) and from square denominators, and also holds at duplicate images. On the full subgroup \(B\), the same factored kernel has Fourier matrix in the normalized cyclic character basis
\[
\widehat K_{k,l}=m \sum_{\substack{b-a=k\\a+b=-l}}\widehat f(a)\widehat f(b),\qquad
\widehat f(a)=m^{-1}\sum_{z\in B} f(z)\overline{\psi_a(z)}.
\]
Indeed expand the two factors by Fourier inversion and sum against the input character \(\psi_l\). There are at most two summands; since \(m\) is even, only equal-parity \(k,l\) can couple. Each \(\widehat f\) is a sum of two normalized Jacobi sums: extend \(\overline{\psi_a}\) to a multiplicative character on the full nonzero field, and use the square indicator \((1+\chi)/2\). Removing \(z=\pm1\), the kernel kills the inversion-odd subspace. On the isometric pullback of functions on \(S\) (divide lifted entries by \(\sqrt2\)) its action is twice the original Seidel matrix. These are exact identities, without additional operator estimates.

The [integer field checker](verify-paley-localization-symmetry.js) tests the action and fixed-point data for 25 primes, 139 normalized anchor triples, and the kernel identity on 118,604 ordered representative pairs.


## A strict finite improvement from prime Fourier uncertainty

For any clique \(A\) of size \(a\ge2\), let \(H\) be its common-neighbor localization and \(T=A\cup V(H)\), of size \(m_T\le(p+3)/4\), since \(T\) is contained in the union of two anchors and their common neighbors. Then
\[
\vartheta(\overline H)\le \sqrt p-a-\frac{2}{(\sqrt p+1)(2(p-1))^{m_T}}.
\]
This only improves the ambient \(\sqrt p-a\) bound by a tiny positive term, not the Hanson–Petridis constant.

Let \(C_P=(\chi(x-y))\) on the full field, and \(b=\sqrt p/(\sqrt p+1)\). The matrix \(B=b(I+C_P)+(1-b)J\) has entries 1 on the diagonal and on Paley edges; thus its compression \(B_T\) is a theta dual witness by the displayed primal. By the quadratic Gauss sum, its eigenvalue is \(\sqrt p\) on the constant and square-frequency spaces; on nonsquare frequencies it drops by \(2p/(\sqrt p+1)\). Denote the orthogonal projections onto nonsquare and nonzero square frequencies by \(Q_-,Q_+\). Both compressions to \(T\) are positive definite: a nonzero vector supported on \(T\) in the kernel of either compression would have Fourier support of size at most \((p+1)/2\), violating [Tao's prime Fourier uncertainty inequality](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308286). Thus
\[
\vartheta(\overline{P_p[T]})\le\sqrt p-\frac{2p}{\sqrt p+1}\lambda_{\min}((Q_-)_T).
\]
The complement of \(P_p[T]\) is the disjoint union of \(a\) isolated vertices and \(\overline H\); theta is additive on disjoint unions. Equivalently in the primal, Gram sums from the separate components can be aligned, with their traces apportioned in proportion to their theta values, giving the sum optimum.

An explicit eigenvalue margin follows by algebraic conjugation. The matrices \(K_\pm=2\sqrt p(Q_\pm)_T=\sqrt p I\pm (C_P)_T-J/\sqrt p\) have \(\sqrt p\det K_\pm\) algebraic integers, by the determinant expansion of the rank-one term \(J/\sqrt p\). They are nonzero, and Galois conjugation at \(\sqrt p\mapsto-\sqrt p\) exchanges them up to sign. The absolute norm is a positive integer, so
\[
p(4p)^{m_T}\det((Q_+)_T)\det((Q_-)_T)\ge1.
\]
The diagonal of \(Q_+\) is \((p-1)/(2p)\), so the determinant is at most \(((p-1)/(2p))^{m_T}\) by AM–GM on its eigenvalues. The eigenvalues of the other compression are in \((0,1]\), hence its smallest one is at least its determinant, and at least \([p(2(p-1))^{m_T}]^{-1}\). Substitution gives the bound.


## Conditional barrier for a one-value dual

Assume [Kunisky's degree-two weak convergence conjecture, 1.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16475), not the stronger spectral edge conjecture. Then a theta dual whose free nonedge entries all share one scalar cannot give a leading constant below \(\sqrt3/2\). Such a dual has form \(B_b=(1-b)J+b(I+C_S)\). A nonsquare scaling identifies Kunisky's common-nonneighbor localization with the complement orientation here. In that orientation adjacency rescaled by \(8/\sqrt p\) has the assumed weak limit of the degree-four Kesten–McKay distribution with support \([-2\sqrt3,2\sqrt3]\). Since \(C_S=J-I-2A_{\overline H}\), for any fixed \(\epsilon>0\) a subspace of dimension tending to infinity has positive Rayleigh quotients of \(C_S/\sqrt p\) at least \(\sqrt3/2-\epsilon\), after intersecting with \({\bf1}^\perp\). Positive limiting mass in any interval immediately inside the negative endpoint suffices; no control at the edge is needed.

For a sequence of these duals with largest eigenvalue \(O(\sqrt p)\), the all-ones unit vector gives the quotient \((1-b)n+b(1+c_0)\), where \(|c_0|\le\sqrt p\) because the full Seidel matrix has norm \(\sqrt p\). Since \(n=(p-5)/4\), this forces \(b>0\) for large \(p\). The positive quotient on \({\bf1}^\perp\) then forces \(b=O(1)\), and the all-ones bound consequently gives \(b\ge1-O(p^{-1/2})\). The largest eigenvalue is therefore at least \((\sqrt3/2-o(1))\sqrt p\). This is conditional and only excludes that one-parameter family, not the general invariant SDP.
