# Degree nine is necessary and tangent-exact on a three-deficient frame family

Let \(I\) be the prime ideal of the fourth secant cone in \(4\times4\times4\), and \(J_{\le8}\) the ideal generated by all homogeneous equations in I of degrees up through 8. At
\[
 S=e_0^{\otimes3}+e_1^{\otimes3}+e_2^{\otimes3}+u^{\otimes3},
 \qquad u=e_0+e_1+e_2,
\]
\(J_{\le8,S}\ne I_S\). In detail, the quintic generators have exact Jacobian rank 10; the known quintic and sextic generators have rank 19 (as do all equations of degrees through 8), and adding the lifted degree-nine determinantal generator makes this 20, the exact Jacobian rank of the prime ideal at S. Thus the known ideal and prime ideal have equal tangent spaces, **not** asserted equal local schemes. In particular S is singular: this tangent dimension is 44, whereas the affine cone has dimension 40. The same ranks and degree-nine necessity hold on a dense open of the locus supported on \(3\times3\times3\).

## Degree-nine equation and finite computation

Use the integer degree-nine Koszul determinant of the \(3\times3\times3\) compression. Its matrix in three \(3\times3\) slices is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&X_2&-X_1\\-X_2&0&X_0\\X_1&-X_0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
It has rank at most two on one simple tensor, hence at most eight on border rank four. It is the Ottaviani form of Strassen's irreducible degree-nine hypersurface equation for the compressed secant; see Bates–Oeding [arXiv:1009.6181](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.6181), Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.4 (also Friedland [arXiv:1003.1968](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1968), Theorem 4.4).

For the order \(16i+4j+k\), the degree-nine gradient on the compressed coordinates has mod 101 nonzero entries (index:value) \(6:1,9:-1,18:-1,24:1,33:1,36:-1\). The companion [`verify-salmon-triple-compression.js`](verify-salmon-triple-compression.js) computes directly by product rule and cofactors. It also computes quintic rows as in the preceding two-deficient frame certificate and sextic rows given by the same alternating slots, allowing two possibly different fixed C coordinates in the two leftover slots (the cancellation proof is unchanged) and all three cyclic permutations. The script finds independent rows modulo 101: 10 quintic, 9 sextic, and 1 nonic. Their 20-square Jacobian minor has determinant 43 modulo 101.

There cannot be more than 10 quintic directions: for each orientation each polynomial vanishes when either of the two matrix modes alone is compressed (all slices share that left or right kernel). Thus every monomial involves an outside coordinate in each of those two modes; a first derivative at S can only use coordinates with at least two outside indices. There are \(3\cdot 3+1=10\). All equations in I of degree at most eight vanish identically on the entire fixed \(3^3\) compression, since their restrictions vanish on its irreducible secant hypersurface of degree nine. To prove the remaining upper bounds we now exhibit genuine tangent directions of the prime ideal.

## Eighteen independent outward directions from a basepoint-free apolar net

The compressed hypersurface is smooth at S, by the nonzero internal derivative just computed, so gives 26 independent tangent directions in the fixed compression. Identify the embedded symmetric cubic with
\[
 f=x^3+y^3+z^3+(x+y+z)^3.
\]
A ternary quadratic \(q\) annihilates \(f\) as a differential operator if and only if \(q(e_i)+q(u)=0\) for each coordinate point \(e_i\). The apolar net is therefore spanned by
\[
 q_1=xy-yz,\qquad q_2=xz-yz,\qquad q_3=x^2+y^2+z^2-4yz.
\]
The common zeros of the first two are precisely the three coordinate points and u; \(q_3\) is nonzero at all four. Thus the net has no base points. It gives a finite morphism \(\mathbb P^2\to\mathbb P^2\): the pullback of \(\mathcal O(1)\) is \(\mathcal O(2)\), so no positive-dimensional fiber can occur. Over a dense open the fibers consist of four distinct points cut out by two apolar quadrics (the fiber of \([0:0:1]\) already has four transverse points). The saturated ideal of a general fiber is generated by these two quadrics. The degree-three perfect pairing gives \(f\) in the span of the four cubes associated to that fiber: the annihilator of its vanishing ideal in degree three is the span of point evaluations. No coefficient can be zero, since the Koszul matrix at S has rank eight (its determinant has a nonzero first derivative) and border rank three would give rank at most six.

Now choose any point v in the dense preimage of the open set of reduced fibers. It occurs in a rank-four decomposition as a symmetric cube with nonzero coefficient. Varying its first-mode factor alone in the fourth coordinate direction gives tangent vector \(e_3\otimes v\otimes v\). These span a 6-dimensional space (the symmetric squares of the 3-space), and the analogous variations on the other two modes give two more 6-dimensional spaces of independent directions. These eighteen directions have disjoint outside-support positions in the three modes, and are also disjoint from the 26 compressed tangent directions.

It follows that the prime tangent dimension is at least 44. The verified minor of rank 20 bounds the tangent dimension of the known equations by 44, so both dimensions are exactly 44, and inclusion gives equality of tangent spaces. For equations of degree at most eight the entire 27-dimensional compression is tangent, in addition to the above 18 directions, so their Jacobian rank is at most 19 and hence exactly 19 by the sextic certificate. In particular, localizing does not remove the need for an equation outside the ideal generated by homogeneous equations of degrees at most eight. The displayed degree-nine equation supplies the missing tangent constraint.

## Genericity scope

For a dense open of rank-four decompositions in the \(3^3\) compression, the four projective factors in each mode are projective frames. Three independent projective changes reduce these tensors to weighted sums of the displayed four symmetric cubes, with all coefficients nonzero. On the irreducible weight space \((\mathbb C^*)^4\), the full apolar-net dimension, its basepoint-freeness, nonzero internal derivative of the degree-nine determinant, and nonzero displayed Jacobian minors give nonempty Zariski-open conditions (checked at all weights one). The same argument works for these weights, giving the stated dense open and hence singularity of the entire closed compressed secant locus by closedness of the singular locus. No scheme-theoretic equality beyond tangent spaces is claimed for this stratum.
