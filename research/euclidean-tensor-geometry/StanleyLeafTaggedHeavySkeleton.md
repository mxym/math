# Leaf tags and marked split recovery for variable-order heavy skeletons

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and mathematical checking; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** Fix a cutoff \(h\). Let the heavy skeleton be nonempty, with \(\ell\ge2\) leaves. Write \(s_v\) for the order of the rooted block at its vertex \(v\). Suppose all block orders are multiples of an integer \(g\ge2\), and all are at least \(h+d+1\), where \(1\le d<g\). Write \(M=2^\ell\). The residues of \(s_v/g\) modulo \(M\) at the skeleton leaves are the \(\ell\) distinct powers \(1,2,\ldots,2^{\ell-1}\), and the residues at all other vertices are zero. Then the ordinary chromatic symmetric function reconstructs the heavy skeleton and each rooted block's normalized cut polynomial through deficit \(d\). There are no restrictions on the repeated orders, rooted types or degrees of internal skeleton vertices.

In particular, \(h=2,d=1\) reconstructs the whole tree, since the block order and number of nonroot leaves specify its numbers of singleton and two-vertex branches. At arbitrary \(h\), take \(d\ge D_h=\sum_{j=1}^h j b_j\), where \(b_j\) is the number of rooted tree types of order \(j\). Full reconstruction then follows from the bounded-branch long-tail lemma. Both conclusions are among all unrooted trees, not just within the displayed class.

## 1. The unmarked split deck recovers the variable-order skeleton

Use the notation of the previous heavy-skeleton proofs. The high degree-\(q\) homogeneous part is the product of the rooted tail factors, whose top variables certify \(q\) and each block order even for a rival. No block has order at most \(h\). Specializing \(x_1=\cdots=x_h=0\) gives the ordinary weighted cut polynomial of the heavy skeleton with weights \(s_v\): a nonheavy cut necessarily creates a nonempty component on the small off-skeleton side, so it disappears. In particular all unordered one-edge split masses \(\{W,N-W\}\) are known, including multiplicities.

For any side, \(W/g\bmod M\) is the binary mask of the tag vertices it contains. There is no wrap because distinct tags add to at most \(M-1\). Every tag vertex is forced to be a leaf in any \(U\)-equal tree: the observed split with a side of exactly its block order has the singleton mask of that tag, so its side contains that vertex and can contain no other positive block weight. Conversely, any extra untagged leaf would give a split side of residue zero, which is impossible in the target because every side of a tree edge contains a skeleton leaf.

Orient splits away from one chosen tagged leaf \(r\). Exactly one side of every split has mask not containing \(r\); call its set of tag leaves \(S\) and mass \(W\), both known. These oriented sets of vertices are laminar (disjoint or nested). Each is nonempty in tags. If two masks satisfy \(S\subsetneq T\), the oriented vertex sets intersect in a tag and must be nested; the orientation of inclusion follows from the strict mask inclusion. If \(S=T\), the vertex sets still intersect in a tag, and are strictly nested when the edges differ. Their order is then given by the numerical masses, since all vertex weights are positive. Thus the known masks and masses recover the exact inclusion poset of the oriented sides. Its Hasse diagram, with a full-tree root added above it, is the original rooted skeleton: a side corresponds to the away endpoint of its edge, and the minimal strictly containing side corresponds to its parent, or to the root when none exists. In particular the positions of all repeated internal block orders are recovered, by subtracting children's side masses from the parent side mass.

Also, the masses of all oriented sides in both directions are pairwise distinct. Equal masses would give equal masks. For the same mask both sides contain a common tag and their complements contain a common other tag. Two sides of cuts in a tree that intersect and whose complements intersect must be nested; this can also be seen by orienting both away from that other tag. Strict nesting contradicts equal mass. The distinctness is needed below to separate the two possible large components by mass.

## 2. Deficit marks recover all the specified block data

For a block \(B_v\) with root \(v\), use its normalized rooted cut series
\[
A_v(t)=\sum_{F\subseteq E(B_v)}t^{s_v-|C_v(F)|}\prod_{C\ne C_v(F)}x_{|C|},
\]
where \(C_v(F)\) contains the block root. Its constant term is 1. For an edge side \(I\), set \(A_I=\prod_{v\in I}A_v\), and write \(W=\sum_{v\in I}s_v\). For every \(1\le k\le d\) the coefficient of \(x_{W-k}x_{N-W}\) in \(U\), taken as a polynomial in \(x_1,\ldots,x_h\) and setting all other high exponents to zero, is exactly \([t^k]A_I\). Both selected sizes exceed \(h\) by the size assumption; they cannot coincide, since \(W-k\) is not divisible by \(g\) and \(N-W\) is.

Indeed, the remaining factors have total order \(k<g\). If a skeleton root were in a low factor, its entire original block would be contained in low factors: other vertices of its block only leave its root component by cutting into pieces not meeting the skeleton. This would force at least \(s_v\ge g\) low weight. Therefore every root belongs to one of the two large components, which means the retained skeleton has exactly two components, separated by one cut. Their intact masses are multiples of \(g\). Since the total detached mass is \(k<g\), the large component of divisible size \(N-W\) must be intact and the other must have lost exactly \(k\) from original mass \(W\). The distinct split-side masses identify \(I\). Choices within its blocks are independent, giving precisely the coefficient of the product \(A_I\). The same root-budget argument for a sole large factor gives \([x_{N-k}]U=[t^k]\prod_v A_v\) when only low factors are allowed. In all cases constant terms are known to be 1.

Rooting at \(r\), recover \(A_v\bmod t^{d+1}\) by dividing its away-side product by the corresponding products of its child sides. At \(r\) use the full-tree product for the numerator. All divisors are units as formal series. For \(h=2,d=1\), \([t]A_v\) is \(x_1\) times the number \(u+v'\) of nonroot leaves, with \(u\) one-vertex branches and \(v'\) two-vertex ones. Together with \(s_v-1=u+2v'\) this gives the whole rooted block.

For completeness, at arbitrary \(h\), \(A_v\) factors over its off-skeleton branches \(B\) as a product of polynomials \(f_B(t)\) of degree at most \(|B|\) and constant term 1: either a branch-attachment edge is cut and its unrooted cut polynomial contributes with deficit \(|B|\), or it is retained and the branch's normalized rooted cut series contributes. There are \(b_j\) possible rooted branch shapes of order \(j\). If two such products agree through order \(D_h\), the difference of their logarithmic derivatives is \(O(t^{D_h})\). Write it over the common denominator obtained by multiplying all possible \(f_B\)'s once per rooted type. Its numerator has degree at most \(D_h-1\), so it must vanish. Both products have constant term 1 and thus coincide. Equality of the full \(A_v\) gives equality of the rooted \(U\)-polynomials, which [distinguish rooted trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08186). Hence all rooted blocks, and consequently the original tree, are determined. This extends the long-tail method without using any centroid bound.

**Family.** At cutoff two let an arbitrary branching skeleton have three leaves. Give its three leaf blocks orders 18, 20, 24, which after division by two have residues 1, 2, 4 modulo 8. Give every internal block order 16. Each block may be any combination of singletons and length-two arms consistent with its mass. All the skeleton edges are heavy and all pendant edges are not. Internal orders may repeat arbitrarily and may carry arbitrary varying interior block shapes, including maximal repetition. The internal blocks can also be chosen with repeated identical full rooted factors; the method does not need to distinguish them by factorization.

**Scope.** Weighted-split reconstruction in general has ambiguities and even hardness results; see [Gaspers–Liedloff–Stein–Suchan, Complexity of Splits Reconstruction for Low-Degree Trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.1733). This result uses explicit arithmetic leaf tags to decode the split masks and marked cut coefficients to recover the unweighted ornaments. Unlike [Zeng's multiplicity-isolated proper-tree skeleton theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18179), it permits repeated internal masses and rooted factors, but requires strong separation by modular tags and says nothing about untagged general trees.
