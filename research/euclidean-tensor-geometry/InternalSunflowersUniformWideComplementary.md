# Uniformly wide complementary fibers: arbitrary simple shadow

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and mathematical checking; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** Fix \(L,R\ge1\). There is a constant \(F_0=F_0(L)\) with the following property. Let \(H\) be a finite simple four-partite 4-uniform family on \(A,B,C,D\) with \(C\)-degree at most \(Lm^2\) and \(AC,BC,CD\) multiplicity at most \(Lm\). Suppose all occupied \(ABD\)-fibers have sizes in \([F,RF]\), where \(F\ge F_0m^2\). The \(ABD\) shadow and all pure pair multiplicities are arbitrary. Then \(H\) contains a full internally \(m\)-sunflower-witnessed subfamily of size \(\Omega_{L,R}(|H|/m^4)\).

This removes the complementary cap on a uniformly wide band, even when all simple shadow pair degrees are unbounded. It is not a theorem for medium-sized fibers or for arbitrarily nonuniform wide fibers.

## Clean wide fibers at cost \(m\)

Sample individual edges independently at \(\rho=1/[4096(L+1)m]\). Delete initially sampled conflicts at \(AC,BC,CD\) and reject a \(C\) label whose initial sampled degree exceeds \(128m\). Given a fixed edge selected, its deletion probability is bounded by \(3Lm\rho+(1+Lm^2\rho)/(128m)<1/100\). In any original projected fiber \(g\) with size \(f\), put \(\lambda=\rho f\). Reject the entire fiber also if its initial sample is outside \([3\lambda/4,2\lambda]\), or if fewer than \(\lambda/2\) survive cleanup. Given one edge in \(g\) sampled, the initial sample is \(1+\operatorname{Bin}(f-1,\rho)\), so Chebyshev bounds the out-of-band event by \(20/\lambda\) for \(\lambda\ge4\). The other edges in \(g\) have distinct \(C\) labels by simplicity; their projection-conflict and degree-cutoff tests only involve sampling at their own \(C\) labels, so conditioning on the fixed edge selected does not modify those tests. The conditional expected number of cleanup deletions among sampled edges of \(g\) is at most \(1+\lambda/100\). Markov bounds the probability of more than \(\lambda/4\) such losses by \(4/\lambda+4/100\). Choose \(F_0\) so that \(\lambda\ge\max(1000,4m)\). All three bounds together show that a fixed edge survives with probability \(\Omega(\rho)\). In a positive outcome the clean family has mass \(\Omega_L(|H|/m)\), cross-pair injectivity, and \(C\)-degree at most \(Km\), \(K=128\). Its occupied complementary fibers have sizes between \(G=\rho F/2\ge2m\) and \(4RG\).

## A reservoir lift of the clean-shadow theorem

In a clean host of the preceding form let its simple \(ABD\) shadow have \(N\) triples. We prove that at least \(\Omega_K(N/m^3)\) projected triples can be selected with all projected nonempty kernels witnessed internally, and each selected projected triple has a reservoir of at least \(G/2\) original \(C\)-extensions, these reservoirs globally pairwise disjoint. The result then has size \(\Omega_{K,R}(|H_{\rm clean}|/m^3)\). Any projected witness lifts by choosing one extension per distinct projected triple; within one fiber there are at least \(m\) distinct \(C\)-extensions, so the projected-triple kernel is valid too.

Use precisely the pure-shadow count budgets of [InternalSunflowersNoPurePairCaps.md](./InternalSunflowersNoPurePairCaps.md) and its [one-cap subroutine](./InternalSunflowersOneQuadraticSideCappedProjections.md). Their input notion of a pure edge is now one distinct \(ABD\) triple, not one physical edge. Every trial's operation of making \(C\) globally unique is replaced with this rule:

> First choose initial pure candidates, keeping the whole \(C\) reservoir of each. Erase any \(C\)-extension whose label appears in another initial candidate reservoir. Reject an entire candidate if fewer than half its initial extensions survived.

In any trial for which the conditional initial-selection probability of each \(C\)-competing projected triple, given a fixed projected triple chosen, is at most \(r\), conditional per-extension loss is at most \(Kmr\). There are no \(C\) repeats inside one projected triple. Thus the probability of rejecting the entire triple is at most \(2Kmr\), by Markov. Rejected triples never return. Amounts of any prescribed pure conflicts and subsequent peel costs are still upper bounded by the old estimates relative to the initial pure sample, even if some candidates now fail by reservoir rejection. It suffices to verify the conditional probabilities trial by trial; choose sampling constants with enough slack for the extra \(2Kmr\) in place of the earlier \(Kmr\).

In the no-pure-cap proof, linear/quadratic degree designations, support locks and restrictions depend only on the pure shadow, so need no change. First-lock exit uses independent labels in the sampled coordinate, \(r=p_f\); a \(C\) competitor cannot share that coordinate by the corresponding cross injectivity. All-low and all-high branches independently sample distinct projected triples, so \(r\) is the edge sample rate. Mixed two-high and one-high branches sample a pure label or pure pair bucket at \(p\) and then distinct projected triples at \(q\); a \(C\) competitor lies outside that outer object by cross injectivity, giving \(r=pq\). The touched high-bucket cap and subsequent interleaved peel count projected triples and work unchanged.

In the one-cap and two-cap proofs, the pure-label lock trial likewise has \(r=p_f\). Their light and diffuse branches sample distinct projected triples independently, giving \(r=p_{\ell}\) and \(r=p\), respectively. In the concentrated branch with both supports capped, activate pairs at \(p'\); candidates sharing a \(C\) label cannot belong to the same activated pair (it fixes one or more pure labels), so \(r=p'\). In the singly concentrated branch select at most one pair per fixed locked-side label with marginal \(p\) per pair, independently at different labels. Any \(C\) competitor has different locked-side label, giving \(r=p\) again. The later weighted graph or unweighted pure two-injective extraction acts on already repaired projected triples and introduces no further reservoir conflicts.

The only asymmetric-degree exit used by those proofs is replaced by the following shadow procedure. A pure coordinate \(X\) there has degree \(O(m^2)\), measured in projected triples. Split complementary \(YZ\)-buckets at \(2m\). In the light case independently sample projected triples at \(r=c/m^2\), erase \(X\) and \(YZ\) collisions and apply the reservoir rule (which also has conditional rate \(r\)). The initial mass retained is a constant fraction of expected, the \(YZ\) support is simple, and \(X\) is unique. Apply the weighted bipartite graph nonempty lemma with unit weights on \(YZ\), losing \(O(m)\). In the heavy case independently activate occupied \(YZ\) pairs at \(r=c/m^2\). Remove every projected triple whose \(X\) label lies in another active pair, and apply the reservoir rule. Competitors at \(X\), or at any \(C\) label of a candidate's reservoir, lie in different \(YZ\) pairs (simplicity and \(CY\) cross injectivity respectively). Given its \(YZ\) pair active, each projected triple therefore fails with probability at most \(O(m^2)r+2Kmr\), small by choice of \(c\). Drop pairs shrinking below \(m\) projected triples, an event of at most twice this probability by Markov given its activation. The surviving \(YZ\) support graph has globally private \(X\) labels and positive fiber sizes at least \(m\). Apply the weighted bipartite lemma with weights equal to those sizes, lifting each kept pair by its whole repaired projected fiber. Nonempty projected kernels lift: pair \(YZ\) uses its fiber, Y and Z use distinct opposite neighbors and private X, and X never repeats. The exit retains \(\Omega(N/m^3)\) in both cases, and C reservoirs remain intact at their required half-size.

All clean-shadow procedures are thus justified with the same pure retention rates and witnesses. In particular finite owner/lock restrictions charge the same projected counts, and expected survivor lower bounds only acquire the extra small conditional losses just tracked.

## Empty kernel

If some reserve \(m\)-matching avoids at least half the host, remove its endpoints and work on the residue. Any occupied projected fiber in the residue either disappeared entirely due to a pure endpoint or lost at most \(m\) distinct \(C\)-extensions. Thus the remaining sizes lie in \([F-m,RF]\), comparable within \(2R\) and still above the preprocessing threshold if the original \(F_0\) was doubled. Restore the matching after nonempty extraction. Otherwise some vertex has degree at least \(|H|/(8m)\) by the usual matching endpoint cover (or endpoints of a maximal shorter matching). Use the full rank-three theorem in its link and lift to get the same exponent without assumptions on that link.
