# E6 seven-blocker compatibility lemmas

In the squared-norm-two normalization of the previously audited [E6 boundary analysis](KissingE6TwentyFivePairs.md), call the set of signed roots with product >1 the blocker mask. The following are local statements: in a kissing code at most one outsider can have any given signed seven-mask, and a seven-mask cannot coexist with a contained five- or six-mask. By themselves these conclusions do not exclude the 24-pair boundary.

Work in a fundamental Weyl chamber. The upward-closed seven-root ideals have four possibilities, with the first and fourth exchanged by the Dynkin diagram symmetry and the others having different signed-root Gram types. The [exact verifier](verify-kissing-e6-seven-blockers.js) checks these assertions and enumerates the vertices of each closed polytope using all active bases. The listed upper constraints bound every nonnegative chamber coordinate. Highest-root vectors in this chamber have simple-product coordinates proportional to the final coordinate vector.

**Clipping the sphere slice.** Write the chamber polytope as the image of a barycentric simplex on its vertices. Call a vertex high if its squared norm exceeds two, low otherwise. If none is high, a norm-two point must itself be a norm-two vertex by strict convexity. Otherwise, for every low vertex V_j choose the largest grid number t_j in {0,1/96,…,1} for which all edge points t_j U_i+(1−t_j)V_j from the high vertices U_i have squared norm at most two. All t_j<1. Cut the simplex by Σ_i λ_{U_i}=Σ_j [t_j/(1−t_j)]λ_{V_j}. Its intersections with high-to-low edges are precisely the listed edge points; a low vertex with t_j=0 lies on the cut. The polytope on the low side is generated by the low vertices and these intersections. Strict convexity shows that an image point of norm two on the low side must be a norm-two generator. The verifier checks that every low vertex with norm exactly two has t_j=0. Thus the hull of all high vertices and all edge cuts contains the sphere slice, also for cells with multiple high vertices.

**Capacity and containment.** For two points with the same seven-mask, transform both to the canonical chamber type and compare its generator hull with every isometry allowed by the Gram matrix of the seven roots, using both possible signs on any one-dimensional orthogonal complement. This overcounts relative symmetries, so passing every comparison is sufficient. For the four chamber types, the numbers of generator comparisons are 196, 5808, 4704, 196. All cosines are at least 1/2, with equality only for two highest-root rays. Bilinearity and the triangle inequality extend the bound to all positive combinations; a genuine non-root point cannot be obtained from highest-root-ray generators alone, hence its inequality is strict.

For 6⊂7 enumerate every deletion of one root from an ideal and every Gram isometry to each six-mask chamber representative. All generator cosines are at least 1/2, with equality possible only when the *six*-side generator is a highest-root ray. The other generator need not be a root. This one-sided equality characterization suffices for strictness, since the six-mask point is an outsider.

For 5⊂7, the common five roots have Gram I+J by the preceding boundary lemma. Let x range over the twelve generators of the previous five-mask outer hull and y over the seven-mask hull. For each pair, lower bound x·y by pairing the five root-product lists in reverse sorted order in the common span and allowing opposite signs orthogonal to it, as in the earlier five-mask proof. A bound on cosines of both *generators* is unnecessarily strong: only the actual seven-mask point has to have norm √2. Instead, verify the **linear** inequality x·y≥||x||/√2 for every generator y. The verifier performs 84,132,132,84 comparisons for the four inclusions; equality is possible only for x on the highest-root ray. For fixed x the inequality extends by convexity to every actual seven-mask point Y. Then for any five-mask point X=Σλ_x x, X·Y≥Σλ_x||x||/√2≥||X||/√2=1, strictly if X is a genuine outsider. This proves all three claims without relying on the failed generator-cosine relaxation.
