# A strict directional bias for hypothetical 73-point extensions of E6 halves

**Theorem.** Suppose a six-dimensional kissing configuration X of 73 points contains a representative of every one of the 36 root lines of a fixed E6. Normalize roots to squared norm two. For **every** selection T⊂X of one signed E6 root on each line,
```
‖Σ(T)‖² ≥ 48.
```
Moreover, the convex hull of these sums stays at squared distance at least **38** from the origin. Equivalently there exists a single unit direction n such that n·Σ(T)≥√38 for *all* these transversals. This is a necessary condition, not a proof that no such X exists.

The [balanced-half result](KissingE6BalancedHalves.md) handles the zero-sum case independently. Here the [at-most-17-pairs theorem](KissingE6EighteenPairs.md) allows us to rule out *177,031* orbits of near-balanced transversals.

## The exact tests

Write U=−T, fix a positive system P, and put ρ=(ΣP)/2. Encode U by its flipped subset F⊂P, so that λ=(ΣU)/2=ρ−ΣF. Use the automorphisms of E6 to take λ dominant, up to the diagram involution. To enumerate all dominant possibilities, write yᵢ=αᵢ·λ≥0. These are integers: ρ is checked to have integer simple-root coefficients, and hence λ lies in the root lattice. If G is the simple Gram matrix, simple coordinates are G⁻¹y and ρ−G⁻¹y is entrywise nonnegative. Enumerate these finite inequalities exactly, discarding nonintegral root coefficients and values of ‖λ‖²=yᵀG⁻¹y above ten. Join subsets of the two lists of 18 positive roots with total sum ρ−λ exactly. For a fixed λ, the stabilizer is generated by the simple reflections with yᵢ=0, and additionally by the diagram involution if it fixes y. Apply these permutations directly to the signed 36-bit half. The orbits are:

| ‖λ‖² | dominant simple products y (node order in verifier) | signing orbits |
|---:|:---|---:|
|0|(0,0,0,0,0,0)|156|
|2|(0,0,0,0,0,1)|7,496|
|4|(1,0,0,0,1,0)|20,501|
|6|(0,0,1,0,0,0)|41,281|
|8|(0,0,0,0,0,2)|3,204|
|8|(0,0,0,1,1,0)|36,226|
|10|(1,0,0,0,1,1)|68,167|

A nonroot may not block any point of T, so its signed blocker mask lies in U. By the [local mask geometry](KissingE6GlobalMaskCapacity.md), masks have capacity one and size 5 through 16. Build the conservative pair-compatibility graph of all signed masks inside U using the rational hull procedure of the [previous census](KissingE6NineteenPairs.md). If X contains k outsiders, it has 73−k original roots, so there are only k−1 unused original roots for the **union** of all their blocker masks. Moreover k≥20, since there are at most 36+17 originals in X.

Greedy coloring and exact clique branching rule out a 20-clique in all but 82 signing orbits. Seven of them have squared norm parameter at most eight (relaxed maxima 20, 21, 24, 21, 20, 20, 20); the other 75 have parameter ten. For each integer k from 20 up to that maximum, rerun clique search requiring the running blocker union to have cardinality at most k−1. All 82 are excluded. As an independent implementation layer on these 82 graphs, the second search builds binary adjacency directly and uses word-level coloring; the exact arithmetic for the *geometry* is unchanged. The [standalone Node verifier](verify-kissing-e6-near-balanced-halves.js) checks all orbits and all 82 residual cases. This proves that no qualifying transversal has squared sum at most 40. Since ΣT is twice an even-lattice vector, its squared norm is an integer multiple of eight, proving the bound 48.

## From a discrete constraint to one direction for all sign choices

Let μ be the sum of the retained roots on singly represented axes, and write r₁,…,rₚ for one root from each doubly represented axis. The convex hull of all allowed transversal sums is the zonotope
```
K = μ + Σᵢ [−rᵢ,rᵢ].
```
Let z be its nearest point to the origin. Write z=μ+Σcᵢrᵢ, −1≤cᵢ≤1, using an extreme point of the coefficient fiber representing z. The directions of the fractional coefficients (|cᵢ|<1) are linearly independent; otherwise a small movement in both directions within the fiber would be possible. Hence there are at most six. Independently round every cᵢ to ±1 with mean cᵢ. The resulting legal sum S satisfies
```
E‖S‖² = ‖z‖² + 2 Σᵢ (1−cᵢ²).
```
If z=0, this is at most 12, contrary to the discrete lower bound 48. Thus z≠0. For each fractional direction, minimizing the norm within K implies z·rᵢ=0, since cᵢ can vary in either direction. All these independent directions therefore lie in a space of dimension at most five. The same rounding formula now gives 48≤‖z‖²+10. Finally z·S≥‖z‖² for every S∈K by nearest-point optimality. Taking n=z/‖z‖ proves the common directional bound.
