# Eliminating 22 antipodal pairs in the complete E6 branch

**Conditional theorem.** A hypothetical 73-point six-dimensional kissing configuration containing an orientation of every E6 root line has at most **21 antipodal pairs**. This does not claim an unconditional upper bound on the six-dimensional kissing number.

We use the [previous 23-pair exclusion](KissingE6TwentyThreePairs.md), its geometric hulls and conventions. With exactly 22 pairs there are 14 singly represented original axes, hence 14 available signed roots for blockers, and 15 outsider points. Each outsider has at least five blockers; the negative graph joins outsiders with product <−1 in squared-norm-two coordinates, is triangle-free, and its edge endpoints have disjoint signed masks. Thus a negative-edge endpoint has at most nine blockers. An isolated outsider can initially have more.

## Nine- and ten-mask capacity

Extend the earlier exact spherical-cell method to the seven upward chamber ideals of nine roots and eight ideals of ten roots. Enumerate bounded rational chamber vertices by active bases, clip away the low side as in the [seven-mask lemma](KissingE6SevenBlockers.md), and compare every outer-hull generator pair under each Gram-preserving permutation of the signed mask (and both orthogonal signs where relevant). Inner products are bounded below by half the product of norms; equality, if present, requires two highest-root rays. No genuine outsider can span just a root ray, so capacity is one for each relevant nine- or ten-mask. The only necessary orbit identifications between distinct chamber ideals are diagram reversals: the checker enumerates every group element for the four nine-mask types, and explicitly checks that each ten-mask used below belongs to no second non-diagram-equivalent type.

For nine-mask ideals the exact comparison counts are 4056, 484, 484, 968, 968, 4056 and 484. For ten-mask ideals the counts are 1352, 2028, 2028, 200, 200, 1600, 1352 and 2028. Smaller mask capacities are [already established](KissingE6TwentyThreePairs.md). No sharp containment assertion about nine- or ten-masks is required.

## Classify candidate two-edge matchings

The enumeration is a relaxation: only proven exclusions are used. For each pair of mask types of sizes five through nine with sum ≤14, anchor the smaller type's mask and use all relative chambers to bound the smallest product via the rational generator hulls. Retain every axis-disjoint candidate for which some generator pair has product <−1, saturating by the first mask's stabilizer. Over all type pairs, there are 629 possible first-edge orbits under this procedure.

For each first-edge representative, enumerate two additional signed masks. Check that all four are distinct and sign-consistent, their union has size at most 14, the two extra masks can themselves form a negative edge, and no cross-edge pair violates the previous containment exclusions among masks of sizes ≤7. When joining candidates, the numbers of new signed blockers outside the first union add because the second-edge masks must be disjoint. These tests leave 27,883 anchored four-mask patterns. Apply the three positive-product incompatibility tests (linear, row-wise or reversed row-wise, with both signs and all stabilizer orientations) from [the preceding theorem](KissingE6TwentyThreePairs.md) to all four cross-edge pairs. Exactly 2,551 patterns survive. Quotient by the full E6 automorphism group and the eight reorderings of the paired endpoints, leaving 323 matching classes.

Of these classes, 315 already use 14 distinct signed blockers and determine the available set U exactly. The other eight use 13, permitting one more signed root on any unused axis, giving 46 choices apiece. We retain all 315+8·46=683 cases, without requiring that completions belonging to different cases be inequivalent.

## Count all outsiders, including the isolated ones

For each U enumerate all its signed submasks of sizes five to nine that occur in the full precomputed mask orbits. For the required 15 outsiders to coexist, there must be at least 15 distinct admissible masks. The necessary capacity-one bounds let us count masks instead of continuous points. Only 33 of the 683 cases have any nine-mask. Without a nine-root chamber ideal no larger blocker ideal can occur, because removing minimal elements gives an upward nine-root subideal. In the 33 remaining cases enumerate every possible ten-root chamber ideal modulo diagram reversal, applying all 103,680 root isometries. At most two ten-masks occur in any U, and no eleven-root chamber ideal occurs in any. Hence larger masks are impossible throughout. For these exceptional ten-masks we pessimistically permit them all, without imposing any further compatibility.

Proceed in each case as follows.

* If the total number of admissible masks is less than 15, stop.
* Retain the four required endpoints. From the other masks of size at most nine discard any mask proved incompatible with at least one endpoint. If fewer than 11 extras (including all ten-masks) remain, stop.
* In the seven residual cases test the remaining small-mask compatibility graph. If there are h ten-masks, the small masks beyond the four fixed endpoints would have to contain a clique of size 11−h, since negatives are permitted between outsiders but every pair of code points must pass the positive-product upper bound. Absence of this clique follows by an exhaustive recurrence: branch on the first vertex of a prospective clique and restrict the subsequent list to vertices not proved incompatible with it. The seven pairs (available small masks beyond the endpoints, unconditionally permitted ten-masks) are (14,0), (11,0), (33,1), (26,1), (27,1), (18,2), and (9,2). All fail the requested clique test.

Thus no matching of size two in the negative graph is compatible with 15 outsiders. A triangle-free graph of matching number at most one has a vertex cover of size at most one. Delete a single outsider and combine the 14 independent remaining outsider lines with the 22 paired original axes. The resulting 36 lines constitute another E6 system by [Anstreicher's uniqueness theorem](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/), sharing exactly 22 axes with the first. The common roots form a simply-laced root system of rank at most six, but none has 44 roots (irreducible rank-six counts are 42, 60 and 72; reducible systems have at most 42). This contradiction proves the conditional bound.

The [standalone verifier](verify-kissing-e6-22-pairs.js) performs all finite checks with Node.js and no external packages. Chamber construction uses integer determinants; the scaled integer dot products and their squared comparisons have checked bounds within the exact binary64 range. The code does not optimize approximate point configurations or treat a failed relaxation as an actual configuration.
