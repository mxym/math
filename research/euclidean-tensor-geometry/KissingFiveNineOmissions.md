# A hypothetical 41-point configuration shares at most 30 points with D5

**Theorem.** A five-dimensional kissing configuration containing at least **31** signed roots of one D5 system has at most 40 points. The [32-root theorem](KissingFiveNearD5.md) leaves the case of nine omitted original roots. This is still conditional; we do not exclude unrestricted 41-point configurations.

Retain all 31 original roots and ten other points. Write U for the missing roots, |U|=9. As before, enumerate upward ideals in a signed-permutation chamber and their rational outer enclosures for the sphere slice, now also at size nine. All seven new ideals pass the conservative sphere test, and the generator comparisons again give capacity one for each fixed signed mask. All definitions, the safe integer inequalities, and the proof of the enclosure are in the preceding theorem. The [standalone verifier](verify-kissing-5d-nine-omissions.js) rebuilds every cell.

Two additional necessary tests make this boundary short.

1. **Restore an omitted root.** Each r∈U must be blocked by at least two distinct, mutually compatible outsider masks. Otherwise restore r and discard at most one point (if needed discard any surplus outsider). This would give a 41-point counterexample with only eight omissions.
2. **A strongly negative edge is necessary.** At least 20−9=11 original axes still occur with both signs, whether or not U contains an antipodal pair. If all ten outsiders have pairwise product ≥−1 in squared-norm-two normalization, their lines are distinct, compatible with one another and with these paired axes. There would be at least 21 projective kissing lines in dimension five, contradicting [Anstreicher’s upper bound 20](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/). Thus two outsiders have product <−1; their strict blocker sets are disjoint, since sharing a blocker puts both points less than 60 degrees from it, and hence less than 120 degrees apart. For disjoint masks C,D compare the enclosure of −C with every admissible chamber of D relative to it. If every generator pair has product ≤1, actual points of C,D cannot have product <−1 by bilinearity. Treat every unexcluded pair as possible.

Enumerate all omission sets that contain any candidate mask inductively up to size nine under signed permutations. Begin with minimal candidate ideal seeds; every larger mask either is itself a seed or contains a lower seed, as checked by the canonical chamber list. From every current class add each remaining signed root and canonicalize by sorting the root sum and then minimizing within its stabilizer. This generates all classes by equivariance. The successive counts at sizes 3 through 9 are 1, 6, 47, 318, 1,988, 10,512 and **46,581**. The intermediate map contains 368,555 distinct signed sets before the final quotient across all levels.

At size nine, 41,790 classes have fewer than ten possible masks, 4,590 more lack two masks for some missing root, and 116 more lack a mutually compatible such pair for some root. Of the remaining 85, **64** admit no candidate negative edge and **21** have no clique of ten mutually compatible masks. Nothing remains. All product tests concern continuous sphere cells, not a restriction of the points to rational coordinates.
