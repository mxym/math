# Exact Waring rank of the permanent with rank-one coefficient matrices

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted derivation and internal verification; no external peer review or priority claim.

**Corollary.** For n≥2 over C, restrict allowed summands in a Waring decomposition of Per_n(X) to powers of linear forms whose n×n coefficient matrix has rank one. Then the restricted Waring rank and restricted border Waring rank both equal 4^(n−1). This leaves the unrestricted problem open.

Proof. Substitute x_ij→a_i b_j. This gives a linear map from the degree-n forms in X to S^n(C^n)⊗S^n(C^n). It sends Per_n to n!·m⊗m, where m=x_1⋯x_n, and each allowed power (sum_ij u_i v_j x_ij)^n to (sum_i u_i a_i)^n⊗(sum_j v_j b_j)^n. Choose the Young flattening L from Han, arXiv:2609.09141v1, proof of Theorem A and Proposition 4.3 (https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09141v1). If t is its rank on a pure n-th power then rank L(m)≥2^(n−1)t. Apply the tensor product of this linear map with itself to the substituted forms. It produces a matrix of rank ≥4^(n−1)t² on the target and of rank at most t² on each allowed power, since matrix ranks multiply under the Kronecker product. Rank subadditivity and Zariski closedness of matrices of bounded rank give the restricted border lower bound.

For the matching upper bound, Glynn expresses Per_n as a sum of 2^(n−1) terms proportional to products ∏_i (sum_j ε_j x_ij), with ε in {±1}^n and ε_1=1 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2010.01.010). Polarizing each product expresses it as a sum of 2^(n−1) n-th powers of linear forms ∑_ij δ_i ε_j x_ij, with δ in {±1}^n and δ_1=1. The coefficient matrices are δ ε^T, all rank one. Therefore both restricted ranks are exactly 4^(n−1), in particular 64 for n=4.

The substantive bridge is the multiplicative flattening certificate, not an assumption that border rank is multiplicative. This result uses Han's recent squarefree monomial result and is a corollary of its explicit flattening certificate, not a new method. The general tight-flattening multiplicativity mechanism for partially symmetric products also appears in [Christandl–Gesmundo–Oneto, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03487). This special application shows that any construction below 64 for the generic quartic permanent must leave the rank-one coefficient-matrix ansatz, even when limits are permitted.
