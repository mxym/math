# Internal sunflower extraction with two injective projections and capped remaining multiplicity

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** Let H be a tripartite 3-uniform family on A,B,C. Suppose AB and AC projections are injective, and max BC codegree is at most D, where 1≤D≤m. Then some F⊂H of size Ω(|H|/(m^(3/2)√D)) internally witnesses every nonempty pairwise intersection by an m-petal sunflower. This order is sharp jointly in m and D. In particular this gives Ω(m^-2) for D=m, with no bound on vertex degree or diversity. Here and below m≥2 and all constants are absolute. This is a special case, not the unrestricted question in [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

A linear triple family is valid as soon as each nonzero vertex degree is 1 or at least m, since its link is a matching. Also, a vertex-disjoint union of single triples and m-or-larger stars with disjoint petals is valid for nonempty intersections. We use expected sizes for existence, and do not need any independence not explicitly noted.

## 1. Initial decomposition and the core

Repeatedly peel any positive-degree vertex with current triple degree less than 64m in A, or less than 64mD in B or C. Assign a deleted edge to the first vertex that deletes it. The four pieces are the core and three owner parts. The A-owned piece has max A degree <64m; the B-owned and C-owned pieces have max owner degree <64mD. This is because no edge assigned to a vertex can be removed except at that vertex's single deletion step.

In the core with E edges, sample A with probability p=1/(4D); keep a triple iff its A vertex is the sole sampled A in its BC fiber. Each edge has probability at least p(1−(D−1)p)≥3/(16D). The result is linear. Peel all vertices below degree m. Each A vertex available to this peeling was sampled, so expected deletions are at most m[p|A|+|B|+|C|], taken over positive-degree vertices before sampling. Since |A|≤E/(64m) and |B|,|C|≤E/(64mD), this costs at most 2.25E/(64D). The resulting m-core keeps Ω(E/D) in expectation.

## 2. A-owned piece

For each dyadic w≤D handle fibers whose actual size f lies in [w,2w). In the resulting simple BC projected graph, peel below degree K=ceil(4096m^(3/2)/√w), orient deleted pairs from the first removed endpoint to the other, and pass this orientation to every triple in their whole fiber. Treat the dense core and two orientations as separate pieces. In a dense piece with E edges there are at most 2E/(wK) active B/C vertices.

For this dense piece sample B,C independently with probability p=√(w/(512m)). For each surviving BC pair pick a uniform representative independently of other pairs conditional on the sample. Drop all triples whose A appears more than once. For a fixed A, its original triples use disjoint B/C endpoint sets (both injectivities), so the events of being representatives are independent for those triples. The chance for an edge to be a representative is p²/f∈[1/(1024m),1/(512m)]. Conditional on this event the chance of any competing representative of the same A is at most 64m/(512m)=1/8. Thus expected retained size is at least 7E/(8192m). Peel B/C degrees below m in the now-linear family, losing at most 2mpE/(wK)≤E/(32768m) in expectation. This is Ω(E/m).

For a sparse orientation, say B→C, choose a maximum-size vertex-disjoint family of C-centered stars with disjoint petals, each of size one or at least m. Write F for the union. Edges meeting its A/B leaves number at most (64m+2wK)|F|. After these are removed, any edge at a new centre or an existing large star could be appended. At a singleton centre an unused matching of m−1 edges in the link would extend it, so a maximum matching is smaller. A endpoints in a C-link have degree at most one by AC injectivity, hence each remaining such link has fewer than m active B endpoints; at most 2wm edges remain there. Sum over singleton centres, then add the removed edges. This gives |F|≥E/(64m+2wK+2wm)=Ω(E/(m^(3/2)√w)). Opposite orientation uses AB injectivity instead. Thus for each bucket, if T is the best valid size in H, its total edge count is O(Tm^(3/2)√w). Sum the geometric series over w≤D, giving the target with no logarithmic loss.

## 3. B-owned piece (C-owned symmetric)

Now |H_b|<64mD for each b. The AC projection is simple. Peel its degree below K=ceil(1024m^(3/2)√D); partition into the dense part and two sparse orientations as above.

For the dense part sample each A and C vertex with probability p=1/(32√(mD)). Keep edges with both sampled endpoints, then delete all triples whose B label repeats. Conditional on evaluating a particular sampled edge abc, all competitors at its same C have another A endpoint: at most D choices, each sampled with probability p. Other competing edges have another A endpoint (AB injectivity) as well as another C; at most 64mD choices, each with conditional probability p². By the union bound, a sampled edge remains with conditional probability ≥1−Dp−64mD p²≥1−1/32−1/16=29/32, using D≤m. The family is now linear. Peel A/C degrees below m, losing at most 2mpE/K in expectation. Here p²=1/(1024mD), and 2mp/K≤1/(16384mD), so Ω(E/(mD)) survives, enough since mD≤m^(3/2)√D.

In either sparse orientation, choose a maximum-size vertex-disjoint family of stars at the head, with disjoint petals and size one or at least m. Edges meeting its B leaves or its oriented AC-side leaves cost at most (64mD+K)|F|. Remaining edges at new centers or existing large stars could be appended. At any singleton centre the remaining link has matching size less than m−1, else upgrade. The endpoints of a maximal matching cover this link and are fewer than 2m vertices, each of link degree ≤D by AC/AB injectivity and BC codegree cap. Thus at most 2mD edges remain per singleton center. Hence |F|≥E/(66mD+K)=Ω(E/(m^(3/2)√D)).

Choose a largest piece at each finite decomposition step, or equivalently sum its established bounds in terms of the globally best valid size. The theorem follows.

## A sharp general functional obstruction

Even allowing only nonempty-kernel conditions, the desired m^-2 retention for arbitrary AB-injective data could not be improved in order. Choose |B|=|C|=m−1, and for each c use m−1 private A vertices, each adjacent to every b with color c. There are (m−1)^3 triples and AB is injective. Each A,C pair and B,C pair has codegree m−1, so neither can repeat in a valid family. Consequently each surviving B and C has degree ≤m−1 (for B use distinct C, for C use distinct B). A repeated such vertex would give a singleton intersection and need ≥m petals, impossible. Thus at most m−1 triples are retained, attained by any matching selecting distinct B and C. All A vertices have only a single C color. This disproves an Ω(m^-1) clean-up hypothesis for a one-side monochromatized functional family, but does not affect the two-injective theorem (the example's AC projection is not injective).

## Matching upper bound across the full cap range

The lower bound has the best order jointly in m and D, even under both injectivity conditions. It suffices to construct the examples for all sufficiently large m uniformly over 1≤D≤m; finitely many small values can be included by enlarging the absolute constant. Let t=min(D,m−1), n=m^8, d=floor(m^(3/2)/(100√t)), and M=ceil(8ndt/m). Take a random simple graph G on B,C of size n each, with independent edge probability d/n. By binomial tails and a union bound, with probability tending to one it has between nd/2 and 2nd edges and maximum degree ≤2d. With probability tending to one it also satisfies e_G(X,Y)≤m(x+y)/4 for every X⊂B, Y⊂C with x=|X| and y=|Y| both ≤M/m. To see this, x=0 or y=0 is trivial, and for fixed positive x,y the mean obeys
    μ=(d/n)xy ≤ dM(x+y)/(2nm) ≤ .001 m(x+y)
for all sufficiently large m because d²t≤m³/10000. The binomial probability of at least ceil(m(x+y)/4) edges is at most 2^[-m(x+y)/4] by the standard upper tail (eμ/u)^u. For each size pair there are at most n^(x+y) choices, and summing over all positive x,y gives o(1) because log₂n=o(m). Fix a graph realizing both properties.

Replace each edge by t parallel copies. By König's bipartite multigraph edge-coloring theorem partition the copies into at most 2dt matchings. Split each matching into chunks of at most m−1 edges and give every chunk a private A vertex, lifting each copy to the triple with that A label. Since each chunk is a matching, AB and AC projections are injective. The BC codegree is exactly t for occupied pairs, and every A degree ≤m−1. The number of A vertices is at most 2ndt/(m−1)+2dt≤M for large m.

Any valid subfamily F has at most one triple at each A (a shared A would give singleton intersection by both injectivities, and its degree is below m), and at most one per BC pair (codegree t<m). Thus F is linear, and its size s≤M. Each vertex in B or C with at least two retained edges must have degree at least m. Let their sets be X,Y; then x,y≤s/m. By injectivity of BC within F, at most e_G(X,Y)≤m(x+y)/4≤s/2 of its edges join the two high-degree sets. Other edges meet degree-at-most-one vertices in B or C, so at most 2n of them occur. Thus s≤4n. Meanwhile |H|≥ndt/2=Ω(nm^(3/2)√D) uniformly because t≥D/2 and d→∞. This proves the exact worst-case nonempty retention order Θ(m^-3/2 D^-1/2) for the class.
