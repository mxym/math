# Explicit overhead and sharp binary repair depth for exact Fourier circuits

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted analysis and internal checks; no external peer review claimed. References to the source are file names of https://github.com/openai/math/tree/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/build. The gate model is nonuniform exact complex binary linear gates with arbitrary prescribed coefficients. The paper explicitly leaves the exact endpoint open (intro.tex). This analysis does not claim the endpoint or a result about uniform coefficient preparation.

**Results.** In the nonuniform complex binary weighted-sum model, tracking the parameters of OpenAI's published Fourier construction gives an explicit upper bound of order \(N\sqrt{\log N}\exp(O(\sqrt{\log\log N\,\log\log\log N}))\) for exact transforms on finite abelian groups of order \(N\). The constant in the overhead is absolute. Separately, the binary polynomial predictor with per-frequency correction rank budget \(Wm^{-\alpha}\) has sharp leading degree \(\sqrt{2\alpha m\log m}\). This is a barrier for that prediction/repair model, not for unrestricted Fourier circuits. The \(O(N\sqrt{\log N})\) circuit endpoint remains open.

## Matrix-valued prediction/repair barrier
Over any field of characteristic other than two, let P(x) be W-by-W with entries of total multilinear degree at most d on x in {+1,-1}^m. Put chi(x)=prod_i x_i, E=P-chi I and r=floor((m-d-1)/2). For d<m, sum_x rank E(x) >= W * sum_{i=0}^r binom(m,i). Indeed, a vector polynomial v of degree <=r has the latter number of free coefficients, while the constraint E(x)v(x)=0 has codimension rank E(x) for each x. If the total rank were smaller, such a nonzero v would exist. But Pv has Fourier support only in degrees <=d+r and chi*v only in degrees >=m-r. They cannot agree nontrivially. The scalar argument is the classical [Smolensky parity bound](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/tss/files/papers/Smolensky.pdf); this is its matrix-valued form, allowing arbitrary inter-row and non-diagonal predictors.

In the binary instance of kernel.tex, every query-eigenvalue tuple x is realized: set the auxiliary digit frequencies to zero and use first-column distinguished X_j=1 and Z_j=(1-x_j)/2. A pencil stage becomes an affine row-matrix in x. Pointwise row maps in repair.tex are fixed on the ordinary addresses, hence degree 0 and frequency preserving. In a network of d pencil stages with compressed spectral tables as side operations, remove all their returned streams. The remaining transfer is a matrix P of degree <=d. At a fixed frequency restoring those returns changes the transfer by rank at most their total width h: cut the wires, regard the d-stage remaining downstream circuit as a fixed linear map from all returned h scalars to W outputs, and substitute the actual returned values. This argument permits several spectral tables and circulation of their outputs through subsequent pencil stages; no identification of depth and number of stages issue, since every path uses at most d of them. Consequently if h/W is O(m^-3) as in the paper, d=Omega(sqrt(m log m)); more generally d + m h/W = Omega(sqrt(m log m)). For an elementary tail estimate, for d<=c sqrt(m log m) and every i in [r-floor(sqrt(m)),r], central Stirling estimates give binom(m,i)/2^m >= c0 m^-1/2 exp(-C(d+sqrt(m))^2/m), for absolute positive constants. Sum the sqrt(m) terms and pick c small to obtain h/W >= m^-1/3 for sufficiently large m. If d is larger it already pays the bound. The weighted expression is the standard stage/full-conversion ledger of the template, NOT a lower bound on gate size for unrestricted Fourier circuits, or a lower bound assuming that exact recursive conversions and different pencils cannot share gates.


## Sharp stage count for polynomial-width binary repair

Fix \(\alpha>0\). In the idealized binary prediction/repair problem, require a matrix predictor \(P(x)\) of degree at most \(d\) with \(\operatorname{rank}(P(x)-\chi(x)I)\le Wm^{-\alpha}\) at every Boolean query pattern. Then necessarily
\[
 d\ \ge\ (\sqrt{2\alpha}-o(1))\sqrt{m\log m}.
\]
This leading constant is attainable with \(W=2^m\) (and hence with any multiple of that width) over \(\mathbb C\).

For the lower bound, the preceding rank inequality gives the binomial lower tail \(\mathbb P(B\le\lfloor(m-d-1)/2\rfloor)\le m^{-\alpha}\) for \(B\sim\mathrm{Bin}(m,1/2)\). For \(d=c\sqrt{m\log m}+O(1)\) with fixed \(c>0\), its logarithm is \(-d^2/(2m)+o(\log m)\): Hoeffding gives the upper estimate, and summing Stirling estimates on the \(\lfloor\sqrt{m/\log m}\rfloor\) layers just below the threshold gives the lower estimate. In detail, the point probabilities there are \(m^{-1/2}\exp(-d^2/(2m)+O(1)+o(1))\) up to constant factors, and the number of layers cancels the power of \(m\), leaving a logarithmic factor. Monotonicity handles smaller \(d\), which proves the lower constant.

For the matching upper constant, interpolate parity as a polynomial \(Q(b)\) of degree at most \(d\) on \(d+1\) consecutive central Hamming weights \(b\) of the \(m\)-cube. Index diagonal predictor rows by \(s\in\{1,-1\}^m\); in that row put \(\chi(s)Q(\mathrm{wt}(s\cdot x))\), where \(\mathrm{wt}\) counts negative entries. At each fixed \(x\), these \(s\cdot x\) run through all cube points, so the rank defect is bounded by the number of points outside the central interval. Hoeffding bounds this by \(2W\exp(-(d-O(1))^2/(2m))\). For instance \(d=\lceil\sqrt{2m(\alpha\log m+\log 4)}\rceil+3\) is sufficient. Over \(\mathbb C\) each diagonal univariate polynomial factors into affine stages, and at each frequency the diagonal error is supported on at most the prescribed number of rows; the polynomial predictor therefore reaches this count even within the diagonal-pencil design. This sharpness assertion concerns stage depth at a prescribed per-frequency rank budget, not the total gate complexity of DFT.

As a corollary, in the abstract normalized ledger \(d+mh/W\), the optimum over predictors and widths is \((1+o(1))\sqrt{m\log m}\). If the ledger were smaller than \((1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{m\log m}\) along a subsequence, then \(h/W=O(\sqrt{\log m/m})\); for any fixed \(\alpha<1/2\) this is at most \(m^{-\alpha}\) eventually, forcing the degree constant to tend to at least 1. Conversely choose degree \((1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{m\log m}\) in the construction above. Its error fraction is \(m^{-(1+\varepsilon)^2/2+o(1)}\), so the correction term is negligible. Let \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero after the asymptotic estimate. The coefficient 1 is for this idealized ledger, not the number of gate operations in the Hermite repair implementation.

## Explicit complex upper bound at fixed radix
For each fixed prime ell, D_ell tensor k has size at most C_ell ell^k sqrt(k+2) exp(C_ell sqrt(log(k+2) log log(k+3))) (adjust logarithm floor at small k). This strengthens the preprint's stated O_{ell,eps}(ell^k k^{1/2+eps}) to a concrete overhead in the complex nonuniform model, not necessarily the strongest possible bound or an unconditional novelty claim. The following parameter audit derives it from repair.tex and kernel.tex.

1. In characteristic zero, at each shift beta in repair.tex, the predictor equals a single univariate polynomial in c=sum b_j of degree O_ell(sqrt(m log m)). Indeed L_h=binom(c-u,h) as polynomial functions on the query cube, and c is affine-linear in Q_j^a by Fourier indicators. Factor that univariate polynomial over C. Its affine factors implement that shift's predictor in O_ell(sqrt(m log m)) pencils, with all coefficient row matrices diagonal; pad factors by the identity. There is just one row for each shift, R=ell^m, no Clifford root or field-extension inflation. The bad fraction and Hermite repair lemma of repair.tex apply verbatim with width s=floor(R/(2m^3)). The ell+2 predictor applications retain diagonal pencils.
2. For the Hermite checks choose nodes to be all R-th roots. H and H^T are truncation/padding of an R-point radix-ell DFT or its transpose, so the four pointwise row-map passes (form HPX,HX and lift a,za) cost O_ell(Rm) per ordinary address, using classical radix FFT. At a frequency, let j<=s be the known number of bad rows. Use the first j of the s moments to solve H_J and return a supported only on the first j entries, using inverse transposed/ordinary square Vandermondes on the j known nodes. Each inverse costs O(j log^2(j+2)) gates: interpolate by Lagrange leaves y_i/Q'(a_i), combine partial numerators P_left Q_right + Q_left P_right with fixed product-tree polynomials Q, and use ordinary FFT convolution at each level; transpose this fixed linear circuit for the inverse transpose. The diagonal multipliers and all constants are precomputed in this nonuniform model. Hence the additional spectral-table arithmetic at a fixed frequency costs O_ell(s m²), no more than O_ell(R) for s<=R/(2m³); row conversions remain recursive calls counted by the template. This pointwise implementation is necessary: blindly evaluating an arbitrary dense table would give exponential in m overhead.
3. In kernel.tex take ell fixed, L=max(ell,ceil(sqrt(log m/log log(m+3)))), t=floor(L/ell), and q the smallest power of two at least m^{1/(L-t)}. Rank of the Gram form is D <= sum_{j=0}^{ell-1} binom(L,j) q^j = O_ell((Lq)^{ell-1}); the paper's monomial expansion proof gives this uniformly in L. Charged rectangles number <=2^L q^t and each source/destination label has at most (1+log_2 q)^L incidences across the disjoint equality/first-differing-bit rectangles. A charged common transform costs <=2DR per j,a; each free endpoint costs <=2D per incident row and a. Therefore the call count per pencil per row is O_ell(D[(1+log_2 q)^L + 2^L m q^{-(L-t)}]). This is <= exp(O_ell(sqrt(log m log log m))) because log q=O_ell(log m/L), m q^{-(L-t)}<=1 and L log(1+log q)=O(sqrt(log m log log m)). The total across all pencils plus the sparse repairs is b<= sqrt(m) exp(O_ell(sqrt(log m log log m))), and A=2(m+D-1)=Theta(m). The paper's routing is used unchanged to share free-endpoint Fourier calls.
4. All non-call overhead is m^{O_ell(1)} per entry. To see this without hiding growing template constants: q^L <= 2^L m^{L/(L-t)} <= m^{O_ell(1)}; the number of all rectangles is at most (5q)^L by writing their unequal coordinates as first-bit prefix rectangles and all equal coordinates as fixed letters, hence is polynomial. For non-call work it suffices a crude enumeration over all rectangles, label pairs, queries and powers; the input and output coordinate changes and phases are monomial and thus O(one gate per entry), aggregation over rectangles and labels is bounded by the polynomial enumeration. The all-shift row matrices of each pencil are diagonal by step (1), so even the brute-force aggregation never performs a dense R-by-R row-map. The few non-diagonal repair passes were charged in step (2). Padding from the work-row count R q^L is at most a factor two using r_m=ceil(log_ell(R q^L))=O_ell(m) inactive digits as in kernel.tex. The remaining O_ell(m) digits are handled directly.
5. For each large current k choose m ≡1 mod ell between k^delta and 2k^delta for a small constant delta>0, with delta times the polynomial exponent in step (4) less than 1/4. Run the above template with f=floor((k-r_m)/A), so f<=k/A<= k^{1-delta} and f>0 for large k. If T(k) is normalized cost per entry, T(k)<=O(k^{1/4})+b_m T(f). Divide by sqrt(k) so b_m sqrt(f/k)<=exp(C sqrt(log k log log k)). Along a recursion path log f <=(1-delta)log k. Thus the logarithms of the overhead multipliers sum to O(sqrt(log k log log k)) by a geometric series with ratio sqrt(1-delta). The O(log log k) additive terms are absorbed into that exponential, and bounded k use direct radix FFT. This proves the stated bound. Every constant in the exponent depends only on the fixed radix ell.


## Transfer to all exact complex finite abelian transforms
Let F(k)=sqrt(k+2) exp(C sqrt(log(k+2) log log(k+3))), monotone after fixing small k. The fixed-radix bound applies to the binary radix. In layers.tex, Lemma [Tensor powers of a shear] expresses U^{tensor j} over C via tensoring one fixed GL_4 word for U^{tensor 2} in monomial matrices and local binary Fourier operations. Tensoring each Fourier letter decomposes it into disjoint Walsh blocks whose total width is <=2^j and whose Walsh exponents are <=j. Thus a shear block of width 2^j costs O(2^j F(j)); an entire monomial or synchronized shear layer on O(n) scalar registers costs O(n F(log n)), independent of auxiliary field arguments (E=C).

Complex polynomial convolution follows by a simplified packing.tex. Let B=C_0 ceil(log n); choose distinct primes <=B whose product exceeds n (prime number theorem, or Chebyshev with large C_0); let N be a longest prefix product <=n/B. For large n its next prime is <=B, so n/B²<N<=n/B. Let r=ceil(8n/N); 8n<=rN<=9n and r=O(B²). The original packing lemma applies with e=1: put both arrays in C[z]/z^r[X]/(X^N-1), occupying only the lower half of each axis, perform its cyclic convolution and gather standard coefficient indices. This prevents wrap on both axes. CRT identifies the N-address cyclic convolution with a product prime grid. Over C all prime-order roots are available, so there is no characteristic-zero descent or deformation step. Each prime-side Fourier transform of size p<=B has a word of O(log B) shear/monomial layers acting as F_p⊕I on O(p) slots: use its geometric evaluation form with the occupied workspace lemma of layers.tex and a binary radix working length between 2p and 4p, whose elementary radix word has length O(log p). By the fiber synchronization lemma, the tensor product over the prime grid retains O(log B) layers and O(N) total registers, uniformly in the number of prime axes. Run this separately over the r coefficient positions of both operands, then one inverse grid transform on the result. Total gate cost O(n log B F(log n)). The N pointwise products of jets use elementary exact FFT convolution on O(r) entries with O(r log r) gates each, hence O(n log B). Thus polynomial multiplication over C of arbitrary length n has O(n log log n F(log n)) gates. Its fixed-operand specialization is linear at constant-factor cost by propagating constant and linear parts through every gate. Bluestein’s identity with eta²=omega gives an arbitrary cyclic DFT as two diagonal scalings and a fixed Toeplitz convolution of length O(n), giving this same refined bound for every cyclic size n.

For arbitrary finite abelian groups, use the bounded number of large axes and small groups of grids.tex, with c=1/24. Large axes a>N^c, at most 1/c, use the above individual cyclic bound on each fiber. For a small group, the working lengths b_a between 2a and 6a are factored into pairwise coprime d<=3 (ceil(log_2 N))² by the prime-prefix construction of grids.tex. Each short F_d admits a word of O(log log N) layers and O(d) workspace by binary padding and the occupied workspace lemma. Good’s CRT factorization and fiber synchronization give such a word for F_{b_a}; apply occupied workspace and synchronization once more to the actual small-group axes, exactly as in the published grids.tex argument. Over C the extension degree is one, so each such O(log log N)-layer group costs O(N log log N F(log N)) gates across all its complement fibers. Summing over a bounded number of groups, and absorbing log log N into the exponential overhead, proves:
  C_C(G) <= C N sqrt(log N) exp(C sqrt(log log N * log log log N))
for N sufficiently large, all abelian G of order N, in the exact nonuniform binary weighted-sum model. Forward and inverse have the same count up to O(N) normalizing scalars, and J independent transforms separately give J times the upper bound. This argument assumes the published complex Fourier routing/occupied-workspace/fiber synchronization lemmas; it does not independently reproduce all those earlier lemmas. No endpoint claim, no other fields claim, and no bit complexity or uniform construction claim.
