# Rank-four internal sunflower extraction for rich path and paw patterns

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

We prove the full \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\) internal sunflower bound for two additional patterns of overlapping rich pair types, without any gap assumption on the support sizes of leaf labels. This is a conditional result, not the unrestricted rank-four case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

## Statement

Let \(H\) be a finite simple four-partite four-uniform hypergraph with \(E\) edges and \(m\ge2\). All four triple projection types and all nonrich pair projection types have maximum multiplicity at most \(Lm\), for a fixed \(L\ge1\). The graph of rich coordinate pair types is one of:

* The path \(A-X-Y-B\). Each occupied \(AX,YB\) fiber has at least \(C_Lm^3\) edges; each occupied \(XY\) fiber has at least \(C_Lm^2\).
* The paw, a triangle on \(X,Y,Z\) with a pendant coordinate \(A\) joined to \(X\). Each occupied \(XY,XZ,XA\) fiber has at least \(C_Lm^2\) edges, and each occupied \(YZ\) fiber has at least \(C_Lm^3\).

There are no constraints on the number of distinct rich neighbors of a leaf label. For sufficiently large \(C_L\) there is a subfamily of size \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\) whose every realized intersection has an \(m\)-petal sunflower witness internally. For nonempty intersections alone the bounds are \(\Omega_L(E/m^3)\) for the path and \(\Omega_L(E/m^2)\) for the paw.

## Initial thinning and support locks

First activate edges independently with probability \(\alpha/m\), choosing \(\alpha>0\) sufficiently small depending only on \(L\). Delete an activated edge if another activated edge agrees with it at some capped projection. Conditioning on its activation, a union bound over at most ten capped types shows that the deletion probability is at most \(10L\alpha\). Thus choose an outcome of size at least \(\alpha E/(2m)\) with all capped projections injective.

Write \(S\) for the hub types adjacent to leaves of the rich coordinate graph: \(S=\{X,Y\}\) for the path and \(S=\{X\}\) for the paw. Each hub has a unique leaf. Fix \(Q\) a large absolute constant. Call a rich type quadratic or cubic according to the statement. Before entering the lock routine, and after each ensuing restriction, iteratively delete every occupied rich pair fiber whose size is below \(Qm\) for a quadratic type or below \(Qm^2\) for a cubic type. Charge this to the original fiber count \(E/(C_Lm^2)\) or \(E/(C_Lm^3)\). Over the entire nested process each fiber is deleted at most once, so this costs at most \(4QE/(C_Lm)\) edges. This bound holds without assuming the intermediate restrictions preserve rich fibers.

Initially assign every hub sampling probability \(p_U=1/64\). At every stage, for each unlocked leaf \(V\) with hub \(U\), view its incident edges as buckets indexed by pairs \((v,u)\), the bucket weight being its current edge count. For each label \(v\) take its \(K=\lceil64m/p_U\rceil\) heaviest buckets (or all if fewer), with ties arbitrary. If the sum of their weights over the labels \(v\) is greater than one sixty-fourth of the current host mass, restrict to those buckets, lock the leaf, and change only this hub's sampling rate to \(1/(64K)\). Reapply the rich-fiber peeling and repeat. There are at most two locks and each hub's rate changes at most once; all final rates satisfy \(p_U\ge c_0/m\), for example \(c_0=1/(64\cdot4097)\). The restrictions lose at most a factor \(64\) each, with additive losses bounded as above. For \(C_L\) large compared to \(Q/\alpha\), the final cleaned host \(H_*\) has \(M=\Omega_L(E/m)\) edges, all capped projections injective, all occupied rich pair fibers above the prescribed \(Q\)-thresholds, and all unlocked leaves satisfy the weighted top-\(K\) test.

## Hub activation and the two-hub top-weight estimate

Independently activate each label in each hub \(U\) with its assigned probability \(p_U\); keep only edges whose hub labels are all activated. Put \(P=\prod_{U\in S}p_U\). The expected initial sample size is \(PM\). For each locked leaf label, if at least two of its current-host hub neighbors were activated, delete all its edges. Conditioned on an edge's selection, at this leaf there are at most \(K-1\) other eligible hub labels, and the deletion probability is at most \(p_U K\le1/64\). The loss in expectation over all locked leaves is at most \(PM/32\).

Now repeatedly peel every occupied rich fiber of size below \(3m\), and every unlocked positive leaf label having fewer than \(m\) distinct surviving hub neighbors. Each rich pair \(R\) is charged at most \(3m\) once, and only if its fixed hub labels (if any) were activated. If \(F_R=Qm\) or \(Qm^2\) is the current minimum fiber size of its type, the expected loss for a given rich type \(R\) is at most
\[
 \frac{3mM}{F_R}\prod_{U\in R\cap S}p_U.
\]
In these two patterns the quadratic types contain all hubs and the cubic types omit exactly one. Therefore this is at most \(3PM/(Qc_0)\) in all cases.

It remains to bound the mass charged when an unlocked \(v\) is peeled. At that time at most \(m\) activated buckets \((v,u)\) contribute, so bound by the sum of the \(m\) heaviest sampled buckets for \(v\), referring to the entire initial activation sample, before any deletions. Let \(M_v\) be the weight at \(v\) in \(H_*\); its top-\(K\) buckets contribute in expectation at most \(P\) times their total weight.

Every other bucket has original weight \(w\le M_v/K\). In the paw there is no other sampled hub, so the light buckets contribute at most \(m M_v/K\le PM_v/64\), using \(P=p_X\). In the path put \(p'=P/p_U\), the other hub rate. Within a fixed bucket its edges use distinct labels in the other hub by the triple injectivity. Conditional on \(u\) being activated, the activated bucket weight is therefore \(Z\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(w,p')\). If there are light buckets then all these buckets have \(w\ge Qm^2\); in particular \(p'w\ge Qc_0m\). For any such bucket, when \(Q\) is large,
\[
 \mathbb E[Z\,1_{\{Z>2p'M_v/K\}}]
 \ \le\ \mathbb E[Z\,1_{\{Z>2p'w\}}]
 \ \le\ \delta\, p'w
\]
with \(\delta=1/64\). The second estimate follows from the binomial size-bias identity: \(\mathbb E[Z1_{\{Z>2p'w\}}]=wp'\Pr(1+\operatorname{Bin}(w-1,p')>2p'w)\); when \(p'w\ge4\), the last event is contained in exceeding \(1.5p'w\) in the binomial variable, with probability at most \(e^{-c p'w}\) by Chernoff. Choose \(Q\) sufficiently large in terms of \(c_0\). Deterministically, the \(m\) largest sampled light buckets have total at most \(2mp'M_v/K\) plus the sum of all activated light-bucket overshoots, bounded here by the whole weight on the overshoot event. The latter has expectation at most \(\delta PM_v\), since activating \(u\) is independent of the other hub. The first bound is at most \(PM_v/32\). Independence between different buckets is neither asserted nor needed.

Summing over \(v\), the unlocked leaf cost is at most \(PM(1/64+1/32+\delta)\) for each path leaf, and \(PM(1/64+1/64)\) for the paw leaf. Combining this with lock and rich-fiber losses, with \(Q\) a sufficiently large fixed constant, leaves expected mass at least \(PM/2\) after all peeling. Since \(P=\Omega(m^{-2})\) for the path and \(\Omega(m^{-1})\) for the paw, the claimed nonempty size follows.

## Internal witnesses, including the empty kernel

Any nonempty realized pair kernel belongs to a rich type and has at least \(3m\) edges in its fiber; its two-coordinate link is a matching by triple injectivity. No triple kernel is realized. A locked leaf has only one hub label, so cannot occur as a singleton intersection. Every unlocked positive leaf has at least \(m\) distinct hub neighbors; choose one petal per neighbor. In each rich fiber at most \(2(m-1)\) edges meet previously used labels of the other two free coordinates, again by triple injectivity. For a nonleaf coordinate label, two rich neighbor types exist. In its linear three-partite link, a positive vertex in either neighbor side has degree at least \(3m\). From a positive vertex in the first side obtain \(3m\) distinct neighbors in the second. If a maximal matching has fewer than \(m\) edges, its fewer than \(3m\) vertices cover the link; one of these second-side vertices is outside that cover. By linearity each cover vertex meets at most one edge incident to it, contradicting its degree at least \(3m\). Thus all nonempty kernels have internal witnesses.

For the empty kernel, if an \(m\)-matching has at least \(E/2\) edges avoiding its endpoints, apply the nonempty proof on that residue and add back the matching. In an occupied surviving rich pair, its two fixed endpoint labels avoid the removed labels, and the removal of \(m\) labels from each of its other two parts destroys at most \(2Lm^2\) edges by the original triple caps. So the rich hypotheses survive after increasing the original constants by \(2L\); capped hypotheses persist. The matching creates no new nonempty kernels. Otherwise some vertex has degree at least \(E/(8m)\), by the removal cost of an existing matching of size \(m\) or by the cover supplied by a shorter maximal matching. Apply the [established full rank-three internal extraction theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) to its simple link and lift, losing another \(\Omega(m^{-3})\) proportion. This proves the full result.

The method's key additional ingredient over one-hub activation is preservation of the weighted top-bucket budget under a second independent activation: distinct labels from triple injectivity give binomial light-bucket concentration, while re-peeling after every lock keeps the mean at least a constant times \(m\). The cubic thresholds presently ensure this and pay for peeling if a fiber does not fix all activated hubs. Quadratic thresholds on all rich pairs in these two patterns, disconnected rich matchings, and unrestricted rank four are not claimed.


## General reusable extension: incidence-adjusted quotas under arbitrary many sampled types

The same proof is not specific to two hubs: let \(k\ge3\) be fixed, assume all triple and nonrich pair projections capped by \(Lm\), and let the graph \(J\) of rich pair types on the \(k\) coordinates have no isolated vertices. Let \(\ell\) count its degree-one vertices. For each type \(U\), let \(b_U\) count its degree-one neighbors, and set \(S=\{U:b_U>0\}\). For any rich pair \(R\) of types put \(a_R=\sum_{U\in S\setminus R}b_U\). If each occupied rich fiber of type \(R\) originally has at least \(C_{k,L}m^{2+a_R}\) edges, one keeps \(\Omega_{k,L}(E/m^{1+\ell})\) with all nonempty intersections witnessed internally. This statement allows adjacent degree-one vertices (isolated rich edges) and does not impose any gap on leaf-label support.

After the same initial conflict thinning, the clean-fiber threshold for type \(R\) is \(Qm^{1+a_R}\); global peeling costs \(O_k(QE/(C_{k,L}m))\) and is allowed after every lock. For each leaf of \(J\), the lock procedure affects the probability \(p_U\) of its unique hub \(U\) only; after \(j\) locks there it satisfies \(p_U\ge c_km^{-j}\) for an absolute \(c_k>0\) depending on \(k\). Later locks at that same hub decrease the rate, so all locked support collision bounds persist. For the unlocked concentration tests use the current \(K=\lceil C_km/p_U\rceil\) with sufficiently large \(C_k\) instead of 64, also in the test fraction and lock rate. Since \(p_U\) can drop only when a new leaf locks, all surviving unlocked leaves are tested at their final rates. Nesting preserves mass up to an absolute factor depending on \(k\). Final peeling of a rich fiber at threshold \(km\) can be charged only when its fixed hub labels are active. Relative to expected mass \(PM\), its type's charge is at most \(k/[Qm^{a_R}\prod_{U\in S\setminus R}p_U]=O_k(1/Q)\).

For a bucket fixing a leaf label \(v\) and its rich neighboring label \(u\), condition on those fixed labels that belong to \(S\) being active. Distinct edges in this bucket have distinct labels in *each* remaining hub type, by triple injectivity. Their survival events under the remaining activations are mutually independent. Thus the bucket weight is binomial with success probability \(p'=\prod_{W\in S\setminus\{V,U\}}p_W\), and its original cleaned weight \(w\ge Qm^{1+a_{\{V,U\}}}\) gives mean at least \(c_k^{k}Qm\). If the leaf type \(V\) itself is sampled, every bucket—and hence the deterministic \(O(mp'M_v/K)\) light-bucket contribution—is zero when \(v\) is inactive, providing the factor \(p_V\). Otherwise that factor is 1. Averaging the heavy and light bounds therefore charges \(O_k(PM/C_k)\) per leaf, as in the two-hub proof. Final support collapse for locked leaves and greedy selection for unlocked leaves are unchanged, now with \(k-2\) free petal coordinates. A nonleaf coordinate still has linear link (triple injectivity), and the usual matching-cover argument uses \((k-1)(m-1)\) vertices; choose the final fiber minimum \(km\). Since \(P=\Omega_k(m^{-\ell})\), this establishes the bound.

In rank four this yields the stated full bound when \(\ell\le3\), taking the rank-three link fallback if there is no reserved \(m\)-matching. The rich-star, path and paw thresholds in this note are exactly the incidence-adjusted exponents \(2+a_R\). A disconnected rich matching has four leaves and this argument alone can give only the nonempty \(m^{-5}\) scale; the next section handles it by a different activation. Together the results cover all no-isolated four-coordinate patterns at the stated incidence-adjusted thresholds. The unrestricted case is not claimed.


## Complete missing rich-pattern case at quartic fiber threshold

In rank four, if the rich coordinate graph is the disconnected matching \(AB,CD\), the general estimate just above can lose four powers of \(m\) in the lock step. Nonetheless the full \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\) result holds in this case when both rich types have all occupied fibers at least \(C_Lm^4\), all triple and nonrich pair caps being \(Lm\), and there is no leaf-support gap. Here \(a_R=2\) for both types, so this uses the general lemma's same thresholds. Apply its initial cleaning and adaptive lock routine. If there are at most three locks, that proof already gives a sample of size \(\Omega(E/m^4)\). It remains to replace only the final all-four-locked outcome.

Every coordinate has a unique leaf neighbor and therefore can only be targeted by one lock. All lock rates are initially \(1/64\), so every locked profile has support at most \(K=\lceil4096m\rceil\) after all four locks. In each of the two rich coordinate pairs independently, the occupied pair-label support graph is simple bipartite and has maximum degree \(K\). Color each of these two support graphs properly with \(2K\) edge colors (greedy on the edge-conflict graph suffices, or use König's line coloring theorem with \(K\)). Select one uniform color for each of the two support graphs, independently across the two graphs. Retain a quadruple if both of its rich label pairs have selected colors. If \(D=2K\), expected retained mass is \(M/D^2\). A fixed rich label pair can survive only with probability \(1/D\). Iteratively delete any positive rich fiber with fewer than \(4m\) remaining edges. The number of original occupied fibers in the current host is at most \(M/(Qm^3)\) per rich type by its cleaned threshold. Total expected loss for both types is at most \(8mM/(Qm^3D)=O(M/(QmD^2))\), since \(D=O(m)\). It is much smaller than \(M/D^2\). After this selection and peeling, each coordinate label is paired to at most one neighbor in its rich type, so no singleton intersections are possible. Rich pair intersections have internal matching links, nonrich pairs and triples are injective. This gives actually \(\Omega(E/m^3)\) in the all-locked branch. Reserve plus rank-three fallback handles the empty kernel as above.

This edge-color substitution couples activations inside each fully locked support component: it collapses both endpoints and costs a single \(m\) factor per component instead of two. It does not require independent retention between fibers of the same component, since with all leaves locked there is no remaining support-diversity test.
