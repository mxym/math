# Root markers beyond caterpillar heavy cores

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

We reconstruct families of trees with arbitrary heavy-core topology from the ordinary chromatic symmetric function, among all trees. These are restricted families, not a resolution of [the Stanley tree isomorphism question](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18940). Write \(U\) for the unsigned tree cut polynomial recovered by removing the known signs from the power-sum coefficients, and use the heavy-skeleton decomposition at cutoff 2 in [the high-layer factorization](StanleyHeavyEdgeCore.md#1-factorization-by-heavy-components). Each rooted block consists of its skeleton vertex, single leaves, and branches of order two. The high-layer factorization recovers \(q\), the multiset of block sizes and their rooted high tails. In particular it recovers each rooted type of order at least four by the first deficit coefficient, which counts the nonroot leaves. At order three there are just the rooted star with two leaves and the rooted path beginning at an end. To avoid needing to identify them from the high tail, the second result below assumes orders at least four.

## A weighted rooted polynomial

For a positively integer-weighted tree with root \(v\), enumerate edge cuts, recording the root component of total weight \(i\) by \(z^i\), and all other components of weight \(j\) by \(x_j\). Write this polynomial as \(R_T\). Its unrooted polynomial \(U_T\) is obtained termwise by \(z^i\mapsto x_i\). The recursion at the root is
\[
R_T=z^{w(v)}\prod_{B}(R_B+U_B),
\]
over the rooted branches at \(v\), because each branch is either joined to the root component or detached. If a branch has total weight \(n\), then \(R_B+U_B\) is monic linear in \(x_n\): the term \(x_n\) comes precisely from the uncut \(U_B\); all remaining \(x_j\) have \(j<n\), since each proper component omits at least one vertex of positive weight, and the uncut rooted term is \(z^n\). Hence each factor is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q[z,x_1,x_2,\ldots]\). The \(z\)-valuation of \(R_T\) determines \(w(v)\), and unique factorization determines the multiset of these normalized factors. Evaluating each at \(z=0\) and subtracting gives \(R_B\). Induction reconstructs every branch. The unweighted version was proved by [Aliste-Prieto, de Mier and Zamora, Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08186); the argument here includes arbitrary positive weights.

## Residue marker after collapsing typed leaves

Assume the typed hypotheses of [the two-rooted-type extraction](StanleyCaterpillarRootedTypeMarkers.md#extract-both-type-counts) with \(q\ge3\), exactly two distinct block types, one on all skeleton leaves and the other on all its internal vertices. The core no longer needs to be a path. The extraction before the path-order step recovers the whole typed skeleton cut deck, in any rival. The one-edge singleton cuts certify the leaf roles. Keep only records with no leaf-only component; this is equivalent to keeping every edge incident to a skeleton leaf. On the remaining internal core, let \(a(v)\) count the attached skeleton leaves. Every component \(C\) of any core cut now has its size \(r(C)\) and attachment count \(a(C)=\sum_{v\in C}a(v)\) encoded in the retained data. The fully cut record gives the attachment multiset.

Suppose some \(M\ge2\) divides all the attachment counts except one. This is certified by the recovered multiset, so it also holds in every rival. A component contains the unique exceptional vertex if and only if \(a(C)\not\equiv0\bmod M\). Give \(v\) the positive weight \(J+a(v)\) for any fixed integer \(J>q\). Replacing the unique marked component in each record by \(z^{Jr(C)+a(C)}\), and all others by \(x_{Jr(C)+a(C)}\), gives \(R_T\) for this weighted rooted core, including its cut multiplicities. Rooted weighted reconstruction recovers the \(a(v)\), hence the typed skeleton and then the whole tree.

More generally the residue condition may be replaced by a distinguished slot in the attachment multiset whose subset-sum supports \((|S|,\sum_{v\in S}a(v))\) for sets containing and avoiding that slot are disjoint. The same marking procedure works by testing the recovered component labels. The disjointness itself certifies that the slot's attachment count is unique (compare singleton sets).

## A sole rooted type as a tag, without any leaf-role condition

Suppose \(q\ge2\) and there are just two distinct rooted block types \(A,B\), of masses \(s_A,s_B>4\) with \(\gcd(s_A,s_B)>2\). Suppose \(B\) occurs once. The first high deficits and masses certify both types and their multiplicities. For each prescribed intact mass list \(m_i\), the high factors \(x_{m_i-k_i}\) with \(k_i\in\{0,1,2\}\), together with \(x_1^{\sum_i k_i}\) and with \(x_2=0\), give the labeled truncated rooted series, using the same factorial labeling as in the two-type extraction. Here no bound growing with \(q\) is needed: each requested high component has order \(>2\), so contains a skeleton root. Its actual intact mass is the sum of the full block sizes of its contained roots; it is at least the requested order \(m_i-k_i\) and divisible by \(\gcd(s_A,s_B)\). Thus it is at least \(m_i\). Because these actual intact masses use disjoint root sets and \(\sum_i m_i\) is the whole tree order, all inequalities are equalities and all roots are high. In particular the assertion holds equally for rivals. This same argument permits dropping the total-low-mass hypothesis from the prior high-signature extraction, as long as the requested indices are strictly above the cutoff and the common divisor exceeds the deficit bound.

At a fixed mass \(m\), there are at most two type-count pairs, depending on whether the component contains \(B\). If both are possible their rooted singleton series have a nontrivial ratio \(F_B/F_A^{s_B/s_A}\) (here \(s_B/s_A\) must be integral). Writing \(F_i=(1+y)^{s_i-1-2v_i}(1+y+y^2)^{v_i}\), the ratio is a product of integer powers of \(1+y,1+y+y^2\), not identically 1 because the types differ (if both exponents vanish, comparing their size relation forces \(s_B=s_A\) and \(v_B=v_A\)). Its difference from 1 has leading degree 1 or 2. Thus the two functions at each ambiguous index are linearly independent modulo \(y^3\). For every list, taking the products in independent variables and recovering their coefficients lifts the deck to the tagged component, which can now be used as the root component in the weighted \(R_T\). Give skeleton vertices their intact block masses as weights. The rooted weighted tree plus the root marker determines every attached type even when the weights coincide. Restoring the blocks reconstructs the full tree.

For the equal-mass case the same conclusion holds at any common mass \(s\ge4\), using only \(k_i\le1\). Indeed the singleton coefficient of a rooted block is \(s-1-v_i\), so the equal-mass types differ already at that degree. For instance at mass four one can mark a vertex of an otherwise arbitrary skeleton by replacing its three single leaves with one single leaf and a two-vertex arm. Every root-to-root edge is heavy and every arm cut is light.

## The direct proper-tree case, no padding

A proper tree is one in which every nonleaf has an adjacent original leaf. Consider a proper tree of order at least three and let \(q\) count its original nonleaves, read from the total order and the number of one-edge cuts with a singleton side. For each nonleaf \(v\) give the induced nonleaf core weight \(w(v)=1+\#\{\text{original leaves adjacent to }v\}\). Then \(U\) specialized at \(x_1=0\) is precisely the weighted cut polynomial of this core: each original leaf edge must be kept to avoid a singleton, and keeping them guarantees that each core component contains at least one attached leaf. In any rival, the same \(q\) is the maximum possible number of nonsingleton components. A cut reaches \(q\) components in \(U|_{x_1=0}\) only if each of those components contains exactly one original nonleaf, and hence every nonleaf has an attached original leaf. The degree-\(q\) term therefore certifies properness and gives the multiset of weights to both trees.

If for some \(M\ge2\) all but one weight is divisible by \(M\), mark the component of weight not divisible by \(M\) in each monomial. The rooted weighted reconstruction applies exactly as above, and \(w(v)-1\) restores the original leaves. In particular the ordinary chromatic symmetric function reconstructs the family formed by adding one new leaf at every vertex of an arbitrary tree and one additional new leaf at a single designated vertex. The same proof allows any repeated even weights and just one odd weight.

For prior art on this extraction see [Orellana–Tomba, Proposition 3.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18940#page=7), where the weighted core also gives the singleton-free star-basis layer. The distinctive step here is marking a single component when almost all weights may repeat, rather than requiring unique weights at every branching core vertex as in [Zeng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18179).
