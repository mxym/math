# Root markers beyond caterpillar heavy cores

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

We reconstruct families of trees with arbitrary heavy-core topology from the ordinary chromatic symmetric function, among all trees. These are restricted families, not a resolution of [the Stanley tree isomorphism question](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18940). Write \(U\) for the unsigned tree cut polynomial recovered by removing the known signs from the power-sum coefficients, and use the heavy-skeleton decomposition at cutoff 2 in [the high-layer factorization](StanleyHeavyEdgeCore.md#1-factorization-by-heavy-components). Each rooted block consists of its skeleton vertex, single leaves, and branches of order two. The high-layer factorization recovers \(q\), the multiset of block sizes and their rooted high tails. In particular it recovers each rooted type of order at least four by the first deficit coefficient, which counts the nonroot leaves. At order three there are just the rooted star with two leaves and the rooted path beginning at an end. To avoid needing to identify them from the high tail, the second result below assumes orders at least four.

## A weighted rooted polynomial

For a positively integer-weighted tree with root \(v\), enumerate edge cuts, recording the root component of total weight \(i\) by \(z^i\), and all other components of weight \(j\) by \(x_j\). Write this polynomial as \(R_T\). Its unrooted polynomial \(U_T\) is obtained termwise by \(z^i\mapsto x_i\). The recursion at the root is
\[
R_T=z^{w(v)}\prod_{B}(R_B+U_B),
\]
over the rooted branches at \(v\), because each branch is either joined to the root component or detached. If a branch has total weight \(n\), then \(R_B+U_B\) is monic linear in \(x_n\): the term \(x_n\) comes precisely from the uncut \(U_B\); all remaining \(x_j\) have \(j<n\), since each proper component omits at least one vertex of positive weight, and the uncut rooted term is \(z^n\). Hence each factor is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q[z,x_1,x_2,\ldots]\). The \(z\)-valuation of \(R_T\) determines \(w(v)\), and unique factorization determines the multiset of these normalized factors. Evaluating each at \(z=0\) and subtracting gives \(R_B\). Induction reconstructs every branch. The unweighted version was proved by [Aliste-Prieto, de Mier and Zamora, Theorem 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08186); the argument here includes arbitrary positive weights.

## Residue marker after collapsing typed leaves

Assume the typed hypotheses of [the two-rooted-type extraction](StanleyCaterpillarRootedTypeMarkers.md#extract-both-type-counts) with \(q\ge3\), exactly two distinct block types, one on all skeleton leaves and the other on all its internal vertices. The core no longer needs to be a path. The extraction before the path-order step recovers the whole typed skeleton cut deck, in any rival. The one-edge singleton cuts certify the leaf roles. Keep only records with no leaf-only component; this is equivalent to keeping every edge incident to a skeleton leaf. On the remaining internal core, let \(a(v)\) count the attached skeleton leaves. Every component \(C\) of any core cut now has its size \(r(C)\) and attachment count \(a(C)=\sum_{v\in C}a(v)\) encoded in the retained data. The fully cut record gives the attachment multiset.

Suppose some \(M\ge2\) divides all the attachment counts except one. This is certified by the recovered multiset, so it also holds in every rival. A component contains the unique exceptional vertex if and only if \(a(C)\not\equiv0\bmod M\). Give \(v\) the positive weight \(J+a(v)\) for any fixed integer \(J>q\). Replacing the unique marked component in each record by \(z^{Jr(C)+a(C)}\), and all others by \(x_{Jr(C)+a(C)}\), gives \(R_T\) for this weighted rooted core, including its cut multiplicities. Rooted weighted reconstruction recovers the \(a(v)\), hence the typed skeleton and then the whole tree.

More generally the residue condition may be replaced by a distinguished slot in the attachment multiset whose subset-sum supports \((|S|,\sum_{v\in S}a(v))\) for sets containing and avoiding that slot are disjoint. The same marking procedure works by testing the recovered component labels. The disjointness itself certifies that the slot's attachment count is unique (compare singleton sets).

## A sole rooted type as a tag, without any leaf-role condition

Suppose \(q\ge2\) and there are just two distinct rooted block types \(A,B\), of masses \(s_A,s_B>4\) with \(\gcd(s_A,s_B)>2\). Suppose \(B\) occurs once. The first high deficits and masses certify both types and their multiplicities. For each prescribed intact mass list \(m_i\), the high factors \(x_{m_i-k_i}\) with \(k_i\in\{0,1,2\}\), together with \(x_1^{\sum_i k_i}\) and with \(x_2=0\), give the labeled truncated rooted series, using the same factorial labeling as in the two-type extraction. Here no bound growing with \(q\) is needed: each requested high component has order \(>2\), so contains a skeleton root. Its actual intact mass is the sum of the full block sizes of its contained roots; it is at least the requested order \(m_i-k_i\) and divisible by \(\gcd(s_A,s_B)\). Thus it is at least \(m_i\). Because these actual intact masses use disjoint root sets and \(\sum_i m_i\) is the whole tree order, all inequalities are equalities and all roots are high. In particular the assertion holds equally for rivals. This same argument permits dropping the total-low-mass hypothesis from the prior high-signature extraction, as long as the requested indices are strictly above the cutoff and the common divisor exceeds the deficit bound.

At a fixed mass \(m\), there are at most two type-count pairs, depending on whether the component contains \(B\). If both are possible their rooted singleton series have a nontrivial ratio \(F_B/F_A^{s_B/s_A}\) (here \(s_B/s_A\) must be integral). Writing \(F_i=(1+y)^{s_i-1-2v_i}(1+y+y^2)^{v_i}\), the ratio is a product of integer powers of \(1+y,1+y+y^2\), not identically 1 because the types differ (if both exponents vanish, comparing their size relation forces \(s_B=s_A\) and \(v_B=v_A\)). Its difference from 1 has leading degree 1 or 2. Thus the two functions at each ambiguous index are linearly independent modulo \(y^3\). For every list, taking the products in independent variables and recovering their coefficients lifts the deck to the tagged component, which can now be used as the root component in the weighted \(R_T\). Give skeleton vertices their intact block masses as weights. The rooted weighted tree plus the root marker determines every attached type even when the weights coincide. Restoring the blocks reconstructs the full tree.

For the equal-mass case the same conclusion holds at any common mass \(s\ge4\), using only \(k_i\le1\). Indeed the singleton coefficient of a rooted block is \(s-1-v_i\), so the equal-mass types differ already at that degree. For instance at mass four one can mark a vertex of an otherwise arbitrary skeleton by replacing its three single leaves with one single leaf and a two-vertex arm. Every root-to-root edge is heavy and every arm cut is light.

## The direct proper-tree case, no padding

A proper tree is one in which every nonleaf has an adjacent original leaf. Consider a proper tree of order at least three and let \(q\) count its original nonleaves, read from the total order and the number of one-edge cuts with a singleton side. For each nonleaf \(v\) give the induced nonleaf core weight \(w(v)=1+\#\{\text{original leaves adjacent to }v\}\). Then \(U\) specialized at \(x_1=0\) is precisely the weighted cut polynomial of this core: each original leaf edge must be kept to avoid a singleton, and keeping them guarantees that each core component contains at least one attached leaf. In any rival, the same \(q\) is the maximum possible number of nonsingleton components. A cut reaches \(q\) components in \(U|_{x_1=0}\) only if each of those components contains exactly one original nonleaf, and hence every nonleaf has an attached original leaf. The degree-\(q\) term therefore certifies properness and gives the multiset of weights to both trees.

If for some \(M\ge2\) all but one weight is divisible by \(M\), mark the component of weight not divisible by \(M\) in each monomial. The rooted weighted reconstruction applies exactly as above, and \(w(v)-1\) restores the original leaves. In particular the ordinary chromatic symmetric function reconstructs the family formed by adding one new leaf at every vertex of an arbitrary tree and one additional new leaf at a single designated vertex. The same proof allows any repeated even weights and just one odd weight.

For prior art on this extraction see [Orellana–Tomba, Proposition 3.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18940#page=7), where the weighted core also gives the singleton-free star-basis layer. The distinctive step here is marking a single component when almost all weights may repeat, rather than requiring unique weights at every branching core vertex as in [Zeng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18179).

## Two markers in a weighted core

These results take the positive-weight unsigned cut deck as input. In particular, the proper-tree specialization above supplies it and certifies the weight multiset, even in arbitrary rivals. Let \(a,b\) be the two marked vertices; \(q\) is the number of core vertices. In the cases below the deck recognizes every cut separating \(a,b\), because its component residue multiset recognizes whether their contributions lie together or apart. The number of such cuts is \(2^{q-1}(1-2^{-d})\) for \(d=\operatorname{dist}(a,b)\), counting all subsets of the \(q-1\) edges and then those avoiding the entire \(a\)-to-\(b\) path. Thus it also determines \(d\).

**Identical markers at distance one.** Suppose precisely two vertex weights are odd, all others even, and the two odd vertices are adjacent. A cut separates them exactly when it has two odd components. Distance one is certified by the preceding count. Root the two sides \(A,B\) of their connecting edge at \(a,b\). For each cut separating the vertices, replace the two odd components by powers of \(z,w\) in both orders, counting both even when their weights coincide. The resulting polynomial is
\[
S(z,w)=R_A(z)R_B(w)+R_A(w)R_B(z).
\]
To extract the factors work over \(K=\mathbb Q(x_1,x_2,\ldots)\) with finitely many relevant variables. The linear substitution on monomials \(z^i w^j\mapsto t_i t_j\) converts \(S\) into twice a product of two linear forms with coefficients in \(K\). Unique factorization recovers the unordered linear forms up to scalars, including a repeated form. Each genuine \(R\) has coefficient exactly 1 at its highest root exponent, the complete branch weight. Normalize each recovered form at its highest index and undo the substitution to recover \(R_A,R_B\). Reconstruct the weighted rooted sides and join their roots. Thus the two odd core weights may be equal, yet reconstruction still holds.

**Distinct residues at arbitrary distance.** The new case beyond a single recognizable root is when the two residues are additive inverses, e.g. 1 and 2 modulo 3: together they disappear into residue zero. If their sum is nonzero, the one-root argument already works by marking the components with residue in \(\{r,r+s\}\). Suppose all weights except the two marked ones vanish modulo \(M\), while \(a,b\) contribute distinct nonzero residues \(r,s\). A separated cut has separate \(r,s\) components; the joined residue \(r+s\bmod M\) is neither \(r\) nor \(s\). On separated cuts replace the two distinct residue components with their weight powers of \(z,w\), respectively, to obtain \(S(z,w)\). Let \(A,B\) be the endpoint sides of the marked path, each obtained by cutting the first edge from that endpoint. The largest possible \(z\)-exponent is total weight outside \(B\): the root component avoids \(b\) and therefore avoids the entirety of \(B\), and attains that weight by keeping every edge outside \(B\) and cutting the final path edge. Its coefficient as a polynomial in \(w,x_1,x_2,\ldots\) is exactly \(R_B(w)\), since only the cuts inside \(B\) remain free. Similarly the top \(w\)-coefficient gives \(R_A(z)\). When \(d=1\), join their roots.

For \(d\ge2\), remove the endpoint sides \(A,B\) and let \(I\) denote the nonempty remaining subtree, with endpoint attachment sites \(p_1,p_{d-1}\) on the original marked path. Choices of cuts inside \(A,B\) are independent of all edges in \(I\) and of the two attachment edges. In a separated cut the root components inside \(A,B\) always contribute the \(z,w\) powers of their respective internal cuts, whether or not those components attach to additional vertices in \(I\). Therefore \(Q=S/(R_A(z)R_B(w))\) is a polynomial in which \(z,w\) count only the extra weights from \(I\), and \(x_j\) still count unmarked components inside \(I\). Taking \([w^0]Q\) and subtracting its \(z\)-constant term forces the attachment to \(B\) to be cut and the attachment to \(A\) to be retained (all core weights are positive). The result is precisely \(R_I(z)\) rooted at \(p_1\). Interchanging roles extracts \(R_I(w)\) at \(p_{d-1}\).

These two rooted reconstructions determine their joint orbit in the unrooted weighted \(I\), since the distance between them is \(d-2\). In detail, center \(I\) at its unique middle vertex or the midpoint of its unique middle edge, using half-unit lengths if subdividing the middle edge. Every automorphism preserves this center. Each individual orbit specifies its depth and root-to-vertex path up to automorphism. Their given distance specifies the depth of their last common ancestor. First map their common prefix; at the branching point the two child subtrees can be matched simultaneously since same-type children can be permuted, and automorphisms within different child subtrees are independent. If one is an ancestor or the two sites coincide, only the longer path has to be mapped. This argument respects weights. Graft the recovered \(A,B\) at the two designated attachment sites. This reconstructs the full weighted core; identically marked nonadjacent vertices are not covered by the distinct-residue argument.


## Identical markers at distances two and three, and a path interior at any distance

Keep precisely two odd vertex weights, with no need for them to differ. The separated-cut count certifies their distance \(d\), and the odd components of separated cuts give the symmetric two-port polynomial \(S(z,w)\) as above. The following argument also obtains aggregate information for every \(d\ge2\), when there may be substantial branching between the marks.

Let \(A,B\) be the endpoint sides rooted at the odd vertices, as in the distinct-residue proof. Let \(I\) be the remaining interior tree of total weight \(M\), with attachment sites \(u,v\). Enumerate interior cuts together with the two attachment edges while forbidding a join between the marks. Define \(Q(z,w)\) by counting only extra weights from \(I\) joined to the first and second mark in \(z,w\), and all other components in \(x_j\). The endpoint-side cuts remain independent, so
\[
S(z,w)=R_A(z)R_B(w)Q(z,w)+R_B(z)R_A(w)Q(w,z).
\]

**Extract the interior diagonal and both endpoint sides.** In the polynomial \(Q(z,z)\) the sole term using \(x_M\) is \(x_M\) itself (cut both attachments and keep all interior edges). It is therefore irreducible, monic linear in \(x_M\), over \(\mathbb Q[z,x_1,x_2,\ldots]\). Its highest \(z\) term is \(d z^M\): to have no unmarked interior components, there must be exactly one cut on the \(d\)-edge marked path, and no other interior edge cut. In the diagonal factorization
\[
S(z,z)/2=R_A(z)R_B(z)Q(z,z),
\]
all irreducibles other than \(z\) and this interior factor come from off-root attachment factors of \(A,B\). Each is monic linear in its largest \(x\) variable and has highest \(z\) coefficient 1. Normalize all factors by their largest \(x\) variable; then the highest \(z\) coefficient \(d>1\) singles out \(Q(z,z)\). Its constant term in \(z\) is \(U(I)\), and division recovers \(R_A(z)R_B(z)\).

The highest \(z\) coefficient of the full two-variable \(S\) recovers either \(R_{\rm small}(w)\) if the endpoint-side total weights differ, or \(R_A(w)+R_B(w)\) if those weights agree. In fact the largest \(z\) exponent keeps everything outside the smaller opposite endpoint side, cuts the last path edge, and leaves only cuts in that opposite side free; if side weights agree both orientations contribute. The leading coefficient in \(w\) of the result is 1 in the first case and 2 in the second. Use the recovered product to obtain the other side in the first case, or use sum and product to recover the unordered pair of polynomial roots in the second. The weighted rooted factorization reconstructs both sides.

**Distance two.** There is one attachment vertex in \(I\), the same for both ports. Here \(Q(z,w)=U(I)+R_I(z)+R_I(w)\). Its diagonal gives \(R_I\), which reconstructs the rooted interior. Attach both recovered endpoint sides to that root.

**Distance three.** Cut the single interior path edge \(uv\) to obtain two rooted halves \(I_1,I_2\), with polynomials \(R_i,U_i\). Keeping neither attachment gives \(U(I)\); keeping only one gives the corresponding rooted interior polynomial; keeping both forces a cut at \(uv\). Consequently
\[
P(z):=Q(z,z)-U(I)=R_1(z)U_2+U_1R_2(z)+3R_1(z)R_2(z).
\]
Let \(\mathcal L\) be the linear substitution of \(x_i\) for \(z^i\) in positive-degree terms, fixing coefficients. Then
\[
U(I)=U_1U_2+\mathcal L(R_1R_2),\qquad
\mathcal L(P)=2U_1U_2+3\mathcal L(R_1R_2).
\]
It follows that \(U_1U_2=3U(I)-\mathcal L(P)\), and
\[
3P+U_1U_2=(U_1+3R_1)(U_2+3R_2).
\]
Each factor is irreducible and monic in its highest \(x\) variable, just as for ordinary attachment factors. Its positive \(z\) terms divided by 3 give the corresponding \(R_i\), so \(I\) with its two sites is determined up to exchanging the sites.

**Path interior at any distance.** If \(|V(I)|=d-1\), which is certified by the maximum number of components appearing in \(U(I)\), the interior really is the path between the two attachment sites. If the vertex weights of \(I\) satisfy \(\max w<2\min w\), the [weighted-path coarsening lemma](StanleyEqualOrderGraftMarkers.md#all-scalar-decorated-path-cores-after-deck-extraction) reconstructs this weighted path up to reversal from \(U(I)\). The ratio hypothesis is certified by the singleton weight multiset in the fully cut term. In particular the argument works for uniform interior weights.

**An endpoint-mass criterion for a branching interior at any distance.** There is another way to recover the full transfer using only its degree. Choose an arbitrary order for the recovered endpoint polynomials \(R_A,R_B\). Work over \(K=\mathbb Q(x_1,x_2,\ldots)\) in the finitely many variables in the deck, and let \(a=R_A/g,\ b=R_B/g\), where \(g\) is their gcd in \(K[z]\). If \(\deg a+\deg b\ge M\), the bivariate equation for \(S\) has a unique transfer among polynomials of total \((z,w)\) degree at most \(M\). Indeed the difference \(D\) of two transfers would satisfy
\[
a(z)b(w)D(z,w)+b(z)a(w)D(w,z)=0.
\]
The two displayed prefactors are coprime in \(K[z,w]\). Hence \(D=b(z)a(w)H\) with \(H(w,z)=-H(z,w)\). If \(H\ne0\), its degree is at least 1, so \(\deg D>M\), impossible. Solving the linear coefficient equations therefore finds \(Q\) uniquely. The two specializations \(Q(z,0)-U(I)\) and \(Q(0,w)-U(I)\) give the two individual rooted interiors, and their known distance \(d-2\) certifies their joint orbit. This recovers a branching interior at arbitrary distance subject to the computable endpoint-degree criterion.

**Peel identical outer layers.** If the recovered endpoint sides satisfy \(R_A=R_B\) and \(d\ge3\), divide \(S\) by \(R_A(z)R_A(w)\) to recover \(Q(z,w)+Q(w,z)\). For \(d\ge3\) the two attachment sites \(u,v\) in \(I\) are distinct. This symmetrized transfer consists of \(2U(I)\), the two one-variable polynomials \(R_I^u+R_I^v\) at \(z\) and \(w\), and the symmetric two-port polynomial for separating \(u,v\) by cuts in \(I\). Indeed the last summand is precisely the case in which both attachments are kept, and its two tagged weights are positive. Evaluate at one argument zero to recover the one-variable terms, and subtract them and \(2U(I)\) to recover the new two-port polynomial. Repeat the diagonal and endpoint argument on \(I\) at distance \(d-2\), which only requires this polynomial, \(U(I)\), and the known distance, not an intrinsic odd marking. Any number of identical outer layers can thus be removed until another reconstruction criterion applies. Reattach the stripped identical pairs in reverse order. A swap of the two current sites causes no ambiguity because the two attachments from that layer are isomorphic.

**Orient the two sides.** Once \(I\) and its two attachment sites are known up to simultaneous swap, compute its transfer \(Q\). The data predicted by the two possible assignments of \(A,B\) to these sites differ by
\[
\big(R_A(z)R_B(w)-R_B(z)R_A(w)\big)\big(Q(z,w)-Q(w,z)\big).
\]
If the first factor vanishes, normalized endpoint polynomials agree, so the sides are isomorphic as rooted weighted trees. If the second vanishes, evaluating at one variable zero and subtracting \(U(I)\) shows that the rooted interior polynomials at the two sites agree. The sites thus have the same individual orbit, and the ordered-pair orbit argument above gives an automorphism exchanging them. In either case the two assignments are isomorphic; otherwise \(S\) selects the assignment.

For example, the equal-order two-type graft deck with exactly two blocks of the second type can encode those vertices with odd weight \(J+1\) and every other core vertex with an even weight \(J\). Whenever the full two-type cut deck is extracted, this proves reconstruction at distances two and three without requiring a path core, and at arbitrary distance if all off-path branching is in the two endpoint sides. It also handles a branching interior under the endpoint-degree criterion, but not unrestricted branching along a longer interior.


## Hidden rooted outliers supply the two-port data directly

In the [equal-order graft setup](StanleyEqualOrderGraftMarkers.md), suppose precisely two blocks differ from the common background \(A\), with types \(B,C\ne A\), not necessarily distinct. Use the certified tail \(D\ge T_h\). Their positions can be on an arbitrary core tree. Put \(H_B=F_B/F_A,\ H_C=F_C/F_A\) in the truncated series over \(K=\mathbb Q(x_1,\ldots,x_h)\), with both \(P=H_B-1\) and \(Q_0=H_C-1\) nonzero. Choose a linear functional \(\ell\) with \(\ell(1)=0\) and both \(\ell(P)\) and \(\ell(Q_0)\) nonzero.

For each list of component cardinalities \((r_1,\ldots,r_k)\), take the labeled high-deficit query, divide by the background factors, and set all deficit variables except the first two to zero. Apply \(\ell\) to both remaining variables. A first or second side with no mark kills the term; both sides marked implies the marks were separated and there is exactly one mark on each. Every surviving labeling contributes \(\ell(P)\ell(Q_0)\). Divide this factor out, and divide by the factorial multiplicity of each size among labels \(3,\ldots,k\), which are just unmarked bookkeeping labels. Assign weight \(J+1\) (odd) to the two marks and weight \(J\) (even, positive) to every other core vertex. Give the two selected components powers \(z^{Jr_1+1},w^{Jr_2+1}\) and give the remaining ones \(x_{Jr_i}\). This gives the symmetric two-port polynomial \(S\) with both ordered occurrences counted even when \(r_1=r_2\). It also gives the separated-cut count, hence the distance; the total core weight is known. Thus the symmetric reconstruction conclusions above already apply at the ordinary certification precision \(D\ge T_h\) for these hidden marks, without recovering the full typed cut deck.

If \(B\ne C\) and \(P,Q_0\) are linearly independent in \(K[t]/(t^{D+1})\), choose linear functionals \(\ell_B,\ell_C\) with \(\ell_B(1)=\ell_C(1)=0\), \(\ell_B(P)=\ell_C(Q_0)=1\), and \(\ell_B(Q_0)=\ell_C(P)=0\). Use these on the first and second axes instead. The query now counts precisely the labelings in which the first side contains \(B\) and the second \(C\). The resulting **oriented** \(S\) therefore gives the two individual endpoint-side polynomials from its extremal coefficients, and then the transfer and both interior rooted polynomials by direct division and specialization. This is exactly the distinct-tag reconstruction above, now valid for all distances on an arbitrary branching core without any residue assumption. The hidden rooted types themselves are known from the certified multiset, so reconstruction of the marked core gives the whole tree.


**Distinct outliers even with linearly dependent profiles.** If \(B\ne C\), all arbitrary cores and distances are covered at sufficient precision \(D\ge2T_h\). In the dependent case the actual extra requirement is \(D\ge2\nu\) where \(\nu=\operatorname{val}(P)\); the independent case needs only the certification precision. To prove this, write \(Q_0=\lambda P\) in the dependent case, where \(\lambda\ne0,1\) in \(K\). The term \(P Q_0\) has valuation \(2\nu\) and is independent of \(P\) in the truncation.

Use cardinality, without the odd fake weights, to encode each core component. Let \(J_{\rm join}(z)\) be the rooted cut contribution from cuts keeping the two marks in one component, rooted at that common component. Let \(B_{\rm split}(z), C_{\rm split}(z)\) be the contributions from separating cuts rooted at the first or second mark, respectively. All other components are recorded in variables \(x_j\). For each list of component sizes choose only one distinguished label and its deficit variable, set the others to zero, and divide by the background factors and the unmarked-label factorials. Applying a functional \(L\) with \(L(1)=0\) produces, upon collecting all sizes,
\[
L(P)B_{\rm split}+L(Q_0)C_{\rm split}
+L(P+Q_0+PQ_0)J_{\rm join}.
\]
Choose \(L\) vanishing on \(P,Q_0\) but with \(L(PQ_0)=1\) to obtain \(J_{\rm join}\). From the symmetric separated two-port polynomial, now recorded with ordinary cardinalities, substitute \(x_i\) for the second marked power \(w^i\); this obtains \(B_{\rm split}+C_{\rm split}\). Choose another \(L\) with \(L(P)\ne L(Q_0)\), subtract the known joined contribution, and solve the two linear equations. Adding \(J_{\rm join}\) to each gives the full rooted cut polynomials of the core rooted at \(B\) and \(C\). The two rooted reconstructions and their already known distance determine the ordered pair orbit by the center argument. This handles all distinct rooted outliers without a ratio or residue assumption; identical outliers on unrestricted interiors are not covered.


**Different block orders with a large common divisor.** The distinct-outlier result also holds if the block orders are \(s_A,s_B,s_C\), with
\[
D=\min\{\gcd(s_A,s_B,s_C)-1,\ \min(s_A,s_B,s_C)-h-1\}\ \ge 2T_h.
\]
Use intact masses \(m_i\) instead of component cardinalities. Their common modulus and the mass-sum argument certify the high-deficit queries through \(D\), while the block types are certified by the same tail test. Divide each component series by \(F_A^{m_i/s_A}\), where rational powers of formal units with constant 1 are well defined over \(K\). The two marked ratios are \(H_B=F_B/F_A^{s_B/s_A}\) and \(H_C=F_C/F_A^{s_C/s_A}\). Each is nontrivial and they are distinct: raise a hypothetical equality to the power \(s_A\), clear the powers, and compare highest deficit degrees, which are one less than each block order before powers. This first forces equal relevant block orders; then injectivity of taking a positive power of a formal unit, together with rooted-block certification, forces equal types, a contradiction. For these rational powers the logarithmic derivative still has denominator the product of the fixed attachment factors and numerator of degree at most \(T_h-1\); nontrivial comparisons first differ through degree \(T_h\). Thus the same selector and joined/split arguments apply, now using intact block orders as positive vertex weights on the core. The result requires the common divisor bound and does not claim arbitrary unequal orders.
