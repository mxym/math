# From the signed spin complex to a cyclic complex

Use the length-changing supercharge, projectors and Hodge identification of [Hagendorf, §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.0357). Its normalized sum comprises the \(N+1\) translates of one split, including the seam. On the signed momentum spaces with \(\mu_N=(-1)^{N+1}\) the translated contributions agree after projection, because \(\mu_N\mu_{N+1}=-1\). Omitting the seam would only rescale the projected split sum, but for the dual boundary on coinvariant representatives we retain the seam.

If the transpose of a split is a projected multiplication on \(V\) in a unital associative algebra \(A=\mathbb C1\oplus V\), the dual spin complex identifies with the signed cyclic Hochschild chain complex modulo all unit-containing words. A length \(N\) word has cyclic chain degree \(N-1\). Concretely at a finite twist \(\theta\) take \(\theta^{\otimes N}\)-invariants, and the coinvariants for \((-1)^{N-1}\) times right rotation with \(\theta\) on the moved entry. The Hochschild last-first product is \(\theta(a_{N-1})a_0\). In characteristic zero the invariant cyclic object has its cyclic homology computed by these coinvariants and the Hochschild boundary. Reversing or dualizing the convention may invert \(\theta\), which does not affect our counts. This is the twisted cyclic construction in [Hadfield–Krähmer, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4102). Adjoint versus transpose preserves the dimensions of the finite-dimensional Hodge spaces, even if the basis change is not unitary.

For clarity, the subcomplex \(K\) of chains with some scalar letter is quasi-isomorphic to the all-scalar subcomplex. The only terms removing the last scalar are its two neighboring product terms, and they cancel in signed coinvariants. Filter the other chains of \(K\) by the number \(r>0\) of nonscalar letters. On the associated graded, mark a nonscalar letter and write the word as a cyclic sequence of \(r\) blocks \(v_i1^{k_i}\) with \(\sum k_i>0\). Merges of two nonscalars lower the filtration. A gap of \(k>0\) units has \(k+1\) boundary terms with alternating signs, all merging to shorten that gap by one. This remains true at the seam, where its last unit is fixed by the twist. Thus the gap complex has a singleton at \(k=0\) and contractible pairs \(2j\to2j-1\) for \(j\ge1\); the positive-total-gap part of their tensor product is acyclic. Forgetting the mark is a finite signed rotation quotient on the global invariant subspace, exact by averaging. Moving a block of length \(a\) through the other \(b\) letters gives the sign \((-1)^{ab}\) and applies \(\theta\) to its nonscalar letter. This is the standard permutation chain map for the tensor factors and also works with one block. The filtration is finite per degree, proving the normalization. Therefore positive weights, or odd parity if the unit is even, have exactly the ordinary (twisted if needed) cyclic homology of \(A\), without a scalar contribution.
