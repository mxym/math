# Sharp \(\Omega(E/m^2)\) for linear rank four; gap-free rank four with all pair caps \(O(m^2)\)

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

For a 4-uniform family \(H\) of \(E\) edges and integer \(m\ge2\), seek a subfamily \(F\) where each intersection of two distinct edges of \(F\) is the kernel of an \(m\)-petal sunflower entirely in \(F\). A family is *linear* when two distinct edges share at most one vertex. The adjective *nonempty* below means only the nonempty intersections are required to be witnessed.

This proof strengthens the earlier \(O(m)\) pair-cap theorem and does not require any degree gaps, high-fiber stability, or rank-three result. It does not settle the unrestricted fourth rank of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, Combinatorica §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

## Linear 4-partite input: full \(\Omega(E/m^2)\)

Call degrees bounded by \(\beta m\) **small** and by \(\Gamma m^2\) **medium**, where \(\beta,\Gamma\) are constants specified below. The classifications are promises about entire sides of a current host, not statements about a handful of high labels. Suppose \(m\ge2\). In a linear hypergraph every vertex link is a matching, so nonempty validity is precisely that each positive vertex degree is either one or at least \(m\).

First peel any vertex with positive degree less than \(m\), assigning each deleted edge to the side of its deleting vertex. If the resulting core has at least half the input mass, it already has nonempty witnesses. Otherwise a largest owner piece keeps at least one eighth of the input mass and has one designated small side (cap \(m\), which can of course be upgraded to \(\beta m\)). Caps on other already restricted sides persist under taking subhosts.

**Exit by bounding three sides.** If at least three sides already have small/medium caps, choose the fourth as a center side. Take a largest vertex-disjoint collection of stars centered on that side, each either a singleton or of size at least \(m\); stars have pairwise disjoint vertex sets. Write \(F\) for their union. Remove all host edges that hit any used leaf vertex, losing at most \(3\max(\beta,\Gamma)m^2 |F|\) edges total. Any remaining edge at an unused center or a star of size \(\ge m\) augments the collection by one edge, because used leaves have been deleted and all petals at a fixed center are disjoint (linearity). At a singleton center, \(m-1\) remaining edges would upgrade its star. Thus there are fewer than \(m\) remaining edges per singleton center, in total at most \(m|F|\). This gives \(|F|=\Omega(E/m^2)\); all realized nonempty kernels are centered at genuine large stars. The counting includes all host edges once or more and does not require the discarded edges themselves to be disjoint.

**Core or further caps.** Before that exit, the host has at least one designated small side and at most two designated small/medium sides, hence at most one designated medium one. Choose as special side \(U\) that medium side if present, or any undesignated side otherwise. Peel positive degree \(<\beta m\) at \(U\) and positive degree \(<\Gamma m^2\) at each still undesignated nonspecial side, assigning deleted edges to their deleting side. Never peel any already designated nonspecial side in this step; they are all small.

If a large owner piece is selected instead of a core (choose it when the core has less than half the mass), it keeps at least one eighth of the current host. If its owner is \(U\), then \(U\) becomes small, so either a new side is bounded or a previous sole medium cap is replaced by a small one. If its owner is an undesignated nonspecial side, that side becomes medium, increasing the number of bounded sides. This can iterate only a fixed number of times: the count of bounded sides increases at most twice before the three-side exit, and every medium-to-small conversion consumes a previously added medium designation. The original small designation never goes away. Consequently all piece selections together cost only an absolute constant.

If there is a large core use the following sampling. Set, e.g., \(\beta=128\), \(c=1/(64\beta)\), \(\Gamma=\lceil128/c\rceil\), and \(p=c/m\). Independently activate \(U\) labels with probability \(p\). Keep edges there only if no other edge in the initial activated sample shares a label in any small designated nonspecial side. Conditional on an edge being selected, each competing edge at a small label has a different \(U\) label by linearity, so the union-bound chance of any conflict is at most \(3\beta m p\le3/64\). All such small labels thereafter have degree at most one. Next repeatedly peel positive degree \(<m\) at \(U\) or at still undesignated nonspecial sides. Each original \(U\) label has degree at least \(\beta m\), so expected \(U\) loss is at most \(p E_{\rm core}/\beta\) (only activated labels can be charged). Each still undesignated nonspecial side originally had at most \(E_{\rm core}/(\Gamma m^2)\) vertices, so their total loss, even deterministically, is at most \(3E_{\rm core}/(\Gamma m)\le (3/128)p E_{\rm core}\). These bounds use initial-core counts and each label is charged at most once; no regularity on the shrinking host is assumed. Thus \(\Omega(E_{\rm core}/m)\) edges survive, all positive degrees one or at least \(m\), proving nonempty validity. The weaker \(\Omega(E/m^2)\) bound covers both exits.

**Empty kernel.** If some \(m\)-matching leaves at least \(E/2\) edges after deleting its endpoints, use the nonempty extraction on that residue and add the matching back. This introduces only witnessed empty intersections. Otherwise some vertex has degree at least \(E/(8m)\), by counting the endpoints of an existing \(m\)-matching whose removal costs \(>E/2\), or the cover from a maximal matching of size \(<m\). Its full star in a linear hypergraph is an \(m\)-sunflower if the star size is at least \(m\); otherwise \(E<8m^2\) and a singleton suffices for \(\Omega(E/m^2)\). This proves the full assertion.

The exponent is optimal in this class. For an odd prime \(q<m\) of order \(m\), take the \(q^2\) edges \((x,y,x+y,x+2y)\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). Two coordinates uniquely determine an edge, so the hypergraph is linear; all four degree bounds are \(q\), and a matching likewise has at most \(q\) edges. There is no \(m\)-sunflower of any possible kernel; a fully valid subfamily therefore has at most one edge. A prime \(q\asymp m\) exists for all sufficiently large \(m\), by Bertrand's postulate.

The same linear statement holds without partiteness: randomly partition vertices into four parts and retain the crossing edges before applying the proof. This preserves a fixed fraction in expectation.

## Arbitrary 4-uniform input with pair cap at most \(Lm^2\)

Take a random four-partition of vertices and keep crossing edges (constant factor in expectation). In that subhost form the conflict graph on edges that agree in any pair coordinate. Its maximum degree is at most \(6Lm^2\), so a largest proper color class keeps \(\Omega_L(E/m^2)\) edges and is linear. Apply the just-proved full linear rank-four result for another \(\Omega(1/m^2)\) factor. This gives the target \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\), including the empty kernel, without degree-gap or high-fiber assumptions. The same proof gives \(\Omega(E/[m^2(D+1)])\) for arbitrary global pair bound \(D\).

This is sharp at \(D\) of order \(m^2\): the complete four-partite family with \(m-1\) elements in each part has \((m-1)^4\) edges, no \(m\)-sunflower, and global pair bound \((m-1)^2\).
