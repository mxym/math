# Weighted two-injective extraction and a rank-four theorem with uncapped lifts

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and mathematical checking; no external peer review claimed.

An m-petal witness for a kernel is required to lie within the output family. Nonempty witnessing only demands such witnesses for realized nonempty intersections. Use \(m\ge2\), nonnegative weights, and finite simple partite hosts. The general unweighted internal problem is [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

## Lemma 1. Weighted vertex-disjoint stars

In a weighted simple tripartite 3-graph, choose one side as centers. Suppose each of the other two sides has vertex degree at most \(M\), and the bipartite link at every center has maximum degree at most \(D\). Put \(R=\max\{M,mD,1\}\). There is a vertex-disjoint union of stars of total weight \(\Omega(W/R)\), each either a single triple or a sunflower with at least \(m\) petals. In particular this output witnesses nonempty kernels.

For each center edge-color its link into \(D\) matchings, allowing empty colors. Split the triples according as their color's matching has fewer than \(2m\) edges or not. If the short colors have at least half the mass, this subhost has center degrees at most \(2mD\), so ordinary greedy coloring of the edge-conflict graph gives a matching of weight \(\Omega(W/R)\). If the long colors have at least half, independently at each center choose at most one long color, each with probability \(p=1/(128R)\); \(Dp\le1\), so this defines a distribution. Remove every selected edge sharing either leaf label with a selected edge at a different center. Conditional on a specific matching chosen, failure probability for each edge is at most \(2Mp\), since every competitor is outside this center and has marginal at most \(p\). Conditional on the same choice, Markov makes the chance that fewer than \(m\) petals survive at most \(4Mp\), as losing half the original petals is necessary. Discard the whole star in that case. Each original long edge then survives with probability at least \(p(1-6Mp)\), proving the lemma.

## Lemma 2. Weighted two-injective rank three

Let \(ABC\) be a weighted simple tripartite family with \(AB,AC\) injective and maximum \(BC\) multiplicity \(D\), where \(1\le D\le m\). There is a nonempty-witnessing subfamily of weight
\[
\Omega\!\left(\frac{W}{m^{3/2}\sqrt D}\right).
\]
This extends the [unweighted theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0/TwoInjectiveInternalSunflowers.md), preserving its parameter order. The proof below handles arbitrary nonnegative weights without a logarithmic factor. Write \(\operatorname{Top}_K(V)\) for the sum of the \(K\) heaviest incident individual edge weights per label on side \(V\), unless a different bucket list is specified.

**Core and concentration.** Set \(p_0=1/(8D)\), \(K_0=\lceil 64m/p_0\rceil\). If \(\operatorname{Top}_m(A),\operatorname{Top}_{K_0}(B),\operatorname{Top}_{K_0}(C)\) all are at most \(W/64\), sample A labels independently at \(p_0\) and drop every initially selected edge sharing its BC pair with another initially selected edge. Each conditional conflict probability is at most \(Dp_0\) since distinct triples with the same BC pair use different A. The residue is linear. Repeatedly peel any positive degree below \(m\). A peeling charge is at most the sum of the \(m\) heaviest initially active incident weights at that label. At A the total expected charge is at most \(p_0\operatorname{Top}_m(A)\). At B or C the original top \(K_0\) list charges in expectation at most \(p_0\operatorname{Top}_{K_0}(V)\); any other charged weight is at most the label's mass divided by \(K_0\), and at most \(m\) of these can be charged. Thus their total per-side expected charge is at most \(p_0 W/64+mW/K_0\). A linear family of positive minimum degree \(m\) witnesses every repeated singleton. The expected mass gives \(\Omega(W/D)\). Otherwise restrict to the offending side's heaviest lists, obtaining constant mass and an A degree cap \(m\), or a B/C degree cap \(K_0=O(mD)\), with all original projection caps preserved.

**B capped** (C capped is symmetric). Rename the restricted mass \(W\). Pick \(p=1/(128\sqrt{mD})\), \(K=\lceil128m/p\rceil\). If the top \(K\) incident weights at A or at C have total \(>W/64\), restrict to those lists. Use the opposite AC-side as centers in Lemma 1, with leaf degree bound \(O(K+mD)\) and link maximum degree \(D\); it retains \(\Omega(W/(m^{3/2}\sqrt D))\). Otherwise independently sample A and C labels with probability \(p\), keep edges with both labels chosen, and drop all initially sampled edges at a repeated B label. For a fixed sampled edge \(abc\), competitors with the same C have distinct A and number at most \(D\); all others have distinct A and C by AB injectivity and number at most \(K_0\). Their union probability is at most \(Dp+K_0p^2<1/8\). The residue is linear, since AC was injective. Repeatedly peel active A/C labels with positive degree below \(m\). Given that its own label is selected, every edge in its top \(K\) list initially appears with probability \(p\), and the other charged weights are bounded by its original mass times \(m/K\). Multiplying by the label's activation probability \(p\), total expected cost per side is at most \(p^2 W/64+pmW/K\). Thus \(\Omega(p^2W)\) remains, enough since \(mD\le m^{3/2}\sqrt D\).

**A capped.** Rename the restricted mass \(W\). Order weights decreasingly within each BC fiber; split ranks into dyadic intervals \([w,2w)\), \(w=1,2,4,\ldots\le D\). Let \(E_w\) be the tier weight summed over all fibers. Take a tier maximizing \(E_w/\sqrt w\), so \(E_w\ge W\sqrt{w/D}/4\). For each bucket \(bc\) represented in this tier let \(P_{bc}\) be the sum of its first \(w\) weights in the original A-capped host. A tier edge in this bucket has weight at most \(P_{bc}/w\), and the total prefix mass \(P=\sum_{bc}P_{bc}\) satisfies
\[
P\le \sum_{v\le w}E_v\le 4E_w
\]
by maximality and the geometric sum over smaller dyadic tiers. Only these rank-tier edges are used in the output; prefixes are merely accounting weights.

Put \(p=\sqrt{w/(64m)}\), \(K=\lceil 1024m/p\rceil\). At each B and C label form its top \(K\) list of incident BC buckets ordered by their prefix masses \(P_{bc}\). Compute the total **tier mass** in those lists on each side. If either exceeds \(E_w/64\), restrict to its listed buckets' tier edges. The capped side has raw degree at most \(wK=O(m^{3/2}\sqrt w)\), A retains its cap \(m\), and each center link has maximum degree at most \(w\). Apply Lemma 1 using the other BC-side as centers; the result retains \(\Omega(E_w/(m^{3/2}\sqrt w))\), as required.

If both list totals are small, independently activate B and C labels at \(p\). At each bucket with both labels activated, independently choose at most one tier edge, each with conditional marginal \(1/w\); there are at most \(w\), so leftover probability chooses none. Conditional on an edge chosen, each competitor at A has a different B and C by both injectivities and is independently chosen with probability \(p^2/w\). At most \(m\) rivals give failure at most \(1/64\). Drop all A-colliding edges and peel positive B/C degree below \(m\). Conditional on a B or C label being activated, total expected potential peel charge from its top list is at most \(p/w\) times its listed tier mass; each other charged edge weighs at most its label's total prefix mass divided by \(wK\), and at most \(m\) can be charged. Hence the expected charge per side is at most
\[
p\left(\frac p w\frac{E_w}{64}+\frac{mP}{wK}\right)
\le \left(\frac1{64}+\frac4{1024}\right)\frac{p^2E_w}{w}.
\]
Thus a linear nonempty-valid subfamily of weight \(\Omega(p^2E_w/w)=\Omega(E_w/m)\) survives. This dominates \(\Omega(E_w/(m^{3/2}\sqrt w))\), and the choice of tier proves Lemma 2. Throughout, a peeled vertex is charged once to initially available edges whether or not it actually survived earlier deletions.

## Theorem. Rank four with arbitrary complementary fiber sizes

Let \(H\) be finite simple four-partite on \(A,B,C,D\), with C degree at most \(L m^2\). Form the simple shadow \(S\) of distinct complementary labels \(ABD\): one triple for each nonempty projected \(ABD\)-fiber. Suppose two of the three pair types in \(S\) have multiplicity \(O_L(1)\), and the third has multiplicity at most \(Lm\). Here multiplicity counts distinct triples of \(S\), not edges of \(H\). There are no assumptions on any actual pair-fiber or triple-fiber multiplicities in \(H\) beyond the C-degree bound. Then there is a full internally witnessing subfamily of \(H\) of size \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\).

For any weighted subshadow, color conflicts at the two constant-capped pair types and keep a heaviest color, ensuring both injective at constant loss. The remaining linear cap \(Lm\) can be reduced to \(m\) at constant cost by assigning its incident triples in each bucket to at most \(\lceil L\rceil\) classes of capacity \(m\) and taking a heaviest common class. Lemma 2 with \(D=m\) therefore has cost \(O_L(m^2)\) for the simple shadow throughout. A shadow subfamily with C globally unique lifts every nonempty shadow witness by arbitrary representatives in its distinct fibers.

Split the original fibers at size \(2m\). In the light subhost independently sample edges at \(p=1/[128(L+2)m^2]\) and discard every edge with a competitor at C or at its complete \(ABD\) projection. Each conflict removes a selected edge with probability at most \((Lm^2+2m)p\). Thus \(\Omega_L(E_{\rm light}/m^2)\) edges remain with C unique, and projected triples injective. Apply the shadow theorem with unit weights and lift.

For the heavy subhost instead sample occupied \(ABD\)-triples, keeping their **entire** fibers initially, each independently with the same \(p\). Remove every selected edge having a competitor at C. Simplicity makes C distinct within a fiber, so given the fiber chosen each of at most \(Lm^2\) competitors is in a different projected group and has marginal \(p\). Conditional on the chosen fiber, Markov bounds the probability it falls below \(m\) petals by \(2Lm^2p\), since it started with at least \(2m\). Drop all fibers that do fall below \(m\). Thus edge survival is at least \(p(1-3Lm^2p)\), and some outcome has \(\Omega_L(E_{\rm heavy}/m^2)\) mass. Give each surviving projected triple weight equal to its surviving fiber size, and apply the weighted shadow theorem. Keep every surviving edge of each chosen fiber. Repeated nonempty intersections between different fibers are precisely their shadow kernels and lift as above. A repeated \(ABD\)-kernel is witnessed within its own fiber (at least \(m\) distinct C extensions); C cannot belong to any pairwise intersection.

The heavier light/heavy branch yields \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\) for nonempty intersections. The hypotheses are hereditary, so either reserve a disjoint \(m\)-matching leaving at least half the host avoiding its endpoints, prove the nonempty bound on that residue, and restore the matching; or find a vertex of degree at least \(|H|/(8m)\) by counting endpoints of an existing \(m\)-matching or of a maximal shorter matching. The [full rank-three theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) in its link lifts with the fixed vertex and gives the target scale. This proves full witnessing.

Scope: the result permits arbitrary ABD multiplicities and arbitrary C-cross multiplicities subject to C's quadratic degree, with structural restrictions only on the simple complementary shadow. It does not remove the ABD bound in the prior no-pure-pair-cap class when shadow pair multiplicities and vertex spans are all uncontrolled, and it does not prove the general one-bounded-side or unrestricted rank-four problems.

**Parameterized improvement.** If the shadow pair caps are \(K_1,K_2,D\), with \(1\le D\le m\), the first conflict coloring uses at most \(K_1+K_2-1\) colors rather than a constant. Lemma 2 then retains \(\Omega(W/[(K_1+K_2)m^{3/2}\sqrt D])\). The same light/heavy lift gives nonempty witnessing of size \(\Omega_L(E/[(K_1+K_2)m^{7/2}\sqrt D])\). The full bound is the smaller of this and \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\), due to the empty-kernel fallback. In particular the target full bound holds whenever \((K_1+K_2)\sqrt D=O_L(\sqrt m)\). This includes all three shadow caps \(O_L(m^{1/3})\), so each shadow multiplicity may itself grow. A remaining cap \(D\le Lm\) instead of \(m\) can be reduced by the constant-class procedure above.
