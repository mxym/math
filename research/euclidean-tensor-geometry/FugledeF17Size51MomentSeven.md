# Size 51 over F_17³: conditional three-blocking at degree seven; moments through seven

Assume the size-51 reductions in [FugledeF17Size51MomentSix.md](FugledeF17Size51MomentSix.md) and the uniform-pencil cap eight of [FugledeF17Size51PencilCapEight.md](FugledeF17Size51PencilCapEight.md). Each spatial line has cap3 and plane cap9; there are no layers or zero lifts; all occupancy degrees are at most nine. Write W=429, C=21, and every tight pencil has b≥10.

## Conditional three-blocking

Let F be a nonzero homogeneous degree-seven form vanishing on U_B. If some projective line has at most two U sites, its seventeen parallel affine slices of the partner B' must all be full collinear triple supports, since a noncollinear triple determines three directions. At least nine are parallel to a common axis e; fix nine of them. The nine quotient positions along e have line cap3, since four full supports would put twelve points in one affine plane. For any pair at distinct quotient sites, their axial difference set gives at least five finite transverse direction roots on their radial line through e, besides e itself. Let k count the distinct rational factors of F through e.

If k≥3, every pair forces a radial factor, since a nonfactor contains at most 1+7−k≤5 U points total. The quotient cap-three nine-set needs at least eight directions (size51 planar certificate), contradiction to k≤7.

### k=0

Every radial has at most six finite transverse roots. Fix an origin independently on each support at its arithmetic mean and subtract that mean from its three axial coordinates. These normalized triples have sum zero. Use the following exact facts from [verify-fuglede-f17-size51-f7-radial.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size51-f7-radial.js): the graph on the forty centered triples with an edge when their difference set has size≤6 has eight two-point components (no loops), and eight shape components of size three, each a scalar multiple of
```
A={0,1,−1},   B={4,6,7},   C={10,11,13}.
```
In a three-point component only A has a loop, and each distinct pair has difference size six. Most importantly, for the finite difference sets of admissible ordered pairs of these shapes, two root sets related by an arbitrary translation and a nonzero scaling can have union of size≤6 only if their relative scaling is ±1. In this statement the comparison can be between different ordered pairs; the verifier checks all such pairs.

All nine shapes must belong to the same three-point component, since every pair is admissible and a two-point component cannot support repeats. Whenever two quotient chords have the same direction, normalize by the same projective representative; their two transverse root sets are subsets of one polynomial's ≤6 roots, so the preceding comparison gives identical quotient vectors up to sign. But no nine noncollinear quotient sites satisfy this equal-parallel-chord constraint, by the size51 moment-six verifier (4090 states, zero). Contradiction.

### k=2

Write F as its two radial factors times a residual H of degree five; these two slopes are exceptional. The graph of ordinary chords between the nine quotient sites is connected: each vertex loses at most four incident edges to the two exceptional slopes by cap3, so it has ordinary degree at least four and each connected component would have ≥5 vertices. A nonexceptional chord gives five finite roots of H, so H(e)≠0 (otherwise the degree in the axial variable is at most four). Make H monic in that variable. Every ordinary chord has exactly five transverse roots; by [Vosper's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-31.2.200) both its supports are same-step AP3s, and connectivity makes the step σ globally common up to sign. The linear coefficient of X^4 sets the AP5 root mean, so a common shear centers H, and its X^3 coefficient Q is binary quadratic and takes the same nonzero value −5σ² on each actual ordinary quotient difference. Scale Q by this value. Finite-field quadratic forms of ranks one/two taking value one are equivalent to rank one x², split rank two xy, or nonsplit rank two x²−3y²; a rank-one nonsquare form cannot take value one. The exact verifier tests all six-site cap3 sets that can have Q=1 on all nonexceptional chords, for all two exception directions and these canonical forms. The rank-two cases have zero completions. In rank one, each of the 2774400 completions has the radical as an exceptional direction and lies on two lines parallel to it. Applied to every six-subset of the original nine, this puts all nine sites on only two such lines (if there were three groups, choose a six-subset meeting all three). Cap3 makes this impossible.

### k=1, residual has axial degree five

Divide the sole radial factor, whose slope is exceptional, to get the degree-six form H. If H(e)=0, its finite axial degree is≤5. Each ordinary chord requires exactly five transverse roots and so produces AP5, again with a common axial step on the connected complete multipartite graph of chords between different exceptional-slope groups. Two same-slope ordinary chords agree up to sign as quotient vectors, because their five root sets agree and the squared deviations from the mean detect the squared AP step. In particular no three quotient sites are collinear in an ordinary slope, so each ordinary slope contributes at most four pairs. There are at least 36−9=27 ordinary pairs by cap3, hence at least seven ordinary slopes.

Write H=A X^5+B X^4+C X^3+D X^2+E X+…, with binary coefficient degrees 1,2,3,4,5. The nonzero leading A is nonvanishing on all ordinary slopes. The centered third elementary coefficient of AP5 is zero. Clearing denominators of the shift by μ=−B/(5A) gives the homogeneous binary identity
```
25 A² D−15 A B C+4 B³=0.
```
It holds identically because it has degree six and at least seven zeros. Reducing modulo the linear A gives A|B, so the mean is linear. Shear globally to center. The centered AP5 coefficients of orders two and four are nonzero fixed multiples of σ_0²,σ_0⁴ respectively in any slope coordinate, so A E'−c(C')²=0 for a fixed nonzero c. Again degree six and at least seven slopes yield an identity and force A|C'. Thus Q=C'/A is binary quadratic, nonzero constant on ordinary differences in their actual quotient coordinates. Such a binary Q cannot accommodate these nine sites. Indeed choosing one representative per group gives at most two groups in rank one and at most three in rank two by equidistant Gram rank. In rank two a group of three would force the exceptional direction isotropic and orthogonal to an outside difference by checking Q along that line at three distinct sites, contradiction. The rank-one/cap3 case and rank-two/group-cap2 case each allow at most six sites.

### k=1, residual is monic axial degree six and an irregular triple occurs

Suppose H(e)≠0, so scale H monic in the axial coordinate. Its root mean is a binary linear form; shear to make this zero, and denote each selected arm's resulting axial centroid by α_i. Group the quotient positions in parallel exceptional-slope lines (groups have at most three sites). Their ordinary chord graph is connected, and the centered triple shapes must lie in a single near-Cauchy-Davenport component above. A two-point component would put each of its two shapes in one exceptional-slope group, allowing only six sites. Thus use the scalar multiple of the three shapes A,B,C. Say the common scale is σ. A is regular; B,C are irregular. Each irregular shape may only occur in a single exceptional-slope group, since its self-difference size is seven.

When the types B and C occur in different groups, any regular site outside those two groups would give an ordinary triangle of all three types. This is impossible because the root means of the axial difference sets A−B, A−C, B−C are σ times 3,−3,0, respectively; an ordinary mixed-type chord uses exactly six roots, so its total mean is the sum of this shape mean and the centroid difference, which is zero by the shear. These shape means are not a cocycle on a triangle. Hence if B and C occur in different groups all sites are within those two groups, impossible. Therefore all irregular sites are in one group.

Every site outside the one irregular group is regular, and they all have the same α_i since each makes an ordinary mixed chord to a fixed irregular anchor. There are at least six such sites. On ordinary chords between outside groups, the five AP5 roots are centered at zero; the sixth root is then forced to be zero by the total root mean. Thus the centered X^4 coefficient Q of H has value 12σ² on their actual quotient differences. On chords from regular outside sites to the fixed irregular anchor it instead has value 4σ² (these are the centered second coefficients of the six-root difference sets A−B and A−C).

A rank-two binary Q cannot have six outside sites in groups of this kind. It allows at most three groups and no group of size three, as in the previous case. Also it cannot have two groups of size two: taking the second difference of the four equal quadratic values on their cross chords forces the exceptional direction isotropic, and another subtraction makes it orthogonal to an outside cross difference, contradiction. Thus it can accommodate at most four outside sites. Rank one requires exactly two outside groups of three, and the exceptional direction is its radical (apply the quadratic restriction to a group of three). Write y0 for the irregular group's coordinate transverse to the radical and y1,y2 for the outside groups. The two values Q(y1−y0)=Q(y2−y0)=4σ² imply y0 is the midpoint. Then Q(y1−y2)=16σ², contrary to 12σ². This rules out the irregular case.

### k=1, residual is monic axial degree six and all triples are APs

Make the same global mean shear, and let r=α_i−α_j be the axial centroid difference for an ordinary chord with quotient vector (Y,Z). The five roots of the AP5 are r+jσ for j=−2..2, and the remaining root of H is −5r by its total mean zero, with multiplicities allowed. Hence its second and third centered power sums are
```
P2(Y,Z)=13 r²+10σ²,      P3(Y,Z)=−r³+13rσ².
```
Here P2,P3 are homogeneous binary forms of degrees two and three, by Newton identities and the monic homogeneous coefficients. The augmented sites (y_i,z_i,α_i) therefore have constant nonzero quadratic value 10σ² on each intergroup difference, for the ternary Q'=P2(Y,Z)−13r². Put H0={Y=0}; the span V_i of within-group differences is contained in H0 and these spans are pairwise Q'-orthogonal by taking second differences of the cross values. Equidistant representatives give at most four groups by Gram rank (n representatives give Gram proportional to I+J in n−1 dimensions). At least three groups have nonzero spans: with at most two nonsingleton groups and at most four groups, cap3 permits at most eight sites.

The restriction of Q' to H0 cannot have rank zero because of the axial square term. If it were nondegenerate, three nontrivial pairwise orthogonal subspaces in H0 would all be the same isotropic line R: two independent spans would leave zero common orthogonal complement for the third. But using two sites separated along R and equal quadratic distances to any external group representative would make all intergroup representative differences lie in R^⊥. Its restricted rank is at most one, so three equidistant representatives there are impossible. Hence Q'|H0 has rank one. At most one V_i is nonradical in H0, so there is a group with a nontrivial difference in its radical line R. Equidistance to an external group implies this R is orthogonal to an intergroup vector outside H0, hence R is the global radical. Thus Q' has rank at most two and there are at most three groups; exactly three groups of size three are needed. The global rank is two (rank one allows only two groups). In the quotient by R, each group has all three sites at one position or at most two positions, since the quadratic value to an outside anchor along H0/R is a nonconstant quadratic. At most one group can have two positions because the V_i are pairwise orthogonal. Select a common-image subset in each group of size ≥2, with at least two groups' selections of size3.

The radical R projects nontrivially to the Z coordinate (a purely axial vector is not isotropic). Hence in the selected subsets the centroids have the form α_i=a z_i+β_g with common a and one intercept for each group g. On any pair of selected groups, fixing Y=y_g−y_h gives at least four distinct Z differences by Cauchy–Davenport. The cubic equality for P3 with r=aZ+β_g−β_h therefore holds as a polynomial in Z for each of the three pairs. If a≠0, compare Z² coefficients, which are −3a²(β_g−β_h) and the fixed Z²Y coefficient of P3 times Y. Thus β_g−β_h=bY for a fixed b. The P2 equality then gives a binary quadratic P2−13(aZ+bY)² with constant nonzero value on the intergroup quotient differences. The three groups each have at least two selected sites, impossible by the same binary rank/group argument in the irregular case.

If a=0, P2 and P3 are constant along the Z variable at a fixed nonzero Y by these ≥4 values, so both are purely in Y by homogeneity. Normalize σ=1 and group levels to 0,1,t with t≠0,1; translate centroid intercepts to 0,A,B. The pairs (Y,r)=(1,A),(t,B),(t−1,B−A) must have equal P2/Y² and equal P3/Y³. The exact verifier tests all 4335 values of (t,A,B) and finds none. This contradiction finishes the last branch.

Thus any degree-seven F vanishing on U_B entails three-blocking.

## Excluding nonic profiles (M7)

A nonic profile would make the degree-seven moment form F7 nonzero, with its nonzero evaluations exactly its nonic normals. A tight pencil containing any nonic has b≥11, and the others lie among at most seven rational factors and have b≥10. All positive weights at current plane cap9 and degree≤9 are ≤8: at least eight positive levels by the degree root bound, and the maximal squared sum at mass51 with eight or more positive levels and cap9 is attained by (9^5,4,1,1), giving w≤135/17<8. The support identity therefore gives 21N≥11·429−7·8=4663, or N≥223, so u=|U_B|≤84. The partner has at most102 uniform normals since all its tight pencils have b≥10, giving support size≥205. Conditional three-blocking gives n_L∈[3,8] in B. In the sum
```
Σ_L (n_L−4)(n_L−7) = u²−181u+8596 ≥448,
```
only n_L=3 and n_L=8 are positive, each contributing four; n_L=8 requires a factor so occurs at most seven times. Hence at least105 lines have exactly three U_B directions. Every one has a uniform pole in the other member because its parallel slices all have size≤3 (a noncollinear four-set needs four directions) and average three. These distinct poles would require at least105 partner uniform normals, exceeding102. Therefore there are no nonic profiles, and all moments through seven vanish.

This proves M7 but does not settle size51.
