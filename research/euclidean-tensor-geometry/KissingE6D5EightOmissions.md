# Eight missing D5 roots with two full spinor layers

**Partial-section theorem.** Work at squared norm two in dimension six. Let a kissing code contain both layers (ε/2,±√3/2), with a full 16-element parity class of five-bit sign strings at each height (the two parities may differ). If the code also contains at least 32 of the 40 equatorial D5 roots ±e_i±e_j, then it has at most **72 points**. The bound is attained even when exactly eight signed roots are absent.

This assumes full outer layers but only a partial equatorial system. It is not the [known theorem for a complete D5 cross section](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202610.0335), nor an upper bound for unrestricted six-dimensional codes. The classical layers and related 72-point examples are discussed by [Cohn and Rajagopal](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00454-026-00841-x).

## 1. Shape of a point with at most eight violations

Let x=(v,z) be any additional non-D5 point. Call a root violated if its scalar product with x is strictly greater than 1. Let a₁≥⋯≥a₅≥0 be the ordered absolute horizontal coordinates and t=√3|z|. From the layer at the same sign of height we get

    a₁+a₂+a₃+a₄−a₅+t ≤ 2,       Σaᵢ²+t²/3 = 2.

Indeed the maximum of ε·v over either parity is at least Σaᵢ−2a₅. The possible positive violated-root products are the twenty forms a_i+a_j and a_i−a_j, i<j.

**Local certificate.** With at most eight violated roots, there are at least four. Their supports form a star with center the largest absolute coordinate. On each of the four incident supports, at least one root with positive coefficient along that *signed* center is violated; no other support is violated. Either a₁>1 or (a₁,…,a₅,t)=(1,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2,0).

Here are the full details of the finite test. Write b_i=a_i−a_{i+1} for i<5 and b₅=a₅, adjoining b₆=t. The twenty forms have nonnegative coefficient rows in b. A strict blocker set is upward-closed in coefficientwise order. Starting at the empty ideal, add any maximal unused row; all ideals through size eight result. The respective counts are 1,1,1,2,2,3,4,5,6. For each ideal, bound its minimal rows below by 1, the complementary maximal rows above by 1, all b_i below by zero, and the row (1,2,3,4,3,1) above by 2. These bounded closed polytopes contain the true chambers. Intersect every six independent boundary equalities by exact Cramer determinants, keep feasible vertices, and maximize the displayed strictly convex squared norm on those vertices. If the maximum is less than 2 the ideal is impossible. If it is exactly 2, a possible sphere point is itself a vertex; test its strict blockers and exclude original D5 roots.

For sizes 0,1,2,3 no nonroot survives. For each k=4,…,8 exactly one ideal remains as a possibility: all four sums a₁+a_j, j=2,…,5, plus the k−4 differences a₁−a_j with largest j. Rerunning with a₁≤1 leaves only the specified isolated four-blocker point. The [exact checker](verify-kissing-e6-d5-eight-omissions.js) implements all these computations without numerical tolerances.

## 2. Pack the extra points by signed center

Let U be the m≤8 missing signed roots of the actual code. At one active signed center s e_i, call a satellite coordinate j *free* if both eligible roots s e_i±e_j lie in U. Write q for the number of free coordinates. Each other satellite has just one eligible sign in U, so all points at this center have the same sign of that satellite coordinate: the corresponding root is strictly violated whereas the other is not. In particular that coordinate is nonzero.

If q≤3, project each such point onto the q free satellites and its height. The projections of any two have strictly negative scalar product. The center-coordinate product is at least 1, strictly above 1 unless both points are exceptional (a₁=1). Fixed-satellite contributions are nonnegative; for two exceptional points, at least one fixed satellite contributes 1/4. The full product is at most 1. Therefore there are at most q+2 of these points. We used the elementary bound that pairwise strictly negative products in dimension d permit at most d+1 vectors: an affine dependence would give equal combinations on opposite sign classes, whose mutual product would be negative.

If q=4, all eight omissions occur at this signed center. Exceptional points have height zero and satellite entries ±1/2. Their four-sign parity is fixed by either spinor layer: if all five signs aligned with a layer string, its product with the point would be 3/2. Thus their residuals lie in a four-dimensional cross polytope (four orthogonal antipodal axes). At most eight exceptional points can occur. If an opposite pair of exceptional residuals occurs it precludes every nonexceptional point, whose residual must have negative product with both. Otherwise there are r≤4 orthogonal exceptional residuals. The residuals of the nonexceptional points have negative product with each exceptional residual and with each other. Project to the complement of the r axes in the five-dimensional residual space: all subtracted products are nonnegative, so the inequalities stay strict. There are at most 6−r nonexceptional points, hence at most six points in this latter case. In either case there are at most eight.

It remains to count centers. Three distinct center coordinates would require at least nine distinct two-coordinate supports, impossible with eight omissions. With just one active signed center, m≥4+q, so the bounds above are at most m. If both signs of one coordinate are active they require all eight omissions, one for each signed center on each of its four supports. Both centers have q=0 and there is no other active coordinate. They contribute at most four points in total. Finally, two distinct center coordinates require seven supports. Each has q≤m−7, and neither coordinate can be active with both signs. The two centers contribute at most 2(m−7)+4≤m. Thus in all cases there are at most m additional points, yielding 32+(40−m)+m=72.

**Sharpness and the limitation of pointwise restoration.** Take both layer parities even. Delete the eight D5 roots e₁±e_j for j=2,…,5, and insert (1,σ₂/2,…,σ₅/2,0) for all eight odd four-sign strings. The verifier checks every norm and pair in the resulting 72-point code; we do not claim its isometry class is new. With just four missing roots one already cannot restore signs pointwise: (5/4,1/2,1/4,1/4,−1/4,0) is compatible with both layers and strictly violates exactly e₁+e₂, e₁+e₃, e₁+e₄ and e₁−e₅. Counting, rather than restoring every missing sign, is necessary.

## 3. Strict expansion down to twelve antipodal pairs

In a hypothetical 73-point code that represents every E6 axis and has p≥12 true antipodal pairs, let S be a nonempty outsider set with all mutual squared-norm-two products at least −1. Its union B of signed original blockers satisfies **|B|≥|S|+1**.

The outsider lines and the original axes not in B are simultaneously projective kissing lines. If |B|<|S| they exceed the 36-line bound. At equality they form another E6 by [Anstreicher’s theorem](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/). The systems are distinct and jointly represented, with at least p common axes (all genuinely paired axes are unblocked). By the [mixed-intersection theorem](KissingE6MixedIntersections.md), their common roots form D5. All 20 axes of D5 are represented and at least 12 are paired, hence at least 32 signed D5 roots occur.

Each system supplies a representative on its 16 axes outside D5, giving 32 distinct points. In any E6 containing D5, an outside root (v,z) has nonzero height (an extra equatorial root would satisfy |v_i|+|v_j|≤1, hence ‖v‖²≤5/4). The index formula 4z²=3I² forces z²=3/4. Integrality against D5 gives integral or all half-integral horizontal coordinates; the required squared norm 5/4 and the root constraints force v=ε/2. At either height, adjacent sign strings in the five-cube cannot both occur because their product is 3/2. Its independent sets have at most 16 vertices, and equality is a parity class: a maximum set and its complement must each be independent by counting edges in the regular bipartite graph, whose bipartition is unique by connectedness. Hence the 32 distinct points fill a complete parity layer at each height. The partial-section theorem contradicts 73 points, ruling out equality.

This is an independent-set pruning lemma, not a claim that p=12 itself is excluded.
