# No 91-point spectral subset of \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\): geometric stage

**Theorem.** No spectral subset of \(\mathbb F_{13}^{3}\) has \(91=7\cdot13\) points. The proof starts here and continues with the [weighted-profile stage](FugledeF13Size91WeightedProfiles.md) and the [final incidence contradiction](FugledeF13Size91FinalIncidence.md). They use two exact JS verifiers; each runs under Node with no dependencies. The earlier [104/117 note](FugledeF13DenseCardinalities.md) gives the basic spectral-pair reductions. This theorem is one cardinality result, not a resolution of the full finite-field Fuglede conjecture. Main inputs adapted from Ferguson–Iosevich, [Fuglede's conjecture for \(p=11\), arXiv:2610.30712v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), are incidence rigidity, the pencil-profile identity, weighted incidence, and line-factor estimates. The \(p=13\) cross-slice argument, tight-pencil audit, and final paired support bound are different.

Put \(p=13,m=7\). For a hypothetical pair \((E,A)\), let \(D(B)\) be the projective directions of differences of \(B\), and \(U_B\) the directions of normals whose plane occupancies all equal seven; \(V_B\) is the complement of \(U_B\) in \(\mathrm{PG}(2,13)\). Fourier orthogonality and cyclotomic irreducibility give \(D(A)\subseteq U_E,D(E)\subseteq U_A\). Every affine plane contains at most thirteen points, every affine line contains at most \(p-m=6\), and no \(U_B\) contains a projective line. A pencil through an affine line with \(a\) points has total occupancy \(91+13a\). In particular if |U_B∩P|≥r for every projective line \(P\), its occupancies give
\[
 91+13a\le 7r+13(14-r).
\]
Every plane direction has some slice with at least seven points.

## 1. Every projective line meets \(D(E)\) and \(D(A)\) at least six times

Use \(\mathbb F_{13}^2\) planar direction verifications in [verify-fuglede-f13-size91-geometric.js](verify-fuglede-f13-size91-geometric.js). The verifier puts a noncollinear ordered triple at \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\); iterates every mask of the prescribed number of directions including its three slopes; and then enumerates all increasing compatible point extensions, maintaining each affine line's occupancy. Noncollinearity is forced by the stated caps. There are no seven-point sets with at most four directions and cap six, and no eight-point sets with at most five directions and cap four. All nine normalized seven-point completions with at most five directions and cap four are affine images of the cross
\[
 H_0(1,0)\cup H_0(0,1),\qquad H_0=\{0,1,3,9\}.
\]
Checking an affine image simply tries the center and two basis arms among the seven points and verifies equality of the two seven-point sets.

The first exclusion makes both direction sets, and hence both \(U\) sets, meet every projective line at least five times. The pencil bound now gives affine line cap four. Suppose there were a projective line with \(D(B)\) intersection of size exactly five. The thirteen parallel slices in that orientation all have at most seven points by the second planar exclusion; the total \(91\) forces exactly seven points in each. Their five directions are the same fixed set and every slice is one of the above crosses.

Write \(H=\{1,3,9\}\le\mathbb F_{13}^{\times}\). The two arm directions are uniquely determined by the five directions: in the reference mask \(\{0,\infty,-H\}\), among the ten choices of an unordered pair to send to \(0,\infty\), only the axes leave the other three slopes in one \(H\)-coset. The verifier checks this via determinant ratios, which are unchanged up to common scale or inversion under choices of coordinates. Thus all slices have a four-point arm in the same absolute direction. In coordinates along that direction, each such arm is a translate of \(rH_0\), with only four options for the coset \(rH\). Two slices share a coset. The elementary exact check \(H_0-H_0=\mathbb F_{13}\) shows their cross differences contain an entire thirteen-point affine line of vectors parallel to the arm direction. Its displacement has nonzero component transverse to the slices. Thus those differences give every projective point on a projective line except the arm direction, already in \(D(B)\). This is forbidden. We conclude the six-point lower bound on every line.

## 2. Moments through degree nine vanish

For a size-\(N\) projective set \(S\) and \(n_L=|S\cap L|\), \(\sum_L n_L=14N\) and \(\sum_L n_L^2=N^2+13N\), by counting points and ordered distinct pairs. If a nonzero degree-eight form vanished on \(D(B)\), \(6\le n_L\le8\) on nonfactor lines and \(6\le n_L\le13\) on at most eight factor lines. Hence \(\sum_L(n_L-6)(n_L-8)\le8\cdot35=280\); the incidence identities give \(N^2-183N+8784\ge412\) for integer \(N\). Multiplying by a power of a nonzero linear form also rules out smaller degrees. Each \(M_j^B(v)=\sum_{x\in B}(v\cdot x)^j\) for \(1\le j\le 11\) vanishes on \(U_B\) by field power sums. Therefore both sets' moments vanish through degree eight.

If \(F=M_9^A\) is nonzero, it vanishes on \(D(E)\). At most four rational line factors of \(F\) pass through one projective point: if at least five did, any nonfactor line through that point would have at most \(1+(9-5)=5\) different zeros, contradicting the six-point bound for \(D(E)\). The planar verifier establishes that with at most nine directions, line cap four permits at most ten points, and cap three permits at most nine points.

If \(E\) had a four-point line \(\ell\), the plane pencil through \(\ell\) would contain at least six uniform planes (seven points), at most four planes with factor direction lines (at most thirteen points), and all other planes would have at most ten points (their tangent directions lie on nonfactor lines, with at most nine roots of \(F\)). Even allowing no overlap, total occupancy is at most \(6\cdot7+4\cdot13+4\cdot10=134\), below the required \(91+13\cdot4=143\). Thus \(E\) has line cap three.

Then in every direction plane corresponding to a nonfactor projective line, every parallel affine \(E\)-slice has at most nine points. Vanishing moments through degree eight imply that the thirteen plane counts modulo thirteen are evaluations of a cubic or lower: the orthogonal complement of evaluations of \(1,t,\dots,t^8\) is exactly evaluations of \(1,t,t^2,t^3\) by field power sums and dimension. The verifier checks all \(13^4\) coefficient vectors: the only such profile with integer counts at most nine and mass 91 is constant seven. Hence at most nine normals are nonuniform, which cannot cover all \(183\) projective lines (\(9\cdot14<183\)). This contradicts the complement of \(U_E\) being blocking. Therefore \(M_9^A=0\), and symmetry yields \(M_9^E=0\).

## 3. Full planes restrict the nonuniform profiles

The same geometric verifier gives another fact: a thirteen-point planar set with cap four determines at least eleven directions. For a hypothetical counterexample choose two missing directions as the horizontal and vertical axes, and obtain a permutation graph; translate and scale values to normalize \(f(0)=0,f(1)=1\). Exact search over remaining values rejects many branches early by direction and cap monotonicity; visits 98,331 states with no full permutation.

Consequently, a normal \(v\) of \(B\) having a thirteen-point plane gives at least eleven uniform normals of \(B'\) on \(v^\perp\). The [arithmetic-stage note](FugledeF13Size91WeightedProfiles.md) describes the profiles of such pencils and completes the proof.
