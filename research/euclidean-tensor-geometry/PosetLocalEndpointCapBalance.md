# Local endpoint compression finds near-half comparisons between specified chains

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted development and internal verification; no external peer review or priority claim.

Take a poset generated by a disjoint family of chains together with cross-chain comparisons involving only their early and late caps. Comparisons within the early caps, within the late caps, and from an early cap to a late cap are arbitrary subject to acyclicity. There are no late-to-early generators. Cap and interior lengths may be different on each chain; a cap may be empty.

For two specified chains \(A,B\), let \(m_i\) be the number of interior elements and \(M=\min(m_A,m_B)\). Let \(d_i^-\) count the principal ideal of the last early vertex in the early-cap poset, including itself; let \(d_i^+\) count the principal filter of the first late vertex in the late-cap poset. An absent cap has \(d_i^\pm=0\). Put \(a_i=d_i^-/m_i,\ b_i=d_i^+/m_i\).

**Local criterion.** Along any sequence with \(M\to\infty\), suppose the four ratios for \(i=A,B\) are bounded and there is a fixed \(\epsilon>0\) such that
\[
a_A b_B,\ a_B b_A\le 1-\epsilon.
\]
Then there is a pair consisting of one interior vertex of \(A\) and one of \(B\) with ordering probability \(1/2+O(M^{-1/2})\) in a uniform linear extension. The constant depends only on the four-ratio bound and the margin \(\epsilon\), not on relative interior lengths, the number of other chains, or their sizes.

In particular, for \(w\) equal-length chains of length \(n\) with caps of length \(k\), the criterion holds uniformly whenever \(wk/(n-2k)\le\alpha<1\). For width three one may take any fixed fraction \(k/n<1/5\). If the relevant early ideals and late filters on \(A,B\) have no vertices from other chains, any fixed fraction \(k/n<1/3\) works for that pair. In contrast to the [prescribed midpoint theorem](PosetEndpointCapBalance.md), both interior ranks may move.

## 1. Compress only the needed ideal or filter

Represent a uniform linear extension by uniform order-polytope coordinates, conditional on all generating comparisons. With cap coordinates fixed, the interior coordinates of each chain \(i\) are the order statistics of \(m_i\) independent uniforms on \((l_i,u_i)\), independently across chains. Here \(l_i\) is the last early coordinate (or 0) and \(u_i\) the first late coordinate (or 1). The cap marginal density over its feasible domain is proportional to \(\prod_i(u_i-l_i)^{m_i}\); this is the product of the volumes of the intervening ordered simplices, up to constant factorials.

For a present early cap, take the event \(l_i>\eta\), where \(0<\eta<1\). Multiply only the coordinates in its principal early ideal by \(\rho\in(0,1)\), leaving all other coordinates fixed. This is an injective map into the feasible domain. Within the early layer the ideal property prevents any comparison from pointing into the compressed coordinates from outside, and early-to-late inequalities can only improve. No interval width decreases. The target interval, originally of width at most \(1-l_i\), gains at least \((1-\rho)\eta\); its relative gain is at least \((1-\rho\eta)/(1-\eta)\). The Jacobian is \(\rho^{d_i^-}\). Comparing its image integral with the total integral gives
\[
\Pr(l_i>\eta)\le
\rho^{-d_i^-}\left(\frac{1-\eta}{1-\rho\eta}\right)^{m_i}.
\]
For \(\eta/(1-\eta)>a_i\) with fixed positive slack, a \(\rho<1\) close enough to 1 makes the bound exponentially small in \(m_i\). The reflected map compresses only the late filter toward 1 and bounds the event \(1-u_i>\eta\) with \(a_i\) replaced by \(b_i\). Empty caps already obey the desired endpoint bounds.

The identity
\[
1-\frac{a}{1+a}-\frac{b}{1+b}=\frac{1-ab}{(1+a)(1+b)}
\]
gives a uniform positive crossed gap under the hypotheses. For each early threshold take \(a_i/(1+a_i)\) plus a sufficiently small fixed positive shift, and for each late threshold \(b_i/(1+b_i)\) plus the same shift. The ratio slacks are then uniformly positive. Bounded ratios and the product margin allow a common positive \(\gamma\) and common exponential rate in the four bounds. Therefore, except on an event of probability \(O(e^{-cM})\), both \(u_B-l_A\) and \(u_A-l_B\) exceed \(\gamma\). No lower bound on the width of either individual sampling interval is assumed in this conclusion.

## 2. Move the two ranks through the central portions

Choose a fixed small \(\delta<\gamma/8\). In each interior let the low and high ranks be the rounded \(\delta m_i\) and \((1-\delta)m_i\). On the good cap event, the conditional means of \(A_{\mathrm{low}}\) and \(B_{\mathrm{high}}\) are separated in that order by at least \(\gamma-2\delta-O(M^{-1})\), using the mean of a uniform order statistic and interval widths at most one. Their conditional variances are at most \(1/[4(m_i+2)]\). Chebyshev and the cap error therefore give
\[
\Pr[A_{\mathrm{low}}\prec B_{\mathrm{high}}]=1-O(M^{-1}),\qquad
\Pr[A_{\mathrm{high}}\prec B_{\mathrm{low}}]=O(M^{-1}).
\]

Starting at the first pair, increase \(A\)'s rank while \(B\)'s rank stays high, and then decrease \(B\)'s rank while \(A\)'s rank stays high. The comparison probability varies monotonically. For any one step, condition on the caps and the value \(v\) at the fixed rank on the other chain. The chance that \(v\) falls between ranks \(r,r+1\) of the moving chain is exactly \(\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(m_i,p)=r]\), with \(p\) the relative position of \(v\) in that chain's sampling interval clipped to \([0,1]\). The point mass is maximized at \(p=r/m_i\); by Stirling it is \(O_\delta(m_i^{-1/2})\) when \(r/m_i\) is away from zero and one, as along this whole path. This estimate needs neither good caps nor comparable widths or interior lengths. The crossing of \(1/2\) therefore has a step of size \(O(M^{-1/2})\), with a pair on either side as claimed.

## 3. Exact fixtures and scope

The [integer checker](verify-poset-local-cap-balance.js) counts chain-prefix walks with and without a specified comparison. At \(n=36,k=10\) in three chains use \(A_{10}<B_1,\ B_{36}<C_{27},\ C_2<A_{27}\). The crossed products are \(200/256\), which meets the local criterion despite exceeding the uniform cap-length regime. This scales to length 36t and cap length 10t by using the constraints A_(10t) < B_1, B_(36t) < C_(26t+1), and C_(2t) < A_(26t+1), preserving the crossed products. At t=1, \(A_{20}\prec B_{16}\) has probability approximately \(0.5030981569\). A second case at \(n=42,k=8\) gives \(0.5081453129\). An unequal-length fixture with interior lengths \(12,28,20\) gives \(0.5386047081\). They verify the exact comparison counts and local cap sizes, not the asymptotic bound.

[Aires–Kahn (2025), Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26134v1) already yields qualitative **global** near-balance for the fixed-width endpoint-cap families with growing interiors, since interior vertices of different chains are incomparable in this model. Their Theorem 1.5 also localizes to a vertex whose rank variance diverges, without prescribing the partner chain. The results here give an explicit \(M^{-1/2}\) rate and keep both vertices inside two specified chains, with conditions local to their endpoints; the earlier version also prescribes a midpoint under stronger hypotheses. [Aires–Chan–Pak–Panova](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30888v2) treat the general balance constant. Neither of these cap criteria resolves general width three.
