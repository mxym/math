# Internal rank four with one quadratic-degree side and no pure-pair caps

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** Let \(H\) be a finite simple four-partite 4-uniform family on \(A,B,C,D\). Suppose C degree is at most \(Lm^2\), and \(AC,BC,CD,ABD\) multiplicities are at most \(Lm\), with \(L\ge1,m\ge2\). There is an internally \(m\)-sunflower-witnessed subfamily of size \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\), including the empty kernel. There are no assumptions on other vertex degrees or any of \(AB,AD,BD\) multiplicities. The unrestricted rank-four problem and rank four with just the C-degree bound remain open. This is a restricted case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

## Initial reduction

Apply the edge sampling in the [capped-projection proof](./InternalSunflowersOneQuadraticSideCappedProjections.md#initial-thinning-and-the-clean-host-claim), omitting its AB cutoff: independent edge sampling at \(1/[128(L+1)m]\), delete all initially sampled projection conflicts at \(AC,BC,CD,ABD\), and reject C labels with initial sampled degree exceeding \(16m\). The conditional conflict probability is at most \(4Lm/[128(L+1)m]\), and the expected count of competing sampled edges at C is at most \(m/128\). Thus a constant fraction of the expected sample survives, there are \(\Omega_L(|H|/m)\) edges, the four indicated projections are injective, and C-degree is at most \(Km\) for \(K=16\). Call such a subhost clean. We seek \(\Omega_K(E/m^3)\) nonempty-witnessed edges in any clean host with \(E\) edges. Use the earlier clean-host theorem from the linked proof: if any one pure pair type \(AB,AD,BD\) has multiplicity at most \(Rm\), the bound holds with constant depending on \(K,R\).

Choose constants in order: the all-high edge-sampling constant \(c\) small in terms of \(K\), then a quadratic cutoff \(Q_2\) large relative to \(1/c\), and \(\alpha>0\) small relative to \(K,Q_2\). Put \(p=\alpha/m\). Choose the endpoint budget constant \(C_0\) sufficiently large absolute, the vertex count constant \(T\) sufficiently large in terms of \(K,Q_2,\alpha\), and the linear cutoff \(Q_1\) sufficiently large absolute with \(T\le Q_1 C_0/\alpha\). All constants are fixed independently of \(m,E\); they may absorb the absolute retained fraction \(\delta\) below.

## Linear owners and lock exits

Repeatedly peel positive pure pair fibers of size less than \(Q_1m\), assigning each deleted edge to its deleting pair type. Each owner subhost has that pair type capped by \(Q_1m\), because each concrete pair is charged once. If at least half the mass is peeled, the heaviest owner gives an exit by the capped theorem. Otherwise retain the residual of mass \(E_0\ge E/2\), with all positive pure pairs of size at least \(Q_1m\).

For an ordered pair of distinct pure coordinates \(X,Y\), let \(\operatorname{Top}_{K_0}(X\to Y)\) sum the masses of the \(K_0\) largest \(XY\) buckets for each \(X\) label, where \(K_0=\lceil C_0m/p\rceil\). If one of these six quantities exceeds \(E_0/C_0\), sample the coordinate \(Y\), with \(X\) one of the two nonsampled sides. Restrict to those top buckets and lock \(X\) immediately, reducing the rate to \(1/(C_0K_0)\). Repeatedly peel the two pure pair types incident to \(Y\) at \(Q_1m\); a large owner exits by the capped theorem, otherwise keep the core. Retest the still-unlocked side at its cutoff \(\lceil C_0m/p'\rceil\), where \(p'\) is the updated rate. If it concentrates above current mass divided by \(C_0\), similarly lock it, restrict, reduce the rate and clean once again. Restrictions/cleanings lose only fixed factors, and at most two locks give final rate \(p_f=\Omega(m^{-3})\). At each locked side its support is bounded by the cutoff at its own lock, and that cutoff times \(p_f\) is at most \(1/C_0\).

Independently sample \(Y\) labels at \(p_f\). Drop any sampled edge sharing C with another initially sampled edge, and all edges at locked endpoints with two or more initially sampled \(Y\) neighbors. A rival at C always uses a different \(Y\) label by C-cross injectivity, so conditional loss is at most \(Kmp_f+2/C_0\). The original cap at the pair of nonsampled sides is not needed: every surviving locked endpoint uses just one \(Y\) value and \(ABD\) injectivity then makes the nonsampled pair unique. If the mass before sampling is \(M_f\), interleave peeling positive \(Y\)-incident pairs below size \(m\) and positive unlocked labels with fewer than \(m\) \(Y\) neighbors. The former costs at most \(2p_f M_f/Q_1\) in expectation, since original fibers had size at least \(Q_1m\), and each is available only when its \(Y\) label is sampled. Each latter peel charges at most the \(m\) largest initially sampled buckets at that label. Its expectation summed over one side is at most \(p_f M_f/C_0+m M_f/\lceil C_0m/p_f\rceil\), by the top test and the bound on individual bucket weights outside the top. Thus a constant fraction of \(p_f M_f\) survives. Surviving pair kernels use their fibers. An unlocked nonsampled label uses \(m\) distinct \(Y\) neighbors, with remaining nonsampled labels distinct by pair injectivity; a locked label cannot appear as a singleton kernel. A positive \(Y\) label has a projected link of positive minimum degree at least \(m\) on both sides, yielding an \(m\)-matching. Indeed, a smaller maximal matching would leave a positive uncovered vertex and fewer than \(m\) covered vertices in its opposite side, contradicting its degree. C is globally unique. This proves the lock exit.

Now assume all six top budgets are at most \(E_0/C_0\). If a pure label has degree less than \(Tm^3\), its number of neighbors in any other pure coordinate is at most \(Tm^2/Q_1\le K_0\), by the \(Q_1m\) pair minima. Its entire mass lies in the corresponding top budget. Remove all such labels across the three pure sides, losing at most \(3E_0/C_0\). The remaining three sides have at most \(3E_0/(Tm^3)\) original labels in total, a count bound that persists under restriction. The six top budgets also persist as absolute bounds.

## Quadratic designation

Peel positive pure pair buckets below \(Q_2m^2\) on all not-yet-designated types. If the residual keeps at least half, use it and designate outstanding types high (positive minimum \(Q_2m^2\)); otherwise use the largest owner (at least \(1/6\) of current mass), designate that type low (maximum \(Q_2m^2\)), and repeat skipping the designated low types. Low caps persist. At most three low restrictions and one core exit give a final host \(J\) of mass \(M\ge\delta E_0\) with absolute \(\delta=1/1024\) if \(C_0\ge6\). The inherited label count and top budgets remain true.

## All-low and all-high exits

If all types are low, independently sample edges at \(r=1/[128(3Q_2+K+1)m^2]\), remove all initial C and pure pair conflicts, then peel positive pure labels below degree \(m\). Conditional conflict probability is at most \((3Q_2m^2+Km)r\), and the total peel costs at most \(3E_0/(Tm^2)\), negligible versus \(rM\) for large \(T\). All surviving pure singletons have \(m\) disjoint petals by pair injectivity and global C uniqueness, and no other nonempty kernel repeats.

If all types are high, sample independent edges at \(c/m\), remove C conflicts at conditional cost at most \(Kc\), then peel positive pure pair sizes below \(m\). The total peel costs at most \(3M/(Q_2m)\), negligible versus \(cM/m\) for large \(Q_2c\). Pair kernels use their fibers, and a surviving pure label's projected link has positive minimum degree at least \(m\) on both sides, giving an \(m\)-matching as above. Again C is globally unique.

## Mixed exits: select buckets, then edges

Use \(p=\alpha/m\) for the outer sample and \(q=1/m\) for the independent conditional edge sample. When exactly two types are high, name them \(AD,BD\), independently sample D labels at \(p\), then sample edges at \(q\) inside selected labels. When exactly one type is high, name it \(AD\), independently sample occupied \(AD\) buckets at \(p\), then sample edges at \(q\) inside selected buckets.

This switch from singleton sampling to pair-bucket sampling ensures that (i) each high pair bucket belongs to just one outer object and (ii) for a given edge, every competing edge sharing a low pure pair or C belongs to a different outer object. For two high types, (ii) is \(ABD\) and \(CD\) injectivity. For one high type, two edges sharing \(AB\) or \(BD\) cannot share \(AD\) by \(ABD\) injectivity; two edges sharing C cannot also share \(AD\) by C-cross injectivity. Thus every such rival is initially sampled with probability \(pq\) conditional on the given edge being initially sampled.

Erase any initially sampled edge sharing C or any low pure pair with another initially sampled edge. Conditional loss is at most \((Km+2Q_2m^2)pq\). At each high bucket of original size \(f\), also erase its whole sample if its initial inner sample exceeds \(4qf\). Conditional on a specific edge in it being sampled, the count has mean at most \(1+qf\), so failure probability is at most \((1+qf)/(4qf)\le0.26\) using \(qf\ge Q_2m\). At most two bucket tests affect a given edge. For small enough \(\alpha\) the remaining expected mass is at least \(0.4pqM\).

Interleave the following peels until none apply.

* Delete positive high buckets of size below \(m\). Expected cost per type is at most \(pM/(Q_2m)\), since each bucket initially had size at least \(Q_2m^2\) and is charged only when its outer object was selected.
* At labels requiring \(m\) distinct high-pair neighbors for singleton witnesses, delete at positive support less than \(m\). These are A and B in the two-high case, and A and D in the one-high case. Once a label is peeled, its cost is bounded by \(4q\) times the sum of its \(m\) largest originally outer-activated bucket weights. The top \(K_0\) original bucket masses have inherited global total at most \(E_0/C_0\) in that orientation and each bucket is active with probability \(p\). Each smaller bucket weighs at most its label's original mass divided by \(K_0\). Therefore the expectation per orientation is at most
\[
4q\big[pE_0/C_0+mM/K_0\big]\le 8pqE_0/C_0.
\]
Only linearity of expectation is used, not independence between bucket activations for this charge.
* In the one-high case only, peel all positive B labels below degree \(m\). Cost is at most \(3E_0/(Tm^2)\) by the inherited label count.

These costs sum to less than \(0.2pqM\) by the choice of constants and \(M\ge\delta E_0\). Some sample therefore retains \(\Omega_K(M/m^2)\) edges. C and all low pure pairs are injective, and high pair kernels have \(m\) petals in their surviving fibers. In the one-high case a surviving A or D label takes a representative at each of \(m\) distinct surviving high-bucket neighbors; the low pair involving B prevents any other repeated label. A B label's link is a matching by \(AB,BD\) injectivity, and its degree is at least \(m\). In the two-high case A and B likewise use distinct D neighbors and \(AB\) injectivity; D uses an \(m\)-matching in its AB link by the high \(AD,BD\) minima. This accounts for every nonempty kernel.

The initial owner and lock exits retain \(\Omega_K(E/m^3)\); the four quadratic cases retain at least this much. Including the initial thinning yields \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\) nonempty-witnessed edges.

## Empty kernel

The hypotheses are hereditary. If some disjoint \(m\)-matching leaves at least half of \(H\) avoiding its vertices, apply nonempty extraction to that residue and restore the matching. This witnesses all empty intersections and creates no new nonempty ones. Otherwise a vertex has degree at least \(|H|/(8m)\): the endpoints of any \(m\)-matching meet more than half the edges, and, if no such matching exists, the endpoints of a smaller maximal matching cover all edges. Use the [full rank-three theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) in its link and lift by the fixed vertex, giving the same order and all needed witnesses.
