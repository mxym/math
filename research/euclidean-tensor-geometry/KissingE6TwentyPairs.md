# Eliminating 20 antipodal pairs in the complete E6 branch

**Conditional theorem.** A 73-point six-dimensional kissing configuration containing a representative of every E6 root line has at most **19 true antipodal pairs**. This is not a bound on arbitrary 73-point configurations or a solution of the six-dimensional kissing problem.

By the [preceding exclusion](KissingE6TwentyOnePairs.md), it suffices to exclude exactly 20 pairs, leaving 16 singly represented original axes and 17 outsiders. Let U be the 16 unused signed original roots. Use squared norm two unless stated otherwise. The signed blocker mask of an outsider is the set of original roots with scalar product strictly above 1; these blockers all lie in U. No other antipodal pair can occur: that would extend the original projective code of 36 lines. A negative edge joins outsiders with product below −1, and its masks are axis-disjoint, as in the preceding exclusions.

## Boundary exchange

Each sign in U must be blocked by at least two outsiders. Otherwise remove its sole blocking outsider (or any outsider if there is none) and insert the sign. The result still has 73 points, represents all 36 original lines, and has at least 21 pairs, a contradiction. In fact any nonempty subset of U must be blocked by more outsiders than signs, by the same exchange. We use only the singleton statement.

## A two-edge matching is impossible

The [global mask capacity theorem](KissingE6GlobalMaskCapacity.md) gives at least five blockers per outsider, at most sixteen, and at most one outsider per signed mask. Endpoints of any negative edge therefore have at most eleven blockers. Extend the positive upward ideals to obtain the six additional size-eleven types up to diagram symmetry. As in the [earlier census](KissingE6TwentyThreePairs.md), a negative edge requires that two outer-hull generators have scalar product strictly below −1 in some relative chamber. For positive exclusions we use the proved linear and row-wise angular tests, keeping every candidate not excluded.

1. The 2,059 first-edge orbits give 3,279,578 anchored two-edge patterns. After positive-product exclusions there are 300,270 patterns, giving 52,558 symmetry classes up to E6 automorphisms and the reordering of the two unlabelled edges. Of these, 8 use 13 signed roots, 315 use 14, 5,054 use 15, and 47,181 use 16.
2. Drop the endpoint labels and complete each union to 16 signed roots by freely choosing signs on previously unused axes. The 10,381 distinct indexed unions produce 129,280 distinct indexed completions and 10,618 symmetry classes. To canonicalize, carry the sum of the roots to the dominant chamber, account for diagram reversal, and minimize the transported mask under the stabilizer of that sum. Every identification is by an explicit root-system automorphism.
3. In each signed universe U, enumerate all candidate outsider masks. Start with both size-five seed families, including the impossible fork for extensions only. For every canonical ideal, and every one-root addition, carry the sum to the dominant chamber and recognize the resulting ideal up to diagram symmetry. Use the resulting group transporter to generate the analogous allowed extensions of every encountered mask. Every positive upward ideal has a one-smaller upward subideal. The [dominant-sum stabilizer lemma](KissingE6GlobalMaskCapacity.md) ensures that the chamber recognition misses none. There are 74 formal diagram-types through size sixteen (including the fork) and 9,072 signed size-five seeds. An empty spherical outer hull excludes an outsider, not an intermediate extension seed.
4. Make a compatibility graph on the remaining masks, discarding a pair only when the rational-hull tests prove incompatibility in every relative chamber. For any unbounded chamber coordinate, the cap 2 is valid by Cauchy–Schwarz. Large squared comparisons use exact big integers. When checking relative chambers, the stabilizer of a positive upward ideal in the Weyl group is generated by the simple reflections perpendicular to its dominant sum. Diagram reversal adds a generator precisely when it fixes the ideal, since distinct dominant positive ideals cannot be Weyl-conjugate. Group all candidates into greedy color classes of pairwise incompatible masks. A clique has at most one representative per color. Branch on the last participating color and recurse on compatible earlier colors; this yields an exact clique decision.
5. Of the 10,618 classes, **534** contain fewer than 17 candidates. Another **8,006** fail the singleton exchange test. The remaining **2,078** have no 17-clique. Hence no 73-point code at this boundary has two disjoint negative edges.

## The remaining case and the known D5 bound

The negative graph is triangle-free: three squared-norm-two vectors with all mutual products below −1 have negative squared norm for their sum. A triangle-free graph with no two disjoint edges has a vertex cover of size at most one. Delete one outsider, leaving 16 independent outsiders. These and the 20 paired original lines give 36 lines with all unit absolute scalar products at most 1/2. By [Anstreicher’s uniqueness theorem](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/), they form a second E6 line system. Its common roots with the original system are precisely the 40 roots on the paired original lines. The intersection is closed under root reflections. The only simply-laced root system with exactly 40 roots and rank at most six is D5. Therefore the original 73-point code contains D5.

This last obstruction is known: [Aleksei Novgorodtsev, Theorem 2 (2026 preprint)](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202610.0335) bounds a six-dimensional code with a D5 cross section by 72 points. We give the D5 upper-bound argument to make explicit why containing D5 suffices. In its five-dimensional span write the roots as ±e_i±e_j. The horizontal part v of any additional code point satisfies |v_i|+|v_j|≤1, hence ‖v‖²≤5/4. If its largest absolute coordinate a is at most 1/2 this is immediate; otherwise maximize the convex upper bound a²+4(1−a)² on [1/2,1]. Thus no further point lies in this span. After unit normalization all additional points have absolute height at least √(3/8).

On either side project horizontally and normalize to the unit sphere in dimension five. Two points at positive heights h,k≥√(3/8) have projected scalar product at most (1/2−hk)/√((1−h²)(1−k²))≤1/5. The expression is decreasing in each variable, by the derivative signs h/2−k and k/2−h. A pole cannot coexist with a point on the same side. For the projected points take the cubic f(t)=(t+3/5)²(t−1/5), nonpositive on [−1,1/5]. In the positive spherical kernels 1,t,t²−1/5,t³−3t/7 its coefficients are 16/125, 3/25+3/7, 1, 1. Summing over all ordered pairs gives N²·16/125≤N f(1)=N·256/125, so each side has at most 16 points. The total is at most 40+16+16=72, a contradiction.
