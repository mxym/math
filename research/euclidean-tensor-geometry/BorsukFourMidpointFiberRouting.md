# Five strict pieces near three equilateral midpoint fibers

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** Let \(a_0,a_1,a_2\) be a unit regular triangle in a plane \(P\subset\mathbb R^4\), let \(W=P^\perp\), put \(D=\sqrt3,\ r=D/2\), and set
\[
\mathcal L=\{a_0,a_1,a_2\}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{i=0}^2 \bigl(-a_i/2+B_W(0,r)\bigr)
\]
where the disks are closed. Set \(\delta=10^{-8}\) and \(\epsilon=10^{-6}\). Every compact set \(X\) in the closed \(\delta\)-neighborhood of \(\mathcal L\), of diameter at most \(D\), is covered by five sets of diameter at most \(D-\epsilon+2\delta<D\). The centers \(a_i\) need not belong to \(X\); if they are contacts, this is a quantitatively thick, but restricted, subcase of the centered regular-triangle spindle. Scale by \(1/\sqrt3\) for diameter one. This concerns a narrower locus than the [ProofAtlas regular-triangle spindle route](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/borsuk-problem-r4/) and leaves the full four-dimensional problem open; for the general six-part bounds see [Chalmers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17375) and [Tolmachev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16775).

**Reduction.** Map each point to any closest point of \(\mathcal L\), and close the image to form \(Y\). Then \(\operatorname{diam}(Y)\le D+2\delta\). Pulling back any cover adds at most \(2\delta\) to its piece diameters. Write \(Y_i\) for the portion on disk \(i\), and reserve color \(i\) for this disk together with the apex \(a_{i+1}\) (subscripts modulo three) when present. Any disk \(i\) point and that apex have squared distance at most \(3/2\): their planar separation squared is \(3/4\), and their transverse radius is at most \(r\). Colors 4 and 5 are initially unused. A disk is called bad when \(\operatorname{diam}(Y_i)>D-\epsilon\). Points of transverse norm \(<r-\epsilon\) are safe within their own disk color against every point of that disk. Call all points of transverse norm at least \(r-\epsilon\) near-boundary points.

**Two directional facts.** In a bad disk there are two near-boundary points with transverse vectors \(w,w'\) at separation \(>2r-\epsilon\). The parallelogram identity gives \(\|w+w'\|\le\sqrt{4r\epsilon}\). For unit directions \(v=w/\|w\|,\ v'=w'/\|w'\|\), it follows that \(\|v+v'\|\le(\sqrt{4r\epsilon}+\epsilon)/(r-\epsilon)<0.0024\), so the angular deviation from antipodality is \(<0.005\). In different disks the planar separation squared is \(3/4\); hence any near-boundary points have unit directions satisfying \(v\cdot u\ge-0.501\). To check the latter, if the dot product is below \(-0.501\), the squared separation is strictly larger than \(3/4+2(r-\epsilon)^2(1+0.501)>(D+2\delta)^2\). The displayed constants follow from \(0.866<r<0.867\), \(D<2\) and \(r^2=3/4\).

**At most two bad disks.** If exactly one is bad, cut it into three transverse sectors of width \(2\pi/3\) and use both spare colors for two of these sectors. Within a disk, transverse vectors of norms at most \(r\) and angular separation at most \(\theta\) have squared distance at most \(\max(r^2,2r^2(1-\cos\theta))\), by convexity separately in the two norms. Thus each of these sectors has squared diameter at most \(9/4\). If two disks are bad, each has a near-boundary witness in the other. By the dot product bound, this witness excludes an open cap of halfwidth \(\pi/4\) around its opposite from the near-boundary directions of the first disk. Split the remaining closed \(3\pi/2\) arc into two arcs of width \(3\pi/4\). Use a different spare color for each bad disk, and allocate each disk's other half and its radial interior to its reserved color. The sector diameter squared is at most \(21/8\), using \(\cos(3\pi/4)>-3/4\). Interior points cost at most \(D-\epsilon\) within the disk; every other color retains its original strict bound. The zero-bad case needs no adjustment.

**Three bad disks.** Select one nearly antipodal pair per disk and let its first ray define a projective axis (direction modulo \(\pi\)). Any near-boundary direction in another disk has dot product at least \(-0.501\) with each ray of the pair. The pair has angular deviation \(<0.005\) from antipodality, so the absolute dot product with the first ray is at most \(0.506\). Thus the projective angular distance is at least \(\pi/3-0.01\), since \(\cos(\pi/3-0.01)>0.506\). Apply this to the three chosen axes: all three cyclic projective gaps are in \([\pi/3-0.01,\pi/3+0.02]\). For a fixed disk rotate its axis to zero. The other two axes in \(\mathbb R/\pi\mathbb Z\) are within \(0.02\) of \(\pi/3\) and \(2\pi/3\), respectively. Every near-boundary point in the fixed disk must therefore be at projective distance at least \(\pi/3-0.03\) from both of those canonical axes. The intersection of those two complements lies in the \(0.03\)-neighborhood of zero modulo \(\pi\). Hence each disk's near-boundary points occupy two opposing spots of angular halfwidth \(0.03\).

Move one of the two spots from each disk out of its reserved color. For two disks choose the moved spots' signs so their central rays have ordinary angular separation at most \(\pi/3+0.02\). Then the spots' points have mutual separation in direction less than \(\pi/2\), so pooled squared diameter is at most \(3/4+2r^2=9/4\) and they can share spare color 4. Use spare color 5 for the third moved spot. Each reserved color keeps only one near-boundary spot within its disk; these have squared diameter at most \(2r^2\), and radial interior points or the permitted apex obey their previous bounds. Therefore all five pieces have diameter at most \(D-\epsilon\), as desired. Ties may be assigned arbitrarily.

## A barrier to the nearest-apex rule on the full star locus

Even on the spherical edge locus, a single nearest-apex color for each contact fails on all three cells at once. Choose \(t=25/41>1/2\), \(k=\sqrt{t/(1-t)}=5/4\), \(R=\sqrt{3t(1-t)}\), and \(\theta=\arccos(-25/32)\in(2\pi/3,\pi)\). For \(b(\phi)\) the unit vector of angle \(\phi\) in \(W\), take the six points
\[
p_{i,+}=t a_i+(1-t)a_{i+1}+R b(2\pi i/3),\qquad
p_{i,-}=t a_i+(1-t)a_{i-1}+R b(2\pi i/3+\theta).
\]
Together with \(a_0,a_1,a_2\) these form a diameter-\(\sqrt3\), unit-circumradius set. Every displayed point lies on the unit sphere and has nearest apex \(a_i\); each pair \(p_{i,+},p_{i,-}\) is diametric. To verify the rest, unit points with barycentric vectors \(\lambda,\mu\) and angular directions \(\phi,\psi\) have squared distance \(\le3\) if and only if \((3/2)\lambda\cdot\mu+R^2\cos(\phi-\psi)\ge0\). Within one sign, distinct indices share a mixed-weight component \(t(1-t)\), giving equality at angular distance \(2\pi/3\). Across signs at distinct indices, their planar supports either coincide, in which case \(\lambda\cdot\mu=2t(1-t)\) is always enough, or meet at the shared nonnearest vertex, in which case their angular distance is \(\theta-2\pi/3<\pi/3\), automatically safe. With any apex compatibility follows from nonnegative barycentric coordinates. The three apices force unit circumradius by averaging their squared distances from any center. Thus each of the three nearest-apex candidate parts needs some color change beyond this naive rule. The set itself is 3-colorable: give \(a_i,p_{i,+}\) color \(i\) and \(p_{i,-}\) color \(i-1\). This does not obstruct five parts by mixed routing nor settle the full contact-star problem.
