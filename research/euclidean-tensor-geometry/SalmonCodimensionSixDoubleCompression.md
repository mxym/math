# Completing the codimension-six double-compression estimate

Use the kernel-line parametrization and conjugation/fiber bounds in the codimension-five proof. This note bounds the remaining bad locus of type \((3,3,4)\) by dimension 34.

## Semisimple and one-dual degenerations

In the semisimple case, zero or one vanishing kernel coordinate, or one on each side with differing missing indices, was already certified. With two zeros and a repeated factor or shared collinear triple, the positive-dimensional fibers from the codimension-five proof give dimension at most 34. Exactly three zero coordinates force a repeated factor on at least one side. Every repeated-factor tensor, independently of other zeros, lies in the image of the entire two-zero-on-one-side parameter locus (dimension 38), where varying the two independent lines on the corresponding Segre line gives fiber dimension at least ten, as their A-side repetition persists; hence the repeated-factor image is always at most \(38-10+6=34\). A kernel supported on one coordinate would mean a zero projected factor, impossible when C has full flattening. Four or more zeros are in any case at most 34 by the basic bound.

In the single-dual case the dimension before additional flags is at most 37. Three or more independent zero conditions therefore suffice. On one kernel line, the possible codimension-two flags, up to swapping singleton factors, are (using the original basis \(\epsilon,1,e_2,e_3\)):
- \(k_0=k_1=0\): the singleton factors \(a_2,a_3\) are proportional. Their independent matrix lines can vary in the Segre line they span, holding the tensor fixed by rechoosing C factors. This varies the projection parameters within the flag locus, and gives additional fiber dimension.
- \(k_1=k_2=0\): \(a_3\) is proportional to the tangent anchor \(a_0\). Keep the anchor and the derivative matrix fixed, and vary the singleton matrix \(a_3b_3^\top\) along the Segre line through it and the anchor \(a_0b_0^\top\). Rechoose the C basis for those two rank-one slices. This preserves the flags and gives at least one new fiber parameter.
- \(k_2=k_3=0\): \(a_1\) is proportional to \(a_0\); the derivative matrix \(a_1b_0^\top+a_0b_1^\top\) is itself rank one. The tensor is now in the semisimple repeated-factor locus and inherits its bound.

In the preceding fiber arguments, the independent third basis is adjusted uniquely once the ordered slice matrices are changed, and the extra parameters change their projective matrix lines. Unit and automorphism stabilizer orbits do not change the corresponding singleton matrix lines (possible block swaps are discrete). Thus at least one parameter is genuinely in addition to the previously subtracted unit/automorphism orbit.

It remains to put one flag in each kernel line. The mixed flags of types 1 and 2, and the crossed singleton flags 2 and 3, are both regular by the exact checks below. Two flags of type 1 put the projected tangent anchor and the two singleton matrices in a fixed \(2\times2\) compression. They span a three-dimensional matrix space. Its projective plane meets the rank-one quadric in a conic; fixing the anchor and derivative matrix and varying the two singleton lines on that conic provides extra fiber parameters. Two flags involving the same singleton (both type 2) instead put the tangent anchor, derivative and that singleton in the \(2\times2\) compression. Their matrix plane cuts a smooth conic: choose the ordered bases \(a_0,a_1\) and \(b_0,b_1\) of the two supports, so after unit normalizations the singleton projections are \(-a_0-a_1\) and \(-b_0-b_1\). The matrix space is the space of symmetric \(2\times2\) matrices, with smooth determinant conic. Choose any nearby tangent anchor on the conic and its projective tangent line, together with any nearby independent simple site on the conic. The tangent line is a Segre tangent direction, represented by \(a_1 b_0^\top+a_0 b_1^\top\) with suitable derivative factors. Rechoose the C basis to keep the tensor. The other singleton remains fixed outside the two supports. This produces extra fiber parameters and retains the two flags. In particular the moving singleton line is not accounted for by the previously subtracted group orbit. This bounds all remaining one-dual images by 34.

## Other algebra types and checks

For two dual factors or a length-three factor with a singleton, the general image bound is 36, so two additional zero conditions suffice. Unit transitivity classifies the one-flag boundary: for two dual factors \(k_1=0\) (or \(k_3=0\) by swapping the blocks); for length three \(k_2=0\) with \(k_1k_3\ne0\), or \(k_3=0\) with \(k_2\ne0\). Additional coordinate zeros within these boundaries have dimension at most 34 by the same kernel map; all three named types are regular by the checks. For the curvilinear length-four algebra the bound is 35 and the generic \(k_3\ne0\) is one unit orbit on each side by triangular Toeplitz multiplication; its regular representative is checked. The Gorenstein \((1,2,1)\) type has automorphism dimension at least four, from a two-dimensional similitude group of a nondegenerate binary pairing and two socle shifts. It is already at most 34.

For non-Gorenstein and exceptional modules the only remaining dimension bound of 35 comes from the source \((\mathbb C[x,y]/(x,y)^2)\times\mathbb C\), of source dimension at most 36, and the earlier one-dimensional bound on stabilizer dimensions for the fixed-projection group. This source has generic stabilizer dimension zero, which lowers its whole image bound to 34: the nonfree subset has dimension at most 35 and orbit dimension at least seven; the free subset has orbit dimension eight; then add six for the varying supports. Here is the free check. If C has an inverse slice, the normalized side module is regular or dual-regular and hence has centralizer equal to its commutative algebra. The identity-slice argument from the codimension-four proof then forces the stabilizer trivial. If C is its non-inverse output factor, it suffices to choose projections injective on the local length-three factor, with nonzero singleton projections and with full C flattening. These conditions hold, for example, for columns \(e_0,e_1,e_2,u\) on both sides. In uncompressed bases, its four C-slices are \(a_0b_0^\top,\ a_1b_0^\top+a_0b_1^\top,\ a_2b_0^\top+a_0b_2^\top,\ a_3b_3^\top\). Fixing these matrices forces \(g_A a_0=\lambda a_0\), \(g_A a_i=\lambda a_i+\mu_i a_0\) for \(i=1,2\), and \(g_Aa_3=\nu a_3\); the B actions are determined reciprocally. This follows by comparing the two rank-one slices first, then the coefficients of \(a_ib_0^\top+a_0b_i^\top\). Injectivity of the projection on the length-three block, and the nonzero singleton image, yield \(\lambda=\nu=1\), \(\mu_i=0\). As each relevant source is one orbit and the fiber-stabilizer condition is generically minimal on it, this proves the bound. Other non-Gorenstein types have source dimension at most 35, as stated in the main note; their prior orbit-dimension bound already suffices.
