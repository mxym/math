# One-sided projectivization: sharp A4, A5 and D6 equatorial bounds

Use squared norm two throughout. A kissing code requires every off-diagonal inner product to be at most one. In the following table the only assumption is that the code **contains the indicated complete root system** in a hyperplane. There is no symmetry hypothesis on the other points.

| ambient dimension | equatorial subsystem | maximum whole code | configurations at equality, up to isometry |
|---:|:---|---:|:---|
| 5 | A4 (20 roots) | 40 | D5 or Q5 |
| 6 | A5 (30 roots) | 72 | E6 only |
| 7 | D6 (60 roots) | 126 | two: E7, or opposite choices of half-spin parity on the two sides |

These are conditional upper bounds, not determinations of the unrestricted kissing numbers. The root/layer constructions themselves are not new. Compare [Cohn–Jiao–Kumar–Torquato](https://msp.org/gt/2011/15-4/gt-v15-n4-p11-p.pdf) and, for the descriptions of D5 and Q5 via A4, [Cohn–Rajagopal, §2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00454-026-00841-x). [Novgorodtsev](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202610.0335) treats the different D5/L5/Q5/R5 sections in dimension six and E6 in dimension seven. The proof below uses published sharp projective-code bounds to force equality from a different set of sections.

## A general hemisphere lemma

Let C be an antipodal code spanning the hyperplane H, and suppose its polar in H, defined by v·c≤1 for every c∈C, has squared circumradius at most 3/2 about 0. For any additional point write x=v+h n with n a unit normal. Its projection v belongs to the polar and |h|≥1/√2, so no other equatorial point can occur. For two points on the same side,
```
x·y ≥ hh′−‖v‖‖v′‖ ≥ 1/2−3/2 = −1.
```
Taking both signs of every point on a **single** side and retaining C consequently gives an antipodal kissing code. Denote by P the maximum number of kissing lines in the ambient dimension (squared norm two, |x·y|≤1 on distinct lines). If |C|=2q, either side has at most P−q points; the whole code has at most 2P. Equality forces *both* one-sided completions to be maximum projective codes. By [Anstreicher, Table 1 and §4](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/), P=20,36,63 in dimensions 5,6,7 respectively, and in dimensions six and seven the maximizing line systems are uniquely E6 and E7. We do not use or assert a corresponding uniqueness theorem in dimension five.

## Five-dimensional A4 section

In the sum-zero hyperplane of R⁵ take the roots eᵢ−eⱼ. The horizontal polar has coordinate spread at most one. It is the projection of the cube [0,1]⁵ onto the sum-zero hyperplane: subtract the minimum from any horizontal vector before projecting. Its generators are q_S=1_S−(|S|/5)1, of squared norms |S|(5−|S|)/5≤6/5, with equality only at |S|=2 or 3. By strict convexity the only squared-norm-6/5 points in the polar are these generators. The hemisphere lemma proves the bound.

At equality each side has ten points, with |h|≥2/√5. No pole can occur on that side since it would have product at least √(8/5)>1 with any other point. For two points at positive absolute heights s,t on one side their unit horizontal directions have product at most
```
(1−st) / sqrt((2−s²)(2−t²)) ≤ 1/6.
```
The expression is strictly decreasing in each argument for 2/√5≤s,t<√2 (the derivative in s has the sign of s−2t). Equality requires s=t=2/√5. The ten normalized projections must be the Petersen code by the [Bachoc–Vallentin uniqueness theorem](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~cbachocb/articles/tenpoints.pdf). Each vertex in that code is incident to some pair of product 1/6, so every height is minimal and every projection is one of ±q_S with |S|=2. The Petersen code has no antipodes: exactly one sign is selected for each of the ten S. For disjoint S,T, the opposite-sign pair has normalized product 2/3>1/6. The graph on 2-subsets of a 5-set joining disjoint pairs is connected (the Petersen graph), hence all signs on one side agree. Either layer may independently use either sign, with opposite choices giving D5 and identical choices Q5 as in Cohn–Rajagopal. Cross-side products are at most 6/5−4/5=2/5.

## Six-dimensional A5 section

Repeat the cube projection for six coordinates: the squared polar radius is max k(6−k)/6=3/2, attained exactly at triples. This proves the 72 bound. At equality each one-sided completion is an E6 root system containing the same fixed A5. The following short determinant argument determines that extension with no orientation freedom. If an additional root has height h, the lattice generated by a basis for A5 and this root has determinant 6h², since det(A5)=6. As a full-rank sublattice of E6 (determinant 3), its determinant is 3I² for a positive integer index I. Hence h²=1/2 or 2. In the former case the projection has squared norm 3/2 and is one of the twenty cube projections with three entries +1/2 and three −1/2. In the latter case the root is a pole. The two poles and forty half-height roots exhaust exactly the required 42 additional roots. Both one-sided completions are thus the **same** E6 in the fixed coordinates, and the whole 72-point code is E6.

## Seven-dimensional D6 section

Let D6 consist of ±eᵢ±eⱼ in R⁶. In the polar, |vᵢ|+|vⱼ|≤1. If a=max|vᵢ|≤1/2 then ‖v‖²≤3/2. Otherwise 1/2≤a≤1 and ‖v‖²≤a²+5(1−a)²≤3/2 by convexity on this interval. Equality requires every coordinate to have magnitude 1/2. The hemisphere lemma gives 126.

At equality each one-sided completion is E7. As above, 4h²=2I² because det(D6)=4 and det(E7)=2. Thus h²=2 at a pole or h²=1/2 above a sign vector with entries ±1/2. Each side contains 33 points. At most 32 can be at the lower height, since two sign vectors at Hamming distance one have product 3/2 at that height. The 6-cube has independence number 32 (pair vertices along one coordinate); every independent set of size 32 is a full parity class: its occupied vertices account for every edge by regularity and hence its complement is independent too, so connectedness forces the bipartition. Therefore the pole and one full parity class occur on each side. Conversely all choices work: within a layer the sign distance is at least two; across layers the product is at most 3/2−1/2=1. Changing the sign of a horizontal coordinate interchanges the two parities. There are two classes: the equal-parity choice is the antipodal E7, while differing parities leave only the D6 points and the poles paired (62 points versus 126). Thus the classes are distinct.

A [short exact checker](verify-kissing-equatorial-projectivization.js) verifies the finite configurations, their antipodal counts and the small graphs appearing in the equality arguments. The upper-bound and exhaustion arguments above are analytic, not numerical searches.
