# The regular \(2p\)-gon under only its prime-order rotation subgroup

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal verification.

Let \(p\) be an odd prime, and let \(P\) be the regular \(2p\)-gon. Let \(C_p\) be generated by rotation through \(2\pi/p\). Among affine polyhedral extensions for which every rotation in \(C_p\) lifts to an affine automorphism, whether or not the lifts form a coherent group action, the minimum facet count is **exactly \(2p\)**. The upper bound is \(P\) itself. In particular, retaining only the index-two rotation subgroup, not the full rotation group, still prevents a reduction in the facet count.

## Reduction to a cyclic simplex slice

By the [symmetric bounding lemma of Tonelli Cueto, Lemma 3.2](https://tonellicueto.xyz/pdf/symmetric_extension_polytopes.pdf), it is enough to consider bounded extensions. Suppose such an extension \(Q\to P\) has fewer than \(2p\) facets. Normalize each facet slack to 1 at the barycenter of \(Q\). Any lift permutes these slacks. Choose the barycenter of the fiber over each target vertex as its section point, as in Tonelli Cueto, Lemma 3.3. Every lift of a rotation takes the barycenter of a fiber to that of the rotated fiber. Thus the action on the distinct normalized facet slack functions restricted to these section points is well defined, independent of the choice of lifts (an automorphism over the identity fixes each section point).

Each target facet slack, pulled back to \(Q\), is a nonnegative combination of the lift facet slacks on \(\operatorname{aff}Q\): this follows from the affine Farkas lemma, with zero additive constant because each pulled-back facet slack vanishes at some point of \(Q\). Restrict to the vertex section and merge equal restricted functions. The coefficients can be chosen equivariantly by averaging over the rotation action on the target facets and on the distinct restricted functions. Hence the target slack matrix factors as \(S=T U\) with \(T,U\ge0\), and the two factors intertwine the corresponding permutation actions.

Any fixed function factor has values constant on each of the two target vertex orbits. Every target facet contains one vertex from each orbit. In each row of \(S\) the product term from that fixed factor is consequently identically zero: if its coefficient were positive, both vertex-orbit values would vanish, by testing the two zeros in that row. Delete all fixed terms. The remaining factors fall into orbits of size \(p\), so exactly one orbit must be left (the target slack matrix is nonzero and the number of factors is less than \(2p\)).

Write \(A_P\) for the affine space of all slack vectors of points in the target plane, not necessarily in \(P\). The cyclic orbit gives a new equivariant lift
\[
 Q'=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^p:T x\in A_P\}.
\]
Indeed \(T\ge0\), so its image gives nonnegative target slacks and hence points in \(P\); all vertices of \(P\) are hit by the columns of \(U\). Under rotation the \(p\) coordinates of \(x\) are cyclically permuted. Summing all target slacks gives a fixed positive constant on \(A_P\) (the target facet normals sum to zero). The sum of the entries in each column of \(T\) is the same positive constant by the transitive coordinate action. Consequently \(\sum_j x_j\) is a positive constant on \(Q'\); rescale it to 1. The invariant affine equations for this lift remove a set of Fourier modes of the cyclic representation: the affine space \(T^{-1}A_P\) has fixed constant component, and the remaining real irreducible summands are the distinct conjugate pairs of Fourier modes, each with multiplicity one. An invariant affine space contains its group average and splits along these summands. Center the target at zero; any equivariant affine map to its rotation plane is, after identifying that plane with \(\mathbb C\), a nonzero complex scalar times
\[
 \chi(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}x_j\zeta^j,\qquad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/p},
\]
up to conjugating the complex identification. In particular mode \(1\) is not among the removed modes.

## A local obstruction for the Fourier image

We prove the underlying stronger fact: a cyclically invariant affine slice of the \(p\)-coordinate simplex cannot have a nontrivial centrally symmetric image under a nonzero Fourier mode. Let \(F\subset\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\) be the set of removed frequencies, closed under negation and not containing zero. Use a retained nonzero mode \(b\). A vertex \(x\) of the slice has support \(J\) of size at most \(s=|F|+1\): otherwise there is a real direction supported on \(J\) satisfying the homogeneous constraints, and a small perturbation in both directions contradicts extremality. Its support has size at least \(s\) as well, since if \(|J|\le |F|\), any \(|J|\) rows from the zero constraints would give an invertible square Fourier minor and force \(x=0\). Nonvanishing of all such minors is proved in the next paragraph. Consequently \(x_J\) solves
\[
  M x_J=e_1,\qquad M=(\zeta^{a j})_{a\in(0,F),\,j\in J},
\]
where the first row is the constant one. Replacing the first row of \(M\) by frequency \(b\) gives a matrix \(M_b\) with \(\chi_b(x)=\det(M_b)/\det(M)\).

Put \(\pi=\zeta-1\), and reduce in the local ring of \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]\) at \((\pi)\), whose residue field is \(\mathbb F_p\). For distinct representatives \(a_1,\dots,a_s\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}\), the generalized Vandermonde determinant \(\det(X_j^{a_i})\) is divisible in \(\mathbb Z[X_1,\dots,X_s]\) by \(\prod_{j<k}(X_k-X_j)\). Its quotient at \(X_1=\cdots=X_s=1\) equals \(\det[\binom{a_i}{h}]_{i,\,0\le h<s}\): expand each column in powers of \(X_j-1\) and compare the lowest total degree. This determinant reduces modulo \(p\) to a nonzero row-index Vandermonde divided by \(\prod_{h=0}^{s-1}h!\). After substituting \(X_j=\zeta^j\) the column Vandermonde is a product of nonzero differences, each equal to \(\pi\) times a local unit, because \((\zeta^k-\zeta^j)/\pi\equiv k-j\bmod\pi\). This proves the nonvanishing of all square minors. In the ratio for \(\chi_b(x)\) the column Vandermonde factors cancel, and the two quotient factors are units. The residue of the ratio is
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Vandermonde}(b,F)}
      {\operatorname{Vandermonde}(0,F)}\in\mathbb F_p^\times .
\]
It is the same nonzero residue at every slice vertex, since it depends on the row frequencies and not on the support.

Each vertex of the image of a polytope is the image of a vertex. If the nontrivial Fourier image is centrally symmetric, its center must be 0 by rotation invariance. The image therefore has vertices \(y,-y\). Both lie in the local ring with the same nonzero residue, which is impossible in odd characteristic. This contradiction applied to \(Q'\) proves the stated lower bound for the regular \(2p\)-gon.

## Scope

[Tonelli Cueto, Theorem 4.1](https://tonellicueto.xyz/pdf/symmetric_extension_polytopes.pdf) proves that retaining the full cyclic rotation group of a regular \(n\)-gon forces \(n\) facets. Here only the proper index-two subgroup of the regular \(2p\)-gon is retained, and the proof uses the Fourier slice and its cyclotomic residue. No lower bound of \(2p\) is claimed for an unrestricted extension, which has the classical logarithmic upper bound of [Ben-Tal–Nemirovski / Fiorini–Rothvoß–Tiwary](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.0371). In particular the unresolved unrestricted small cases in [Engel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11950) are not decided here.
