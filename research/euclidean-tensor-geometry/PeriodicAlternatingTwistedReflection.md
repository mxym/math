# Twisted reflection forces rank loss in alternating periodic trace tensors

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Let \(s\ge2\) be even and \(q\ge2\). In the one-site shared-tensor model of [Gesmundo–Landsberg–Walter, §II](https://arxiv.org/html/1801.09106#S2), the alternating flattening \(M\) of a cyclic tensor on \(2s\) sites of physical dimension \(q\) has both dimensions \(q^s\). Assume additionally the tensor is invariant under reversal about two row sites combined with the same onsite involution \(U\) at every site. Put \(d=\operatorname{tr}U\), necessarily an integer in characteristic zero. Then
\[
\operatorname{rank} M\le q^s-\frac{q^{s/2-1}}2|d^2-q|.
\]
This value is attained as an abstract cyclic, twisted-reflection-invariant tensor, without a restriction on its bond dimension. It does not assert attainment for prescribed local slices or bond dimension. In ordinary reflection, \(d=q\), the defect is at least \(q^{s/2}(q-1)/2\). In particular a symmetric rank-one overlap matrix, including \(I+t(J-I)\), cannot reach the usual parity target for nonsquare equal dimensions and even half-length \(s\ge4\). A targeted search found no statement of this rank formula in GLW or Hastings; inversion symmetry of matrix product states itself is well studied.

## Proof

Choose the physical basis to diagonalize \(U\) with entries \(\pm1\); simultaneous local basis changes preserve flattening rank. Index row words by sites \(2i\), column words by sites \(2i+1\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z\). On word basis vectors let \(R\) move the first letter to last, and \(J\) reverse by coordinates \(i\mapsto-i\). Site rotation by one gives \(M^\mathsf T=MR\), hence \(MR=RM\). Reflection about site 0 acts on row words by \(J\) and on column words by \(RJ\) (their index involutions are \(i\mapsto-i\) and \(i\mapsto-i-1\)). With \(X=J U^{\otimes s}\), twisted reflection is exactly \(X M X = MR\).

Let \(E_-=\ker(R+I)\), and let \(E_+=\bigoplus_{\lambda\ne-1}\ker(R-\lambda I)\). On \(E_-\), \(X\) and \(M\) both preserve the space, \(X\) is an involution, and \(M\) anticommutes with \(X\). Thus relative to the two \(X\)-eigenspaces of dimensions \(d_+,d_-\), \(M\) is off-diagonal and has rank at most \(2\min(d_+,d_-)\). To calculate their difference, use \(P_-=(1/s)\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}(-1)^kR^k\). The index reflection in \(JR^k\) has two fixed positions when \(k\) is even, none when odd. The trace of \(U^{\otimes s}\) times a factor permutation is the product, over its cycles, of \(\operatorname{tr}(U^\ell)\) for cycle length \(\ell\). Each transposed pair contributes \(\operatorname{tr}(U^2)=q\), each fixed site \(d\). Hence
\[
d_+-d_-=\operatorname{tr}(XP_-)
=\frac12(q^{s/2-1}d^2-q^{s/2}).
\]
The deficit on \(E_-\) is at least the absolute value. This also includes the cyclic parity restriction: \(d_+-d_-\equiv \dim E_-\pmod2\).

To check sharpness for the linear symmetry constraints alone, use \(I+R^{-1}\) (invertible on \(E_+\), vanishing on \(E_-\)). It obeys both matrix symmetry equations because \(R^\mathsf T=R^{-1}\) and \(XRX=R^{-1}\). For the \(E_-\) part, the standard bilinear product is nondegenerate and makes its two \(X\)-eigenspaces orthogonal and nondegenerate, since \(R\) and \(X\) are orthogonal and invertible. In orthonormal bases for these spaces add a skew matrix with cross-block equal to a rectangular identity of rank \(\min(d_+,d_-)\), and transpose cross-block its negative. Extend by zero on the orthogonal space \(E_+\). The result satisfies the equations and realizes the upper rank. The two matrix equations encode precisely the action of the one-step cyclic rotation and the twisted reflection on alternating tensor coordinates, so no further symmetry condition is missing.

## Unconditional consequence at physical dimension one away from a square

Suppose physical dimension \(q=r^2-1\), virtual/bond dimension \(r\ge2\), still even \(s\), and slices \(A_a\in\operatorname{Mat}_r(\mathbb C)\) unrestricted. Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}M\le q^s-(r-1)q^{s/2-1}
\]
for every shared tensor. To see it generically, the independent slices span a hyperplane \(W\). Under the nondegenerate pairing \((C,A)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(CA)\), \(W\) is annihilated by one matrix \(C\) up to scalar. For a dense open set of such hyperplanes, \(C\) has distinct eigenvalues. Simultaneously conjugate the slices to make \(C\) diagonal; cyclic traces are unchanged. Then \(W\) is stable under transpose, since \(\operatorname{tr}(CA^\mathsf T)=\operatorname{tr}(CA)\) for a diagonal \(C\). Transpose on all of \(\operatorname{Mat}_r\) has trace \(r\), and its quotient action on \(\operatorname{Mat}_r/W\) is identity (the defining functional is transpose invariant). Its restriction to \(W\) thus yields an onsite involution of trace \(r-1\). Taking transpose of every slice reverses the order of a trace word, giving the required twisted reflection. Substitution uses \((r-1)^2-(r^2-1)=-2(r-1)\). The rank bound extends from the dense open locus to every tuple of slices since the minors are polynomial. No analogous density premise is claimed for codimension greater than one. This is a strict gap from the min-cut \(q^s\): each of the \(s\) source terminals can send half of its log-capacity \(\log q\) to each adjacent sink along distinct virtual edges, since \(\tfrac12\log q<\log r\), and the cut through all source terminals attains \(s\log q\).

For \(r=2\), physical dimension \(3\), this yields \(3^s-3^{s/2-1}\) as a universal upper bound. It is strictly below the bare odd-size rotation ceiling \(3^s-1\) for \(s\ge4\). In contrast the standard square-dimension witness with every matrix unit has onsite transpose trace \(r=\sqrt q\), causing no imbalance, in agreement with [GLW, Remark I.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1801.09106#S1.SS4).

## Implications for overlapping-matrix constructions, and checks

For rank-one slices \(A_i=u_i v_i^\mathsf T\) the trace tensor is the product of consecutive directed overlaps \(K_{ij}=v_i^\mathsf T u_j\). Symmetric \(K\) therefore forces ordinary reflection symmetry. The same holds if \(K\) is diagonally similar to a symmetric matrix, since rescaling overlap entries by a diagonal similarity cancels along any closed walk. For \(q=3\) it suffices to break the unique directed triangle-circulation gauge (for nonzero overlaps), e.g. changing \(K_{02}\) without changing \(K_{20}\). This destroys the ordinary-reflection obstruction, not by itself a rank lower bound.

Exact modular tests over \(\mathbb F_{241}\) on the symmetric matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}2&1&7\\1&3&11\\7&11&13\end{pmatrix}\) gave full rank in every rotation block except \(R=-1\) for \(s=2,4,6,8\). The dimensions/ranks in that block were 3/0, 21/12, 119/92, 831/750 respectively, all matching the symmetric upper bound. The same witness with only \(K_{02}\) changed from 7 to 5 had full other blocks and skew ranks 2,20,118,830 respectively, attaining the unrestricted parity bound in these finite cases. Thus ordinary reversal is already the exact deficit for these symmetric samples, and a single chiral edge demonstrably repairs the excess in them. This is not a proof of either generic exact rank for unbounded \(s\), nor a proof for equal dimension \(q>3\).

For physical \(3\), bond \(2\), the generic hyperplane is represented by \(A_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,t)\), \(A_+=E_{10}\), \(A_-=E_{01}\) after conjugation and local basis choice. The alternating trace is nonzero only when the signs of the nonzero labels alternate in cyclic order, or all labels are zero, so this reduces to a one-parameter masked transfer problem. The corresponding alternating AKLT/KSZ Rényi transfer-matrix treatment [Santos, §II](https://arxiv.org/html/1408.1716#S2) is related, but does not state this maximal rank bound or settle generic lower rank for this one-parameter family. Exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) with \(t=7\) gives ranks 8, 78 and 720 for \(s=2,4,6\), matching the universal upper bound at these lengths. A nonzero minor modulo 101 also certifies the same lower bound over \(\mathbb C\). The generic lower rank for unbounded \(s\) is not established.
