# Size 85 in F_17^3: degree-eight moments vanish

Use [FugledeF17Size85MomentSeven.md](FugledeF17Size85MomentSeven.md) for the spectral caps, moment vanishings through seven, and the fact that at least thirteen uniform normals on a pencil force a layer. Use [FugledeF17Size85OcticLayers.md](FugledeF17Size85OcticLayers.md) for the unconditional exclusion of layers and resulting affine plane cap sixteen, and [FugledeF17Size85DegreeEightGeometry.md](FugledeF17Size85DegreeEightGeometry.md) for four-blocking of U_B whenever a nonzero degree-eight form vanishes on it. This note does not exclude size85 outright.

For B let w_B(v)=(Σ_t binom(n_t(v),2)−170)/17, supported exactly at the nonuniform directions V_B. Equivalently w_B(v)=(Σ n_t²−425)/34. Projective polarity gives T_B=P w_B−30·1 and Σ_v w_B(v)=710. For d in V of the partner, T_B(d)=0 by spectrality; hence that vertex's neighbor pencil has total weight30, called tight. These formulas do not require individual weights to be integers. For an unordered pair of difference direction \(\delta\), there are 18 projective normals perpendicular to both \(d\) and \(\delta\) if \(d=\delta\), and one otherwise. Hence the sum of the 18 occupancy pair counts in \(d^\perp\) is \(3570+17T_B(d)\). Substituting the definition of the weight gives the polarity equation; summing it over all 307 directions gives total weight 710. Every tight pencil has at least six nonuniform profiles: with at most five it would have at least thirteen uniform normals and force a layer, already ruled out.

Moment vanishings through seven give reduced plane-profile degree at most eight, since occupancy residues are orthogonal to evaluated powers 0..7. If B has genuine degree R as its highest such profile degree, put d=16−R. The degree-d moment form F of B is nonzero, with nonzero evaluations exactly at genuine degree-R directions: their top coefficient is read off by multiplication by t^d and summing, using the field power sums. An opposite tight pencil with no degree-R coefficient lies wholly in the degree-d zero locus on its projective line, so is a rational factor if d<18. Consequently there are at most d exceptional pencils. Here R≥4: as in the degree-seven note no affine or quadratic profiles can occur, and if all genuine profiles were cubic, their verified minimum weight six and tight degree≥6 would exceed total30. Write τ_B for the weighted mass of the exceptional opposite vertices. On each nonexceptional tight pencil the top homogeneous Radon congruence requires at least R+2 genuine degree-R coefficients, as any R+1 distinct projective Rth powers are linearly independent. Therefore the weighted degree identity gives
```
30|V_B| ≥ (R+2)·710 − (R−4)τ_B .
```
The correction allows every exception to have the baseline degree six. In particular, if the maximum ordinary weight on the opposite side is M, then τ_B≤(16−R)M.

For all members any positive profile has cap16, and its nonconstant residue of degree≤8 has at most eight zeros, so there are at least nine positive occupancies. The maximum possible square sum with cap16, mass85, and at least nine positive integer entries is1287 (occupancies 16,16,16,16,16,2,1,1,1). This follows by greedily concentrating mass subject to the cap and leaving one in the required other positive entries, which maximizes every prefix of the descending list. Thus the positive weights are always at most 431/17.

If the highest profile degree of a member is seven, it has integer weights by the variance square identity (degree≤14<16, hence variance sum a multiple of 17, and also even). It has at least ten positive occupancies. The same greedy square maximum is1285, uniquely at 16^5,1^5; every other occupancy tuple has square sum at most1257. Indeed with exactly ten positive and at most four 16's, the largest alternative is 16^4,15,2,1^4, and with at least eleven positive the maximum is1255. So the intermediate weight25 would require square sum1275, impossible. Hence such a member has weights at most24. For highest degrees six, five, and four, the exact centered profile catalogue in [verify-fuglede-f17-size85-moment-eight.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size85-moment-eight.js) gives maxima19,17,15, respectively. It enumerates each degree, kills the penultimate coefficient by translation, and lists all remaining coefficients and integer residues of mass85. There are no lifts because the spatial plane cap is sixteen. For each fixed nonconstant polynomial part, one can enumerate constants by shifting its residue histogram instead of re-evaluating all field values.

Suppose one member B has a genuine octic. Let N_B=|U_B|. The weighted degree inequality above with R=8 and M the opposite weight cap gives N_B≤307−ceil((10·710−4·8M)/30). By the geometric degree-eight reduction U_B is four-blocking. Also every line meets it in at most twelve sites (otherwise there would be a layer). As the degree-eight moment form is nonzero, at most eight distinct rational lines are factors; every nonfactor has ≤8 zeros. In the incidence sum
```
Σ_L (n_L−5)(n_L−8)=N_B²−217N_B+12280
```
the positive terms are the term 4 for a four-secant, or terms at most 28 on factors with n_L>8. Thus the number of four-secants is at least (N_B²−217N_B+12280−224)/4. Every four-secant corresponds by polarity to a uniform normal of the partner: every parallel affine slice of the partner with that direction plane has at most five sites (line cap5 and ≤4 directions). The noncollinear six-site case would automatically have line cap3, since four sites on a line plus an exterior one give at least five directions; the cap-three four-direction census in [verify-fuglede-f17-size153-cross.js](verify-fuglede-f17-size153-cross.js) rules it out. Average occupancy five therefore makes all slices uniform. Distinct four-secants have distinct polar normals, so they are at most |U_partner|.

The preceding upper bound on N_B is at most97 even using M=431/17, and the incidence quadratic decreases in this entire range. For each opposite maximum degree R', the following exact table uses its weight cap M to upper-bound N_B, uses the universal octic-side weight cap 431/17 and the same weighted degree computation to upper-bound N_partner, and then lower-bounds the number of four-secants:
```
R'     N_B upper   N_partner upper   four-secants lower
8      97         97                104
7      95         116               117
6      90         134               157
5      88         150               176
4      86         165               198
```
Every last-column entry exceeds the middle-column upper bound for the partner uniform-normal set. This contradiction excludes any octic, whether both members had one or just one did. Therefore all occupancy residues have degree≤7, which is equivalent to vanishing mixed moments through degree eight. The size85 exclusion still requires an endgame for profiles of degrees up to seven.
