# Thirteen arbitrary omissions in E₇ and the seven-dimensional kissing bound

**Theorem.** A seven-dimensional kissing configuration containing at least **113 of the 126 points** of a fixed E₇ root system has at most **126** points. There is no hypothesis that all 63 root lines be represented. In the full-line branch, a hypothetical 127-point configuration has at most **49** true antipodal pairs (previously 50). This does not resolve the unrestricted seven-dimensional kissing number.

Normalize to squared norm two. A signed root r blocks an outsider x if r·x>1. The [six-blocker theorem](KissingExceptionalRootCoverBranch.md) says every nonroot sphere point has at least six blockers. The minimum six-blocker case consists of exactly 4,032 points with distinct masks, all conjugate under the Weyl group. [The earlier boundary exclusion](KissingExceptionalBoundaryPairs.md) rules out a 127-point full-line code with 51 or more antipodal pairs.

## I. A root axis with both signs missing

Suppose a 127-point counterexample exists with m≤13 omitted roots and choose one maximizing the number of roots retained relative to this fixed E₇. A larger putative code can first be reduced to 127 points keeping all retained roots. Every missing root is blocked by at least two outsiders; otherwise restoring it and removing at most one outsider increases root overlap without losing cardinality.

For a pole r in an outsider mask B, define its charge as 2a+o, where a is the number of other members with r-inner-product 1 and o the number with inner-product 0. No other angle is possible: two blockers of the same outsider lie in an open 60° cap. The charge is thus |B|−1+a. Use the same definition for a union of two masks containing r; all roots in that union are still nonobtuse to r.

The [standalone exact verifier](verify-kissing-e7-thirteen-arbitrary-omissions.js) checks these small-charge facts:

* Every outsider mask at a marked member has charge at least 10. Charge 10 is possible only in the six-mask: five acute neighbors and no orthogonal members.
* Charge 11 is possible only in a single rooted eight-mask orbit, with four acute and three orthogonal members. The marked root is the unique member of this mask with that charge. There is just one seven-mask dominant chamber type; its seven roots have Gram matrix I+J.

For completeness, fold into the dominant chamber yᵢ=x·αᵢ≥0; actual blockers form a coefficientwise upper ideal of positive roots. In sizes 0 through 12 generate all upper ideals by adjoining each maximal remaining root, take the closed chamber polytope and solve all active linear bases by exact determinants. Eliminate an ideal if the maximum squared norm on its vertices is below two; at equality strict convexity allows only vertices, so check their actual strict masks and exclude root points. Larger ideals need no test for charge ≤11 since charge≥|B|−1. All remaining small-charge cases and their marked roots are checked directly. Taking a superset of feasible masks would only weaken the test.

Now suppose both ±r are missing. On each side take two distinct outsiders blocking that sign, and let their union charges be w₊,w₋. Even a union of charge 10 is impossible: it would give the same six-mask for both points, but a six-mask identifies its unique outsider. Since the acute neighbor pools are disjoint and both signs themselves are missing, w₊+w₋≤2(m−2). This forces m=13 and w₊=w₋=11. Two distinct six-masks cannot yield 11, since all their other roots are acute and the union then has even charge ≥12. A six-mask and the exceptional eight-mask would need at least five acute and three orthogonal roots, giving charge ≥13. Thus on either side the two outsiders' masks must be the *same exceptional eight-mask*. No assumption on the capacity of that mask is necessary.

Equality in the total charge bound forces the three orthogonal members on the two sides to coincide. Reflecting the exceptional marked pattern enumerates 20,160 translates. With one fixed, there is exactly **one** opposite marked translate with the same orthogonal triple. For their resulting 13-root union, the verifier finds a D₆ section (roots perpendicular to a root of E₇) missing only two of its 60 roots. The [58-of-60 D₆ equator theorem](KissingPartialD5D6Equators.md) therefore rules it out.

## II. Every original root axis represented

Only m=13 remains by the earlier bound. There are 50 paired axes, 13 single root axes and 14 outsiders. No outsider can have its antipode present: its ≥6 blockers have opposite signs represented among the original points. Join two outsiders by a negative edge when their inner product is below −1. This graph is triangle-free by the squared norm of a sum of three points. It must have two vertex-disjoint edges. Otherwise it is an empty graph or a star up to isolated vertices, and at least 13 outsiders are independent. Their lines and the 50 paired lines give 63 projective kissing lines, hence E₇ by [Anstreicher's uniqueness theorem](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/). It would share at least 50 axes with the original E₇ without being that system. Their common roots form a proper simply-laced reflection subsystem of rank at most seven; every such ADE system has at most 84 roots. This contradiction proves the matching claim.

Blocker masks of negative neighbors are disjoint: two outsiders both in the same open 60° cap cannot be more than 120° apart. Thus both endpoints have six or seven blockers and each edge has at least one six-endpoint. Fix such a six-point by Weyl symmetry. Exact dot products test the other six-points. For a seven-mask S={sᵢ}, its full-rank Gram matrix I+J gives
`x·y = Σᵢ (x·sᵢ)(y·sᵢ) − (Σᵢx·sᵢ)(Σᵢy·sᵢ)/8.`
For a fixed six-point x, a lower bound on this expression over all y of mask S follows from the eight exact vertices of the closed seven-mask chamber cell and all permutations of their seven coordinates. Every realized chamber is covered by a permutation; opposite ordering of the two lists minimizes their dot by rearrangement. Checking whether this lower bound is strictly below −1 conservatively includes every genuine negative edge.

There are 576 seven-masks. Relative to the fixed six-endpoint and with all original axes represented, 20 six-partners and at most 20 seven-masks pass the negative test. A six/seven pair already specifies all 13 omissions; a six/six pair specifies 12, so append every remaining signed root not on an already used axis. This yields **2,040** candidate signed deletion sets. In each, enumerate all mask endpoints contained in it and their possible negative edges. Include **two slots per seven-mask** so that a prospective matching is not lost even if the mask could hold multiple outsiders (six-masks have capacity one). None of the 2,040 graphs contains two disjoint edges. This closes the full-line branch and the theorem.
