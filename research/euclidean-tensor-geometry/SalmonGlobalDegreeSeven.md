# Global exclusion of missing Salmon generators through degree seven

Use \(X=\widehat{\sigma_4}((\mathbb P^3)^3)\), \(I=I(X)\), and \(J=\langle M_5,M_6,M_9\rangle\) with all three factor permutations of the known modules included. Then \(I_d=J_d\) for \(d\le7\). More generally in any degree, no missing isotypic type has partition length at most three in at least two modes. This is not a proof of global generation.

## Compression rules out two short partitions in every degree

[Oeding, arXiv:1603.08980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.08980), Theorem 3.1, computes the prime ideal of the \(3\times3\times4\) fourth secant: it is generated by the 10 Landsberg–Manivel sextics and 20 inherited Strassen nonics. A highest-weight polynomial in type \((\lambda,\mu,\nu)\) with \(\ell(\lambda),\ell(\mu)\le3\) uses only the first three coordinates in each of those modes, by its torus weight. Its vanishing on the ambient secant implies vanishing on the compressed secant. Its expression in the compressed generators lifts as a polynomial identity, and those generators are inherited members of \(J\). Applying the whole linear group includes every copy of that irreducible module. Thus a potentially missing type has at least two partitions of length four. This elementary reduction is the inheritance argument used here, not a priority claim.

## Exact Specht multiplicity computation

Let \(S_\lambda\) denote the Specht module of the symmetric group \(S_d\), indexed by a partition of \(d\). In the Schur–Weyl decomposition of degree-\(d\) tensor polynomials, the multiplicity space of type \((\lambda,\mu,\nu)\) is
\[
 U=(S_\lambda\otimes S_\mu\otimes S_\nu)^{\Delta S_d}.
\]
For an ordered tuple of positive integers \(\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4)\) summing to \(d\), let \(H_\alpha=S_{\alpha_1}\times\cdots\times S_{\alpha_4}\) act on four contiguous blocks of tensor slots. Evaluating the polarized polynomial on \(\alpha_i\) copies of the \(i\)-th simple tensor, for all four simple tensors, is represented on \(U\) by independently averaging over \(H_\alpha\) in the A, B and C modes. To prove this, identify a homogeneous degree-\(d\) polynomial with its diagonal-permutation invariant tensor of coefficients. Assign a separate rank-one summand to each block. Coefficients of the resulting polynomial are obtained by symmetrizing the \(\alpha_i\) vector slots within each block, independently in each mode. This is exactly the three independent averages. The same Schur representation factors are untouched.

Thus \(I_d\) has multiplicity space the joint kernel of these averaging maps on \(U\). It suffices to check sorted \(\alpha\), since simultaneous block relabeling leaves \(U\) invariant. Empty blocks need not be included: for \(d\ge4\), refine any multiset with fewer than four nonempty blocks into four nonempty blocks, then specialize the newly split simple tensor arguments to be equal. The same scheme is checked at every degree from five to seven. In particular \(I_5\) comes from the averaging pattern \((2,1,1,1)\), and \(I_6\) from \((3,1,1,1)\) and \((2,2,1,1)\). Degrees at most four have no equations by polarization with each simple tensor separate and spanning its ambient tensor space.

Multiplication by a linear factor in any given Specht multiplicity channel has the following exact implementation. Remove the last tableau box from each of \(\lambda,\mu,\nu\), giving the three predecessor partitions; embed their Specht modules by appending that same final box in each tableau. Then project the tensor product onto diagonal \(S_d\)-invariants by averaging. This is the multiplication map up to a nonzero scalar on the corresponding one-box Pieri branches. Indeed Schur–Weyl branching is restriction from \(S_d\) to the subgroup fixing the final slot, and multiplication appends that slot followed by full symmetrization. Nonzero scalar choices do not affect the image span. [Bates–Oeding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.6181), Theorem 3.1, identifies the degree-five generator types as the permutations of \((2111,2111,311)\), one copy per type; the sextic type is \((222,222,3111)\) and permutations (Proposition 2.1 and inheritance).

## Why the modular calculation is an exact certificate

The verifier uses rational seminormal Specht matrices, whose adjacent-transposition coefficients are \(1/r\) on the tableau and \(1+1/r\) on its adjacent swap if standard, where \(r\) is the difference of the two contents (column minus row). All denominators and the averaging factorials are units at the chosen prime. The exact dimension of \(U\) is the usual character inner product; traces computed modulo the prime recover their integral values uniquely because the absolute value is at most the relevant Specht dimension and the prime is larger than twice that dimension in the degrees used. The rank of the joint averaging map modulo the prime is a lower bound on its rational rank, hence gives an upper bound on the ideal multiplicity. The invariant projectors split the ambient localized integer lattices, so reduction of their images spans the corresponding modular invariant spaces; the verifier checks the computed invariant basis has the exact character dimension. Each known seed line is independently known to exist over \(\mathbb Q\) and is found as a modular kernel of dimension one. Its saturation in the localized integer lattice reduces to the whole detected line. Images of its reductions under the branching and averaging maps therefore give lower bounds on the rational generator-span multiplicities.

The companion [verify-salmon-global-degree-seven.js](verify-salmon-global-degree-seven.js) uses prime 257 and exact arithmetic; its asserted output records degree, the number of unordered types with at least two full partition lengths, the sum of ideal multiplicity upper bounds, and numbers of mismatches. These are respectively `(5,6,1,0)`, `(6,25,9,0)` and `(7,58,48,0)`. The nine nonzero degree-six blocks with at least two length-four partitions each have multiplicity one; in degree seven the algorithm recursively multiplies the modular span generated in lower degrees, not the full detected modular kernel. Every multiplication lower bound matches its kernel upper bound. The two-short types are handled by the Oeding argument, including the independent sextic permutations. As a dimension check the degree-six ideal has dimension 109792, of which 3000 are the three independent sextic modules. The computation treats the full permutation-invariant collection of known modules, not one orientation of the sextics only.
