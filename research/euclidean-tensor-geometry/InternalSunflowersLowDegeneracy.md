# Sharp mixed-fiber extraction with one bounded coordinate

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

This addresses a restricted domain of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

Let H be a finite simple A–B–C-partite triple system with E edges, m ≥ 2, and Δ(A) ≤ L; put M = max(L,m). A set of selected edges is internally witnessed if every intersection of two distinct selected edges (optionally excluding the empty set) is the kernel of an m-petal sunflower in the selected set. With no injectivity assumptions and no restrictions on pair codegrees, there is a nonempty-kernel extraction of size Ω(E/(mM)) and a full-kernel extraction of size Ω(E/(m²M)). For L = m, both orders are sharp even under AB injectivity.

## Weighted graph lemma
Any weighted simple bipartite graph has a subgraph of weight Ω(1/m) of the input whose nonisolated degrees are 1 or ≥ m. Peel vertices of degree below m until no longer possible, orienting removed edges from the endpoint peeled first. The unremoved graph qualifies. Split removed edges by the side containing their tails; each of the two directed bipartite pieces then has tail degree < m. For each piece, split heads according to degree < 8m versus ≥ 8m. In the low piece, choosing edges independently at probability 1/(32m) and deleting edges meeting any other chosen edge gives a matching, each edge surviving with probability Ω(1/m). In the high piece, sample heads independently at probability p = 1/(64m), and discard all sampled edges at every tail meeting more than one sampled head. Conditional on a particular head's selection and for every incident edge, its loss probability is ≤ (m−1)p. Thus the expected fraction lost from any such head is ≤ (m−1)p. By Markov, the conditional probability its residual degree is less than m is at most 2(m−1)p (its initial degree was ≥ 8m). Delete these bad heads. Every edge now survives with probability Ω(1/m), and the result is a union of vertex-disjoint stars of degree ≥ m. Choose the best of the core and the four pieces. This is the weighted nonempty-kernel extraction lemma for graphs used in prior heavy-fiber work.

## Mixed-fiber lower bound
Write f(b,c) for BC-codegree. By choosing the better of the outputs below it suffices to operate on light or heavy edges separately.

Light: retain pairs with f(b,c)<2m. Form the incidence graph of A against BC pairs, one incidence per triple. Its max degree is ≤ 2M, so a greedy matching (remove incident edges after each choice) retains Ω(E_light/M). Now A labels and BC pairs are unique. Apply the weighted graph lemma (unit weights) to the graph of surviving BC pairs, keeping Ω(E_light/(mM)). Every nonempty intersection is a singleton of B or C and is certified by its graph degree.

Heavy: retain pairs with f(b,c)≥2m. Independently activate each such pair with probability p=1/(64M). Keep only triples whose A label appears on exactly one active pair. Conditional on an edge's pair being active, its disappearance probability is at most (L−1)p by a union bound. Hence conditional expected fraction lost per active fiber is at most (L−1)p. Discard whole fibers with fewer than m surviving triples; the conditional probability of this is at most 2(L−1)p by Markov. For each edge the combined conditional loss probability is at most 3(L−1)p. There is therefore an outcome with Ω(E_heavy/M) triples, globally unique A labels and all surviving fiber sizes ≥ m. Apply the weighted graph lemma on projected BC pairs, weighting them by their surviving fiber sizes, and keep the whole fibers corresponding to its output. Repeated BC pairs are certified by their fiber, singleton B or C intersections by picking one edge from each of m distinct adjacent pairs at the corresponding endpoint, and globally unique A labels prevent all other nonempty intersections. The result has Ω(E_heavy/(mM)) triples.

## Full-kernel lower bound
For an unweighted bipartite graph of N edges there is a full-kernel extraction of size Ω(N/m²). If Δ ≥ N/(4m), take a max-degree star when Δ≥m, otherwise take a single edge (N<4m²). If Δ<N/(4m), greedily reserve an m-edge matching, removing its endpoints at cost at most 2mΔ<N/2; use the graph lemma on the residual, then add back the matching. It certifies the empty kernel; no other new intersections arise.

For H, if E≤8mM, one triple suffices. Otherwise if some B or C vertex has degree at least E/(8m), take the full graph extraction of its link and lift back at that vertex. An empty kernel in the link now lifts to the singleton consisting of the center vertex. If not, greedily reserve m disjoint triples; removing all their endpoints costs at most m(2E/(8m)+L)<3E/8, so this greedy process succeeds and retains more than half of H. Apply the nonempty-kernel theorem on the residual, then add back the reserved matching to certify the empty intersection.

## Sharp low-degree example
For m≥3, take |B|=|C|=m−1, assign m−1 A labels privately to each c, and include each triple abc with a assigned to c and b arbitrary. There are (m−1)^3 triples, private degree m−1, and AB projection injective. Pair codegrees are all <m. No singleton has an m-matching in its link: for A this is because each A label is assigned to one C; for B and C use that the opposite public part has fewer than m vertices. Hence all nonempty-kernel outputs are matchings of size at most m−1, and all full-kernel outputs have at most 1 triple because the host has no m-matching. This bounds the relative sizes by O(m^-2) and O(m^-3) respectively, including under the single AB-injective hypothesis.

Scope: refinements with better rates for capped and superheavy special cases remain stronger in those regimes. This resolves the arbitrary fiber-size mixture only when there is a low-degree coordinate; it does not prove the unrestricted problem or the unrestricted AB-injective version.

## Arbitrary \(3\)-uniform \(m\)-degenerate families

The sharp rates \(\Theta(m^{-2})\) for nonempty kernels and \(\Theta(m^{-3})\) for all kernels hold for every simple \(3\)-uniform family with vertex degeneracy at most \(m\): every nonempty subhypergraph has an incident vertex of degree at most \(m\). Randomly color vertices in three parts and keep transversal triples, retaining at least \(2/9\) in some outcome. The transversal subfamily inherits the degeneracy bound. Peel vertices of current degree at most \(m\), recording each triple at the first of its three vertices peeled. Split by the part containing this owner vertex, and take the largest of these three pieces. Its maximum degree in the owner part is at most \(m\); apply the preceding theorem. The sharp example above is itself \(m\)-degenerate, since one can always remove the \(A\) labels first.
