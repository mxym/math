# Local Salmon ideal at a generic duplicated-frame compression

Let \(I\) be the fourth Segre secant ideal in \(4\times4\times4\) and \(J=J_{5,6,9}\) the known homogeneous ideal. Put \(u=b_0+b_1+b_2\), with all the \(a_i,b_i,c_i\) coordinate vectors of their respective four-dimensional spaces. At
\[
 T=a_0b_0c_0+a_0b_1c_1+a_1b_2c_2+a_2u c_3
\]
(where products denote exterior tensor products), \(J_T=I_T\). In fact the quintics and sextics already suffice there, and the tangent cone is a smooth affine factor of dimension 35 times the rank-one \(3\times3\) determinantal cone. This covers the generic \((3,3,4)\) semisimple compression with one repeated projective factor and the other frame generic, and its permutations. It is not a claim of global equality.

## The rank-two cluster and its nine normal coordinates

Write \(T=C+P+Q\) where \(C=a_0(b_0c_0+b_1c_1)\), \(P=a_1b_2c_2\) and \(Q=a_2u c_3\). Around \(C\) a smooth ambient chart for the rank-two cluster has dimension 24: choose the two B- and C-support planes (four parameters each) and an arbitrary \(4\times2\times2\) core (16 parameters). In this chart the border-rank-two constraint is exactly A-flattening rank at most two. Indeed any such core is supported in a \(2\times2\times2\) format and the second secant fills that format, as is seen on the dense open where one slice is invertible and its normalized companion has distinct eigenvalues. This is also a determinantal model scheme-theoretically for the reduced cluster locus: after imposing the support charts, the A-flattening minors cut out the integral matrix rank variety. Near \(C\)'s rank-one A-flattening, the transverse form is rank at most one in a \(3\times3\) Schur complement. The A-rank-one cluster locus is smooth of dimension \(7+8=15\).

Add to this cluster two variable nonzero simple tensors near \(P,Q\), giving a smooth source ambient chart of dimension \(24+10+10=44\). Its addition map has injective differential at the displayed \(T\). Here is a direct check. Let \(B_*=\langle b_0,b_1\rangle\) and \(C_*=\langle c_0,c_1\rangle\). In a relation among cluster and singleton tangent summands, inspect the \(c_2\)-slice modulo \(\langle a_0\rangle\) in A, which kills the cluster outside-support contribution. The remaining terms have form
\[
 \overline{p}\otimes b_2+\overline{a_1}\otimes q+z\,\overline{a_2}\otimes u.
\]
Since \(u,b_2\) are independent, the \(\overline a_2\)-coefficient forces its part of \(p\) and the scalar \(z\) to vanish. The \(\overline a_3\)-coefficient similarly vanishes, and the \(\overline a_1\)-coefficient cancels in the tangent of the same singleton. Thus the unprojected \(c_2\)-slice from singletons is a multiple of \(a_0\otimes b_2\); it cannot cancel with a cluster term in \(a_0\otimes B_*\) unless zero. The \(c_3\)-slice is identical with \(P,Q\) interchanged. Remaining singleton tangents in \(C_*\) are multiples of \(a_1b_2,a_2u\) in each slice; modulo \(\langle a_0\rangle\) in A and \(B_*\) in B they are independent, whereas the cluster tangent vanishes there. This proves the differential injective. The rank remains 44 in a neighborhood.

The source subset where the cluster A-rank is one and the other tensors remain simple has dimension \(15+20=35\) and is smooth. Its local analytic image \(Z\) is smoothly embedded, by injectivity of the differential, and lies in \(X\). Along \(Z\), the entire 44-dimensional ambient source tangent maps into the true tangent of \(X\), since the derivative of the rank-at-most-two condition vanishes at A-rank one, and scheme-theoretic addition sends the determinantal cluster to \(X\). The normal coordinates to \(Z\) in this tangent space are the nine coefficients in
\[
 \langle a_1,a_2,a_3\rangle\otimes
 \langle b_0c_1,b_1c_0,b_1c_1\rangle .
\]
Indeed the omitted \(b_0c_0\) pivot, with \(b_0c_0+b_1c_1\) as the base rank-one row, gives these as a complement to the rank-one A-flattening tangent. In particular any rank-one \(3\times3\) normal array \(N\) gives the actual path \(T+tN\in X\): the perturbed cluster still has A-flattening rank at most two, and \(P,Q\) are unchanged.

## Exact quadratic scheme comparison

The verifier first checks 20 independent known gradients at \(T\). Their local zero locus \(Y\) is smooth of dimension 44 by the Jacobian criterion, and \(T_X=T_Y\) on \(Z\) near \(T\) by the preceding 44-dimensional lower bound. Thus each known polynomial in \(J\), restricted to \(Y\), vanishes along \(Z\) with all first derivatives there zero. In local analytic coordinates adapted to the smooth submanifold \(Z\subset Y\), its Taylor expansion is in the square of the transverse ideal. No mixed or along-\(Z\) quadratic terms occur. These Taylor statements also hold for the completed local ring and for the tangent graded ring.

The augmented Jacobian combines each full 64-coordinate ambient gradient with the 45 quadratic Taylor coefficients on the nine normal coordinates just listed. The exact program checks augmented rank 29, versus full ambient gradient rank 20 (upper bounded by the true tangent). Consequently at least nine independent quadratic forms come from rational linear combinations of the tested equations whose full ambient first derivatives vanish. For such combinations their restricted Hessians in \(Y\) coincide with the ambient Hessians on \(T_Y\); the conormal argument shows these are pure transverse quadrics. Each vanishes on the normal rank-one matrices by the actual paths \(T+tN\). The degree-two ideal of the rank-one \(3\times3\) matrix cone is exactly the span of its nine \(2\times2\) minors. Thus all nine minors are in the initial ideal.

After the 20 linear eliminations the tangent graded ring of the known-equation scheme is a quotient of the integral rank-one \(3\times3\) matrix ring tensored with 35 polynomial variables. This domain has dimension \(5+35=40\), and so does the tangent graded ring of the known equations because its local scheme contains the dimension-40 secant and the 40-dimensional graded domain surjects onto it. A nonzero ideal of a finitely generated domain strictly lowers dimension, so the quotient is the domain itself. A local ring with integral tangent graded ring is integral by Krull intersection. Since \(J\subset I\) and the prime secant has that same dimension, \(J_T=I_T\).
