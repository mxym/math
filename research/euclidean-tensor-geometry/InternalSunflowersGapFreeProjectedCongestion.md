# Gap-free rank-four internal extraction with one arbitrary pair projection and two bounded vertex sides

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

For a finite simple four-partite four-uniform family \(H\) on sides \(A,B,C,D\), let \(E=|H|\) and \(m\ge2\). Suppose every pair fiber of types \(AC,AD,BC,BD\) has size at most \(Lm\), and every vertex on the \(C,D\) sides has degree at most \(Lm^2\). No restriction on \(AB\) fibers, A/B singleton degrees, or relative support. Then \(H\) contains a subfamily of size \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\) whose every realized intersection is the kernel of an internal \(m\)-petal sunflower. In particular \(CD\) needs no linear cap; its automatic quadratic cap suffices. This is a restricted, gap-free case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

## Reduction to a host with rich AB fibers

First seek the nonempty-kernel property. Split \(H\) into its \(AB\) fibers of size at least \(C_*m^2\), and all shorter fibers, for \(C_*\) sufficiently large in terms of \(L\) and fixed constants below. If the shorter fibers have at least half the mass, all pair caps in that subhost are \(O_L(m^2)\), so the known [quadratic pair cap theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/51dd2a2c058094029d98937178b56fc933cc65ed/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/InternalSunflowersSharpLinearFourQuadraticCaps.md) already applies. Otherwise keep the long \(AB\) fibers, with edge count \(E_h\ge E/2\).

Sample edges independently at rate \(\alpha/m\), where \(\alpha>0\) depends only on \(L\), and discard each selected edge sharing a cross pair \(AC,AD,BC,BD\) with another selected edge. Conditional on its activation, the chance of such a conflict is at most \(4L\alpha\). Additionally discard selected edges using a C or D vertex of degree above \(\beta m\) in the *initial* sample. Conditional on activation of the edge, its C/D degrees have expectation at most \(1+\alpha Lm\); Markov bounds each failure probability by \((1/m+\alpha L)/\beta\). Thus fixed \(\beta\ge16\) and small enough \(\alpha\) yield a subhost of at least \(\alpha E_h/(2m)\) edges with all cross projections injective and positive C/D degrees at most \(\beta m\).

Throughout the next two possible restrictions, also remove occupied \(AB\) fibers below \(Qm\), where \(Q\) is a large constant. The total cost over all stages is at most \(QE_h/(C_*m)\), because each original \(AB\) fiber is removed at most once. This permits \(C_*\) chosen large after \(Q,\alpha\) to absorb even the constant-factor losses from the restrictions below. Cleaned surviving \(AB\) fibers are matchings on \(C,D\): equality on either free coordinate would be an \(AC\) or \(AD\) conflict.

## Weighted support dichotomy

On each A or B label, regard its incident edges as weighted buckets indexed by its distinct labels in the opposite side, the weights being current \(AB\) fiber sizes. Initialize \(p_A=\varepsilon/m,\ p_B=\varepsilon\), where \(\varepsilon>0\) is a fixed small constant (depending on \(\beta\)). Set \(C_0\) a sufficiently large fixed constant. For an as-yet unlocked side \(V\) with other side \(U\), put \(K=\lceil C_0m/p_U\rceil\) and compute the total weight of the \(K\) heaviest buckets of each label of \(V\) (or all buckets if fewer), summed over all its labels. If this exceeds a \(1/C_0\) fraction of the current host, restrict to these top buckets for every label of \(V\), lock \(V\), set \(p_U=1/(C_0K)\), and clean the small \(AB\) fibers again. Each opposite side's rate is affected by at most one lock. If neither unlocked side triggers, stop.

At most two restrictions occur, each retaining at least a \(1/C_0\) fraction of the current host before cleaning. By the preceding global charge, choosing \(C_*\) large enough in terms of the fixed constants retains a final host of size \(M=\Omega_L(E_h/m)\), all \(AB\) fibers have size at least \(Qm\), all cross projections are injective, and C/D still have degrees at most \(\beta m\). For unlocked sides the test is at their final opposite side rate. For locked sides their respective recorded support bounds are never increased by restriction.

## Activation and pruning

If there are at most one lock, independently activate A and B labels with final rates \(p_A,p_B\), retaining an edge initially when both its labels are active. Its retention probability \(P=p_Ap_B\) obeys \(\Omega_L(m^{-2})\le P\le\varepsilon^2/m\). At a locked V label, delete its edges if at least two of its current neighbors are active. Conditional on an edge's initial retention the chance of deletion by this rule is at most \(p_U K\le1/C_0\).

If both sides lock, their recorded support caps are \(K_A=\lceil C_0m/\varepsilon\rceil\) and \(K_B=\lceil C_0m^2/\varepsilon\rceil\), since each lock changes the opposite rate and does not affect the rate used for the other lock test. Put \(K_*=\max(K_A,K_B)\). Activate *pairs* of the occupied support \(AB\) independently at probability \(P=\eta/K_*\) for a small fixed \(\eta>0\). Delete every active pair with an active competitor sharing either endpoint. Conditioned on an edge's initial retention, this deletes it with probability at most \(2\eta\) by the support caps. Thus both locked sides are collapsed to a single neighbor per surviving label. In this case too \(P=\Omega_L(m^{-2})\) and \(P\le\varepsilon/m\) by taking \(\eta\) small.

In either activation scheme, also prune all initially retained edges having a competitor at the same C or D label in the initial sample. For any fixed edge there is no competitor in its own \(AB\) fiber at that label, or one sharing even one of its A/B labels, by cross injectivity. Hence the initial-retention events of the two edges are independent in the separate-label scheme, and independent in the pair scheme as well. Conditioned on the fixed edge initially being retained, a union bound gives probability at most \(2\beta m P\le2\beta\varepsilon\) of this deletion. Choose \(\varepsilon\) so this is at most \(1/16\). Afterward C and D positive degrees are one.

Iteratively delete positive \(AB\) fibers of size below \(3m\) and positive labels in any unlocked side which have fewer than \(m\) distinct surviving opposite neighbors. Each \(AB\) fiber can only trigger if its pair was initially active, so expected cost of the first deletion type is at most \(3mP\cdot M/(Qm)=3PM/Q\).

For an unlocked \(v\in V\), only the separate-label scheme occurs. Any deletion at \(v\) loses at most the top \(m\) initial sampled bucket weights. Let \(M_v\) be its original final-host mass, and \(K=\lceil C_0m/p_U\rceil\). The contribution from its top-\(K\) buckets in expectation is \(P\) times their total mass, and across that side is at most \(PM/C_0\) by the stopping test. Each other bucket has weight at most \(M_v/K\); in particular their top \(m\) can contribute at most \(mM_v/K\), and only if \(v\) was activated. Thus their expected contribution summed over the side is at most \(p_V mM/K\le PM/C_0\). This argument only charges against the *initial* sample, hence is unaffected by any pruning dependencies. Summed over both sides the unlocked loss is at most \(4PM/C_0\).

Choosing \(C_0,Q\) large and \(\eta,\varepsilon\) small so that the sum of the displayed pruning bounds is below half the expectation \(PM\), we retain \(\Omega_L(PM)=\Omega_L(E_h/m^3)\) edges in the rich case. No intersection can involve C or D. A realized \(AB\) pair has at least \(3m\) matching petals. A locked A or B label cannot occur as a singleton intersection, because it is paired with a unique opposite label. An unlocked label has at least \(m\) opposite labels. Choose one edge for each of \(m\) distinct neighbors; C/D now have degree one, so these petals outside the label are pairwise disjoint. This establishes all nonempty witnesses.

## Empty kernel and limit of scope

Apply the nonempty result (which uniformly keeps at least \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\)) to the residue of edges avoiding a reserved \(m\)-matching's endpoints if some such matching leaves at least half the mass. All hypotheses are hereditary; add the reserved matching back to witness the empty intersection with no new nonempty kernels. Otherwise a vertex has degree at least \(E/(8m)\): either endpoints of an existing \(m\)-matching touch more than half the mass, or endpoints of a maximal matching of size less than \(m\) cover the family. Apply the [known complete rank-three extraction](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) to its link, and lift by adjoining that vertex. This preserves \(\Omega(E/m^4)\) and witnesses every realized intersection (after lifting, the empty link kernel witnesses the common vertex).

The operation that bridges the quadratic scale here is bounded-degree **independence at conflicts**: injecting cross pairs ensures that all rivals at a low C/D vertex have entirely distinct activation labels on the rich AB pair. It permits the stronger final activation needed for only two leaf locks, and coupling those locks by pair activation prevents an extra loss of \(m\). It neither treats unbounded C/D singleton degrees nor drops the linear cross-pair caps in general.

## Broader theorem: remove all cross-pair caps

In fact the full theorem remains true assuming only that each C/D vertex degree is at most \(Lm^2\), and that the two triple types \(ABC,ABD\) have multiplicity at most \(Lm\) each. Leave \(AB\) and all other projections arbitrary subject to these constraints. This strictly subsumes the theorem above, because its linear caps on the four cross pairs imply the two triple caps.

The same weak/strong \(AB\) split works: except for \(AB\), every pair contains C or D and hence has quadratic cap. In the strong side, use the same independent edge sample of rate \(\alpha/m\) and conditional Markov cutoff for C/D degrees, but remove conflicts only on \(ABC,ABD\) for the initial step. Conditional conflict probability is at most \(2L\alpha\). This ensures that no two edges in the same \(AB\) fiber coincide on C or D, which is exactly what is needed for the fiber to consist of matching petals. All later cleaning and support restrictions are identical.

The change is to start **both** nominal rates at \(p_A=p_B=\varepsilon/m\). A side's lock threshold is now always \(K_*=\lceil C_0m^2/\varepsilon\rceil\): the side's opposite rate can change only because of that very side's lock. The final product retention with zero or one lock is therefore at least \(\Omega_L(m^{-3})\), and each single label rate is still at most \(\varepsilon/m\). With two locks use the same coupled pair activation \(P=\eta/K_*=\Theta_L(m^{-2})\). The weighted top-bucket estimate, support collision/deletion cost, and rich \(AB\)-fiber loss are unchanged.

At a C or D conflict, competing edges may now share one fixed activation label on A or B, but they cannot share *both*, by the two triple injectivities. Thus in the independent scheme their conditional retention probability is at most \(\max(p_A,p_B)\le\varepsilon/m\) even if one label is shared. In the coupled-pair scheme they have distinct \(AB\) pairs and conditional retention probability \(P\le \varepsilon/m\) by the choice of constants. The same \(\le 2\beta\varepsilon\) union bound gives C and D positive degrees one. Surviving expected mass is \(\Omega_L(PM)=\Omega_L(E_h/m^4)\); the same internal witnesses and empty reserve finish the broader theorem. The earlier stronger hypothesis keeps its \(\Omega_L(E_h/m^3)\) guarantee on the strong branch.

The sharper formulation replaces full cross-projection injectivity by a weaker **one-coordinate separation**: competitors need only differ in one activated coordinate if both nominal activation rates are \(O(1/m)\). It applies even when every cross pair takes arbitrary values across the entire available quadratic window, rather than presupposing a dichotomy into uniformly thin and uniformly rich pair types.

## Separate gap-free theorem: bounded congestion of projected pair labels, with no triple caps

Let \(A,B\) be a fixed pair of coordinates in a rank-four partite family. For each vertex \(v\) in any one of the four sides, let \(d_{AB}(v)\) be the number of distinct \(AB\)-pairs occurring in edges containing \(v\). If all \(d_{AB}(v)\le Lm^2\), then the full internal extraction bound \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\) holds, with no constraints on actual edge degrees or triple multiplicities. For A or B vertices this condition is just a degree bound on the ordinary AB projected support; for C and D it bounds only distinct projected pairs, not total edges. Thus repeated edges in a common pair fiber do not count as congestion.

Independently activate each occupied AB pair at rate \(p=1/(8Lm^2)\). Initially keep every edge with activated pair. Discard each such edge \(e\) if some vertex \(v\) of \(e\) also belongs to *any original* edge of a different activated pair. Conditional on the pair of \(e\) being active, a union bound over at most \(4Lm^2\) distinct competitor pairs bounds the deletion probability by \(1/2\); hence at least \(\Omega_L(E/m^2)\) edges survive for some choice. Surviving edges in distinct AB fibers are now vertex-disjoint. In each surviving AB fiber apply the established rank-two full internal extraction lower bound \(\Omega(e/m^2)\) to its simple graph link on C,D, and lift. Within that fiber all intersections now have internal witnesses, including the intersection AB itself (empty in the link). Distinct fibers yield only empty intersections, so this proves the nonempty bound \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\). The reserve-link argument above, with all projected congestion bounds hereditary, finishes the empty kernel case.

More generally for fixed rank \(k\) and an \(a\)-coordinate projection, use each distinct projected \(a\)-tuple as the independently activated group, and suppose that each vertex lies in edges of at most \(D\) distinct such groups. The same proof keeps \(\Omega(E/(kD))\) edges in groups that do not intersect one another. Any lower guarantee \(\gamma(k-a,m)\) for complete internal extraction in rank \(k-a\) then gives the nonempty guarantee \(\Omega(\gamma(k-a,m)E/(kD))\) in the original rank. This is a projection compression for arbitrary internal multiplicities: inter-group conflicts pay in projected group count and intra-group intersections are passed to the lower-rank theorem, rather than deleting every edge sharing a coordinate. For rank four, choice \(a=3\) also gives the full \(\Omega(E/m^4)\) bound when some triple projection has congestion \(D=O(m^3)\), using the trivial rank-one bound \(\gamma=\Omega(1/m)\) (at least \(m\) singleton edges can all be kept, otherwise choose one).
