# The order-599 normalizer at Hadamard order 2396

**Theorem.** Let \(H\) be a real Hadamard matrix of order \(2396=4\cdot599\). Let \(G\) consist of pairs of signed permutation matrices satisfying \(UH=HV\). If \(P\le G\) has order 599, then it acts freely on the underlying row and column positions, is Sylow, and has conjugation image \(|N_G(P)/C_G(P)|\le2\). The kernel of the centralizer's action on the two sets of four orbits is exactly \(P\langle(-I,-I)\rangle\). Consequently \(|N_G(P)|\) is prime to 13 and 23.

**Independent sequence obstruction.** In any unbordered Goethals–Seidel complementary tuple of four sign rows of length 599, at most two rows can each have an affine multiplier with nontrivial odd-order linear part. Their multipliers and centers may differ. If two do, the only possible sorted absolute row-sum patterns are
\[
(1,3,19,45),\quad(1,9,17,45),\quad(1,21,27,35),\quad(9,25,27,31).
\]
The same bound and patterns apply separately to each block-row and block-column after the circulant reduction below. These are only necessary numerical patterns, not assertions that any complementary tuple exists.

## 1. Row-sum certificate

For \(p=599\) and sign rows \(f_i\), set \(s_i=\sum_x f_i(x)\). The complementary condition is that their summed periodic autocorrelation vanishes off zero. Summing it over all shifts gives \(\sum_i s_i^2=4p\), because the zero-shift term is \(4p\). Each \(s_i\) is odd with \(1\le |s_i|\le47\), since \(49^2>2396\).

The multiplier group \(\mathbf F_{599}^{\times}\) is cyclic of order \(598=2\cdot13\cdot23\). A power of an affine map \(x\mapsto ax+b\) whose multiplier \(a\) has nontrivial odd order gives an affine map with multiplier of prime order \(\ell\in\{13,23\}\). It has one fixed point and all other orbits have size \(\ell\). Thus an invariant row has \(s_i\equiv\pm1\pmod\ell\). Within the allowed odd range, the possible absolute sums are \(\{1,25,27\}\) for \(\ell=13\), and \(\{1,45,47\}\) for \(\ell=23\). The exact integer verifier enumerates 28 nondecreasing quadruples of positive odd roots with squared sum 2396; no quadruple has three roots in their union, and exactly the four listed have two. Equivalently, for any three roots in \(\{1,25,27,45,47\}\) (repetition allowed), their squared sum has no positive odd square complement to 2396. This also excludes every common affine multiplier of even order greater than two, by taking the appropriate odd-order power.

This cannot rule out every two-row chamber by sums alone: orbit-constant rows can each attain their listed admissible sums because their signs give precisely \(\pm1+\ell m\) with \(m\) even and \(|m|\le(p-1)/\ell\). The other rows can attain their respective sums freely. Even at the level of an orthogonal compressed integer matrix, quaternion multiplication by \(a+bi+cj+dk\) with one of the four absolute-sum patterns gives a matrix \(T\) with \(TT^{\mathsf T}=2396I_4\); this does not impose the correlations at nonzero shifts.

## 2. Free action and normalizer

Both projections of \(G\) to the signed permutation factors are injective since \(H\) is invertible. A generator \((R,S)\) of \(P\) has underlying cycles of length 1 or \(p\). Every cycle has sign product +1, as \((R,S)^p=1\) and \(p\) is odd. Diagonal sign gauges on each side remove all cycle signs. The resulting ordinary permutation matrices \(R,S\) are conjugate via \(H\), so the numbers of fixed points on both sides agree, say \(f\). There is at least one moving column cycle. Every fixed row is constant along each moving column cycle. Let \(A\) be the fixed-row/fixed-column block and \(B\) the sign matrix with \(f\) rows recording one constant per moving cycle. Then \(AA^{\mathsf T}+pBB^{\mathsf T}=4pI_f\). Therefore \(BB^{\mathsf T}\preceq4I_f\) and every column of \(B\) has squared norm at most 4. That norm is \(f\); since \(f\equiv0\pmod p\), one has \(f=0\).

Index the four cycles on each side by \(\mathbf F_p\) so that \(R,S\) shift by 1. Simultaneous invariance then gives blocks \(h_{ij}(y-x)\), which are circulant. On the fixed-vector subspaces of \(R,S\), the map \(H\) is represented in the normalized cycle-indicator bases by the \(4\times4\) matrix \(T\) with entries \(\sum_z h_{ij}(z)\). Since \(H\) identifies the subspaces and is orthogonal up to scale, \(TT^{\mathsf T}=T^{\mathsf T}T=4pI_4\). The row-sum certificate therefore applies to every row and column of \(T\).

If \(g=(U,V)\in N_G(P)\) conjugates the generator by \(m\in\mathbf F_p^{\times}\), then \(UR=R^m U\) and \(VS=S^m V\). Thus the permutations map cycles to cycles by affine coordinate maps \(x\mapsto mx+a_i\), \(y\mapsto my+b_j\) respectively. The signed coefficients are constant along each input cycle, since applying either intertwining relation to a coordinate basis vector equates the signed coefficient at \(x+1\) to that at \(x\). Suppose the order of \(m\) has an odd prime factor. On cycle labels \(g\) acts by permutations of four labels. The power \(g^{24}\) fixes all labels. It also has all signs +1: a cycle of labels of length \(d\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) accumulates a constant sign over one full traverse, and \(24/d\) is even. The resulting multiplier \(m^{24}\) retains each prime 13 or 23 that divides the order of \(m\). Since this power maps every block to itself, each block sequence is invariant under \(z\mapsto m^{24}z+b_j-a_i\), with shifts now referring to the power. All four entries of a block-row would have forbidden high odd symmetry. It follows that \(N_G(P)/C_G(P)\) has order dividing 2.

For the kernel of the centralizer's action on cycle labels, \(m=1\) and the same affine action gives
\[
h_{ij}(z+b_j-a_i)=\epsilon_i\eta_j h_{ij}(z).
\]
Iterating \(p\) times forces \(\epsilon_i\eta_j=+1\) since \(p\) is odd. Each \(h_{ij}\) is nonconstant as its absolute sum is at most 47. A nonzero shift in a prime field would force it to be constant, so all shifts are equal, and all signs agree. The kernel is exactly \(P\langle(-I,-I)\rangle\), and the quotient injects into \(S_4\times S_4\). This proves the claimed coprimality for the centralizer and, using the conjugation image, also the normalizer.

For the Sylow claim, an arbitrary nontrivial row stabilizer in a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup would contain an order-\(p\) subgroup by Cauchy's theorem, contradicting the free-action statement. Thus a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup acts freely and its order divides \(4p\), so it has order \(p\).

## 3. Generalization and scope

For any odd prime \(p>k\), the same first compression shows that an order-\(p\) signed automorphism of a Hadamard matrix of order \(kp\) is free, as the fixed count is at most \(k\) and is divisible by \(p\). Its \(k\times k\) compressed \(T\) has odd integral entries and \(TT^{\mathsf T}=kpI_k\). For prime \(\ell>k\) dividing \(p-1\), if \(\ell\) also divides the order of the normalizer's conjugation image, then \(kp\) must be a sum of \(k\) positive odd squares whose roots are each \(\pm1\pmod\ell\). Raise the appropriate automorphism to \(2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)\) to remove all cycle permutations and signs while retaining \(\ell\) in the multiplier order.

Neither a Hadamard matrix of order 2396 nor an example with an order-599 automorphism is constructed here. The condition targets a large symmetry class, not unrestricted existence. Interior autocorrelations within each allowable two-row chamber are not solved.

Primary sources: Egan, Kotsireas, London and Ó Catháin, [Recent progress in the construction of Hadamard matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27175v2), for the ordinary order-2396 problem and group-developed Goethals–Seidel context; [Orbit matrices of Hadamard matrices and related codes](https://doras.dcu.ie/31214/1/Orbit%20matrices%20of%20Hadamard%20matrices%20and%20related%20codes%20%28pre%29.pdf) for the fixed-point matching and compression context. Cinna Davis's [Hadamard 2396 partial results](https://itsdavis.com/solved-conjectures/results/hadamard-2396/proof/) exclude signed automorphism order 479 by cyclotomic norm, study common multipliers for length-1197 Legendre pairs, and rule out a centered order-11 common multiplier in the length-598 bordered model. These are distinct from the order-599 subgroup normalizer and the unbordered length-599 independent-row obstruction. No comprehensive priority claim is made.


## 4. Mod-\(p\) compression and an exact completion filter

These additional statements hold for every odd prime \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) and odd-entry integral \(4\times4\) matrix \(T\) with \(TT^{\mathsf T}=4pI\). Its rank over \(\mathbf F_p\) is exactly two, every two rows and every two columns are linearly independent in that field, every \(2\times2\) minor is nonzero there, and the row and column spans are self-dual \([4,2,3]\) codes for the standard dot product.

Indeed the row span is totally isotropic, so its dimension is at most 2. Let \(u,v\) be two distinct rows. If their reductions are dependent, all six minors \(u_iv_j-u_jv_i\) are divisible by \(p\). Since the entries are odd, these minors equal \(2p k_{ij}\) with integer \(k_{ij}\). Lagrange's identity gives \(\sum_{i<j}k_{ij}^2=4\). There cannot be only one nonzero minor: all coordinates of \(u\) are nonzero, and the zero minors with a third index would equate the two otherwise different ratios \(v_i/u_i\). Therefore exactly four minors have magnitude \(2p\), and two are zero. Equality of ratios is an equivalence relation; exactly two equal pairs forces a partition into two groups of two (if the equal pairs shared an index there would be at least three zero minors). The four cross minors have magnitude \(|u_iu_j|\,|v_j/u_j-v_i/u_i|\), with their second factor fixed across the partition. Thus the two \(|u_i|\) in each group coincide. Write them as \(x,x,y,y\); their norm gives \(x^2+y^2=2p\). But \(-1\) is a nonsquare modulo \(p\), so both \(x,y\) would be divisible by \(p\), which contradicts this equation. The same argument applies to columns.

The rank-two codes are self-dual by total isotropy and their dimension. A nonzero totally isotropic vector over \(\mathbf F_p\) cannot have support at most two, again because \(-1\) is nonsquare; the Singleton bound (or a single coordinate constraint on the 2-space) gives distance exactly three. Projection of either span to any two coordinates is injective and hence an isomorphism. Because any two reduced rows form a basis of the span, each \(2\times2\) minor is nonzero.

Write the circulant array as a \(4\times4\) matrix \(M(X)\) over the group ring \(\mathbf F_p[X]/(X^p-1)=\mathbf F_p[X]/((X-1)^p)\), with each sign row represented by its polynomial of degree at most \(p-1\). The conjugate transpose \(*\) applies the ring involution \(X\mapsto X^{-1}\). A Hadamard array satisfies \(MM^*=0\) in this ring. The ring is local with reduction \(M(1)=T\bmod p\), so every \(2\times2\) submatrix cut out by two row and two column indices is invertible, by its unit determinant. Partition the array into quadrants as \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). The identities \(AA^*+BB^*=0\) and \(CA^*+DB^*=0\) give
\[
D=-CA^*(B^*)^{-1}=CA^{-1}B.
\]
Thus, given valid sign candidates for any three such quadrants, the fourth is uniquely forced as a polynomial modulo \(p\); if any coefficient is not \(\pm1\bmod p\), there is no sign completion. When all coefficients are signs, lift them uniquely to \(\pm1\) over the integers and check the original Hadamard identity. The modular test is a necessary exact elimination rule, not in itself a sufficient construction. It applies to the full order-\(p\) symmetry model, not just the Goethals–Seidel four-generator subfamily.

The same reduction fixes the local Smith invariants of the nontrivial Fourier block. Let \(\zeta\) be primitive of order \(p\) and \(\pi=1-\zeta\), the uniformizer at the unique prime above \(p\) in \(\mathbf Q(\zeta)\). In the integer version of the group-ring matrix, \(M(\zeta)\) reduces to \(T\bmod p\) modulo \(\pi\), so its first two Smith exponents are zero. From \(M(\zeta) M(\zeta)^*=4pI\), and because conjugation preserves valuation, its exponents satisfy \(e_i+e_{5-i}=p-1\). They are exactly \((0,0,p-1,p-1)\), or \((0,0,598,598)\) at \(p=599\).
