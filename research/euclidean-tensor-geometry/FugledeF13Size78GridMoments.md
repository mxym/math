# Size 78 in \(\mathbb F_{13}^3\): low moments, line caps and parallel layers

Put \(p=13,m=6\). Here \((E,A)\) denotes a hypothetical size-\(78\) spectral pair. This is the first stage of the proof excluding that cardinality; the [second stage](FugledeF13Size78CrossMoments.md) establishes mixed moments through degree nine, and the [final tight-pencil stage](FugledeF13Size78FinalDivisibility.md) gives the contradiction. The methods of projective incidence rigidity and barycentric projection are adapted from Ferguson–Iosevich [arXiv:2610.30712v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30712v1), §§8 and 12, with the \(\mathbb F_{13}\) arguments and finite inputs below.

As usual \(D(B)\) denotes projective directions of differences of \(B\), \(U_B\) denotes normals with uniform occupancy 6 across the 13 perpendicular affine planes, and \(V_B\) is its projective complement. For a pair, \(D(A)\subset U_E\) and \(D(E)\subset U_A\). The basic pencil counts of the [companion dense-cardinality proof](FugledeF13DenseCardinalities.md), or Ferguson–Iosevich Lemma 8.2, give line cap 6, plane cap 13, and no complete projective line in either \(U\). The fourteen planes through an affine line with \(a\) points have total occupancy \(78+13a\).

## 1. All moments of degrees through seven vanish

First no nonzero homogeneous form of degree at most seven can vanish on \(D(B)\), for either member \(B\). If it did, multiply by a power of a linear form to take its degree exactly seven. If each projective line \(L\) had \(5\le n_L=|D(B)\cap L|\le13\), then \((n_L-5)(n_L-7)\le0\) outside the at most seven rational line factors, and summing gives at most \(7(13-5)(13-7)=336\). On the other hand, for \(N=|D(B)|\) the incidence count gives \(N^2-155N+6405\ge399\). Thus some projective line \(L\) contains at most four directions of \(D(B)\).

By exact planar enumeration (see below), no six-point noncollinear set over \(\mathbb F_{13}^2\) determines at most four directions. Every affine slice parallel to the direction plane \(L\) then has at most six points: at least seven would include a noncollinear six-point subset because line cap is six. Since the total is \(78\), all thirteen slices have exactly six points, necessarily collinear. Their support lines use at most four projective directions, so at least four are parallel with common direction \(d\).

The locations of four such parallel lines in the quotient by \(d\) form a four-point cap (no three collinear), since otherwise some affine plane of \(B\) would have at least \(3\cdot6>13\) points. They determine at least four quotient directions: if only three, all three perfect matchings of the four points would be parallel pairings; normalizing a triangle to \(0,e_1,e_2\), two such pairings force the fourth point \(e_1+e_2\), contradicting the third in odd characteristic. For each determined quotient direction, two parallel six-point support lines are in an affine plane with that quotient direction. Their cross differences give at least \(6+6-1=11\) projective directions distinct from \(d\), by Cauchy–Davenport after choosing a coordinate along \(d\); they also realize direction \(d\) within either line. The associated projective line of twelve or more such directions must divide a degree-seven form vanishing on \(D(B)\). In this way one obtains at least four distinct line factors through \(d\).

Every projective line \(Q\) through \(d\) that is not a factor then contains at most \(1+(7-4)=4\) zeros: the four factors meet \(Q\) only at \(d\), and the remaining factor of degree at most three has no further room. Hence its parallel affine slices all have exactly six points by the same planar bound. Its normal is therefore uniform. At least seven of the fourteen lines through \(d\) are nonfactors. But a six-point affine line of direction \(d\) has total occupancy \(156\) in its pencil; seven uniform planes and seven others give at most \(7\cdot6+7\cdot13=133\), impossible.

Each moment form \(M_j^B(v)=\sum_{x\in B}(v\cdot x)^j\), \(1\le j\le7\), vanishes on \(U_B\supset D(B')\) for the partner \(B'\), by power sums in the field. The proved rigidity forces the forms identically zero. Comparing multinomial coefficients (invertible modulo 13) gives every mixed moment up to total degree seven, including degree zero since \(13\mid78\).

## 2. Exclude six-point affine lines and multiple layered normals

Call a direction layered if the group of its thirteen parallel planes consists of six with occupancy thirteen and seven empty ones. Suppose \(B\) has layered normals in two distinct directions. Project onto the corresponding independent linear forms, obtaining a multiplicity function \(f(x,y)\) on \(X\times Y\) with \(|X|=|Y|=6\), integral entries in \(0,\dots,6\), and row and column sums thirteen. Define \(\lambda_X(x)=(\prod_{x'\ne x}(x-x'))^{-1}\), similarly \(\lambda_Y(y)\). The vanishings force
\[
 f(x,y)\equiv \lambda_X(x)\lambda_Y(y)Q(x,y)\pmod {13},
 \qquad \deg Q\le2.
\]
Indeed evaluations of \(x^iy^j\) with \(i,j\le5\), \(i+j\le7\) are independent (the tensor-product Vandermonde basis), and number \(30\). Their six-dimensional annihilator contains the displayed six-dimensional family: multiplying by a term of \(Q\) gives total degree \(\le9\), so at least one exponent is at most four. Barycentric interpolation gives the corresponding weighted sum zero.

The arithmetic checker makes all 14 affine classes of six-subsets of \(\mathbb F_{13}\), then all \(196\) ordered pairs. For each choice of \(y^2\) coefficient, its candidates on a row are determined by \(y\)-linear and constant coefficients and all tested. The \(y\)-linear coefficient across rows is an affine function of \(x\); the constant coefficient across rows is quadratic. Thus interpolation from three selected rows covers every \(Q\). Require first all entries in \(0,\dots,6\) and all row sums thirteen, and finally the column sums thirteen. Of 1742 interpolation tuples tested, four matrices survive. They come only from the two different classes represented by \(X_0=\{0,1,2,4,5,6\}\) and \(Y_0=\{0,2,3,4,5,7\}\), with either order, and each matrix has an entry equal to six. Applying this to every pair of directions shows there cannot be three layered normals (their level-set classes would all have to be pairwise different yet drawn from two), and any two-layer case has a six-point affine line.

Now suppose \(E\) has a six-point affine line of direction \(d\). At most three normal directions on \(d^\perp\) are uniform for \(E\), since \(156\le13(14-s)+6s\) for \(s\) uniform there. Therefore \(A\) has at most three difference directions in that tangent projective line. The same planar reasoning as above gives thirteen collinear six-point slices, at least five of whose lines are parallel with direction \(e\). Pick three of these lines. They are noncoplanar by the plane cap and their three pair direction planes are distinct; each determines at least twelve directions of \(A\) (eleven cross and the common axial direction). Each such projective line \(P\) therefore contains at least twelve uniform normals of \(E\). The projection along \(P^\perp\) then forces a layered normal lying in \(P\): the Radon identity and Vandermonde relation of the size-91 proof give only constant residue nonuniform profiles when there are one or two nonuniform normals. Reducing the integer Radon identity leaves exactly one such normal; its profile is \(13^6,0^7\). These three layered normals are distinct, since the lines intersect each other only in \(e\), which belongs to \(D(A)\subset U_E\) and is not layered. This contradicts the two-orientation maximum.

By symmetry both members have line cap five. The same four grid witnesses now imply at most one layered normal in either member.
