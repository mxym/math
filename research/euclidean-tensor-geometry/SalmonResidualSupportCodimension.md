# Codimension-three support bound for the Salmon scheme-theoretic discrepancy

> Follow-up: the [two additional boundary checks](SalmonResidualSupportCodimensionFour.md) improve this to codimension at least four.

Let \(X=\widehat{\sigma_4}((\mathbb P^3)^3)\) (dimension 40), with prime ideal I and known set-theoretic ideal \(J=J_{5,6,9}\). The proofs of equality [on the concise open](SalmonConciseDegreeFiveLocalIdeal.md) and [on the dense rank-four frame orbit](SalmonCompressionFrameLocalIdeal.md) of each \(3\times3\times4\) compression imply, with the details below, that \(\operatorname{Supp}(I/J)\) has codimension at least three in X. This does not prove equality, saturation, the \(S_1\) property or unmixedness. In fact if \(J\) is independently proved \(S_1\), irreducibility and generic reducedness suffice for equality.

## Invertible-slice points

On an inverse-slice chart \(X\simeq \mathrm{GL}_4\times C_{3,4,\mathrm{red}}\), where the second factor has dimension 24. The preceding theorem covers all points whose normalized algebra has dimension 4. The locus where that algebra has dimension at most 3 has dimension at most 19 in the triple factor. For dimension 3 the non-square-zero possibilities are \(\mathbb C^3,\mathbb C\times\mathbb C[t]/t^2,\mathbb C[t]/t^3\). Each has finitely many representation types on four dimensions, given by eigenvalue multiplicities and Jordan block sizes. The centralizer always has dimension at least 6: for \(\mathbb C^3\) the multiplicities are (2,1,1); for the product with dual numbers the block dimensions are (2,2) or (3,1), with centralizers respectively of dimensions \(2+4\) and \(5+1\); for the length-three Jordan operator the type is (3,1), centralizer dimension \(2^2+1+1=6\). Hence their conjugated embedded algebras are parametrized by at most \(16-6=10\) parameters, and the triples in one algebra contribute at most 9. For the remaining dimension-three type (square-zero radical of dimension two), choose range/kernel flag \(U\subseteq L\) of dimensions a,b, then choose a 2-plane in \(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb C^4/L,U)\). The dimension bound \(b(4-b)+a(b-a)+2(a(4-b)-2)\) is at most 8 for \(1\le a\le b\le3,\ a(4-b)\ge2\). Again add nine triple parameters. For dimension-two algebras the same Jordan and multiplicity count gives centralizers of dimension at least 8, so \(16-8+6=14\) parameters suffice. Dimension-one triples are scalar. Thus the possibly bad inverse-slice points have codimension at least five in X.

## All-singular-slice points with exactly one deficient flattening

Only multilinear ranks \((r,4,4)\) with \(r=1,2,3\), up to permutation, remain here. For r≤2 the entire support-rank stratum has dimension at most \(\dim \mathrm{Gr}(r,4)+16r\le36\), since a fixed \(r\times4\times4\) compressed space has dimension \(16r\). For r=3 use the following dimension argument.

Fix the 3-dimensional first-mode space \(A\), and a fixed surjection \(\phi:\mathbb C^4\to A\). The unrestriction theorem of Jagiełła–Jelisiejew [arXiv:2604.24879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24879), Proposition 2.4, realizes any such tensor as \((\phi\otimes 1\otimes 1)\widetilde T\) with \(\widetilde T\) concise of border rank four. Indeed one can degenerate from the dense concise locus in the full \(4\times4\times4\) secant, and the restriction maps on the two already-full modes must be isomorphisms; all these maps can be absorbed by changes of basis to the fixed maps above.

If \(\widetilde T\) had an invertible first-mode slice, normalize that slice to the identity. Its first-mode slices then constitute a four-dimensional commuting algebra acting faithfully on \(\mathbb C^4\). The image of \(A^\vee\) selects a three-dimensional subspace of this algebra. If all selected operators are singular, the subspace lies in one maximal ideal, because the nonunits are the finite union of the maximal ideals. The corresponding simultaneous generalized eigenspace has a nonzero common eigenvector for the full algebra; it is killed by the chosen ideal. Thus the selected slices have a common column kernel, contradicting full rank of the other two flattenings. Hence \(\widetilde T\) has no first-mode inverse slice.

The locus of concise \(\widetilde T\) with no first-mode inverse has dimension at most 36. To see this, some other mode must admit an inverse by Jagiełła–Jelisiejew Thm 3.29; normalize a slice in that mode. Its 4-dimensional algebra module from the concise-local proof is either cyclic or transpose-cyclic, or of square-zero \(2+2\) type. On a normalized chart any inverse first-mode slice corresponds to a cyclic vector in the module or its dual (depending on order of matrix modes): evaluation of the four operators at that vector is the slice, so invertibility is exactly cyclicity. A Gorenstein regular module is self-dual and hence admits inverse slices on both those modes. By the faithful module list, the only regular or dual-regular possibilities not Gorenstein are (i) the product of a scalar with a length-three local square-zero algebra, (ii) the length-four local algebra with pairing of rank one on a two-dimensional radical quotient, (iii) the local length-four square-zero algebra. Their automorphism groups have dimension at least 4: GL2 in (i), independent changes \(x\mapsto a x+b y+c x^2,\ y\mapsto d y+e x^2\) with \(ad\ne0\) in (ii), and GL3 in (iii). Automorphisms lift to the module for a regular or dual-regular representation, so the embedded algebra normalizer in GL4 has dimension at least \(4+4=8\), including its 4-dimensional subgroup of centralizer units. With finitely many types, the normalized triples thus have dimension at most \(16-8+12=20\). The square-zero 2+2 case has dimension at most 19, by choosing a 2-plane (four parameters), three maps from the quotient to it (12), and three scalar shifts. Adding 16 for the inverse slice bounds the total by 36. One can cover the non-first-mode inverse open by finitely many fixed-covector charts (a nonzero slice determinant is degree four in the covector).

For the fixed \(\phi\), the group \(\{g\in \mathrm{GL}_4:\phi g=\phi\}\) has dimension four and acts freely on concise unrestrictions in each fiber (first-mode concision forces any stabilizer to be trivial). Thus the image of the 36-dimensional locus has dimension at most 32. Varying the first-mode support contributes \(\dim\mathrm{Gr}(3,4)=3\) more, giving dimension at most 35 for the singular-slice \((3,4,4)\) locus.

## Points with at least two deficient flattenings

They lie in the union of the three closed irreducible secant compression loci of formats \(3\times3\times4\), using projection onto the actual supports. Each has dimension at most \(4(3+3+4-2)+2\dim\mathrm{Gr}(3,4)=38\), from the four-simple-tensor parameter count and the choice of two supports. The preceding exact Jacobian minor proves equality of \(J_{5,6}\), hence J, on an open dense subset of each. Any residual support there has dimension at most 37. Taking the union of all three cases therefore gives the codimension-three bound.
