# An internal sunflower extraction theorem for spread CZ buckets

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review or priority claim.

This is a restricted rank-four case of the internal extraction problem in [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). Compare [the short-CZ and staggered cases](InternalSunflowersConcentratedLocks.md). The method allows cubic support caps on both pure endpoints, and arbitrary mixed, unbalanced pure-cell weights within a stated physical upper bound.

Let \(H\) have \(E\) edges, be simple and 4-partite with sides \(X,Y,Z,C\), and let \(m\ge2\). Write \(f=f_{XYZ}\) for the number of \(C\)-extensions at a pure cell. Assume each \(X,Y\) label has at most \(Bm^3\) neighboring \(Z\) labels, and each \(C\) has physical degree at most \(Lm^2\), for fixed \(B,L\). For a scale \(0<D\le Lm^2\), assume:
- Occupied-bucket counts satisfy \(N_{CZ}\le E/D\) and \(N_{XZ},N_{YZ}\le E/(Qm^3)\). In particular, corresponding lower bounds on each positive bucket's mass suffice.
- The \(CXZ,CYZ\) codegrees are at most \(\rho D/m\).
- Every pure cell satisfies \(f\le U_0m^2\), for fixed \(U_0\). There is no forbidden band or relative distribution hypothesis on \(f\), nor must \(f=O(D)\).

For \(Q\) sufficiently large and \(\rho\) sufficiently small depending on the fixed constants, there is a subfamily of size \(\Omega(E/m^4)\) internally witnessing every pairwise intersection by an \(m\)-sunflower, including the empty intersection. The conclusion covers \(D\) of order \(m^2\) with arbitrary mixed \(f=O(m^2)\), and also intermediate scales without imposing a linear upper bound on \(CZ\).

Choose \(\alpha=32\), an integer \(A\ge256\), and \(\rho\le\min(1/(256\alpha),1/(64A))\). For a nonempty family the cross-cap forces \(D\ge m/\rho\). First handle any reserved matching by endpoint deletion, then split by whole original cells into short \(f<2Am\), regular \(2Am\le f\le D\), and wide \(f\ge2Am,\ f>D\). Keep a heaviest piece. Its mass is at least \(E/6\), so the original bucket counts expressed relative to the current mass grow by at most a factor six. A whole surviving original regular cell has at least \(Am\) edges because a reserved matching deletes at most \(m\) of its \(C\)-labels.

## Regular piece: sample a full pure-cell quota

Independently activate each Z with probability p=δ/m³ for sufficiently small δ>0 depending on B,L. A cell must have the sole active Z at both its X and Y; impose this after making proposals so that unconditional estimates for rival proposals remain available. A target sees lock failure with probability at most 2Bδ conditional on its own Z active.

Independently for each regular XYZ, propose a uniform Am-subset of its C-reservoir with probability α f/(AD), which is at most 1/2 because A≥2α. Every original incidence therefore has the very same proposal marginal αm/D, before imposing its Z activation. Opponents at distinct cells are independent before outer activation. For any proposed incidence in a target cell with active Z, conditioned also on its whole target proposal, consider a collision when (i) its C occurs on another proposed edge in the same Z sharing X or Y, or (ii) its C occurs on any other proposed edge at a different active Z. Within the same Z, at most 2ρD/m other incidences qualify, and proposal marginal is αm/D, giving ≤2αρ. Other Z labels activate independently of the target Z; total number of such original incidences is at most Lm², so union bound gives pLm²(αm/D)=δαL/D. If E>0 the cross cap implies 1≤ρD/m, in particular D≥1. Thus by choosing δ small, total collision probability for a proposed edge is at most 1/64. This computation ignores all lock erasures and is uniform conditional on target proposal labels.

Delete every collision against the initial active proposals, not just against final survivors, and delete failed X/Y locks. For each heavy cell discard it completely if its proposed set has fewer than Am/2 noncolliding incidences. Given the whole proposed set and its Z active, the expected fraction damaged is at most 1/64, so the additional rejection probability is at most 1/32 by Markov. A target originally proposed with active Z therefore survives all these initial tests with probability at least 1/2 (union bound, no independence claimed for collision and lock events). Expected initial physical count is at least pαm times the regular-piece mass divided by 2D. After these operations each C belongs to at most one Z, and inside that Z the C-edges form an XY matching; neither X nor Y belongs to more than one Z.

## Terminal peels and witnesses

Peel positive buckets until stable:
1. CZ with fewer than m edges. Its edges are an XY matching, so this peel costs <m. Only possible if its Z activated.
2. Heavy XYZ with fewer than m remaining C. Costs <m; only possible if its Z activated and its heavy proposal was made. 
3. XZ or YZ with fewer than 2m projected neighbors in the other pure endpoint. Costs <2Am² because no cell ever has more than Am edges. Charge only if relevant Z activated.

Each bucket is charged at most once. Write \(E_{\rm piece}\) for the regular-piece mass, not the full input mass. Summing expectations with the frozen bucket counts gives respective costs
\[
6pmE_{\rm piece}/D,\qquad p\alpha mE_{\rm piece}/(AD),\qquad
24pAm^2E_{\rm piece}/(Qm^3).
\]
The first cost is \(\le6/\alpha\) times the raw proposal expectation, the second at most \(1/A\) of it, and the third is small for large \(Q\), using \(D\le Lm^2\). The initial survival lower bound is half the raw expectation, so some outcome retains \(\Omega(pmE_{\rm piece}/D)=\Omega(E/m^4)\).

Nonempty kernels are exhausted as follows. X and Y are each locked to one Z and C is tied to one Z, so any realized nonempty kernel contains Z. If the kernel contains C as well it can only be CZ, since on each CZ all X and all Y labels are different; use its matching of ≥m edges. If C is absent, the possible kernels are XYZ, XZ, YZ, and Z. XYZ repetition is allowed only for heavy cells, with ≥m distinct C. XZ has at least 2m different Y, and any edges chosen in different Y at that X have distinct C (collision rule), giving the witness; likewise YZ. To witness Z, choose an arbitrary retained Y and its ≥2m different X neighbors, providing ≥2m different X labels in the layer. Greedily select a fresh X (available at each of the first m steps); this X has ≥2m distinct Y neighbors, and a used Y rules out at most one chosen candidate cell, while a used C rules out at most one edge at that X by the collision rule. With fewer than m used labels of each type at least one edge is available, giving an m-term XY and C matching.

For the empty kernel, reserve a matching when possible. If some m-matching can be reserved whose endpoint deletion leaves at least half the edges, apply the nonempty argument to the residue. Deleting X,Y,Z vertices kills entire pure cells, and deleting the matching’s at most m C labels drops each surviving heavy cell by at most m, so it remains eligible (the heavy proof needs only f≥Am); short cells remain short. Then split the remaining edges using the original three cell types as before. All cross and support caps stay valid, and the frozen bucket count inequalities loosen by at most two at the reserve deletion and at most another three at the cell split; D need not be changed. Restore the reserved matching, disjoint from all constructed edges. If no such reserve exists and a matching of length m exists, its at most 4m endpoints cover at least E/2 edges; if no length m matching exists, all edges are covered by the fewer than 4m endpoints of a maximal matching. In either case a vertex has degree ≥E/(8m). Apply the established rank-three result to its full link, then add that fixed vertex to every recovered edge, giving Ω(E/m⁴), with every nonempty intersection witnessed (and no empty pairwise intersection within this alternative).


## Short piece: use matching colors of the CZ graph

Here is a different proposal rule, needed just for the short part \(f<2Am\). In each occupied \(CZ\), properly edge-color its bipartite simple graph on \(X,Y\) with \(\Delta_{CZ}\) colors, where \(\Delta_{CZ}\) is its maximum degree. This is König's line-coloring theorem. Then \(\Delta_{CZ}\le\rho D/m\), as those degrees are CXZ and CYZ codegrees. Equivalently this coloring can be obtained by filling to a regular bipartite multigraph and removing one perfect matching at a time by Hall's theorem. Delete all color matchings of size \(<Am\); total loss is at most
\[
Am\sum_{CZ}\Delta_{CZ}\le A\rho D N_{CZ}\le6A\rho\,E_{\rm piece}< E_{\rm piece}/8.
\]
Make independent \(CZ\) proposals with at most one matching each; give every retained color exact marginal \(\beta/m\) with \(\beta\) small, because at most \(\rho D/m\le\rho Lm\) choices exist. Conditional on the target matching independently proposed and its \(Z\) active, each proposed incidence meets another proposal in the same XYZ with probability at most \(2A\beta\): any rival belongs to a different \(C\) and has conditional marginal at most \(\beta/m\). A collision with the same \(C\) in any other active \(Z'\) occurs with probability at most
\[
p\beta\sum_{Z'\ne Z}\Delta_{CZ'}/m\ \le\ p\beta Lm
\]
because each \(\Delta_{CZ'}\) is bounded by the number of original edges in that bucket. Delete all of both kinds of collisions against initial proposals, and delete both hard-lock failures. With \(\beta,\delta\) small the expected surviving physical count is at least \(p\beta E_{\rm colored}/(2m)\). Every \(CZ\) remains an XY matching, a \(C\) is tied to only one \(Z\), XYZ cells are now singletons, and X/Y tie uniquely to Z.

Peel \(CZ\) with fewer than \(m\) edges (charge \(<m\) only when its own \(Z\) was active and a matching selected), and \(XZ/YZ\) with fewer than \(2m\) projected neighbors (charge \(<2m\), only when its \(Z\) was active). The former cost is at most \(1/A\) times the raw expected proposal size, since each selected matching initially contains at least \(Am\) edges. The latter cost is at most \(4pm\cdot6E_{\rm piece}/(Qm^3)\), small compared with the lower bound for large \(Q\) relative to \(\beta^{-1}\). Thus the short outcome has \(\Omega(pE_{\rm piece}/m)=\Omega(E_{\rm piece}/m^4)\). All nonempty witnesses are as in the earlier proof (there is no repeated XYZ in the short case). Empty matching reserve or the rank-three link argument now works directly as already detailed.

## Wide piece: use matching colors with a pure-cell cap

For the wide case, execute the short piece's König decomposition into CZ matchings, deleting colors of size \(<Am\) at loss at most \(6A\rho E_{\rm piece}<E_{\rm piece}/8\). Write \(f_\tau'\) for the original cell size within this piece **before** trimming off small CZ colors but after any reserve deletion. It is at most \(U_0m^2\) and at least \(D-m\ge D/2\) for every surviving cell (pure-side deletions kill whole cells). Independently per \(CZ\), propose at most one retained color, giving every such color exact marginal \(\gamma m/D\), where \(\gamma=32\). This is feasible since the summed probabilities are at most \(\gamma\rho\). Conditional on an incidence proposed in an active target Z, all other C-label incidences in its cell have independent proposal coins from different CZ buckets and total expected number at most \(\gamma m f_\tau'/D\). Reject the entire cell if its initial proposal count exceeds \(1+\lceil Kmf_\tau'/D\rceil\), with \(K\) a large fixed multiple of \(\gamma\); by Markov the conditional failure is ≤\(\gamma/K\). Delete all cross-Z C collisions against the initial proposals, but **do not** delete competing proposals within an XYZ. For cross-Z clashes the conditional failure is at most \(p(\gamma m/D)Lm^2\); indeed the sum of the matching color numbers across the rival buckets of this C is at most its degree. Add the same hard X/Y locks. With small \(\delta\) and large \(K\) expected surviving mass is at least half the raw expectation \(p\gamma mE_{\rm trim}/D\).

Use terminal peels:
* CZ with fewer than \(m\) edges: budget at most \(1/A\) of the raw expected weight because every initially proposed colored matching had size at least \(Am\).
* Positive XYZ with fewer than \(m\) labels: charge \(<m\) only if its Z was active. Since \(f_\tau'\ge D/2\) before trimming, the sum is at most \(2pm E_{\rm piece}/D\), small versus the raw expectation with \(\gamma=32\).
* Positive XZ or YZ with fewer than \(2m\) projected partners: charge at most \(2m(2+KU_0m^3/D)\) physical edges once, requiring the Z activation. The total expected cost is then at most \(24pE_{\rm piece}(2+KU_0m^3/D)/(Qm^2)\), negligible relative to \(p\gamma m E_{\rm piece}/D\) since \(D\le Lm^2\) and \(Q\) is large relative to \(L+KU_0\).

Choose an outcome with \(\Omega(p mE_{\rm piece}/D)\ge\Omega(E/m^4)\). The proofs of CZ, XYZ, XZ, YZ and Z witnesses apply unchanged, since each CZ remains a matching even though XYZ cells now allow multiple C's. Restore the empty witness using the matching reserve, or otherwise use a vertex of degree ≥\(E/(8m)\) and the rank-three link theorem. 
The theorem does not handle CZ buckets strongly concentrated on one X or Y label, or pure-cell weights unbounded relative to \(m^2\). It does not resolve the unrestricted rank-four problem.

## Complementary dense-count theorem without CXZ or CYZ cap for short cells

If \(f_{XYZ}\le U_0m\), the same E/m⁴ conclusion holds with the same cubic support, \(C\)-degree \(Lm^2\) and \(N_{XZ},N_{YZ}\le E/(Qm^3)\), assuming \(N_{CZ}\le E/(Jm^2)\) for large fixed \(J\) but no upper bound on \(CXZ\) or \(CYZ\). This includes the empty kernel. Choose \(A\) large. Partition before any reserve deletion: first take all edges in \(CXZ\) fibers of size at least \(2Am\); then in the residue take all edges in \(CYZ\) fibers of size at least \(2Am\); the third part is the remainder. Keep the heaviest of these three (mass at least \(1/3\)); if handling the empty kernel by matching reserve, first choose the reserve in the full graph and delete its endpoints in each type, then choose the heaviest surviving type (at least \(E/6\)).

The final remainder has both \(CXZ,CYZ<2Am\), frozen \(N_{CZ}\le6E_{\rm part}/(Jm^2)\), and \(D=Jm^2/6\) (or use inherited count inflation directly), with \(2Am\le\rho D/m\) for \(J\) large. Apply the nonempty conclusion of the CZ matching theorem, using \(Q/6\) in the weakened count assumption. Its proof needs no further reserve and the absolute constants can be taken large enough. Restore the already reserved matching. The alternative high-degree link applies in the full graph if no disjoint reserve exists.

For the first type, each positive \(CXZ\) group has at least \(Am\) distinct Y labels even after reserve deletion (a surviving group loses at most m such labels). Use \(Z\) activation and hard X/Y locks as above. Independently at each \(CXZ\) of size \(F\), activate it with probability \(\beta F/m^2\) and propose a uniform \(Am\)-subset of its Y labels. The proposal probability per original edge is \(A\beta/m\). The prior \(C\) cap gives \(F\le Lm^2\), so these are valid probabilities for small fixed \(\beta\). Conditional on target group proposal and active target \(Z\), discard it entirely if any rival \(CXZ\) group at its \(C\) also proposes with an active \(Z\). Union bound costs at most \(\sum \beta F_{\rm rival}/m^2\le\beta L\), even if all rival \(Z\)'s are already active. Also discard edges having any rival proposal in the same \(XYZ\) cell. Such rivals lie in different groups and have conditional incidence marginal \(A\beta/m\) before considering their own outer activation; the conditional cost is at most \(U_0A\beta\). These deletions against the initial configuration and the X/Y hard locks leave at least half the raw expected mass \(pA\beta E_{\rm part}/m\) for suitably small \(\beta,\delta\).

Interleave peels: positive \(CXZ\) with fewer than \(m\) edges (charge \(<m\) only if group and Z proposed, at expected cost \(p\beta E_{\rm part}/m\)); positive \(XZ\) with fewer than \(m\) distinct C groups (charge \(<Am^2\), only if Z activated); positive \(YZ\) with fewer than \(m\) distinct X partners (charge \(<m\), only if Z activated, because duplicate XYZ was deleted). Frozen counts of XZ and YZ bound their latter sum by \(O(pA E_{\rm part}/(Qm))\), small relative to the raw expectation. Hence \(\Omega(E/m^4)\) is retained as expected.

Inside the selected family each C belongs to only one \(CXZ\) group, and X and Y each to one Z, with no repeated XYZ. All possible nonempty kernels are \(Z, XZ, YZ, CXZ\). A \(CXZ\) group gives \(m\) distinct Y. An \(XZ\) has at least \(m\) distinct C groups, each with at least \(m\) distinct Y, permitting a greedy distinct Y label per C. A \(YZ\) has at least \(m\) different X, forcing different C by the unique \(CXZ\) ownership. To witness \(Z\), a retained Y has at least \(m\) different X neighbors; every such X in the layer has at least \(m\) different Y from any surviving CXZ, so greedy choices of distinct X,Y are possible, with C automatically different for distinct X. The second part is the symmetric Y-heavy version. This theorem removes both CXZ/CYZ spread caps in the short-cell, sufficiently dense-count corner, not for arbitrary medium-sized pure fibers.
