# A thin-source obstruction to refilling the HJSW frame

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review or priority claim.

Fix a degree bound \(r_\ast\ge2\). Let \(S_{p,\gamma}\) be the clean HJSW frame on the two-period board, in the notation of [the quantitative board estimate](NoThreeInLineHJSWQuantitativeSecants.md). In coordinates centered at the old carrier, write it as \(xy=\gamma\) modulo \(p\). Let \(C\) be any geometrically irreducible plane affine curve over \(\mathbb F_p\) of degree between 2 and \(r_\ast\) on which \(xy\) is nonconstant. Its coefficients may vary with \(p\), \(\gamma\), and the chosen deletion set. Let \(C_p\) be all physical board lifts of its points, and let \(D\subseteq S_{p,\gamma}\). For any fixed \(k\), uniformly in these choices, the number of sites in \(C_p\setminus S_{p,\gamma}\) hit by at most \(k\) secants of \(S_{p,\gamma}\setminus D\) is
\[
O_{r_\ast,k}\!\left(
p e^{-c_{r_\ast}\sqrt{\log p}}
+p\left(\frac{|D|+1}{p}\right)^{c_{r_\ast}}
\right)
\]
for some \(c_{r_\ast}>0\). In particular \(o(p)\) arbitrary deletions allow at most \(o(p)\) insertions from a fixed-size collection of such curves. This covers polynomial graphs of degree at least two, among others. It excludes lines, curves with constant \(xy\) in the old centered coordinates, and candidate pools that are not contained in finitely many such curves. It does not rule out arbitrary linear-size augmentation.

## Transfer the complete Fourier estimate to the curve

Keep the bounded interval of negative reduced weights \(t=a/b\), \(b\le R\), and integer free coordinates \(z,v_1,\ldots,v_j\) from the board proof. On the smooth projective normalization \(\widetilde C\) write the affine coordinates of the root as \(x,y\), and let \(c=xy/\gamma\). Its pole degree is at most \(2r_\ast\), by the divisor of the line at infinity. Thus \(c:\widetilde C\to\mathbb P^1\) is nonconstant and separable for \(p>2r_\ast\). The genus is \(O(r_\ast^2)\), so the number of branch values of this map is \(O_{r_\ast}(1)\) by Riemann–Hurwitz. Omit the \(O_{r_\ast}(1)\) weights whose \(\alpha_t=(1-2t)^2\) is such a value. For \(p\) greater than a fixed multiple of \(R^2\), the remaining \(\alpha_t\) are distinct and different from 0 and 1, by the same numerator bound as in the board proof.

For any \(j\) distinct remaining weights, adjoin the \(j\) radicals \(\eta_i^2=(c-1)(c-\alpha_i)\) to the function field of \(\widetilde C\). Each radical has simple zeros over its own unramified fiber \(c=\alpha_i\), where no other radicand vanishes. The square classes are geometrically independent, so the cover is geometrically connected with degree \(2^j\), independent sign flips, and genus \(O_{r_\ast}((j+1)2^j)\) by tame Riemann–Hurwitz. Remove the finitely many singular or infinite root points and the exceptional levels \(c=0,1,\alpha_i\). This costs \(O_{r_\ast}((j+1)2^j)\) points on the cover. On the remaining locus the points of the cover are in bijection with the residue incidences. Indeed the radical roots determine \(r_i=A_{i,x}/x\) and their two endpoints exactly as in the board argument. Thus the zero Fourier coefficient is \(p+O_{r_\ast}((j+1)2^j\sqrt p)\).

For a nonzero frequency, use the invertible linear change from \(z,v_i\) to \(z,A_i\). The phase is a linear combination of \(x,y\) and the pairs \(xr_i,\gamma/(xr_i)\). A flip of radical \(i\) changes it by
\[
(r_i-r_i')(\alpha_i' x-\beta_i' y),
\]
where \(\alpha_i',\beta_i'\) are that endpoint's two coefficients; we used \(r_i r_i'=1/c\). If the phase were constant, all these private coefficients would vanish because \(C\) is not contained in a line. The remaining nonzero linear form in \(x,y\) is also nonconstant. Its total pole degree on the cover is \(O_{r_\ast}((j+1)2^j)\): \(x,y,c\) have bounded pole degrees on the base, and the coefficients \(r_i,1/r_i\) are the fixed-degree rational root expressions pulled back to a cover of known degree. In the range \(j=O(\sqrt{\log p})\) this total is \(<p\), so the nonconstant phase cannot be of the form \(f^p-f+\text{constant}\), which would have a pole of order at least \(p\). The [additive character-sum bound on curves](https://doi.org/10.2307/2373048) now gives \(O_{r_\ast}((j+1)2^j\sqrt p)\) for every nonzero frequency. For \(j=0\) the same reasoning on \(\widetilde C\), using nonconstant linear forms, counts the root points alone.

The completion and Fejér-envelope argument of the board proof now applies with normalized arithmetic mass \(p^{-1}\) instead of \(p^{-2}\), since the complete error divided by \(p\) is still \(O_{r_\ast}((j+1)2^j/\sqrt p)\). Uniform discrete volume in the free variables is unchanged. The common exceptional root set on \(C_p\) has \(O_{r_\ast}(R^2)\) physical sites by the degree of \(c\) and at most four lifts per residue. Hence the comparison errors retain the same form, including the \(R^2/p\) term, and the main term is still the product of the physical kernels \(\lambda_t(z)\) integrated over the square. Uniform lower and upper \(\Theta(\log R)\) bounds on their sum are unchanged after omitting boundedly many weights. Apply the independent-Bernoulli moment argument from the board proof with \(K=\lfloor \epsilon\log R\rfloor\) and \(R\le \exp(\epsilon\sqrt{\log p})\) for sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon>0\). The moment expansion factor \(R^{4K}\) is absorbed by the power-saving discrepancy. This shows that at most \(O_{r_\ast}(p e^{-c\log R})\) physical root sites fail to have \(\gg_{r_\ast}\log R\) incidences.

## Adaptively deleted endpoints

For fixed weight \(t\) and removed endpoint \(A\), the other endpoint is an invertible affine function of the root \(z\). Requiring that endpoint to be on \(xy=\gamma\) gives a translated axis-aligned hyperbola in root coordinates with level \(t^2\gamma\) or \((1-t)^2\gamma\), depending on the endpoint role. By Bézout its intersection with \(C\) has at most \(2r_\ast\) points unless the curves coincide. Coincidence is possible only if \(C\) itself is a translated axis-aligned hyperbola, and then comparing its nonzero level omits at most four field values of \(t\) from the two quadratic equations. The weights have distinct field residues in the range used, so omit these too, for the whole argument. Each remaining intersection residue gives at most four physical roots and each determines the other endpoint uniquely for the given \(t\) and physical \(A\). Thus deleted endpoints destroy \(O_{r_\ast}((|D|+1)R^2)\) incidences, including the bounded repairs between the clean seed and the exact carrier mask.

Outside the low-incidence exceptional set, a root with at most \(k\) remaining secants must lose \(\gg\log R\) incidences once \(R\) is sufficiently large depending on \(k,r_\ast\), by cleanliness of the seed. The normalized bad population is therefore
\[
O_{r_\ast,k}\!\left(e^{-c\log R}
+\frac{(|D|+1)R^2}{p\log R}\right).
\]
Take \(R\) of order the smaller of \(\exp(\epsilon\sqrt{\log p})\) and \((p/(|D|+1))^{1/4}\). If this is bounded, the asserted bound is trivial after enlarging its constant, by the \(O_{r_\ast}(p)\) source population. Otherwise the first term is bounded by the sum of the two error scales in the theorem for a small enough exponent, and the second is at most a constant multiple of \(((|D|+1)/p)^{1/2}\). This proves the claim uniformly, including if the curve and the deletion set are both selected adaptively.

[Prior source work](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/31) treats one explicit transverse conic by fixed first and second moments, as well as other aligned hyperbola levels through a different method. [The complementary aligned-level paper](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/32) does not supply this general varying-curve class. Here the varying-source cover, power-saving physical completion and endpoint Bézout bound are used together. The unrestricted grid problem remains open.


## No linear refill from any bounded-degree algebraic locus

Suppose \(D=o(p)\) and all added sites are drawn from the physical lifts of a nonzero bivariate polynomial's zero locus of bounded total degree. The polynomial may be chosen after the deletions and may vary with \(p\). If the final set is no-three-in-line and retains \(S_{p,\gamma}\setminus D\), it adds only \(o(p)\) sites.

Factor the defining polynomial geometrically. Components not fixed by Frobenius contribute only \(O_{r_\ast}(1)\) residues, since a rational point on any such component lies on a distinct conjugate and Bézout applies. There are only boundedly many components defined over \(\mathbb F_p\), and their nonlinear components of nonconstant \(xy\) are handled by the uniform estimate above.

A modular line cannot by itself supply a linear-size physical cap. Its direction kernel is a lattice of determinant \(p\) in \(\mathbb Z^2\), and pigeonholing differences in a square of side \(O(\sqrt p)\) gives a nonzero kernel vector \(w\) of infinity norm \(O(\sqrt p)\). Divide by the gcd of its coordinates, which is prime to \(p\), to make it primitive. On the physical lifts of the modular line the values \(\det(w,z)\) are all congruent modulo \(p\). Their physical range is \(O(p\sqrt p)\), so these sites lie on \(O(\sqrt p)\) genuine parallel lines. Each holds at most two sites of the cap.

A nonlinear irreducible component with constant \(xy\) is an aligned hyperbola level. For a different nonzero level, [Theorem 2 of the aligned-level work](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/32) gives \(o(p)\) compatible points after \(o(p)\) deletions from the classical arm core, which is contained in the original frame. For the old level itself, the known [sharp single-hyperbola bound in the two-period window](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22275500) of \(3(p-1)\) leaves room to add at most \(|D|\) points while keeping the other old sites. Summing over the bounded components proves the corollary. This combines the prior aligned-level result with the new nonconstant-product case; it is not an obstruction to arbitrary (nonalgebraic) candidate sets or to replacing a positive fraction of the original frame.
