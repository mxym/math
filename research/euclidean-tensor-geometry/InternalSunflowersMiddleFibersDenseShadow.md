# Representative-first extraction for middle fibers and dense projected pairs

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Let \(H\) be a simple 4-partite 4-graph. For each occupied projected triple \(\tau\) on pure sides \(X,Y,Z\), write \(f_\tau\) for the number of its extensions in \(C\); set \(E=|H|=\sum_\tau f_\tau\). Suppose that each \(C\)-degree is at most \(Lm^2\) and \(f_\tau\le Um^2\), for fixed \(L,U\). Then a subfamily of size \(\Omega_{L,U}(E/m^2)\) internally witnesses every pairwise intersection by an \(m\)-sunflower, including the empty intersection, under either condition:
* Every occupied pure pair bucket has physical mass at least \(Qm^3\), where mass sums \(f_\tau\) over its triples. There is no lower bound on \(f_\tau\).
* Every occupied pure pair has at least \(Qm^2\) distinct projected completions and every \(f_\tau\ge Km\), with \(K\ge1\).

The constants \(Q,K\) may be taken sufficiently large. A second result permits only two rich pure pair types, retaining \(\Omega_{L,U,Q_0}(E/m^3)\) under endpoint conditions below. Neither result imposes any \(C\)-cross codegree cap. These are restricted cases of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6), and do not settle unrestricted rank four. Below we also retain a separate theorem for arbitrarily wide reservoirs in a diffuse regime.

## Three rich types: activate in proportion to fiber size

Independently at each \(\tau\), choose one uniform \(C\) label from its reservoir and activate the triple with probability \(\beta f_\tau/m^2\), with \(0<\beta<\min(1/U,1/(10(L+1)))\). Given any activated triple and its actual selected label \(c\), each other triple with \(c\) in its reservoir chooses \(c\) and activates with probability exactly \(\beta/m^2\). Since there are at most \(Lm^2\) such triples, deleting every initial \(C\) collision retains \(\Omega(\beta E/m^2)\) edges in expectation.

Now repeatedly delete the whole neighborhood of any pure pair of positive projected degree below \(m\). In the first hypothesis the total number of original pair buckets, over the three types, is at most \(3E/(Qm^3)\). In the second hypothesis it is at most \(3R/(Qm^2)\), where \(R\) is the original number of projected triples and \(E\ge KmR\). Each deleted pair is charged at most once, at cost fewer than \(m\) edges. Either bound makes the total deletion smaller than half the expected retained count on choosing \(Q\) large. Thus some outcome keeps size \(\Omega(E/m^2)\) with every positive pure pair degree at least \(m\).

All \(C\) labels and projected triples are private. A repeated pure pair has \(m\) projected completions. Each positive vertex has a simple bipartite link with minimum positive degree at least \(m\) and thus an \(m\)-matching: a maximal smaller matching either misses a positive vertex (then covers all its neighbors, impossible) or covers every positive vertex (also impossible by the degree bound). For an empty-kernel witness, avoid fewer than \(m\) already used labels on each of the three pure sides by starting at any occupied projected triple and replacing coordinates one at a time. At every replacement the other two coordinates form an occupied pair with at least \(m\) completions. Greedy repetition obtains an \(m\)-matching of projected triples and the \(C\) labels are globally private. This supplies all the witnesses internally with no matching reserved in advance.

## Two rich types, no minimum reservoir sizes

Assume instead that positive \(XZ,YZ\) buckets each have mass at least \(Qm^3\), while \(XY\) buckets each have mass at most \(Q_0m^3\). For both directions \(X\to Z,Y\to Z\), require that the sum, over the first endpoint, of the top \(B_0=\lceil Bm^2\rceil\) incident bucket masses is at most \(\eta E\). Take \(B,Q\) large and \(\eta\) small. Then the full bound is \(\Omega_{L,U,Q_0}(E/m^3)\).

Activate \(Z\) labels independently at \(p=\alpha/m\). Inside an active \(Z\), apply the same independent representative selection and activation as before. Conditional on target selection, the cost of a \(C\) collision remains at most \(\beta L\), even for competitors in the same active \(Z\). A competing triple of the same \(XY\) lies in a different \(Z\) and activates with probability \(p\beta f_\tau/m^2\), for a total collision probability at most \(p\beta Q_0m\). In each high bucket with mass \(F\), reject all of its marks if their initial count exceeds \(4\beta F/m^2\). Conditional on a particular target in it being initially selected, the initial count has mean at most \(1+\beta F/m^2\), so rejection probability is at most \(1/4+1/(4\beta F/m^2)\) per bucket. There are at most two such tests affecting a target. For \(\beta,\alpha\) sufficiently small, then \(Q\) large, at least \(0.4p\beta E/m^2\) edges remain in expectation after the tests.

Interleave the following peels. Delete a positive high bucket below \(m\) surviving triples; each bucket is charged fewer than \(m\) edges and only if its \(Z\) was active, so the expected cost is at most \(2pmE/(Qm^3)\). Delete the remaining triples incident to a positive \(X\) or \(Y\) with fewer than \(m\) surviving \(Z\) neighbors. Each endpoint is charged once and each charged bucket contributes at most \(4\beta F/m^2\) edges. Its top \(B_0\) original buckets have total mass at most \(\eta E\) over the side and are available at rate at most \(p\); a non-top original bucket weighs at most the original incident endpoint mass divided by \(B_0\), and each endpoint charges fewer than \(m\) buckets. Thus the expected endpoint cost per side is at most \((4\beta/m^2)(p\eta E+mE/B_0)\). Choose \(Q,B\) large enough and \(\eta\) small enough so the sum of the peels leaves \(\Omega(p\beta E/m^2)=\Omega(E/m^3)\) edges in some outcome.

High pairs have \(m\) completions and low pairs do not repeat. Each positive \(Z\) link has minimum positive degree \(m\); positive \(X\) and \(Y\) take \(m\) distinct supported \(Z\) neighbors (the other labels are distinct by \(XY\) injectivity). To construct an \(m\)-matching, at each step pick an unused \(X\) from any surviving \(YZ\) pair's \(m\) completions; this \(X\) has at least \(m\) supported \(Z\) neighbors, so pick an unused one, then choose an unused \(Y\) completing that high pair. This gives the empty witness as well. All steps lift to the chosen private \(C\) labels.

## Arbitrarily nonuniform fibers when endpoint mass is diffuse

One can remove the upper bound on reservoirs and all the projected pair minima by imposing a different hypothesis. Retain the \(C\)-degree bound and \(f_\tau\ge Km\) for every occupied triple, where \(f_\tau=|F_\tau|\). Put \(E=\sum_\tau f_\tau\), and give a pair bucket the weight of all triples in it, with individual weights \(f_\tau\). In each of the six ordered pairs of pure sides \(V\to Z\), suppose the sum, over \(v\in V\), of the weights of its \(U_0=\lceil Bm^2\rceil\) heaviest \(vZ\) buckets is at most \(\varepsilon E\). For large \(B,K\) and small \(\varepsilon>0\) chosen below, the full \(\Omega_L(E/m^4)\) bound holds. There is no upper bound on the size or ratio of occupied reservoirs and no pure pair or \(C\)-cross degree caps.

To prove this, first test, at each pure side, the total weight in the \(\lceil Tm^3\rceil\) heaviest individual incident triples per vertex. If a tested total is at least \(E/12\), restrict to those triples and apply [the existing cubic hybrid-projection result](InternalSunflowersHybridCubicProjection.md), since all \(f_\tau\ge3m\). Otherwise delete these three lists, losing at most \(E/4\); every remaining triple at \(v\) now weighs at most \(W_v/\lceil Tm^3\rceil\) for the original incident mass \(W_v\). A peel of positive degree below \(m\), on any pure side, now always costs at most \(mE/\lceil Tm^3\rceil\) in starting weight. The endpoint bounds persist as absolute bounds with budget \(\varepsilon E\) on every subhost.

Use [quadratic designation](InternalSunflowersArbitraryWideNonuniform.md#no-caps-quadratic-designation-and-final-trials) with \(t_2=\lceil Q_2m^2\rceil\) and \(\varepsilon_{\rm quad}=1/1000\) on the current weighted support, omitting all separate preceding linear or lock trials. This repeatedly either designates a pair type low by an owner restriction, bounding its number of projected triples by \(t_2\), or designates all pending pair types high. The resulting host has weight \(N\ge\delta E\) for a fixed positive \(\delta\) (e.g. \((\varepsilon_{\rm quad}/6)^3/4\)). If all types are low then [the all-heavy case of the existing \(m^{5/2}\) result](InternalSunflowersHybridFiveHalves.md) already gives the desired bound. Otherwise measure the mass \(F\) of each high bucket in this final host; remove its top \(t_2\) triples at total cost at most \(3\varepsilon_{\rm quad}N\), giving each remaining triple weight at most \(F/t_2\) in that bucket.

Use its all-high trial, independently marking each triple at rate \(c/m\) with \(c=1/10\), or its mixed trial: activate outer objects at rate \(p=\alpha/m\), then independently mark triples at rate \(q=1/m\) within activated objects. For two high types use \(Z\) labels as outer objects (name the high types \(XZ,YZ\)); for one high type use those high pair buckets. Mixed low-pair collision deletion is unchanged, since distinct triples competing at a low pair lie in different outer objects and have conditional selection probability \(pq\). In the mixed case also retain its high bucket sample-mass cap \(4qF\), tested on initial marks. Conditional on a target initially marked that cap fails with probability at most \(1/4+1/(4q t_2)\) per bucket.

Replace all original collision tests on \(C\) by the degree-\(D\) truncation at \(D=\lfloor Km/4\rfloor\) on **initial** marks, rejecting a target that loses over half its reservoir. Given a marked target, any other incidence of a label in its reservoir has conditional selection probability at most \(1/m\), even in the same mixed outer object. Thus the expected marked degree is at most \(1+Lm\) and the conditional rejection probability can be made at most \(1/32\) as before. The expectation of retained starting weight is a fixed fraction of \((c/m)N\) or \(pqN\) respectively.

The original high-pair peels cost at most \(3mN/t_2\) (all-high), or \(2pmN/t_2\) in expectation in the mixed case. For each mixed endpoint support peel, at most \(m\) positive buckets are charged per endpoint. Its original top-\(U_0\) bucket masses total at most \(\varepsilon E\) over the side and each such bucket has activation probability \(p\); all remaining buckets at an endpoint weigh at most its incident host weight divided by \(U_0\). The sample cap \(4qF\) therefore bounds this cost by \(4q[p\varepsilon E+mN/U_0]\) in expectation. In the one-high case the extra singleton peel on the third side costs at most \(mE/\lceil Tm^3\rceil\) by the initial trim. These estimates use starting weights, so they bound losses regardless of the evolving reservoir sizes. Choose \(Q_2\) large, then \(\alpha>0\) sufficiently small for the low-pair collision estimates, then \(B,T\) sufficiently large and \(\varepsilon\ll\delta\). All costs are smaller than the guaranteed expected marked weight, leaving \(\Omega(pqE)\) in mixed cases and \(\Omega(E/m)\) in the all-high case. The projected pair and singleton witnesses are those of the cited trials: high buckets have at least \(m\) distinct projected completions, low pairs are injective, and positive singleton links either have minimum degree at least \(m\) on both sides or have an \(m\)-matching furnished by the surviving supports and low-pair injectivity.

For every remaining reservoir of size \(f'_\tau\ge f_\tau/2\ge D\), demand one private label if \(f'_\tau<mD\), or \(\lfloor f'_\tau/D\rfloor\) private labels otherwise. For any collection of reservoirs, the union contains at least their total incidences divided by \(D\), hence at least their total demand. Hall's theorem applied to demand copies therefore supplies disjoint sets of assigned labels. Every demand is either 1 or at least \(m\), and is at least \(f'_\tau/(mD)\). Thus the selected physical edge count is at least a fixed multiple of the selected starting weight divided by \(m^2\). When a triple repeats it has \(m\) physical completions, and all projected witnesses lift using distinct \(C\) labels. This gives the full nonempty bound in the remaining cases.

The empty kernel is automatic in the random-trial outputs. In all-high, the same sequential replacement argument above builds a projected \(m\)-matching. With two high types \(XZ,YZ\), start from any surviving \(YZ\) pair and pick an unused \(X\) from its \(m\) completions. That \(X\) has \(m\) or more \(Z\) neighbors along high pairs, so pick an unused \(Z\). Then complete \(XZ\) with an unused \(Y\). With one high type \(XZ\), start from any surviving \(Z\), choose an unused \(X\) among its at least \(m\) high-pair neighbors, then an unused \(Z\) among this chosen \(X\)'s at least \(m\) high-pair neighbors, and finally an unused \(Y\) completing that pair. Each process works with fewer than \(m\) forbidden vertices on each side. Greedily repeat to obtain a matching of size \(m\), and lift using private \(C\) labels. Owner exits already use full theorems. Concentrated endpoints in the mixed cases are not covered.
