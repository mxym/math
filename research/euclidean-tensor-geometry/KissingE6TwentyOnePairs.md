# Eliminating 21 antipodal pairs in the complete E6 branch

**Conditional theorem.** If a 73-point six-dimensional kissing configuration includes a representative of each E6 root line, it has at most **20 true antipodal pairs**. This is a restriction on a particular branch, not an unconditional upper bound on the kissing number.

The [22-pair exclusion](KissingE6TwentyTwoPairs.md) bounds the pair count by 21. With exactly 21 pairs the code has 15 other original axes and 16 outsiders. All blockers of outsiders belong to the signed set U of 15 roots opposite the represented single original roots. Negative edges (normalized inner product below −1/2) join axis-disjoint signed masks, so an edge endpoint has between 5 and 10 blockers. In squared-norm-two coordinates the edge threshold is −1.

## Exhaust the possible matching

Apply the same conservative signed-mask and rational-hull tests as in the [preceding theorem](KissingE6TwentyTwoPairs.md). Up to diagram reversal there are 19 total mask types for an edge endpoint of size 5 through 10. For every pair of types with sum at most 15, anchor the first mask, compare its chamber outer hull against all group images of the second chamber's hull for a generator product <−1, and saturate by the stabilizer. This gives 1,160 first-edge orbits in the relaxation. Enumerate a second edge, requiring distinct endpoints, consistent root signs, union size ≤15 and the established no-containment rule for cross-edge pairs of size at most seven. Test its negative eligibility by the same saturated table. There are 412,423 anchored patterns. The rigorous positive-product exclusions from [the 23-pair proof](KissingE6TwentyThreePairs.md) reduce these to 35,354.

Quotient by automorphisms and the eight reorientings/reorderings of the pair of edges; there are **5,377** classes. The accelerated canonicalization is equivalent to minimizing over all transporters: choose the smallest first endpoint type available, carry that endpoint to its canonical mask, minimize the second endpoint among its stabilizer images, and carry along every tied transporter. The remaining edge is unlabelled, so sort its two transported masks and minimize this last pair. All endpoint choices and both edge orders participate.

Of these classes, eight use 13 signed roots, 315 use 14 and 5,054 use 15. Complete each union to exactly 15 by choosing one or two signs on previously unused axes where necessary. There are 8·4·binom(23,2)+315·44+5054=27,010 cases (7,029 distinct signed sets as indexed in the certificate, before any further symmetry reduction).

## Outsider count

By the [global E6 capacity theorem](KissingE6GlobalMaskCapacity.md), every distinct signed blocker pattern is usable by at most one outsider. For each U the certificate assembles every admissible pattern, and may conservatively retain some merely formal upward ideals that have no point on the sphere. Start with all upward size-five masks in U, including the fork ideal as an **extension seed**, but do not count that impossible five-mask as an outsider. To find the next size, extend a retained mask by one root of U in every possible way; any larger upward ideal contains a one-smaller upward subideal. Through size ten, test the extensions against the complete signed-mask orbits.

For size 11 through 15, recognize an upward-ideal translate without storing each entire orbit. Sum the roots in the proposed signed subset and carry that sum into the dominant chamber by simple reflections, transporting the subset along with it. Test whether the resulting subset is one of the enumerated positive upward ideals. This is exact for Weyl-orbit membership: an upward ideal has dominant sum, whose stabilizer preserves the ideal, as proved in the [global capacity result](KissingE6GlobalMaskCapacity.md). In particular an arbitrary choice among transporters to the dominant chamber cannot wrongly reject a member of the orbit. Both diagrams are included in the list of upward ideals. Only 44 of the 27,010 completion cases even admit a candidate mask larger than ten.

Use all larger masks unconditionally as a safe upper bound, without requiring their compatibility. For the smaller masks, count them raw, then test their compatibility with the four required endpoints, then apply a clique bound to compatible additional masks only if necessary. In detail, if h larger masks are present and the 16 outsiders include four endpoints, there must be a further clique of at least 12−h in the remaining small-mask graph, in which an edge is retained unless incompatibility is proved by the positive-product tests. Reject a completion when this is impossible. The successive stages eliminate **2,158**, **23,690**, and **1,162** completion cases. No case survives.

Thus the negative graph cannot have a matching of size two. It is triangle-free, so deleting at most one outsider removes every negative edge. Keep 15 independent outsider lines and the 21 paired original axes. By [Anstreicher's antipodal uniqueness theorem](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/) their 36 lines generate a second E6 system, sharing exactly 21 axes with the first.

## The 42-root intersection is impossible

The common roots form a simply-laced crystallographic root system of rank at most six with exactly 42 roots. In contrast to the previous boundary counts, **such abstract root systems do exist**: the only possibilities in rank at most six are A6 and D5+A1. Both have full rank. Their root-lattice determinants (squared-norm-two normalization) are respectively 7 and 8. A full-rank sublattice of the E6 root lattice (determinant 3) must have determinant 3 times the square of an integer index. Neither 7 nor 8 is such a number. This rules out the proposed intersection and concludes the conditional theorem. It does not rule out 20 pairs: a genuine D5 subsystem can account for 40 roots.
