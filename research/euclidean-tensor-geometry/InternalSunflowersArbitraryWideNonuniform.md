# Arbitrarily nonuniform wide complementary fibers, no pure-pair caps

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal mathematical checks; no external peer review claimed.

Claim: Let H be a simple 4-partite 4-graph with pure parts X,Y,Z and reservoir part C. Suppose the degree of C vertices is at most Lm² and each CX,CY,CZ codegree at most Lm. If every occupied pure triple has at least F_0(L)m² C-extensions (no upper bound or ratio restriction), then some subfamily of size Ω_L(|H|/m⁴) internally m-sunflower-witnesses every pairwise intersection. All pure pair multiplicities and the number of occupied fibers are arbitrary. This is a restricted case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). This extends [the uniform-wide complementary theorem](InternalSunflowersUniformWideComplementary.md); the rank-four unrestricted question is not settled. The numerical constants below can be chosen in the order specified.

## Initial reduction and weighted clean lemma

Use precisely the first independent physical-edge sampling and local repairs in [the uniform-wide complementary theorem](InternalSunflowersUniformWideComplementary.md), rate ρ=1/[4096(L+1)m]. For a fiber of its own size f, the proof uses λ=ρ f and bounds each conditional error in terms of λ, not f/F: conflicts and C-degree truncation cost less than 1/100, bad initial fiber count costs at most 20/λ and fewer than λ/2 usable petals costs at most 4/λ+4/100. Taking F_0 sufficiently large retains Ω_L(|H|/m) physical edges in an outcome with CX,CY,CZ injective, C-degree at most K m for absolute K, and every surviving reservoir size ≥2m. Unlike the earlier theorem we impose no uniformity on the surviving sizes.

Weighted clean lemma: Put any nonnegative weights w_e on distinct projected XYZ triples (zero weights allowed). Give each such triple a C-reservoir of at least 2m extensions, with the cross injectivities and C-degree bound above. There is a set of pure triples of total weight Ω_K(W/m³), W=Σw_e, nonempty-valid on XYZ, together with mutually disjoint C-reservoirs each retaining ≥ half the original number of extensions. To apply set w_e equal to the starting reservoir size. A pure witness lifts by choosing one petal per distinct pure triple, and a repeated pure triple lifts by its ≥m retained extensions.

Common trial rule: initially sample projected candidates, retaining each candidate's full reservoir. Erase each C-extension with a C-label repeated in another initially selected candidate, and reject the candidate if more than half its reservoir disappears. If each competitor has conditional selection probability at most s given the target candidate initially selected, union bound and Markov give candidate-rejection probability at most 2Kms. All pure conflicts can also be tested on the initial sample. In each trial below s is ≤ the activation parameter (or product when competitors lie in different outer groups). Cross injectivity ensures competitors never share even one pure coordinate, so none share an outer pure label or pair in hierarchical trials. Rejections can be evaluated concurrently and cost a uniform probability per target, enabling weighted expectations. After the rule, all further deletions are only of whole candidates, so reservoir half-size and uniqueness persist.

Notation: Top_t(G) is the sum of the t largest weights in a list G (all if shorter). At a pair, weights of individual triples are used. At an endpoint u with neighbor type V, the list contains total weights of each uV bucket. Top_t(V→U) sums the latter top lists over all V labels; Top_t of a side's vertices means sum of top lists of individual incident triples. Reference weights are always frozen before the relevant trims; all deletions only decrease actual masses. If a bucket is diffuse, Top_t ≤ ε times its mass F, deleting its top t costs at most εF, after which every remaining unit weighs ≤ F/t. Repeated concentrated peeling: peel an entire currently positive bucket if its Top_t > ε times its mass, and keep just its top t in an owner-restricted piece. A concrete bucket is charged once, and each owner-restricted piece has degree cap t in that type. If at least half the starting mass is peeled over at most three types, one owner piece keeps ≥ ε W/6; otherwise the core of ≥W/2 is diffuse at every active bucket of the tested types. The same holds for designation of a subset of types. We use strict tests and discard zero-weight buckets whenever convenient. Witnesses no longer depend on zero-weight buckets.

Use the established weighted simple bipartite graph nonempty lemma (each positive degree 1 or ≥m, weight retention Ω(1/m)), and the established weighted two-injective triple lemma from [the weighted two-injective proof, Lemma 2](InternalSunflowersUncappedComplementaryLifts.md#lemma-2-weighted-two-injective-rank-three) (third pair cap ≤m, nonempty retention Ω(1/m²)). Both are applied only after C-reservoir repair, so neither must respect additional C structure.

## Auxiliary exit: one pure side capped O(m²) in projected count

Let X be the capped side, cap Jm². Split YZ buckets by whether Top_(2m) > εF for a sufficiently small absolute ε. Choose the heavier piece in projected weight. In the concentrated piece keep only each bucket's top 2m, retaining an ε fraction. Sample individual triples at r=β/m², with β small in terms of J,K. Remove X and YZ collisions and perform common C repair; each costs small conditional probability by these degree caps. Expected weight Ω(rW) survives. Project to YZ, apply weighted graph lemma, and lift since X and C are private.

In the diffuse piece activate whole occupied YZ buckets independently at r=β/m² and remove X collisions plus C-reservoir failures. A competitor at X cannot share the same YZ bucket by simplicity; C competitors cannot either by cross injectivity. Again conditional losses are small, and expected weight is ≥0.9rW. Discard any bucket with fewer than m surviving triples; the extra expected cost is at most εrW, since its remaining weight is at most original Top_(2m) and it must have been activated. Apply weighted graph lemma to the projected YZ supports with their surviving fiber weights; retain every projected triple in a selected support. X and C are private; repeated YZ has m completions; singleton Y or Z comes from m opposite support neighbors, with distinct X. This exit gives Ω(W/m³).

## Two capped pure pair types, third unbounded

Let XY,YZ be capped by D≤Lm, and set M=max(D,m). Treat XZ buckets as concentrated or diffuse at Top_t, where t=⌈Q M m⌉ and Q sufficiently large in terms of L,K. In the concentrated piece keep only their top t, losing only a constant factor. Split each surviving XZ bucket into at most m batches of size ≤⌈t/m⌉. Independently sample triples at c/M and discard any XY, YZ or same-batch conflict, and perform C repair. Choosing c small in terms of Q,L,K gives constant conditional survival. The resulting system has XY,YZ injective and XZ codegree at most m. The weighted two-injective lemma on its projected triples retains Ω(W/m³), lifting with intact reservoirs.

For the diffuse piece trim the top t in each bucket once, losing at most an ε fraction there. Now in every whole bucket of weight F, every unit weighs at most 2F/t. This stays true under whole-bucket restrictions. In each probabilistic subcase below, freeze all bucket weights before the following Y test. If the Y side's total Top_b of individual triples, b=⌈T m²⌉, exceeds ε_b W, restrict to those lists and use the preceding capped-coordinate exit. Otherwise trim all Y top lists, losing at most ε_b W. Any later iterative peel of positive Y degree below m then costs at most mW/b in total: each deleted unit weighs ≤W_y/b for the pretrim incident weight W_y. The post-trim host need not have its original local weight proportions; we always use frozen F and W_y.

If both endpoint Top_m(X→Z), Top_m(Z→X) sums are ≤ ε_a W, apply the Y test and trim, then sample individual triples at p=c'/M, where c' is small in terms of L,K. Delete XY,YZ collisions, repair C, and reject an entire XZ bucket if its initial sample weight exceeds 4pF. Conditioned on an individual target being sampled, the weight expectation in its bucket is ≤pF+2F/t, so cap-failure probability is at most 1/4+1/(2pt); make Q large. Initial survival has weighted expectation at least pW/2. Peel positive Y degree below m, positive XZ count below m, and positive X,Z support degree below m. Their costs are bounded by mW/b, 2mW/t, and 8pε_a W respectively; use large T,Q and small ε_a. Thus Ω(pW) remains. The XZ pair witness uses ≥m completions, Y's link is a matching by injectivity, and X and Z each have ≥m opposite neighbors with distinct Y representatives by injectivity.

Otherwise choose a concentrated endpoint, say X, and keep only its top m whole XZ buckets per label, losing a constant factor and bounding X support by m. Change W to the new weight; let p=c''/M with c'' small in terms of L,K. If the Z top-m support mass is ≥pW/32, restrict to those whole buckets, giving both support degrees ≤m. Activate pairs independently at r=β/m, remove initially activated pairs meeting another at either endpoint (conditional probability ≤2mr), and repair C reservoirs, at cost ≤2Kmr per target. Gives disjoint XZ pairs and Ω(pW/m) projected weight in expectation. Apply the weighted graph lemma to the simple incidence graph between these XZ pair nodes and Y labels. A repeated pair node has m distinct Y neighbors and repeats of a Y label occur with disjoint pair nodes. Gives Ω(W/m³).

Otherwise Z top-m support mass is <pW/32. Apply Y test and trim. At each X independently choose at most one whole original XZ bucket, each with marginal probability p. This is possible since mp≤1. Remove YZ collisions (rivals are at different X so conditional probability ≤Dp), repair C, and obtain expected weight ≥pW/2. XY is automatically injective. Peel Y degrees below m, positive XZ buckets below m triples, and positive Z support degrees below m. Costs respectively are mW/b, at most 2pmW/t in expectation since activated buckets alone can be charged, and <pW/32. All other claims as above except X cannot be a singleton intersection since it is monochromatic in Z. This completes the two-cap case.

## One capped pure pair type

Suppose XY projected count cap ≤Lm; sample Z coordinates. At every stage first perform concentrated peeling on XZ and YZ at threshold t=c_1m (fixed c_1≥1) and sufficiently small absolute ε. A large owner exits via the two-cap theorem. Otherwise retain the core, whose positive XZ,YZ buckets all have Top_t≤εF. A side X or Y may be locked: keep only edges at its labels with exactly one initially sampled original Z neighbor. For a host of starting weight W, sample every Z independently at p. Delete XY conflict (conditional loss ≤Lmp), perform C repair (≤2Kmp), and delete locked conflicts (≤p times original support cap on each such side). Choose p so these costs total <1/8. Iteratively peel positive XZ,YZ buckets below m units, and unlocked pure X,Y vertices whose positive Z support is below m. The pair costs are ≤2εpW in expectation since their Z label must be active. Unlocked endpoint cost, at cutoff U≥c_0m/p, is at most p Top_U(V→Z)+mW/U: the original top U bucket weights each are initially available with probability p; others ≤W_v/U, and at most m are charged at that vertex. If an unlocked side passes Top_U≤W/c_0 its cost is ≤2pW/c_0.

Begin at p=β/m and choose U=⌈c_0m/p⌉, c_0 large. If an unlocked side concentrates above W/c_0, restrict to its top U whole buckets, lock it, redo concentrated pair peeling as above and choose new p=β/U. Repeat at most twice, so final p=Ω(m^-3) and all lock losses are small by construction; if no more concentrations, sample. A constant fraction of pW survives all repairs and peel budgets. XY is injective, repeated pairs have m petals, locked labels cannot be singleton intersections, while each unlocked X/Y label has ≥m distinct Z neighbors yielding a matching by XY injectivity. Every active Z has a simple XY link of positive min degree ≥m, hence matching number ≥m (a maximal smaller matching leaves an uncovered positive vertex whose ≥m opposite neighbors must be covered; if all positive vertices on one side were covered, a positive vertex on the opposite side still has ≥m neighbors, a contradiction). Completes this case.

## No caps: linear concentration and lock exits

Fix small ε_lin and large c_1, and first perform concentrated peeling on all three pure pair types at t_1=⌈c_1m⌉, ε_lin. Large owners exit via one-cap; otherwise W_0≥W/2 remains, with each positive pair's Top_t1≤ε_lin times its mass.

Let p=α/m (α small), U=⌈c_0m/p⌉ (c_0 large). If any of six ordered endpoint top-U bucket sums exceeds W_0/c_0, take that orientation, naming it X→Z. Restrict to those top U whole XZ buckets, lock X, and use the one-cap lock argument (but no XY cap). An XY pair is automatically injective at a locked X, since X has only one surviving Z and pure triples are simple. Pair concentration-cleaning at XZ,YZ yields one-cap exits otherwise uses the diffuse cores. Lower sampling rate to β/U at first lock; if the other side concentrates at its updated cutoff, likewise lock it, clean and lower rate a second time. Nothing else changes in the preceding argument: the final sampling rate is Ω(m^-3), XY conflict deletion no longer needed, and all witness and budget arguments work.

Now suppose all six endpoint top-U sums are ≤W_0/c_0. Freeze all references in this core. Remove all projected units in each of the six top-U ordered bucket lists (cost at most 6W_0/c_0), and remove every pair bucket's top t_1 projected units for each of the three types, cost at most 3ε_lin W_0. Call the residual W_1≥W_0/2. For every retained unit e and each of its labels v, w_e≤W_v/(t_1 U), where W_v is the incident weight in the frozen core: choose any other pure coordinate, its bucket is outside the corresponding endpoint top U list and e is outside the bucket's top t_1 list. Thus across all future deletions, peeling at most m units per label costs ≤3mW_0/(t_1U). Also all original endpoint top-U bounds persist as absolute bounds for any new subhost.

## No caps: quadratic designation and final trials

Fix t_2=⌈Q_2m²⌉ and small ε_quad. Repeatedly perform concentrated peeling at Top_t2>ε_quad F on all not-yet-designated pair types. If residual retains at least half the current mass, keep it and designate every pending type high (all its buckets diffuse). Otherwise take the largest owner-truncated piece (at least ε_quad/6 of current mass), designate its type low (counts ≤t_2), and repeat only on pending types. At most three stages lose such factors; hence final host J has weight N≥δW_0 with δ=(ε_quad/6)^3/4 sufficient. Every high bucket F is measured in this final J. Trim the original top t_2 in every high bucket, cost at most 3ε_quad N; each surviving unit in a high bucket then weighs ≤F/t_2. Use N for all reference budgets, not the trimmed total. Numerically choose ε_quad small absolute (e.g. 1/1000), then choose sampling constants and Q_2 as below, then c_0,c_1 large enough with δ fixed, and ε_lin small absolute so W_1≥W_0/2. This avoids circular dependencies.

All-low: sample projected triples independently at r=β/[ (Q_2+K+1)m²], delete initially sampled C/pure pair collisions (small conditional losses when β small), then peel positive pure degree below m. Its deterministic weight cost is ≤3mW_0/(t_1U), negligible compared with rN by large c_0,c_1. C and pure pairs are injective; surviving singleton links are matchings of size at least m. All-high: sample independently at r=c/m with c small in terms of K, apply C repair, then peel positive high pair counts below m. Total cost is ≤3mN/t_2, negligible versus rN by large Q_2. A pair has m petals and every vertex link has positive min degree ≥m, hence an m-matching.

Mixed: set p=α/m, q=1/m. If two types are high, name them XZ,YZ and activate whole Z labels independently at p. If only one type is high, name it XZ and activate whole XZ buckets at p. In either case independently mark projected units at rate q inside chosen outer objects. Any rival at a low pair falls in another outer object by simplicity (the two low/high named pair types together fix all three coordinates); any C rival does as well by cross injectivity. Thus conditional initial selection probability for each competitor is pq, the C repair costs ≤2Kmpq and low pure pair conflicts ≤2t_2pq. Take α small relative to Q_2,K. For every high bucket reject its full sample if the initial sampled weight exceeded 4qF. Conditional on a particular target's selection this weight has expectation ≤qF+F/t_2, so failure probability per bucket at most 1/4+1/(4qt_2). At most two such tests affect any target. For Q_2 large, α small, and ε_quad small, hence expected initial surviving weight ≥0.4pqN.

Interleave peels: (i) positive high pair counts below m, (ii) positive high-pair support below m at endpoints where singleton links need m such neighbors (X,Y for two high types, X,Z for one high), and (iii) positive Y degree below m in the one-high case. Cost (i) in expectation is ≤2pmN/t_2, since a high bucket is in exactly one outer object and per-unit weight there is ≤F/t_2. Cost (ii) for each applicable orientation is bounded by 4q[pW_0/c_0 +mN/U]: each charge has fewer than m high buckets, the original top U activated weights (measured in J) have expected available total ≤pW_0/c_0 from inherited bounds, all other buckets at a given label weigh ≤N_v/U, and the sample cap is 4q. This estimate needs no independence of activations for that vertex's top list. Cost (iii) is at most 3m W_0/(t_1U), as established. All are negligible compared with pqN by appropriate Q_2,c_0,c_1. For clarity, α is chosen after Q_2; the relative pair count cost is ≤2/Q_2 (since q=1/m), mixed support cost is O(1/(δ c_0)) (since U≥c_0m/p), while low singleton cost relative pqN is O(1/(δc_0c_1)). All-low similarly allows c_0,c_1 increased after α,β,Q_2.

All surviving low pairs and C labels are injective; high pairs have m petals. In the two-high case X/Y take distinct Z neighbors with remaining labels distinct by XY injectivity, and a Z link has positive min degree ≥m from the two high pairs. In the one-high case X or Z takes distinct high-bucket neighbors and other labels distinct by XY or YZ injectivity, and Y's link is an m-matching by injectivity of both low types and the degree peel. This completes the weighted-clean nonempty lemma.

## Empty-kernel restoration

Double the initial minimum-fiber constant. If an m-matching can be reserved so its endpoint deletion leaves at least half of H, run the theorem on the residue: a projected fiber either disappears (a pure vertex was deleted) or loses at most m distinct C-extensions, preserving the lower bound after doubling. Restore the disjoint matching to witness empty intersections, without introducing any other kinds. Otherwise some vertex has degree at least |H|/(8m) by endpoint cover of an m-matching (or a smaller maximal matching if no m-matching exists). Apply the previously established full rank-three result in that link and restore the common vertex. Both cases yield Ω_L(|H|/m⁴). No supplementary hypothesis applies to the link.

Dependencies: the original (uniform) wide complementary clean-up and scope arguments, weighted simple graph lemma, weighted two-injective lemma audited in InternalSunflowersUncappedComplementaryLifts.md, and full rank-three theorem. All constant-factor mass restrictions occur only a bounded number of times, and the arbitrary upper range of w_e never contributes a logarithmic loss.
## Mixed light and wide fibers

As a direct consequence, the same conclusion holds if every occupied fiber is either at most \(L'm\) or at least \(F_0(L)m^2\), with no bound on their ratios or on any pure pair multiplicities. Split the host by these two classes of whole projected fibers, and take one carrying at least half the physical edges. In the small class apply the [no-pure-pair-cap theorem with bounded complementary fibers](InternalSunflowersNoPurePairCaps.md); in the wide class apply the present theorem. Both already include the empty kernel. This does not handle a general middle band of occupied fibers.
