# The unique point-transitive optimal kissing configuration in dimension seven

**Theorem.** Up to an orthogonal transformation, the only point-transitive 126-point kissing configuration in $\mathbb R^7$ is the set of normalized roots of $E_7$. Together with the [point-transitive upper bound](KissingSevenVertexTransitive.md), this gives both the exact optimum and its complete equality classification within the transitive class.

One must not drop “point-transitive”: there are [other 126-point configurations](https://msp.org/gt/2011/15-4/gt-v15-n4-p11-p.pdf), and the unrestricted kissing number is not determined here. The new part of the equality argument is forcing antipodality, not reclassifying optimal antipodal codes.

## 1. A finite-orbit lemma, without angle assumptions

**Lemma.** Let $G\le O(7)$ be finite and irreducible over $\mathbb R$, with $9\mid |G|$. A Sylow 2-subgroup $P$ of $G$ has no common nonzero fixed vector. Consequently every orbit on the unit sphere has even order, and every orbit whose order is twice an odd number is antipodal.

For the consequence, take $x$ in the orbit, choose a Sylow 2-subgroup $Q$ of $G_x$, and choose $P$ containing $Q$. Then $[P:Q]$ is the 2-part of the orbit size. It cannot be one. If it is two, $Q$ is normal in $P$, and for $h\in P\setminus Q$ the vector $x+hx$ is fixed by $Q$ and by $h$ (since $h^2\in Q$). It is therefore zero. The argument applies at each $x$. It suffices below to find one 2-subgroup with zero fixed space, since it extends to a Sylow subgroup and all Sylow subgroups are conjugate.

The complexification of an odd-dimensional irreducible real representation is irreducible: otherwise a complex constituent and its conjugate give a conjugation-stable sum of even dimension, or there is a proper conjugation-stable constituent. We distinguish the imprimitive and primitive cases for this complex representation.

**Imprimitive.** The blocks are seven complex lines, permuted transitively. The permutation kernel $B$ is abelian and diagonal on these lines. It is a 2-group. Indeed all equal-weight classes on the seven lines have the same size. If there is one class, $B$ acts by real orthogonal scalars. Otherwise the seven characters are distinct; conjugation permutes their lines and, being an involution on seven letters, fixes one. Since it commutes with $G$ and the action on lines is transitive, all seven lines are real. Thus $B$ consists of sign changes. If $B\ne1$, the space fixed by $B$ is $G$-invariant and is not the whole representation by faithfulness. It is zero.

If $B=1$, the group embeds transitively in $S_7$. Its order is divisible by nine, so it contains a Sylow 3-subgroup of $S_7$, in particular a 3-cycle. The action is primitive by prime degree. A primitive permutation group of degree seven containing a 3-cycle contains $A_7$ (Jordan's theorem; equivalently the conjugate 3-cycle supports form a connected hypergraph, and their 3-cycles generate the alternating group on the component). Thus the group is $A_7$ or $S_7$ in its action on the seven lines. The complex representation is induced from a linear character of the stabilizer of one line. For $A_7$ the stabilizer $A_6$ is perfect. For $S_7$ both linear characters of $S_6$ extend to $S_7$. In both cases induction gives a twist of the reducible permutation representation, a contradiction.

**Primitive.** If $-I\in G$ the lemma is immediate. Otherwise the determinant twist $g\mapsto(\det g)g$ embeds $G$ into $SO(7)$; it preserves irreducibility and primitivity. Its center consists of real determinant-one scalars, so is trivial. Its Sylow 7-subgroups are abelian: a nonlinear complex constituent of a 7-group has degree at least seven, and must occur together with its distinct conjugate in a real representation (a nontrivial irreducible character of an odd-order group has Frobenius–Schur indicator zero). Apply [Wales, *Finite Linear Groups of Degree Seven II*, Theorem I](https://msp.org/pjm/1970/34-1/pjm-v34-n1-p21-p.pdf). Since nine divides the order, the resulting abstract possibilities, including the indicated extensions, are
\[
 \mathrm{PSL}_2(8),\quad \mathrm{PSL}_2(8):3,\quad
 A_8,\quad S_8,\quad \mathrm{PSU}_3(3),\quad \mathrm{PSU}_3(3):2,\quad \mathrm{Sp}_6(2).
\]
We now use subgroups of the **original** faithful representation of this abstract group; the determinant twist need not preserve its point orbit.

All but the unitary pair contain $E:C_7$, where $E=(C_2)^3$ and the complement cycles the seven nontrivial characters of $E$. In $\mathrm{SL}_2(8)=\mathrm{PSL}_2(8)$ use upper-unitriangular and diagonal matrices. In $A_8$ use affine translations and multiplication on $\mathbb F_8$ (a nontrivial translation is four transpositions, and a generator of the multiplier group is a 7-cycle). In $\mathrm{Sp}_6(2)$ use $\mathrm{SL}_2(8)$ on $\mathbb F_8^2$ with the nondegenerate alternating $\mathbb F_2$-form given by the field trace of the determinant. Faithfulness requires a nontrivial $E$-character; conjugation gives all seven, filling the entire dimension. Hence $V^E=0$.

In the unitary pair, restrict to the normal simple subgroup $S=\mathrm{PSU}_3(3)$ of index at most two. The complex representation remains irreducible: the isotypic components for $S$ are permuted transitively, so their number divides both the index and seven. There is one. Its constituent degree times its multiplicity is seven; degree one is impossible since $S$ is nonabelian simple and acts faithfully. The [ordinary ATLAS character table, printed p. 14](https://gaetan.chenevier.perso.math.cnrs.fr/ALG1/ATLAS.pdf) gives three characters of degree seven, of which only $7a$ is real; $7b,7c$ are a nonreal conjugate pair. For example, in the [published $7b$ generators](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/exc/G22/gap0/U33G1-Ar7bB0.g) one finds $\operatorname{tr}(ab^2)=-1+i$.

For the [integral $7a$ generators $a,b$](https://brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas/exc/G22/gap0/U33G1-Zr7aB0.g), the involutions $a,b^3$ generate a dihedral group of order eight: $r=ab^3$ has order four. The sum of its eight representation matrices $r^j,ar^j$, $0\le j<4$, is the zero matrix. Thus this 2-subgroup has no common nonzero fixed vector. The accompanying verifier checks these finite integer identities directly. This completes the finite-orbit lemma.

## 2. Excluding reducible 126-point actions

**Isoradial bound.** Any seven-dimensional kissing configuration whose points have constant projection length onto a fixed nonzero proper subspace contains at most **125** points, with no symmetry required.

Here the original upper bound of 126 would not suffice. The [new rational charts](kissing-seven-uniqueness-charts.json) certify the stricter inequality. In the notation of the [product-sphere argument, §1](KissingSevenVertexTransitive.md), the splits $1+6,2+5,3+4$ use respectively 11, 7 and 7 closed squared-radius intervals covering $[0,1]$. All supplied nonconstant Gegenbauer-product coefficients are positive and the largest diagonal $1+\sum a_{ij}$ is **125.9800024**. The off-diagonal test covers the permissible part of the square for each endpoint halfplane, hence the union for all intermediate radii. Positive semidefiniteness then gives $N^2\le NF(1,1)<126N$.

The [checker](verify-kissing-seven-uniqueness.py) expands normalized Gegenbauer and Chebyshev polynomials into exact monomials, substitutes the two strip coordinates, and converts monomials to tensor Bernstein coefficients via $\binom{k}{i}/\binom{d}{i}$. It interchanges the sphere factors for splits $2+5,3+4$. It certifies nonnegativity of the negative off-diagonal polynomial by exact dyadic subdivision in **1,259** nodes. An independent recurrence-and-interpolation implementation using the opposite slicing of the same polynomials passed in **1,217** nodes.

## 3. Equality

Let $C$ be point-transitive with $|C|=126$. A nonspanning code has constant zero projection onto a suitable nonzero proper subspace, which §2 excludes. The full symmetry group is finite because it acts faithfully on the spanning set. Any invariant nonzero proper subspace would likewise give constant projection length on its single orbit, so the group acts irreducibly. Orbit–stabilizer gives $9\mid126\mid |G|$. Since $126=2\cdot63$, §1 shows that the orbit is antipodal. [Anstreicher, *Improved Linear Programming Bounds for Antipodal Spherical Codes*, Theorem 7](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2001/01/248.pdf) now identifies the code with the normalized roots of $E_7$. Their Weyl group is transitive, proving attainability and uniqueness.
