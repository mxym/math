# Five smaller-diameter pieces from a transverse contact net in four dimensions

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

The four-dimensional Borsuk number lies between 5 and 6 by the regular simplex example and the independent six-part results of [Chalmers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17375) and [Tolmachev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16775). This note proves a **five-part special case** with squared circumradius bounded by \(1/3\) and a dense transverse contact net. It does not prove \(b(4)=5\). The regime is related to the regular-triangle contact-star route described in [the ProofAtlas working map](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/borsuk-problem-r4/), but the partition uses a specific net hypothesis.

## Statement

Let \(R=1/\sqrt3\), let \(e\) be a unit vector, and let \(X\subset \overline B(0,R)\subset\mathbb R^4\) have diameter 1. Suppose \(D\) is a collection of unit vectors in \(e^\perp\) satisfying
\[
\sup_{u\in D}\langle u,v\rangle\ge79/100
\quad\text{for every unit }v\in e^\perp.
\]
For each \(u\in D\), suppose \(X\) has a point within \(1/500\) of \(-Re/2+u/2\). Then \(X\) can be partitioned into five pieces of squared diameter less than \(997/1000\).

In particular \(D\) can be the twelve directions of a regular icosahedron. Indeed the support lower bound of those directions is their unit-circumradius inradius, \(\sqrt{(5+2\sqrt5)/15}>79/100\). This follows by taking the unit normal along the sum of the three unit vertices of a regular icosahedral face, whose mutual inner products are \(1/\sqrt5\); the origin-centered insphere is tangent to every face. If \(P=Re\) belongs to \(X\) and the net points are exact, all of them lie at distance 1 from \(P\) and on the circumsphere. Approximation is allowed by the statement.

## Proof

Write \(x=-Re/2+ae+w\), \(w\in e^\perp\), and set \(s=\|w\|\). By the ball constraint,
\[
-R/2\le a\le 3R/2,\qquad a^2+s^2\le1/4+Ra .
\]
By the diameter constraint, every point of \(X\) is within \(1+1/500\) of each ideal net point. Taking a transverse direction with support toward \(-w\) therefore gives
\[
a^2+s^2+cs\le M,\qquad c=79/100,\quad M=(1+1/500)^2-1/4.
\]
The case \(w=0\) is immediate; otherwise take the supremum if the support is not attained. Define \(r(a)=(\sqrt{c^2+4(M-a^2)}-c)/2\), so \(s\le r(a)\). This is nonnegative on the whole coordinate interval and decreases for \(a\ge0\). Put \(\alpha=3/8\), \(r_0=r(0)\), and \(r_\alpha=r(\alpha)\).

The top piece \(a\ge\alpha\) lies in a ball centered at \(-Re/2+e/2\). Its squared radius is at most the maximum of
\[
(a-1/2)^2+r(a)^2=M+1/4-a-cr(a)
\]
on \([\alpha,3R/2]\). The expression is convex in \(a\), since \(r(a)\) is concave, so the maximum is at an endpoint. It is less than \(0.993/4\) by \(M=0.754004,\ .57735<R<.57736,\ r_\alpha>.482\); at the upper endpoint one can drop the nonpositive term \(-cr(a)\). Hence its squared diameter is less than \(0.993\).

For \(a<\alpha\), split the transverse vector \(w\) using the four maximum-inner-product cones of a regular tetrahedron in \(e^\perp\), assigning ties arbitrarily. Two unit directions in the same cone have inner product at least \(-\gamma\) with \(\gamma=1/\sqrt3\). Here is a short angular proof. The cone at a tetrahedron vertex is generated by the negatives of the other three vertices, whose Gram matrix is \((4I-J)/3\). For unit conic combinations \(u,v\) attaining the minimum inner product \(-\lambda<0\), let \(p,q,h\) be the numbers of ray indices used only by \(u\), only by \(v\), or by both. Let \(U,V\) be the positive sums of their respective coefficients. Stationarity on their supports gives \(4(v_i+\lambda u_i)=V+\lambda U\) for active \(u\) and \(4(u_i+\lambda v_i)=U+\lambda V\) for active \(v\). Since the cone is contained in an open hemisphere on the sphere, \(\lambda<1\). In the overlap these yield \(u_i=U/4\), \(v_i=V/4\). Summing over the supports gives
\[
\lambda^2=\frac{pq}{(4-p-h)(4-q-h)}\le1/3
\]
by \(p+q+h\le3\). If the minimum were nonnegative there would be nothing to prove. The statement for zero transverse vectors follows without a direction.

In one of these four lower pieces consider a pair of points with heights \(a,a'\) and radii \(s,s'\). Their squared distance is at most \((a-a')^2+s^2+s'^2+2\gamma ss'\). We handle the signs separately.

- If \(0\le a'\le a\le\alpha\), bound \(a-a'\le a\), \(s\le r(a)\), \(s'\le r_0\). The bound becomes \(M+r_0^2+(2\gamma r_0-c)r(a)\), at most \(M+r_0^2+(2\gamma r_0-c)r_\alpha<.997\) since the coefficient is negative.
- If both heights are nonpositive, each lies in \([-R/2,0]\) and each radius is at most \(1/2\) from the ball constraint. The bound is at most \(R^2/4+(1+\gamma)/2<.873\).
- For mixed signs \(a\ge0\), \(a'=-b\le0\), use \(s\le r(a)\), \(b^2+s'^2\le1/4-Rb\), and \(s'\le1/2\). The distance bound is at most
\[
M+1/4+(2a-R)b+(2\gamma s'-c)r(a)
\le M+1/4+(2\alpha-R)R/2-(c-\gamma)r_\alpha<.952 .
\]

All strict numerical inequalities follow by squaring positive rationals in \(r_0<.559,\ r_\alpha>.482,\ .57735<R=\gamma<.57736\) and substituting these bounds. Thus all five pieces have the stated margin, uniformly for the approximate net. This argument controls a particular geometric set of contact directions; existence of such a net for an arbitrary compact unit-diameter set is neither assumed nor claimed.
