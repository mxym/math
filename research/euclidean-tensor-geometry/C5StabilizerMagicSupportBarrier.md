# A sharp stabilizer-map and local magic-support barrier for the pentagon

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

This is a theorem about a restricted tensor-conversion architecture, not a lower bound on unrestricted complex tensor rank or border rank. It complements the graph tensor formulation in [Christandl–Zuiddam, Tensor surgery and tensor rank](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04085).

## Model and theorem

Fix a prime p. Parties are graph vertices. Each edge e carries \(\ell_e\) shared \(p\)-level Bell pairs, packed as one index with \(p^{\ell_e}\) possible values. Put \(L=\sum_e\ell_e\). The source is a \(p^k\)-dimensional GHZ state with the joint computational-basis label \(x\in\mathbb F_p^k\). Party i also has an arbitrary unentangled local pure resource \(\eta_i\), whose support in its computational basis has cardinality \(r_i\).

Each party uses a single stabilizer linear map: the zero map, or a map whose vectorized Choi ket is a nonzero scalar multiple of a pure stabilizer state. In particular a fixed, fully refined postselected branch of local Clifford operations, stabilizer ancilla preparation, and Pauli measurements is such a map. Even operations based on previous classical outcomes are permitted: once all outcomes are fixed, this is one product of local branch maps. This does not allow a coherent sum of different stabilizer maps for free. If a stabilizer protocol discards registers, refine into computational basis projection outcomes before applying the argument.

For any nonzero pure output normalized to a unit vector, the squared fidelity with the normalized target EPR graph tensor is at most
\[
\min\!\left(1,\ p^{k-L}\prod_i r_i\right).
\]
The same bound holds for a stochastic mixture of fully refined branches with the same budgets, since squared fidelity to a fixed pure target is linear in a density matrix. Exact conversion therefore requires \(k+\sum_i\log_p r_i\ge L\). With no magic resources, \(k=L\) is sufficient: unpack one GHZ digit per desired Bell pair by projecting the unwanted parties for that digit onto the Fourier zero character. Hence the no-magic cost is exact. For \(N\) copies of the pentagon graph tensor at base local edge dimension \(p\), \(L=5N\), even with joint processing across copies and across the two ports of each vertex. It again applies for \(p^N\) on each edge, identified with \(N\) prime-dimensional qudits.

## Proof

**Column lemma.** The nonzero columns of a stabilizer linear map in the computational input basis are pairwise either proportional or orthogonal. Use the standard affine quadratic support form of stabilizer amplitudes, [Dehaene–De Moor](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304125) for p=2 and [Hostens–Dehaene–De Moor](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0408190) for prime qudits. In the joint support affine space of the Choi ket, all nonempty output fibers at fixed input are cosets of the same linear subspace U. Distinct fibers are orthogonal. For two columns on the same fiber, their phase ratio is a constant times an additive character of U: restrict the common quadratic phase to the fiber coordinates, and shifting the input changes only the linear and constant terms. In the binary formula the extra factor \(i^\ell\), with \(\ell\) affine binary-linear, likewise changes by a constant times \((-1)^{\ell'}\) under a translation; equivalently one uses the polarization identity for the standard \(\mathbb Z/4\)-valued quadratic refinement. If the ratio character is nontrivial, its sum is zero; otherwise the two columns are proportional. Choose one normalized vector per nonzero column ray; these give a partial orthonormal basis, extendible to a full one. The claim that refined stabilizer branches have stabilizer Choi states follows by running the branch on half of each maximally entangled stabilizer input pair: Clifford gates preserve stabilizers and conditioning a Pauli measurement on any nonzero outcome gives another pure stabilizer state.

Expand the GHZ and all local resources in the computational basis, using at most \(p^k\prod_i r_i\) terms. By the column lemma all resulting local factors belong to a common orthonormal basis for that party, up to scaling. Thus the output has support on at most \(p^k\prod_i r_i\) tuples in one product orthonormal basis. The bases may depend on the operations and need not respect the two ports at a vertex.

**Graph overlap.** The normalized Bell graph tensor has all coefficients \(p^{-L/2}\) on the \(p^L\) valid edge assignments. Its overlap with any unit product of local vectors is at most \(p^{-L/2}\). To see this, delete the constant factor and contract the unnormalized local vector tensors along all graph edges. Fuse adjacent blocks of vertices successively, contracting all their cross-edges at once; this is a matrix multiplication after reshaping, bounded in Frobenius norm by the product of the Frobenius norms. Each partial tensor retains only boundary legs, so no loops are left uncontracted. Disconnected components simply multiply. The final scalar is bounded by the product of the local unit norms. A computational-basis product at a valid assignment attains the bound. Consequently the squared norm of the target projected to \(R\) orthonormal product basis vectors is at most \(Rp^{-L}\). The output is supported there, proving the fidelity statement.

## Consequences and scope

For qubits, every extra local gate \(U=\alpha I+\beta P\) for a Hermitian Pauli \(P\) can be implemented up to scalar using one qubit \(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle\), the controlled-P Clifford, and postselection on \(|+\rangle\). The controlled-P is Clifford by conjugating \(P\) to a single Z, with a control Z correction if the sign is negative. A local Clifford-plus-\(t\)-T protocol therefore obeys \(k+t\ge 5N+\log_2 f\) if its output has squared fidelity at least \(f\) with \(N\) pentagons. In particular, asymptotic qubit GHZ exponent \(25/6\) with nonvanishing fidelity in this architecture requires at least \((5/6-o(1))N\) local T-type gates. This is a necessary budget, not an assertion of existence of a protocol meeting it. The magic-support proof is stronger than merely expanding into stabilizer branches: before contracting the magic input ports, one common product basis works for all terms by the column lemma.

Existing shared Bell or GHZ resources on subsets of parties may be included by adding one digit per prime-dimensional GHZ/Bell factor to the source expansion. Returning a Bell graph resource as a catalyst adds exactly as many digits to the target \(L\), so does not improve this bound. No claim for arbitrary catalysts or coherent sums of independently chosen stabilizer maps.

Related but distinct primary work: [Bravyi–Fattal–Gottesman](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0504208) studies local Clifford interconversion and GHZ extraction from multipartite stabilizer states; [Englbrecht–Kraft–Kraus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04202) studies transformations by party-local Clifford operations. The column and fidelity argument here also handles local postselection and gives a local non-Clifford budget. It does not use Euclidean pricing. In terms of C5 asymptotic rank, unrestricted linear maps can have nonorthogonal, nonproportional columns and escape this result; no change of that unrestricted interval is asserted.

### Basis-free proof of the column lemma

One can avoid all phase conventions in the affine quadratic proof. In the joint Pauli symplectic space, let \(S\) be the Lagrangian of the stabilizer Choi ket, with \(n\) input and \(m\) output qudits, and let \(Z_I\) be the \(n\)-dimensional subspace of input-only computational \(Z\) operators. Set \(W=Z_I^\perp\). An element of \(S\cap W\) has diagonal input part, so after projecting the input onto any computational vector its output part fixes the corresponding column up to an eigenvalue determined by that outcome. The dimension of \(S\cap W\) is \(m+\dim(S\cap Z_I)\): the rank of the pairing from \(S\) to \(Z_I^*\) equals \(n-\dim(Z_I\cap S^\perp)=n-\dim(Z_I\cap S)\). The projection of \(S\cap W\) onto the output Pauli space has kernel \(S\cap Z_I\), so its dimension is \(m\). It is isotropic because all these input parts are diagonal and therefore pair to zero, and so it is a Lagrangian on the output. A fixed commuting choice of representatives has one-dimensional common eigenspaces forming a stabilizer orthonormal basis (equivalently use the trace projector formula for commuting Paulis). Every nonzero column is one of these eigenvectors up to scalar. This version also shows that the output product basis can be chosen to be a product of local Clifford bases, and without adding local magic the minimum logarithmic support in such bases is nonincreasing under the stated fully refined stabilizer branches for any pure source.
