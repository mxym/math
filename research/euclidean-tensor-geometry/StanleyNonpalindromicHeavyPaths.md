# Untagged nonpalindromic heavy paths of narrow-band orders

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal mathematical checks; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** Fix a cutoff \(h\ge1\) and integers \(g>d\ge1\), \(s\ge h+d+1\). Suppose a tree has a nonempty heavy skeleton which is a path, all its block orders are multiples of \(g\) in \([s,2s-1]\), and the order word of the blocks is not palindromic. Then the ordinary chromatic symmetric function determines the skeleton mass order up to reversal and the normalized rooted cut polynomial of every block through degree \(d\) in deficit. There are no restrictions on which block orders and rooted types repeat. At \(h=2,d=1\) the invariant determines the whole tree (allowing \(g=2\)). More generally it determines the whole tree if \(d\ge D_h:=\sum_{j\le h}j b_j\), where \(b_j\) is the number of rooted tree types of order \(j\). Reconstruction is among all ordinary unweighted trees.

The heavy skeleton consists of edges with both cut sides of size \(>h\), with one rooted block at each skeleton vertex after these edges are cut. Write \(U=\sum_{F}\prod_{K}x_{|K|}\) for the unsigned cut polynomial (sum over deleted edge sets, and corresponding components \(K\)). It is equivalent to the chromatic symmetric function for trees. The highest homogeneous layer in the variables of index \(>h\) is the product of the rooted-tail factors, and determines their block orders, including multiplicities (see [the heavy-skeleton proof](StanleyHeavyEdgeCore.md)). In particular any \(U\)-equal rival has the same cutoff, block count, and multiset of block orders. Here the cutoff itself is fixed beforehand in making this comparison. Specializing small variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_h\) to zero gives the weighted cut polynomial of the skeleton: removing any nonheavy edge produces a nonempty fragment on its small side, so no such cut survives.

## Mass order

A skeleton one-edge split has smaller side \(<2s\) precisely when one of its sides is a singleton skeleton vertex. For a path on at least three vertices, there are exactly two such splits. Thus any rival skeleton is a path too. If there are only two blocks, its shape is already forced.

The weighted cut polynomial of a path with composition \(\eta\) of vertex orders is the L-polynomial of that composition: each spanning subforest is exactly the merger of adjacent intervals of the composition. The [Billera–Thomas–van Willigenburg L-equivalence theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405434) says equivalent compositions arise by reversing any of the irreducible composition-product factors. A two-factor decomposition \(\eta=\alpha\circ\beta\) with \(\beta\) of length at least two and some \(\alpha_i>1\) creates a part \(\beta_{\rm first}+\beta_{\rm last}\) at a near-concatenation, and still retains the two intact first and last parts at the two outer endpoints. This violates the narrow band \([s,2s-1]\). Consequently \(\eta\) has at most one nonsymmetric irreducible factor: if there were two, split the product just after the first nonsymmetric factor. The left composition then contains a part \(>1\), because this factor contains such a part, and the composition product never loses it except by merging it into a larger part. The right composition has length at least two because it contains the later nonsymmetric factor, and the product length is no smaller than either operand length. This is forbidden. Reversing the possible sole nonsymmetric factor therefore coincides with reversal of the whole word, since reversals commute with the composition product. This recovers the mass order and allows the two paths to be aligned.

## Low deficits and a propagation rule

Fix the oriented mass order of total order \(N\). Let \(J\subset(0,N)\) be the proper prefix masses. Define \(p_u={\bf1}_{u\in J}\) and \(q_u={\bf1}_{N-u\in J}\). A proper cut mass is paired if \(p_u=q_u=1\); it is asymmetric if \(p_u+q_u=1\). The word is nonpalindromic exactly when some proper cut mass is asymmetric. Let \(P_u\) and \(Q_u\) be the products of the normalized rooted cut series on the prefix and suffix of mass \(u\), respectively, where the relevant segment exists. Write \(T\) for their product over the whole path; all these series have constant term 1.

For any \(1\le k\le d\), a cut term with total small-fragment mass \(k\) and specified large factors has all roots in the large components. Indeed if a root is small, its whole original block is contained in small components, since any component reached from that block without its root stays inside the block and away from the skeleton. This would use at least \(g>k\) vertices of small-fragment mass. Consequently the high components are intervals corresponding to a cut of the weighted path, and each has intact mass a multiple of \(g\). For large factors \(x_{u-k}x_{N-u}\), the first component lost all \(k\) and the second is intact, as indicated by divisibility and total deficit \(<g\). Thus the small-variable polynomial coefficient of these high factors gives \([t^k](p_uP_u+q_uQ_u)\), with absent terms zero. Also the high factor \(x_{N-k}\) gives \([t^k]T\). The bound on \(s\) makes all shortened present interval orders \(>h\), and a marked factor cannot coincide with an intact factor.

Compare a \(U\)-equal rival with the same aligned mass order, using primed notation. If the individual block series differ through \(d\), let \(e\le d\) be the first degree at which one of them differs. Then all interval products agree through degree \(e-1\). At an asymmetric mass the sole prefix/suffix product agrees through \(e\) by the two-part extraction. At a paired mass put \(\delta_u=[t^e](P'_u-P_u)\); the same extraction implies \([t^e](Q'_u-Q_u)=-\delta_u\). Put \(\delta_u=0\) at all other masses, including \(0\) modulo \(N\). Since \(P_{N-u}=T/Q_u\) whenever \(Q_u\) exists and each series has constant term 1, one has \(\delta_{N-u}=\delta_u\) (also at asymmetric or missing masses). Not all \(\delta_u\) can be zero: otherwise all proper prefix products agree along with \(T\), then division between successive products determines the individual block series through \(e\).

Take any paired \(a\) and asymmetric \(b\) with \(a+b<N\), and let \(c=N-a-b\). The available prefix/suffix placement of \(a,b\) has a nonempty interval between them, so \(c\ge s\). Examine the coefficient with exactly three high factors \(x_a x_b x_{c-e}\) and all other factors low; the component of intact mass \(c\) is the only one shortened. The mark is distinguishable from each intact component by divisibility even if \(c=b\). Also \(a\ne b\). If \(c=a\), the desired conclusion \(\delta_a=\delta_c\) is tautological, so omit that case. For endpoint placement of the marked \(c\)-component on the left, there are \(p_c(q_a+q_b)\) ordered possibilities, and the coefficient difference at degree \(e\) from each is \([t^e](P'_c-P_c)\). For right placement there are \(q_c(p_a+p_b)\) possibilities with difference \([t^e](Q'_c-Q_c)\). Indeed, one chooses which intact component is at the opposite endpoint; because all three lengths are positive, the two endpoint cuts then exist and are distinct exactly when both endpoint masses are available on their indicated sides. These are all endpoint placements even when the marked \(c\) and one unmarked mass are equal, since the different roles label the segments.

The combined endpoint difference is \((q_b-p_b)\delta_c\): if \(c\) is paired use \(p_a=q_a=p_c=q_c=1\), and otherwise each available \(c\)-series has zero difference. If the marked component is in the middle, there is exactly one placement, as \(a\) is paired and \(b\) is asymmetric. When \(p_b=1\) its product is \(T/(P_bQ_a)\), and the degree \(e\) difference is \(+\delta_a\). When \(q_b=1\) its product is \(T/(P_aQ_b)\), with difference \(-\delta_a\). Therefore equality of the marked coefficient gives
\[
(p_b-q_b)(\delta_a-\delta_c)=0,\qquad\text{so }\delta_a=\delta_c.
\]

## The narrow band excludes any flip

For any asymmetric \(b\), a nonzero \(\delta_a\) stays unchanged on translation by \(b\) modulo \(N\). If \(a+b<N\), the propagation rule and reflection give \(\delta_{a+b}=\delta_a\). If \(a+b>N\), apply the rule to \(N-a,N-b\), which are again paired and asymmetric respectively, giving \(\delta_{a+b-N}=\delta_{N-a}=\delta_a\). Equality \(a+b=N\) is impossible because the complement of an asymmetric mass is asymmetric. Repeated positive translations in a finite group also realize their negatives, so a nonzero value is constant on its coset modulo
\[
G=\gcd(N,\{b:p_b+q_b=1\}).
\]

In fact \(G<2s\). Otherwise pick an asymmetric prefix cut \(b\in J\), so \(N-b\notin J\). In \(J\cup\{0,N\}\), its immediate predecessor \(u\) and successor \(v\) are each at distance between \(s\) and \(2s-1\). Neither can be asymmetric as every asymmetric mass is a multiple of \(G\). Endpoints are considered symmetric here. Reflect these two neighboring points about \(N/2\): \(N-v,N-u\) are present and bracket the absent \(N-b\). There can be no asymmetric prefix between them, because the only multiple of \(G\) in that open interval is \(N-b\) itself. Any symmetric prefix strictly between them reflects to a prefix strictly between \(u,v\) other than \(b\), also impossible. This produces a consecutive gap of length \(v-u\ge2s\), contradicting the mass band.

A nonzero coset modulo \(G\) now contains an integer at distance at most \(G/2<s\) from \(b\), which lies strictly inside \((0,N)\) because \(b\) is at least \(s\) from both endpoints. It is not \(b\), as \(b\) has zero difference. Since the coset is nonzero at every element, this integer is a paired prefix cut, contradicting the minimum consecutive prefix spacing \(s\). This rules out all first differences through \(d\).

At \(h=2,d=1\), knowing block mass and the number of its nonroot leaves recovers its number of single leaves and two-vertex arms. At general \(h\), recovering degree \(D_h\) of the normalized rooted cut series suffices by the [bounded-branch logarithmic derivative argument](StanleyLeafTaggedHeavySkeleton.md#2-deficit-marks-recover-all-the-specified-block-data). This proves the theorem.

**Example.** Every nonpalindromic word of block orders in \(\{6,8,10\}\) has the theorem's full-tree reconstruction at cutoff \(2\): take \(g=2,d=1,s=6\). Each order occurrence can independently use an arbitrary mixture of single leaves and two-vertex arms, permitting arbitrary repetitions of indistinguishable masses or rooted types. The spine can be arbitrarily long. No tags are needed. This result does not settle unrestricted diameter-six reconstruction or remove the mass band.
