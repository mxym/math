# Unrestricted three-uniform internal sunflower extraction

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

**Theorem.** every nonempty finite simple 3-uniform family H has a subfamily F of size Ω(|H|/m³) in which every intersection of two distinct F-edges is the kernel of an m-petal sunflower contained in F. The constants are absolute. This is the k=3 case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). This is not a statement about larger k. For sharpness, the complete tripartite family with m−1 vertices in each part admits no m-petal sunflower at any possible kernel, yet has (m−1)³ edges, so F has size at most one.

Notation: a “nonempty-valid” family internally witnesses all its nonempty pairwise intersections. The “AC fiber” of (a,c) consists of its triples, with weight the number of triples in the relevant host. The total top-K mass on A (respectively C) of a weighted AC-support graph is the sum, over vertices of the named side, of their K largest incident fiber sizes, or all if fewer; ties arbitrary. At any time the edges at one vertex with fewer than m support neighbors have total mass at most its original top-m mass. A charge at a vertex, fiber, or pair is incurred only when it is removed entirely, so the same object is never charged twice.

## 1. Established auxiliary results

We use three existing solved cases from the research branch, not as claims of this note.

1. [Weighted simple bipartite graph nonempty lemma](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/a7c42a1a2de5106b860e163bd8e1c5bc1a820892/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/InternalSunflowersLowDegeneracy.md): retain Ω(1/m) of the edge weight with each positive degree 1 or at least m; and its unweighted full-kernel variant retains Ω(1/m²).
2. [Bounded coordinate](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/a7c42a1a2de5106b860e163bd8e1c5bc1a820892/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/InternalSunflowersCappedFrontier.md#sharp-degeneracy-range-up-to-order-m2): if one coordinate degree is O(m²), get Ω(E/m³) full witnessing, with constant depending only on that O constant. No codegree hypothesis.
3. [Two injective projections](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/a7c42a1a2de5106b860e163bd8e1c5bc1a820892/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/TwoInjectiveInternalSunflowers.md): if two projections are injective and the remaining projection has codegree at most m, get Ω(E/m²) nonempty witnessing.

## 2. Two capped projections, with no restriction on the third

Assume AB and BC codegrees ≤D, and D≤L m for any fixed absolute L. The claim here is Ω(E/m³) nonempty witnessing (constant may depend on L). Put M=max(D,m), and split the AC fibers at 4096Dm. Whole fibers in the light piece have size <4096Dm. Partition each light AC fiber into ≤m groups of size ≤4096D. Form a conflict graph on triples joining equal AB pairs, equal BC pairs, or triples from the same AC subgroup. Its maximum degree is at most 2(D−1)+(4096D−1). A largest color class has Ω(E/D) edges, injective AB and BC, and at most one per AC subgroup, hence AC codegree ≤m. Use auxiliary result 3. This gives Ω(E/(D m²))≥Ω(E/m³). We treat the two pieces separately and take the better output.

For the wide piece every AC fiber weight f is ≥4096Dm, with no ratio restriction. In each probabilistic subcase below first inspect B vertices of degree <65536mM in that subcase's host. If they carry at least a quarter of the triples, the bounded-coordinate theorem applies to their edges. Otherwise discard these B vertices, retaining at least 3E/4 triples, where E refers to the subcase's original mass. The number of (initially) high B vertices is at most E/(65536mM); after any sample, the total number of triples lost by iteratively deleting all edges at B vertices of current positive degree <m is ≤E/(65536M). For every remaining argument, original counts and original fiber sizes refer to the subcase before discarding low B labels; no high degree or original wide fiber lower bound is asserted on the immediate post-discard residue.

**Diffuse on both endpoints.** If the sum of the two top-m masses is ≤E/64, sample triples independently with probability p=1/(16D). Delete all sampled triples with any other sampled triple at their AB or BC pair. Also discard the sampled triples of a fiber if its sample count exceeds 4pf (where f is its original weight). Conditional on a triple sampled, conflict probability is ≤2(D−1)p, and the cap-rejection probability is ≤(1+pf)/(4pf) by Markov; their sum is <1/2. Retained expectation ≥3pE/8. Iteratively delete active B of degree <m, occupied AC pairs of size <m, and active A or C vertices with fewer than m positive support neighbors. For the first type the budget above is ≤pE/4096. For the second the budget is at most m times the original number of fibers, ≤E/(4096D)=pE/256. For the third the cap and the top-m assumption give ≤4p E/64=pE/16. Thus Ω(pE) survives in expectation. Its AB and BC are injective; any repeated AC pair has m completions; B links are actual matchings of size ≥m; at an A vertex choose m distinct active C neighbors, greedily choosing a new B from each fiber, and symmetrically at C. All nonempty kernels are witnessed.

**Concentrated on an endpoint.** Otherwise one side, say A, has top-m mass >E/128. Restrict to its top m whole AC fibers per vertex (change notation E to the mass of this subcase's new host). Every A support degree is now ≤m. Put p=1/(16M). If the C top-m mass is ≥pE/32, keep just the top m whole fibers per C. On this new graph both support degrees are ≤m. Bipartite edge-coloring of AC into m matchings, keeping the largest in fiber weight, retains ≥pE/(32m). The corresponding graph of individual triples as edges between pair nodes AC and B is simple. Apply auxiliary result 1's nonempty lemma to retain Ω(pE/m²)=Ω(E/m³). Different pair nodes have disjoint A and disjoint C; hence each nonempty kernel is either an AC pair or a singleton B, and is witnessed by the graph degree condition.

If instead the C top-m mass is <pE/32, use the low-B test/discard with the present bounded-A host. Independently at each A choose at most one whole AC fiber, each with marginal probability p (since mp≤1/16). Keep triples in chosen fibers and delete all sharing a BC pair with another chosen triple. Conditional on a triple's fiber chosen, each BC rival has a different A label; the union bound costs ≤(D−1)p. AB is injective automatically since each A is monochromatic. Retained expectation ≥(3/4)(15/16)pE≥pE/2. Iteratively delete low positive B degrees, low AC pair degrees, and low positive C support degrees, all at threshold m. Expected losses are respectively ≤E/(65536M)=pE/4096, ≤mp(E/(4096Dm))≤pE/4096 (only chosen AC fibers can be charged), and <pE/32 (charge C to its original top-m mass, no scaling by the sampling probability needed). Thus Ω(pE) survives. A cannot be a singleton kernel since monochromatic; B has a matching link; repeated AC pairs and singleton Cs have witnesses just as above. This proves the two-capped claim.

## 3. One capped projection: soft budget on sampled top fibers

Assume AB is capped at m. For any current host in this section, run the following clean-up before sampling or choosing hypotheses. Iteratively peel occupied AC or BC pairs of current degree <64m, assigning each removed edge to the deleting pair type. Each of the two charged pieces has AB capped at m and another projection capped at 64m, because the charge at each given pair occurs only once and below threshold. If at least half the edges are removed, the larger charged piece gives Ω(E/m³) by §2 with L=64. Otherwise take the residual of size ≥E/2, in which all occupied AC and BC fibers are of size ≥64m. Any pre-existing upper bound on support degrees persists in the residual and in later subhosts.

A side A or B is called locked if its support degrees into C are ≤ its stage-specific bound (specified below). During a sample, a locked vertex keeps edges only if it sees exactly one **sampled original C neighbor**, so it is monochromatic; the rule acts before other deletions. An unlocked vertex has no such restriction, but later we peel it if it is positive and has fewer than m current C neighbors.

For a cleaned host of size E, sample entire C labels independently at probability p. Conditional on a particular edge being at a sampled C, deleting every AB-conflicting edge costs ≤(m−1)p by the union bound, and every lock at an endpoint of support at most K costs ≤(K−1)p. We may independently define AB conflicts on the initial color-sampled triples, before locks, and delete the union of all forbidden edges. Our choices always give p≤1/(64m) and pK≤1/64 for each lock, so expected initial retained mass is ≥(1−3/64)pE. It is AB-injective, and all locked vertices are monochromatic.

Iteratively delete any occupied AC or BC pair with current degree <m and any unlocked A/B vertex with positive support degree <m. These rules can be interleaved in any order. Pair peeling costs at most m times the number of AC/BC pairs whose C was sampled. The original #pairs of each type is ≤E/(64m), so expected total pair loss is ≤2pE/64. For an unlocked side V, its total peeling loss is bounded by the sum of the top-m **sampled original** fiber weights at its vertices, regardless of the earlier deletions and peeling dynamics. For any K, this latter sum has expectation at most
\[
p\,\mathrm{Top}_K(V)+\frac mK E.
\]
Indeed, the part of a top-m sampled list from the original top-K list has expectation at most p times the entire top-K weight. The other entries each weigh at most the vertex's original total weight divided by K, and there are at most m. Sum over V. No independence of the lists at different vertices is used, and each fiber is active with probability p even though fibers sharing a color have the same sampling event. Therefore if \(\mathrm{Top}_K(V)\le E/64\) and \(K\ge64m/p\), its expected peeling loss is ≤2pE/64. Thus if all applicable budgets are met, Ω(pE) survives.

The stages are as follows. Start with p0=1/(64m), no locks, and K0=ceil(64m/p0)=4096m². Clean the host as above. If both top-K0 masses are ≤E/64, perform the sample at p0. Otherwise restrict to the top K0 whole fibers per vertex on a concentrated side, which retains >E/64 and gives a support bound K0 on that side. Clean again (if the viable answer comes from the charged pieces, stop); the cap K0 survives and this side becomes locked. Put p1=1/(64K0), K1=ceil(64m/p1)=4096mK0. If the unlocked side's top-K1 mass is ≤E/64, perform the sample at p1. Otherwise restrict to its top K1 whole fibers (retaining >E/64), clean again, and lock that side too. Now perform the sample at p2=1/(64K1); no unlocked budgets remain. The number of restrictions is at most two, and cleanups either stop with a two-capped solution or lose only a constant factor. The final p is always Ω(m^-3).

The standing properties of the final residue imply nonempty internal witnesses. Every repeated pair is AC or BC with at least m completions because AB is injective. For an unlocked A vertex, choose m distinct current C neighbors, one triple each; the B endpoints are distinct by AB injectivity, giving an m-matching in its link. Same for B. Locked vertices cannot occur as singleton intersections because they are monochromatic. At each C, its simple bipartite AB-link has positive degrees at least m on both sides by the AC/BC fiber thresholds, and hence a matching of size at least m: a maximal smaller matching would leave an uncovered vertex on either side while all its ≥m neighbors would have to be covered on the other. Hence all nonempty kernels have internal witnesses. This proves the one-capped Ω(E/m³) nonempty claim.

## 4. Unrestricted and empty intersection

Take a random tripartition, preserving at least 2/9 of edges. Treat this tripartite family as the original host for both the nonempty argument and the subsequent empty-kernel restoration; its vertex links are bipartite. To obtain a nonempty-valid family, iteratively peel any occupied pair in any projection of current degree <m; charge to its type. The residual is already nonempty-valid because occupied pair fibers have at least m completions and every nonempty vertex link is bipartite of positive minimum degree ≥m, giving the required matching. If residual is small, choose the largest of the three charged pieces. One projection is capped at m−1, so §3 applies. This gives Ω(E/m³) nonempty for every tripartite host, and hence for every simple 3-uniform input.

To handle the empty kernel, if there is an m-matching whose endpoint removal leaves at least E/2 edges, apply the nonempty result to that residual, and restore the m disjoint triples. They supply an empty-kernel witness while introducing no nonempty intersection with the residual or each other. Otherwise either some existing m-matching removes ≥E/2 and then some vertex has degree ≥E/(6m), or no m-matching exists and a maximal matching with fewer than m edges covers the host, again giving such a high-degree vertex. Apply the unweighted full graph lemma (auxiliary result 1) to its simple link, then restore the fixed vertex to all selected edges. Any nonempty graph kernel lifts by adding the fixed vertex; a realized empty graph kernel lifts to the singleton fixed vertex, and no pair of output triples is disjoint. This keeps Ω(E/m³), completing the claim.
