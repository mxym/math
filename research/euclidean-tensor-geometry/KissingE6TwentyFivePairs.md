# The E6 complete-line branch cannot have 25 antipodal pairs

**Conditional theorem.** A 73-point six-dimensional kissing code containing an oriented representative of each of the 36 lines of an E6 root system has at most 24 antipodal pairs. This does not improve the unconditional kissing-number bound.

Use the setup and exact-five blocker capacity lemma in [the preceding boundary result](KissingExceptionalBoundaryPairs.md). Scale the vectors to squared norm two. A blocker of an outsider is a signed root whose inner product with it is strictly greater than one. Every outsider has at least five blockers, all among the opposites of singly represented original axes. There are no outsider antipodal pairs, by the same signed-blocker observation. The **negative graph** joins outsiders whose inner product is strictly less than minus one. Two endpoints cannot share a signed blocker: the squared norm of the sum of the two outsiders and the negative blocker would then be strictly negative. Likewise the negative graph is triangle-free by summing its three vertices.

## Exact-six masks and mixed masks

For a fixed **signed** six-blocker mask, at most one outsider is possible. Furthermore an exact-five mask and an exact-six mask containing it cannot both occur. The [BigInt checker](verify-kissing-e6-25-pairs.js) certifies these geometric facts as follows.

Move a vector to its fundamental Weyl chamber. The blockers are an upward ideal in the positive-root order. For each size-six ideal the checker enumerates all vertices of the closed rational chamber cell using Cramer's rule with all nonsingular active bases, checking boundedness and all inequalities. There are three ideals; each cell has seven vertices. In two cases there is exactly one vertex U above squared norm two. Write the other vertices as V_i. For each edge U to V_i choose P_i=t_i U+(1-t_i)V_i, with t_i the largest grid point in {0,1/96,...,1} giving squared norm at most two. In particular t_i<1. The section of the barycentric simplex given by λ_U=Σ_i λ_i t_i/(1-t_i) has exactly the edge points P_i as generators. The portion below that section is generated by the V_i and P_i. All of these have squared norm at most two. By strict convexity of squared norm, a sphere point below the section would have to be one of the original V_i of squared norm two; the sole such V_i is the highest root H, whose cutoff can be checked to occur at t_i=0. Thus every sphere point is in the convex hull of U and the P_i (including H). In the third cell the squared norm bound holds at every vertex; only H and one other rational vertex have squared norm two, so the sphere slice is contained in those two vertices.

For fixed six-mask comparisons, the two full-rank chamber ideals are diagram reversals and the rank-five ideal is distinct. Reduce vectors of a given mask to the same chamber ideal using Weyl and, if required, diagram symmetries. The relative isometry induces a permutation preserving the Gram matrix of the six roots. The checker overenumerates all such permutations, determining the image of a generator by its products against a basis among the six roots, and additionally compares both orthogonal signs for the rank-five ideal. These checks compare 588, 128, and 588 generator pairs in the respective cells. Every cosine is at least 1/2, with equality only when both generators are highest-root rays before applying their relative isometry.

For containment of a five-mask in a six-mask, the five roots must have Gram I+J by the previous boundary lemma. The six-ideal census gives exactly two such inclusions, related by the diagram. On the span of the shared five roots, products and norms are determined by their sorted product lists a with Gram inverse I−J/6. In the one-dimensional orthogonal complement the checker uses both possible relative signs. Thus for each pair of generators it suffices to oppose the sorted lists and the orthogonal signs. The same closed outer hull for the five-mask is used as in the previous boundary proof. Each enumeration gives 84 comparisons, all with cosine at least 1/2 and only the highest-root pair giving equality. All comparisons use integer dot products and squared norms.

These positive cosine inequalities extend from generators to points in their convex hulls by bilinearity and the triangle inequality. Non-root points require at least one non-root generator, giving a strict inequality and hence incompatibility in a kissing code.

## Exhaustion of the 11 signed roots and conclusion

At exactly 25 pairs there are 11 singly represented axes, and 12 outsiders off the 36 original axes. Every negative edge consists of disjoint masks within those 11 signed roots, so its endpoint sizes are (5,5) or (5,6). All its masks are drawn from 432 signed five-masks and 15,120 signed six-masks, obtained by simple reflections and diagram reversal of the chamber ideals above. The same-mask capacity makes them distinct across all participating vertices in a kissing code.

To check whether two edges can have four distinct endpoints, use one edge and move its five-mask to the canonical representative (there is one signed orbit). Enumerate the second endpoint from all axis-disjoint five- and six-masks. A five-five union is augmented by each possible signed root on an unused axis, whereas a five-six union already has size eleven. The checker deduplicates all 14,260 signed universes, enumerates their five- and six-subsets, and joins every pair of disjoint admissible masks. In 1,200 of these universes the mask graph has two vertex-disjoint edges, but in **every** pair of disjoint edges at least one mask contains a mask on the other edge. By the mixed-mask incompatibility such a matching in the negative graph is impossible. The matching number is therefore at most one. A triangle-free graph of matching number at most one has an empty edge set or a common endpoint of all edges.

Delete that endpoint (or an arbitrary outsider if there is no edge). The 11 remaining outsiders now give independent unoriented lines together with one representative of each of the 25 true pairs: all absolute products are at most one in our normalization, and outsiders are not on original axes. By [Anstreicher's antipodal uniqueness theorem](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/) these 36 lines are precisely an E6 system. It shares exactly 25 axes with the original. Common roots form a simply-laced root system closed under reflections in its roots. No ADE system of rank at most six has 50 roots. Indeed the irreducible systems of rank at most five have at most 40, and at rank six the irreducible counts are 42, 60 and 72; reducible systems have at most 42. This contradicts the 25 pairs. The cases above 25 were excluded in the preceding boundary result.
