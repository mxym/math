# Exact blocker-pattern capacity and sharp blocker range for E6

Let Φ be E6 with roots of squared norm two and let x in its real span satisfy ||x||²=2. Define the **signed blocker pattern** B(x)={r∈Φ : r·x>1}. If x is not a root, then

1. 5≤|B(x)|≤16, with both endpoints attained;
2. in a kissing configuration (mutual inner products ≤1), distinct non-root points cannot have the same signed blocker pattern.

There is no antipodal-pair count hypothesis. This is a local geometry statement, not a solution of the unrestricted kissing problem. The [previous signed cover proof](KissingExceptionalRootCoverBranch.md) gives the sharp lower endpoint. The upper endpoint and extension of capacity to every mask are proved here.

## Upper endpoint with short dual certificates

Move x to a fundamental chamber and use simple inner products w_i=α_i·x≥0. If a positive root has simple coefficients r, the actual inner product is r·w using the usual coordinate dot product. Hence the strict blocker set is an upward ideal in the positive-root order. Any ideal of size at least seventeen contains a seventeen-root upward subideal by successive removal of minimal roots.

There are 25 upward ideals C of size seventeen. Consider the closed inequalities w_i≥0 and r·w≥1 for the minimal roots r in C, written A w≥b. Here ||x||²=wᵀG⁻¹w for the simple-root Gram matrix G. For every C, the verifier finds a linearly independent selection J of at most six constraint rows with K=A_J G A_Jᵀ, c=K⁻¹ b_J coordinatewise nonnegative, and b_Jᵀc≥2. For any feasible w, Cauchy–Schwarz in the G⁻¹ norm gives
```
wᵀ G⁻¹ w ≥ (cᵀA_J w)²/(cᵀKc) ≥ b_Jᵀc ≥ 2.
```
If the blocker inequalities are strict, slightly rescaling w toward zero keeps all of them feasible, so ||x||² cannot be two. Thus no seventeen-root blocker ideal occurs. For sharpness take simple-product vector proportional to e₄ in the zero-based numbering of the code and normalize to squared norm two: exactly 16 roots have product >1 (checked by comparing squared integers).

## A uniqueness fact for signed chamber ideals

For an upward ideal C of positive roots in a fixed chamber, set s=Σ_{r∈C}r. For each simple reflection s_i, partition Φ into its pairs {r,s_i r} and fixed roots. In each nontrivial pair the larger root differs from the smaller by a positive multiple of α_i, contributing positively to s·α_i if it alone is in C. There are no pairs for which only the smaller belongs to C: positive-root pairs follow from upward closure; negative pairs contribute nothing; in {α_i,−α_i} only the positive root may occur. Thus s lies in the closed dominant chamber, and s·α_i=0 precisely when s_i preserves C.

If two such ideals C,D are Weyl-conjugate, their sums have the same dominant representative and hence are equal. The stabilizer of a vector in the closed dominant chamber in a finite Weyl group is generated by reflections in its chamber walls containing that vector. These reflections all preserve C, so D=C. The only additional identifications under the full E6 root-system automorphism group come from diagram symmetry. The verifier also checks the dominance and simple-reflection characterization explicitly for all the chamber ideals it uses.

## One point per signed pattern

For sizes 5 to 7 use the previous [five/six](KissingE6TwentyFivePairs.md) and [seven-mask](KissingE6SevenBlockers.md) results. For each upward ideal from sizes 8 to 16, enumerate all vertices of the closed chamber cell using active bases, and construct a rational hull containing its norm-two slice by the [multiple-high-vertex cut](KissingE6SevenBlockers.md). If a chamber coordinate had no upper constraint from a complementary root, impose its valid bound w_i≤2 by Cauchy–Schwarz. This also handles the cells first unbounded at size 16. Compare the generator pairs under every Gram-preserving permutation of the signed blockers, including either orthogonal sign for a one-dimensional complement. Every Gram isometry needed for two chamber points with the same mask is included: the preceding dominant-sum argument reduces to the same ideal (using diagram symmetry when necessary).

In each comparison the two generator rays have cosine at least 1/2. Equality for two positive combinations can hold only if every contributing generator pair has equality **and** all nonzero generators used on each side are positively collinear, by equality in the triangle inequalities. Thus it suffices to check, for every generator pair realizing equality, that the two rays after normalizing to squared norm two do not both have blocker mask C in their original chambers. The verifier does this by exact root-product comparisons. At size 16 this extra check matters: the ray with simple products proportional to (1,0,0,0,1,0) appears in equality comparisons but has only eight actual blockers, not sixteen. Consequently two genuine outsiders with exactly the same signed mask have inner product strictly above one and cannot coexist.
