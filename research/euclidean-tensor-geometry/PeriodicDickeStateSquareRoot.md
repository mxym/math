# Square-root bond dimension for fixed-weight periodic Dicke states

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal verification; no external peer review claimed.

For the exact \(n\)-site Dicke state of fixed weight \(k\ge1\), let \(d_k(n)\) be the least bond dimension of a single-site uniform matrix-product representation closed by a trace, over \(\mathbb C\), with no separate boundary matrix. Then
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_k(n)}{\sqrt n}\ge 1,\qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_k(n)}{\sqrt n}\le \sqrt{k}.
\]
Thus the growth order is \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) for every fixed \(k\). For \(k=1\) the constants agree with [the W-state result](PeriodicWStateOptimalAsymptotics.md); for \(k>1\) neither a leading constant nor exact finite dimensions are determined here.

A general finite bound is \(n\le kD^2\), as in [the W-state proof](PeriodicWStateOptimalAsymptotics.md#trace-lower-bound). The stronger fixed-weight asymptotic lower bound is proved below. Uniform open-boundary dimension \(k+1\) and a conversion to periodic dimension \(n(k+1)\) are discussed by [Raveh–Nepomechie, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04729#S3); their open-boundary value is not in this same model. [The MPS-under-permutations paper, §VIII and Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19541v2#S8) discusses the earlier periodic lower estimate \(\Omega(n^{1/(3+\delta)})\). This problem is distinct from asymptotic tensor subrank and from approximate MPS.

## A stronger lower bound for fixed weight

In fact, if \(D\ge k\), any exact representation obeys \(n\le D^2+k-1\). Use the notation \(P,N,r,s,\pi\) and \(\phi(X)=\operatorname{tr}_P(A_P^n X|_P)\) from [the W-state proof](PeriodicWStateOptimalAsymptotics.md). If \(r=0\), the clustered word \(A^{n-k}B^k\) already gives \(n\le D+k-1\). Suppose \(r>0\). Consider \(U_t\subset\operatorname{Hom}(W,P)\) spanned by \(A_P^{-t}\pi M_w\) for all length-\(t\) words containing at least one \(B\), without recording the count. As before, prepending \(A\) gives nesting, and appending \(A,B\) gives the time-independent recurrence with source \(A_P^{-1}\pi B\). The space therefore stabilizes by \(t_*=rD\). Write \(U=U_{t_*}\), let \(E\) be the right-\(P\) Fitting projector for \(T_A(X)=A_P^{-1}XA\), and identify \(E(U)\) with its \(P\times P\) matrices. The earlier argument gives \(J\subset E(U)\), where \(J\) is the positive-word ideal of the unital algebra generated by all conjugates of \(B_{PP}A_P^{-1}\) under \(X\mapsto A_P^{-1}XA_P\).

Choose the fixed word \(M=B A^s B\cdots A^s B\) with \(k\) copies of \(B\), of length \(L=k+(k-1)s\). Its \(P\times P\) block is computed on \(P\) alone, because the internal gaps kill \(N\). Therefore \(Q=(\pi M|_P)A_P^{-L}\) is a product of \(k\) of the conjugate generators and belongs to \(J\) (right normalization differs from left normalization by conjugation). Assume \(n\ge rD+s+L\). Appending \(M\) to any word defining \(U\) and padding with \(A^{n-t_*-L}\) on the left yields a length-\(n\) word with more than \(k\) copies of \(B\). Its trace is zero and, by left padding, equals \(\phi(A_P^{-L}XM)\) for the corresponding \(X\). For \(X\in E(U)\) this equals \(\phi(XQ)\), by cyclicity on \(P\). In particular \(\phi(JQ)=0\).

Let \(R\) be the Jacobson radical of the conjugate-generated unital algebra. The image of \(J\) in the semisimple quotient has a unit of its own, so \(Q\in JQ+R\). The same radical argument as before gives \(\phi(R)=0\), thus \(\phi(Q)=0\). But \(\phi(Q)=\operatorname{tr}(A^{n-L}M)\) since \(n-L\ge s\), and this word has exactly \(k\) copies of \(B\); its trace is required to be nonzero, a contradiction. We obtain
\[
n\le rD+s+L-1=rD+ks+k-1\le D^2+k-1
\]
when \(D\ge k\) and \(s\le D-r\). For fixed \(k\), \(D\ge k\) eventually follows from \(n\le kD^2\). This proves the stated lower liminf.

## Fourier-weighted cyclic construction

Fix positive integers \(p,m\) with \(k<p\), such that
\[
(p,n)=(p,k)=1,\qquad n\equiv k(m+1)\pmod p,\qquad km<p,\qquad n<(p+k)(m+1).
\]
Write \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/p)\) and \(P(z)=\prod_{u=0}^{m-1}(z-\zeta^u)\). On the states \(x=0,\ldots,p-1\), let \(A\) take the forward step \(x\mapsto x+1\bmod p\) with weight one. Let \(B\) take the backward step \(x\mapsto x-m\) without wrapping, with weight \(P(\zeta^x)\), and have zero row for \(x<m\). Equivalently it takes the backward step modulo \(p\) everywhere but with the indicated weights (which vanish at those forbidden starts).

A nonzero closed walk on a word with \(h\) copies of \(B\) satisfies
\[
n=(m+1)h+p\ell,\qquad \ell\ge0,
\]
where \(\ell\) counts the wraparounds of the forward \(A\)-steps. The coprimality and congruence assumptions imply \((m+1,p)=1\), hence \(h\equiv k\pmod p\). The length inequality forces \(h<p+k\). Since \(0<k<p\), only \(h=k\) can contribute.

For a word with exactly \(k\) copies of \(B\), starting at \(x\) gives a closed modular walk independently of \(x\). At the \(j\)-th copy of \(B\), its position is \(x+d_j\bmod p\) for a shift \(d_j\) depending on the word but not on \(x\). The trace is exactly
\[
\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}\prod_{j=1}^k P(\zeta^{d_j}\zeta^x).
\]
The summand is evaluation at \(\zeta^x\) of a polynomial of degree \(km<p\), so the sum is \(p\) times its constant coefficient, namely \(pP(0)^k\ne 0\). This is independent of all spacings of the excitations. Rescale \(B\) by a \(k\)-th root of the desired common amplitude divided by \(pP(0)^k\).

## Parameters exist uniformly

Fix any rational \(c=a/b>k\) with positive integers \(a,b\), and pick a real \(\gamma\) with \(k/c<\gamma<1\). Let \(q_0\) be the least positive integer congruent to \(n\bmod k\) with \(cq_0^2\ge n\), and \(p_0\) the least integer congruent to \(1\bmod ka\) with \(p_0\ge cq_0\). Then \(q_0=\sqrt{n/c}+O_k(1)\), \(p_0=\sqrt{cn}+O_{k,c}(1)\), and \(p_0q_0-n=O_{k,c}(\sqrt n)\) is nonnegative. Set
\[
p=p_0+ka j,\qquad q=q_0-kb j,\qquad
R(j)=n-pq=n-p_0q_0+kj(bp_0-aq_0)+k^2ab j^2.
\]
Here \(bp_0-aq_0\) is nonnegative and bounded independently of \(n\), and \(R(j)\) is always divisible by \(k\). The least \(j_0\ge0\) with \(R(j_0)\ge \gamma p\) is \(O_{k,c}(n^{1/4})\), by the displayed quadratic. It is positive, and the overshoot is \(O_{k,c}(n^{1/4})\), since that bounds the last increments.

Now choose \(j\) in the next \(2\cdot4^{\omega(n)}\) consecutive possibilities starting at \(j_0\) such that \((p,n)=1\). Every prime dividing the step \(ka\) is automatically avoided by \(p_0\equiv1\bmod ka\); for every other prime divisor of \(n\), at most one residue class of \(j\) is prohibited. Inclusion–exclusion counts at least \(L2^{-\omega(n)}-2^{\omega(n)}>0\) allowed choices in an interval of \(L=2\cdot4^{\omega(n)}\) integers. Here \(L=n^{o(1)}\), since the product of \(v\) distinct primes is at least \((v+1)!\). It follows that \(p=\sqrt{cn}+O_{k,c}(n^{1/4+o(1)})\), \(q\sim p/c\), and \(R(j)=\gamma p+o(p)\).

Set \(m+1=R(j)/k\). For large \(n\), \(m\ge1\), \(km<p\) because \(\gamma<1\), and \(n<(p+k)(m+1)\) because \(\gamma c/k>1\). The remaining conditions follow from the definitions and coprimality. Thus \(d_k(n)\le \sqrt{cn}+O_{k,c}(n^{1/4+o(1)})\) for every fixed rational \(c>k\), proving the limsup bound.

## A boundary-removal lemma

A separate construction gives the weaker constant \(k+1\) but works for any cyclically invariant amplitude with a uniform boundary representation. Suppose a length-\(n\) amplitude is \(\operatorname{tr}(Q M_{i_1}\cdots M_{i_n})\) with \(d\)-dimensional matrices and is invariant under cyclic rotation. Take unnormalized W-state trace matrices \(P_0,P_1\) of size \(d_1(n)\) and define
\[
\widetilde M_i=P_0\otimes M_i+\frac1n\,P_1\otimes M_i Q.
\]
Expanding a trace over \(n\) factors, the W-state trace vanishes unless there is exactly one \(P_1\). Each of the \(n\) surviving terms gives \(1/n\) times the original amplitude by cyclic rotation, so this removes the boundary with dimension \(d\,d_1(n)\). If the original amplitude is not cyclically invariant, the same construction gives its cyclic average. For Dicke, take \(M_0=I\), \(M_1\) a nilpotent Jordan shift of size \(k+1\), and \(Q\) the opposite corner matrix (scaled to normalize) so that \(\operatorname{tr}(Q M_1^h)\) is the desired amplitude exactly when \(h=k\).

The companion `periodic_dicke_fourier_check.py` checks two exact modular examples over all weight-(k) words and samples the arithmetic parameter construction at large lengths. These checks illustrate the proof; they are not used to infer the asymptotic bound.
