# Sharp internal sunflower retention in very large fibers without injectivity

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Let \(H\) be a nonempty simple tripartite 3-uniform family with parts \(A,B,C\), and \(m\ge2\). Assume \(A\) has maximum degree at most \(m\) and every occupied \(BC\) pair has at least \(256m^2\) petals (distinct \(A\) labels). No pair-projection injectivity is assumed. The optimal worst-case fraction retained with witnesses inside the output is **\(\Theta(m^{-3/2})\)** if only nonempty intersections require an \(m\)-petal witness, and **\(\Theta(m^{-2})\)** if all intersections require one. In fact the nonempty lower bound holds already when the minimum occupied fiber size is \(128m^2\). The nonempty upper construction even has injective \(AB\). These are restricted results for the [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu question](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). Compare the [stronger nonempty rate with two injective projections](InternalSunflowersLargePairFibers.md).

## Nonempty lower bound

We aim for a simple projected \(BC\) graph with degrees 1 or at least \(m\) wherever positive, at least \(m\) retained triples per occupied fiber, and no shared \(A\) among retained triples. This structure gives all required nonempty witnesses: a fiber witnesses its pair, and \(m\) different neighbors of a vertex provide singleton petals. We use repeatedly the Hall allocation rule: if remaining \(A\)-degree is at most \(D\), all fibers of size \(f\) can simultaneously be given \(\lfloor f/D\rfloor\) globally disjoint \(A\) labels. The union of any collection of fibers contains at least its total number of incidences divided by \(D\) labels, which proves Hall's condition after making the prescribed number of copies of each fiber.

Peel the simple projection below degree \(m\), orienting deleted pairs away from their first peeled endpoints. In the core use Hall with \(D=m\); every fiber retains at least \(m\) labels, and the rate is \(\Omega(1/m)\). Each of the two oriented pieces has tail degree below \(m\); split each by whether its head had at least \(K=1024m^{3/2}\) neighbors in that orientation. Work in the largest by original triple count of the five resulting pieces.

**Low head degree.** Put \(\rho=1/(16(K+2m))\) and sample projected pairs independently at rate \(\rho\). Keep only isolated pairs in this sample and triples with no other occurrence of their \(A\) label in the sample. Conditioned on a given pair being sampled, the loss probability of any of its triples is at most \((K+2m)\rho\) by union bound, from adjacent pairs or other incidences of its label. Remove fibers retaining fewer than \(m\) triples. Since the original fiber size was at least \(2m\), the probability of this additional loss, conditioned on the pair being sampled, is at most \(2(K+2m)\rho\) by Markov. Hence each original triple survives with probability at least \((1-3/16)\rho\). The resulting matching has the required structure.

**High head degree.** Put \(p=1/(64\sqrt m)\) and \(Q=\lceil64\sqrt m\rceil\). Independently sample every head and independently mark every projected pair, each with probability \(p\); a pair is active if both its head and its own coin were selected. Discard pairs at a tail with more than one active neighbor, and discard every triple whose \(A\) label occurs more than \(Q\) times among active pairs (before discarding conflicting tails). For any fixed triple conditioned on its pair being active, the chance of conflict is at most
\[
 \delta=\frac{1}{4096}+\frac{1+\sqrt m/64+1/4096}{Q}<\frac1{80}.
\]
The tail part is bounded by \(mp^2\); all other pairs at that tail have different heads and coins. For the label, other incidences with the same head have expected count at most \(mp\); others have expected count at most \(mp^2\). An extra conditioning that a second specified pair at the same head is active increases this estimate by at most one and does not affect the tail estimate, because that second pair has a different tail. Thus the displayed bound is valid with or without this extra conditioning. Markov at threshold \(Q\) for other label incidences gives the label loss bound.

Discard fibers with fewer than half of their original triples after these losses. Conditional on activity, the probability of this is at most \(2\delta<1/40\), also under the extra conditioning. Finally discard any head left with fewer than \(m\) pairs. To bound this last loss for a target triple, condition on its own pair being active. A head of degree \(s\ge K\) then has at least as many active pairs as a \(\mathrm{Bin}(s,p)\) variable in stochastic order. Set \(\mu=sp\ge16m\). The chance it has fewer than \(\mu/2\) active pairs is at most \(\exp(-\mu/8)\). The conditional expected number of its active fibers losing half is at most \((1+\mu)/40\), using the preceding estimate with two active pairs fixed for every nontarget fiber. The chance of at least \(\mu/4\) such failures is at most \((1+1/\mu)/10\). Outside those events it has at least \(\mu/4\ge m\) good neighbors. Adding also the individual and own-fiber loss bounds, the conditional failure probability for the target triple is less than \(1/2\). Hence the expected number of retained triples before final allocation is at least \(p^2 |H_{\mathrm{piece}}|/2\).

The projected output is a disjoint union of large stars. Each surviving fiber has \(f'\ge f/2\ge64m^2\ge mQ\), including \(m=2\), and every \(A\) has degree at most \(Q\). Allocate by Hall with \(D=Q\), retaining at least \(f'/(2Q)\) per fiber. Taking a realization with at least the expected preallocation size proves \(\Omega(|H_{\mathrm{piece}}|/m^{3/2})\), as required.

## Matching nonempty obstruction with AB injective

Put \(t=\lceil\sqrt m\rceil\), split \(B\) into \(t\) groups of \(m-1\) vertices, and put \(m-1\) vertices in \(C\). For each pair of a \(B\)-group and a \(C\) vertex, introduce \(w\) private \(A\) labels. Each such label is used with every \(B\) in that group and the specified \(C\), so all occupied \(BC\) fibers have size \(w\), arbitrarily large, \(AB\) is injective, and \(A\)-degree is \(m-1\). A valid subfamily cannot repeat an \(A\), since any repetition at \(A\) would share its \(AC\) pair, of multiplicity only \(m-1\). A \(B\) cannot be used with two different \(C\)'s, since witnessing the singleton \(B\) needs \(m\) different \(C\)'s. A \(C\) center with one \(B\)-neighbor contributes at most \(w\); a \(C\) used with multiple \(B\)'s needs at least \(m\) \(B\)-neighbors and contributes at most \(tw\), the size of all its \(A\) pools. There are fewer than \(t\) such large centers because their \(B\) supports are disjoint within \(t(m-1)\) vertices. Overall at most \((m-1+t^2)w=O(mw)\) triples survive out of \((m-1)^2tw\), establishing the sharp nonempty power.

## The empty kernel

Write \(E=|H|\) and \(\Delta_{BC}\) for the largest triple degree in \(B\) or \(C\). If \(\Delta_{BC}\ge E/(8m)\), focus on such a vertex. If it has fewer than \(m\) projected neighbors, take its largest fiber, keeping at least \(\Delta_{BC}/m\). Otherwise allocate by Hall with \(D=m\) across all of its fibers, keeping at least \(\Delta_{BC}/(2m)\) and witnessing the single center and all pairs that occur. In both cases there are no empty intersections.

Otherwise greedily reserve \(m\) disjoint projected pairs: each chosen pair deletes from the search at most \(2\Delta_{BC}\) triples by removing its endpoints. Over these pairs choose one triple each with distinct \(A\) labels (all original fibers have at least \(m\) labels). Remove their \(A\) labels and all their projected endpoints from the remaining family. At most \(2m\Delta_{BC}+m^2<E/2\) triples in total are discarded, since \(E\ge256m^2\); every remaining occupied fiber loses at most \(m\) labels and keeps at least \(128m^2\). Apply the nonempty theorem and add the reserved edges, which witness the empty kernel. This lower bound is \(\Omega(E/m^2)\) in either case.

For a matching upper bound let \(B,C\) each have \(m-1\) vertices and give every pair \(w\) private \(A\) labels of degree one. No \(m\)-matching exists. Nonempty validity forces a projected matching, because neither projected side has \(m\) neighbors available. Empty validity then restricts the extraction to a single fiber. The retained proportion is at most \(1/(m-1)^2\), attained by taking that full fiber.
