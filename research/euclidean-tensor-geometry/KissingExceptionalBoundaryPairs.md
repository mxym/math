# Boundary pair counts for complete exceptional root line systems

**Theorem.** If a 73-point six-dimensional kissing configuration contains an oriented representative of each of the 36 lines of an E6 root system, it has at most 25 true antipodal pairs. If a 127-point seven-dimensional configuration contains all 63 E7 root lines, it has at most 50. These are conditional bounds, not improvements on the unrestricted kissing numbers.

Use the [sharp blocker lower bounds and the preceding conditional reduction](KissingExceptionalRootCoverBranch.md). On any root line whose point strictly conflicts with an outsider, the code orientation must be the opposite signed root. Hence all pairs lie on the original axes. At the previous boundary pair counts, each negative outsider edge has disjoint and complementary blocker masks, by the triangle exclusion. We exclude those boundary cases below.

## A five-root blocker set can hold at most one E6 outsider in a kissing code

Use unnormalized roots and outside vectors of squared norm two as in the previous checkpoint. The five signed blockers of a minimizing outsider form an upward ideal in its fundamental chamber. The [E6 exact checker](verify-kissing-e6-five-blocker-cell.js) finds three possible size-five ideals. In the one with a fork, the closed chamber polytope has only the highest root on the norm-two sphere. The other two ideals are related by diagram reversal, and each is a set C of five roots with Gram I+J. Fix the canonical one whose roots all have coefficient two at node 1 in the earlier numbering.

Let a=(r·x)_{r∈C}, and let z be the component of x perpendicular to span C in a choice of unit coordinate. The inner product in the span is a·b−(Σa)(Σb)/6, from (I+J)⁻¹=I−J/6. The closed chamber polytope imposes blocker products ≥1 and nonblocker products ≤1. In simple-inner-product coordinates its seven vertices are
e₅, e₂/2, e₁, e₁/2, (e₁+e₄)/2, (e₁+e₃)/3, e₀.
Only V=e₀ has nonzero z. It has a=(1,1,1,1,1) and |z|=1/√2. The center vertex U=e₁ has a=(2,2,2,2,2), z=0, and squared norm 10/3. The other five vertices have squared norm at most two. All vertices are verified by exact active bases.

Write a chamber point as θV+(1−θ)Q, with Q in the hull of the other vertices. Norm squared two forces θ≤1/2. At any other vertex than U, a mixture with V of positive weight has squared norm strictly below two by convexity. For U, its midpoint with V has squared norm two and V itself has squared norm 4/3, so any θ>1/2 again has squared norm strictly below two. Convexity in Q establishes the claim. Thus the norm-two slice lies in the hull of the six original vertices other than V and their six midpoints with V.

For points with the same signed mask C, reduce each by a Weyl symmetry and, when required, diagram reversal to this same chamber case. The relative isometry permutes the five blockers and may reverse the one-dimensional orthogonal coordinate. For any pair of the twelve hull generators, the smallest possible mutual inner product is bounded below by pairing their a-lists in opposite sorted orders and choosing opposite z signs. With a and η=√2·|z| both multiplied by 12, compare the integers
D=6(a↑·b↓)−ΣaΣb−3η_aη_b
and N_a=6||a||²−(Σa)²+3η_a².
All 78 unordered pairs satisfy D>0 and 4D²≥N_a N_b. Equality is possible only when both generators are e₅, the highest-root ray. Passing to positive combinations preserves the lower bound on normalized cosine by the triangle inequality. Equality cannot hold for outside points, since each needs a generator other than the root. Consequently two genuine outsiders with the same signed five-blocker set have inner product strictly greater than 1/2 when normalized. They cannot coexist in a kissing code.

## Closing the boundary pair counts in E6 and E7

**E6.** With exactly 26 true pairs, ten originally selected axes are single and there are eleven outsiders off all original axes. A negative outsider edge requires complementary five-element blocker masks among these ten signed roots, as any shared blocker would make a triangle. The signed mask orbit has 432 elements. In 47,520 axis-disjoint unordered pairs, their signed union has either two or three such masks, with counts 38,880 and 8,640. The new capacity-one lemma makes the negative outsider graph a matching. Once one edge exists, its masks exhaust all ten available signed roots. With at most three admissible five-masks in that union, a second disjoint edge cannot occur. Removing one endpoint leaves ten independent outsiders. With representatives of the 26 true pairs, they give 36 independent lines and hence a new E6 system. It shares exactly 26 axes with the original. The common roots form a simply-laced root system under reflections in their common vectors. ADE types of rank at most six have no 52-root system. Hence 26 is impossible. If there were no edge, the original 26 pair representatives and all eleven outsiders already contradict the 36-line bound.

**E7.** At 51 pairs there are twelve singly occupied axes and thirteen outsiders. Any negative outsider edge has disjoint six-blocker masks. [The previous covering certificate](verify-kissing-exceptional-root-covering.js) proves that the six-conflict outsider points form the Weyl orbit of simple-inner-product vector (e₅+e₆)/2. The exact signed orbit of vectors and masks has 4,032 members each. To fix one endpoint, apply an element sending it to that representative. The [E7 exact checker](verify-kissing-e7-six-blocker-edges.js) finds exactly twenty orbit vectors with axis-disjoint blocker set and normalized inner product strictly below −1/2 with it. In all twenty cases the union of their blocker sets contains exactly the two masks of the endpoints. The bijection between vectors and masks then precludes a second negative edge. Deleting one endpoint leaves twelve independent outsiders and gives a second 63-line E7 system sharing exactly 51 axes with the first. The common set would have 102 roots, impossible by ADE at rank at most seven for a proper subsystem. With no edge the projective bound already fails. Thus 51 is impossible.

The later [E6 twenty-five-pair exclusion](KissingE6TwentyFivePairs.md) strengthens the six-dimensional conclusion to at most 24 pairs under the same hypothesis.

## Reproduction and context

Run `node verify-kissing-e6-five-blocker-cell.js` and `node verify-kissing-e7-six-blocker-edges.js`. The irreducible simply-laced systems have root counts (r(r+1)) for (A_r), (2r(r-1)) for (D_r), and 72, 126, 240 for the exceptional systems. Reducible systems in rank at most six have at most (D_5+A_1)'s 42 roots; in rank at most seven they have at most (E_6+A_1)'s 74 roots. Thus neither disputed intersection size occurs. The identification of 36 or 63 independent unoriented lines with E6 or E7 uses [Kurt Anstreicher's antipodal optimality and uniqueness](https://optimization-online.org/2001/01/248/). No new claim about unrestricted equality or upper bounds is made.
