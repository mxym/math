# Salmon residual support has codimension at least four

> Follow-up: the [additional compressions and dimension analysis](SalmonResidualSupportCodimensionFive.md) improve the support bound to codimension at least five.

Notation: \(X=\widehat{\sigma_4}((\mathbb P^3)^3)\) is the dimension-40 fourth secant cone, I its prime ideal, and \(J=J_{5,6,9}\) the ideal of the known modules. This strengthens the preceding [codimension-three bound](SalmonResidualSupportCodimension.md) to \(\dim\operatorname{Supp}(I/J)\le36\), using two additional exact checks. It does not assert the discrepancy is nonempty or resolve the global homogeneous ideal.

## The two boundary checks

Use \(u=e_0+e_1+e_2\). The fixed \((3,3,4)\) representatives are:
\[
 T_{\mathrm{col}}=\sum_{i=0}^2 e_i^{\otimes3}+(e_0+e_1)\otimes u\otimes e_3
\]
and
\[
 T_{\mathrm{tan}}=(e_1\otimes e_0+e_0\otimes e_1)\otimes e_0+
 e_0\otimes e_0\otimes e_1+e_2^{\otimes3}+u\otimes u\otimes e_3.
\]
The latter is border rank four by the tangent limit of two simple tensors plus two simple tensors. The companion script [`verify-salmon-compression-boundary.js`](verify-salmon-compression-boundary.js) checks 24 independent derivatives at each point, 20 from Strassen quintics and four from sextics. The sextic construction is the same as in the frame proof with the two leftover C indices \(k,l\) allowed to differ (they still remain unchanged under the color-swap proof). The selected integer minors are \(90\) and \(28\), respectively, modulo 101. As in the frame proof, the local zero scheme is regular of dimension 40 and must equal the true secant scheme there. No degree nine is needed locally at either sample.

## Why the checks cover all possible codimension-three discrepancies in (3,3,4)

Fix the two three-dimensional support spaces \(A,B\) and fixed surjections \(\phi_A,\phi_B:\mathbb C^4\to A,B\). By Jagiełła–Jelisiejew's unrestriction theorem (arXiv:2604.24879, Prop. 2.4), any tensor of multilinear ranks \((3,3,4)\) is \((\phi_A\otimes\phi_B\otimes1)\widetilde T\) for some concise tensor \(\widetilde T\) of border rank four. The third restriction map is invertible and absorbed; the two others have exactly rank three and can be put in the fixed form. Let \(G_A=\{g:\phi_Ag=\phi_A\}\), \(G_B=\{g:\phi_Bg=\phi_B\}\), each of dimension four. Their product acts along fibers, preserving all the source types. Its stabilizer dimension at a concise lift is at most one, as follows. Projection of the stabilizer to \(G_B\) is injective by A-mode concision. Applying \(\phi_A\) to the stabilizer identity forces \(g_B-1=u_B\otimes m\), where \(u_B\) spans \(\ker\phi_B\) and \(m\) is in the B-mode contraction kernel of the partially restricted tensor \((\phi_A\otimes1\otimes1)\widetilde T\). This kernel has dimension at most one, since the final restriction has B-mode rank three.

For a Gorenstein regular source the product stabilizer is trivial. Such a tensor admits a C-invertible slice: it is equivalent in its three modes to the evaluation tensor \(f(xyz)\) of its algebra with a perfect Frobenius functional. Normalize the C-slices to the full four-dimensional regular algebra \(R\subset\mathrm{Mat}_4\), including the identity. The stabilizer equations imply \(g_B^\top=g_A^{-1}\) from the identity and that \(g_A\) centralizes \(R\) from all slices. The regular centralizer equals \(R\), so \(g_A-1\in R\), and \(\phi_A(g_A-1)=0\). A nonzero such element would be in the kernel of the C-flattening of the final tensor. Thus \(g_A=1=g_B\). Accordingly any invariant source stratum of dimension \(d\) contributes image dimension at most \(d-8+6=d-2\) when Gorenstein regular, and at most \(d-7+6=d-1\) otherwise; the added six parameters vary \(A,B\) in two Grassmannians.

For the dimension classification, refer to the faithful-module list in the [concise-local theorem](SalmonConciseDegreeFiveLocalIdeal.md). For a regular or dual-regular faithful algebra, the normalizer in GL4 has dimension \(4+\dim\operatorname{Aut}(R)\), by regular multiplication and all automorphisms lifting to the module. The normalized triple coordinates contribute 12 parameters (three vectors from the algebra), and the inverse slice another 16; hence each abstract algebra type gives a source dimension at most \(40-\dim\operatorname{Aut}(R)\). There are finitely many types in dimension four: products of lower length local algebras, monogenic length four, square-zero length four, or length four with embedding dimension two and pairing of rank one or two. The only Gorenstein regular types with automorphism dimension below two are \(\mathbb C^4\) (dimension zero automorphisms) and \(\mathbb C[\epsilon]/\epsilon^2\times\mathbb C^2\) (dimension one); two dual factors have dimension two, length-three or length-four monogenic factors have dimensions two or three, and rank-two pairing length four allows independent translations of its two generators by the socle. Non-Gorenstein regular or dual-regular sources have automorphism dimension at least four, as in the preceding support bound. The remaining square-zero \(2+2\) module case has normalized triple dimension at most 19 and source dimension at most 35, by choosing the common two-plane and three maps/three scalar shifts. These counts can use finitely many fixed-covector inverse-slice charts, since \(\widetilde T\) is concise.

Therefore every source type except the semisimple and single-dual types contributes images of dimension at most 36. The single-dual type is one orbit under GL4 in each mode, by its regular multiplication/evaluation description; its closure of compressed images (also varying the support spaces) is irreducible, of dimension at most 37. Its compressed representative \(T_{\mathrm{tan}}\) lies in that image by projecting each fourth spatial basis vector to \(u\) in the standard triple \(a_1b_0c_0+a_0b_1c_0+a_0b_0c_1+a_2b_2c_2+a_3b_3c_3\). Because \(J=I\) at that sample, the closed bad support meets this irreducible image closure in dimension at most 36.

For the semisimple lifts \(\widetilde T\), the compressed tensors are sums of four simple tensors with independent C factors. The A and B factor matrices have rank three, each with a kernel line in \(\mathbb P^3\). A single missing frame minor is exactly one zero coordinate on one of these lines. If exactly one coordinate vanishes across both lines, one frame has precisely one collinear triple and no coincidences. A projective transformation makes it the frame of \(T_{\mathrm{col}}\), while the other is an ordinary projective frame, and the C basis absorbs the individual rescalings. If two or more coordinates vanish, this is at least two conditions on the full-rank pair of matrices (the kernel maps have fibers \(\mathrm{GL}_3\)). For fixed supports there are \(12+12+16=40\) factor parameters, and eight independent simple-factor rescalings leave the tensor unchanged, so the bad image has dimension at most \(40-2-8=30\), or 36 allowing two supports. The case with no zeros is the prior frame orbit.

These arguments exhaust all unrestrictions of a tensor of multilinear ranks \(3,3,4\), and each potentially bad source contributes dimension at most 36.

## Other rank strata

The preceding inverse-slice proof covers all generated dimension-four algebras and bounds the rest in dimension at most 35. The preceding dimension bound for all-singular exactly one deficient flattening gives dimension at most 36 (and at most 35 for rank tuple \((3,4,4)\)). For two deficient flattenings not of type \((3,3,4)\), three deficient flattenings give at most \(27+9=36\) from the compression and support choices, and a deficient rank at most two gives at most \(24+7=31\) from the \((2,3,4)\) case. Thus the only additional needed check is the \((3,3,4)\) one above.
