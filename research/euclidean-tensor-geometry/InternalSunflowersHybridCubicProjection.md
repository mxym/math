# One quadratic vertex side and one cubic hybrid-projection side

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Let \(H\) be a finite simple four-partite four-uniform family with sides \(A,B,C,D\). For a projected \(ABD\) triple \(\tau\), let \(f_\tau\) be the number of its \(C\)-extensions, and assume
\[
\deg(c)\le Lm^2\quad(c\in C),\qquad
\sum_{\tau\ni d:\,f_\tau<3m} f_\tau+\#\{\tau\ni d:f_\tau\ge3m\}\le Lm^3\quad(d\in D).
\]
Then there is a subfamily of size \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\) internally witnessing all realized intersections, including the empty one. No condition is imposed on any pair codegree or on the other triple types. This is a partial case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6).

In particular it suffices that \(C\)-degrees are \(O(m^2)\) and \(D\)-degrees are \(O(m^3)\). The general hypothesis is strictly weaker than that edge-degree bound: a heavy \(\tau\) contributes just one unit however many distinct \(C\)-extensions it has. No upper bound whatsoever on a single \(D\)-degree follows (fix one \(ABD\) triple and vary its \(C\) labels).

## Weighted low-degree rank-three lemma (nonempty intersections)

Let \(J\) be a finite simple three-partite three-uniform family on \(U,V,W\) with arbitrary nonnegative edge weights and \(U\)-degree at most \(M_0 m\). There exists a nonempty-kernel-valid subfamily of total weight \(\Omega_{M_0}(w(J)/m^2)\). The graph ingredient is the weighted simple bipartite nonempty extraction lemma, retaining \(\Omega(w/m)\) with positive degrees one or at least \(m\), as established in the [rank-three release](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0). Its proof via weighted charge by peeling, sampling large stars or choosing a matching, already treats arbitrary weights. Here are the details for extending its three-uniform bounded-coordinate corollary to arbitrary weights.

Separate the \(VW\) fibers of size below \(2m\) and at least \(2m\), and take the heavier class. In the light class, form a simple bipartite graph on \(U\) and the occupied \(VW\) tuples, one graph edge per input triple. Its maximum degree is at most \(\max(M_0,2)m\), so by bipartite edge coloring a heaviest color class retains proportion at least \(1/(\max(M_0,2)m)\) of the weight. Now \(U\) and \(VW\) labels are both individually injective. Apply the weighted graph lemma to the simple graph of \(VW\) tuples, weighting each tuple by its unique selected extension; lift the result. It only has singleton \(V/W\) nonempty intersections, each certified by degree at least \(m\).

For the heavy class, activate each \(VW\) tuple independently with probability \(q=1/[64(\max(M_0,2))m]\). Drop an edge if its \(U\) label occurs in any other originally activated tuple; conditional on its own tuple being active, this costs probability at most \(M_0mq\le1/64\). Conditional on activation, Markov bounds by \(2/64\) the probability that a tuple loses at least half its original edges in this step, so discard an entire tuple if fewer than \(m\) survive. Each original triple survives with probability at least \(q(1-3/64)\), regardless of its weight. In an outcome of weight \(\Omega_{M_0}(w(J)/m)\), distinct surviving tuples use disjoint \(U\) labels and each surviving tuple has at least \(m\) extensions. Apply the weighted graph lemma on the \(VW\) support, now weighting tuples by their total surviving weight. Keep all extensions of the output tuples. Their fibers witness all repeated \(VW\) pairs, and \(m\) distinct projected neighbors supply singleton \(V/W\) witnesses with disjoint \(U\) labels. This proves the weighted rank-three lemma. Zero-weight edges in these arguments cause no issue and can be discarded before the initial split, or used as possible witnesses.

## Main nonempty bound

Classify each occupied \(\tau\) once as light if \(f_\tau<3m\), heavy otherwise. The construction below applies both to \(H\) and to the residual \(H'\) obtained after deleting the vertices of a reserved disjoint \(m\)-matching, always retaining the **original** classification. Allow empty fibers to disappear. A surviving originally heavy fiber loses at most \(m\) extensions, because its fixed constituents avoid the deleted vertices and the only other deletions in that fiber are at the \(m\) reserved \(C\) labels. It still has size at least \(2m\). The hypotheses give a bound \(Lm^3\) on the number of light incident **edges** or on the number of heavy incident **tuples** at each \(D\) label, in \(H'\) as well as \(H\).

Choose the class with at least half of the current mass \(E'\). Set \(p=1/[128(L+3)m^2]\) (we may assume \(L\ge1\)).

*Light class.* Activate individual edges independently with probability \(p\). Delete each sampled edge if another initially sampled edge shares its \(C\) label or its \(ABD\) tuple. Conditional on activation, this failure probability is at most \(p(Lm^2+3m)\le 1/128\). Also drop all edges incident to any \(D\) label with initial sampled degree greater than \(2m\). Given a fixed edge's activation, the mean number of other sampled edges at its \(D\) label is at most \(pLm^3\le m/128\); Markov at threshold \(m\) bounds this additional deletion probability by \(1/128\). Hence a deterministic outcome retains \(\Omega_L(E'/m^2)\) edges with unique \(C\) labels, unique projected triples, and projected \(D\)-degree at most \(2m\). Apply the weighted low-degree rank-three lemma, with unit weights, to the simple projected \(ABD\) family. The unique extensions preserve every nonempty witness, giving \(\Omega_L(E'/m^4)\).

*Heavy class.* Activate each nonempty heavy projected triple independently with probability \(p\). Delete every edge in an active fiber if its \(C\) label occurs in another originally activated fiber; a conditional union bound costs at most \(pLm^2\le1/128\) for each edge. Conditional on the fiber being active, fewer than \(m\) edges survive with probability at most \(2/128\), by Markov and the lower bound \(2m\) on its size. Discard every such fiber. Also discard all fibers at any \(D\) label with more than \(2m\) initially active tuples: conditional on a fixed tuple being activated, the mean number of its competitors at that label is at most \(pLm^3\le m/128\), and the probability of this last deletion is at most \(1/128\). These deletions are all assessed using the initial activation or the C-conflict step only; whole-fiber deletion does not shrink other surviving fibers. Thus some outcome keeps \(\Omega_L(E'/m^2)\) edges in surviving fibers of size at least \(m\), with different fibers using disjoint \(C\) labels and with projected \(D\)-degree at most \(2m\). Apply the weighted rank-three lemma to their simple \(ABD\) support, assigning weight equal to surviving fiber size. Keep whole output fibers, giving \(\Omega_L(E'/m^4)\) edges. Within one fiber the intersection is the full \(ABD\) tuple, witnessed there by \(m\) distinct \(C\)-extensions. Across fibers the intersection is exactly the projected intersection. When nonempty, lift its projected \(m\)-sunflower by choosing one extension from each of its distinct projected petals, which have pairwise disjoint \(C\) labels.

## Empty-kernel completion

If there is a disjoint \(m\)-matching whose vertices avoid at least half the original edges, apply the nonempty theorem to that remaining family \(H'\), using the original classification as above, and restore the matching. New intersections are only empty, witnessed by the matching. Otherwise a vertex of \(H\) has degree at least \(E/(8m)\): the endpoints of any \(m\)-matching meet more than half the edges, or the endpoints of a maximal smaller matching cover all edges. Apply the [unrestricted full rank-three bound](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) to its simple link and lift by the fixed vertex. The size is \(\Omega(E/m^4)\), and even empty link intersections correspond to internal singleton kernels after lifting. The unrestricted rank-four and the sole quadratic-side cases remain open.

**Mechanism.** Thin either edges or projected tuples, not both. The \(D\)-cutoff counts the objects actually activated, while the \(C\)-conflict pruning is charged against original degree. Heavy fibers survive whole-fiber thinning and later serve both as multiplicities in a weighted rank-three extraction and as internal witnesses for the full projected triple kernel.
