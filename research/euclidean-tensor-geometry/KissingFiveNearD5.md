# Thirty-two retained D5 roots preclude a 41st kissing point in dimension five

**Theorem.** Let X be a finite subset of the radius-√2 sphere in R⁵, with x·y≤1 for distinct points. If X contains at least 32 of the 40 vectors ±eᵢ±eⱼ (i<j), then |X|≤40. The same is true for any rotated D5. The bound on |X| is attained by D5. We do not assert that the overlap threshold 32 is optimal or that the unrestricted five-dimensional kissing number is 40.

This is a continuous statement: the other vectors are arbitrary real vectors. For context, [Cohn–Rajagopal](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00454-026-00841-x) construct and compare extremal-size examples; [Cohn–Jiao–Kumar–Torquato](https://msp.org/gt/2011/15-4/gt-v15-n4-p11-p.pdf) study rigidity. Related [public finite-grid experiments](https://github.com/wustep/maths/blob/main/problems/kissing-5d/ATTACK.md) treat discretized outsider sets. The finite enumeration below is of blocker masks enclosing **continuous** cells, not of a grid of possible kissing points.

## Certificate and reduction

Assume a counterexample; retain all of its original roots and enough outsiders to obtain 41 points. Write U for the missing signed roots, m=|U|≤8. A nonroot outsider x has blocker mask C(x)={r∈D5 : x·r>1} contained in U. We do not require that U have at most one sign per axis.

The [standalone Node.js verifier](verify-kissing-5d-near-d5.js) enumerates the signed coordinate group B5. In a signed-permutation chamber a₁≥⋯≥a₅≥0, the only roots with possibly positive product are among the 20 positive roots eᵢ±eⱼ, i<j. Let bᵢ=aᵢ−aᵢ₊₁, with a₆=0. The 20 root products are linear forms with nonnegative integer coefficients in b. The strict blockers form an upper ideal for coordinatewise domination of coefficients. For each such ideal through size eight, intersect the nonnegative orthant with the corresponding closed product inequalities ≥1 or ≤1 and 2a₁≤3 (valid on the sphere). The code uses one extra dummy variable constrained to zero. It enumerates all active constraint bases by exact determinants. The numbers of ideals for m=0,…,8 are 1,1,1,2,2,3,4,5,6; exactly 0,0,0,1,2,3,4,5,6 survive the sphere test. If no vertex has squared norm >2, strict convexity allows only vertices of norm √2; discard the original roots and vertices without the prescribed strict mask.

For the other cells, form an outer enclosure of the sphere slice. In the barycentric simplex on its vertices, partition them into high vertices (squared norm >2) and low vertices. For each low vertex v take the largest tᵥ in {0,1/96,…,1} such that (1−tᵥ)v+tᵥu has squared norm ≤2 for every high vertex u. The hyperplane Σ_high λᵤ=Σ_low [tᵥ/(1−tᵥ)]λᵥ clips all these edges at the selected points. The low side is generated by low vertices and cuts, so by strict convexity its norm-√2 points must themselves be generators of norm √2. Every low norm-√2 vertex is checked to have tᵥ=0 and hence is retained. The high vertices and all the cuts therefore enclose every real outsider in the cell.

To certify two cells incompatible, write their generator sets as A, B in actual coordinates. Any of the following sufficient comparisons is allowed:
1. a·b≥1 for all generator pairs.
2. For each a, either a·b≥‖a‖/√2 for all b, or a·b≥‖a‖‖b‖/2 for all b. The same test with A and B exchanged is allowed.

In the second test each row implies a·y≥‖a‖/√2 for a sphere point y in the second hull, using the triangle inequality for the cosine test. Averaging now gives x·y≥‖x‖/√2=1. For either test the verifier requires strictness for any pair of generators unless at least one is on its own chamber's root ray R₊(1,1,0,0,0). Each actual nonroot in a chamber uses a generator off that ray; combining a positive-weight off-ray generator from each side makes the inequality strict. Thus x·y>1. Signed permutations account for all chambers realizing a signed mask. Products are exact: generator coordinates scaled by 192 are integers bounded by 288, so the largest squared-product comparison is bounded by 4(5·288²)²<2⁵². All 21 candidate mask types have capacity at most one for every relative chamber with the same mask.

## Enumerating the missing signs

Anchor one realized mask by a signed permutation, augment it with arbitrary missing signed roots to size at most eight, and identify copies under B5. For canonicalization, first move the sum of the selected roots to sorted nonnegative coordinates, then minimize the bit mask under the stabilizer of that sum. The 569,952 indexed sets give 12,872 distinct classes. For each, generate every allowable signed blocker mask inside U. Since each has capacity one, 41 points require m+1 distinct mutually compatible masks. The clique search only marks a pair incompatible if all relative chambers pass the sufficient test above. Greedy proper coloring bounds clique size within exact branching. Counts are:

| missing m | classes | fewer than m+1 candidate masks | eliminated by clique bound | left |

|3|1|1|0|0|
|4|6|6|0|0|
|5|47|46|1|0|

|8|10,512|9,746|759|7|

Five of the eight residual classes, including the only class at m=7, contain only roots involving the first coordinate. All 24 roots of the D4 equator on coordinates 2–5 are present. Here is its elementary bound. For any other point (z,v) with four-dimensional horizontal projection v, the full D4 gives |vᵢ|+|vⱼ|≤1. Hence ‖v‖²≤1: if a=max |vᵢ|≤1/2 this is immediate, and otherwise use ‖v‖²≤a²+3(1−a)²≤1 for 1/2≤a≤1. Consequently |z|≥1. On each side of the equator, v·v′≤1−zz′≤0. A pole has the side to itself. Otherwise normalize all projections. At most eight unit vectors in R⁴ can have nonpositive pairwise products: for their Gram matrix G of order n, ΣGᵢⱼ≥0 and Gᵢⱼ²≤−Gᵢⱼ off the diagonal, implying tr(G²)≤2n; by rank, n²≤4 tr(G²). This gives 24+8+8=40. In particular **all m≤7 are excluded**.

In each of the remaining three classes the sole missing D4 root is r=e₂+e₃. It must be blocked by at least two outsiders: if blocked by at most one, restore r and discard its blocker (or discard any outsider if none), obtaining 41 points with m=7. The numbers of candidate masks containing r in the three classes are 0,7,15; the corresponding numbers of compatible distinct pairs are 0,0,2. For each residual pair both masks consist of the positive triangle e₁+e₂, e₁+e₃, e₂+e₃ plus one of e₁±e₄ and one of e₁±e₅.

## One strict 30-degree cap eliminates the last two pairs

In its canonical chamber that five-mask C is {e₁+e₂,e₁+e₃,e₂+e₃,e₁+e₄,e₁+e₅}. Its center 1 is intrinsic (unique support-graph vertex of degree four), and 2,3 are the triangle neighbors. We claim any x with this mask has x·w>3/2 for w=e₁+(e₂+e₃)/2. To see this, add the contrary inequality 2a₁+a₂+a₃≤3 to the closed chamber polytope. Exhausting all its bases gives exactly the following six vertices:
```
(1,1,1,1,1)/2      (3,3,3,1,1)/4      (1,1,0,0,0)
(2,1,1,1,1)/2      (2,1,1,1,0)/2      (2,1,1,0,0)/2
```
All have squared norm ≤2. Neither norm-√2 vertex satisfies the strict mask; hence by strict convexity no point of the required slice remains. The signed permutations relating the two masks preserve the common signed triangle and its center, so the *same* w works for both points. Since ‖w‖²=3/2 and ‖x‖²=2, both points are strictly within 30 degrees of w. Their angle is less than 60 degrees, contradicting kissing. This eliminates the last cases.
