# A simultaneous quadratic-data barrier for length 17 at M3

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checking; no external review or priority claim.

The [projective Cartan reduction](Matrix3BorderApolarityProjectiveCartan.md) leaves 29 necessary triples. The [exact surviving shapes](Matrix3BorderApolarityCubicRigidity.md) force a single cubic inverse system of dimension 17. Here we demonstrate that extending those tests to all the degree-two data simultaneously does not by itself close the search. This is a barrier for the indicated tests, not a length-17 border-apolar witness.

There exist six explicit upper-Borel-stable quadratic ideal spaces, in the degrees \(200,020,002,110,011,101\), with codimension 17 in their respective ambient spaces, satisfying the following.

1. The three pair inverse systems have indices \((11,196,29)\) in the projective enumeration and meet apolarity. The ideal \(J\) generated by these six spaces has \(H_{S/J}(111)=17\) and \(H_{S/J}(\mathbf d)\ge17\) for every \(\mathbf d\) of total degree 3 or 4.
2. Every square test of total degree four stated in [Mańdziuk, §§4,7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12121v1#S7) passes simultaneously on these same spaces. In multidegrees with all three coordinates nonzero the lower bound is \(25\cdot17=425\); in two nonzero coordinates it is \(17\cdot17=289\); and in one coordinate it is \(9\cdot17=153\) (the Veronese case applies at \(n=8,r=17\)). There are no linear ideal generators, so every ideal extending these degree-two data has the same square piece in total degree four; adding higher-degree generators cannot change these tests.

The [standalone exact verifier](verify-mm3-quadratic-low-degree-barrier.js) provides the three explicit pure degree-two signatures. In \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(A)\), use weights indexed by a row pair and a column pair of weakly increasing zero-based indices. A weight is one-dimensional when either pair is repeated, otherwise it has the two monomials corresponding to the two matchings. In the latter case their symmetric and alternating combinations correspond to the two Cauchy summands. The verifier lists 0 (empty), 1 (symmetric line), or 4 (whole weight) for each cell, first increasing in the row pair, then decreasing in the column pair. It checks the annihilator rank and directly applies the four upper-root derivations to each pure annihilator to verify Borel stability in characteristic zero. The pair data are as in the projective calculation.

The complete square quotient dimensions by \(\mathbf d\) are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
\text{pure degree four}&004:243&040:220&400:221\\
(3,1)\text{ degrees}&013:437&031:389&103:408\\
&130:390&301:422&310:403\\
(2,2)\text{ degrees}&022:309&202:296&220:290\\
(2,1,1)\text{ degrees}&112:470&121:425&211:425
\end{array}
\]
These are codimensions of products of the corresponding quadratic generators, checked with fraction-free rational elimination. The ordinary test checks all products of lower generators by arbitrary monomials of the complementary degree in total degrees three and four. It is not a proof that one can choose the remaining cubic and higher generators so that all Hilbert dimensions are simultaneously exactly 17. It also gives no membership in the closure of radical ideals. The next decisive step would require genuinely coupled higher-degree or topological constraints, not just the same square bounds with the pure quadrics incorporated.

## A smaller model for higher degrees

Independently of the choice of pure quadrics, let \(K=\langle I_{111}\rangle\) be the ideal generated by the forced cubic equations. In degree \((111)\), all 27 matched multiplication monomials have the same class \(m\); the 16 box monomials are free, and all other monomials vanish. The class \(m\) is annihilated by every variable. For a variable in the \(A\) or \(B\) slot, choose one of \(a_{20}b_{00}c_{02}\) and \(a_{21}b_{10}c_{02}\) whose variable in that slot differs. For a variable in the \(C\) slot, use \(a_{20}b_{00}c_{02}\) and \(a_{20}b_{01}c_{12}\) instead. In each case, replacement in the chosen matched monomial by the given variable makes a non-matched cubic outside the box. The product of the given variable with \(m\) can be factored as this vanishing cubic times the replaced variable.

In every degree coordinatewise at least \((111)\) other than \((111)\) itself, every monomial containing a variable outside the box has a non-box cubic factor. Such a factor vanishes or is \(m\), which is annihilated by the additional variables. All box monomials still survive because the box restriction annihilates \(I_{111}\), hence \(K\). Thus the higher part of \(S/K\) in those degrees is just the box ring. This gives a smaller arena for imposing further equations, not the missing extension or component-membership test.
