# Size 85 over F_17³: degree-nine geometry

Assume the published size85 M8 and no-layer results. Use the same notation: B and B' are partner sets, U_B is the uniform-normal set, and D(B') is contained in it. Both sets have affine line cap5 and affine plane cap16. This note is conditional on a nonzero homogeneous degree-nine form F on the normal space with U_B in its zeros.

## Four-blocking

If a projective line has at most three U_B directions, its 17 parallel affine slices in B' are full collinear five-sets (a noncollinear four-set determines at least four directions). At least six are parallel to a common direction e, and their quotient sites along e have line cap3 (four such supports coplanar would give twenty points). They determine at least five quotient directions: a cap3 six-set is noncollinear, and the exact planar census excludes six noncollinear sites with at most four directions. Each pair of full supports gives at least nine transverse directions by Cauchy–Davenport, plus the axial e. Thus their radial lines through e are factors of F and the number k of all radial factors is at least five.

On each remaining line through e, at most 1+9−k≤5 U_B points are possible. In the affine pencil through an actual five-support of direction e, every nonfactor plane therefore contains exactly that support: a point off it contributes five transverse directions in addition to e. If k≤7 the total of the eighteen plane occupancies is at most 90+11k≤167, below the required 85+17·5=170. If k=9 all factors are radial and the quotient along e has at least seventeen sites but at most nine directions. With more than seventeen sites it determines all eighteen directions (pigeonhole in each direction); at exactly seventeen all fibers are full, so the quotient has cap3 and determines at least fourteen directions by the full-plane cap-five lemma in [FugledeF17Size85DegreeEightGeometry.md](FugledeF17Size85DegreeEightGeometry.md). These are contradictions.

If k=8, take any radial nonfactor. It has at most two U_B sites; its 17 affine slices in B' are therefore all full collinear five-supports, with axes drawn from e and at most one other direction e'. If at least four use e' distinct from e, their quotient locations have cap3 and at least four directions: four noncollinear points always determine at least four. These force four radial factors through e' by the same Cauchy–Davenport argument. The two factor families share at most one line, so total ≥8+4−1>9. Consequently at least fourteen supports have axis e, and all their quotient directions force radial factors; this is impossible because a cap3 set with at most eight directions has at most nine sites by exact census. This final contradiction proves four-blocking.

## If both degree-nine moment forms are nonzero, both sets have spatial cap4 and plane cap15

Take a full five-support in B' with direction e and let k be the radial factor count of the other moment form. Four-blocking for U_B gives k≤6, since its radial nonfactors contain e and at most 9−k further sites. Every affine plane in the pencil through the support corresponds to a radial line through e; its directions are contained in the U_B points on that line. At least four planes of this pencil are uniform for B' (own U_B' is four-blocking), with occupancy five.

For k=0,1 use the cap10 planar bound for nonfactor planes with at most nine directions (the total degree9 root bound also applies at k=0). Even allowing the four uniform planes to be disjoint from factor planes, the total is at most 4·5+(14−k)·10+16k=160+6k≤166. For k=2,3 use the cap9 planar bound with at most eight directions, getting 146+7k≤167. For k≥5 each nonfactor has at most five directions, so a plane through the actual five-support has exactly five sites and the total is at most 90+11k≤156.

At k=4 a radial nonfactor has at most six directions. The following direct argument gives its exact nine-point equality shape. In affine coordinates the main support is X×{0}, with |X|=5. An outside site (u,v) specifies all five transverse parameters (u−X)/v, which must comprise the same set S for every outside site. Fix the first outside site. All others correspond bijectively to affine symmetries of X, because u−vS=X. Each such symmetry fixes the arithmetic mean x̄=ΣX/5. Thus the symmetries embed in the cyclic multiplicative group, and all outside sites lie on the affine line u−v·mean(S)=x̄. A nontrivial group order divides four or five by orbit sizes on X, with at most one fixed point. It also divides sixteen; hence the maximum order is four and there are at most four outside points. Equality forces their direction line through O=(x̄,0) and all five transverse projective parameters have arithmetic mean equal to that direction, in affine transverse coordinate (up to sign convention). This assertion about the mean is invariant under an affine change of the projective coordinate fixing e at infinity.

The eighteen-plane total at k=4 is at most 4·5+10·9+4·16=174. Since the actual total is170, at least six nonfactor planes have equality size nine: even if uniform and factor planes overlap, one can assign four uniform slots by giving each displaced slot its higher nominal cap, so any deficit from overlap can only reduce the total. More explicitly, if t of the four selected uniform planes are factors, the nonfactor family contains 4−t selected uniform planes and at most 10+t other planes, and the upper bound is 174−7t; therefore t=0 and at most four of those ten have <9 points.

Write the projective coordinates as [X:Y:Z] with e=[1:0:0]. Divide F by its four radial factors, all binary in Y,Z, to obtain a degree-five form H. Its X^5 coefficient may initially be zero, but on any size-nine nonfactor line H evaluated at [X:Y:Z] has five distinct finite roots (the transverse directions), and is nonzero, so the constant X^5 coefficient is nonzero. Their arithmetic mean is a fixed binary linear form in (Y,Z), by the coefficient of X^4 and Vieta. Thus the direction of every secondary arm lies in the same projective line; the secondary affine arms all pass through the same O on the main support, the mean of X independent of the choice of radial plane. Six such arms give at least 24 distinct outside points in one affine plane through O, contrary to cap16. Hence no full five-support exists and both members have line cap4.

Any affine plane with 16 sites of a cap4 member determines at least fourteen directions by completion and the full17 cap5 lemma. These would give ≥14 uniform normals on a projective line of the partner, which in the degree-seven argument forces a layer. There are no layers, so such a plane does not occur and both plane caps are fifteen.
