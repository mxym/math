# Restricted Cartan-free border-apolarity certificates for matrix multiplication

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review or priority claim.

**Scope.** Work over \(\mathbb C\). The full complex border rank of \(M_{\langle3\rangle}\) remains \(17\)–\(20\) in the primary sources checked: [Conner–Harper–Landsberg](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07981) Theorem 1.1 gives the lower bound, and [Smirnov](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0965542513120129) gives the upper bound (also explicitly attributed in [Landsberg–Ottaviani](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v011a011/)). In September 2026 [Mańdziuk, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12121v1#S6) reproduces the known lower bound 17 via the square test at \(r=16\). The results below do not exclude Cartan-enabled witnesses or improve the unrestricted bounds.

## 1. A finite exclusion at length 17, without saturation

Let \(U,V,W\) be \(n\)-dimensional with basis indices \(0,\ldots,n-1\) and put \(A=U\otimes V^*, B=V\otimes W^*, C=W\otimes U^*\). Thus \(M_n=\sum_{i,j,k}a_{ij}\otimes b_{jk}\otimes c_{ki}\) in the coordinate primal bases. Choose the upper-triangular Borels in \(U,V,W\). The inverse system \(E_{AB}=I_{110}^\perp\) of an apolar ideal contains \(\langle \sum_j a_{ij}\otimes b_{jk}:i,k\rangle=U\otimes\mathbb C I_V\otimes W^*\). Under the stabilizer torus, \(A\otimes B=U\otimes\operatorname{End}(V)\otimes W^*\); each outer index \((i,k)\) distinguishes a different character, and at fixed \((i,k)\) the middle zero-weight part is the diagonal. By “Cartan-free” mean that no diagonal direction beyond the required identity is present in any of the three cyclic pair inverse systems. Invariance under the upper-triangular unipotents upgrades this to a sign restriction: if a negative root \(E_{l,j}\), \(l>j\), appeared, acting by the positive \(E_{j,l}\) would produce a nonzero traceless diagonal, contrary to the model. Each remaining positive-root weight space is one dimensional and separate from all others and the identities. Thus each Cartan-free pair space is exactly the required 9-dimensional identity space plus the direct span of chosen positive-root coordinate lines, with no omitted continuous parameters in this subclass.

For \(n=3\), a line indexed \((i,k;l,j)\) with \(l<j\) requires \((i-1,k;l,j)\) when \(i>0\), and \((i,k+1;l,j)\) when \(k<2\). At fixed \((i,k)\), either simple root \((0,1)\) or \((1,2)\) requires \((0,2)\); these are all requirements, since the three Borels are generated by diagonal matrices and the simple root actions. At \(r=17\) the set of lines has size \(8\); the code enumerates all 82 order ideals in this poset.

The standard \((210),(120)\) necessary tests require dimension at least \(17\) of the corresponding prolongations. To calculate dimensions exactly, use variables given by fully ordered coordinates in \(A\otimes A\otimes B\) (or \(A\otimes B\otimes B\)) and impose equality under the repeated-factor swap. In any \(AB\) slice the three matched coordinates at each \((i,k)\) must be equal; forbidden off-diagonal coordinates must be zero. All the equations are just identifications or zeros. Use a disjoint set with one distinguished zero variable: solution dimension is the number of components minus one, over any field. There are 14 pair spaces passing both tests. The identical argument counts the \((111)\) prolongation with three sets of slice conditions: 37 of \(14^3\) triples pass the standard test (27 dimension 17, 9 dimension 18, 1 dimension 19).

The new result is that **none of these Cartan-free triples can extend to a length-17 border-apolar witness**, even a nonsaturated one. Use [Mańdziuk, Corollary 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12121v1#S4) in \((\mathbb P^8)^3\): length 17 requires \(H_{S/I^2}(220)\ge(2\cdot8+1)17=289\), applicable since 289 is at most \(\dim S_{210}=405\). In particular the codimension of \(I_{110}^2\) in \(S_{220}\) must be at least 289, since the full squared ideal contains that part. By Lemma 4.6 of the same primary paper, this codimension is \(\dim\ker(E_{AB}\otimes A\otimes B\to\Lambda^2 A\otimes\Lambda^2 B)-\binom{17}{2}\). The map sends \((\sum a'\otimes b')\otimes a\otimes b\) to \(\sum(a'\wedge a)\otimes(b'\wedge b)\). Thus the wedge map rank must be at most \(17\cdot81-136-289=952\). Every tested column has at most three signed entries. Exact modular Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) yields 12 failing candidate ranks (960–963); any modular nonzero minor is nonzero over \(\mathbb C\). The two remaining masks need not be accepted as feasible: both have modular rank 945, but the eight ordered triples of these two masks have exact \((111)\) dimension 9 and fail. In the checker mask indexing \(3(3i+k)+r\), where \(r=0,1,2\) orders the roots \((0,1),(0,2),(1,2)\), the two masks are 110808 and 221616. No finite-field **ordinary tensor rank** claim is made; the finite field is only a valid minor certificate for the complex linear maps. This strengthens the purely saturated statement below at \(r=17\), but applies only to three Cartan-free inverse systems. Strict subspace restrictions and the direction of modular inference are essential.

## 2. Sharp saturated torus-fixed obstruction in every dimension \(n\ge3\)

Let \(Z\) be any zero-dimensional subscheme of \(\mathbb P A\times\mathbb P B\times\mathbb P C\), invariant for the separate diagonal tori of \(U,V,W\), whose saturated ideal satisfies \(I(Z)_{111}\subseteq M_n^\perp\). Assume that each pair inverse system is contained in the span of the identity directions and strictly positive root coordinates just described; no particular Borel stability is needed for this part. Then
\[
\operatorname{length}(Z)\ge (2n-2)^3 .
\]
This lower bound is sharp even for smoothable schemes satisfying these hypotheses. For \(n=3\) the minimum is **64**, in this *saturated Cartan-free torus-fixed model only*. Here “saturated” means the full ideal of the subscheme; the lower proof uses that sections vanishing on the scheme belong to this ideal, not merely to the saturation of a candidate graded ideal.

**Lower bound.** Use the same letters for coordinate functions when writing scheme equations. The pair condition gives \(a_{ij}b_{lk}=0\) in the local ring for \(l>j\), and \(a_{ij}b_{jk}\) is independent of \(j\) for every \(i,k\). Similarly for the other two pairs. At a support point choose a nonzero entry in each of \(A,B\); the two pivots must use indices \(j_A>j_B\). The forbidden negative equation excludes \(j_B>j_A\), and the diagonal equalities exclude equality: if a matched product is nonzero at the support point, all its shared-index alternatives are nonzero, contradicting a forbidden negative product. If \(j_A<n-1\), the unit \(a_{i,j_A}\) forces all of row \(n-1\) of \(B\) to vanish scheme-theoretically in this chart, so all matched \(AB\) products vanish by diagonal equality. If \(j_B>0\), the unit in \(B\) similarly forces column \(0\) of \(A\) to vanish and gives the same conclusion. Thus for a matched \(AB\) product to survive at a support point, every nonzero pivot of \(A\) uses column \(n-1\), and every nonzero pivot of \(B\) uses row \(0\). Applying this to all pairs, a matched cyclic monomial \(a_{ij}b_{jk}c_{ki}\) can survive only at the one extreme point \(P=([a_{0,n-1}],[b_{0,n-1}],[c_{0,n-1}])\). Since it pairs to 1 with \(M_n\), it is not in \(I(Z)_{111}\) and indeed survives locally at \(P\).

In the affine chart normalizing those three pivot coordinates to 1, all matched cyclic monomials equal one common element by diagonal equality in each shared index. For every \(j=1,\ldots,n-2\), put \(x_{V,j}=a_{0j},\ y_{V,j}=b_{j,n-1}\) and \(h_V=b_{n-1,n-1}\). Then \(h_V=x_{V,j}y_{V,j}\) by diagonal equality. Define \(x_{W,j},y_{W,j},h_W\) and \(x_{U,j},y_{U,j},h_U\) cyclically. The common matched product is \(H=h_Uh_Vh_W\): one choice of matched cyclic monomial is \(a_{0,n-1}b_{n-1,n-1}c_{n-1,0}\), and \(c_{n-1,0}=c_{n-1,n-1}a_{n-1,n-1}=h_Wh_U\) by the \(CA\) equality and the pivot normalization. For each of the three factors the \(2n-2\) functions \(1,h,x_j,y_j\) are divisors of that factor's \(h\). Every product of one choice from each list is a divisor of the nonzero \(H\), hence nonzero in \(\mathcal O_{Z,P}\). The weights in one list, up to simultaneous sign convention, are \(0,\epsilon_{n-1}-\epsilon_0,\epsilon_{n-1}-\epsilon_j,\epsilon_j-\epsilon_0\). They are pairwise distinct, and the three lists use separate diagonal tori. Hence their \((2n-2)^3\) products have distinct weights, making them linearly independent in the finite local ring. This proves the lower bound. Non-torus schemes are not claimed to obey it.

**Sharp construction.** For each shared index use a local algebra \(Q\) with basis \(1,x_j,y_j,h\) for \(1\le j\le n-2\), with the only nonzero positive-degree products \(x_i y_j=\delta_{ij}h\). Let \(R=Q_U\otimes Q_V\otimes Q_W\). On the pivot chart, represent \(A\)'s coordinate matrix by the outer product \((1,\{y_{U,j}\},h_U)^t(h_V,\{x_{V,j}\},1)\), and similarly \(B,C\) cyclically. Each \(x\) and \(y\) appears directly as a chart coordinate, so this defines a closed immersion of the finite local scheme. In any pair \(AB\), the contraction inner matrix has entries \(c_j r_l\) where \(c=(h_V,\{x_{V,j}\},1)\), \(r=(1,\{y_{V,j}\},h_V)\). They vanish for \(l>j\) and equal \(h_V\) at \(l=j\). Thus all three pairs satisfy the restriction; the chart scheme is torus-fixed with the stated weights and is supported only at \(P\). Evaluation of a trilinear monomial on the scheme factors into three such inner products. The coefficient functional selecting \(H\) returns 1 precisely at the matched cyclic terms, and zero at every other term. This shows \(I(Z)_{111}\subset M_n^\perp\).

To see smoothability of \(Q\), write \(m=2(n-2)\). For \(m\ge2\), the \(m+2\) distinct points \(0, t e_1,\dots,t e_m,t(1,\ldots,1)\) in affine \(m\)-space have the flat limit whose local dual basis is evaluation at the origin, its \(m\) first derivatives, and the nondegenerate quadratic functional with polar matrix \(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}-I_m\), up to scalar. Indeed rescale the first differences of the coordinate-point evaluations by \(t^{-1}\); subtract the sum of coordinate-point evaluations and add \((m-1)\) times the origin evaluation to the last evaluation and divide by \(t^2\). All limiting functionals are independent already on polynomials of degree ≤2. Their limits annihilate degrees ≥3 and thus describe a length-\(m+2\) local algebra. Equivalently choose degree≤2 polynomials giving a dual basis at \(t=0\); the invertible evaluation matrix in this rescaled functional basis gives regular structure constants for their quotient algebra near 0, establishing flatness. Over \(\mathbb C\) the nondegenerate quadratic form is equivalent to that defining \(Q\). The product of the three flat smoothings, embedded by the same chart coordinate formulas (the \(x,y\) generators are always retained), smooths \(Z\). For \(n=3\), one can alternatively just use six variables with squares zero and smooth each variable's equation \(z^2=0\) to \(z(z-t)=0\).

**Caveat and prior comparison.** Border apolarity uses ideals in the closure of the radical generic-Hilbert-function locus, and does not require the limiting ideal to be saturated; its saturation need not still be apolar. See [Buczyńska–Buczyński](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01944), and the explicit distinction in [Mańdziuk](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12121v1#S3). Therefore this exact model minimum is not a lower bound for either the full border rank or full cactus rank. A targeted primary-source search found Mańdziuk's recent \(M_3\) calculation at \(r=16\), but not the restricted length-17 elimination or the sharp saturated Cartan-free formula above; no priority claim.
