# Two capped projections and a broad ratio band of wide fibers

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

**Proposition.** For finite simple tripartite \(3\)-uniform \(H\) with \(AB\) and \(BC\) codegree at most \(D\le m\), suppose each occupied \(AC\) fiber has size between \(w\) and \(2w\), where \(w\ge8192Dm\). Then there is an internally valid subfamily of size \(\Omega(|H|/m^3)\), including witnesses for empty intersections. No bound is imposed on the total weight of the top \(m\) fibers, unlike the [robust wide-fiber theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/1409fcd805a90f25559834af04b465d9648bb4af/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/InternalSunflowersRobustWideFibers.md). This does not address unbounded ratios of wide sizes or one-capped systems.

First prove the nonempty-only bound assuming the slightly wider band \([w,4w]\) and \(w\ge4096Dm\). Let \(K=2048\), \(K_B=2^{24}\), \(K'=2^{30}\). In the *unweighted* support graph of \(AC\) pairs peel any vertex of current positive degree less than \(Km\), assigning the removed *whole fibers* to the owner side. The resulting core has minimum positive support degree at least \(Km\) on both sides. Each owner piece has support degree at most \(Km\) on the owner side. Always choose a largest piece up to constant loss. Fiber sizes stay in the same band. In any probabilistic case below, if original \(B\)-vertices of degree less than \(K_B m^2\) carry at least a quarter of the piece's triples, apply the [bounded-coordinate full lower bound](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/bd26fabdad5655c11934e39034148bd448511080/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/InternalSunflowersCappedFrontier.md#sharp-degeneracy-range-up-to-order-m2) to that subfamily, obtaining \(\Omega(|H|/m^3)\). Otherwise discard those vertices *before sampling*. All budgets use the surviving labels' counts and the fiber weights *before* this discard; they do not assume the remaining labels or fibers still meet any lower degree bound.

**The dense support core.** Let \(E\) be its original triple count. The sum of top-\(m\) incident original fiber weights over \(A,C\) is at most \(8E/K\): each endpoint's top sum is at most \(4wm\), whereas its own full incident weight is at least \(wKm\). Use independent *triple* sampling at \(p=1/(16D)\) in the residual after discarding the small \(B\)-vertices. Remove all sampled AB and BC conflicts, and discard a sampled fiber if its sample size exceeds \(4p\) times its original weight \(f\). For a sampled edge the two deletion probabilities together are at most \(2(D-1)p+(1+pf)/(4pf)<1/2\). Expected survivors are at least \(3pE/8\). Next repeatedly peel positive \(B\) degrees below \(m\), occupied \(AC\) fibers below \(m\), and positive \(A,C\) support degrees below \(m\). These delete at most
\[
 \frac{E}{K_Bm}+\frac{mE}{w}+4p\frac{8E}{K}<\frac{pE}{8}.
\]
For the first bound charge each original high \(B\) at most \(m\) edges. For the second use the original count of occupied pairs. For the last use the cap \(4pf\) and the top-m mass condition computed in the original core. These budgets are deterministic. In the residue AB,BC are injective, each AC fiber has at least \(m\) distinct \(B\)-completions, every active \(B\) has an \(m\)-matching link, and every active A/C has at least \(m\) AC neighbors, so an \(m\)-matching link follows by greedily choosing distinct \(B\)-completions. Thus the residue witnesses all nonempty kernels, at size \(\Omega(E/m)\) or larger.

**A bounded support side.** Say \(A\) has support maximum \(Km\) (interchanging \(A,C\) if needed), and partition by whether the \(C\)-support degree in this piece is at least \(K'm^2\). On the low side split the support graph into at most \(K'm^2\) matchings by bipartite edge coloring. A largest in weight retains at least \(\Omega(E/m^2)\) triples. Keep all of its fibers. The incidence graph between these pair nodes \(ac\) and \(B\) is simple, and its [graph nonempty extraction lemma](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/ed2d8f37c98e60ac70be85ac0275566e11bb4c3b/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/InternalSunflowersLowDegeneracy.md) retains \(\Omega(1/m)\) with each positive degree 1 or at least \(m\). If two selected triples share a pair-node their intersection is exactly \(ac\), and if they share a \(B\) but different pairs their intersection is exactly that \(B\). Thus the modified family meets the target.

For the high \(C\) piece, whose original edge count is now \(E\), discard the low \(B\)-degree vertices as above. Independently for each \(a\), choose at most one original \(AC\) fiber, each with marginal probability \(p=1/(4Km)\), which is possible by the support bound at \(A\). Keep only triples in chosen fibers. Remove all selected triples sharing a BC pair with another selected triple. Conditional on an edge's fiber being chosen, the probability of any such rival is at most \((D-1)p\): rival A labels are different from its own and choose independently, and there are at most \(D-1\) rival triples. The \(AB\) projection is automatically injective since each \(a\) has a unique chosen \(c\). The expected number of remaining triples is at least \(pE/2\). Peel low positive \(B\) degrees (<m), low occupied \(AC\) fibers (<m), and low positive \(C\) support degrees (<m), until stable. The respective budgets are at most
\[
 \frac{E}{K_Bm},\qquad \frac{mpE}{w}\ \text{in expectation},\qquad \frac{4mE}{K'm^2}.
\]
For the middle bound only chosen \(AC\) pairs can be charged, and in expectation at most \(p E/w\) are chosen. For the last, each deletion at a \(C\) loses fewer than \(m\) fibers of original weight at most \(4w\), and \(|C|\le E/(wK'm^2)\). The sum of budgets is less than \(pE/4\). Singleton \(A\) cannot arise because each \(a\) is monochromatic. Singleton \(B\) and \(C\), and repeated \(AC\) kernels, have witnesses by exactly the same link arguments as in the dense core. This gives \(\Omega(E/m)\) in the high case. Overall the worst nonempty guarantee is \(\Omega(E/m^3)\).

**Empty kernel.** If an \(m\)-matching can be reserved with endpoint set meeting at most half the triples, remove those endpoints. Of the original fibers \(ac\), only ones with an unremoved \(a,c\) remain, and each loses at most \(m\) \(B\)-completions. Thus the new band lies in \([w/2,2w]\) with \(w/2\ge4096Dm\), so the nonempty theorem applies. Restore the disjoint matching to witness empty intersections, without creating any other new intersections. Otherwise some matching endpoint has degree at least \(E/(6m)\) (or, if there is no \(m\)-matching, endpoints of a maximal smaller matching cover all \(E\) triples). The [full graph extraction lemma](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/ed2d8f37c98e60ac70be85ac0275566e11bb4c3b/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/InternalSunflowersLowDegeneracy.md) in its link gives a family of size \(\Omega(E/m^3)\). Restoring the fixed vertex changes the empty graph kernel into that vertex and does not create empty intersections among triples.
