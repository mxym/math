# Separated interval masses remove the small-deficit divisor for heavy paths

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal mathematical checks; no external peer review claimed.

Assume the weighted heavy path skeleton at cutoff \(h\) is already reconstructed among the rivals. The [narrow band](StanleyNonpalindromicHeavyPaths.md#mass-order) and the [common-residue block-count method](StanleyModularBlockCountPaths.md#1-unmarked-mass-order-via-block-count) are two ways to ensure this. Say the path is **deficit separated to order \(d\)** if any two distinct numerical masses of contiguous nonempty intervals of its block-mass word (including the full path) differ by more than \(d\).

Suppose all blocks have size at least \(\max(h+d+1,2d+1)\). Then deficit separation replaces the common divisor used in the marked-cut extraction. For the nonpalindromic narrow-band case, use [one-mark propagation](StanleyNonpalindromicHeavyPaths.md#low-deficits-and-a-propagation-rule), and the size assumption can be \(s_v\ge h+d+1\). For the palindromic case use [two independent deficit marks](StanleyPalindromicTwoDeficit.md). For the common-residue family use the corresponding orientation proofs after the masses are reconstructed. In all these cases the ordinary chromatic symmetric function recovers the rooted series through \(d\); the same bounded-branch criterion reconstructs the whole tree. No common divisor of the masses is needed here.

**Extraction.** Request one or two shortened high indices \(l_i-k_i\), where \(l_i\) are designated intact interval masses of a weighted path cut, \(0\le k_i\le d\), and the remaining indices are intact (\(k_i=0\)). The total small-component mass is \(\sum k_i\le2d\). All roots belong to large components, since a low root would put its entire original block among these small components. Thus the actual intact masses are some interval masses \(l'_i\), assigned to the requested high factors, with \(l'_i\ge l_i-k_i\). If \(l'_i<l_i\) it differs by more than \(d\), so \(l'_i<l_i-k_i\), impossible. All \(l'_i\ge l_i\) and the sums of both lists are the whole order; hence equality holds for each \(i\) and the deficits are the prescribed ones. Two observed high factors are equal only when both their intact mass and their deficit agree: unequal masses would differ by more than the difference between two deficits in \([0,d]\). Thus the same labeled multiplicity rule applies.

**Generic implication.** For a fixed number \(q\) of blocks and fixed \(d\), choose each mass independently uniformly from \([s,2s-1]\). For two distinct indexed intervals, condition on every weight except one in their symmetric difference. The difference of their sums has one remaining coefficient \(\pm1\), so the probability its absolute value is at most \(d\) is at most \((2d+1)/s\). A union bound over the \(\binom{q(q+1)/2}{2}\) pairs proves deficit separation with probability tending to one as \(s\to\infty\). At fixed \(h\), take \(d\ge \sum_{j\le h}j b_j\) to reconstruct every type of compatible rooted ornament on such paths. This probability concerns the mass words, not a distribution on all trees.

**Explicit wide coprime family.** Let the masses satisfy \(s_i\equiv K\bmod M\) with \(K>d\) and \(M>qK+d\), and the same size conditions. Modulo \(M\), the multiples of \(K\) with coefficients \(0,\ldots,q\) are distinct, so the block-count reconstruction applies directly to the raw masses (use \(g=1\) for that mass-only part). For any nonzero difference of interval masses, a difference of block counts \(j\ne0\) has residue \(jK\) at distance \(>d\) from multiples of \(M\); if \(j=0\), the nonzero mass difference is itself a multiple of \(M\). Separation follows. Once the cut products are extracted, the modular orbital propagation uses only block counts and is independent of a common divisor. This permits unbounded mass ratios and, by taking for example two consecutive progression entries with \(\gcd(K,M)=1\), a family with overall gcd one.

**Why certify the order separately?** Without a condition on the mass word, the entire unmarked weighted cut polynomial can be ambiguous. For instance \((1,3,1,4,3)=(1,2)\circ(1,3)\) and \((1,4,3,1,3)=(2,1)\circ(1,3)\) are not reversals but are L-equivalent by [Billera–Thomas–van Willigenburg](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405434). This is not a collision of ordinary chromatic symmetric functions of the ornamented trees.
