# Internal sunflower extraction with all triple facets capped and mixed pair caps

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Let H be a finite simple four-partite hypergraph, m≥2 and let L≥1 be a fixed absolute constant. All triple-coordinate projections have fiber size at most Lm. Each pair-coordinate projection type is either capped (all fibers ≤Lm) or rich (all occupied fibers ≥C m² for sufficiently large C depending on L). Form a graph R on the four coordinate types by adding the rich types; assume R has no vertex of degree 1. For every isolated type assume its individual vertices each either have degree ≤Lm or degree ≥C m². Then H contains a fully internally valid subfamily on Ω_L(E/m⁴) edges. For nonempty intersections the stronger Ω_L(E/m) bound holds. This covers a mixed pair-cap regime with all four triple facets capped, not general rank 4.

Proof. Set E=|H|, p=1/(64(L+1)m), and allow any starting residue H' on ≥E/2 edges, with budgets still counted in H. Independently sample each edge of H' with probability p. Delete an edge whenever another sampled edge shares one of its four triple projections, any capped pair projection, or a label of degree at most Lm on an isolated side. Conditional on a particular edge sampled, the union probability of any conflict is at most (4+6+4)Lmp<1/4. The retained family is triple-injective across every type, capped-pair-injective, and all tested low-degree labels have become unique. Iteratively peel (i) any occupied rich pair fiber with size less than 3m and (ii) any positive isolated originally high-degree vertex of degree less than m. Any pair or vertex is paid at most once; the number of rich pairs of each type is ≤E/(C m²), and the number of originally high-degree isolated vertices on each such side is ≤E/(C m²). Thus the total further loss is ≤(18+4)E/(Cm), deterministically. Take C≫ L to retain Ω_L(pE) edges in expectation.

Witnesses. No triple is a realized intersection by injectivity. Any repeated pair is rich and its link is a matching: two link edges sharing a vertex would share an original triple. There are at least 3m link edges. For a vertex v in an isolated type, its link is a matching by injectivity of each pair containing v, so every repeated v has the required m petals. At a nonisolated type, fix any v of positive degree. Its link is a simple linear tripartite hypergraph. Two distinct partite classes correspond to rich pair types adjacent to v’s type, and each positive vertex in both those classes has link degree ≥3m. At least one vertex occurs in the first class, and the distinct incident link edges have distinct vertices in the second class by linearity; hence the second class contains ≥3m positive vertices. If a maximal link matching had fewer than m edges, choose a positive vertex in that second class outside its cover. Its degree is ≥3m. Every incident link edge must hit the matching cover, yet every cover vertex is in at most one such incident edge by linearity, contradiction to cover size <3m. This certifies the singleton intersection condition.

Empty intersection. If some m-matching leaves at least E/2 host edges after deleting the vertices it touches, use the nonempty proof on that residue and add back the m disjoint edges. They introduce no nonempty intersections. Otherwise, either an existing m-matching touches ≥E/2 edges, or there is no m-matching and the vertices of one maximal shorter matching touch all edges. In either case some vertex has degree ≥E/(8m). Use the already proved 3-uniform full internal theorem on its unweighted simple link, at Ω(deg(v)/m³) edges, and lift by restoring v. This gives Ω(E/m⁴) fully valid, as claimed.

Source target: [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu, Section 6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6). A no-degree-one hypothesis is necessary for the specific final singleton argument: a linear 3-link consisting of two disjoint large stars, both centers in its only rich-side class, meets positive rich-side degree ≥3m but has matching size two; edges from different stars realize the singleton original kernel. It is not a counterexample to the unrestricted conjecture or any extraction result.

## Degree-one regime with only AB rich and two capped singleton sides

Suppose all AB fibers are at least C m², all other pair projections and the C,D individual vertex degrees are capped at Lm. Then Ω_L(E/m²) nonempty-valid edges and Ω_L(E/m³) full exist. Every triple contains a capped pair, so no additional triple assumption is needed.

For full extraction, first strengthen the known weighted bipartite nonempty lemma to weighted full extraction retaining Ω(1/m²): if an m-matching has endpoints whose removal loses at most half the weight, apply the weighted nonempty lemma to the residual and restore the matching. Otherwise some vertex has incident weight at least E/(4m), either by counting an existing m-matching’s endpoint costs or by looking at the cover formed by a maximal shorter matching. Keep the entire star if it has at least m edges, or its heaviest edge otherwise. That retains at least E/(4m²) by a simple weight average; the star/singleton is fully valid.

Now the AB support is simple; give its edges the fiber sizes and apply the weighted bipartite full lemma (nonempty variant if desired). It keeps Ω(E/m²) (respectively Ω(E/m)) of the mass. Retain whole fibers of selected support edges. Make a conflict graph on the retained hyperedges, joining edges if they share a capped categorical pair or a C/D label. Its maximum degree is ≤7Lm. Let D'=ceil(7Lm) and k=3D'−1. Divide every retained AB fiber separately into full blocks of k, discarding the tail of each. The fibers start at least C m², so for C large enough each has at least m full blocks, and the division loses at most half of its weight. By [Haxell's strong coloring theorem](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/abs/on-the-strong-chromatic-number/943BC5E191E2E3ED8624880CA2962604), this conflict graph admits a proper k-coloring with every color present once in every block. Consequently any color class simultaneously keeps every selected AB support edge at least m times, and retains Ω_L(E/m³) (respectively Ω_L(E/m²)) mass. Both C,D labels are unique. Any repeated AB pair is witnessed by its ≥m completions. Any A, B, or empty kernel requires distinct AB projections, and its projected m-petal witness lifts by choosing one quadruple per support edge, since all C,D labels are globally unique.

This auxiliary use of strong coloring is structurally different from sampling then peeling: it gives deterministic per-fiber survival, rather than an expected total budget, which preserves the projected witnesses. The additional exponent in the full variant comes from preserving a disjoint AB matching, not any four-uniform link argument.
