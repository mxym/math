# Salmon residual support has codimension at least five

Let \(X=\widehat{\sigma_4}((\mathbb P^3)^3)\) have dimension 40, \(I=I(X)\), and \(J=J_{5,6,9}\) be the ideal of the known equations. Then \(\dim\operatorname{Supp}(I/J)\le35\). This is only a support bound; it does not prove saturation or global generation.

## Reduction to type (3,3,4)

The [earlier support proof](SalmonResidualSupportCodimensionFour.md) bounds possible bad inverse-slice points and all-singular points of type \((3,4,4)\) by dimension 35. An all-singular \((2,4,4)\) tensor lies in a proper determinant-zero subset of the \(2\times4\times4\) core: any one fixed coordinate slice already has zero determinant. Adding \(\dim\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)=4\) gives dimension at most \(31+4=35\). A single support of size one only gives \(16+3\) parameters. The \((3,3,3)\) compression has dimension at most \(26+9=35\), since the 27-dimensional core must satisfy its nonzero Strassen nonic (projection preserves the border rank bound). Strata with two deficient modes including a rank at most two have dimension at most \(24+7=31\). It remains to control (3,3,4) and permutations thereof.

Use the [previous unrestriction counts](SalmonResidualSupportCodimensionFour.md): nongorenstein or exceptional module images have dimension at most 35, and Gorenstein regular types with automorphism dimension at least three have images of dimension at most 35. Among the remaining Gorenstein regular types are four simple factors, one dual-number factor plus two simple factors, two dual-number factors, or a length-three curvilinear factor plus a simple factor. Indeed the local length-four Gorenstein Hilbert function \((1,2,1)\) has automorphism dimension at least three: independently add socle terms to two generators, and scale them both while scaling the socle by the square. The monogenic length-four automorphism group has dimension three. Products of shorter local algebras give precisely the listed types or nongorenstein cases.

## Fixed-support parametrization and normal forms

For a Gorenstein algebra \(R\) of dimension four, each concise source of its type is mode-wise equivalent to its Frobenius trilinear tensor. Compress its first two factors by rank-three maps \(P_A,P_B\), and transform its third factor by an arbitrary \(C\in\mathrm{GL}_4\). This gives a 40-dimensional parameter space with fixed support spaces; varying supports adds six. Each projection-kernel line lies in \(\mathbb P^3\) with fibers \(\mathrm{GL}_3\), so a locus cut out by \(b\) independent coordinate zero conditions on the lines has dimension \(40-b\) before varying supports.

Along the fibers there is a group of dimension \(8+\dim\operatorname{Aut}(R)\), from independent multiplications by units on A and B, and a common algebra automorphism \(\theta\) acting on all factors. For clarity, under the duality of a Frobenius functional \(\lambda\), the tensor corresponds to \(\lambda(xyz)\), multiplication by any element can be transferred between the three factors, and the action of the same automorphism on all three factors is equivalent to multiplication by a unit on one factor. The last assertion follows because the adjoint of \(\theta\) for \(\lambda(xy)\) is \(M_w\theta^{-1}\), where \(w\) is the unit with \(\lambda\circ\theta=\lambda(w\,\cdot)\); inserting three such adjoints again gives a Frobenius functional differing by a unit. Thus the third map can be compensated by an action of form \(M_z\theta\). The action is free when the compressed tensor has C-flattening rank four: fixing \(C\) implies \(M_z\theta=1\), so \(z=1,\theta=1\) by applying it to the unit. The A/B units are then reciprocal. If \(P_A(M_x-1)=0\) for the first unit \(x\), the compressed C-slice of \(x-1\) vanishes, since its uncompressed slice is \(M_{x-1}G\) with \(G\) an invertible pairing matrix. C-concision forces \(x=1\). Therefore invariant projection-kernel loci with \(b\) zero conditions have image dimension at most \(38-b-\dim\operatorname{Aut}(R)\).

* For a single dual factor, use the ordered basis \((\epsilon,1_D,e_2,e_3)\); the unit action on a kernel vector is \((k_0,k_1,k_2,k_3)\mapsto(a k_0+b k_1,a k_1,c k_2,d k_3)\), \(acd\ne0\). Thus the open type has \(k_1k_2k_3\ne0\), and its sample is the prior tangent tensor. The codimension-one boundary types are \(k_1=0\) with other entries nonzero, and \(k_2=0\) with \(k_1k_3\ne0\) (including the singleton swap \(k_3=0\)). These are each a single orbit on the kernel, by the displayed unit formula. A second zero condition (including \(k_0=0\) in the \(k_1=0\) case) bounds the image by \(38-2-1=35\). These boundary classes are invariant, allowing the swaps of the two singleton factors, and apply to either projection.
* For two dual factors the basis is \((\epsilon_1,1_1,\epsilon_2,1_2)\), with independent upper triangular unit actions on its paired coordinates. The generic \(k_1k_3\ne0\) locus is a single orbit on the kernel; the complement satisfies one extra coordinate condition and has image dimension at most \(38-1-2=35\).
* For a length-three factor and a singleton use \((t^2,t,1_D,e_3)\). Units on the first three coordinates are upper triangular Toeplitz matrices with nonzero diagonal, independently of the singleton scaling. So \(k_2k_3\ne0\) is a single orbit on the kernel, and its complement contributes at most 35.

Transitivity on both kernels implies mode-wise equivalence of compressed tensors, because the two unit actions are independent, while the compensating C action is absorbable by the invertible third basis. Two rank-three maps with the same kernel differ by an invertible change of output basis.
