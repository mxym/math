# Internal rank four with one quadratic-degree side and capped cross projections

**Yongxian Zhang.** AI-assisted proof development and internal checks; no external peer review claimed.

Fix \(L\ge1,m\ge2\). Let \(H\) be finite simple four-partite on coordinates \(A,B,C,D\), with C-degrees at most \(Lm^2\). Suppose the three pair types \(AC,BC,CD\) and the triple type \(ABD\) have multiplicities at most \(Lm\). Finally assume \(AB\) multiplicity at most \(Lm^2\) (up to relabeling the three non-C coordinates). No maxima are imposed on A/B/D vertex degrees or on either AD or BD pair multiplicities. Then there is an internally \(m\)-sunflower-witnessed subfamily of size \(\Omega_L(|H|/m^4)\), including the empty kernel. These are hypotheses on maxima only, with no gap or fiber-size uniformity assumption. This is a restricted case of [Janzer–Jin–Sudakov–Wu §6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-026-00222-1#Sec6), not the unconditional fourth rank or the solely bounded side case.

## Earlier results used

Use the [rank-three release](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/internal-sunflower-triples-unrestricted-v1.0.0) for (i) weighted simple bipartite graph extraction: retain \(\Omega(1/m)\) of the weight with positive degrees equal to \(1\) or at least \(m\); (ii) the nonempty bound \(\Omega(m^{-2})\) for simple tripartite triples with two injective pair projections and third pair multiplicity at most \(m\); and (iii) unrestricted full rank-three extraction \(\Omega(m^{-3})\).

We also use the following nonempty asymmetric degree exit (parameter version of the [hybrid-cubic theorem](./InternalSunflowersHybridCubicProjection.md)): if a rank-four host has C-degree at most \(K m\) and one other side vertex degree at most \(R m^2\), it has a nonempty-valid subfamily of size \(\Omega_{K,R}(E/m^3)\). To see this, use the hybrid proof with \(p\) a sufficiently small constant times \(1/m\) rather than \(1/m^2\). In the light projected \(ABD\)-fibers (size \(<3m\)), activate individual edges; the C and same-projected-tuple conflicts still have small conditional probability, and at the second bounded side the conditional initial expected number of other sampled edges is \(O(m)\). Cap the sampled degree there at a suitably large constant times \(m\). In the heavy fibers, activate whole \(ABD\) groups, make C labels disjoint across groups, cap the number of groups touching the other bounded-side label in the same way, and discard groups shrunk below \(m\) using the conditional Markov bound. Use the weighted simple rank-three bounded-side lemma from the hybrid proof in either case; its \(\Omega(m^{-2})\) bound on the simple support multiplies by the initial \(\Omega(m^{-1})\) retention. No empty-kernel claim at the \(m^{-3}\) scale is needed.

## Initial thinning and the clean-host claim

Independently sample each input edge at \(p_1=1/[128(L+1)m]\). Remove every sampled edge that collides in the initial sample at \(AC,BC,CD\) or \(ABD\), and all sampled edges at C labels or AB tuples with initially sampled degree or multiplicity exceeding \(16m\). Given one edge sampled, the conditional chance of a projection collision is at most \(4Lm p_1\le1/32\), and the expected count of other sample members at its C label or AB tuple is at most \(Lm^2p_1\le m/128\). Markov bounds the two cutoff losses by \(2/128\) (with room to spare). Hence a deterministic outcome keeps \(\Omega_L(E/m)\) edges, all four listed projections injective, C degree \(O(m)\), and AB cap \(O(m)\). It suffices to prove:

**Clean-host claim.** A simple four-partite host with every C-cross pair injective, the complementary projected triple \(ABD\) injective, C-degree at most \(K m\), and one non-C pair type capped by \(K m\), admits a nonempty kernel extraction of size \(\Omega_K(E/m^3)\).

Every intermediate restriction below inherits both injectivity promises and the C degree cap. Once C labels are made globally unique in an output, projected nonempty witnesses lift without collisions, and there are no extra nonempty kernels. In every use of whole fibers below the fibers are grouped by pairs of non-C coordinates and have distinct labels in each of the two other coordinates, by C-cross injectivity and complementary-projection injectivity.

## Two capped non-C projections

First prove the clean-host claim under the stronger assumption that two of the three non-C pair types are capped by \(K_1m\). Write the non-C coordinates as \(X,Y,Z\), with \(X\) common to the capped pair types \(XY,XZ\). The \(YZ\) fiber is the set of extensions with those two labels fixed. Split this case into light fibers of original size \(f<Qm^2\) and wide fibers of original size \(f\ge Qm^2\). Throughout the proof take a sufficiently small positive \(c=c(K,K_1)\), put \(p=c/m\), and take constants \(Q,R\) large enough in terms of \(1/c\), \(K\), and \(K_1\).

In the light class sample edges independently at \(p_\ell=c_\ell/m\), with \(c_\ell\) chosen additionally small in terms of \(Q\). Delete every initially sampled edge colliding at \(C,XY,XZ\) and remove every initially sampled \(YZ\) fiber with sampled size greater than \(m\). Conditional on one edge sampled, the conflict bound is \((K+2K_1)m p_\ell\) and the expected number of other initially sampled edges in the same fiber is at most \(p_\ell Qm^2\), so the cap-failure probability is at most \(c_\ell Q\) by Markov at threshold \(m\). Thus some outcome keeps \(\Omega(E/m)\) edges. The simple projected triples have \(XY,XZ\) injective and \(YZ\) capped at \(m\); apply (ii) and lift via globally unique C labels.

For wide fibers, define \(\operatorname{Top}_m(Y)\) as the sum, over \(Y\) labels, of the original total sizes of their \(m\) largest \(YZ\) buckets (or all if fewer); define \(\operatorname{Top}_m(Z)\) analogously. All references below to fiber counts/weights use the current branch base *before* optional X-low deletion, and every occupied \(YZ\) fiber of such a base is still wide since the preliminary restrictions choose whole fibers.

**Diffuse on both endpoints.** Suppose \(\operatorname{Top}_m(Y)+\operatorname{Top}_m(Z)\le E/64\). If the edges at labels \(x\) of degree \(<Rm^2\) have mass at least \(E/4\), use the asymmetric degree exit on those edges. Otherwise discard those edges; at least \(3E/4\) remains and there were at most \(E/(Rm^2)\) remaining positive X labels. Independently sample remaining edges at \(p\), forbid collisions at \(C,XY,XZ\), and reject any sampled \(YZ\) fiber whose initial sample size exceeds \(4pf\), where \(f\) is its base size. Conditional on a fixed sample edge, union bound gives collision probability at most \((K+2K_1)mp\), and Markov bounds the fiber rejection probability by \((1+pf)/(4pf)\). Thus initial retained expectation is at least \(pE/3\) for appropriate \(c,Q\).
Iteratively remove a positive \(X\) label of degree below \(m\), a positive \(YZ\) fiber of size below \(m\), or a positive \(Y\) or \(Z\) label with fewer than \(m\) current opposite neighbors. The three combined budgets are respectively \(E/(Rm)\), \(E/(Qm)\) and \(4pE/64\). The first two use the original number of high X labels and of wide YZ fibers, respectively. The third charges each peeled endpoint once, against its original top \(m\) buckets and the cap \(4pf\). Choosing \(R,Q\) large enough makes the sum less than \(pE/4\). All surviving C labels are unique, and all positive \(XY,XZ\) pairs are unique. A \(YZ\) pair has \(m\) distinct X and C extensions. A positive X label has an \(m\)-sunflower since its other three labels never repeat. A positive Y or Z label has \(m\) distinct opposite neighbors and can use one edge per neighbor, with X and C labels disjoint. These witness every nonempty kernel.

**Concentrated on an endpoint.** Otherwise one of \(Y,Z\), say \(Y\), has top-\(m\) mass greater than \(E/128\). Restrict to its top-\(m\) buckets and reset \(E\) to that mass. Thus Y support in the projected \(YZ\) graph is at most \(m\), and all fibers are still wide. If the new \(\operatorname{Top}_m(Z)\ge pE/32\), take the top \(m\) buckets at each Z as well. This keeps at least \(pE/32\) edges, in a weighted projected graph with maximum support degree \(m\) on both sides. Activate each occupied \(YZ\) pair independently at \(p'=c'/m\), and drop all active pairs with an active competitor sharing either endpoint. Additionally drop edges sharing a C label with any other initially active \(YZ\) fiber. Such a conflict always uses a different pair because \(CY\) is injective. Conditional on an edge's \(YZ\) pair being active, union bound on the at most \(2m\) projected competitors and \(K m\) C competitors gives total loss at most \((2+K)mp'\). Choose \(c'\) small. Obtain an outcome with \(\Omega(pE/m)\) edges in a matching of \(YZ\) buckets and globally unique C labels. Compress \(YZ\) to a supervertex, making a simple bipartite graph between \(X\) and such pairs (simplicity follows from complementary triple injectivity). Apply (i) with unit weights, losing \(O(m)\) and retaining \(\Omega(pE/m^2)=\Omega(E/m^3)\). Nonempty repeated kernels are \(X\) or \(YZ\), both witnessed by the bipartite degrees exactly because the selected \(YZ\) buckets form a matching and C labels are unique.

It remains to consider \(\operatorname{Top}_m(Z)<pE/32\). Apply the same X-low branch and discard as in the diffuse case (these operations do not increase top masses or Y support). At each Y, choose at most one whole nonempty \(YZ\) fiber, each with marginal probability \(p\), independently across Y labels; the residual probability produces no choice, since there are at most \(m\) such fibers and \(mp\le1\). Delete every retained edge sharing a C label or an \(XZ\) pair with another initially retained edge. A C rival has distinct Y by pair injectivity, and an XZ rival has distinct Y by complementary triple injectivity. Conditional on the fixed edge retained initially, both kinds of rival are therefore retained with probability \(p\). The probability of these deletions is at most \((K+K_1)mp\), giving initial surviving expectation at least \(pE/2\). The \(XY\) projection is automatically injective because each Y is monochromatic and the complementary triple is injective. Interleave peeling positive X degree below \(m\), positive \(YZ\) fiber size below \(m\), and Z support below \(m\), with budgets respectively \(E/(Rm)\), \(pE/(Qm)\) (only chosen pairs charged), and \(pE/32\) (top-\(m\) bound without scaling). This leaves \(\Omega(pE)\) in expectation. The witnesses are as in the diffuse case, except that Y is monochromatic and cannot be a singleton intersection. Finite initial splits cost only constant factors; this proves the two-cap case.

## One capped non-C projection

Suppose just AB is capped by \(Km\). Run a cleaning routine at the start and after each restriction below: repeatedly peel any positive \(AD\) or \(BD\) fiber with size less than \(Q_1m\), \(Q_1\) a sufficiently large absolute constant. Assign edges deleted at a fiber to its deleting projection type, so each owner subhost has that type capped at \(Q_1m\) (each emptied label tuple is charged just once). If this loses at least half the mass, the larger owner piece keeps at least a quarter and is covered by the two-cap theorem. Otherwise retain the residual, with all positive AD/BD pair fibers of size at least \(Q_1m\). At most three cleanings are needed.

The cleaned host is subjected to soft endpoint locks, with \(A,B\) initially unlocked and \(p=c_1/m\) for \(c_1\) sufficiently small relative to \(K\). For each unlocked side, count the summed original top-\(K'\) mass by the bucket indexing its distinct D neighbors, \(K'=\lceil C_0m/p\rceil\) and \(C_0\) large absolute. If a side's top mass exceeds \(E/C_0\), restrict to precisely these buckets on that side, lock that side with support cap \(K'\), reduce \(p\) to \(1/(C_0K')\), and clean again. This preserves a constant fraction unless we already exit by the two-cap theorem. Locked caps persist through further restrictions; later rate decreases only improve their conflict bounds. There are at most two locks, so final \(p=\Omega_K(m^{-3})\), and each unlocked side has top mass \(\le E/C_0\) at its final \(K'\).

Independently sample D labels at the final \(p\). Delete every sampled edge colliding at AB or at C in the initial sample, and for each locked A or B label delete its incident edges if two or more of its original D neighbors are sampled. In an AB or C collision, the rival has different D by the complementary triple or CD injectivity, respectively; both rival counts are at most \(Km\). Conditional on an edge being initially sampled, the probability it fails is therefore at most \(2Kmp+2/C_0\). The survivors have AB and C injective, and the locked labels are monochromatic.

Interleave deletion of positive AD/BD pairs of current size below \(m\) and deletion at unlocked positive A/B labels with fewer than \(m\) D neighbors. There are at most \(E/(Q_1m)\) occupied pairs per relevant type in the original cleaned host, and only a pair with D sampled can ever trigger, so both types together cost at most \(2pE/Q_1\) in expectation. At an unlocked label, charge its \(m\) largest initially sampled bucket weights. In its original top \(K'\) the expected sampled mass is \(p\) times their total weight; outside the top \(K'\) each bucket weighs at most the label's mass divided by \(K'\). Thus the total expected cost at each unlocked side is at most \(pE/C_0+mE/K'\le 2pE/C_0\); this only uses initial sample statistics regardless of later deletions. Altogether a constant fraction of \(pE\) survives in expectation.

Every repeated pair kernel has type AD or BD and \(m\) witnesses in its own fiber. An unlocked A or B label uses \(m\) distinct D neighbors, with the other A/B labels distinct by AB injectivity and C labels unique; a locked label cannot occur as a singleton kernel. For a positive D label, its AB link has positive minimum degree at least \(m\) on both sides by the two fiber thresholds, so it has an \(m\)-matching (a smaller maximal matching would leave an uncovered positive vertex outside its endpoints, with at least \(m\) neighbors all inside them on the other side). Lift using C uniqueness. This covers all nonempty kernels, proving the clean-host claim.

## Empty kernel

The original hypotheses are hereditary. If a reserved \(m\)-matching leaves at least half the edges avoiding its vertices, prove the nonempty bound on that residue and restore the matching. This adds only witnessed empty intersections. Otherwise a vertex has degree at least \(E/(8m)\), by counting the endpoints of any \(m\)-matching if it exists, or of a smaller maximal matching. Apply (iii) to the simple link at that vertex and lift. The link's empty kernel, when realized, lifts to a singleton; the size is \(\Omega(E/m^4)\), as claimed.

**Mechanism.** C behaves as a private extra label in the rank-three procedure. Whenever the projected proof actually samples an edge, a pair, or a coordinate at rate \(O(1/m)\), cross injectivity lets that very sample also deconflict C for free. In the doubly capped projected-support branch, pair sampling with conflict pruning replaces edge coloring, so the same operation collapses both endpoint supports and makes C private. The case with three unbounded complementary pair types (outside this theorem), and the case of unrestricted C-cross multiplicities, remain open.
