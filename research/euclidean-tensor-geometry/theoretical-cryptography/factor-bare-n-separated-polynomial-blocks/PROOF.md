# Finite correspondences and two-anchor degree normalization

## 1. Input, precision and promises

Fix an integer K≥1 and an integer c≥0. N≥2 is the only input; L is its binary length. A fixed deterministic algorithm on the unary length 1^L lists a dictionary of ordered pairs (U,V) of nonconstant univariate integer polynomials in dense representation, in poly(L) bit operations. Thus the number of pairs, every degree and the coefficient bit lengths are polynomially bounded. Define R=max(1, all degrees), using 1 for the empty dictionary. A directed relation p→q labelled (U,V) means U(p)=V(q). The graph on distinct support primes uses either orientation on distinct vertices and then forgets directions for purposes of connectivity. There is no assertion that the edge determines a single-valued positive map. Extra edges and cycles are allowed.

**Theorem.** With at most K distinct prime divisors, the following are deterministic polynomial-bit-time recognizable and completely factorable from N alone: (A) connected graph, with no further condition on the polynomials; (B) exactly two components with respective smallest primes x≤y and (y−x)^2≤x L^(2c), for a dictionary with all leading coefficients positive and a polynomial bound in L on
M=max(1,ceil(max |a_j/a_d|)), where the maximum ranges over all nonleading coefficients in all dictionary polynomials of degree d. Set M=1 when there are no such coefficients. The multiplicities of the support, the graph and the anchors are unknown. In (B) leading coefficients themselves may be exponentially large. The public dictionary and any fixed polynomial bounds are uniform, not advice supplied with an individual N.

Enumerate the support size s=1,...,K, guessed vertex exponents n_v=1,...,L (since 2^(sum n_v)≤N on a true assignment), and one labelled tree or a partition into two nonempty labelled trees. Enumerate a designated anchor in each tree, an orientation for each edge, and one dictionary pair on each edge. This costs O_K(L^K(1+size of dictionary)^(K−1)) templates up to a fixed geometric factor. In (B) try the two roles. The true graph has appropriate spanning trees rooted at its component minima. On every prospective output check all assigned values are pairwise distinct positive primes, their powers give exactly N and the known dictionary yields the claimed component decomposition. Check all guessed edges and true minimum anchors, including the squared gap for (B). Retain only exact matches. Constant-size singleton components are allowed. Every calculation below is rational/integer; logarithms are only used in estimates.

## 2. A finite fiber once the two-anchor sum is known

Consider any guessed tree system, including incorrect guesses. In (A) leave the root coordinate X free. In (B) suppose positive integers a,b and an integer S have been chosen, and set the anchor of the other tree to (S−aX)/b. For every edge, ordering vertices away from their chosen anchors, its equation can be written
    W(child)=Z(parent)
with W,Z nonconstant rational polynomials: swap the dictionary pair when necessary. We seek all solutions of the edge equations and the additional equation product of coordinate^n_v = N.

Here is an explicit one-variable eliminant and its size bound. Start with Q[X] and the anchor coordinates just specified. Adjoin one child variable Y at a time, quotienting by
    (W(Y)−Z(parent))/lc(W).
This is monic of positive degree in the new variable over the algebra built so far, so the next algebra is free over the previous one with basis 1,Y,...,Y^(deg W−1), even if the relation is reducible or has repeated roots. The final algebra is finite free over Q[X] of rank T≤R^(K−1). Let H(X) be the determinant of multiplication on this module by the product of the vertex coordinates to their guessed powers, minus N times the identity. This means the matrix of the algebra element (product of the powers)−N.

At fixed complex X=t, the algebra specializes to a nonzero finite-dimensional commutative complex algebra, by the same monic bases. The determinant vanishes there exactly if the displayed element belongs to a maximal ideal: a nonunit has singular multiplication and conversely, and the residue field in a finite complex algebra is C. Thus a zero is equivalent to actual complex vertex values satisfying the equations and the product. No assumption about separability or absence of nilpotents is needed. For |t| tending to infinity the first anchor tends to infinity in modulus and in case (B) so does the second, because a,b>0. On any edge |W(z)| tends to infinity precisely when |z| does, for a fixed nonconstant polynomial W; in particular along any sequence of solutions one vertex tending to infinity forces its neighbor to tend to infinity. Every vertex therefore goes to infinity. Their product in modulus tends to infinity, incompatible with fixed N. If H were identically zero we could specialize at each positive integer t and choose solutions, contrary to this argument. Hence H is a nonzero univariate polynomial, even on an incorrect template. If H is constant nonzero this template has no solutions.

List its integral roots in [2,N] by factoring the cleared integer polynomial over Q and retaining its integral linear roots. For each such first anchor, the second anchor when present is determined and tested for integrality. Enumerate the children given a now-known integer parent by solving the univariate W(Y)=Z(parent) over the integers, keeping only [2,N]. This costs at most R per edge in branching. Final verification in §1 removes spurious roots/assignments. Fixed anchors, as in §3's small case, can use this last recursion directly.

To be explicit about cost, assume log(2+|S|), log(2+a+b) are polynomial in L. In a monic basis the new variable acts by a block companion matrix with scalar rational blocks from W and the single old-algebra block Z(parent)/lc(W); prior matrices extend block diagonally. Evaluate Z at the previous parent matrix by ordinary polynomial evaluation. At each stage matrix dimensions are ≤R^K=poly(L). If entries of the current matrices have common denominator, numerator degree and coefficient bit length polynomially bounded, then evaluating a dense polynomial of degree ≤R preserves those bounds: denominators divide the R-th power of the old common denominator times the coefficient denominators, degrees grow by at most R, and the coefficient 1-norm of a product is at most the product of the 1-norms times the matrix dimension per multiplication for a maximum-entry norm. With entries measured after clearing denominators, log-norms of powers grow at most linearly in the exponent times the previous bound plus log dimension. Taking a common denominator for polynomially many new entries still costs polynomially many bits. The induction has at most K levels, so polynomial exponents do not increase with L. The initial anchor expressions satisfy these bounds; the final product uses at most K L powers in total. A T by T polynomial-matrix determinant has degree at most T times the entry degree and, after clearing denominators, log 1-norm at most T times the maximum entry log 1-norm plus log(T!). It can be computed in polynomial bit time, for example by evaluating at degree-bound-plus-one small consecutive integers, exact rational determinants, and interpolation. Deterministic univariate rational polynomial factorization is polynomial in the resulting dense sizes. Integer primality is likewise in deterministic polynomial time. This proves (A) immediately and gives the last step of (B).

## 3. Two close anchors: computing the missing list without knowing a factor

Write Q=L^c, P=x, and use P as a lower bound for every true support prime, not just the two anchors. Put
    E=8 K^2 L M R^K,          P0=16(E+Q^2+1).
These are explicitly computable integers polynomial in L under the assumptions in (B). If P≤P0 then both anchors ≤P0+Q ceil(sqrt(P0)); enumerate both anchors to this bound, solve each tree recursively, and verify. This does not assume that the other vertices are small. Always run this branch, without trying to determine the unknown P.

Suppose now P>P0. For any dictionary polynomial W with degree d and leading coefficient w>0, and any real z≥P, the relative difference W(z)/(w z^d)−1 has magnitude at most M sum_{i≥1} z^(−i)=M/(z−1)≤2M/P≤1/2. In particular W(z)>0. The estimate |log(1+u)|≤2|u| gives
    log W(z)=log w+d log z+epsilon_W(z),      |epsilon_W(z)|≤4M/P.

For an edge W(child)=Z(parent) let w,z0 be the positive leading coefficients of W,Z, and u=deg Z, v=deg W. Then
    log child=(u/v) log parent + log(z0/w)/v + an error of magnitude ≤8M/P.
This is valid in either tree traversal direction, even if multiple real preimages exist. Define positive rational exponents alpha_i and formal positive scales C_i inductively. At an anchor take alpha=1, C=1. Along this edge take alpha_child=(u/v)alpha_parent and
    log C_child=(u/v) log C_parent + log(z0/w)/v.
The algorithm represents log C_i only as a formal rational linear combination of symbols log(integer leading coefficient), never by floating-point numbers. Since the path has at most K−1 steps and u/v≤R, the true vertex at such a position in the block with anchor t_j satisfies
    log p_i=alpha_i log t_j+log C_i+eps_i,       |eps_i|≤8 K M R^K/P.
Let W0 be the product of the integers v over the edges of the chosen oriented traversal (W0=1 for no edges). Every alpha_i and every formal coefficient in log C_i has denominator dividing W0: a path denominator divides the product of the v on that path. Also W0≤R^K, and the absolute formal coefficients are at most K R^K, by the same recursion. Define
    a=W0 sum_{block 1} n_i alpha_i,      b=W0 sum_{block 2} n_i alpha_i,
    Cstar=product_i (C_i)^(W0 n_i).
Thus a,b are positive polynomial-sized integers, at most W0 K L R^K each, and Cstar is an exactly computable positive rational. Integer exponents used to form Cstar from leading coefficients have polynomial magnitude; their sizes and all resulting numerator/denominator bit lengths are polynomial. Both a and b are at least W0 because the corresponding anchor is present. Repeated leading coefficients cause no problem; their terms can simply be added.

Let D=a+b, g=(N^W0/Cstar)^(1/D) and g0=(x^a y^b)^(1/D). From the preceding formulas and the true product,
    |log(g/g0)| ≤ (W0/D)·8 K^2 L M R^K/P ≤ E/P.
Since P>P0, this is below 1/16 and y≤P+Q sqrt(P)≤5P/4. In particular |g−g0| ≤ (5P/4)·2E/P ≤3E. The D-term arithmetic mean A=(ax+by)/D of a copies of x and b copies of y differs from its geometric mean g0 by at most Q^2: in detail, log A−mean(log t) lies between 0 and (y−x)^2/(2P^2) by Taylor's theorem at A, and 1−exp(−u)≤u converts this into A−g0≤(5P/4)(y−x)^2/(2P^2)≤Q^2. Consequently the true integer S=ax+by satisfies
    |S−Dg| ≤ D(Q^2+3E).

Compute J=floor(Dg) by positive integer powers: j≤Dg for positive integer j iff Cstar j^D ≤D^D N^W0. Cross multiply its rational denominator; double and binary-search. All searched j have polynomial bit length: g≤max(1,N^W0/Cstar), whose bit length is polynomial; D,W0 and all powers used are polynomial-sized exponents. Enumerate the closed integer interval from J−D(Q^2+3E) to J+1+D(Q^2+3E). This interval is polynomial in L and intentionally two-sided. On any guessed template the same exact construction and bit bounds apply. The large-anchor true branch has an S in this interval, after which §2 finds its full decomposition. This proves (B). There is no assumed oracle supplying leading-scale values, degrees used, relations selected, an approximate prime, or the weighted sum.

## 4. Scope checks

Because the dictionary depends only on bit length and s≤K, each shape/degree/label/multiplicity assignment has polynomially many descriptions up to a fixed power of log X for N≤X. In (A) use the least support prime as designated anchor: ≤X^(1/s) and, after fixing it, there are at most R^K possible assignments along a tree. In (B) the first minimum is likewise ≤X^(1/s), and for each x there are at most 1+L^c sqrt(x) choices of the other anchor. The same finite branching applies. Thus for fixed exactly s≥2 the respective counts are at most X^(1/s) polylog(2X) and X^(3/(2s)) polylog(2X); these are thin classes. No bound X^(1/K) is asserted merely for s≤K. There is no claim of infinitely many simultaneous prime values for a fixed nontrivial dictionary. Separated polynomial equations are essential to the elementary propagation to infinity; the proof does not extend without checking this property to arbitrary plane curves or to rational maps with poles.
