# Earlier work and scope of the synthesis

* **Separated-variable equations.** Yuri F. Bilu and Robert F. Tichy, [*The Diophantine equation f(x)=g(y)*](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-95-3-261-288), Acta Arith. 95 (2000), 261–288, study the structure of polynomial pairs admitting infinitely many solutions in the relevant rational bounded-denominator setting. Neither separated-variable equations nor their finite algebraic projections are introduced here. Our product fixes N, and the elementary vanishing argument is only for a tree of such equations together with that fixed product (and, for two blocks, a linear anchor constraint); it is not a classification of integral points. The paper's structural results are not needed to run the proof.

* **Known relations and univariate elimination.** Manindra Agrawal, Nitin Saxena and Shubham Sahai Srivastava, [*Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), MFCS 2016, introduction on printed p. 6:3 before Theorem 1.2, explicitly explains reducing a supplied suitable polynomial relation between two factors and their product to univariate integer roots. They also study finding and exploiting further families of small dependencies. A connected tree with a short enumerable dictionary is a standard fixed-variable elimination/finite-enumeration synthesis, not a new factorization primitive, and we make no originality claim for the two-prime known-dependency case. Neither the list for two unknown anchors extracted from normalized polynomial degrees nor a general pair of nonlinear hidden multi-prime components is supplied to the algorithm here.

* **Weighted sums.** Jonathon Hales and Ghaith Hiary, [*A generalization of Lehman's method*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-00959-7), Ramanujan J. 65 (2024), 1773–1790, published §2, Proposition 2.1 and following argument, use an arithmetic/geometric-mean comparison to enumerate a linear combination of two factors in N=p^r q, then univariate root recovery. Weighted Fermat recovery and polynomial roots are not new. Our two-block argument uses inferred rational degree weights, leading-coefficient powers and a necessarily two-sided error before this step. It allows several related but unknown primes inside each block. Coron, Faugère, Renault and Zeitoun, [*Factoring N=p^r q^s for Large r and s*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/071), give complementary results for large multiplicities; we impose no lower multiplicity promise, and make no claim to improve their bounds.

* **Close factors versus close anchors.** Hui Zhang and Tsuyoshi Takagi, [2014 IEICE paper](https://doi.org/10.1587/transfun.E97.A.1533), give a Fermat-type factorization result for r comparable primes with spread below N^(1/r²). Here only two minima are near; some other primes can be polynomial values of degree greater than one in a minimum, with a completely different scale. The ordinary close singleton case is not a new claim. Known one-variable small-root methods, such as the theorem in Alexander May's [dissertation, Thm. 7, printed p. 37](https://d-nb.info/972386416/34#page=38), should also be distinguished from reconstructing the graph and obtaining our two-anchor sum from the single numeric input. We do not claim a globally optimal gap.

* **Prior affine result in this repository.** [Frozen earlier note](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/f2d900c214788ed575fe388c121850ba27d611bd/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-affine-one-or-two-blocks) treated q=mp+1, with its own rational-affine shift bound and one-sided center, under the same one/two-component convention. Those affine cases are not a second new result; the new scope considered here is a uniform separated-polynomial dictionary, including multivalued algebraic edges, normalized rational degrees and two-sided center error. The original affine one-sided estimate remains stronger in that special setting. Both notes require thin structural promises and do not solve general factoring.

* **Standard exact algorithms.** [Lenstra, Lenstra and Lovász](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf) give polynomial-time univariate factorization over the rationals. [Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781) give deterministic polynomial-time primality testing. The finite-free algebra determinant above is the usual multiplication norm and is not itself novel.

A bounded primary-source comparison covered polynomial dependencies of integer factors, power divisors, generalized Fermat and close-factor approaches, and separated-variable Diophantine equations. No identical two-block normalization statement was identified; this is not an exhaustive priority claim. All examples are deliberately finite number-theory data rather than operational evaluations.
