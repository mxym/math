# Source and priority notes

- **Maxence Jauberty and Pierrick Méaux, *On the Cardinality of the Walsh Support of a Boolean Function***, [IACR ePrint 2025/1651](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1651), [28 April 2026 revision](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1651/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf), §8 “Open Questions,” final paragraph, p. 49. The paper's main Theorem 5 is for binary-valued functions on a binary domain and is not used in the proof here. The final paragraph asks for possible Walsh-support cardinalities in the p-ary setting. We answer the ternary case for the union over dimensions, plus the first three dimension-specific sets. The same-title [journal publication](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-026-00925-8), *Cryptography and Communications* 18 (2026), 2181–2234, is listed for bibliographic context. Its complete text was not accessible to this literature check; the precise question is anchored to the public April revision. No result is claimed for all odd primes or all dimension-specific sets.
- **Ternary and p-ary plateaued constructions.** For comparison see [Constructions and Characterizations of s-Plateaued Partitions (arXiv:2606.27776)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27776); its p-ary setup uses the same root-of-unity transform, while Parseval forces powers of \(p\) as the support sizes for its plateaued functions. The present question allows arbitrary ternary functions and arbitrary nonzero Walsh magnitudes. That work is not an input to the proof.
- **Broader Fourier sparsity.** [On Fourier Analysis of Sparse Boolean Functions over Certain Abelian Groups, MFCS 2024](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2024.40) studies sparse **sign-valued** functions over abelian groups, not the three-root phase \(\omega^f\) considered here. In particular our midpoint constraint relies on the ternary identity \((\omega^f)^2=\overline{\omega^f}\). No theorem from this source is used.

Public literature searches through 27 November 2026 included queries combining “ternary”, “Walsh support”, “cardinalities”, “attainable”, “possible”, “6”, “7”, “10”, “11”, “p-ary”, and the authors' names. The accessible primary sources did not reveal a matching result that determines the full union of attainable scalar ternary Walsh-support sizes. This is not a priority claim over inaccessible text. All finite inputs needed to replay the exact result are generated by the scripts or explicitly included as truth tables; no third-party catalogue is used.
