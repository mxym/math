# Prior work and scope

- Our pinned [separated-monic degree-potential note](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/80a493d8dc4f2eefe4eda3a988c0ca6ee33c215f/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/rational-degree-potential-prime-support/PROOF.md), §§2–4, already proves the cyclic log-degree potential argument, the site-independent common denominator of the aggregate, the additive localization step for monic equal-value edges, and connected-support recognition from an anchor. These are reused. The contribution here is the certified mixed Newton-gap edge condition, uniform drift bound and the strictly more expressive correspondence model in §§2, 5–6. A fixed cyclic group of degree ratios remains a global public assumption.
- The repository's [mixed-tree constant-fibre treatment](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/80a493d8dc4f2eefe4eda3a988c0ca6ee33c215f/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-mixed-tree-constant-fibers/PROOF.md) and [Gauss-content refinement](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/80a493d8dc4f2eefe4eda3a988c0ca6ee33c215f/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/mixed-tree-gauss-content-certificates/PROOF.md) already permit genuinely mixed bivariate equations. They work with bounded support size and bounded degrees; their product-fibre analysis handles different, more general local polynomials, sometimes with an arithmetic certificate. In contrast, the present argument has no fixed support-size bound and needs a Newton gap and coefficient-radius bound. It neither recovers the general mixed-tree theorem nor assumes a finite complex fibre of the whole hidden system. The earlier bimonic anchor notes allow arbitrary support size but require a special path or network anchor certificate instead of the automatic growth estimates here.
- Monomial balancing and Newton polygons are classical. See Philippe Flajolet and Robert Sedgewick, [*Analytic Combinatorics*](https://ac.cs.princeton.edu/home/AC.pdf), §VII.7, Theorem VII.7 and the immediately adjacent Newton polygon discussion. The estimate here uses a direct two-leading-term inequality, not a new Newton–Puiseux theorem.
- Patrick Ingram, [*Canonical heights for correspondences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1041v2), Theorem 1.1, proves height scaling for polarized correspondences beyond separated equations. His [*Critical dynamics of variable-separated affine correspondences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6954), §3, Lemma 3.3, is a close local precedent for degree-normalized growth. Neither canonical heights nor use of logarithmic degree quotients are claimed new. The weighted-gap estimate here is used to get a finite precision window in an N-only connected prime-support model.
- Manindra Agrawal, Nitin Saxena and Shubham Sahai Srivastava, [*Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), MFCS 2016 article 6, p. 6:3 and Theorem 1.3, consider supplied nondegenerate algebraic dependencies for two factors or a prime and a cofactor; basic elimination for a supplied dense equation is an established ingredient. The present model supplies a length-dependent catalogue, but no one dependency on a named pair, and does not do simultaneous elimination in the whole hidden graph.
- Mengce Zheng, [*Revisiting RSA-polynomial problem and semiprime factorization*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114634), Theor. Comput. Sci. 1004 (2024), 114634, [author PDF](https://mengcezheng.github.io/docs/Zheng24b.pdf), §2 (Newton polygon definition and Definitions 4–5), Theorems 3–5, uses Newton polygons to analyze a different problem based on bounded roots of a bivariate integer polynomial. Here every edge is an exact integer equality on hidden prime coordinates, the decimal or binary position of a prime is not supplied, and there is no small-root computation in two unknown coordinates. No security evaluation follows.
- The known-coordinate step invokes A. K. Lenstra, H. W. Lenstra, Jr. and L. Lovász, [*Factoring Polynomials with Rational Coefficients*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454), Math. Ann. 261 (1982), 515–534, [Theorem (3.6)](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf). It lists linear roots of the remaining univariate polynomial in polynomial bit time; it does not give the initial integer factor in the present proof. Deterministic primality follows from [*PRIMES is in P*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781).

The search is bounded. The additional scope is nonseparated Newton-gap edges with a public weighted coefficient bound in the fixed cyclic degree model. This is not a claim of unrestricted factorization, a solver for arbitrary bivariate Diophantine equations, or a priority claim for a classical ingredient.
