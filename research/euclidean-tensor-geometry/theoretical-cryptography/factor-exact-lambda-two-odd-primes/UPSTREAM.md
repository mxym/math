# Prior work and scope

- C. Adelmann and A. Winterhof, [*Interpolation of Functions Related to the Integer Factoring Problem*](https://doi.org/10.1007/11779360_12), WCC 2005, LNCS 3969 (published 2006), Proposition 2 on p. 149, already gives a deterministic factorization of a product of two distinct odd primes from its exact Carmichael value, using a quotient and a quadratic. We do not claim that squarefree case as new.
- F. Morain, G. Renault and B. Smith, [*Deterministic factoring with oracles*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444v2), arXiv:1802.08444v2, §5, Lemmas 5.2–5.3 and Algorithm 4, printed pp. 12–13. They give the exact squarefree two-prime identity \(\phi(N)=\lambda(N)\gcd(N-1,\lambda(N))\) and recover the primes by a quadratic. Their Theorem 1.1 and Remark 5.1 also recover a sufficiently large prime in a squarefree input, while §2.3 and Appendix A discuss randomized known-exponent splitting. The new algebra here normalizes the prime-power valuation contributions before taking the gcd. It yields a shifted product even in the prime-power cross-divisibility case and needs only a single exact input value, not values on other integers.
- B. Żrałek, [*A deterministic version of Pollard's p-1 algorithm*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102v5), v5, §6, printed p. 14, gives the arbitrary-multiplicity two-prime case from exact **Euler totient** and discusses equal-order decompositions. The use of \(n/\gcd(n,\phi(n))\) there is an antecedent of the valuation split. With \(\lambda\), the valuation is a maximum instead of a sum, so \(p\mid q-1\) may leave both primes in the surviving radical; the proof here handles precisely that branch as well.
- N. Gupta, A. Sikarov and I. Volkovich, [*A Computational Perspective on Carmichael Numbers*](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/212/download/), ECCC TR26-212 (2026), Facts 2.5–2.6 on printed p. 8 (known distinct-prime product) and Lemma 1.1 / §3 (simple squarefree Carmichael numbers with three primes and access to a \(\lambda\) oracle). Their §§1.4 and 5 also compare the squarefree algorithms and pose broader questions. Neither unrestricted reduction between \(\lambda\), \(\phi\), and factoring nor the Carmichael-triprime problems are resolved here.
- E. Costa and D. Harvey, [*Faster deterministic integer factorization*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.2116), Math. Comp. 83 (2014), DOI [10.1090/S0025-5718-2013-02707-X](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2013-02707-X), Proposition 9, arXiv pp. 5–6, proves a deterministic bounded small-factor routine in the ordinary no-advice model by generalized factorials and multipoint evaluation; see also its Theorem 1. D. Harvey, [*An exponent one-fifth algorithm for deterministic integer factorisation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05450), Proposition 2.5, is a later reference point for bounded small factors; D. Harvey and M. Hittmeir, [*A log-log speedup for exponent one-fifth deterministic integer factorisation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11105), Theorem 1.1 and the small-factor discussion on p. 2, give a general-input bound. The present promise is about the number of distinct odd primes and the availability of an exact Carmichael value, not any upper bound on the smallest factor. Thus the algorithm is not a competing general-input bound or an improvement to those small-factor routines.
- The optional route of reading integer roots from linear polynomial factors uses A. K. Lenstra, H. W. Lenstra Jr. and L. Lovász, [*Factoring polynomials with rational coefficients*](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf), Math. Ann. 261 (1982), 515–534. The main decoder avoids this subroutine by integer convexity. Polynomial-time primality testing is the standard [AKS theorem](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781); the finite verifier uses small deterministic trial-primality checks instead.
