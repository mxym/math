# Frobenius cubic fibers of separable elliptic quartic pencils

This is finite-field algebraic geometry, not a discrete-logarithm procedure or cryptosystem analysis. It extends [the simply branched pencil calculation](EllipticResidualPencils.md). The general twisting method is standard; see [Dèbes–Legrand, *Twisted covers and specializations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.6154). The results here specialize to elliptic degree four and use elementary low-degree resolvents, rather than claiming novelty for Chebotarev or for resolvent theory.

Let k=F_Q have odd characteristic. Let f:E→P¹ be a separable degree-four map from an elliptic curve (equivalently a base-point-free degree-four pencil). Let A⊆S4 be the arithmetic monodromy and G◁A the geometric monodromy, both acting on the four sheets. Let B⊂P¹ be its geometric branch locus, and let N count rational fibers containing an irreducible closed point of degree three. Since such a fiber consists of that point and a rational point, all with multiplicity one, no branch fiber is counted. At an unramified rational point it is counted exactly for Frobenius cycle type (3,1).

**Classification.** In all cases other than A=G=A4 or A=G=S4, N is exactly zero. In the regular A4 case the main term is 2(Q+1)/3 with error at most 4√Q/3+4; in the regular S4 case the main term is (Q+1)/3 with error at most 10√Q/3+8. The statements include wild characteristic 3; the group and resolvent formulas do not assume simple branching. As finite estimates, the positive main terms need not imply existence over every small field.

## Monodromy and the excluded constant-field case

The exact sequence of monodromy has cyclic quotient A/G, the constants Galois group, and Frobenius at any rational unramified place lies in its generator coset. G is transitive since E is geometrically irreducible. Transitive subgroups of S4 containing a 3-cycle have order divisible by 12 (the 4 factor is by transitivity); hence they are A4 or S4. A normal transitive subgroup of S4 is V4, A4 or S4; the V4 option for G when A=S4 is excluded by the noncyclic quotient S3. If A=S4 and G=A4, all rational unramified Frobenius elements are odd and therefore are not 3-cycles. If A=A4 and G differs from it, the only transitive normal possibility is V4.

The abstract permutation-group case G=V4 and A=A4 would put all rational unramified Frobenius elements into a coset of 3-cycles, but cannot occur for E. Indeed the geometric quartic would be a Galois cover with deck group V4, since the point stabilizer is trivial. The homomorphism Aut(E_geom)→Aut(E_geom,O) obtained by taking the origin-preserving part has translation kernel. In odd characteristic its restriction to V4 has image of order at most 2: any nontrivial order-two origin-preserving automorphism α equals [−1], since (α−[1])(α+[1])=0 in an endomorphism ring without zero divisors (any nonzero endomorphism is an isogeny). Its image is nontrivial, because a quotient of E by a finite group of translations is again genus one. Therefore precisely one nonidentity deck transformation is a translation. This membership is invariant under Frobenius conjugation: conjugation by any automorphism of E, and by descent on the geometric curve, preserves the intrinsic group of translations. Arithmetic conjugation on the geometric deck group identifies, up to the opposite action, with conjugation on the normal regular V4 in S4 (the centralizer of V4 is itself). In A4 the nontrivial quotient element cyclically permutes the three nonidentity V4 elements. This contradicts preservation of the distinguished translation.

## Exact resolvent formulas

Now A=G (so the Galois closure X is geometrically connected). Use smooth projective models of the intermediate curves and, for such a curve T→P¹, write n_T(t) for the number of distinct k-rational points above t; in particular Σ_{t∈P¹(k)} n_T(t)=#T(k).

* A4: write Z=X/V4 (cyclic cubic). At an unramified rational t, Frobenius maps to a nonidentity element in A4/V4 precisely when it is a 3-cycle. Consequently the desired indicator is 1−n_Z(t)/3. Thus
  N=(Q+1)−#Z(k)/3−Σ_{t∈B(k)}(1−n_Z(t)/3).
* S4: set C=X/H where H is the preimage of a subgroup of order 2 under S4→S4/V4≅S3 (thus |H|=8), and D=X/A4 (quadratic sign cover). A 3-cycle in S3 occurs precisely when a lift in S4 is a 3-cycle: the eight lifts of the two 3-cycles have orders divisible by 3, and the only such cycle type in S4 is (3,1). The pairs (n_C(t),n_D(t)) for quotient cycle types 1, (12), (123) are (3,2),(1,0),(0,2), respectively. Therefore the desired indicator is (1−n_C(t)+n_D(t))/3. Hence
  N=(Q+1−#C(k)+#D(k))/3−(1/3)Σ_{t∈B(k)}(1−n_C(t)+n_D(t)).

The count formulas are exact for each finite k, with no exceptional rational points omitted beyond the explicit corrections.

## Different budgets and square-root errors, including wild ramification

Work over the algebraic closure at each branch value. In the Galois closure let I_i denote lower ramification groups (I_0 inertia). For a permutation action on a finite sheet set Ω of an intermediate cover, the sum of local different exponents in the intermediate fiber is
  δ_Ω = Σ_{i≥0} (|I_i|/|I_0|)(|Ω|−#orbits_Ω(I_i)).
For clarity, this is the usual permutation conductor formula, following directly from Hilbert's different formula (cf. [F. Pop, *Lifting of curves*, Section 2](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~pop/Research/files-Res/LiftCrv_Aug13.pdf)). For one I_0-orbit pick a stabilizer J. The contribution for the corresponding completion is Σ_i (|I_i|−|I_i∩J|)/|J|, by the different tower rule and lower numbering for subgroups. Normality of I_i in I_0 makes all I_i-orbits inside that I_0-orbit have equal size |I_i|/|I_i∩J|, so the displayed formula follows by summing. In the tame case only i=0 appears; in odd characteristic, only characteristic 3 can contribute higher nontrivial groups for A4 or S4.

Riemann–Hurwitz for E→P¹ gives total quartic different 8. The codimension |Ω|−#orbits of each inertia group on resolvent sheets is at most its codimension on the four sheets: for C the only possible exception would be quartic codimension 1, in which case the group is generated by a transposition and its image has cubic codimension 1. The cyclic cubic resolvent Z only has codimension 2 for an inertia subgroup mapping nontrivially to C3, which contains a 3-cycle, forcing at least 2 on the quartic sheets. The quadratic sign cover's codimension is at most 1, immediately bounded. Thus total differents of Z,C,D are at most 8, yielding genera g_Z≤2, g_C≤2, g_D≤3. Each branch value has quartic different ≥1, and in A4 ≥2 (quartic codimension 1 would be a transposition). Hence |B|≤8 and ≤4, respectively. In the two exact formulas each per-branch correction has absolute value at most 1 by the cover degrees, and Hasse–Weil for the resolvent curves gives the advertised errors. The Galois-closure genus itself is also bounded: the ratio (1−1/|I_i|)/(4−#orbits(I_i)) is at most 1/2 in S4 (codimension 1 is a transposition), and at most 1/3 in A4, checking C2, C3, V4, A4. Thus g_X≤25 or 5, respectively, by the same different comparison.

## A regular A4 pencil and its constant-field variant

Here is a uniform geometric construction, with explicit small fields below. Let char k>3, choose nonzero a,b∈k with a³≠b³, and put k'=k(ζ) for a primitive cube root ζ. Define, indices modulo 3,
  F_i(z)=(z−ζ^i a)(z−ζ^i b), t=z³,
  w_i²=F_{i+1}F_{i+2}, w_0w_1w_2=F_0F_1F_2.
Use the field L=k'(z,w_0,w_1); w_2 is determined by the product relation. Distinct simple zeros of the F_i prove independence of the two square classes even over the algebraic closure. Thus L/k'(z) is a regular V4 extension with branch at the six simple roots, each inertia 2, and no branching at zero or infinity. The automorphism σ:z↦ζz,w_i↦ζ²w_{i−1} has order 3, preserves all relations, and permutes the three nontrivial deck elements of V4. It follows that L/k'(t) is regular and Galois with group V4⋊C3=A4. At t=0,∞ inertia is 3 (the cyclic cubic base cover ramifies, not the V4 stage); at t=a³,b³ it is 2 (only the V4 stage ramifies). The degree-four intermediate field fixed by σ has branch cycles three-cycles at the first two places and double transpositions at the other two. Its different is therefore 8, its genus is one, and it has a rational point over any finite field of definition by Hasse–Weil. The quartic map gives an elliptic pencil.

* k=F7, ζ=2,a=1,b=3: A=G=A4, branch values 0,∞,1,6. Each of the four unramified rational parameters 2,3,4,5 is a noncube in F7, so its Frobenius in A4/V4 is nontrivial, giving exactly four cubic fibers.
* k=F5, a=1,b=2: k'=F25. The automorphism τ of L fixing z and t sends ζ↦ζ⁻¹,w_i↦w_{−i}. It has order 2, conjugates σ to σ⁻¹ and exchanges two of the three nontrivial V4 elements. The group over k(t) is V4⋊S3=S4, with A4 the geometric subgroup. The field fixed by ⟨σ,τ⟩≅S3 is degree four over k(t); over the algebraic closure it is the same genus-one construction, since ⟨σ,τ⟩∩A4=⟨σ⟩. Its constant field is k, and its odd rational Frobenius coset gives exactly zero cubic fibers. Thus even geometrically A4 pencils need not have the 2/3 main term over their field of definition.
