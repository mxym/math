# A short aggregate word instead of a list of prime factors

**Uniform model.** Fix integers h,c≥0. For each binary length L≥2 a fixed algorithm on 1^L outputs a finite list S_L of reduced positive rational numbers m≥1 in polynomial bit time. Let B≥1 bound both numerator and denominator bit lengths at that L (read it off the list, taking B=1 for an empty list), and put E=L^c. The multiplicative group generated by the list is assumed to have rank at most h at every length: equivalently its prime-valuation vectors span a rational vector space of dimension at most h. This is a hypothesis on the fixed public family, not an additional object supplied for each N. The algorithm need neither factor the labels, compute their rank, nor find or guess a basis. Empty dictionaries and the label 1 are allowed.

For N≥2 let its vertices be all distinct prime divisors. Add an undirected edge between p<q if |q−mp|≤E for at least one m in S_L. This is a rational inequality, not an extra divisibility or integral-offset constraint. The graph with one vertex is connected. No other promise on N is imposed.

**Theorem.** In this model there is a deterministic algorithm taking only N, running in time polynomial in L=bitlength(N), that recognizes connected-support inputs and returns their verified full prime-power decomposition. Neither the number of vertices nor the multiplicities are fixed. Output not in this class is allowed on disconnected graphs. Exponents of the polynomial time bound may depend on h,c and the public listing algorithm.

## 1. A polynomial word ball, with no basis available

Let W_r contain the distinct rational products of at most r letters from S_L and their inverses, with 1 as the empty product. If the rank is h'≤h then
    |W_r| ≤ (2rB+1)^h.
Indeed only finitely many rational primes occur in the numerators and denominators of this finite list. On the h'-dimensional span of their valuation vectors some h' coordinate projections together are injective: choose independent rows from a full-column-rank basis matrix. No particular projection is needed by the algorithm. Each single letter or its inverse has each valuation between −B and B. A word of length ≤r has at most 2rB+1 possibilities per selected coordinate. Equality in every valuation determines equality of positive rational numbers. The dimension-zero case gives one product.

Generate W_0={1}, and to obtain W_{i+1} insert W_i and all products of elements of W_i with each allowed letter/inverse. Use exact reduced numerator/denominator pairs to deduplicate, by a deterministic ordered dictionary or by sorting. This is elementary positive-rational arithmetic; no discrete logarithms or prime factorization operations are involved. For r=L^2, at most polynomially many intermediate elements appear, each with numerator and denominator of at most rB+1 bits. The at most r rounds and poly(L)-sized dictionary therefore give deterministic polynomial bit complexity. The rank need not be explicitly verified before this computation; the complexity claim is for the stipulated dictionary families.

## 2. What the hidden tree implies for an unknown minimum P

Let the support size be s, its minimum P, and let n_v be the multiplicity of vertex p_v. Put D=sum_v n_v=Omega(N), where Omega here counts prime factors with multiplicity. Trivially s≤D≤L. If the graph is connected, choose any of its spanning trees, rooted at P, and a witnessing slope per edge. This tree is for the proof only. Define a scale c_v at vertex v by c_root=1 and c_q=m c_p on an edge from the smaller p to the larger q; when traversing backwards divide by m. Thus c_v is a word of length ≤s−1. The aggregate C=product_v c_v^n_v is a word of length ≤(s−1)D<L^2, so it occurs in the list from §1. Intermediate and final scales may be nonintegers. The algorithm has not enumerated any tree or any exponent vector.

Suppose P>4LE. For an edge p<q write q=mp(1+u); since m≥1 and p≥P, |u|≤E/P<1/2. It follows that |log(q/(mp))|≤2E/P. In a forward step this log ratio is added to log(p_v/(c_v P)), in a reverse step it is subtracted. A path has at most s−1≤L edges, hence
    |log(p_v/(c_v P))| ≤ 2LE/P < 1/2.
Use |exp(z)−1|≤2|z| for |z|≤1/2 to get
    |p_v/c_v−P| ≤ 4LE,                    for every v.
The positive D-th root g=(N/C)^(1/D) is the geometric mean of the values p_v/c_v with their positive integer multiplicities. It lies between their smallest and largest, so |g−P|≤4LE. This bound uses absolute local errors only; signs, edge directions and multipliers can vary. It does not require the largest prime to be comparable to P.

## 3. From a list of seeds to complete recognition

First test integers 2,...,min(N,4LE) as prospective seed divisors. This covers the small-P case, not by assuming the whole support small, but by the connected exploration below. For larger P enumerate D=1,...,L and C=A/B0 in W_(L^2), with positive coprime numerator A and denominator B0. Compute J=floor((B0 N/A)^(1/D)) by testing the monotone integer comparisons A j^D≤B0 N. Doubling and bisection use polynomial bit lengths, since the numerator and denominator of C have O(L²B) bits and D≤L. Test every candidate integer in [J−4LE,J+1+4LE] intersected with [2,N]. If N is promised connected, a true minimum P occurs in some list. A false word, degree or candidate never has to satisfy the estimate. The algorithm can cache tested seeds.

Keep a seed t only if it is a prime divisor of N, using exact division and deterministic primality testing. Recover the entire true graph component of t without factoring any unknown cofactor. For every reached prime v and every m from the list, the prospective neighbors larger than v are integers in [mv−E,mv+E]; the prospective smaller neighbors are integers in [(v−E)/m,(v+E)/m]. Use exact ceiling and floor, clip to [2,N], respect strict ordering and recheck the original inequality. Because m≥1, each interval contains at most 2E+1 integers. Add a candidate exactly when it is a prime divisor of N not yet visited, and continue until the frontier is empty. These tests follow precisely the definition of adjacency; induction on graph distance proves that the visited set is the full component containing the seed. Its size is ≤L regardless of the total number of candidate integers tested.

On the resulting set, divide N repeatedly by its distinct visited primes to determine their whole positive exponents. Output the verified factorization if the remaining cofactor is 1. Otherwise this component does not account for all of N and cannot certify connectedness. Cache unsuccessful completed components if desired. If the candidate procedure ends without success the graph is disconnected. In particular nonmembers cannot yield a false output; for a prime power the one reached vertex suffices. There are polynomially many candidates globally, and each frontier expansion has polynomially many dictionary and integer operations with polynomial bit lengths. Primality is in deterministic polynomial time. This proves the theorem.

## 4. Two quantitative limits

**Sparsity, independently of multiplicative rank.** For any poly-size uniform family of slopes ≥1, any fixed c and J>0, the number of N≤X with connected graph and at least two distinct prime factors is O_J(X/(log X)^J), with constants also depending on the rule and c. Write T=ceil(log_2(2X)). The union of all label lists up to length T has cardinality ≤O(T^d) for some fixed d; the errors throughout are bounded by T^c. Fix A large compared with J,d,c and let Y=T^A. If the largest support prime q≥Y occurs, connectivity with at least two vertices gives a neighbor p<q. For each p and m there are O(T^c+1) possible integer q in [mp−T^c,mp+T^c]. Count multiples of pq at most X/(pq). For p≥Y we have 1/(pq)≤1/p², and the sum over even all integers p≥Y is O(1/Y). For p<Y use 1/(pq)≤1/(pY), giving O(log(2Y)/Y) after summing p. Summing multipliers bounds both contributions jointly by O(X T^(d+c) log(2Y)/Y), which is O_J(X/T^J) for large enough A. The estimate permits double counting.

For q<Y, all prime divisors are ≤Y. An elementary Rankin bound, for sigma=1−1/(2A) with A≥1, is
    count ≤ X^sigma product_{p≤Y} (1−p^(−sigma))^(−1).
Its log Euler-product factor is O_A(sum_{2≤n≤Y} n^(−sigma))=O_A(Y^(1−sigma))=O_A(sqrt T); use −log(1−u)≤u/(1−2^(−sigma)) for u≤2^(−sigma). Thus it is also O_J(X/T^J). The excluded prime powers are a separate class: every prime itself is trivially connected and their count does not have power savings of arbitrary logarithmic order. This is a count of ordinary integers, not a statement about security parameters or a particular data source.

**Unbounded support is nonvacuous, even beyond trial division.** Set S_L={1} and c=2. Partition [X,2X] into O(X/(log X)^2) intervals of length at most ceil((log X)^2), with natural logs. By the prime number theorem the full interval contains asymptotically X/log X primes. Some subinterval contains at least a positive constant times log X primes. Form N from all primes in that subinterval, once each. Its bit length satisfies L≥log_2 X and L=O((log X)^3), the latter by even counting all integers in the subinterval. For sufficiently large X, its width ≤L², so the support graph is complete for the unit slope. Its size tends to infinity, and its smallest prime is at least X, larger than L^a for every fixed a as X grows. This unconditional witness concerns the elementary unit-slope subcase; it is not a new prime-gap theorem or a claim of a similarly unbounded nonunit chain for a specific sparse dictionary.

**Difference from fixed-support results.** Guessing a slope-labelled tree or a full multiplicity vector over a number of primes that grows with the input would not give a polynomial bound. Here only a short multiplicative word and one total exponent are enumerated. In exchange, the dictionary has bounded multiplicative rank and the absolute edge error is polynomial in bit length. The theorem does not cover an unrestricted list of small independent slopes or errors as large as arbitrary positive powers of the prime sizes.
