# Sources and scope of the comparison

* [Predecessor: prime polynomial values across a fixed collection of disks](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/8ee6ab9d6e5f86852881466b198e5261602806ef/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/multidisk-clustered-prime-values/PROOF.md), §§1-3 and §5. It uses a first-order product envelope and integer monotonicity to the right of every centre. It allows an individual polynomial's roots in multiple disks without a label. This note instead attaches one public disk to each polynomial, so its root sum in the group is integral by Vieta; that more restrictive catalogue condition lets us drop the earlier side restriction even when the hidden arguments only lie in a cluster rather than coincide. Neither setting subsumes the other without its relevant additional hypothesis. The earlier cyclotomic index and common-base statements are not reprised as originality.
* The logarithmic derivative and critical-point interlacing for real-rooted polynomials are classical; see the logarithmic-force interpretation in [NIST DLMF §31.15](https://dlmf.nist.gov/31.15) for context and F. Alberto Grünbaum, [Electrostatic interpretation for the zeros of certain polynomials and the Darboux process](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0377-0427%2800%2900661-0), J. Comput. Appl. Math. 133 (2001), 397-412. The derivative and strict curvature used here follow immediately by differentiation, and the second-order remainder by the power series for log. The claim is the finite enumeration of integer total offsets that permits recentering without knowing the factors or their witnesses, and the resulting uniform level-or-critical argument.
* [Computing Real Roots of Real Polynomials](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4088) by M. Sagraloff and K. Mehlhorn (J. Symbolic Comput. 73 (2016), 46-86; DOI [10.1016/j.jsc.2015.03.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2015.03.004)) gives certified univariate root isolation/refinement; see the exact integer-coefficient case and the results on isolating intervals. We only need polynomial bit time, not their sharper bounds. For the optional public test in fixed dimension see J. Renegar, [On the computational complexity and geometry of the first-order theory of the reals, part I](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/305759aa-f544-4006-aa85-f70ddce54e00/content), 1992. Standard exact primality: M. Agrawal, N. Kayal and N. Saxena, [PRIMES is in P](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781).
* Classical close-factor methods, integer recovery from a supplied relation, and factoring a composite polynomial value rather than recognizing its exact prime evaluations are different tasks. Compare M. Agrawal, N. Saxena and S. S. Srivastava, [Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), MFCS 2016, 6:3-6:4; and the [repository's fixed-count centered digits predecessor](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/2376fba2686cd79fa3de230638f3477d660456a5/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-near-affine-centered-digits/README.md). Here the allowed geometric closeness is between arguments, not between primes and not between individual public disk centres. No statement about unrestricted factoring, unknown-cofactor recovery, or general rarity of the promised data is made. The literature comparison is bounded, not a global priority claim.
