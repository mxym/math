# Exceptional constants on a tree of mixed plane correspondences

## 1. Quantifiers

Let k >= 2 and delta >= 1 be fixed integers. A deterministic public rule on 1^L produces in time polynomial in L a catalogue D_L of integer bivariate polynomials of total degree at most delta. Its dependence is on the length L alone. Every member has positive degree in both variables and satisfies

  F(c,Y) is not identically zero and F(X,c) is not identically zero for every complex c.                 (1)

These tests are equivalent to the two univariate contents being constant over Q (clear a common scalar first); they do not require irreducibility. All heights below are for coefficients in the actual displayed equations, not their roots. Let H_L be the maximum of 2 and the absolute values of the coefficients. There are polynomially many catalogue entries; log H_L is polynomial in L. We make no smoothness or irreducibility assumption, and (1) is not finiteness of a morphism over the whole affine line.

For a labelled tree with r <= k vertices, assign one oriented catalogue equation to each edge, and choose positive weights e_i of sum <= L. Call the resulting curve equations V, and put m_e = product_i X_i^{e_i}. The theorem concerns the complex affine set V intersect {m_e = n}; nonzero n automatically excludes all coordinate zeros.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic poly(L)-bit-time construction of a finite set E_L of positive L-bit integers and a finite list of nonzero integers D*_L, with these properties.

* Both lists have poly(L) entries; the entries of D*_L have magnitudes at most (2+H_L)^{C(k,delta)}, with a constant independent of the number of entries, exponents and L.
* For every allowed tree and weight vector, if V intersect {m_e=n} has a positive-dimensional component for a positive L-bit n, then n belongs to E_L and every rational prime dividing n divides at least one member of D*_L. In fact the latter support conclusion holds for all entries of E_L as constructed. E_L is only a superset of the bad values.
* Consequently outside E_L all such complex affine intersections are finite, including the empty set; their real points can be listed in time polynomial in L for fixed k and delta. The empty catalogue is allowed; a one-vertex tree causes no difficulty.

**Arithmetic corollary.** For a positive integer n of bit length L, use its distinct prime divisors as abstract vertices; two different vertices are adjacent if in some order they satisfy an equation in D_L. On inputs with at most k distinct prime divisors and connected graph, apart from the explicitly listed E_L, complete factorization has deterministic polynomial bit complexity from n alone. If H_L is polynomial in L, the same statement holds without exceptions. This is a promise-class result, with no lower bound or computational assertion for the excluded values in the large-height case. Off that list, the procedure decides membership as well as returning the decomposition on members. The integer 1 is handled separately as an empty product. A singleton graph is connected by convention.

## 2. A finite common slice for every constant component

The fibre of projection V -> A^1 to any vertex is finite: once that coordinate is fixed, each neighbor has at most delta possible coordinates by (1), and this propagates down the tree. In particular, on any positive-dimensional irreducible component C of a nonzero monomial-level intersection, every coordinate is nonconstant (otherwise C would be a subset of one finite fibre). Choose one root, let T denote its coordinate, and reorient/swap the edge polynomials to agree with the rooted tree. The coordinate ring of C is a domain into which C[T] injects, where here C[T] means the polynomial ring over the complex numbers.

We write CC for the complex field and A(C) for the coordinate ring of this component from this point.

For each vertex obtain a polynomial P_v(T,Y) by P_root = Y-T and, for a child v of w, by

  P_v(T,Y) = Res_U(P_w(T,U), F_wv(U,Y)).                                                       (2)

At an affine solution the two polynomials have a common finite U, so (2) vanishes at (T,X_v), also when a leading coefficient specializes to zero (use the Sylvester determinant identity, not specialization of a degree-trimmed resultant). The polynomials are nonzero and have positive Y-degree. Indeed over an algebraic closure of C(T), initially the only root is T, transcendental over C. Inductively each root xi of P_w is transcendental over C. Therefore F(xi,Y) has its actual positive Y-degree, since its nonzero leading coefficient in U cannot vanish at xi. No root of F(xi,Y) can be a complex constant c, since F(U,c) is nonzero by (1). The resultant over C(T) is a nonzero scalar times the product of those nonconstant F(xi,Y), with multiplicities. This proves the induction, including nonvanishing, on every tree, not only the one eventually chosen. For this particular recursion a factor Y cannot occur generically. More generally, a coordinate annihilator obtained with an extra zero factor may be divided by its full power of Y on C, since X_v is nonzero there. Use the stripped form in what follows.

Let q(T) be the product over v of the leading and constant Y-coefficients of these polynomials. Both are nonzero polynomials in T. Pick 1 <= a <= deg(q)+1 with q(a) != 0. The size of this search is bounded solely by k,delta. On the domain A(C)[1/q(T)], each coordinate is integral over the injected base CC[T,1/q]: divide its coordinate annihilator by the leading coefficient, a unit in that base. The extension is integral and finite because it has finitely many integral algebra generators. Lying-over provides an ideal above (T-a); its residue is a finite extension of C, hence C itself. Thus **every** such component meets the same affine slice T=a, even for a reducible tree curve, singular intersections and branches presenting missing values before the localization. This argument uses neither a point at infinity nor a rational point. At this slice each coordinate alpha_v is nonzero and satisfies the integer polynomial R_v(Y)=P_v(a,Y), with nonzero leading and constant coefficients.

The number of tree edges and their degrees are fixed. At each step in (2) the Sylvester determinant has size bounded in terms of the previous degree and delta. Consequently degrees in T,Y remain bounded solely by k,delta; each new coefficient is a sum of a bounded number of products of bounded length in the old coefficients. By induction their magnitudes, and the magnitudes at the bounded integer a, are <= (2+H_L)^C with C depending only on the fixed parameters. Polynomial contents need not be divided out. Constant scalar factors are harmless, increasing the support list but not creating false exclusions.

## 3. Exceptional support and integer values

Include both nonzero endpoint coefficients of each R_v in D*_L. For a rational prime ell dividing none of these endpoints on the template, R_v has unit leading coefficient over Z_(ell) and so does its reciprocal. Thus both alpha_v and its reciprocal are integral over Z_(ell). Their products and powers are integral too. If product alpha_v^{e_v}=n is a nonzero rational integer, then 1/n is integral over Z_(ell). This subring of Q is integrally closed, so ell cannot divide n. This proves the support conclusion by taking the slice point guaranteed in section 2. It only uses a point on each constant component; coordinates of arbitrary points elsewhere on the curve need not be units. Combining templates uses a list of endpoint integers. Its product B_L has polynomial bit length but need not have polynomial magnitude even if H_L has polynomial magnitude. In particular, the exceptional integer values themselves need not be small. If desired, inclusion of the support of n in that of B_L equals gcd(n,B_L^L)=n, since all valuations of n are <= L. This is not a splitting argument inside the exceptional support.

Here is a finite superset of exceptional values. For each template and weights form

  H_v(Z)=Res_Y(R_v(Y),Z-Y^{e_v}).

Its roots are exactly the e_v-th powers of roots of R_v; nonzero roots only, and polynomial degree in Z bounded by deg R_v. For univariate A,B with nonzero constant and leading coefficients, replace them by

  Res_W(A(W), W^{deg B} B(Z/W)).

Its roots are exactly all pairwise products of a root of A and a root of B. The product formula for the resultant proves this without assuming square-freeness. Repetition gives an integer polynomial whose degree is bounded by the product of the r fixed coordinate degrees; it may include products of mutually incompatible coordinate choices, which is deliberate. Take its integer roots of length L and positive sign. This root set, unioned over choices, is E_L. Every constant component supplies such a root by the slice; every rational integer in the list is a product of admissible slice roots and so satisfies the support conclusion. No enumeration of divisors of the constant coefficients is required.

For fixed k,delta, there are at most a fixed constant times (1+|D_L|)^{k-1} tree/orientation choices, and at most (L+1)^k lists of weights for each. Thus the list has polynomial cardinality. To bound the arithmetic work for H_v, the first Sylvester matrix has dimension O(L), with coefficient degrees <=1 in Z and entries of polynomial bit length. Exact determinant interpolation on O(L) small integers and rational interpolation cost polynomially many bit operations (integer determinant heights from the row-norm bound). Its output degree is bounded independently of L as above. The product resultants have fixed dimensions hence preserve polynomial bit lengths. Extracting linear rational factors of the primitive part over Q and checking integral, positive L-bit roots costs polynomially many bit operations; multiplication by a scalar cannot change a root. This proves effective list construction without factoring an unrelated integer.

## 4. Arithmetic corollary and boundaries

A connected true pattern has a spanning tree; its prime multiplicity vector sums to <=L. Off E_L every candidate intersection is finite over C. For each tree and multiplicity vector the r equations have total degree at most max(delta,L), polynomial coefficient bits and at most k variables. Standard fixed-dimensional effective real algebraic geometry samples their finite real solution sets in polynomial bit time; every point of a finite real set is its own component. For example use sign-invariant cylindrical algebraic decomposition for these equations, with its degree and number bounds doubly exponential in the number of variables, not in the bit length or polynomial degree. For a finite set, only point cells can satisfy the equations. Exact algebraic descriptions allow integrality and positivity tests in polynomial time, including by refining coordinate isolation and testing exact equality to the candidate integer. Retain only tuples of distinct positive primes (deterministic primality), their specified exact product, and the required graph. A valid factorization is self-verifying; no tree or labels are given as advice. If no pattern matches outside E_L, the input does not meet the promise. Mixed-dimensional behaviour across different components of the same template causes no omission: outside E_L none of its components has positive dimension on that level; on a listed value the small-height fallback works from the value itself, independently of which component contained a putative prime tuple.

In the polynomial-magnitude case let M be the largest absolute value of the endpoint integers (or 1 for an empty list). It is polynomial in L. If n is in E_L the support lemma ensures every prime divisor is <=M; ordinary trial division through M suffices, including multiplicities. One may check the graph directly on the resulting tuple. No conclusion follows for n in E_L at unrestricted polynomial bit height. In particular a two-vertex hyperbola XY-C_L=0, where C_L is an efficiently specified positive L-bit constant, has an entire constant component at exponent vector (1,1), n=C_L. If a catalogue were instead allowed to depend on n itself, XY-n=0 could be written for every input. This explains why exceptional **sparsity across all integers of a length** needs the uniform length-only rule; it is not a proof that factoring a particular C_L is hard.

Two mixed equations XY-15=0 and YZ-35=0 give the exact curve (t,15/t,7t/3) for nonzero t. At weights (1,1,1) the monomial is 35t, so n=105 has the isolated point (3,5,7). At weights (1,2,1) the monomial is identically 525; an entire component is present and its integer prime support is contained in the slice endpoints. These hyperbolas are not polynomially separated correspondences: U(X)=V(c/X) as a Laurent identity for c!=0 forces both polynomials to be constant. This separates algebraic representations, not the sets of particular integers that might satisfy other relations as well. The curve (T-1)Y=1 illustrates why the common slice is chosen by removing leading-coefficient zeroes: T=1 has no affine point. If an eliminant for its torus branch is inflated by Y^m, remove that extraneous power before testing its trailing coefficient. None of the checks in this package implements a generic factorization routine.
