# Sources and scope of the comparison

* Agrawal, Saxena and Shubham Sahai Srivastava, [Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), MFCS 2016, article 6, pp. 6:3-6:4 and Theorems 1.2-1.3. They explicitly discuss exact nondegenerate dependencies supplied for a prime and its complementary factor (also composite in Theorem 1.3, with p smaller than the largest prime factor). Substitution of the quotient in a given two-variable relation and univariate polynomial factoring is an established baseline. Their speed bounds for an alternative cyclotomic approach use conjectural hypotheses. Neither knowing a relation nor the elementary two-variable elimination is an originality claim of this note. Boneh and Shoup also discuss important elementary special cases; see [their textbook](https://crypto.stanford.edu/~dabo/cryptobook/), version 0.6, chapter 16, discussion near Exercise 16.14.
* Repository predecessor: [Separated polynomial blocks, section 2](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b088b4a85c4f9b53d2a35b95aa5bd72aa01c7eb9/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-separated-polynomial-blocks/PROOF.md). For a single connected block the equations there compare nonconstant one-variable polynomials U(x)=V(y). In that case escape to infinity of a coordinate propagates to all coordinates and no nonzero constant-product curve occurs. Here the class of edge correspondences is strictly larger: the hyperbola example in section 4 proves this algebraically. Not claimed: inclusion of their separate two-block statement, or a proof that a particular integer admits no alternative separated representation. At polynomial bit height, the separated case already has no true geometric exceptions; only the new mixed branch needs the exception analysis.
* The [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.36.17](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00GQ) is the lying-over fact used after localization; see also [Section 10.36](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00GH) on finite and integral algebras. The determinant and root-product properties of the Sylvester resultant are standard elimination identities, not new.
* Deterministic univariate factorization over Q: A. K. Lenstra, H. W. Lenstra Jr., L. Lovasz, [Factoring polynomials with rational coefficients](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf), Math. Ann. 261 (1982), 515-534. Fixed-dimensional exact real algebraic methods: G. E. Collins, [Quantifier elimination for real closed fields by cylindrical algebraic decomposition](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-07407-4_17), 1975; J. Renegar, [On the computational complexity and geometry of the first-order theory of the reals, part I](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/305759aa-f544-4006-aa85-f70ddce54e00/content), J. Symbolic Comput. 13 (1992), 255-299. Primality testing in polynomial bit time: Agrawal, Kayal, Saxena, [PRIMES is in P](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781).
* The useful combination here is different: a finite two-way correspondence on each edge gives one computable common slice for all constant-monomial components, with rational-prime support controlled by its endpoint coefficients. A uniform catalogue yields polynomially many possible exceptional integer values per length; polynomial coefficient magnitudes absorb all of them in the arithmetic corollary. A targeted search for earlier work combining these tree, weight, and exceptional-height hypotheses located the comparisons above, not a claim to exhaustive priority. No particular deployed construction or general-purpose complexity improvement is evaluated.
