# One public relation graph, or two near-minimum blocks: exact statements and proof

**Setup.** N≥2 is an ordinary integer, L its binary length. Fix K≥1, an integer c≥0, and a single uniform deterministic algorithm which, on 1^L, lists a finite set S_L of positive integers in time polynomial in L (bit model). In particular both cardinality and maximum bit length are polynomial in L. It is not enough that a short list exists as nonuniform advice; the same public algorithm works for every integer of a given length. Discard labels larger than N without changing realizable edges. Let G(N) have the distinct prime divisors as vertices and a directed edge p→q precisely when q=mp+1 for m in S_L. Components below are undirected. A single vertex is connected. For a component its anchor is its smallest prime. Prime exponents are unrestricted positive integers.

**Theorem.** One deterministic algorithm taking only N runs in time polynomial in L (depending on K,c and the public rule). It returns and verifies the complete prime-power decomposition, with class membership, exactly when N has at most K distinct primes and (A) G(N) is connected, or (B) G(N) has exactly two components with anchors x≤y satisfying (y−x)^2≤x L^(2c). Otherwise it can return not in this class. There is no oracle. In case (B) the trees, their directed shapes, anchor values, labels and exponents need not coincide or be known; the two block products themselves need not be close.

## 1. Enumerating finite templates

Enumerate 1≤s≤K, either one labelled tree on s vertices or a partition into two nonempty labelled vertex sets with a tree on each, a distinguished anchor vertex for each, an orientation for each edge, a label m from S_L for each edge and exponents 1≤n_v≤L at all vertices. The bound on exponents follows from 2^(sum n_v)≤N on a true template. Graph shapes, bipartitions and choices of anchors contribute a constant depending on K. The total number is at most O_K((1+|S_L|)^(K−1) L^K). Empty lists, one-vertex trees, prime powers, and the empty edge set in a two-singleton forest are included. Additional edges and multiple incoming edges do not obstruct the existence of a spanning forest.

Starting at the chosen anchor t, assign l_root(t)=t. In traversal order, if the edge orientation agrees with traversal set l_new=m l_old+1; if opposite set l_new=(l_old−1)/m. Thus l_v=(a_v t+b_v)/c_v with a_v,c_v positive integers, b_v a signed integer. There is no requirement that a reversed edge be divisible at arbitrary candidate t. Keep the rational form and impose integer conditions only at final verification. After at most K−1 operations its coefficient bit lengths are O_K(L+1) for the filtered labels: representations update by (ma,mb+c,c) or (a,b−c,mc). Write s_v=a_v/c_v>0 and h_v=b_v/a_v, so l_v=s_v(t+h_v). Forms include the identity in each block. Define its polynomial F(t)=product of l_v(t)^n_v. Its degree d=sum n_v is positive and ≤K L; it is strictly increasing where all l_v≥2. The two-block product uses independent F,G with degrees d,e and D=d+e≤K L.

On any completed candidate verify that every l_v at its assigned anchor is an integer ≥2, that all are distinct and deterministically prime, and that their indicated powers multiply to N exactly. Compute the entire permitted graph from this support and the list. Check the claimed components, all specified edges and labels, anchor minima and, in (B), (y−x)^2≤x L^(2c). A false candidate can never be reported. Standard deterministic primality testing suffices. On a true input this enumeration includes at least one actual spanning tree per component and the true exponents.

## 2. A single component

Intersect 2≤t≤N with 2≤l_v(t)≤N for all v. Positive slopes make this a (possibly empty) rational interval; obtain its integer endpoints using signed exact floor and ceiling. On its integer points F is strictly increasing even where an intermediate l_v is nonintegral. Integer bisection with cross-multiplied rationals finds the only possible t satisfying F(t)=N, if any. Verify as above. At most O(L) comparisons are needed; do not first remove integer t that fail some congruence in a way that might break a binary search predicate. This argument needs undirected connectivity only: an oriented rooted spanning tree pointing out of a single vertex need not exist. For two primes and known m this is elementary quadratic inversion in the squarefree case, not a new primitive.

**Corollaries.** If the full graph p|q−1 on these primes is connected and p_max/p_min≤L^b for fixed integer b≥0, choose S_L={1,...,L^b}. Every edge multiplier (q−1)/p is in this set. No forest hypothesis or small-prime fallback is needed here. A different legitimate rule is S_L={2^j:0≤j<L}, with no size comparability at all.

**Scope of the elementary step.** In (A) alone a uniform finite dictionary can even consist of invertible positive-slope affine relations q=(a p+b)/c with a,c>0 and b signed, all of polynomial bit length. Each direction is a positive-slope rational affine transformation, so the same interval and monotonicity proof applies. For this generalization an O_K(B+L) bound replaces O_K(L) for template coefficients if dictionary coefficients have maximum bit length B. We do not extend (B) to that dictionary: §3 uses the particular unit intercept and positive integer multiplier.

## 3. Normalized shifts along an arbitrary spanning tree

Set H=K·2^K. On a true block of size at most K, take the minimum prime t, and let the true value at vertex v be p_v=l_v(t). The normalized ratio p_v/(s_v t) starts at 1. At a forward edge p→q=mp+1 it is multiplied by q/(q−1); when traversing that edge backwards it is multiplied by (q−1)/q. Each edge's larger prime q is at least t. Thus (1+1/(t−1))^(−K) ≤ p_v/(s_v t) ≤ (1+1/(t−1))^K. Since h_v=t(p_v/(s_v t)−1), the inequality (1+u)^K−1≤K 2^(K−1)u for 0≤u≤1 gives |h_v|≤H for every vertex. Both signs are allowed. This bound is independent of the multipliers' numerical sizes. Testing |h_v|≤H in a guessed two-block template is safe. It only needs to hold for the true anchor assignment. This is why merely selecting an arbitrary vertex as anchor in (B) is not justified.

## 4. Two anchors and one integer to guess

Order the anchors x≤y; write P=x, W=y−x, Q=L^c. Make the two labelled block roles both available. Put P0=16(H+Q+1)^2. If P≤P0, then y≤P0+Q sqrt(P0). Enumerate both integer anchors up to that explicit polynomial bound and verify. The other primes may be very large; they are computed by the template and are not trial-divided. This small-anchor branch is always available without knowing whether a true P is small.

For the remaining proof assume P>P0. Then H≤P/4 and W≤Q sqrt(P)≤P/4. The multiset containing n_v copies of u_v=t_j+h_v in its block j has D positive entries, minimum m0≥3P/4, maximum ≤3P/2 and range R≤W+2H. Define computable rational constants C0=product of s_v^n_v>0 and B0=sum n_v h_v. The geometric and arithmetic means are, respectively,

    g=(N/C0)^(1/D),       A=(S+B0)/D,       S=d x+e y ∈ Z.

There is no assumption that g or A is known exactly as a real number. Taylor's theorem for log about A gives log A−mean(log u)≤R^2/(2m0^2), since the linear deviations sum to zero. AM–GM gives the lower bound zero. Using 1−exp(−z)≤z for z≥0 gives 0≤A−g≤A R^2/(2m0^2)≤2R^2/P. Consequently

    0 ≤ S−(Dg−B0) ≤ 2D(W+2H)^2/P ≤ 2D(Q+2H)^2.

Compute J=floor(Dg−B0) exactly. Write C0=c1/c2 with positive integers c1,c2 and B0=b1/b2 with b2>0. For integer j, j≤Dg−B0 iff u=j b2+b1≤0, or (for u>0) c1 u^D≤(D b2)^D N c2. Double lower/upper endpoints until bracketed and binary-search this monotone integer predicate (or use the coefficient-bit bound in §5). Try J through J+1+2D(Q+2H)^2 inclusive. This is a one-sided polynomial list for S; it does not replace the nonlinear condition with equality at g. Negative candidate sums or positive nonintegral intermediate coordinates cannot cause false positives.

For each trial S, substitute Y=(S−dX)/e into F(X)G(Y)−N. Clear denominators to get a nonzero integer polynomial: its degree is D, with top rational coefficient lc(F)lc(G)(−d/e)^e ≠0. List its integer roots by deterministic univariate polynomial factorization over Q, retaining linear factors with integral root. For each integer x compute the rational y=(S−d x)/e and check integrality. Then verify all case-(B) conditions in §1, including the graph and exponent product. There is no assumption about uniqueness of a polynomial root, monotonicity after substitution or squarefreeness. A genuine large-anchor branch succeeds because the true S is in the list and the true x is an exact root. Exhausting all verified branches proves the recognition claim.

## 5. Bit complexity and size of the classes

In one template the O_K(L)-bit affine coefficients can be multiplied to total degree D≤K L, giving dense rational polynomials with common denominators and numerator coefficient bit lengths O_K(L^2+L log(L+1)) by the product of the integer coefficient 1-norms. This also bounds log-height of C0. B0 has polynomial bit length (at most K rational terms with O_K(L)-bit coefficients and weights ≤L). The center Dg−B0 has polynomial bit length in the sense log_2(2+|Dg−B0|)=poly(L); indeed g≤max(1,N/C0). Its floor is found using poly(L)-bit operands even after powers to exponent D. The count of trial S and both small-anchor bounds are polynomial since K,c are fixed. Substitution adds at most O(D(log(2+|S|)+log(2+D))) to coefficient bit lengths after a common denominator is used. Univariate rational factorization is polynomial in dense degree and coefficient bit length; no lattice heuristic about integer factoring is used. The same bounds cover exact evaluations, graph reconstruction and deterministic primality tests on candidate values (or discard values outside [2,N] immediately). Together with §1 this is polynomial bit time on all inputs, including nonmembers.

For fixed exactly s≥2 distinct primes and X≥2, write T=ceil(log_2(2X)). There are O(T) bit lengths up to X; at each length the preceding number of guessed shapes, labels and multiplicities is bounded by a polynomial in T. In (A) always choose the least support prime as template coordinate. It is ≤X^(1/s) because N≥p_min^s. Each coordinate and template determines at most one N. This proves O(X^(1/s) polylog(2X)). In (B), P≤X^(1/s) likewise, and each P permits at most 1+Q sqrt(P) choices of the other anchor, with Q≤T^c. Summation gives O(X^(3/(2s)) polylog(2X)). These are elementary upper bounds, not asymptotics. They rely on S_L depending on L alone; labels cannot be picked after seeing each individual factorization. Both bounds imply density zero. They do not imply any worst-case improvement for unrestricted factoring or establish infinitely many primes in prescribed chains.
