# Source comparisons and limits of the claim

1. **Known relation, two factors.** Manindra Agrawal, Nitin Saxena and Shubham Sahai Srivastava, *Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies*, MFCS 2016, Article 6: [paper and metadata](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), [PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol058-mfcs2016/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6.pdf). The introduction on printed p. 6:3, before Theorem 1.2, explicitly substitutes Y=N/X in a supplied nondegenerate bivariate relation and obtains the integer roots of the cleared polynomial. Section 4.2 also discusses a small norm for the base-p expansion, a genuinely N-only related restriction; with q=mp+1 and m<p, the small-multiplier two-prime case is especially close. The quadratic case and the use of a univariate equation are not new. Our connected spanning-tree case is an elementary synthesis of one-parameter elimination with polynomial enumeration, not a new integer-factoring primitive. The two-block case does not suppose the relation between the two minima is known: the short list for its constant has to follow from N and the template's normalization.

2. **Weighted Fermat / Lehman.** Jonathon Hales and Ghaith Hiary, *A generalization of Lehman's method*, Ramanujan J. 65 (2024), 1773–1790, [published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-00959-7); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15586). The published §2, Proposition 2.1 and discussion after its proof, compares aq+rbp with (r+1)(a b^r N)^(1/(r+1)) for N=p^r q and tests a candidate sum with a degree-(r+1) univariate polynomial. Thus even weighted sums and subsequent elimination are precedented, including the a=b=1 two-singleton example. Fermat's square-difference method is the ordinary squarefree singleton-pair baseline. Our square-root gap is not claimed best even there. We make no claim to improve Hales–Hiary's general power-divisor algorithms or any other general complexity bound. The further statement studied here is that two hidden graph components with different, possibly reversed, prime-affine relations reduce to a *shifted* weighted-product problem with bounded offsets about their respective minima, even for large sparse-dictionary multipliers. Raw block products need not have a Fermat-sized difference.

3. **Prime chains are classical.** Kevin Ford, Sergei V. Konyagin and Florian Luca, [*Prime chains and Pratt trees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0473), study the relation p|(q−1), including counts and height. Douglas S. Stones, [*On prime chains*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2166), studies fixed affine recurrences including Cunningham-type chains. We do not introduce these relations or prove a distribution theorem for chains. No infinity claim is deduced from a finite example.

4. **Other algorithms and the exact information regime.** Bartosz Źrałek, [*A deterministic version of Pollard's p−1 algorithm*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-09-02262-5), gives deterministic recovery results involving smooth shifted prime factors and studies totient reductions. No smoothness condition is imposed here; some instances can of course also be handled by those or other methods. François Morain, Guénaël Renault and Benjamin Smith, [*Deterministic factoring with oracles*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08444), study access to number-theoretic oracles including exact totients. Markus Hittmeir and Jacek Pomykała, [*Deterministic Integer Factorization with Oracles for Euler's Totient Function*](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2020-1891), consider, in particular, a prime-power decomposition of the totient. Our structural promise is different; we do not transform either oracle problem into a general bare-N result. Nor is prior knowledge of high bits or an auxiliary CRT residue part of our input.

5. **Standard computational tools.** A. K. Lenstra, H. W. Lenstra, Jr., L. Lovász, [*Factoring polynomials with rational coefficients*](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf), Math. Ann. 261 (1982), 515–534, gives deterministic polynomial-time rational polynomial factorization. M. Agrawal, N. Kayal and N. Saxena, [*PRIMES is in P*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781), Ann. Math. 160 (2004), 781–793, supplies the deterministic prime test in the bit bound. Tiny numerical tests below use exact elementary certificates instead.

6. **Nearby elementary all-close baseline.** If N has a fixed number s of prime factors counted with multiplicity and all are close to B=floor(N^(1/s)), one can expand the product of B plus their displacements. When the elementary symmetric coefficients of these displacements each have absolute value <B/2 they are recovered as signed base-B digits; integer roots of the resulting polynomial give the displacements. This simple observation neither requires nor addresses separated internal sizes in the two-block problem. We do not present it as a new bound. Distinguish assertions about the minima from closeness of all prime divisors or block products.

A bounded comparison looked for factoring under enumerated affine prime relations, generalized Fermat/Lehman weighted sums and prime-power divisors, prime chains, and small dependencies. We found the relevant component methods above; we did not identify an explicit theorem with these two graph-block hypotheses and arbitrary unknown multiplicities. This is not an exhaustive priority search, an optimality claim or a claim to have invented AM–GM interpolation. Tests are predetermined number-theory examples, not sampled real-world inputs.
