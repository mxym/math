# Sources, attribution and limits

* James Maynard, [*Dense clusters of primes in subsets*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2593#page=3), Theorem 3.2 (also [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X16007296)). This is the quantitative input for the local 1/s lower bound: a fixed, sufficiently large integer window y_s can be chosen with c₀ log y_s≥s for the theorem's absolute cluster constant c₀. The theorem then supplies ≫u exp(−√log u) starts in [u,2u] whose intervals contain s primes. Any particular selected s-prime set occurs for at most y_s+1 integer starts. We use its order of magnitude only, not a conjectural twin-prime asymptotic or even infinitely many twins. Prime clusters themselves are not our contribution.
* [Connected-support counting, fixed repository version](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/fbcb2d80cf4ce4ceb9d2e65f383db765e7b1a59c/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/connected-prime-support-counting). That theorem treats a *single* connected component and gives its sharp supremum exponent when at least two support primes must occur. The present problem permits any number of components, charges one genuine s-vertex seed in each wholly large component, and sums over distinct component minima. The lower construction keeps bins with enough primes and drops the others; it does not require a connected scaffold or the Baker–Harman–Pintz short-interval theorem.
* C. Borgs, J. Chayes, J. Kahn and L. Lovász, [*Left and right convergence of graphs with bounded degree*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0115), Lemma 2.1: anchored connected-set/subtree counts in bounded-degree graphs are classical. The elementary plane-tree and Dirichlet-series bounds used here are related standard tools, not new enumeration primitives. For a series with nonnegative terms the convergence exponent is controlled by the upper counting exponent using dyadic summation; §5 gives the argument directly.
* G. Tenenbaum, [*Normal largest gap between prime factors*](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.1107); J.-M. De Koninck and I. Kátai, [*Consecutive neighbour spacings between the prime divisors of an integer*](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/colloquium-mathematicum/online/114711/consecutive-neighbour-spacings-between-the-prime-divisors-of-an-integer). These study relative logarithmic spacings and typical integers, not the exceptional-set counting exponent for a local lower bound on all additive-gap components. Searches through isolated/paired prime factors, bounded clusters, and consecutive-prime-divisor spacings did not locate an identical statement; no global priority claim follows.

The upper-bound and dense-bin arguments are purely combinatorial/analytic; the quantitative fixed prime-cluster theorem and PNT are imported with attribution. No result here determines the density of twin primes, handles a prescribed target integer, or has a cryptographic conclusion.
