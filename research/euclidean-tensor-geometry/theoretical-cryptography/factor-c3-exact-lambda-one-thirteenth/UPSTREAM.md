# Upstream and scope

1. Nikhil Gupta, Alan Sikarov and Ilya Volkovich, [*A Computational Perspective on Carmichael Numbers*](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/212/), ECCC TR26-212 (public September 26, 2026). The fixed report's [PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/212/download/) §1.1 (printed pp. 4–5) distinguishes \(C_3\) from its simple class \(a=1\) and explains the unresolved polynomial-time case beyond that class. Fact 2.10 and Lemma 2.11 (printed p. 10) give pairwise coprimality of \(a,b,c\), \(\lambda(R)=gabc\), and the square-decoding interval for \(g\). Section 3 (p. 12) proves the simple-class guarantee by taking \(p=1+g\) and then treating a two-prime cofactor. Section 4, Fact 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 (pp. 12–13), also record the identity \(Jabc=g(ab+ac+bc)+(a+b+c)\); the identity itself is not new here. Their Theorems 1–2 (pp. 5–6) concern given-totient and size-bounded cases. This note instead obtains a numeric \(R^{1/13}\)-times-polylog bound throughout \(C_3\) given one exact exponent, and gives direct bit-polynomial recovery in the stated structural windows. It does not settle their bit-polynomial question throughout \(C_3\).
2. Andrew Granville and Carl Pomerance, [*Two contradictory conjectures concerning Carmichael numbers*](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/paper125.pdf), *Math. Comp.* 71 (2002), 883–908, §5a, Eq. (6), p. 894. They derive the normalized pairwise coprimality and the three local congruences for \(g\) used by the independent fixture generator. The section studies counts and parametric families, not the single-input exact-exponent recovery time. See also [G. J. O. Jameson, *Carmichael numbers with three prime factors*](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=07b3d3c4b20d0aac456ac03b8af9265fd58f178e&repid=rep1&type=pdf), for the \(C_3\) notation and elementary structural bounds.
3. François Morain, Guénaël Renault and Benjamin Smith, [*Deterministic factoring with oracles*](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08444v2), particularly Theorems 1.1–1.2 and §§4–5. Their one-value algorithms have size hypotheses in the three-prime regime. The present note has a different promise (a three-prime Carmichael radical), a different running-time target, and no assumption that one prime exceeds \(\sqrt{R}\). Neither result implies a general bit-polynomial exact-exponent algorithm for all three-prime inputs. As a concrete separation from the size regions, \(R=252601=41\cdot61\cdot101\) is non-simple and has \(r<g^2\). Its normalized logarithms satisfy \(\alpha_r<3/8\), \(\alpha_q<1/3\), \(\alpha_p>1/4\) by integer powers. Thus \(2\alpha_r+3\alpha_q<2\), and \(\alpha_q^2<(1-\alpha_q)\alpha_p\), so neither size test applies, nor does \(\alpha_r>1/2\). It also fails the MRS Remark 5.1 exact-\(\lambda\) threshold: \(r^4=101^4=104060401<R\lambda(R)=252601\cdot600=151560600\), so \(\alpha_r<(1+\theta)/4\) for \(\theta=\log_R\lambda(R)\). This is a finite witness to distinct conditions, not an infinitude claim.
4. The block-product technique is classical. Edgar Costa and David Harvey, [*Faster deterministic integer factorization*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.2116), §2 and Proposition 9 (pp. 5–6), explain the Pollard–Strassen/Bostan–Gaudry–Schost framework and a small-factor bound using sieved products. The new use in this note is evaluating one arithmetic progression of length \(O(g/J)\) derived from the exact Carmichael identities, and trading its square-root cost against the exact cubic coefficient search. The proof uses generic monic remainders, not the invertibility hypotheses of specialised progression evaluation. Standard integer multiplication is available in [Harvey–van der Hoeven, *Integer multiplication in time \(O(n\log n)\)*](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~davidharvey/research/nlogn/index.html); for primality see [Agrawal–Kayal–Saxena, *PRIMES is in P*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2004/160-2/p12).
5. Richard Pinch [*The Carmichael numbers up to \(10^{21}\)*](https://antsmath.org/ANTSVIII/files/pinch.pdf) and Andrew Shallue–Jonathan Webster [*Advances in Tabulating Carmichael Numbers*, v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14495v3) give tabulation algorithms for all Carmichael numbers below a bound. Their aggregate complexity is not the worst-case complexity of factoring one supplied integer with its exact exponent. Bartosz Żrałek, [*A deterministic version of Pollard's \(p-1\) algorithm*, v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.4102v5), Remark 7.4, instead bounds deterministic Carmichael factoring in terms of factoring \(n-1\); its stated subexponential reduction needs that different auxiliary factorization, not one unfactored \(\lambda(R)\).

A bounded search on 2026-11-28 included the above primary papers, their available later versions and related citation searches for deterministic \(C_3\) factoring, exact lambda advice, and the \(1/13\) and \(1/7\) exponents. Repository commit searches for “Carmichael”, “triprime” and “FactorLambda” did not locate a matching note; the adjacent exact-lambda two-odd-prime note is a different structural range. No checked source was found to combine the progression and cubic searches in this way. This is a statement about the searched materials, not a claim of exhaustive priority.
