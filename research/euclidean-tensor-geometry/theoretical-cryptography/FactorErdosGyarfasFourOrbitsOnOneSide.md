# Four abelian orbits on one side of a bipartition

**Theorem.** Let \(G\) be a nonempty finite simple bipartite graph of minimum degree at least three. Suppose a free abelian action preserves both parts and has at most four orbits on one part, with no restriction on how many orbits occur on the other part. If \(G\) has no simple cycle of length \(4,8,16\), its quotient is the simple \(K_{3,3}\): there are exactly three orbits on both sides, with one translation matching per base edge. In particular, each vertex lies on a simple \(32\)-cycle. The minimum order is \(798\), with three nonisomorphic graphs at equality.

Here only the extension from at most three to at most four orbits on a specified side is new. The rooted result and the three minimal graphs are consequences of the previously checked [rooted theorem](FactorErdosGyarfasK33Rooted.md) and [fixed-base census](FactorErdosGyarfasFixedBaseMinima.md). This does not establish the unrestricted conjecture or cover non-part-preserving actions with more than six total orbits.

## 1. Necessary quotient shapes

Let \(A\) be the acting group. Each vertex orbit is a copy of \(A\); each orbit of cross edges is a matching \(g\mapsto g+t\) between the two corresponding vertex fibers. The earlier [one-side theorem and incidence bound](FactorErdosGyarfasThreeOrbitsOnOneSide.md) dispose of the case where either part has at most three orbits, and force \(n\le6\) opposite a side of exactly four orbits if there are no \(4,8,16\)-cycles. Thus a counterexample to the extension must have four row fibers and \(n=4,5,6\) column fibers. The same reference gives the first two of these necessary conditions; here a common column has a positive entry in both rows.

1. Quotient entries are \(0,1,2\), and no two entries \(2\) share a row or column. Each pair of rows has at most three common columns.
2. A pair of rows that has a double in one of its common columns has at most two common columns. Three common columns would then produce four length-two translation paths, of which only one pair uses the same intermediate fiber and the same last matching.
3. Delete a matching whenever its two endpoint fibers both have degree greater than three. The degree is common to all vertices of that fiber. Repeat to termination; the graph is still simple, has the same free action and fibers, has minimum degree at least three, and cannot have gained an excluded cycle. Exactly because this deletion has stopped, every remaining positive cell has an endpoint of degree three.
4. For any nonempty row and column subsets with at least one subset of size at most three, take the subgraph induced by their full fibers. If all restricted degrees are at least three, the one-side theorem requires that these subsets each have size three and their induced quotient is all ones. All other such restrictions are forbidden.

Any surviving graph has connected layer quotient: otherwise every component of the quotient contains at most three row fibers (all rows are nonisolated), and condition 4 forces each component to have exactly three row fibers. There cannot be two such components among four rows. The actual lift may still be disconnected; no connectedness of the lift is required in the rest of the argument.

The replay enumerates all column support multisets of sizes \(n=4,5,6\). A support has size at least two. Each two-row support must carry a double; bigger supports may or may not carry a double. If a pair of rows has three common supports, neither row may be doubled in any of those columns. The enumeration assigns at most one double per column and row, and checks all restricted degree conditions. Sorting the columns and minimizing over the 24 row permutations gives representatives for precisely the shapes satisfying the stated tests. There are respectively **8, 17, and 6** types. The order of this enumeration is different from the one used to search for chart data: the replay chooses supports first, whereas generation chose complete columns.

## 2. An all-label certificate for each shape

For each fixed connected shape, choose a spanning tree greedily in column-then-row order, using the first matching for each occupied cell. Change the sheet origins so that all tree matchings have label zero. Orient all matchings from row to column. Give a formal basis coordinate in \(\mathbb Z^r\) to each non-tree matching, including the second matching of each doubled cell. Reverse darts negate the relevant label. An actual assignment is a homomorphism \(\phi:\mathbb Z^r\to A\) with each difference of the two labels in a doubled cell nonzero. All simple lifts give such an assignment; no assumption of generic labels, prime order, cyclicity, or torsion freeness is made.

Let \(R\subseteq\ker\phi\) denote a lattice of label relations. Start with \(R=0\). At each listed state \(R\) the checker reads a cyclically nonbacktracking quotient walk of length \(4,8,\) or \(16\), verifies that its total voltage belongs to \(R\), and computes all differences \(b\) between distinct prefix positions in the same fiber. It verifies that none of those \(b\) is already in \(R\). If no such difference vanishes under \(\phi\), the closed lifted walk is simple. Otherwise, for at least one difference \(b\), \(\phi\) also annihilates \(R+\mathbb Z b\), and the checker traverses that child. A terminal child is admitted only if its lattice contains the explicitly required nonzero parallel-label difference in at least one doubled cell, a contradiction. All other children are proved by their own stored walks. Integer row Hermite form makes child deduplication exact. The replay also verifies that every supplied nonterminal state is reachable and that every child strictly enlarges its relation lattice.

For opposite side sizes \(4,5,6\) there are respectively \(66,174,52\) nonterminal states, with maximum nonterminal depth at most four. Thus every surviving shape entails a forbidden cycle and the case of exactly four orbits on one side is impossible. Combining with the one-side theorem gives exactly the quotient and the previously known rooted/minimum consequences stated above.

## Replay and scope

Run `python3 verify_erdos_gyarfas_four_on_one_side.py` in this directory, in ordinary Python 3. The [chart file](erdos_gyarfas_four_on_one_side_certificate.json) contains only small integers and explicit dart words. The Python replay independently verifies the representative atlas, all **292** nonterminal states and all **90** distinct contradiction leaves counted by chart; it uses only the standard library. To search for the words we instead used BigInt meet-in-the-middle on pairs of half walks: they have the same quotient endpoints and reduced total voltage, and their internal lifted positions must be disjoint modulo the present relation lattice. The search is not a premise of the proof.

This is a consequence for the free abelian action hypothesis, not a replacement for results on arbitrary graphs, such as [Ducoffe–Dumitru](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28594) or [Tranquilli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02675). It extends the scope of the [three-on-one-side theorem](FactorErdosGyarfasThreeOrbitsOnOneSide.md), while the [general six-orbit theorem](FactorErdosGyarfasGeneralSixOrbitClassification.md) independently allows nonbipartite inputs and actions not preserving a part, under its different total-orbit hypothesis.
