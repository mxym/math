# Residual fibers and numerator pencils on an elliptic quartic

Pure elliptic divisor geometry. Baseline: https://arxiv.org/html/2206.10327v2#S6 Lido's cubic numerator and fixed-orbit Möbius interpolation, as used in [the fixed-cover incidence note](FixedDoubleCoverIncidence.md). We do not claim novelty of Riemann–Roch, symmetric products, trace, or finite-field Chebotarev. The results here describe exactly what those tools give for these projective numerator fibers; the generic-pencil counts do not carry over to a pencil further restricted to split denominator roots. For twisting context see Dèbes–Legrand https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.6154. Characteristic is odd throughout.

Let E/k, k=F_Q, be elliptic with origin O, and D a rational effective degree-four divisor. In the two-quadratic-map setting, D is the sum of pole divisors, and the projective space |D| is identified linearly with coefficient space of the sparse denominator via the four-term numerator. This identification includes singular matrices, unless excluded. Every effective cubic divisor C, not necessarily irreducible or reduced, has a unique residual point R satisfying C+R∼D: equivalently R=ΣD−ΣC under Abel–Jacobi. Sending C to Z=C+R defines Sym³E→|D|. It identifies Sym³E with the incidence variety {(R,Z):R∈Z}, fiberwise inverse (R,Z)↦Z−R; as a scheme this uses subtraction of the universal degree-one divisor on the incidence variety. Over E this is a projective plane bundle, since evaluation H⁰(D)→D|_R is surjective with kernel dimension three by RR. Over rational Z the rational preimages are exactly its distinct rational support points, up to deleting that point to get C. If C is required reduced, keep exactly the R for which Z−R is reduced. If Z is reduced the fiber has #supp(Z)(k) points. If C is required irreducible cubic, the fiber has exactly one point iff Z has splitting type (3,1), and zero otherwise; in particular a nonreduced Z cannot contain an irreducible cubic. Any rational effective C is Galois-stable, not necessarily a sum of rational points.

## Exact count at each rational residual point

For fixed R∈E(k), the number of irreducible cubics C with that residual (equivalently, their sections in the plane |D−R| embedded in |D|) is
 [#E(F_(Q³))/#E(F_Q) − #{P∈E(F_Q):[3]P=ΣD−R}]/3.
Indeed the trace endomorphism T=1+F+F² (F is Q-Frobenius) is a nonzero isogeny because (F−1)T=F³−1. For any S∈E(k) all of its geometric preimages under T satisfy F³P=P. Hence the restriction onto E(k) from E(k³) is surjective with equal fiber sizes #E(k³)/#E(k). The degree-one exceptions are exactly the rational P with 3P=S, and all other points have Frobenius orbit length three. The correction is |E(k)[3]| if S∈3E(k), zero otherwise. Thus the variation across residual points is explicit and at most |E(k)[3]|/3 (zero variation when E(k)[3] trivial).

## Projective line (pencil) count and degeneracies

A k-line in |D| generated by two sections has a fixed divisor B and a basepoint-free moving map E→P¹ in degree d=4−deg B. A two-dimensional section subspace forces deg B≤2 by RR. For deg B=2 no section has an irreducible cubic; in fact neither B nor any moving fiber of degree two can contain one. For deg B=1, B is rational, and a section contains an irreducible cubic iff its moving degree-three fiber is irreducible. For deg B=0 the desired moving fiber is of type (3,1).

Suppose d∈{3,4} and the moving morphism is separable and simply branched, here defined to mean each geometric branch value has exactly one ramified point of index two and all others unramified. Geometric monodromy is S_d: in its normal closure all inertia groups are generated by transpositions (ramification is tame); the group generated by all inertia is normal and has unramified quotient over geometric P¹, hence is the entire group. A transitive subgroup generated by transpositions equals S_d by the connected graph of its transpositions. Arithmetic monodromy is also S_d, since it injects into S_d and contains the geometric group. In particular the normal closure is regular. Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2d branch values and Galois closure genus g=1+|S_d|(d−2)/2, namely 4 or 25. The requisite cycle type in either S_d is a single 3-cycle, whose centralizer has size 3. Twist the smooth complete normal closure by a 3-cycle (or its inverse, depending on Frobenius convention). For any unramified rational base point, the twist has exactly three rational points over it if its Frobenius is conjugate to the 3-cycle, zero otherwise; this is the equation g=a Frob_t a^(-1) on fiber labels. Its genus is unchanged, so Hasse–Weil bounds its total count by Q+1±2g√Q. At most 2d·d! points over rational branch values can enter this count. Thus the number of rational sections containing irreducible cubic differs from (Q+1)/3 by at most (2g√Q+2d·d!)/3. Branch sections do not contain a cubic in either case, since they are already nonreduced (including any fixed point for d=3).

For a basepoint-free moving degree-four map which factors over k as two separable degree-two maps, no fiber contains an irreducible cubic: at unramified t the intermediate quadratic block system is Frobenius-invariant, excluding a 3-cycle; a branch section is nonreduced. This obstruction requires factorization over k, not merely imprimitivity of geometric monodromy without descent to k. Such pencils really exist in some classes, e.g. |4O| and pencil from x², with the 2∘2 quotient through x. In contrast, for a basepoint-free simply branched pencil, the total number of reduced rational cubics of all splitting types over the line is #E(k)+O(1), because the incidence restricted to the line identifies each rational R with its one section, and only O(d²) choices can occur over branch values.

## Exact toy pencil and trace test

Use E:y²=x³+x+1 over F_5, T=(2,1) of order 3, and D=2O+2(−T). Then D−T∼3O by Abel–Jacobi. The degree-three morphism t=y/x has simple poles at the two x=0 points and O (fiber at infinity reduced), and its pole divisor is equivalent to 3O, so it induces a line in |D| with fixed point T and that moving map. Finite fibers have cubic polynomial x³−t²x²+x+1, with no point over x=0 there. Its discriminant mod 5 is 4t⁶+t⁴+2t²+4. The derivative is 4t(t⁴+t²+1); no root is common: at t=0 the discriminant is 4, and modulo u²+u+1 for u=t² the discriminant reduces to u+2, coprime to u²+u+1. Thus all geometric branching is simple (a simultaneous triple root would give a repeated discriminant zero). Exactly t=0,1,4 give irreducible cubics; infinity is split.

The [exact JS toy](verify-elliptic-residual-pencil-toy.js) computes traces of all 99 genuinely cubic points of E(F_125) and obtains three rational traces with 9 such points each and six with 12; dividing by three gives the predicted residual histogram 3 and 4, respectively. The field is represented as F_5[u]/(u³+u+1). The companion verifier checks all finite F_5 pencil fibers; checked with exact finite-field arithmetic.
