# Exact-energy lattice shells: arithmetic constraints and the residue TV cutoff

Let \(L\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a fixed full-rank Euclidean lattice such that \(Q(x)=\|x\|^2\) is integer-valued and primitive on \(L\), meaning \(\gcd\{Q(x):x\in L\}=1\). Write \(B(x,y)=Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)=2x\cdot y\). Fix an integer \(q\ge2\), the quotient \(A=L/qL\), and the uniform law \(U_A\) on it. Put
\[
 b_L=\gcd\{B(x,y):x,y\in L\}\in\{1,2\},
             \qquad c=q\gcd(q,b_L).
 \tag{1}
\]
The residue of \(Q(x)\) modulo \(c\) depends only on \(x\bmod qL\). Thus for \(R=(r_1,\ldots,r_n)\in A^n\), define
\(\mathcal C_m=\{R:\sum_iQ(r_i)=m\bmod c\}\). The choice of the representative of each \(r_i\) does not matter.

Let \(D_t\) be the centered width-\(t\) discrete Gaussian on \(L\) with masses proportional to \(e^{-\pi Q(x)/t^2}\), let \(P_t=\mathcal L(D_t\bmod qL)\), and put \(\mu_t=\mathbb E_{D_t}Q(x)\), \(\sigma_t^2=\operatorname{Var}_{D_t}Q(x)\). Take \(t=t_n\to\infty\) with \(t=n^{o(1)}\), and integers \(m=m_n\) with \((m-n\mu_t)/(\sigma_t\sqrt n)\) bounded. The uniform law on the exact shell \(\{x\in L^n:\sum_iQ(x_i)=m\}\), reduced coordinatewise modulo \(qL\), has law \(P_{\mathrm{shell},n}\). The shell and each conditioning event below are nonempty for all large \(n\), as the proof shows.

**Theorem.** On this exact shell the residual arithmetic constraint is, asymptotically in total variation, the single aggregate congruence in (1):
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\big(P_{\mathrm{shell},n},
            P_t^{\otimes n}(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal C_m)\big)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{2}
\]
This compares the entire residue vector, not just any fixed number of its coordinates. Let \(\lambda=\lambda_1(L^*)\), \(K=|\{w\in L^*:\|w\|=\lambda\}|\), and put
\[
 \rho_t=e^{-\pi t^2\lambda^2/q^2},\qquad
 V_q=\begin{cases}K,&q\ge3,\\2K,&q=2.\end{cases}
\]
If \(\sqrt n\,\rho_t\to z\in[0,\infty]\), then
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}\big(P_{\mathrm{shell},n},
             U_A^{\otimes n}(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal C_m)\big)
      \longrightarrow 2\Phi\!\left(\frac{z\sqrt{V_q}}2\right)-1 ,
 \tag{3}
\]
with limit 1 when \(z=\infty\). Here \(\Phi\) is the standard normal cdf. In particular the critical squared width is \(t^2=(q^2/(2\pi\lambda^2))\log n+O(1)\); equivalently the critical energy per block is \(m/n=(d q^2/(4\pi^2\lambda^2))\log n+O(1)\). On the wide side (\(z=0\)), the distance to *unconditioned* product uniform still tends to \(1-1/c\). For \(L=\mathbb Z\), \(c=q\) for odd \(q\), and \(c=2q\) for even \(q\) (so the exact parity-coordinate vector knows the energy modulo 4 for \(q=2\)). The triangular norm \(Q(a,b)=a^2+ab+b^2\) instead has \(b_L=1\) and \(c=q\) even at \(q=2\).

This is a fixed lattice, fixed modulus, growing product dimension statement. It does not address arbitrary growing-dimension lattice families, growing \(q\), or far-off-center energy deviations. It requires no model of an implemented system. [Mazo–Odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/arch/high.dim.spheres.pdf) study high-dimensional ball counts, including Gaussian/theta methods. [Ellenberg–Michel–Venkatesh, Theorem 1.4](https://www.math.ias.edu/~akshay/research/gg.pdf) treat modular equidistribution in fixed dimension three with the radius growing, under arithmetic conditions. The results here instead quantify TV over a growing full residue vector, at the logarithmic energy cutoff, and identify the proper shell congruence for arbitrary fixed primitive forms. The earlier [integer energy-shell comparison](discrete-gaussian-energy-shells.md) deals with *unwrapped coordinate marginals* and proves stronger one-dimensional uniform local estimates there. Here all coordinates are observed only through residues of a general fixed integral form; the conditional local spans and congruence shell matter. Neither statement follows simply from unconditioned product Gaussian smoothing, because conditioning on one exact energy level has probability tending to zero.

## 1. Which congruence survives the quotient?

For \(x,y\in L\),
\[
                      Q(x+qy)-Q(x)=qB(x,y)+q^2Q(y).
 \tag{4}
\]
The gcd of all differences in (4) is precisely \(\gcd(qb_L,q^2)=c\): taking \(x=0\) gives the subgroup \(q^2\mathbb Z\) generated by the primitive \(Q(y)\), and subtracting those terms leaves the subgroup \(q b_L\mathbb Z\). Conversely the displayed gcd divides every difference. Also \(b_L\) divides 2 because \(B(x,x)=2Q(x)\), proving (1). In particular \(c\) is the largest common span for energy differences over all residue classes. Some individual residue classes can have larger spans; nothing here asserts a class-by-class span of \(c\).

For any nontrivial character \(u\mapsto e^{2\pi iku/c}\) of \(\mathbb Z/c\), its values on \(Q(r)\), \(r\in A\), are not all equal. The value at the zero class is 1, and equality everywhere would contradict \(\gcd(c,Q(L))=1\). In particular the expectation of this character under \(U_A\) has modulus less than one, and the same uniform gap holds under \(P_t\) for all large \(t\), since \(P_t\to U_A\) by Poisson summation. The finite character expansion therefore gives
\[
                  U_A^{\otimes n}(\mathcal C_m)=1/c+O(\vartheta^n),
       \qquad P_t^{\otimes n}(\mathcal C_m)=1/c+O(\vartheta^n)
 \tag{5}
\]
for some fixed \(\vartheta<1\), uniformly over \(m\).

## 2. Gaussian cosets and square-law local limits

For a residue class \(r\), let \(\mu_{t,r},\sigma_{t,r}^2\) be the mean and variance of \(Q(x)\) for \(x\sim D_t\) conditioned on \(r\). We need
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mu_t&=d t^2/(2\pi)+o(1),&
 \sigma_t^2&=(d/(2\pi^2))\,t^4(1+o(1)),\\
 |\mu_{t,r}-\mu_t|&=O(t^4\rho_t),&
 \sigma_{t,r}^2/\sigma_t^2&\longrightarrow1
 \quad\text{uniformly over }r,
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{6}
\]
and uniformly bounded centered third moments of size \(O(t^6)\) on both the lattice and each coset. To check (6), write the coset normalizer by Poisson summation as
\[
 \sum_{x\in r+qL}e^{-a Q(x)}
   =\frac{(\pi/a)^{d/2}}{q^d\operatorname{covol}L}
      \sum_{w\in q^{-1}L^*}e^{-\pi^2\|w\|^2/a}e^{2\pi i w\cdot r},
       \quad a=\pi/t^2 .
\]
Its log derivative in \(a\) is minus the mean and its second log derivative is the variance. At \(a\to0\) the nonzero frequencies and their first two derivatives contribute respectively \(O(\rho_t)\), \(O(t^4\rho_t)\), \(O(t^8\rho_t)\), uniformly in the finitely many classes: differentiation introduces fixed-degree polynomial weights in \(t,\|w\|\), and the tails factor at the shortest squared norm. The whole lattice version uses \(\rho_t^{q^2}\) in place of \(\rho_t\). These estimates justify termwise differentiation by locally normal convergence, give (6), and show the \(o(1)\) in the mean since \(t^4\rho_t^{q^2}\to0\). A Gaussian sum comparison on a fixed coset gives \(\mathbb E Q(x)^3=O(t^6)\), for example by the bound \(\|x\|^6 e^{-\pi\|x\|^2/t^2}\le C t^6 e^{-\pi\|x\|^2/(2t^2)}\) and the normalizer ratio at \(t,\sqrt2t\). This also gives the requisite centered bound.

Let \(X_i\) be independent \(D_t\) samples, \(R_i=X_i\bmod qL\), and \(S=\sum_iQ(X_i)\). We prove the uniform relative local statement
\[
 \begin{split}
 \Pr(S=m)&=\frac{\varphi(u_n)+o(1)}{\sigma_t\sqrt n},\\
 \Pr(S=m\mid R)&=\frac{c}{\sigma_t\sqrt n}
    \left(\varphi\left(\frac{m-\sum_i\mu_{t,r_i}}{\sigma_t\sqrt n}\right)+o(1)\right)
     \quad\text{for }R\in\mathcal C_m\text{ with every class count }\ge\eta n ,
 \end{split}                                                        \tag{7}
\]
where \(\varphi\) is the standard normal density, \(u_n=(m-n\mu_t)/(\sigma_t\sqrt n)\), and \(\eta>0\) is any fixed lower class-frequency bound. The second error is uniform over these sequences; it is an additive error in the normalized density but will be used where the Gaussian factor is bounded below. For \(R\notin\mathcal C_m\) the conditional probability is zero.

Here are the full characteristic-function bounds for (7). Choose finitely many pairs of points in equal residue classes whose integer energy differences \(d_j\) have gcd \(c\); such a finite selection is guaranteed by (4). For the unconditional law choose pairs with gcd 1, using \(x=0\) and primitivity. Each of the chosen atoms in its respective Gaussian law has probability \(\ge C_j t^{-d}\) for all large \(t\), since its squared energy is fixed and the normalizer is \(O(t^d)\). For \(\phi_r(\theta)=\mathbb E[e^{i\theta Q(X)}\mid r]\),
\[
  1-|\phi_r(\theta)|^2
      \ge C t^{-2d}\operatorname{dist}(\theta d_j/(2\pi),\mathbb Z)^2
\]
when \(d_j\) came from that residue class. This follows by expanding the characteristic function times its conjugate and keeping the two selected atoms in the nonnegative cosine deficit. An integer Bézout combination of the \(d_j\)'s shows that the maximum of the distance terms is at least a fixed multiple of \(\operatorname{dist}(c\theta/(2\pi),\mathbb Z)\), with \(c=1\) for the unconditional law. For every selected class there are at least \(\eta n\) factors, so on the fundamental Fourier domain \([-\pi/c,\pi/c]\), the product of the moduli is bounded by \(e^{-C_\eta n t^{-2d}\theta^2}\). For the unconditional law the same argument uses \(n\) equal factors. Therefore the integral over \(|\theta|\ge n^{-1/4}\) in the fundamental domain is \(O(\exp[-n^{1/2-o(1)}])=o((\sigma_t\sqrt n)^{-1})\).

For \(|\theta|\le n^{-1/4}\), center each energy factor and use the uniform third moment bound to expand its characteristic function as \(1-\frac12\sigma_{t,r}^2\theta^2+O(t^6|\theta|^3)\), or the analogous whole lattice expansion. For normalized arguments \(v=\sigma_t\sqrt n\,\theta\) fixed, the product of centered functions tends to \(e^{-v^2/2}\) uniformly over the class sequences, since all variances have common leading scale and the total third-order error is \(O(|v|^3/\sqrt n)\). Throughout the whole small-frequency interval its modulus is bounded by \(e^{-C n t^4\theta^2}\), using \(t^2 n^{-1/4}=o(1)\). Thus dominated Fourier inversion gives (7), with uniform error even in the oscillatory factor determined by \(m-\sum_i\mu_{t,r_i}\). On the compatible class event the full integrand for the integer Fourier inversion is \(2\pi/c\)-periodic: each individual energy is congruent to its class's fixed \(Q(r_i)\), and the phase from \(m\) cancels the total. There are \(c\) periods in the ordinary inversion interval of length \(2\pi\), giving the factor \(c\). This proves the local result without a uniform aperiodicity assumption on individual classes and without needing estimates for high-denominator quadratic exponential sums.

## 3. From the shell to conditional product residues

Choose a deterministic \(J_n\to\infty\) slowly enough that \(J_n=o(\sqrt n)\) and \(J_n t^2\rho_t\to0\), which is possible because \(t^2\rho_t\to0\). Under the product law of the \(R_i\)'s, every class count \(N_r\) differs from \(nP_t(r)\) by at most \(\sqrt n J_n\) with probability tending to 1 by Chebyshev and the fixed number of classes. This remains true under \(P_t^{\otimes n}(\cdot\mid\mathcal C_m)\) by (5). Call these class sequences good. Their counts have a fixed lower frequency limit because \(P_t\to U_A\). Moreover by (6) and the exact identity \(\sum_r P_t(r)\mu_{t,r}=\mu_t\),
\[
 \left|\sum_i\mu_{t,r_i}-n\mu_t\right|
   =\left|\sum_r (N_r-nP_t(r))(\mu_{t,r}-\mu_t)\right|
    \le C \sqrt n J_n t^4\rho_t=o(\sigma_t\sqrt n)
\]
uniformly on the good event. As \(u_n\) is bounded, (7) gives
\(\Pr(S=m\mid R)/\Pr(S=m)=c(1+o(1))\) uniformly on good compatible sequences. In particular \(\Pr(S=m)>0\) eventually. Conditioning \(D_t^{\otimes n}\) on \(S=m\) is exactly the uniform law on the shell, by the Gaussian weights. Its residue likelihood ratio against \(P_t^{\otimes n}(\cdot\mid\mathcal C_m)\) is
\[
            \frac{\Pr(S=m\mid R)\,P_t^{\otimes n}(\mathcal C_m)}
                 {\Pr(S=m)}
                =1+o(1)
\]
uniformly where the reference has mass tending to 1, by (5). Integration on the good region then also gives shell mass tending to 1 there. Hence (2) follows in total variation. This last normalization step is necessary: merely conditioning a high-probability event under the canonical iid law on the much rarer \(S=m\) does not itself preserve high probability.

## 4. The residue likelihood and sharp cutoff

Poisson summation on each Gaussian coset gives the one-copy likelihood
\[
 \frac{P_t(r)}{U_A(r)}
    =1+\rho_t F(r)+O(\rho_t^{1+\delta}),\qquad
 F(r)=\sum_{\substack{w\in L^*\\\|w\|=\lambda}}
                                e^{2\pi i w\cdot r/q}
 \tag{8}
\]
with a fixed \(\delta>0\) from the gap to the next dual norm and the whole-lattice denominator. The error function in (8) has exactly zero \(U_A\)-mean because the likelihood mean is 1 and the shortest shell has no trivial character. The function \(F\) is real by paired signs and is centered. Its variance is the sum of squared multiplicities of the shortest vectors grouped modulo \(qL^*\), by character orthogonality. For \(q\ge3\) no two are congruent because their difference has length at most \(2\lambda<q\lambda\). For \(q=2\) any nonzero such difference has length exactly \(2\lambda\), forcing opposing vectors, which are indeed congruent. Thus the variance is \(V_q\).

Under \(U_A^{\otimes n}(\cdot\mid\mathcal C_m)\), the normalized sum \(n^{-1/2}\sum_iF(R_i)\) still converges to \(\mathcal N(0,V_q)\). For a real argument \(u\), expand the congruence event by its \(c\) characters. The trivial term has the ordinary iid characteristic function; each nontrivial term factors as
\((\mathbb E_{U_A} e^{iu F(R_1)/\sqrt n} e^{2\pi i k Q(R_1)/c})^n\) times a unit phase. The base modulus is bounded away from 1 for large \(n\) by the strict nontrivial-character gap in §1. After division by the probability in (5) only the iid CLT remains. Also any \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) remainder under ordinary uniform remains such conditionally, again by (5).

Suppose \(\sqrt n\rho_t\to z<\infty\). Taylor-expand the log product likelihood in (8). The summed one-copy error is \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) under product uniform by its zero mean and variance \(O(n\rho_t^{2+2\delta})=o(1)\). The quadratic term converges to \(z^2 V_q\) before multiplication by \(-1/2\), by the law of large numbers for \(F^2\) and the uniform error bound. Cubic terms cost \(O(n\rho_t^3)=o(1)\). Equation (5) changes the conditional log likelihood by \(o(1)\). Hence its limit under conditional uniform is \(z\sqrt{V_q}\,Z-z^2 V_q/2\) for \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\). For a likelihood \(e^W\), the TV distance is \(\mathbb E(1-e^W)_+\). This is a bounded continuous function of \(W\), so the limit requires no unproved likelihood uniform integrability. Completing the square in the Gaussian integral gives \(2\Phi(z\sqrt{V_q}/2)-1\).

If \(\sqrt n\rho_t\to\infty\), use the event \(\sum_iF(R_i)>\frac12 n\rho_t V_q\). Its probability tends to 0 under product uniform and to 1 under the canonical residue product, by Chebyshev: the first mean is zero and the second is \(n\rho_t V_q(1+o(1))\) by (8), and each variance is \(O(n)\). Conditioning either law on \(\mathcal C_m\) preserves these limits by (5). This proves (3) via (2). When \(z=0\), the conditional uniform itself is at TV distance \(1-U_A^{\otimes n}(\mathcal C_m)\to1-1/c\) from its unconditioned product, giving the stated wide-side corollary. Finally (6) and the centrality condition give \(m/n=d t^2/(2\pi)+o(1)\), which converts the squared-width scale into the claimed energy scale. The companion checker verifies finite gcd, congruence, shell, and shortest-character collision identities; the limiting theorem is not an extrapolation of those finite checks.
