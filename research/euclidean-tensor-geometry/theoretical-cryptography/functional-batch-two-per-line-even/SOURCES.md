# Sources and scope

- **[KRS]** Altan B. Kılıç, Alberto Ravagnani, and Flavio Salizzoni, [*The Length of Functional Batch and PIR Codes*, arXiv:2508.02586v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02586v2). Definitions 2.2–2.4 specify pairwise disjoint recovery sets for an arbitrary nonzero-vector list. Section 3, Eq. (6) gives the known binary equality; Open Problem 3.13 asks for \(\mathrm{FB}(k,(q^k-1)/(q-1)+1,q)=2(q^k-1)/(q-1)\). We write \(s\) for their dimension \(k\). Open Problem 3.12 is the separate unprojectivized length and is not presented here as a new result.
- **[EZ]** Isaac Barouch Essayag and Aryeh Lev Zabokritskiy (Yohananov), [*The Functional Batch Conjecture And Its Extension To All Finite Fields*, arXiv:2611.34382v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34382v1). Theorem 2.3 proves the two-side statement for odd \(q\), Corollary 1.2(b) gives the corresponding optimal length, and §5 (paragraph *The list size \(N+1\)*) explicitly leaves even \(q\ge4,\ s\ge3\). Lemma 2.5 is the general length lower bound. We use and reproduce their quotient determinant \(B\), its recovery implication and its Fourier factorization from §§3.1–3.2, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3. Their §3.3 falling-factorial method (Lemmas 3.5–3.6) is also the starting point. Our even-field step strengthens the valuation inequality, handles the nonunit \(N+1\) in Proposition 3.4, and records the direct normalized congruence. Theorem 1.1 in that source gives a different threshold using all nonzero vectors, including in characteristic two, and is not the result claimed here. The separate §5 algorithmic question is not answered here.
- **Binary and neighboring work.** The binary equality is recalled with sources in [KRS, Eq. (6)] and [EZ, Corollary 1.2(a) with \(m=2\)]. [Hollmann–Khathuria–Riet–Skachek, *On Some Batch Code Properties of the Simplex Code*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-022-01173-6) is the binary simplex precedent cited by both. [Oksner–Hollmann–Riet–Skachek, arXiv:2601.12302v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12302v2) treats neighboring small-recovery questions; [Zabokritskiy, arXiv:2611.32557v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32557v1) concerns prescribed differences in the additive binary group and is cited in [EZ, §4], not an even nonbinary projective \(N+1\) result.
- **Bounded check for overlap.** On 2026-11-28 we checked the two papers above and searched for their arXiv identifiers alongside “functional batch”, “Open Problem 3.13”, “two servers per line”, and “even q”, and for the projectivized \(N+1\) problem independently. We found no public statement of the same even-field extension in the material checked. This is not an absolute priority claim. No numerical result in the checkers is used to prove the theorem.
