# Primary sources and precise comparison

* The classical factorization of X^m-1 and the size bound for cyclotomic values are explicitly in S. P. Glasby, Frank Lübeck, Alice C. Niemeyer and Cheryl E. Praeger, [Primitive prime divisors and the n-th cyclotomic polynomial](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788715000269), J. Aust. Math. Soc., §2 Lemma 5(a),(f). They also credit Hering's Theorem 3.6 for sharper estimates. The self-contained quarter bound in this note is not an originality claim. Their algorithm concerns small values of the primitive-divisor part of Phi_m(x), not the unknown product of prime evaluations.
* R. Granger and A. Moss, [Generalised Mersenne Numbers Revisited](https://eprint.iacr.org/2011/444), §2 Definition 2, use the same notion of a cyclotomic prime and explicitly remove the linear cases m=1,2; see their known-parameter arithmetic setting. The novelty is not the definition or this restriction; our corollary concerns an integer product of prime values at clustered unknown arguments, with no indices supplied.
* R. P. Brent, P. L. Montgomery and H. J. J. te Riele, [Factorizations of Cunningham numbers with bases 13 to 99](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3169), §1; and R. P. Brent, [On computing factors of cyclotomic polynomials](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstreams/a7afcaa5-e489-4c1a-a502-2ec46b89ce29/download), §§1, 4.4. Cunningham factorizations start from binomial expressions with known base and exponent; cyclotomic or Aurifeuillian identities give arithmetic factors but need not give primes. Our input supplies none of those parameters and instead promises that the unidentified prime factors are exact prime evaluations at hidden arguments in a short cluster. No claim is made to factor arbitrary factors of composite cyclotomic values.
* E. Bach and J. Shallit, [Factoring with cyclotomic polynomials](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1), Math. Comp. 52 (1989), 201-219, begin with a multiple of a cyclotomic value at the unknown prime in their structural approach. G. Donadze and A. Vasiu, [On the Factorizations of Integers via Division Algorithms for Polynomials](https://doi.org/10.3390/math14132378), Mathematics 14 (2026), 2378, §6 Definition 3 and Proposition 9, study varying gcds of polynomials over prime components of the input modulus. Neither is the prime-value promise at a clustered integer argument in this note. The use of the word cyclotomic alone does not identify the hypotheses.
* The elementary degree-growth bound is only the ordinary triangle inequality at the complex roots; likewise exact integer-root search and candidate division are standard. After aggregating root counts across a fixed number of public disks, the combination gives a polynomially bounded argument set even if the number, degrees and multiplicities of the prime values are not bounded by constants. Related in spirit are the [repository's centered-digits method](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/2376fba2686cd79fa3de230638f3477d660456a5/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-near-affine-centered-digits/README.md) for fixed distinct prime counts and near-affine relations among the prime values themselves; that uses a different promise and extracts signed coefficients. Here no coefficient recovery and no near-affine factor relation are asserted. The prior search is bounded, not a claim of global priority.
* Deterministic primality: M. Agrawal, N. Kayal and N. Saxena, [PRIMES is in P](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781), Annals of Mathematics 160 (2004), 781-793. Fixed-dimensional exact real decision for the optional public root check: J. Renegar, [On the computational complexity and geometry of the first-order theory of the reals, part I](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/305759aa-f544-4006-aa85-f70ddce54e00/content), J. Symbolic Comput. 13 (1992), 255-299.
