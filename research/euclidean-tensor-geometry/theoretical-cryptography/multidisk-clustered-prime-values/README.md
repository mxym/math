# Prime values of polynomials at clustered unknown integer inputs

A publicly enumerated catalogue of polynomial total dense encoding size contains monic integer polynomials, with their complex roots in a fixed number of public disks of polynomial radius. The disk centers can be very far apart. Suppose each prime factor of an integer is itself a value of a catalogue polynomial, and the hidden input integers are in a short but unknown interval in the common right-hand exterior of the disks. Then the integer has a deterministic polynomial-bit-time prime decomposition on this promise. The prime factors need not be comparable: different polynomial degrees create different scales. No bound on the number of distinct prime factors, multiplicities, or degrees independent of the length is needed.

The cyclotomic corollary needs no bound on the indices as extra data. It covers prime **values** of cyclotomic polynomials, not arbitrary prime divisors of possibly composite values. The abstract classical bit-complexity proof is in PROOF.md, with the single-disk small-argument cases treated separately. The two finite checkers take no input and verify mathematical bounds and fixed toy identities, not general-purpose factorization or any deployed construction.
