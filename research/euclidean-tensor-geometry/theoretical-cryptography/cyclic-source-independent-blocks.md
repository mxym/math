# Exact independent blocks in cyclic-source forward noise laws

**Scope.** A known linear transform of known independent finite scalar
weights is studied as a forward probability law. We give an exact
product-block test and a deterministic way to construct the finest
blocks in a negacyclic coefficient model. There is no unknown transform,
no observation-to-parameter algorithm and no claim about a concrete
encryption scheme. The coefficient ring in the specialization is the
one used abstractly by Ring-LWE.

## 1. A maximal-cyclic-character criterion

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group, and let
\[
       Y=y_0+\sum_i L_i U_i
\]
where \(U_i\) are independent variables in cyclic groups of prime-power
order (different primes are allowed), and \(L_i\) are supplied
homomorphisms into \(G\). A fixed proposed output product is a direct
sum \(G=\bigoplus_{j=1}^k G_j\).

The following input class permits both uniform directions and
deterministic directions. For each \(i\), suppose the support
\(\{\chi: \widehat\mu_i(\chi)\ne0\}\) of the characteristic function
of \(U_i\) is a subgroup. Write it as \(K_i^\perp\); equivalently
\(U_i\) is uniform on each used coset of \(K_i\), and its law on the
quotient has a nonvanishing characteristic function. This includes
the case that all character values are nonzero (\(K_i=0\)).
Set
\[
       H=\sum_i L_i(K_i),\qquad \overline G=G/H.
\]
On \(\overline G\) the \(i\)-th variable is now a homomorphic image of
the input quotient \(U_i\bmod K_i\). Let \(C_i\le\overline G\)
be the subgroup generated by differences of points in the support
of that image. It is cyclic of prime-power order, and can be zero.
This definition discards deterministic shifts rather than treating
them as genuine output dependencies.

**Theorem 1.** The projections of \(Y\) onto the \(G_j\) are mutually
independent if and only if both of the following hold:

1. \(H=\bigoplus_j(H\cap G_j)\);
2. every nonzero \(C_i\) lies in one of the induced blocks
   \(G_j/(H\cap G_j)\) of \(\overline G\).

Only the law of the supplied transform is involved. In particular
the test does not require estimating the input weights from data.

### 1.1 Uniform subgroups and the product reduction

For a character \(\xi\) of \(G\), independence of the inputs gives
\[
       |\widehat P_Y(\xi)|=\prod_i
              |\widehat\mu_i(\xi\circ L_i)|.
\]
The support is therefore exactly \(H^\perp\). Fourier inversion
identifies \(P_Y\) as uniform within each used coset of \(H\).
Equivalently one can first draw the independent input quotient
variables and then independently add the uniform sum of the
\(L_i(K_i)\), which is uniform on \(H\).

If the output were a product across the \(G_j\), its characteristic
support would be the product of the marginal supports. For a
subgroup \(H^\perp\) this happens only if \(H^\perp\) is a direct
product in the dual coordinates, which by annihilators is
precisely condition 1. Under condition 1, \(P_Y\) is a product
if and only if its quotient law on \(\overline G\) is a product
over the induced blocks; uniform lifting on the split \(H\)
commutes with taking products. Each projected input quotient
law still has nonvanishing characteristic function.
After translating a support point to zero, the \(i\)-th
projected law can be taken as a law on \(C_i\) with support
differences generating \(C_i\). Translation does not affect
independence. Condition 2 suffices by grouping disjoint sets
of input variables. Necessity follows next.

### 1.2 A generator of a maximal subgroup survives

For this step write \(T=\overline G\) and let \(F(\xi)\) denote
the logarithm of the absolute characteristic function of the quotient
output on \(\widehat T\). It is real, even, finite everywhere, and
a sum of terms of the form
\(\log|\varphi_i(\xi|_{C_i})|\).
The finite Fourier transform of each term on \(\widehat T\) is
supported in \(C_i\subset T\), by orthogonality on the annihilator.

Take \(C\ne0\) maximal by inclusion among the \(C_i\).
Sum all terms with \(C_i=C\) as a function \(F_C\) on \(\widehat C\).
Each summand is nonpositive and zero at the trivial character.
For every nontrivial character of \(C\) its modulus is strictly
less than one: equality would put all support differences in the
kernel of that character, which is impossible because they
generate \(C\). Consequently \(F_C(\chi)<0\) at every nontrivial
character.

For a cyclic \(p\)-group the set of nongenerators is the unique
maximal proper subgroup \(pC\). The sum of the Fourier
coefficients over its generators, with the averaged Fourier
normalization, is
\[
 \begin{split}
    \sum_{t\in C\setminus pC}\widehat F_C(t)
    &= F_C(1)-\frac1p\sum_{\chi\in(pC)^\perp}F_C(\chi)\\
    &>0 .                                                     \tag{1}
 \end{split}
\]
The averaging identity follows by summing characters over
\((pC)^\perp\), which has order \(p\).
In particular at least one generator has a nonzero coefficient.
No other input subgroup except the equal ones can contribute at
that generator: any subgroup containing it contains \(C\),
contradicting inclusion-maximality unless equal. Hence it
survives in \(F\).

A product quotient law has \(\log|\widehat P|\) equal to a sum
of block functions, so all of its nonzero Fourier positions in
\(T\) lie within the union of the individual output blocks.
Therefore a surviving generator forces its whole cyclic group
\(C\) into one block. Every nonzero \(C_i\) is contained in a
maximal member of the finite family, giving condition 2 and
proving Theorem 1. The prime-power hypothesis is used to obtain
generators in (1), rather than just to obtain a spanning support.

### 1.3 Exact data and a quantitative consequence

For a rational table on \(\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\), Fourier
vanishing at one primitive character of order \(p^a\) means
vanishing at every character of that order, by Galois
conjugacy. It is tested by divisibility of the coefficient
polynomial by \(\Phi_{p^a}\), after clearing denominators.
The nonzero support is a subgroup exactly when the surviving
orders are an initial segment of \(1,p,\ldots,p^e\).
This makes the input class itself decidable with ordinary
exact arithmetic on the supplied tables. All other tests in
Theorem 1 use spans, quotients, and cyclic subgroups. For
explicit coefficient data over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\) these reduce
to polynomial-bit integer or field linear algebra.

In particular, every rational probability table on a prime field
meets the support hypothesis. A vanishing nontrivial coefficient
would force divisibility by \(1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}\); since the
polynomial has degree at most \(p-1\), the table is then uniform.
A nonuniform table thus has no Fourier zeros.

For a specified output decomposition in the quotient, a maximal
\(C\) meeting more than one block gives an explicit gap against
every product law \(Q\). Put
\[
 \gamma=\prod_i\min_{\chi\in\widehat C_i}|\varphi_i(\chi)|>0,
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{-\sum_{\chi\in(pC)^\perp}F_C(\chi)}
                  {p\,|C\setminus pC|}>0 .
\]
Nontrivial deterministic factors, if any, only affect phases
and can be left out. From (1), at least one mixed Fourier
coefficient of \(F\) is at least \(\kappa\); all are real
since \(F\) is even. If the TV distance to \(Q\) is
\(t<\gamma/2\), every characteristic value of \(Q\)
is nonzero and
\[
     \|\log|\widehat P_Y|-\log|\widehat Q|\|_\infty
       \le -\log(1-2t/\gamma)
\]
on the quotient, by \(|\widehat P_Y-\widehat Q|\le 2t\).
The mixed coefficient of the product log is zero.
It follows that \(t\ge(\gamma/2)(1-e^{-\kappa})\),
and the same bound is automatic for \(t\ge\gamma/2\).
The constants require the known law, are not claimed strong
when many inputs make the output close to uniform, and the
test remains exact without using them. If \(H\) is split, the
same lower bound also holds before quotienting by contraction
of TV.

It would be wrong to apply the criterion when the characteristic
support is not a subgroup. For example for \(p=5\) the positive
real probabilities \(p^{-1}(1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi a x/p))\)
with \(a=1,2\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1\) have complementary
nontrivial Fourier supports, so their convolution is exactly
uniform even though neither input is. Two copies of this
construction along two independent directions (one mixed across
the coordinate blocks) give an independent output despite
nonuniform mixed input directions. These tables need not be
rational. The prime-power restriction on each input cyclic group
is essential as well: a single cyclic \(pq\) input with independent
nonuniform CRT coordinates can split across the \(p\)- and
\(q\)-blocks without its cyclic full difference subgroup lying
in either one.

## 2. A finest negacyclic product decomposition and its frequency

Take \(A=\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z\), \(R=A[X]/(X^n+1)\) for
dyadic \(n\) and odd \(p\). Suppose the supplied transform is
\[
              Y=\sum_{i=0}^\ell f_i U_i\in R ,
\]
with \(f_0\) a unit. The scalar power-basis coordinates of
all \(U_i\) are independent. Assume their scalar laws have
nonvanishing characteristic functions and support differences
generating \(A\), as happens, for example, with a full support
table and no Fourier zeros. Their weights can otherwise differ
arbitrarily with the coefficient location and layer. The
assumptions in this section rule out a uniform subgroup and
remove any risk of erasing a column direction without notice.

Normalize the output by the known linear map \(f_0^{-1}\).
The zeroth layer now contributes every standard basis line
\(Ae_j\). Put \(g_i=f_i/f_0\), and denote its nonzero
coefficient support by \(S_i\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\).
All shifts of \(S_i\) arise as the supports of individual
columns of multiplication by \(g_i\), since wrapping only changes
a sign. Define
\[
        d=\gcd\bigl(n,\{s-t:s,t\in S_i,\ 1\le i\le\ell\}\bigr)
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
using \(d=n\) for an empty set of nonzero differences.

**Theorem 2.** The finest independent direct-sum decomposition
into additive subgroups has exactly \(d\) indecomposable blocks,
given, in the normalized coordinates, by the residue classes
of coefficient positions modulo \(d\). In particular a
polynomial-bit algorithm recognizes and constructs them from the
supplied multipliers; if each block dimension is \(O(\log n)\)
and \(p^e\) is fixed, the entire factored forward law, or the
mass at a supplied output, can be computed in polynomial time.

For necessity, in any independent decomposition the zeroth layer
forces every standard basis line \(Ae_j\) wholly into some
output block by Theorem 1. Each block is exactly the coordinate
subgroup spanned by its allocated basis lines: express any
element in that basis and compare its components in the direct
sum. Any other input column forces all nonzero coordinates of
that column into the same block, again by Theorem 1. The
transitive closure of these constraints is the undirected
Cayley graph on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) with generator
differences \(\cup_i(S_i-S_i)\). Its components are the
cosets of the subgroup \(d\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\).
Conversely each column lies inside one such component, so the
component law is a product. This also proves that no finer
decomposition can arise from a different linear basis, not
just that a particular coordinate order has disconnected
factors.

To compute the factored law use any basis order within each
block and convolve the supplied scalar tables along their known
column directions inside the block. A block of dimension \(b\)
has \((p^e)^b\) additive states, and each incremental convolution
with one scalar table uses at most \(p^e\) shifts per state.
Rational bit complexity is controlled by clearing denominators
in the explicit input tables and bounding the size of the
integer mass sums. The product representation is compact;
listing an exponentially large full table is not required.

Finally, if \(f_1,\ldots,f_\ell\) are independently uniform
in this abstract coefficient ring with \(f_0\) fixed unit, the
\(g_i\) are also independent uniform elements. Write \(q=p^e\).
For any divisor \(a\) of \(n\), condition \(a\mid d\) is
exactly that each \(S_i\) is contained in one residue class
modulo \(a\). Such a coefficient vector is either zero, or has
one uniquely determined nonempty class. Therefore
\[
          \Pr[a\mid d]
          =\left(\frac{1+a(q^{n/a}-1)}{q^n}\right)^\ell .
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
For dyadic \(n\) the full distribution follows by subtracting
successive powers-of-two tail probabilities. This is an exact
rarity statement for complete independence splitting in the
specified forward law. It is not a result about the complexity
of unrestricted random noisy searches.

## Prior work and relation to this criterion

* [Mazur, *The Skitovich-Darmois theorem for finite Abelian groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4134)
  concerns independence of several forms with automorphism
  coefficients and characterizes source laws. The theorem here
  assumes known source laws and includes rectangular transforms,
  zero maps, explicit uniform quotients, and output blocks.
* [Yeredor, *ICA over finite fields: Separability and algorithms*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165168411003513)
  and [Painsky, Rosset and Feder, arXiv:1809.05815](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05815)
  treat finite-field source separation, notably identifiability
  with a square invertible mix. We do not estimate an unknown
  mixing matrix or solve their data-recovery problem.
  Log characteristic functions and finite Fourier orthogonality
  are classical. The specialization here uses the exact surviving
  generator in (1), the uniform quotient, and the resulting
  residue-class decomposition/rareness formula over prime powers.
* In [positive-group-weight-factorization.md](./positive-group-weight-factorization.md)
  the logarithm is applied to supplied **pointwise** weights
  of a product. Here the object is a **convolution pushforward**;
  logarithms are taken after the Fourier transform, with exact
  treatment of uniform zeros and cyclic subgroup containment.

The companion finite checks use generated forward tables and
explicit rational or integer arithmetic, not actual cryptographic
instances.
