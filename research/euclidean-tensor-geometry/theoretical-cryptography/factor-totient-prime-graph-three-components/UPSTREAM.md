# Original sources and comparison

* Ford, Konyagin and Luca, [*Prime chains and Pratt trees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0473): the relation p | q-1 and its prime chains and trees are classical. The graph here is restricted to the unknown divisors of one integer; orient its edges toward larger primes. Neither these chains nor their short affine parametrization are claimed as a discovery.
* Erdős, Luca and Pomerance, [*On the proportion of numbers coprime to a given integer*, §2 Lemma 1 and Algorithm A](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/gcdnphin14.pdf): the reduced totient ratio depends only on squarefree support. Their reconstruction procedure uses largest prime factors of reduced denominators. The invariants here start from that classical ratio, rather than claiming an unconditional general polynomial-time computation of the radical.
* Źrałek, [*A deterministic version of Pollard’s p−1 algorithm*, v5 §6, printed p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102v5): the two-distinct-prime case with arbitrary multiplicities is known, as is the three-distinct-prime unequal-exponent case from a single exact numeric totient. Do not count these as new.
* Morain, Renault and Smith, [*Deterministic factoring with oracles*, v2, §§5.1–5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444v2): Landau's unrestricted equal-order decomposition uses several oracle calls; §5.2 gives the elementary gcd split on squarefree input. Neither a call on each new divisor nor factoring the numeric totient is assumed here. Hittmeir and Pomykała, [*Deterministic Integer Factorization with Oracles for Euler's Totient Function*](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2020-1891), work with the prime-power decomposition of the totient, a stronger type of input.
* [Stacks Project, properness of projective morphisms](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01WC): used solely for closedness of the indicated projection. Multihomogenization, the univariate resultant criterion, the isolated-point Bézout bound and deterministic integer primality testing are standard mathematical ingredients.
* J. Renegar, [*On the Computational Complexity and Geometry of the First-order Theory of the Reals, Part I*](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/305759aa-f544-4006-aa85-f70ddce54e00/content): fixed-variable existential real-algebraic feasibility is used in the bit model. The paper is not a factoring result.

The specific claim is the stated deterministic full-factorization promise theorem via finitely many three-invariant fibers. Targeted prior comparisons do not establish absolute priority. In particular no theorem on infinitely many instances is implied: even one edge with the ratio bounded asks for simultaneous primality of p and ap+1 for one of finitely many slopes. No density, cryptographic insecurity, unrestricted one-totient factoring, or ordinary factoring assertion follows.
