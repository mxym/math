# Prior work and scope

The ingredients of the argument have precedents. The claimed scope is the combination of an explicitly finite rational degree potential and a complete deterministic recognition theorem for a hidden connected support, not an original height technique or a priority claim.

- Patrick Ingram, [*Critical dynamics of variable-separated affine correspondences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6954), §3, Lemma 3.3 and proof, defines a local escape rate for paths of an equation g(y)=f(x) by normalizing logarithms using the degree quotient, and proves local growth estimates. The same author's [*Canonical heights for correspondences*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.1041v2), Theorem 1.1, proves the canonical-height scaling statement for polarized correspondences. Thus neither logarithmic comparisons for polynomial equalities nor degree-ratio normalization along paths is new. Here all edges can have different quotients in a fixed cyclic subgroup, they can be traversed in either direction, and the bounded local drift forces integer consistency on closed walks in the large-prime range. The extra step is an enumerable rational exponent for an unknown prime product, not a canonical height.
- Yuri F. Bilu and Robert F. Tichy, [*The Diophantine equation f(x)=g(y)*](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-95-3-261-288), Acta Arithmetica 95(3) (2000), 261–288, [§1 and Theorem 1.1](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa95/aa9534.pdf), study when a fixed separated-variable equation has infinitely many rational solutions of bounded denominator, in particular the finiteness question for integer solutions. We make no abundance or classification claim about prime solutions. The relation catalogue is public and the relevant coordinates are hidden by the product.
- Manindra Agrawal, Nitin Saxena and Shubham Sahai Srivastava, [*Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), MFCS 2016, pp. 6:1–6:14. The discussion around p. 6:3 already notes that for a known two-factor dependency one can substitute the known product divided by the first variable and solve a univariate equation, when it is nonzero. Theorem 1.3 also treats a supplied suitable dependency involving a prime and its cofactor. We do not claim novelty for either elimination or using a known dependency. There is no supplied relation between a designated pair or between a factor and the cofactor in our model, and the proof does not solve a polynomial system with one variable per unknown factor.
- Jonathon Hales and Ghaith Hiary, [*A generalization of Lehman's method*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-00959-7), Ramanujan J. 65 (2024), 1773–1790, treat a particular prime-power divisor problem using an extension of Fermat/Lehman. Their monomial growth and close-center arguments are useful comparison, not a hidden multi-relation connected-support theorem. Markus Hittmeir, [*Deterministic factorization of sums and differences of powers*](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3197), Math. Comp. 86 (2017), 2947–2954, gives a different result when the whole input integer is expressed as a sum or difference of two powers. Do not identify those forms for the whole input with relations among hidden factors. Don Coppersmith, [*Finding a Small Root of a Bivariate Integer Equation; Factoring with High Bits Known*](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-68339-9_16), EUROCRYPT 1996, studies conditional factor-position information; no supplied high bits or small-root machinery are used here.
- The elementary root-radius and monotonicity estimates are self-contained. Deterministic primality is available by Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena, [*PRIMES is in P*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781), with its [erratum](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v189-n1-p06-s.pdf). No algorithm for general integer factorization is assumed.
- Repository comparison at the pinned snapshot `bc555d0be5d59f88ec2f172a73c77745056ee5a8`:
  - [`factor-bare-n-separated-polynomial-blocks`](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/bc555d0be5d59f88ec2f172a73c77745056ee5a8/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-separated-polynomial-blocks/PROOF.md), §§1–2, already has the same separated equal-value graph and recognizes the connected case with a fixed maximum K on the support size by enumerating trees and multiplicities. Its §3 already uses degree-ratio and leading-coefficient normalization for two close roots at fixed K. That theorem allows much more general degrees and coefficients; it does not establish the unbounded-support statement proved here, and here we make no two-component claim.
  - [`factor-bare-n-unbounded-low-rank-slopes`](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/bc555d0be5d59f88ec2f172a73c77745056ee5a8/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-unbounded-low-rank-slopes/PROOF.md), §§1–3, already avoids fixing the support size, guessing multiplicities or enumerating trees. It bounds a word ball for a low-rank slope dictionary in the ordinary prime coordinate and forms an aggregate multiplicative scale near a power of the least prime.
  - [`factor-bare-n-canonical-sparse-aggregate`](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/bc555d0be5d59f88ec2f172a73c77745056ee5a8/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-canonical-sparse-aggregate/PROOF.md), §§1, 3–4, relaxes the global slope rank condition in that same coordinate, instead requiring a hidden canonical sparse aggregate whose span is tested after exact closure.
  - The scope here is the nonlinear unbounded-support intersection: the leading comparison acts on log(prime), and the small-radius estimate and fixed cyclic degree group bound the denominator of an aggregate rational **degree sum**. This is not an unrestricted rank claim: the degree quotient group itself is globally rank one. No affine coefficient sparse certificate is required. These results are in different catalogue models and we do not claim strict containment of their instance sets. The earlier bimonic short-cycle and parallel-path notes also use neighbour closure, but in combination with a finite algebraic anchor certificate; no such anchor is supplied or required here.

A bounded search of these sources and related Cunningham/prime-chain and structured-integer work did not identify the precise connected product theorem. This does not assert a literature-wide priority. The result is deliberately limited to a mathematical promise class, with no general-input or real-system inference.
