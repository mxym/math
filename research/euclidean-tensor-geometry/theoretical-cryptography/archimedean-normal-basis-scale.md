# Archimedean orthogonal normal bases and their exact integral scale

A modular normal basis and an orthogonal normal basis for the canonical Euclidean geometry are different objects. [Grover–Ling–Vehkalahti, *Non-Commutative Ring Learning With Errors From Cyclic Algebras*, Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.01834) discusses cyclic bases and spherical errors when passing from coefficient sampling to canonical embedding blocks. The mathematical question isolated here is what is required for an **unwrapped** isotropic Gaussian image under such a basis map. Modular splitting alone does not imply orthogonality of characteristic-zero lifts. This does not address a claim about noise after wrapping, and does not infer an attack, a flaw in a concrete scheme, or a change to the hardness theorem.

We use the ordinary canonical inner product, counting both complex conjugate embeddings. For relative extensions with a totally real or CM top field, let bar mean complex conjugation (the identity on a totally real field) and set b(x,y)=Tr_{L/K}(x bar(y)). Orthogonality here means that the relative normal basis {gα:g∈G} is orthogonal for b, i.e. in each corresponding characteristic-zero embedding block. This is the covariance condition before any modular operation.

## Results

1. For abelian L/K with K totally real, a rational-over-K orthogonal normal basis exists in the totally real case exactly when [L:K] is odd. In the CM case one exists exactly when [L:K]=2m with m odd and K(i)⊆L.
2. For abelian CM-over-CM L/K whose 2-Sylow subgroup has exponent at most two, write the real 2-primary part of L⁺/K⁺ as K⁺(sqrt(d_1),…,sqrt(d_r)). A relative orthogonal normal basis exists exactly when each d_j lies in N_{K/K⁺}(K^×). This includes all quadratic CM-over-CM extensions, and the test is independent of a choice of independent radicands.
3. Suppose L/Q is abelian of odd degree n. Write its inertia order at each ramified odd rational prime p as e_p=t_p p^{a_p}, with t_p prime to p. Among all algebraic integers α whose rational normal orbit is orthogonal in the canonical geometry, the exact least column length is

       T(L) = product_{p ramified} p^(a_p + 1_{t_p>1}).

   In particular, the minimum index of the resulting orthogonal orbit lattice in the full integer lattice is T(L)^n/sqrt(|disc L|). When the absolute abelian field has even degree and admits such a basis, L=M(i) with M/Q abelian of odd degree, and the exact corresponding least length is 2T(M). For instance, for the unique degree p^a subfield of Q(ζ_{p^{a+1}}), the least length is p^a, including deep wild levels not covered by the classical weakly ramified result.

The odd-degree rational existence theorem originates with [Bayer–Fluckiger and Lenstra, §5](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1990e/art.pdf). For odd abelian extensions of Q, [Erez–Morales, Theorem 4.1](https://jorgem.codeberg.page/assets/files/Erez-Morales1992_Hermitian_Structure.pdf), also summarized in [Erez’s survey](https://www.numdam.org/article/AST_1991__198-199-200__133_0.pdf), characterize when the square root of the inverse different has a self-dual integral normal basis. The ideal-basis statement concerns weak ramification. Here the integral vectors span a possibly proper Z[G]-sublattice of O_L, rather than that fractional ideal; the new quantity is its optimal scale under arbitrary ramification.

## Existence and the Hermitian obstruction

Put n=[L:K] and t=Tr_{L/K}(α). Since conjugation commutes with all the automorphisms under discussion, an orthogonal normal orbit has a common diagonal entry. Taking the squared norm of sum_g gα=t proves that the diagonal is t bar(t). In particular t is nonzero and one can normalize the orbit by multiplying α by 1/t to make the diagonal one.

For any nontrivial quadratic character χ:G→{±1}, its resolvent v=sum_g χ(g)gα, using the normalized α, belongs to the χ-eigenspace of L over K. The diagonal-one identity gives b(v,v)=n. Since v bar(v) is fixed by G, this is equivalent to v bar(v)=1.

If K is totally real, bar belongs to G in the CM case and is the identity in the real case. Thus v²=χ(bar). When χ(bar)=+1, v would be ±1 and have trivial character. Hence every nontrivial quadratic character must take bar to -1. A real abelian extension therefore has odd order. A CM extension can have only one independent quadratic character (otherwise two multiply to one that is +1 at bar). Its abelian 2-Sylow is cyclic, and cannot have order divisible by 4: then every involution would lie in the kernel of the unique quadratic character. Therefore the 2-Sylow has order two and its quadratic field is K(i), by v²=-1. Conversely, conjugation on K(i)/K has an orthonormal orbit generated by (1+i)/2 for this relative trace metric. In the CM case tensor it with a real trace-self-dual normal basis of the odd part furnished by Bayer–Fluckiger–Lenstra. In the real case use that theorem alone. Trace pairings factor on products of linearly disjoint Galois fields.

For K,L CM, L=L⁺K and L⁺∩K=K⁺. For a quadratic character of the real 2-primary part, the χ-eigenspace has the form K sqrt(d), with sqrt(d)∈L⁺. Now v bar(v)=1 holds for such a v if and only if d is a norm from K to K⁺. This proves necessity. For sufficiency choose c_j∈K with c_j bar(c_j)=d_j and u_j=sqrt(d_j)/c_j, so u_j bar(u_j)=1 and each nonempty product of distinct u_j has a nontrivial character. Their products u_S (including 1) are pairwise trace-orthogonal with common squared length 2^r in the 2-primary extension. Expanding 2^(-r) product_j(1+u_j), the conjugates are the corresponding Walsh sign combinations. Orthogonality of those combinations makes the conjugates orthonormal. Tensor again with the odd real piece. This argument does not classify CM-over-CM when the 2-Sylow has an element of order at least four.

For example K=Q(i), L=Q(i,sqrt(3)) has no such relative basis: valuation at the inert Gaussian prime 3 of a norm to Q is even, and the valuation of 3 is odd. Yet 3 is a square in F_13 and this quadratic extension has split fibers over primes of K above 13, each with a modular idempotent normal basis. In contrast Q(i,sqrt(2))/Q(i) has the required norm since 2=(1+i)(1-i). The first example is an obstruction to an inferred real orthogonality, not a statement about a modular Gaussian or any deployment.

## Exact minimum scale over Q

Let L/Q have odd degree n, so L is totally real. An orthogonal orbit of an integral generator has length |t| by the trace identity. Its covolume is |t|^n, giving the claimed index expression. In particular, for any p ramified in the field of the orbit, v_p(t)≥1.

We will also use trace descent. If L/M is a Galois subextension of relative degree h, then α_M=Tr_{L/M}(α) has its conjugates given by disjoint sums of h columns of the original orbit. Their norms squared in L multiply by h and relative to the canonical geometry of M divide by h. Thus the descended orbit again has the same column length and trace to Q. The same argument works for CM/real descent with the canonical inner product.

### Lower bound for every ramified prime

Consider first a single p-primary cyclotomic component E/Q of odd ramification degree e=t_p p^a, with all ramification at p. It is totally ramified at p. For a≥1 its local different exponent d, divided by e, is

       d/e = a+1 - (sum_{j=0}^{a-1} p^j + 1/t_p)/p^a.

Indeed, the conductor-discriminant formula sums the primitive conductor exponent of each Dirichlet character. There are t_p-1 nontrivial tame characters of conductor exponent 1, and t_p φ(p^j) characters of exact wild order p^j of conductor exponent j+1 for 1≤j≤a. Expanding that sum yields the formula. Unramified local base change leaves d and e unchanged.

In a general odd abelian L/Q the same formula holds for local inertia at p. To see this concretely, write a containing cyclotomic field according to the coprime prime-power factors of its conductor. In its Galois group, the p-coordinate is precisely its inertia at p. Taking the quotient of each coordinate by the kernel of its restriction to L gives a single-prime field E_p with degree e_p. The compositum U of all E_p contains L; the Galois-group restriction Gal(U/Q)→Gal(L/Q) is injective on every inertia subgroup and consequently U/L is unramified. At p, U is obtained locally from E_p by an unramified extension, giving the formula for L also.

For a_p=a≥1, let M be the fixed field in L of the p-Sylow subgroup of the inertia at p. At a prime over p, L/M is totally ramified of degree p^a and M is tame at p with inertia t_p (possibly 1). The tower formula for the different gives

       d(L/M)/p^a = t_p a + 1 - (t_p sum_{j=0}^{a-1} p^j + 1)/p^a.

The last fraction is strictly between zero and one: t_p is an odd divisor of p-1, hence t_p≤(p-1)/2. Consequently floor(d(L/M)/p^a)=t_p a. For a finite local extension of ramification degree e_rel and different exponent d_rel, the trace ideal of the integer ring is the base prime to power floor(d_rel/e_rel). Indeed containment of all traces in that prime to power b is equivalent, by the definition of the trace dual and stability under multiplication by integers of the top field, to the base prime to power -b lying in the inverse different after extension. This gives exactly e_rel b≤d_rel. Here the trace ideal at p is therefore p^a times the integer ring of M at p, since p has valuation t_p there. It follows that

       α' = Tr_{L/M}(α)/p^a

is integral globally: at every prime above p use the trace ideal, and away from p division by p is harmless. By trace descent its own orbit has column length |t|/p^a. In particular the trace of α' is an integer, so v_p(t)≥a. If t_p>1 then p still ramifies in M, and the lattice index formula forces p to divide the trace of α' as well. This proves the exponent a+1 in that case. If a=0 and t_p>1, invoke the index formula directly on L. Multiplying all the necessary constraints proves the lower bound.

### Attainment: treat tame and wild parts separately

For a single-prime tame field E⊂Q(ζ_p) of odd degree d_0>1, let η=Tr_{Q(ζ_p)/E}(ζ_p), and write g_x for the automorphism induced by x∈F_p^×. Define the integral element

       α = sum_{x∈F_p except 0,1} g_{x(1-x)}η + 2(p-1)/d_0.

For a character χ of Gal(E/Q) its resolvent r_χ(β)=sum_g χ(g)gβ is the corresponding Fourier coefficient of the embedding orbit. The trivial resolvent of η is -1, so the displayed element has trace p. Every nontrivial character has odd order, and the resolvent of η is a primitive degree-one conductor Gauss sum of magnitude sqrt(p). The displayed integral group-ring multiplier has character value (up to replacing χ by its inverse) J(χ,χ), a Jacobi sum of magnitude sqrt(p), because τ(χ)^2=J(χ,χ)τ(χ²) and χ² is nontrivial. Thus all resolvents of α have magnitude p. The circulant Gram identity, or diagonalization by the character transform, gives column length p and orthogonality.

For the pure wild degree p^a field E_a, let E_j denote its degree p^j subfield and η_j=Tr_{Q(ζ_{p^{j+1}})/E_j}(ζ_{p^{j+1}}), for 1≤j≤a. Let σ generate Gal(E_a/Q) and use

       α = 1 + sum_j A_j,
       A_j = p^((j-1)/2) η_j                                for j odd,
       A_j = p^((j-2)/2) sum_{u=1}^{p-1} (u|p) σ^(u p^(j-1))η_j  for j even.

Here (u|p) is the quadratic character. This is integral. The resolvent of η_j vanishes for characters of order greater than p^j (it is fixed by the stabilizer of E_j), and for characters of smaller order (the trace down to E_{j-1} is zero by summing all p lifts of a primitive root exponent). For order exactly p^j it is p^(a-j) times the primitive Gauss sum of conductor p^{j+1}, of magnitude p^((j+1)/2). At even j the quadratic multiplier has magnitude sqrt(p). Each nontrivial character now has one contribution of magnitude p^a, and the trivial character receives only the constant trace p^a. So every orbit column has length p^a and they are orthogonal. The elementary primitive Gauss magnitude used here follows from additive Fourier orthogonality: its Fourier transform vanishes at nonunits by primitivity, and at units has constant magnitude, hence Parseval gives the squared magnitude equal to the conductor.

For a mixed inertia e=t_p p^a, multiply the generators for its two linearly disjoint tame and wild subfields. Field trace factors on their product, so this preserves orbit orthogonality and multiplies the lengths, giving p^(a+1) when both parts are present. Finally multiply across the pairwise linearly disjoint single-prime fields E_p in U and take the trace down to L. This gives an integral orthogonal normal orbit of length T(L). In the allowed even case M(i), multiplication of the minimizing generator from M by 1+i contributes the factor two. Conversely trace descent to M forces the odd-prime exponents, and the discriminant at 2 forces the factor two: M is unramified at 2 (the cyclotomic 2-primary groups have 2-power order), so the absolute discriminant of M(i) has 2-valuation 2[M:Q].

These are exact optimal scale and index statements, not constructions of cryptographic sample distributions. The adjacent arithmetic checker verifies the Gram and ramification identities on generated small cyclotomic examples (2,396 exact checks).
