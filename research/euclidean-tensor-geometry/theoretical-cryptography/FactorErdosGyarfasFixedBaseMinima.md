# Exact minimum orders for two regular abelian voltage-cover classes

For a graph having a simple cycle of length a power of two, its **dyadic minimum** is the least such length. Consider connected regular abelian covers of a fixed, labeled, finite simple base. The number of sheets is the order of the deck group.

**Theorem.** Among these covers of \(K_{3,3}\) with dyadic minimum \(32\), the minimum order is **798**, i.e. 133 sheets. At 133 sheets there are 56 covering maps up to isomorphism over the fixed labeled base. Allowing base automorphisms gives three map classes, of sizes \(24,24,8\), representing exactly three nonisomorphic lift graphs. For the base obtained by subdividing one edge of \(K_4\) once, the corresponding minimum order is **95**, i.e. 19 sheets. At 19 sheets four labeled-base maps qualify; they give one class allowing base automorphisms, hence one lift graph.

This extends the known sharp witnesses by proving their minimality within the specified fixed-base classes. The 798-vertex cyclic witness was already recorded in project work following the [six-orbit construction](FactorErdosGyarfasBipartiteSixOrbits.md), and the [rooted abelian \(K_{3,3}\) theorem](FactorErdosGyarfasK33Rooted.md) ensures a \(4/8/16/32\)-cycle through each vertex. Cinna Davis previously gave the [95-vertex witness and a universal \(4/8/16/32\) theorem for the subdivided tetrahedron base](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/erdos-gyarfas-abelian-covers/PROOF.md), including the signed chord-count and simplicity framework. The minimum over all smaller finite abelian deck groups and the equality classifications are the additions here. The subdivided base has a degree-two vertex. This result neither classifies nonabelian covers nor proves the unrestricted Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture.

The original finite census and fast bit-set proof replay were developed by the research Dreamer. An independent replay assembles voltage words by joining two half paths, has a different congruence solver, checks the 133-sheet case again by a Chinese-remainder chart, and uses literal lift graphs for the second base. The proof below describes the mathematical conditions implemented, including the reductions that must be valid for an exhaustive exclusion.

## 1. Covering maps and integer sublattices

Fix an orientation on each edge not in a spanning tree, and number these \(r\) cotree edges. Choose sheet origins along the tree; then all tree voltages are zero. A connected cover with specified deck group \(A\) is given by the \(r\) oriented cotree voltages generating \(A\), with the inverse voltage for the reversed edge. Indeed a loop's accumulated labels are generated by the cotree labels, each realized by a fundamental cycle. The assignment defines a surjection \(\mathbb Z^r\to A\) and hence a finite-index kernel \(K\). Conversely every such \(K\) gives a connected cover using \(A=\mathbb Z^r/K\). Relabeling \(A\) or changing the common residual origin leaves \(K\) unchanged. A covering-map isomorphism over the fixed base conjugates the two deck actions, and after a common translation it induces exactly such a deck-group relabeling on the normalized cotree voltages. Thus distinct kernels give distinct labeled-base maps.

Use the upper-triangular **column** Hermite form \(H\) for \(K\). Its diagonal entries \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\) are positive with product \(n=[\mathbb Z^r:K]\), and its entry in row \(i\) above the diagonal is independently in \(0,\ldots,a_i-1\). Completeness and uniqueness follow by projecting to the last coordinate (image \(a_r\mathbb Z\)), applying induction to the kernel of the projection, and reducing the earlier coordinates of a lift of \(a_r\) to the unique mixed-radix representative. This also gives an elementary check of the count at each index:
\[
                 \sum_{a_1\cdots a_r=n} a_1^{r-1}a_2^{r-2}\cdots a_{r-1}.
\]
Back substitution from the last row to the first tests membership: for each pivot require the current coordinate divisible by its positive diagonal, and subtract the corresponding multiple of that column. Alternatively Euclidean remainders give the unique quotient representative. This procedure enumerates all finite abelian groups that can act in the designated cover, not just cyclic groups or decks of particular prime-power exponent.

## 2. \(K_{3,3}\): exact cycle conditions and grouped scan

Take parts \(0,1,2\) and \(3,4,5\), tree all edges incident to 0 or 3, and cotree edges \(14,15,24,25\) directed left to right. For a closed base walk \(W\), let \(t\in\mathbb Z^4\) be its total signed cotree vector. For each pair of different positions in the same base fiber before the final return, record the difference \(b\) of their cotree prefixes. A lift from a specified sheet closes iff \(t\in K\); subject to this, it is simple iff every such \(b\notin K\). A simple lifted cycle projects to a cyclically nonbacktracking word, since immediately reversing a matching returns to the same vertex. Starting on the left is harmless. DFS over these words or joining compatible nonbacktracking half paths supplies all candidates.

For length 4, closure of a cyclically nonbacktracking lift already implies simplicity: a repeated vertex would split it into a closed length-two walk and its complement. For length 8, in the absence of length 4, this also holds. Any collision would split the walk into two even closed segments of lengths at least four; each would be a simple four-cycle, since a nested collision would produce an immediate reversal in one of the segments. Thus once all closing vectors at lengths 4 and 8 are forbidden in \(K\), any further simple 16-cycle is witnessed by a clause
\[
                      t\in K,\qquad b\notin K\quad (b\in B).
\]
Changing a blocker \(b\) by sign or by any integer multiple of \(t\) preserves the test in a closing candidate. A blocker congruent to 0 modulo \(\mathbb Zt\) makes the clause impossible; a blocker congruent to a forbidden short vector up to sign is automatic. For one target \(t\), only minimal sets of blockers need be kept. The fast replay uses the full base path traversal and small multiples of \(t\) for reduction; it checks **501 empty-blocker and 126 single-blocker** 16-clauses and asserts no clause has two or more blockers before taking that shortcut. The independent program pairs compatible half paths and reduces exactly modulo \(\mathbb Zt\) using a nonzero pivot coordinate, resulting in **501 empty-blocker and 84 single-blocker** clauses. Both representations are equivalent to the cycle condition where the short closing vectors are forbidden.

Write the HNF entries as
\[
       H=\begin{pmatrix}
           a&u&v&w\\
           0&b&x&y\\
           0&0&c&z\\
           0&0&0&d
         \end{pmatrix}.
\]
After fixing the diagonal and \(x,y,z\), bottom-row substitution, including exact integrality, either makes membership of a specified vector \(t\) impossible, or reduces it to
\[
                  wq_3\equiv t_0-uq_1-vq_2\pmod a.
\]
For each \(u,v\), mark all \(w\) satisfying each unconditional short-cycle or empty-blocker predicate. In a conditional clause mark the target's mask less the union of the blocker masks. A missing bottom-row membership gives an empty mask. The JavaScript replay gathers masks by a finite residue table; the independent replay constructs them using the gcd and a reduced modular inverse. These masks check **every** \(w\) in \(0,\ldots,a-1\), including repeated residues when \(q_3\) is not a unit and the case \(q_3=0\). No floating-point optimization or heuristic graph search is used; all JavaScript integer intermediates for the designated range fit exactly in integer-valued Numbers or 32-bit bitwise words as appropriate.

The per-index count agrees with the divisor formula. The original census split 1–102 (word certificates with exact path fallback) from 103–133 (the grouped static calculation). For this audit the guarded grouped calculation was replayed on **every index 1–133**. The version with an extra per-index count assertion was then replayed in two complete ranges, **1–102** and **103–133**, covering 59,193,424 and 110,046,865 kernels. The separate Python HNF implementation was also checked on the lower prefix 1–63, the start of the upper range 103–113, and the delicate final indices **132–133**. It is not claimed to have been run independently over every index. The entire equality-index census was independently rebuilt by the CRT chart in §3. The aggregate output is
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r}
\text{sheet indices}&\text{labeled-base kernels}&
                    \text{no simple cycles of lengths }4,8,16\\\hline
1\text{--}132&166{,}344{,}289&0\\
133&2{,}896{,}000&56.
\end{array}
\]
The rooted \(K_{3,3}\) theorem, or the literal 32-cycle checks on the representatives below, completes the 133-sheet sharpness claim. At lower indices the modular tests exclude every cover from the claimed class.

## 3. \(K_{3,3}\): equality maps and abstract graphs

Since \(133=7\cdot19\) is squarefree, its abelian deck group is cyclic. Equivalently, a 133-index kernel is the intersection of a normalized nonzero linear character kernel modulo 7 and one modulo 19. Projective characters modulo \(p\) are normalized by making their first nonzero coordinate 1. The chart therefore contains
\[
            (7^3+7^2+7+1)(19^3+19^2+19+1)=2{,}896{,}000
\]
pairs, independently of HNF. The independent program also classifies all survivors through this chart: a vector belongs to the intersection iff both characters vanish. It evaluates each 16-clause with bitsets in the second chart, conditional on its first character. This is a separate replay of the full equality-index enumeration.

There are 72 automorphisms of \(K_{3,3}\): independently permute each side, and optionally interchange the sides. For any base permutation, pull back the old directed labels, compute the new cotree labels as sums along the fundamental cycles, and normalize them in each of the two prime fields. This is exactly the action on kernel maps allowing base automorphisms by §1, not a substitute for checking abstract graph isomorphism. The resulting disjoint sets of sizes \(24,24,8\) exhaust the 56 chart pairs. Representatives, in the chosen cotree order modulo 133, and exact adjacency traces are
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r}
\text{representative}&\text{orbit size}&\operatorname{tr}(A^{20})&
\operatorname{tr}(A^{30})\\\hline
(77,84,8,26)&24&48368435850&1242076788981126\\
(1,122,107,20)&24&48368435850&1242076788622026\\
(1,122,103,64)&8&48368340090&1241736424975746 .
\end{array}
\]
In each explicit lift graph, a unit vector at sheet zero of each base vertex is multiplied by adjacency twenty or thirty times; sum the starting coordinates and multiply by 133 using deck transitivity on every fiber. The trace pairs, which count closed walks and are graph invariants, are different, so the lift graphs are pairwise nonisomorphic. Covering-map isomorphism within a class implies graph isomorphism. Thus the exhaustion gives exactly three underlying graphs. Literal half-path checks on all three representatives give no \(4/8/16\) and yes \(32\).

## 4. The once-subdivided tetrahedron

The original \(K_4\) uses base vertices \(0,1,2,3\) with edge \(01\) subdivided using vertex \(4\). Use tree \(04,41,02,03\) and oriented cotree edges \(12,13,23\). Over every rank-three HNF of index at most 19, build the literal graph using the quotient representatives in §1. To check a length \(2h\), collect all internally simple \(h\)-step paths from a chosen actual vertex, grouped by endpoint. A cycle exists through that vertex iff two paths in one endpoint group have disjoint interiors; each path avoids its endpoints internally. It suffices to check sheet zero over each base vertex, because a deck translation moves any vertex of a cycle in its fiber to sheet zero. The same reasoning permits discarding walks whose base index is smaller than that of the chosen root: the cycle can instead be rooted in the smallest fiber which it visits. The original census additionally compared this half-path oracle with full simple-path traversal.

The complete count through 19 is \(4559\), with \(4178\) below 19. Exactly four qualifying maps remain, all cyclic at 19 sheets, each having a verified 32-cycle and no \(4/8/16\). Their cotree labels normalized modulo 19 are
\[
                      (1,8,0),\quad(1,8,7),\quad(1,12,0),\quad(1,12,11).
\]
The two swaps \(0\leftrightarrow1\), \(2\leftrightarrow3\) are the four base automorphisms with their products; the degree-two vertex \(4\) is fixed. Computing the fundamental-cycle sums as above gives the four displayed triples in the orbit of Davis's known \((1,8,0)\) witness. This proves uniqueness in the fixed-base class.

There is also a short upper-bound connection to the two-step theorem, independent of the census. The three length-two paths from 0 to 1 through 4, 2, and 3 have different interior fibers. Compress them to three matchings and apply the [three-matching lemma for class-two groups](FactorErdosGyarfasClassTwo.md#3-three-cross-layer-matchings), or use the four-cycle from coincident labels; expansion is simple. Hence every regular cover of this second base with a deck group of class at most two has a dyadic cycle of length at most 32, with the bound through each vertex of fibers 0 and 1. This separate existence argument does not extend the 95-vertex minimality assertion to nonabelian covers.

## Replay and limitations

* Fast full HNF census: `node verify_erdos_gyarfas_k33_hnf.mjs 1 133`. It generates all certificate clauses from the base, checks every determinant, and verifies the stated totals. Smaller inclusive bounds allow sharded replays.
* Independent checks: `python3 verify_erdos_gyarfas_fixed_base_independent.py` runs the full equality-index CRT chart, classifies its orbits and traces, and enumerates the smaller base using literal graphs. Pass two bounds to run the independent HNF sieve on that range instead; optional `--arrays` uses exact NumPy arrays for the same modular masks. No external certificate data are needed.
* The enumeration toolbox (tree normalization, HNF representatives, closure and collision masks, literal half paths) transfers to other fixed bases. The short-vector shortcut in §2 relies on the bipartite \(K_{3,3}\) setting: in a nonbipartite graph an eight-walk could split into lengths 3 and 5, which is why §4 tests literal paths instead.
* Nearest broader census work includes [Tranquilli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02675) and [the later structured search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28594). Their numerical lower bounds are for broad graph classes and do not imply the fixed-base minima established here.
