# Exact local-generator minima in totally ramified cyclotomic ideals

A bookkeeping step in the Ring-LWE reduction chooses an element that generates an ideal modulo the modulus. If one adds a requirement that this element be as short as a shortest lattice vector, that requirement can fail by an unbounded factor. We calculate the exact gap in explicit families. No hardness, security, attack, parameter choice, or algorithmic conclusion is drawn.

## Model and results

Let $p$ be a prime, $h\ge1$, $m=p^{h-1}$, $n=(p-1)m$, $\zeta=\zeta_{p^h}$, $R=\mathbb Z[\zeta]$, and $P=(1-\zeta)R$. The canonical squared Euclidean norm is $\|x\|^2=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q}(x\bar x)$. Put $I_k=P^k$ and define

$$
G(k)=\min_{\substack{x\in I_k\\x\notin I_{k+1}}}\|x\|,\qquad
L(k)=\min_{x\in I_k\setminus\{0\}}\|x\|.
$$

For any fixed positive $b$, $G(k)$ is the shortest norm of a multiplier giving an isomorphism $R/p^b R\to I_k/p^b I_k$, $y\mapsto xy$. Indeed, dividing by a generator of $I_k$ reduces this to multiplication by a unit in the local ring $R/p^bR$, whose only maximal ideal is the image of $P$. Any global ideal generator is a local generator, but the converse need not hold.

Because $P^n=pR$, the two norms each scale by $p$ when increasing $k$ by $n$. Work first with $0\le k<n$. For $k=jm+r$ with $0\le r<m$, let $W_p(r)=\prod_i(r_i+1)$ where $r_i$ are its base-$p$ digits.

**Complete binary and ternary formulas.** For $p=2$ and $p=3$, respectively,

$$
G(k)^2=
\begin{cases}
m W_2(k),&p=2,\\
2m\,3^j W_3(r),&p=3,\quad j=0,1.
\end{cases}
\qquad
L(k)^2=\min\left(p^2 n,\ \min_{k\le s<n}G(s)^2\right).
\tag{1}
$$

In both cases a global generator attains $G(k)$. Maximizing the squared ratio over all depths gives $\max(1,m/4)$ for $p=2$, and $\max(1,m/3)$ for $p=3$. At every depth where the ratio is strictly greater than 1, all shortest vectors fail the modular condition. This remains possible despite the usual identity $\lambda_1(I_k)=\lambda_n(I_k)$: multiplying a shortest vector by successive powers of $\zeta$ gives $n$ linearly independent shortest vectors, but they can all lie in $I_{k+1}$.

**All primes, top block of layers and an interior depth.** Write $C_p=p^2(p^2-1)/12$. For $(p-2)m\le k<n$, put $k=(p-2)m+r$ and $T_p(r)=\min_{r\le a<m}W_p(a)$. Then

$$
G(k)^2=m C_p W_p(r), \qquad
L(k)^2=m\min\{p^2(p-1),\, C_p T_p(r)\}.
\tag{2}
$$

This is a local-generator statement; it does not assert a global generator at every depth for $p>3$. A different exact calculation, at $k=m-1$ for every prime, gives

$$
G(m-1)^2=(p-1)m^2,\qquad
L(m-1)^2=\min\{(p-1)m^2,\ 2pm\}.
\tag{3}
$$

At this depth a global generator attains the local minimum. For odd $p$ and $m>1$ this is distinct from depth $n-1$. Formulas (2) and (3) agree when their ranges overlap. At $k=n-1$, the squared ratio from (2) is $\max\{1,m(p+1)/12\}$.

## Orthogonal blocks and exact order

Use the integral basis with exponents $a+bm$, $0\le a<m$, $0\le b<p-1$. For a fixed $a$ we may use $p$ integer coefficient slots $v_{a,b}$, $0\le b<p$, modulo a common integer translation in those slots, by $\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}\zeta^{bm}=0$. The trace of $\zeta^d$ is $pm\,{\bf1}_{pm\mid d}-m\,{\bf1}_{m\mid d}$ by subtracting the sum over multiples of $p$ from the sum over all residues. Consequently

$$
\|x\|^2=m\sum_{a=0}^{m-1} Q_p(v_a),\qquad
Q_p(v)=p\sum_bv_b^2-\left(\sum_b v_b\right)^2=\sum_{b<c}(v_b-v_c)^2.
\tag{4}
$$

A nonconstant integer vector has $Q_p(v)\ge p-1$: an integer cut between its smallest and largest entry puts $s$ entries on one side and $p-s$ on the other, giving at least $s(p-s)\ge p-1$ unit contributions. Equality is attained by a single $1$. If $\sum_b v_b\equiv0\pmod p$ and the block is nonzero as an integer class, an integer common shift gives sum zero, so $Q_p(v)\ge2p$; equality is attained by $(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)$.

Modulo $p$, the polynomial $\Phi_{p^h}(Z)=\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}Z^{bm}$ becomes $(Z-1)^n$. Thus $R/pR\cong\mathbb F_p[U]/(U^n)$, with $U=Z-1$, and the $P$-valuation of each element not in $pR$ is its exact order in $U$. There is a unique prime over $p$, generated by $1-\zeta$, and $P^n=pR$: the unique prime modulo $p$ follows from this quotient, while $|N(1-\zeta)|=\Phi_{p^h}(1)=p$ gives the generator and the exponent. More generally $|N(1-\zeta^{p^i})|=p^{p^i}$ for $0\le i<h$, by the norm in the subfield of conductor $p^{h-i}$. Hence these elements generate $P^{p^i}$.

In the quotient expand $Z^m=1+U^m$ and put

$$
c_{a,j}=\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}\binom b j v_{a,b}\pmod p,\qquad
F_j(Z)=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}c_{a,j}Z^a .
$$

For $0\le j\le p-2$ these moments are invariant under common shifts, since $\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}\binom b j=\binom p{j+1}\equiv0\pmod p$. The residue equals $\sum_{j=0}^{p-2} U^{jm}F_j(1+U)$. The ranges of degrees are disjoint, so exact order $jm+r$ means all $F_{j'}=0$ for $j'<j$ and $F_j$ has exact root order $r$ at $Z=1$. Each active block is an $a$ with $c_{a,j}\ne0$.

The elementary weight-retention inequality gives at least $W_p(r)$ active blocks. Here is a proof in the length needed. More generally for any $F$ of degree $<p^\ell$ and exact order $r<p^\ell$, split it into $p$ blocks of coefficient length $m'=p^{\ell-1}$ and write $r=jm'+r'$. As above, the earlier moment polynomials vanish; the $j$th moment polynomial has exact order $r'$. For each of its nonzero coefficients, at least $j+1$ original coefficients in that column are nonzero: if the number $s$ were at most $j$, the first $s$ zero moments would give an invertible $s$-point Vandermonde matrix (the factorials up to $(s-1)!$ are invertible), forcing those coefficients to be zero. Induction from length 1 proves the product lower bound, and $(Z-1)^r=\prod_i(Z^{p^i}-1)^{r_i}$ has exactly that many terms because all digitwise exponents are distinct and all binomial coefficients for a single digit are nonzero modulo $p$. This is the classical weight-retention property, not a new coding-theoretic theorem.

## Solving the layers

**Binary and ternary.** In the binary case the active blocks cost at least $p-1=1$ each. In the ternary case the active blocks for $j=0$ cost at least 2, and those for $j=1$ have zero zeroth moment and cost at least 6 by (4). These lower bounds are attained jointly by actual global generators of $I_k$. To see this, for each digit $r_i=1$ use the factor $\zeta^{p^i}-1$. For each ternary digit $r_i=2$ use $1+\zeta^{3^i}+\zeta^{2\cdot3^i}=(\zeta^{3^{i+1}}-1)/(\zeta^{3^i}-1)$. Their product has valuation exactly $r$ and principal ideal supported only at $P$, by the norm identities above. In the low-exponent range it has exactly $W_p(r)$ entries of magnitude 1 and other entries zero. For ternary $j=1$, multiply by $\zeta^m-1$, so each active block becomes a signed copy of a $(-1,1,0)$ block. This proves the $G$ part of (1). All remaining nonzero elements have valuation at least $n$ iff they lie in $pR$, whose squared lattice minimum is $p^2n$ by (4). This gives $L$.

For the maxima of the binary and ternary ratios, first take $r=m-1$, where $W_p(r)=m$. The binary denominator in units of $m$ is $\min(4,m)$; the ternary denominator in units of $2m\,3^j$ is $\min(3,m)$. The latter holds for $j=0$ since the entire next layer starts at cost $3$, and for $j=1$ by the $pR$ option. At $r=0$ the ratio is 1. At $1\le r<m-1$, at least one digit is smaller than $p-1$, so $W_2(r)\le m/2$ and $W_3(r)\le 2m/3$. For all remaining layers within that block the weight is at least 2, and every later block or the $pR$ option costs at least two units of that block's factor. Thus the ratio squared there is at most $m/4$ and $m/3$, respectively. The endpoint formulas also cover $m=1$, and $m=2$ for binary. For binary $m\ge4$, the same maximal gap occurs already at $k=m/2-1$, as the next depth has weight 2.

**Top block for every prime.** If all earlier moments of an active block vanish, there is just one remaining degree below $p-1$ after replacing $Z$ by $1+U$: modulo $p$, take its representative with last slot zero as the coefficient vector of $c(Z-1)^{p-2}$ with $c\ne0$. Since $\binom{p-2}{b}\equiv(-1)^b(b+1)\pmod p$, adjoining the last slot zero gives all residue values $0,\ldots,p-1$, in some order. This identity includes $p=2$. Their integer representatives are pairwise distinct, and (4) is minimized by consecutive representatives: upon sorting, every pairwise difference is at least the index difference, and consecutive integers attain all those bounds. The minimum of $Q_p$ is $C_p$ by summing the squared differences. The weight lower bound then implies $G(k)^2\ge m C_p W_p(r)$. For equality, choose $F_{p-2}=(Z-1)^r$, set every inactive block to zero, and take a consecutive integer lift in each active block as just described. Earlier moments still vanish, and the valuation is exact. Minimizing across the remaining layers and $pR$ proves (2).

**Depth $m-1$ for every prime.** In the $j=0$ block range, the only available order at this depth is the one whose weight lower bound is $m$. The element $\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}\zeta^a=(\zeta^m-1)/(\zeta-1)$ is a global generator and attains the lower bound of $p-1$ per active block. An element of $I_m$ has zeroth moment zero in every block (including when $p=2$ and $I_m=pR$); every nonzero block in (4) has cost at least $2p$. The element $\zeta^m-1\in I_m$ shows equality, with $\zeta^m=-1$ for $p=2$. Competing with this alternative gives (3).

## Relation to the original literature

- [Lyubashevsky–Peikert–Regev, Lemmas 2.14–2.15 and §4.1](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/papers/ideal-lwe.pdf) use a multiplier with $xI^{-1}$ coprime to the modulus ideal. They do not bound it by the shortest vector length, and the noise calculation uses the original sampled element in $I$, not the norm of this multiplier. The gap above refutes an optional shortness assumption; it does not invalidate that reduction or give a better one.
- [Massey–Costello–Justesen, Theorem 1.1](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/polynomial-weights-and-code-constructions/) give the Hamming weight-retention property used here; [Castagnoli–Massey–Schoeller–von Seemann](https://www.isiweb.ee.ethz.ch/papers/arch/gcasta-mass-inspec-1991-1.pdf) relate such digit weights to repeated-root cyclic-code distances. A shortest vector in the integer ideal can come from a *higher* depth or the zero residue layer. It is the additional exact order that constrains a local generator.
- [Porter–Ling–Sarti–Leibak, proof of Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.16870) already use the same cyclotomic trace blocks and the fractional ideal $P^{-1}$, whose scalar multiple $pP^{-1}=P^{n-1}$ is the terminal example here. In particular the growing discrepancy at that endpoint is not a new counterexample. The scoped additions are the exact all-depth binary and ternary formulas (and sharp maximum across this chain), the full top block for arbitrary $p$, and the exact depth $m-1$ calculation away from the terminal ideal when $p$ is odd and $m>1$. No claim is made about all principal ideals or exhaustive priority.

The earlier [ramified ideal smoothing note](ramified-ideal-smoothing.md) computes the ordinary binary lattice minimum as part of its theta-series analysis. The binary quantity added here is the minimum at an *exact* depth, and its comparison with that ordinary minimum.

The accompanying verifier enumerates small residue classes and integer lifts, checks the digit-weight and trace-block identities, and verifies attaining witnesses. The proof, not finite enumeration, supplies the general results.
