# Growth of monomial HSOP cyclotomic generating functions

We address a natural submonoid in [Billey–Swanson, *Cyclotomic generating
functions*, Problems 61–62](https://doi.org/10.37236/12687).
Write \([a]_q=(1-q^a)/(1-q)\) and let \(\mathcal H\) be their HSOP monoid,
the Hilbert series of finite-dimensional quotients by homogeneous systems
of parameters in positively weighted polynomial rings over \(\mathbb C\).
Let \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal H\) consist of the Hilbert series obtainable
with a monomial system of parameters. A subscript \(n\) denotes exact
polynomial degree \(n\).

**Results.**
1. The limit \(\lambda_{\mathcal M}=\lim_{n\to\infty}
\log|\mathcal M_n|/\sqrt n\) exists. We give a formula in terms of smooth
cyclotomic blocks, with an explicit \(O(Y^{-1/2})\) approximation error for
the prime cutoff \(Y\). This defines a computable constant.
2. An explicit extra choice beyond the elementary independent generators
gives, in particular,
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|\mathcal H_n|}{\sqrt n}
 \ \ge\lambda_{\mathcal M}\ \ge\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{15}}.
\]
A stronger prime-series lower bound is given in §4.
3. The prime-step ratios are exactly the atoms of \(\mathcal M\).
   The two-variable family in §5 gives
\(\frac{3}{\pi^2}X\log X+O(X)\) distinct atoms of degree at most \(X\)
in the *full* monoids \(\mathcal H\) and \(\Phi^+\), lying outside
\(\mathcal M\). Section 6 gives the unique least-degree polynomial
containing a prescribed cyclotomic factor in the HSOP and Gale monoids.

This does not identify the leading constant for \(\mathcal H\) itself,
or classify all its atoms. It does not revisit Conjectures 33 or 48.
For Problem 60, see
[Gatzweiler–Levicán-Santibáñez–Yoshida, Proposition 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22226v1).

## 1. Independent smooth blocks

A monomial system of parameters in \(m\) variables generating an ideal
of finite colength must consist of one pure power of each variable:
some power of every variable belongs to the ideal and must be divisible
by one of the \(m\) generators. Conversely pure powers give finite
colength. Thus \(\mathcal M\) is generated by \([a]_q/[b]_q\) with
\(b\mid a\), \(a>b\). Telescoping along a prime factorization of \(a/b\)
shows that it is generated by
\[
             G_{p,b}(q)=\frac{[pb]_q}{[b]_q},
     \qquad p\text{ prime},\quad b\ge1.
\]
The degree of this generator is \((p-1)b\). Since \(G_{p,b}(1)=p\)
is prime, a product of two nonconstant basic CGFs (each with value
at least 2 at 1) cannot equal it. Distinct pairs \(p,b\) give distinct
generators, as one reads \(p\) from the value at 1 and \(b\) from the
first positive exponent. Thus these are exactly the atoms of
\(\mathcal M\), all of which are also atoms of \(\Phi^+\).
In each positive degree \(n\) there is one for each prime \(p\) with \(p-1\mid n\);
there are \(\sum_{p\le X+1}\lfloor X/(p-1)\rfloor\sim X\log\log X\)
up to degree \(X\), by the prime reciprocal-sum estimate.

For an integer \(Y\ge2\), let \(S\) be the primes at most \(Y\). The
\(S\)-smooth positive integers have prime factors only in \(S\).
Call an integer \(S\)-rough here if it is relatively prime to
\(\prod_{p\in S}p\), including 1. Set
\(\delta_S=\prod_{p\in S}(1-1/p)\).
Let \(\mathcal L_S\) be the *local* monoid generated by \(G_{p,b}\)
with \(p\in S\) and \(b\) \(S\)-smooth, and let
\[
        H_S(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}|(\mathcal L_S)_n|z^n.
\]
The letter \(z\) tracks degree; \(q\) is the variable of the individual
polynomial. Let \(\mathcal A_S\) be generated by all \(G_{p,b}\) with
\(p\in S\) (with no restriction on \(b\)), together with all
\([m]_q\) for \(m\ge2\).
We claim its degree enumerator is exactly
\[
 P_S(z)=
 \prod_{\substack{r\ge1\\ r\;S\text{-rough}}}H_S(z^r)
 \prod_{\substack{r\ge2\\ r\;S\text{-rough}}}(1-z^{r-1})^{-1}. \tag{1}
\]

Indeed \(b=r s\), with \(r\) \(S\)-rough and \(s\) \(S\)-smooth, gives
\(G_{p,b}(q)=G_{p,s}(q^r)\); also
\([rs]_q=[r]_q[s]_{q^r}\), with the second factor obtainable by
\(S\)-prime telescoping. So the proposed block products are onto.
Every cyclotomic index \(u\) in a local block is \(S\)-smooth and
at least 2. Since \(\gcd(u,r)=1\),
\[
                         \Phi_u(q^r)=\prod_{d\mid r}\Phi_{u d}(q).
\]
For example, the identity follows by classifying the orders of roots
whose \(r\)th powers are primitive \(u\)th roots; all roots are simple.
The largest rough part \(r\) in a finite block product recovers its
entire \(\mathcal L_S\)-factor from the exponents of the \(\Phi_{ur}\).
Subtract that block and proceed downwards to prove uniqueness.
The separate \([r]_q\)'s on rough \(r\ge2\) have only rough indices
(i.e. smooth part 1), disjoint from the local blocks. They are
multiplicatively independent by their distinct largest indices,
again by descending induction. This proves (1).

## 2. Logarithmic growth for fixed cutoff

Write
\[
      I_S=\int_0^\infty \log H_S(e^{-x})\,dx,\qquad
      K_S=\delta_S\bigl(I_S+\zeta(2)\bigr),\qquad
      \lambda_S=2\sqrt{K_S}.                              \tag{2}
\]
The integral is finite. Indeed, unique cyclotomic factorization
bounds \(H_S\) by the weighted partition series with weights \(\varphi(u)\),
one for each \(S\)-smooth \(u\ge2\).
Here \(\varphi(u)\ge\delta_S u\) and
there are \(O_S((1+\log U)^{|S|})\) such \(u\le U\), by bounding
each prime exponent. For \(x\downarrow0\) the logarithm of the
majorant is \(O_S((1+\log(1/x))^{|S|+1})\):
for \(u\le1/(\delta_S x)\) use
\(-\log(1-e^{-y})\le\log(2/y)\) at \(0<y\le1\), and bound the
remaining dyadic ranges by the exponential tail there.
The same majorant decays exponentially in logarithm at infinity.
These bounds also show continuity of \(\log H_S(e^{-x})\) for \(x>0\).

The positive rough integers form periodic residue classes of
density \(\delta_S\). For a nonnegative decreasing integrable
function on \((0,\infty)\), the corresponding Riemann sum over
these progressions, multiplied by the mesh, tends to that density
times its integral. This follows for each progression by bounding
with upper and lower integral sums; the first term near zero
vanishes after multiplication by the mesh, by monotonicity and
integrability. Apply this to the two logarithms in (1), using
the shifted rough indices \(r-1\) in the second and
\(\int_0^\infty-\log(1-e^{-x})dx=\zeta(2)\).
It gives
\[
                  \lim_{t\downarrow0}t\log P_S(e^{-t})=K_S. \tag{3}
\]

For clarity, the elementary exponential Tauberian step used here
is as follows. If \(F(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_ne^{-tn}\) with \(a_n\ge0\)
and \(\log F(t)\sim K/t\), \(K>0\), then
\(\log\sum_{n\le N}a_n\sim2\sqrt{K N}\).
The upper bound uses \(e^{tN}F(t)\) with \(t=\sqrt{K/N}\).
For the lower bound, put probability \(a_ne^{-tn}/F(t)\) on \(n\).
The exponential bounds using \(F((1-\eta)t)/F(t)\) and
\(F((1+\eta)t)/F(t)\) put asymptotically all mass in
\((1-\epsilon)K/t^2\le n\le(1+\epsilon)K/t^2\):
for example the log upper-tail bound is
\((K/t)\eta(1/(1-\eta)-1-\epsilon)+o(1/t)<0\)
for sufficiently small fixed \(\eta>0\), and the lower-tail
analogue is \((K/t)\eta(1-\epsilon-1/(1+\eta))+o(1/t)<0\).
Take \(t=\sqrt{(1+\epsilon)K/N}\), multiply the mass in this
interval back by \(F(t)e^{(1-\epsilon)K/t}\),
and let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).
In the present application the exact degree counts are
nondecreasing, because multiplication by the fixed \([2]_q\)
injects one degree into the next in \(\mathcal A_S\).
The cumulative count and the exact count therefore differ by at most
a factor \(N+1\). Hence (3) gives
\[
                     \log|(\mathcal A_S)_n|\sim\lambda_S\sqrt n. \tag{4}
\]

## 3. An effective passage to all primes

For every \(f\in\mathcal M_n\), use any prime-step factorization.
For each large-prime step \(G_{p,b}\), \(p>Y\), multiply it by
\([b]_q\) to turn it into \([pb]_q\).
The product \(B\) of the added \([b]_q\)'s has degree \(d\le n/Y\),
since \(b-1\le (p-1)b/Y\). The completed \(fB\) lies in
\(\mathcal A_S\) of degree \(n+d\). The pair \((fB,B)\) determines \(f\).
Products of the \([b]_q\)'s of total degree \(d\) are counted by the
ordinary partition number \(p(d)\), by the same distinct-largest-index
argument. Consequently
\[
 |(\mathcal A_S)_n|\le|\mathcal M_n|
 \le\sum_{0\le d\le n/Y}|(\mathcal A_S)_{n+d}|\,p(d).
\]
Using (4) and the partition logarithm \(\log p(d)\sim2\sqrt{\zeta(2)d}\)
(the same argument as §2 with all positive integer part weights),
we obtain
\[
 \lambda_S\ \le\ \liminf_n\frac{\log|\mathcal M_n|}{\sqrt n}
 \ \le\ \limsup_n\frac{\log|\mathcal M_n|}{\sqrt n}
 \ \le\ \sqrt{1+1/Y}\,\lambda_S+\frac{2\sqrt{\zeta(2)}}{\sqrt Y}. \tag{5}
\]
The \(\lambda_S\)'s increase with \(Y\), since the \(\mathcal A_S\)'s do.
They are bounded: the full signed cyclotomic monoid has logarithmic
growth \(O(\sqrt n)\), as recalled by Billey–Swanson before Problem 49.
An elementary bound suffices: the number of \(u\) with
\(\varphi(u)\le X\) is \(O(X)\), since
\(\sum_{u\le T}(u/\varphi(u))^2=O(T)\) and dyadic splitting by \(u/X\)
then gives a convergent geometric bound.
The mean-square bound follows by expanding
\(\prod_{p\mid u}(1-1/p)^{-2}\) into squarefree divisor terms,
whose Euler product after division by the divisor converges.
Counting cyclotomic products by \(\varphi(u)\)-weighted partitions
and summing the logarithms then gives a Laplace upper bound \(O(1/t)\).
Let \(Y\to\infty\) in (5).
This proves \(\lambda_{\mathcal M}=\lim_Y\lambda_S\), with the
claimed effective cutoff inequality.

Each \(I_S\) is itself computable. The exact local counts up to any
degree \(N\) are obtained by enumerating the finitely many generators
of degree at most \(N\) and recording the distinct cyclotomic exponent
vectors of their products of degree at most \(N\).
The explicit logarithmic and exponential majorants above bound the
integral tails near zero and infinity. On a compact interval
\([\epsilon,L]\), coefficient truncation at \(N\) has error bounded by
\(e^{-\epsilon N/2}H_S(e^{-\epsilon/2})\), which is bounded by
the convergent Euler-product majorant. Since the constant term is 1,
the same error bound applies to the logarithm. Rational interval
quadrature on the remaining compact interval and (5) give
arbitrarily accurate bounds on \(\lambda_{\mathcal M}\).
No practical-runtime claim is implicit in this approximation result.

## 4. A strict enlargement of the elementary independent family

For any \(S\)-smooth \(u>1\), the factor
\(G_{p,u/p}=[u]_q/[u/p]_q\) with \(p\) the least prime factor of \(u\)
has largest cyclotomic index \(u\) with exponent 1 and
weight \(w(u)=u-u/p\) in the degree series.
They are all independent as a free commutative monoid.
When \(2,3\in S\), a stronger block on the indices \(2,3,6\) is
generated by
\[
   \Phi_2,\qquad \Phi_3,\qquad \Phi_2\Phi_6,\qquad \Phi_3\Phi_6
\]
of respective degrees \(1,2,3,4\). All four are \(S\)-prime steps:
the last two are \([6]_q/[3]_q\) and \([6]_q/[2]_q\).
Their generated exponents \((a,b,c)\) at \(2,3,6\) are exactly
\(a,b,c\ge0,\ c\le a+b\).
The degree series is
\[
       \frac{1-z^5}{(1-z)(1-z^2)(1-z^3)(1-z^4)}.
\]
To verify it, replace one pair of first and fourth generators by a
pair of second and third until at least one of the first/fourth
multiplicities is zero. In terms of \((a,b,c)\), the third
multiplicity in this normal form is \(\min(a,c)\), the fourth is
\(c-\min(a,c)\), and the remaining two are determined. Thus this
restriction gives one representative of each exponent vector.
Compared with the independent choices at \(2,3,6\), the series
gains a factor \((1-z^5)/(1-z^4)\).

More generally, do this for the three indices \(2r,3r,6r\) for each
\(r\) smooth for \(S\setminus\{2,3\}\), by substituting \(q^r\)
in the four-generator block. Leave the elementary choices at all
other indices. There are no collisions. Sort the factors by their
largest part supported on \(S\setminus\{2,3\}\); a factor with such
part \(r\) has support only at divisors with that part dividing \(r\).
At a fixed largest \(r\), only the special block uses exclusively
the smooth cores \(2,3,6\). Recover the other generator exponents
in descending order of core at this \(r\); the special block has
no support at those other cores. Subtract those factors and
recover the full triple on \(2,3,6\). Repeat for smaller \(r\).
The three elementary choices replaced in each block were precisely
the first, second and third generators above, with the same \(q^r\)
substitution. The additional integral is therefore
\(\zeta(2)(1/4-1/5)\sum_r1/r\).

For \(S\) all primes up to \(Y\), \(Y\ge3\), integrating the
independent series and this enlargement gives
\[
 \frac{K_S}{\zeta(2)}
 \ \ge\ \delta_S+
       \sum_{p\le Y}\frac{1}{p-1}\prod_{\ell<p,\ \ell\text{ prime}}
                          (1-1/\ell)+\frac1{60}. \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(\int_0^\infty-\log(1-e^{-wx})dx=\zeta(2)/w\).
Group the independent \(u\)'s by their least prime factor \(p\)
and sum the reciprocal weights over smooth integers; multiplication
by \(\delta_S\) yields the displayed summands.
The block correction after multiplication by \(\delta_S\) is
\(\zeta(2)(1/20)(1-1/2)(1-1/3)=\zeta(2)/60\).
At \(Y=3\) the right side is \(8/5\), proving the stated simple
constant \(4\pi/\sqrt{15}\). The numerical bound can be strengthened
directly by increasing \(Y\) in (6), independently of computing \(H_S\).

## 5. Mixed two-variable atoms of the whole HSOP monoid

For coprime integers \(a,b\ge2\), define
\[
                  F_{a,b}(q)=
        \frac{[a+b]_q[ab]_q}{[a]_q[b]_q}.
\]
It is an HSOP polynomial: for variables of weights \(a,b\),
the two forms \(XY\) and \(X^b+Y^a\) have only the origin in their
common zero set. The Hilbert series is as displayed.
A direct identity gives
\[
        F_{a,b}(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}q^{ia}+\sum_{j=1}^{a}q^{jb}.
\]
It follows also by writing
\((1-q^{a+b})/((1-q^a)(1-q^b))=1/(1-q^a)+q^b/(1-q^b)\).
The two support pieces do not overlap. This generalizes
Billey–Swanson's Example 47, \(F_{2,3}=\Phi_5\Phi_6\).

In any factorization into two nonconstant polynomials with
nonnegative integer coefficients and constant terms 1, each factor's
support lies in this support. Neither factor contains degree \(ab\),
and so each positive exponent is a proper positive multiple
of \(a\) or of \(b\), exclusively. If opposite types occur in
different factors, their sum is neither a multiple of \(a\) nor of \(b\),
by coprimality and the strict inequalities on the multipliers.
This contradicts the displayed support. If both types occur in
one factor, any positive exponent of the other has opposite type
to one of them, with the same contradiction. Since both types must
occur to supply the exponents \(a,b\), no factorization is possible.
Thus \(F_{a,b}\) is an atom in \(\Phi^+\) and in \(\mathcal H\).
It is not in \(\mathcal M\): as an atom it would have to be
a single elementary geometric sum, whose positive exponents
all lie on one progression beginning at its smallest positive exponent.
The support of \(F_{a,b}\) has the smaller of \(a,b\) as its
smallest positive exponent but also a positive exponent not divisible
by it.

The smaller parameter can be read as the smallest positive support
point and the larger as the least support point not divisible by
it, so distinct unordered pairs give distinct atoms.
For \(n\ge2\) there are \(2^{\omega(n)-1}-1\)
unordered coprime pairs \(a,b\ge2\) with \(ab=n\), by assigning
each prime-power divisor to one factor.
Thus the family has \(\frac{3}{\pi^2}X\log X+O(X)\) elements of degree
at most \(X\): use
\(2^{\omega(n)}=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu^2(d)\) and
\(\sum_{d\le X}\mu^2(d)/d=(6/\pi^2)\log X+O(1)\).
This counts the given family, not all atoms.

## 6. Exact least degree containing a prescribed cyclotomic factor

Let \(F\ne1\) lie in the HSOP monoid or the Gale monoid, and suppose
\(\Phi_n\) divides \(F\), where \(n\ge2\). Cancel common entries in an
equal-length \(q\)-integer presentation and sort the two lists,
\(a_1\le\cdots\le a_m,\ b_1\le\cdots\le b_m\).
The Gale inequality \(a_i\ge b_i\) survives cancellation, and it
holds throughout the HSOP monoid by Billey–Swanson Lemma 56.
The maximum denominator \(b_m\) divides some numerator
(by cyclotomic divisibility, or trivially if \(b_m=1\)).
Disjointness implies \(a_m\ge2b_m\), so \(a_m\) is the largest
cyclotomic index. Some numerator is divisible by \(n\).
If \(a_m\ge2n\), the largest gap alone is at least \(n\).
If \(n<a_m<2n\), some other numerator must equal \(n\). In that case
the two largest gaps sum to at least \(a_m+n-2b_m\ge n\).
Therefore at degree less than \(n\) we necessarily have \(a_m=n\).

If \(b_m\mid n\), it is at most \(n/p_{\min}(n)\), and the total degree
is at least \(n-b_m\ge n-n/p_{\min}(n)\).
Otherwise some smaller numerator is a proper multiple of \(b_m\),
so \(a_{m-1}\ge2b_m\); the two largest gaps alone then give
\[
        \deg F\ge (n-b_m)+(a_{m-1}-b_{m-1})\ge n.
\]
The lower bound \(n-n/p_{\min}(n)\) is attained by the elementary
ratio \([n]_q/[n/p_{\min}(n)]_q\).
Equality requires \(b_m=n/p_{\min}(n)\) and all other gaps zero.
After cancellation no gap can be zero, so there is no other entry.
Thus the displayed ratio is the unique minimizer in either monoid.
