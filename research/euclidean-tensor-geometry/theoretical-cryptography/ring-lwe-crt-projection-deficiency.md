# Sharp deficiency for erasing a CRT component of a wrapped ring label

We ask for a sample transformation, not a decoder: can a block of wrapped ring observations be turned into one standard observation whose label is the CRT projection of the unknown input label? The transformation may be any Markov kernel, with no algebraic or computational restriction, and may change both the coefficients and responses. Below the entropy scale of the discarded component, even \(\Theta(d^2)\) input rows give no asymptotic advantage over ignoring the input, to the sharp scale of the one-row output experiment.

## 1. Experiment and theorem

Fix an odd prime \(q\ge3\) and \(\beta,\beta'>0\). Let \(d\) range over sufficiently large powers of two and put \(R_d=\mathbb F_q[X]/(X^d+1)\). The ring is a product of its residue fields. Pick nonzero complementary ideals \(J=J_d\), \(I=I_d\) with \(R_d=J\oplus I\), and let \(\pi_J\) be the projection onto \(J\). Write
\[
 \chi_d=d^{-1}\log|I|,\quad b=\beta/d,\quad b'=\beta'/d,\quad
 t_d=k_d b^2,
\]
where \(k_d\) is the input row count and \(t_d\) belongs to a fixed compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\).

Let \(P_s\) be the law of \(k_d\) independent rows \((A_i,Y_i)\) given \(s\in R_d\), each with \(A_i\) uniform in \(R_d\) and \(Y_i=A_i s/q+E_i\) on the coefficient torus, where the independent scalar errors have density
\[
 f_b(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}b^{j^2}e^{2\pi i jx}.
\]
Let \(Q_z\) be the one-row law defined in exactly the same way with label \(z\in J\), a fresh uniform coefficient in the **same ring**, and error-density parameter \(b'\). This is not a new subfield or quotient error definition. Denote the smallest worst-case simulation error by
\[
 \delta_d=\inf_K\max_{s\in R_d}
       \operatorname{TV}\bigl(KP_s,Q_{\pi_J(s)}\bigr),
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(K\) is any Borel Markov kernel from the full input block to one output row. Total variation is half the \(L^1\) difference for densities.

If
\[
 \liminf_{d\to\infty}\frac{\chi_d-t_d}{\sqrt{2\chi_d\log(d)/d}}>1,
 \tag{2}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle\delta_d=(1+o(1))\,b'\sqrt{d/\pi}
               =(1+o(1))\,\frac{\beta'}{\sqrt{\pi d}}.}
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular (2) holds if \(t_d\) stays strictly below \(\chi_d\) by a constant margin. For a fixed strict margin in (2), the asymptotics are uniform over the choices of complementary nontrivial ideals. We also show that the coarse experiment obtained by mixing over the discarded component has distance \(o(d^{-1/2})\) to a single reference law uniformly across all retained labels. Equation (3) concerns approximate simulation of a weakly informative output. It neither rules out coarser simulations that ignore the output signal scale, nor asserts anything above (2).

## 2. A fixed-block representation for all ideals

Fix an initial dyadic \(d_0\) large enough that \(\operatorname{ord}_{2d}(q)/d\) has stabilized. Such a \(d_0\) exists because the order is a power of two and \(v_2(q^{2^j}-1)=v_2(q^2-1)+j-1\) for \(j\ge1\). Every root of \(X^d+1\) has order \(2d\), so all irreducible factors have this common degree. Put \(\ell=d/d_0\) and factor \(X^{d_0}+1=\prod_i F_i(X)\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). Then each \(F_i(X^\ell)\) is irreducible: it divides \(X^d+1\), and its degree is exactly that of a single irreducible factor by stabilization. Each ideal in \(R_d\) is therefore generated by a product of some of these factors, and corresponds to a fixed ideal of \(R_{d_0}\) generated by the same product of \(F_i\)'s.

The substitution \(X\mapsto X^\ell\) embeds the base ring. Sorting coefficient positions by their residue modulo \(\ell\) gives the direct module decomposition
\[
 R_d=\bigoplus_{a=0}^{\ell-1} X^a R_{d_0}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Multiplication by a base generator preserves each summand. Hence the coefficient set of each ideal is the product of \(\ell\) copies of its base ideal, not just an isomorphic abstract group. There are a bounded number of ideals, and every nontrivial residue-field component has size at least \(e^{\alpha d}\) for a fixed \(\alpha>0\). In particular \(\chi_d\) takes finitely many positive values bounded away from zero and from \(\log q\).

Use the coefficient dot product and set \(C=I^\perp\); it is an ideal, because multiplication by \(a\) is adjoint to multiplication by \(\overline a=a(X^{-1})\). We have \(|C|=q^d/|I|\), \(0<C<R_d\), and \(C^\perp=I\). In the same product decomposition \(C_0=I_0^\perp\), where subscripts zero indicate their base ideals.

A proper ideal of an odd-modulus dyadic ring contains no signed monomial or signed binomial with distinct positions (all nonzero coefficients \(\pm1\)). In fact under reduction to any residue field the image of \(X\) has order \(2d\); an exponent difference of magnitude \(<d\) cannot give \(1\) or \(-1\). All these words are units. We will use this both for the base ring and for \(R_d\).

## 3. Hiding the shared discarded component below its entropy scale

Use \(g_b(y)=q^{-1}\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_q}f_b(y-u/q)=f_{b^{q^2}}(qy)\), and the input reference \(\nu\) with independent uniform coefficients and all scalar response densities \(g_b\). With \(M=k_dd\), write \(G=\prod_{a=1}^M g_b\), \(L=\prod_{a=1}^M f_b(y_a)/g_b(y_a)\), and \(Z=\log L\). For \(z\in J\) let
\[
 \bar P_z=|I|^{-1}\sum_{v\in I}P_{z+v}.
\]
The mixture uses **one common** \(v\) for the entire block, not independently selected \(v_i\)'s. We prove
\[
 \max_{z\in J}\operatorname{TV}(\bar P_z,\nu)=o(d^{-1/2}).
 \tag{5}
\]

For a general nonnegative retained density \(U\le L\), let \(m\) be its zero grid sector (its average under all grid translations), and \(\eta=\int(L-U)G\). Then \(0\le m\le1\), since the grid average of \(L\) is one. Replacing \(L\) by \(U+(1-m)\), shifted by the same actual label, gives a probability density at TV cost at most \(\eta\). The correction \(1-m\) is invariant under all label shifts. Denote the conditional replaced mixture by \(\bar P_z^*\).

Let \(\Gamma_w\) be the squared norm of the \(w\)-residue sector of \(U\) in \(L^2(G)\), \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_{k_d})\in R_d^{k_d}\). The mixture retains a nonzero sector iff \(\sum_i\overline A_i w_i\in C\). This follows by averaging a nontrivial character over the common \(v\in I\). Its retained squared norm is independent of \(z\), since shifting by \(z\) only supplies a unit complex phase. Over uniform \(A_i\), the sum is uniform on the ideal \(K(w)=\sum_i w_iR_d\). For \(K(w)\subseteq C\) this probability is one. For proper \(K(w)\not\subseteq C\) it is at most \(e^{-\alpha d}\), since \(K(w)/(K(w)\cap C)\) has a nonzero residue-field component. For \(K(w)=R_d\) it is \(|I|^{-1}\). Thus
\[
 \chi^2(\bar P_z^*\Vert\nu)
 \le \|\mathcal P_C U-m\|_2^2
       + O(e^{-\alpha d})+|I|^{-1}\|U\|_2^2 .
 \tag{6}
\]
Here \(\mathcal P_C\) is the projection onto sectors with each \(w_i\in C\). It averages over independent phase translations by \(I\) on the rows, and is used only to bound the indicated **sector mass**, not to replace the common-label mixture law. To check the \(O(e^{-\alpha d})\) term, cover all proper \(K(w)\) by the bounded number of maximal ideals. For a proper ideal \(K\), the projection \(\mathcal P_K\) is positive and \(\|\mathcal P_K U\|_2^2\le\|\mathcal P_K L\|_2^2=O(1)\), as seen next.

For scalar residue \(a\), the squared norm of the \(f_b/g_b\) sector is \(\tau_0=1\), \(\tau_{\pm1}=O(b^2)\), and \(\tau_a=O_q(b^8)\) for the other residues, by Parseval and \(g_b=1+O_q(b^{q^2})\). By (4) a proper ideal contains products of \(\ell\) copies of a proper base code. The sum of its base weights \(\sum_{u\in K_0}\prod_j\tau_{u_j}\) is \(1+O(b^6)\): the base dimension is fixed, and the no-signed-words property excludes both weight-one and weight-two terms with all signs \(\pm1\), while every other nonzero contribution is \(O(b^6)\). Thus
\[
 \|\mathcal P_K L\|_2^2=(1+O(b^6))^{\ell k_d}=1+O(d^{-3}),
 \qquad
 \|\mathcal P_K L-1\|_2=O(d^{-3/2}).
 \tag{7}
\]
Nontrivial ideals were essential for the probabilities, but (7) itself also holds for the zero ideal. This proves the proper-sector estimate in (6).

We choose \(u_d=\chi_d d-4\log d\), \(U=L\mathbf1_{\{Z\le u_d\}}\), so \(|I|^{-1}\|U\|_2^2\le d^{-4}\). To obtain (5) on the moderate-deviation margin in (2), we need an \(L^2\) bound on the lost part **after** the positive projection \(\mathcal P_C\), rather than taking a square root of the entire discarded mass. We claim
\[
 \|\mathcal P_C(L-U)\|_\infty\le
 O(1)\exp\!\left(-\frac{(u_d-t_d d)^2}{4 t_d d}\right).
 \tag{8}
\]
Under (2) the gap \(u_d-t_d d>0\) eventually.

To see (8), fix arbitrary response coordinates and average over the \(I_0\)-phase translations independently across all \(k_d\ell\) base blocks. For a single block of \(d_0\) scalar coordinates, let \(W\) be its likelihood under a given phase shift. For \(a\ge0\) in any fixed compact range, uniformly in the unshifted coordinates,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm{phase}} W^{1+a}
       =1+d_0 a(a+1)b^2+O(b^3),\qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm{phase}} W=1+O(b^3).
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed for each coordinate \(f_b/g_b=1+2b\cos(2\pi y)+O(b^4)\). The phase average over \(I_0\) has zero mean for each cosine, mean \(1/2\) for its square, and zero cross products at distinct positions, as the relevant nonzero frequencies are unit monomials (also \(2\) times a monomial) or signed binomials, excluded from \(C_0\). The second-order expansion of \(W^{1+a}\) therefore leaves precisely the displayed coefficient. Normalizing the phase law in each block by \(W/\mathbb E W\) makes the logarithmic moment \(a(a+1)t_d d+O(1)\) for the full \(Z\): the summed \(O(b^3)\) error is \(O(\ell k_db^3)=O(1)\), uniformly, and the product of the block normalizers is \(O(1)\). Chernoff at \(a=(u_d-t_dd)/(2t_dd)\), which remains in a fixed compact range, yields (8).

Also \(\eta\le\|\mathcal P_C(L-U)\|_\infty\). By (2), the exponential factor in (8) is \(O(d^{-1/2-\varepsilon})\) for some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). Since \(m\) is the zero part of \(\mathcal P_C U\), orthogonal centering and (7) give
\[
 \|\mathcal P_C U-m\|_2
   \le \|\mathcal P_C L-1\|_2+\|\mathcal P_C(L-U)\|_2
   =o(d^{-1/2}).
\]
Now (6), Cauchy–Schwarz for TV and the uniform \(\eta\)-cost prove (5). All estimates use only exact uniform finite grids and the fixed product wrapped density.

## 4. The sharp radius of the one-row projected-label family

Let \(\nu'\) be the one-row reference with uniform \(A'\) and scalar response density \(g_{b'}\). For any fixed \(z,A'\), the true conditional density relative to the reference is
\[
 L'_z(A',y)=\prod_{i<d}\frac{f_{b'}(y_i-(A'z)_i/q)}{g_{b'}(y_i)}.
\]
In \(L^1(\nu')\), uniformly over \(z\), this differs from
\[
 1+2b'\sum_{i<d}\cos(2\pi(y_i-(A'z)_i/q))
 \tag{10}
\]
by \(O(d(b')^2+\sqrt d\,(b')^4)=o(b'\sqrt d)\). Indeed the centered scalar differences are orthogonal across supports under the product reference: each nonconstant factor \(f_{b'}/g_{b'}-1\) has mean zero and squared norm \(O((b')^2)\), so the norm squared of the support-at-least-two remainder is \(O(d^2(b')^4)\). The sum of the scalar first-order errors has norm \(O(\sqrt d\,(b')^4)\) since these too have mean zero. Grid invariance preserves each bound after the shifts.

An upper bound on the best radius \(\inf_H\max_{z\in J}\operatorname{TV}(H,Q_z)\) is obtained by taking \(H=\nu'\). Under \(\nu'\), after any fixed grid shift the cosine terms in (10) are independent with mean zero and variance \(1/2\). The central limit theorem and bounded normalized second moments give \(b'\sqrt{d/\pi}(1+o(1))\) as the corresponding TV, by (10).

For the matching lower bound, let \(H\) be any probability law on the output space. Take its density \(h\) relative to \(\nu'\) for the absolutely continuous part; a singular part can only add to the absolute deviation. Then
\[
 2\max_{z\in J}\operatorname{TV}(H,Q_z)
 \ \ge\
 \mathbb E_{\nu'}\,\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
                   \mathbb E_{z\sim U_J}|L'_z(A',Y')-c|.
 \tag{11}
\]
This follows by averaging over \(z\) and replacing the proposed density value \(h(A',Y')\) pointwise by the best constant. The right side can be restricted to unit \(A'\) at the cost of omitting a nonnegative contribution. The failure probability of a unit is \(O(e^{-\alpha d})\), since a nonunit vanishes in one residue field. On unit \(A'\), \(A'z\) is uniform on \(J\) when \(z\) is. We may replace \(L'_z\) by (10) in the right side up to \(o(b'\sqrt d)\), by the \(L^1\) bound and the reverse triangle inequality for the infimum.

For the random \(Y'\) under \(\nu'\), the following conditional limit holds:
\[
 \inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
 \mathbb E_{V\sim U_J}\left|
       \frac{\sum_{i<d}\cos(2\pi(Y'_i-V_i/q))}{\sqrt{d/2}}-c
                    \right|\longrightarrow \sqrt{2/\pi}
 \tag{12}
\]
in probability over \(Y'\), and also in expectation. To prove (12), decompose \(V\) into independent base-ideal blocks \(V_j\in J_0\) by (4). Each coordinate of \(J_0\) projects surjectively to \(\mathbb F_q\), since an ideal is closed under signed cyclic shifts and is nonzero. Conditional on \(Y'\), the cosine block sums are therefore independent and centered, and bounded by \(d_0\). Their conditional variances depend on disjoint \(Y'\) blocks and have mean \(d_0/2\): after averaging over \(Y'\), the individual cosine variances are \(1/2\) and cross products vanish. The sum of the conditional variances is \(d/2+o(d)\) in probability, by bounded independent variance averaging. Since the blocks are of fixed size, the conditional Lyapunov condition holds uniformly where the variance is at least \(d/4\). It follows on events of probability tending to one that the normalized conditional law converges to \(N(0,1)\). Its second moment is uniformly bounded since every conditional variance is at most \(d_0^2\). Weak convergence with this bound gives convergence of the first absolute moments, uniformly after truncating at large radius, hence convergence in Wasserstein-1; the map \(\mu\mapsto\inf_c\int|x-c|\,\mu(dx)\) is 1-Lipschitz in that metric. The normal value is attained at median zero. The same second-moment bound gives the stated expectation convergence. These arguments are uniform because only finitely many base ideals occur.

The factor from (10) and (12) is \(2b'\sqrt{d/2}\sqrt{2/\pi}=2b'\sqrt{d/\pi}\). Dividing (11) by 2 proves the matching lower radius. We have thus proved
\[
 \inf_H\max_{z\in J}\operatorname{TV}(H,Q_z)
       =(1+o(1))\,b'\sqrt{d/\pi}.
 \tag{13}
\]

## 5. From coarse experiment to deficiency

For any kernel \(K\), convexity over the same discarded label gives
\[
 \max_{s\in R_d}\operatorname{TV}(KP_s,Q_{\pi_J(s)})
 \ \ge\ \max_{z\in J}\operatorname{TV}(K\bar P_z,Q_z).
\]
By data processing and (5), \(K\bar P_z\) is at distance \(o(d^{-1/2})\) from the one fixed law \(K\nu\), uniformly in \(z,K\). Apply (13) to \(H=K\nu\). This gives the lower bound in (3), while the constant kernel whose output law is \(\nu'\) gives the same leading upper bound. In particular exact transport preserving the specified weak output noise is impossible under (2) for all sufficiently large \(d\), even with unrestricted computation or response-dependent changes to the public coefficients.

The error conclusion is on the informative scale of the weak target experiment. It is not a hardness statement for learning an unrestricted label, for output experiments with different dimensions, or for ordinary subfield/trace reductions with restricted input coefficients or altered errors. We make no claim at equality in (2).

## 6. Related results

* The definition (1) is directed Le Cam deficiency; see [Genon-Catalot, Laredo and Nussbaum, Sec. 2.3, Def. 1](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~nussbaum/papers/02-1.pdf). The use of contraction of TV is standard. The sharp conditional median radius (13) is specific to the Fourier and fixed-block structure above.
* [Our exact joint sample symmetries note](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/0493a4bb320cd5e2740381680810b5b326a672d6/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-joint-sample-symmetries.md) concerns bijective relabelings of one row, not proper CRT projections or many-to-one deficiency. The [public degree and TV note](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/1aaf3b754c946fbefcfadcdee20fd3ae10648a1b/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-coordinate-degree-and-tv.md) treats the full uniform label in a different statistical comparison. The proper-ideal Fourier identities and positive trimming have those antecedents; here the shared discarded component and output best-center radius are both essential.
* LPR's [original formal model](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/fac/cpeikert/pubs/ideal-lwe.pdf) provides the ring-learning setting. This is an artificial fixed-odd-prime broad wrapped experiment, not a statement about actual Ring-LWE parameter sets, security of a scheme, or an algorithm. The checker uses finite algebraic identities and synthetic probability diagnostics only.
