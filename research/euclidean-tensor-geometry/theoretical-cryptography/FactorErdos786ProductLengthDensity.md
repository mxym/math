# Sharp logarithmic density for distinct-factor product-length sets

Research note, 27 November 2026.

Say that \(A\subseteq\mathbb N\) has property \(P\) if two finite subsets of \(A\) with the same product always have the same cardinality. In particular, repetitions within either product are disallowed. Write
\[
 \overline d_{\log}(A)=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log x}
              \sum_{\substack{a\in A\\a\le x}}\frac1a,\qquad
 \overline d(A)=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap[1,x]|}{x}.
\]

**Theorem.** Every set with property \(P\) satisfies
\[
             \overline d_{\log}(A)<1/e.
\]
The supremum \(1/e\) is sharp, even among sets with ordinary natural density. More strongly, whenever \(\overline d_{\log}(A)>0\), one has \(\overline d(A)<1/e\).

### Literature and scope

- [Erdős's 1980 survey, printed p. 114](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1980-03.pdf) explicitly formulates property \(P\) using subsets, and reports a \(<1/e\) upper natural-density bound credited to Ruzsa without giving its proof. This note proves the unconditional logarithmic bound and the indicated conditional upper natural-density bound; it does not settle the upper natural density when the upper logarithmic density is zero.
- [I. Z. Ruzsa, *General multiplicative functions*, Acta Arith. 32 (1977), §§1, 4, 6](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa32/aa3241.pdf) proves a \(<1/e\) bound for nonidentity atoms of completely multiplicative maps into torsion-free abelian groups, Theorem (4.3)(b). Its Corollary (4.4)(b) gives the upper natural-density bound for the variant allowing repetitions: the additive representation in Statement (1.13) and §6 uses unrestricted multiplicities. We use Theorem (4.3), not an additive representation of \(A\).
- [S. Li, arXiv:2609.37471v1, Theorems 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37471v1) answers both original yes-or-no questions negatively in the distinct-factor setting, bounding upper logarithmic density by \(1/2\) and establishing an absolute gap for finite subsets. The lifting argument below extends Li's finite ratio-pairing idea to the quotient by all recurrent ratios. The Fourier step identifies a finite subgroup from positive concentration; compare Ruzsa (3.10) and (11.3). The remaining prime-bin transport step is what transfers the sharp nonzero-atom bound to ordinary upper density in the stated range.
- No claim is made about the sharp cardinality constant for finite \(A\subseteq[1,N]\), and no claim that the original questions are newly answered.

## 1. A quotient determined by recurrent ratios

A positive rational \(r\) is recurrent if there are infinitely many pairs \(x,y\in A\) with \(y=rx\). Let \(H\) be the subgroup of \(\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}\) generated by the recurrent ratios, and let \(f:\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}\to G=\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}/H\) be its quotient homomorphism. We write \(G\) additively. Inverses of recurrent ratios are recurrent.

**Lifting.** No nonempty set of distinct \(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell\in A\) has \(a_1\cdots a_\ell\in H\). In fact, write the product as \(r_1\cdots r_t\) with recurrent ratios \(r_j\ne1\). For each \(j\), choose a pair \((x_j,y_j)\) with \(y_j=r_jx_j\), avoiding all \(a_i\) and previous endpoints. Infinitely many pairs and a fixed ratio make this possible. Then
\[
 (a_1\cdots a_\ell)x_1\cdots x_t=y_1\cdots y_t
\]
contradicts property \(P\). If \(t=0\), the product is 1 and property \(P\) already excludes 1 from \(A\). In particular, if \(g\in G\) has finite order \(m\), there are at most \(m-1\) elements of \(A\) with \(f(a)=g\).

For any finite prime set \(P\), write \(n=ut\) with \(u\) \(P\)-smooth (all prime factors in \(P\)) and \(t\) \(P\)-free. Fix \(M\). For any two \(P\)-smooth \(u,v\le M\) with \(f(u)\ne f(v)\), only finitely many \(t\) have \(ut,vt\in A\): otherwise \(v/u\in H\). Thus, ignoring finitely many cores \(t\), the \(u\le M\) that occur in a fixed core all belong to one \(f\)-class.

## 2. Positive logarithmic density forces concentration modulo a finite group

Denote \(\rho_P=\prod_{p\in P}(1-1/p)\) and let \(\mu_P\) be the probability law on \(G\) obtained from independent geometric valuations \(V_p\), with \(\mathbb P(V_p=j)=(1-1/p)p^{-j}\), of \(\sum_{p\in P}V_pf(p)\). Equivalently, \(\mu_P(g)=\rho_P\sum_{u:\ f(u)=g}1/u\) over \(P\)-smooth \(u\).

For every finite \(P\),
\[
                       \overline d_{\log}(A)\le \|\mu_P\|_\infty.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, with \(u\le M\) the preceding same-core exclusion gives an upper bound \(\rho_P^{-1}\|\mu_P\|_\infty\sum_{t\le x,\,t\ P\text{-free}}1/t+O_{P,M,A}(1)\) for the harmonic mass. By finite inclusion-exclusion the core sum is \(\rho_P\log x+O_P(1)\). The discarded \(u>M\) contribute at most \((1+\log x)\sum_{u>M,\ u\ P\text{-smooth}}1/u\); after dividing by \(1+\log x\), the tail series tends to 0 as \(M\to\infty\).

Suppose \(\overline d_{\log}(A)=\delta>0\). On the compact character group \(\widehat G\) with normalized Haar measure, the Fourier absolute value of \(\mu_P\) is
\[
 |\widehat\mu_P(\chi)|=
 \prod_{p\in P}\frac{1-1/p}{|1-\chi(f(p))/p|}.
\]
By character orthogonality, each atom of \(\mu_P\) is at most \(\int_{\widehat G}|\widehat\mu_P|\). Let \(P\) increase to all primes; the integrals remain at least \(\delta\) by (1). The factors are at most 1, and writing the squared reciprocal of the \(p\)-factor as \(1+2p^{-1}(1-\operatorname{Re}\chi(f(p)))/(1-p^{-1})^2\) shows that the product tends to zero off
\[
 E=\left\{\chi:\sum_p\frac{1-\operatorname{Re}\chi(f(p))}{p}<\infty\right\}.
\]
Here the logarithm of \(1+s\) is bounded below by a positive constant times \(s\) on \(0\le s\le 8\). By dominated convergence \(E\) has positive Haar measure. It is a measurable subgroup: \(2(1-\operatorname{Re}z)=|1-z|^2\) on the unit circle, and the triangle inequality handles products of characters while inverses preserve the summands. A positive-measure subgroup of a compact group is open (continuity of translations of its indicator makes \(E E^{-1}\) a neighborhood of the identity) and has finite index. Its annihilator \(K=\{g:\chi(g)=1\text{ for all }\chi\in E\}\) in \(G\) is finite, since it embeds into the dual of the finite quotient \(\widehat G/E\).

To make a summability conclusion, use Haar probability on \(E\) and choose a sublevel set \(F\subseteq E\) on which the defining series is bounded by \(C<\infty\) and which has measure greater than \(3/4\). For \(g\notin K\), character orthogonality on \(E\) gives \(\int_E (1-\operatorname{Re}\chi(g))=1\). The integrand is at most 2, so its integral over \(F\) is greater than \(1/2\). Integrating the series over \(F\) gives
\[
                      \sum_{p:\ f(p)\notin K}1/p<\infty.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus positive upper logarithmic density forces (2) for some finite subgroup \(K\).

## 3. A prime-bin transport lemma

Let \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\) be sets of \(P\)-free integers for a finite prime set \(P\). Let \(\mathcal G\) be a set of primes avoiding \(P\) whose complement has finite reciprocal sum. Suppose for every \(i\ne j\) and \(p,q\in\mathcal G\) (allowed to be equal), only finitely many \(z\) satisfy \(pz\in C_i,\ qz\in C_j\). Then for any fixed real \(\tau_i>0\),
\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^m
       \frac{|C_i\cap[1,\tau_i X]|}{\tau_i X}\le\rho_P.
 \tag{3}
\]
For a proof, fix \(0<\eta<1\) and \(Z_b=3^b\), with finitely many bins \(1\le b\le B\) used initially. Put
\[
 \mathcal P_{i,b}=\{p\in\mathcal G:\tau_i Z_b<p\le\tau_i(1+\eta)Z_b\},
 \qquad
 \lambda_i=\sum_{b=1}^B\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,b}}1/p.
\]
Within each color these bins are disjoint. Let \(\omega_i(n)\) count the primes from their union dividing \(n\). For fixed \(B\), the mean and variance of \(\omega_i\) on \([1,\tau_i X]\) tend to \(\lambda_i\) and \(\sum_p p^{-1}(1-p^{-1})\le\lambda_i\), by the density of multiples of \(p\) and \(pq\). Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\sum_{n\in C_i,\,n\le\tau_i X}\omega_i(n)}{\tau_i X}
 \ \ge\ \lambda_i\frac{|C_i\cap[1,\tau_i X]|}{\tau_i X}
                  -\sqrt{\lambda_i}-o_B(1).
\]
For a fixed bin \(b\), write each summand pair as \(n=pz\). Then \(z\le X/Z_b\) is \(P\)-free. For all but finitely many \(z\) (for the fixed bin and its primes), at most one color can contribute at that \(z\), by the hypothesis. Its contribution, with weights \(1/\tau_i\), is at most \(\max_i|\mathcal P_{i,b}|/\tau_i\). Summing the last two observations bounds the limsup, as \(X\to\infty\), of \(\sum_i\lambda_i |C_i\cap[1,\tau_i X]|/(\tau_i X)\) by
\[
 \rho_P\sum_{b\le B}\frac{\max_i|\mathcal P_{i,b}|/\tau_i}{Z_b}
           +\sum_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}.
\]
The prime number theorem and partial summation give, with \(S_B=\sum_{b\le B}1/\log Z_b\to\infty\),
\[
 \sum_{b\le B}\frac{\max_i|\mathcal P_{i,b}|/\tau_i}{Z_b}
       \le(\eta+o(1))S_B,\qquad
 \lambda_i=(\log(1+\eta)+o(1))S_B.
\]
In the second relation, excluding primes outside \(\mathcal G\) removes only a bounded reciprocal sum. In the first, use the bound with all primes, and that there are finitely many fixed \(\tau_i\). Divide by \(\min_i\lambda_i\), first let \(B\to\infty\), and then let \(\eta\downarrow0\) to obtain (3). This is an ordinary-counting bound: the factors \(\tau_i\) are retained separately, so the estimate works at distinct fixed dilations of the same cutoff \(X\).

## 4. The nonzero-atom bound and sharpness

Assume (2), independently of how it was obtained, and let \(\bar f\) be \(f\) followed by quotienting out the whole torsion subgroup of \(G\). The target \(\bar G\) is torsion-free. Given \(\epsilon>0\), choose a finite \(P\) so that the reciprocal sum of primes outside \(P\) with \(f(p)\notin K\) is at most \(\epsilon\). By counting multiples these primes divide at most \(\epsilon x\) integers at any cutoff. After excluding them, \(f(n)\) takes values in the finitely generated group \(\langle K, f(p):p\in P\rangle\), which has finite torsion subgroup. By lifting, the \(n\in A\) in these finitely many torsion classes form a finite set. Thus, up to an upper density error \(\epsilon\), restrict to \(n=ut\in A\) with \(\bar f(t)=0\) and \(\bar f(u)\ne0\). Further restricting \(u\le M\) costs at most \(x\sum_{u>M,\,u\ P\text{-smooth}}1/u\) in counts.

For each nonzero \(g\in\bar G\) represented by a \(P\)-smooth \(u\le M\), let \(\mathcal U_g\) be the finite set of such \(u\) with \(\bar f(u)=g\). Let \(C_g\) be the \(P\)-free \(t\) for which \(ut\in A\) for at least one \(u\in\mathcal U_g\). Apply the transport lemma with \(\mathcal G=\{p\notin P:f(p)\in K\}\). Indeed, infinitely many \(pz\in C_g,\ qz\in C_h\) for \(g\ne h\) would give, by the finiteness of \(\mathcal U_g,\mathcal U_h\), infinitely many original pairs \(upz,vqz\) with fixed \(u,v\). Their ratio has nonzero \(\bar f\), and hence cannot be recurrent. This verifies all hypotheses.

For fixed \(x\), the remaining number of elements of \(A\) up to \(x\) is at most \(\sum_{g}\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_g}|C_g\cap[1,x/u]|\). On dividing by \(x\), each term has weight \(1/u\) times the ordinary density of \(C_g\) up to \(x/u\). For each \(g\) choose one \(u\in\mathcal U_g\) maximizing this density; the choices range over a finite collection, so (3) holds for their sum also when the choices depend on \(x\). The resulting upper density is at most \(\rho_P \max_{g\ne0}\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_g}1/u\). Letting \(M\to\infty\) and restoring the discarded primes gives
\[
       \overline d(A)\le\epsilon+\sup_{g\ne0}\bar\mu_P(g),
 \tag{4}
\]
where \(\bar\mu_P\) is the projection of \(\mu_P\) to \(\bar G\); an empty supremum here and below is 0.

By (2), \(\sum_{\bar f(p)\ne0}1/p<\infty\). The finite-prime laws \(\bar\mu_P\) thus converge in total variation to a probability law \(\bar\mu\): coupling geometric valuations at all primes costs at most the sum of \(1/p\) over omitted primes with \(\bar f(p)\ne0\). Each atom of the limit is the natural density of the corresponding level set of \(\bar f(n)\), by the finite-prime valuation densities and the same multiple-count tail bound. Exhaust the primes in (4), with \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), to get
\[
                         \overline d(A)\le\sup_{g\ne0}\bar\mu(g).
\]
Ruzsa's Theorem (4.3)(b) gives \(\bar\mu(g)<1/e\) at each nonzero atom since \(\bar f\) is completely multiplicative into the additive torsion-free target (the \(p_0=\infty\) case in his notation). A positive supremum of the atoms of a probability law on a countable set is attained, and hence is strictly smaller than \(1/e\). This proves the claimed upper natural-density bound under (2), in particular whenever \(\overline d_{\log}(A)>0\). In all cases the upper logarithmic bound follows: if it is positive, use \(\overline d_{\log}(A)\le\overline d(A)\) by partial summation; if it is zero, there is nothing to prove.

For sharpness, take the known Selfridge construction with the total selected-prime valuation equal to 1:
\[
 A=\left\{n:\sum_{p\in P}v_p(n)=1\right\}.
\]
The additive map ensures property \(P\) even with repetitions. Its density is \(\prod_{p\in P}(1-1/p)\sum_{p\in P}1/p\). By divergence of the prime reciprocal sum, one can choose larger and larger finite prime blocks with all primes tending to infinity and reciprocal sum tending to 1. This density approaches \(1/e\).
