# Explicit strict improvement after optimizing a DI tradeoff

All logarithms in an entropy or \(D_2^{\rm KL}\) below are base 2.
The subscript 2 in \(D_2^{\rm KL}\) denotes the **logarithm base**,
not a Rényi divergence.

## 1. Settings, acceptance and the two objectives

Let \(A_0,A_1\) and \(B_0,B_1\) denote Alice's and Bob's binary
observables. Their respective local input bits are \(x,y\); outputs are
\(a,b\in\{+1,-1\}\). A device receives only its own setting in a round;
the choice to test or generate is not furnished to the devices before
they output. The usual within-round tensor-product separation of the two
devices is assumed. Their state may otherwise have arbitrary
finite-dimensional quantum side information \(E\) and vary over rounds.

Consider the tilted Bell expression
\[
 {\cal I}=t\langle A_0\rangle+
 \langle A_0(B_0+B_1)+A_1(B_0-B_1)\rangle,\qquad
 t=\frac{48}{\sqrt{674}}.
\]
Define \(L=100/\sqrt{674}\), \(\tau=2^{-40}\),
\(\gamma=2^{-8}\), and \(q=1/8\).
Independently of the device state and of the past, use a test flag
\(T\sim\operatorname{Ber}(\gamma)\). In a test round \(x,y\) are uniformly
random; in a generation round \((x,y)=(0,0)\) with probability \(q\)
and \((1,0)\) with probability \(1-q\). In every round keep
\(A=(a,b)\). The public setting registers are \(T\) and \(B=(x,y)\).
The frequency register \(C\) holds \((x,y,a,b)\) in a test round and
\(\perp\) otherwise. In the paper's Definition 5.2, the common
instrument is \({\cal M}^{(a,b)|(x,y)}\) on the two devices'
memory, with \(R_{i-1},R_i\) allowed to contain both devices' local
memories. The test function is the deterministic concatenation
of the setting and output bits (\(d=4\)). Thus Equation (34) is
satisfied, with independent \(TB\) of the law just specified.
In particular the \(TB\) marginal is independent of all earlier
outputs and of the reference even on a correlated input, verifying
Definition 5.1. Classical outcomes can be generated by arbitrary
local instruments using binary POVMs. Retaining their output memories
does not change the current round's marginal \(AE\).

On test symbols define
\[
 \ell(x,y,a,b)=4(-1)^{xy}ab+2t\,1_{\{x=0\}}a.
\]
Its test expectation is \(\beta={\cal I}\). Indeed the four
correlators each enter once, and the positive tilt contribution is the
\(x=0\) Alice marginal by within-round no-signaling.
Let \(f(v)=1-v(\perp)\) for a probability distribution on \(C\).
Fix the compact convex frequency region
\[
 {\cal V}=\left\{v:\frac{\gamma}{2}\le f(v)\le\frac{3\gamma}{2},\
    \sum_{c\ne\perp}v(c)(\ell(c)-L+\tau/2)\ge0\right\}.       \tag{1}
\]
At \(n\) rounds acceptance is exactly \(\Omega_n=\{\operatorname{freq}
(C^n)\in{\cal V}\}\). We use the compact convex superset
\(\Delta_{\Omega_n}={\cal V}\) in Corollary 5.3, not its discrete
rational grid. At the independently repeated ideal strategy below,
\(f(p)=\gamma\) and \(\mathbb E[\ell\mid T=1]=L\), so the honest
one-round \(p=p_C\) is strictly inside both acceptance inequalities. Its
empirical frequencies pass with probability tending to one (bounded
i.i.d. sample means, with strictly positive margins). In particular
acceptance is possible for finite \(n\) and for all sufficiently large
\(n\). For example, at \(n=256\) the frequency consisting of 255
generation symbols and one \(x=y=0,a=b=+1\) test symbol is
accepted and has positive honest probability: the test symbol's
score is \(4+2t>L\).

Write \(s=\alpha-1>0\). For each device strategy including input state
and side information \(\tilde E\), let
\[
 h_z=H_\alpha^\downarrow(A|\tilde E)_{00},\qquad
 h_x=H_\alpha^\downarrow(A|\tilde E)_{10},
\]
where the subscripts denote fixed generation settings. Put
\[
 \begin{split}
 d_s &= -s^{-1}\log_2\!\big(q\,2^{-s h_z}+(1-q)2^{-s h_x}\big),\\
 p_s &= -\alpha s^{-1}\log_2\!\big(q\,2^{-(s/\alpha)h_z}
                           +(1-q)2^{-(s/\alpha)h_x}\big).
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{2}
\]
These are respectively WBF's unoptimized and partially optimized
generation entropies in Equation (35) and the two expansions following
it. The common environment reference in each conditional state is its
actual \(\tilde E\) marginal; only the weight on the public classical
setting varies in the latter entropy. The setting label \(T=0\) is fixed
in the generation channel and contributes no entropy. Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 h_{\rm down}(\alpha)
 &=\inf_{\mathcal M,R,\tilde E,\omega,\ v\in{\cal V}}
       \big\{s^{-1}D_2^{\rm KL}(v\Vert p)+v(\perp)d_s\big\},\\
 h_{\rm part}(\alpha)
 &=\inf_{\mathcal M,R,\tilde E,\omega,\ v\in{\cal V}}
       \big\{s^{-1}D_2^{\rm KL}(v\Vert p)+v(\perp)p_s\big\}.
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{3}
\]
In both expressions the strategy class is the same two-party
tilted-CHSH experiment with the prescribed test and generation setting
laws, arbitrary memories and arbitrary finite-dimensional input
\(\omega_{R\tilde E}\). \(D_2^{\rm KL}\) is infinite for \(v\) not
supported on \(\operatorname{supp}p\).
The second line is the displayed lower-bound objective on
\(h_\alpha(\Omega_n)\) of Corollary 5.3. The first is its comparator
displayed immediately before Equation (35), the formulation WBF
attributes to the published Arqand–Hahn–Tan formulation after dropping
the test rounds' entropy (see version locators in SOURCES). The comparison concerns these optimized scalar bounds,
not a claim of equality with the actual multiround entropy. We permit
all strategies in this protocol class, not merely honest or i.i.d.
ones and not merely qubits.

More precisely, the unrestricted enlargement in the printed
Corollary 5.3 ranges over all channels of the common-instrument form;
that enlargement would include nonlocal instruments and is not the
protocol-specific objective used here. The proof of Corollary 5.3
(Appendix C.6, Equations (85)–(87)) first uses the channel that
selects one of the actual round channels with an input flag. The
algebra converting (86) to the partially optimized objective applies to
this chosen channel before enlargement. Such a flagged channel for
our protocol has only two-party quantum output behaviors: copy the
classical flag to both devices before the setting draws, let each
local instrument act controlled on its flag, and use the corresponding
mixture of joint input states with reference. The state is an
arbitrary flagged classical mixture, still a joint quantum input
independent of the new settings. Thus our allowed class contains all
behaviors needed for the WBF proof in this protocol. Infimizing over
it bounds the fixed-round-channel expression from below. The two
generation-only objectives in (3) use exactly this same restricted
operational class throughout.

## 2. Dimension-free tilted-CHSH score certificate

Set \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), \(X=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0
\end{smallmatrix}\right]\), and \(Y=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-i\\i&0
\end{smallmatrix}\right]\). Arbitrary binary POVMs reduce to reflections
using one common dilation per party: for each observable \(O\) (a
Hermitian contraction), use the block reflection with blocks
\(O,\sqrt{I-O^2},\sqrt{I-O^2},-O\), and embed the state into the first
summand. Its compressed effects are the original effects, so both the
Bell score and conditioned outcome states on \(\tilde E\) are preserved.

For two reflections, each party's space decomposes into simultaneous
invariant subspaces of dimension at most 2. To see this, diagonalize the
unitary product of the two reflections. Conjugating it by either
reflection inverts it; orthogonal bases of each conjugate eigenvalue
pair are paired by that reflection, giving invariant 2-dimensional
blocks. In the real eigenvalue sectors the reflections coincide or
are opposite and have a common eigenbasis. In each Alice block we
therefore use \(A_0=Z,\,A_1=gZ+hX\) with \(g^2+h^2=1\), \(h\ge0\)
(choose the phase of the second eigenvector). On Bob's side,
\((B_0+B_1)/2\) and \((B_0-B_1)/2\) are anticommuting
with squares summing to \(I\), and in an irreducible block their squares
are scalar (they commute with both reflections). Hence
\(B_0=cZ+dX,\, B_1=cZ-dX\), \(c,d\ge0,c^2+d^2=1\); this follows
by diagonalizing the first sum and choosing the phase of the other
axis. The endpoints \(c=0,1\) and the 1-dimensional blocks are included
by embedding a simultaneous eigenvector in a 2-dimensional block with
the same two eigenvalues. With all outcome effects and the Bell
observable block diagonal, probabilities are the blockwise averages.
The following argument applies also after any measurement on
\(\tilde E\), by conditioning first on its outcome and then on the
device blocks.

On a single block the Bell operator is
\[
 M=t Z\otimes I+2c Z\otimes Z+2dg Z\otimes X+2dh X\otimes X.
\]
The Pauli products give
\[
 M^2=(t^2+4)I+4tc\,I\otimes Z+4tdg\,I\otimes X-8cdh\,Y\otimes Y.
\]
The three nonidentity matrices in the second line anticommute and
square to \(I\). With \(u=c^2\), \(k=h^2\), it follows that
\[
 \|M\|^2=t^2+4+4\sqrt{F},\qquad
 F=t^2+k(1-u)(4u-t^2).
\]
Here the nonidentity term has upper eigenvalue \(4\sqrt F\) since its
square is scalar and it has trace zero. For
\[
 u_*=\frac{4+t^2}{8}=\frac{625}{674},\quad
 G=\frac{(4-t^2)^2}{16}>\frac1{64},\quad F_*=t^2+G,
\]
completing the square yields the exact deficit
\[
 F_*-F=(1-k)G+4k(u-u_*)^2.                                  \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\|M\|\le\sqrt{8+2t^2}=L\). The ideal parameters
\(g=0,h=1,c_*=25/\sqrt{674},d_*=7/\sqrt{674}\) yield a Bell
operator \(M_*\) whose multiple \(\sqrt{674}M_*=48\,ZI+50\,ZZ+14\,XX\)
has eigenvalues \(100,0,0,-100\) (split by parity).
Its top unit vector is
\[
 \psi_*=(7|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt{50}.                   \tag{5}
\]
This proves exact attainability and identifies the relevant ideal
observables and probabilities. We now use a deliberately simple
quantitative stability bound, proved directly rather than importing
robust self-testing constants.

For a block state \(\rho\) write \(\eta=L-\operatorname{Tr}(M\rho)\).
If \(\eta\le2^{-10}\), then \(\|M\|\ge L-\eta>0\), and since
\(F_*\le4\) and \(L<4\),
\[
 F_*-F\le L^2-\|M\|^2 \le 8\eta .
\]
The first inequality is
\(L^2-\|M\|^2=4(\sqrt{F_*}-\sqrt F)\) with denominator
\(\sqrt{F_*}+\sqrt F\le4\) when rationalized (or both are equal).
By (4), \(g^2=1-k\le512\eta\), \(k\ge1/2\), and
\(|u-u_*|\le2\sqrt{\eta}\). From \(c_*>3/4,d_*>1/4\) we get
\[
 |c-c_*|\le3\sqrt{\eta},\quad
 |d-d_*|\le8\sqrt{\eta},\quad
 \|A_1-X\|=\sqrt{2(1-h)}\le32\sqrt{\eta}.
\]
Consequently \(\|M-M_*\|\le86\sqrt{\eta}\), by separating the
differences \(2(c-c_*)Z\otimes Z\) and
\(2(dA_1-d_*X)\otimes X\), with
\(\|dA_1-d_*X\|\le |d-d_*|+d\|A_1-X\|\). The gap between \(L\) and the next eigenvalue of
\(M_*\) is \(L>3\), so
\[
 T(\rho,\psi_*\psi_*^\dagger)
 \le\sqrt{1-\langle\psi_*,\rho\psi_*\rangle}
 \le \sqrt{(\eta+86\sqrt{\eta})/L}\le6\eta^{1/4}.             \tag{6}
\]
The trace-distance inequality here follows by decomposing \(\rho\)
into pure states and using convexity and the pure-state formula.
For Alice at \(x=1\), the effect difference from the ideal is at most
\(16\sqrt{\eta}\); each ideal outcome has probability \(1/2\).
For the pair \(++\) at \(00\), the effect difference is at most
\((3+8)\sqrt{\eta}/2\), since Alice's projector is the same.
Combining with (6) gives, for either Alice outcome and the \(++\)
pair respectively,
\[
 \left|P(a\mid x=1)-\tfrac12\right|\le22\eta^{1/4},\qquad
 \left|P(++\mid00)-P_*\right|\le22\eta^{1/4},
 \quad P_*=\frac{49}{50}\frac{1+c_*}{2}>\frac{24}{25}.        \tag{7}
\]
The strict last bound follows, for example, from
\(c_*>24/25\), giving \(P_*>2401/2500\).
For \(\eta>2^{-10}\) the inequalities in (7) hold trivially, since
their right side exceeds 1. For any state or ensemble of conditional
states, average (7) and apply concavity of the fourth root. Thus, writing
\(\delta=L-\beta\) for the actual strategy,
\[
 P(++\mid00)\ge P_*-22\delta^{1/4},\qquad
 P_{\rm guess}(a\mid x=1,\tilde E)\le\tfrac12+22\delta^{1/4}.  \tag{8}
\]
For the second claim, take any Eve POVM with its outcome labeled as
her guess; for each outcome apply (7) to the conditional device state
and that guessed sign. The average conditional Bell deficit is
\(\delta\) because the Eve measurement acts on a separate register
and is independent of the fresh input when conditioning on the fixed
generation setting. Taking the supremum of the success probability
proves (8). Guessing a pair is no easier than guessing its Alice
component: summing POVM elements over the guessed Bob label yields
an Alice guess POVM with at least the same success probability.

## 3. From the score to a uniform entropy separation

For completeness, if a CQ state at fixed settings has subnormalized
blocks \(\rho_a\) and marginal \(\rho_{\tilde E}=\sum_a\rho_a\),
the sandwiched fixed-marginal collision quantity satisfies
\[
 2^{-H_2^\downarrow(A|\tilde E)}
   =\sum_a\operatorname{Tr}
       (\rho_{\tilde E}^{-1/4}\rho_a\rho_{\tilde E}^{-1/4})^2
   =\sum_a\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_a M_a)
   \le P_{\rm guess}(A|\tilde E),
 \quad M_a=\rho_{\tilde E}^{-1/2}\rho_a\rho_{\tilde E}^{-1/2}. \tag{9}
\]
Inverses act on the support. The \(M_a\) sum to its projector and
complete to a POVM arbitrarily outside it. The equality uses cyclicity
of trace and says the collision quantity is this particular
measurement's success probability, not that it is optimal. By
order monotonicity of the sandwiched Rényi divergence,
\(H_\alpha^\downarrow\ge H_2^\downarrow\) for \(1<\alpha\le2\).
Both properties extend from a normalized reference to
\(I_A\otimes\rho_{\tilde E}\) by scaling, since its trace contributes
an order-independent logarithmic constant. Data processing discarding
\(\tilde E\) gives
\(H_\alpha^\downarrow(A|\tilde E)\le H_\alpha(A)\le H(A)\).
Thus at either fixed setting \(0\le h_x,h_z\le 2\).

If \(\delta\le\tau\), (8) gives \(P_{\rm guess}(a\mid1,\tilde E)
<17/32\) and \(P(++\mid00)>15/16\): the correction is at most
\(22/1024\). Then (9) and the pair guessing bound imply
\(h_x>9/10\) (use \(17^{10}<2^{41}\)). With at most four outcomes,
\(h_z\le H(A_{00})\le h_2(1/16)+(1/16)\log_2 3<1/2\),
where \(h_2\) in this sentence denotes binary Shannon entropy.
For the strict inequality, expand the first term as
\(1/4+(15/16)\log_2(16/15)\), use
\((16/15)^{15}<e<4\), and \(\log_2 3<2\). The entropy bound
as a function of the exceptional mass is increasing on
\([0,1/16]\), by the derivative of binary entropy, and the other
three outcomes have entropy at most \(\log_2 3\).
In particular \(|h_x-h_z|>3/8\) uniformly for all near-maximal
strategies, including their side information, and every \(1<\alpha\le2\).

Let \(U\) take the values \(2^{-(s/\alpha)h_z}\) and
\(2^{-(s/\alpha)h_x}\) with probabilities \(q,1-q\). Then \(U\in[1/2,1]\)
for \(0<s\le1\), and from (2),
\[
 p_s-d_s=\frac{\ln\mathbb E U^\alpha-\ln(\mathbb E U)^\alpha}
                 {s\ln2}.
\]
This is nonnegative. The curvature of \(v^\alpha\) on \([1/2,1]\) is
at least \(\alpha s\). Therefore
\(\mathbb E U^\alpha-(\mathbb E U)^\alpha
\ge\alpha s\,\operatorname{Var}(U)/2\).
For \(0<b\le a\le1\), \(\ln(a/b)\ge a-b\). The mean value derivative
of \(2^{-(s/\alpha)h}\) in \(0\le h\le2\) gives
\(\operatorname{Var}(U)\ge q(1-q)(s\ln2/(2\alpha))^2|h_x-h_z|^2\).
These imply
\[
 p_s-d_s\ge\frac{s^2\ln2}{8\alpha}q(1-q)|h_x-h_z|^2
         \ge\frac{s^2}{32}q(1-q)|h_x-h_z|^2.                 \tag{10}
\]
We used \(1/2<\ln2<1\). In the near region \(q(1-q)>1/16\) and
\(|h_x-h_z|^2>1/8\), so \(p_s-d_s>s^2/4096\).

## 4. The strict gap persists under the full optimization

If \(\delta\le\tau\), then \(v(\perp)>1/2\) by (1) and
the two objectives at the same \((\mathcal M,\omega,v)\) differ by
at least \(s^2/8192\). Thus the second is at least
\(h_{\rm down}(\alpha)+s^2/8192\).

If \(\delta>\tau\), put \(g(c)=\ell(c)-L+\tau/2\) on test symbols
and \(g(\perp)=0\). Then \(\|g\|_\infty<12\):
\(|\ell(c)|\le4+2t<8\) and \(0<L-\tau/2<4\). By the score
constraint, \(\mathbb E_v g\ge0\) but
\(\mathbb E_{p}g=\gamma(\beta-L+\tau/2)<-\gamma\tau/2\).
So \(\|v-p\|_1\ge\gamma\tau/24\) and Pinsker in bits yields
\[
 D_2^{\rm KL}(v\Vert p)\ge \frac{\|v-p\|_1^2}{2\ln 2}
                   \ge\frac{\gamma^2\tau^2}{1152}.
\]
This includes \(D_2^{\rm KL}=+\infty\). Since
\(s\le2^{-109}\), \(\gamma^2\tau^2=2^{-96}\) and
\(8192/1152>4\), the KL term divided by \(s\) is at least 4.
The two entropies in (2) are nonnegative, while
\(h_{\rm down}(\alpha)\le2\) by plugging in the ideal device and
\(v=p\in{\cal V}\). The second objective is therefore again at least
\(h_{\rm down}(\alpha)+s^2/8192\). Infimizing yields the claim,
without requiring either infimum to be attained. It is the same
\(\alpha\), event, KL divergence and acceptance penalty in both
displayed accumulation expressions, so substituting the improved
tradeoff increases that expression by at least \(n\,2^{-13}s^2\)
when its conditional event has positive probability. No comparison of
the independently order-optimized final extraction bounds follows
from this statement.

### Ideal check at order 2 (not the optimized bound at order 2)

In (5) at \(00\) the outcome collision is
\(C_z=(1201/1250)(1299/1348)\): Alice's \(Z\) law has mass
\(49/50\) on plus and Bob has conditional matching probability
\((1+c_*)/2\) in either branch. At \(10\) it is
\(C_x=(1+576/674+2401/(674\cdot625))/4\):
the four probabilities are determined by the three moments
\(0,\,24c_*/25,\,7d_*/25\); orthogonality of the signs gives the
sum of squares. The exact local gap is
\[
 \log_2
 \frac{q C_z+(1-q)C_x}{(q\sqrt{C_z}+(1-q)\sqrt{C_x})^2}>0.
\]
It vanishes for only one generation setting, or when the two
conditional collision probabilities coincide. This order-2 example
does not replace the uniform accepted-region optimization in Section 4.

### Reusable part of the argument

The acceptance transfer needs no special feature of tilted CHSH:
for a bounded Bell score and \(v(\perp)\) bounded away from zero,
a fixed uniform positive difference between two fixed-setting
entropies near the maximal score yields a positive gap of order
\(s^2\) in (10) for bounded outcome alphabet. A test threshold strictly
between that near-region cut and the honest maximal score creates a
constant Pinsker barrier for far strategies. At sufficiently small
positive \(s\) the barrier dominates any common alphabet-size entropy
upper bound. The calculation here supplies all hypotheses explicitly,
with a finite-score experiment and arbitrary finite-dimensional quantum
side information.
