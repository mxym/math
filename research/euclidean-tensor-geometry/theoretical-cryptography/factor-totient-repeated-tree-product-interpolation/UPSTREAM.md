# Prior ingredients and limits

* L. Bruni Bruno and W. Erb, [*Polynomial Interpolation of Function Averages on Interval Segments*, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.00328). They use one zero per nonoverlapping interval to prove uniqueness from linear interval means. The intermediate-value and sign-change principles are classical; here they apply to inequalities forced by positive discrete products, not to known linear interval means. The ordered sign-word calculation is a root-counting certificate, not a claim to have invented the intermediate value theorem or ordinary polynomial interpolation.
* C. Adelmann and A. Winterhof, [*Interpolation of Functions Related to the Integer Factoring Problem*](https://doi.org/10.1007/11779360_12): lower bounds on interpolating functions such as the totient on composite inputs. This is not the recovery of one particular monic polynomial of component roots from a given single numerical totient.
* D. J. Bernstein, [*Factoring into coprimes in essentially linear time*, §§7,9,13](https://cr.yp.to/lineartime/dcba-20040404.pdf), [published version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgor.2004.04.009): the natural coprime basis groups proportional valuation vectors. Valuation-ray partitioning is prior; the slower explicit gcd formula is included for self-containment. Erdős, Luca and Pomerance, [*On the proportion of numbers coprime to a given integer*, §2](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/gcdnphin14.pdf), study the reduced ratio phi(n)/n; Ford, Konyagin and Luca, [*Prime chains and Pratt trees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0473), study the classical p | q-1 relation.
* Źrałek, [v5, §6, printed p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102v5), records earlier single-totient cases; Morain, Renault and Smith, [*Deterministic factoring with oracles*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444v2), distinguish a value from general oracle access and give further size-sensitive cases. Hittmeir and Pomykała, [*Deterministic Integer Factorization with Oracles for Euler's Totient Function*](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2020-1891), assume the *prime-power decomposition* of the totient, stronger than one numeric value.
* Preceding work in this line: [one-totient valuation-rank criterion, fixed artifact](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/1e0839692cc219956052192c8b38cff000b8a9a4/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-totient-valuation-rank). The repeated-template interpolation here can apply even with incidence rank one and more than two components. The current proof does not rely on a mutable file or require the other artifact to run.
* J. Renegar, [*On the Computational Complexity and Geometry of the First-Order Theory of the Reals*, Part I](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/305759aa-f544-4006-aa85-f70ddce54e00/content), supplies the fixed-dimensional existential real decision step.

The proposed result is the conversion from these special one-totient inputs to grouped positive polynomial data plus an anchor, with a sufficient combinatorial certificate for unique reconstruction. The bounded comparison above is not proof of worldwide priority or optimality.
