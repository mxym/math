# Primary sources and scope

1. László Csirmaz, [*The Size of a Share Must Be Large*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/secretsharing/sizelarge.pdf), Journal of Cryptology 10 (1997), 223–231, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001459900029). §3, Theorem 3.2, defines the ordered subset and tail-prefix access structure. Lemmas 3.3–3.4 give the perfect whole-distinguished-set entropy chain and its bound \(2^k-1\). These are the origin of the access structure and telescope.
2. Shahram Khazaei, [*Reverse Substitution in Secret Sharing: The Critical One-Half Threshold and a Route to Superpolynomial Lower Bounds*](https://opencryptology.org/2026/2652/versions/20261111T084015Z.pdf), ePrint 2026/2652, fixed 11 November 2026 version. §2.2 defines the normalized contribution vector; Lemma 2.1 supplies the unit-share operation; §4.1 Eq. (24) and Appendix A give the whole-set bound and optimal total on the distinguished set \(A=I_k\). Appendix A, Proposition A.1, gives the linear perfect constructions for each ordering of the geometric costs on any specified group of participants. Appendix B describes the whole perfect \(\mathcal C_2\) convec region and linear realizations. These perfect constructions and the scalar bound are not new here. The present note obtains the all-subset projected refinement, then the non-perfect prefix inequalities, the four-player entropic frontier, and the critical-order classification. It does not resolve the full inner-capacity or reverse-factor questions of §§7–9.
3. Tarik Kaced, [*Almost-perfect secret sharing*, arXiv:1103.2544v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.2544), §2, definitions 2.2–2.3. The finite normalized residual-entropy and mutual-information caps used here are exactly Definition 2.2, and the zero-residual specialization is Definition 2.3. Our use of a closure is coordinatewise on the resulting share entropy projection; it is not a claim about error-probability-based security or the equivalence of asymptotic models.
4. Oriol Farràs, Torben Brandt Hansen, Tarik Kaced, and Carles Padró, [*On the Information Ratio of Non-Perfect Secret Sharing Schemes*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-016-0217-9), Algorithmica 79 (2017), 987–1013; [ePrint 2014/124](https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/124), author-manuscript text [reproduced here](https://paperzz.com/doc/8943319/on-the-information-ratio-of-non-perfect-secret-sharing-sc...). §5, Proposition 5.5 gives the general access-function submodularity relation underlying our (6); §§6–7 discuss minors/duality and the optimal information ratio for uniform access functions. A fixed access function assigns the information of every set, whereas our model has two caps and allows nonuniform profiles. We use the known submodularity rule, and contribute its truncated Csirmaz-chain consequence and tight attainment in the stated scopes, not a new entropy inequality.
5. Amir Jafari and Shahram Khazaei, [*Partial Secret Sharing Schemes*](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2023.3265093), IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 69(8) (2023), 5364–5385, [ePrint 2020/448](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/448). Their partial information ratio normalizes by the advantage gap, and their equivalence for linear sharing does not specify the raw two-cap coordinate frontier or rank of the restricted \(A\) part of a Csirmaz structure. We do not call the (non-perfect or partial) models new.
6. Maki Yoshida, [SCIS 2026 programme, session 4E1-2](https://www.iwsec.org/scis/2026/program.html), talk on share sizes realizing Csirmaz access structures. The public Japanese abstract analyzes improved asymptotic gaps for existing schemes, including costs on tail participants and total shares. We did not access a separate full paper and do not make claims about it beyond the abstract. Our exact projected entropic caps do not require any smallness of the tail shares.

7. Shahram Khazaei, [*General versus linear secret sharing under statistical security*, ePrint 2026/1646](https://opencryptology.org/2026/1646), 12 November 2026 version metadata and public abstract. This concerns separation of linear/perfect and asymptotic statistically secure convec regions on five participants. It is distinct from the raw finite entropic caps and ordered-prefix projection treated here; the linked fixed PDF was not readable in this check.

We did not find a matching result in the sources examined. This is a bounded search, not an absolute priority claim.
