# Sparse aggregate certificates for prime-relation graphs of unrestricted slope rank

A public fixed polynomial-time rule lists rational slopes m≥1 at bit length L. Distinct prime divisors p<q of the input integer N are adjacent if |q−mp|≤L^c for at least one listed slope; c is fixed. Neither the graph, a factorization, nor selected edge labels are supplied. We specify a canonical labelled spanning tree of the connected graph, root it at its least prime, multiply or divide slopes along paths, then multiply all vertex scales with their actual prime multiplicities. This gives one distinguished positive rational C.

**Theorem.** If the valuation vector of C is in the rational span of at most h listed slope vectors (fixed h), class membership and the entire factorization are computable in deterministic polynomial bit time from N alone. There is no cap on dictionary multiplicative rank, rank of the selected edge slopes, number of vertices, or multiplicities. The fixed parameter h concerns representation of the one aggregate relative to the listed vectors; it does not refer to the tautological rank of a single rational number.

An elementary gcd-refinement constructs pairwise-coprime atoms for the slopes without prime factorization. Enumerating bounded *integer vectors in each candidate rational subspace*, rather than integer powers of the chosen generators, includes possible rational-span saturations. Once a seed prime has been found, ordinary graph exploration and direct product checks certify the result and allow the canonical-tree condition to be tested. A 12-prime example forces six independent edge labels on its unique tree while the aggregate has a one-label certificate. A separate unconditional construction gives unbounded separation.

These are thin structural classes, not unrestricted factoring. The prerequisites, hypotheses and distinction from the previously established globally low-rank case are stated in PROOF.md and UPSTREAM.md.
