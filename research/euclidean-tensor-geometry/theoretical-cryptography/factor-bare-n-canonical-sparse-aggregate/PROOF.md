# One sparse aggregate in a high-rank dictionary

## 1. A membership predicate independent of hidden choices

Fix h,c≥0 integers. For each binary input length L a fixed deterministic algorithm on 1^L enumerates a finite set S of positive rationals m≥1, in poly(L) bit time. Reduce them, remove duplicates, put them in increasing rational order, and let B≥1 be the maximum numerator or denominator bit length (1 if S is empty). E=L^c. No rank restriction on S and no supplied gcd/prime basis or factorization are assumed.

For N≥2 join distinct prime divisors p<q when |q−mp|≤E for at least one m∈S; assign the least such m to this edge. A singleton is connected. If connected, order edges lexicographically by their pair (p,q), form the spanning tree by accepting each edge in order unless it closes a cycle, and root the resulting tree at the least prime P. For an oriented edge from smaller p to larger q with assigned slope m, require c_q=m c_p; with c_P=1 this defines positive rational scales, dividing by m on reverse traversals. Given the true exponents n_v=v_(p_v)(N), let
    C=product_v c_v^n_v .
For a positive rational z write val(z)=(v_ell(z)) over rational primes ell, with finite support. Say N belongs to the class if its graph is connected and val(C) is in the Q-linear span of val(m) for at most h elements m of the public list. For h=0 the span is {0}. This definition refers to one canonical tree and one canonical label per edge, not to unverifiable existence of an arbitrary good tree. A single nontrivial val(C) is not automatically sparse relative to the given list.

**Theorem.** The class is recognizable, and its full prime-power decomposition computable, in deterministic time polynomial in L using N alone. No bound on the number of support primes, their exponents, dictionary rank, or rank of the canonical tree's labels is imposed.

## 2. Explicit rational coordinates by gcds, not prime factorization

Start with the set A of distinct numerator and denominator integers >1 of all slopes. Represent each original integer as a product of nonnegative powers of elements of A. While two distinct a,b in A have g=gcd(a,b)>1, replace them by g,a/g,b/g, discarding 1 and merging all equal values. Rewrite each current representation: old exponents u,v on a,b contribute u+v to g, u to a/g, and v to b/g. Add contributions to any pre-existing or coincident entry. Fix a deterministic ordering for pairs and the resulting bases, e.g. numerically increasing. No primality test is needed here; this is a basic version of classical factor refinement.

The potential sum_(a in A) log_2(a) drops by at least log_2(g)≥1 at each replacement, before accounting for additional merges. Initially it is at most 2 |S| B. At most that many replacements occur. Every base is a divisor of an original input integer, has at most B bits, and the number of bases is bounded by the potential since every base ≥2. Every nonnegative exponent in a representation is ≤B: the product always equals its original integer. Greatest common divisors, pair scanning, representation rewrites and sorting thus use polynomial bit time. At termination the bases b_1,...,b_t are pairwise coprime positive integers >1. The reduced slope m has integer coordinate vector v(m) in Z^t given by numerator coordinates minus denominator coordinates, each absolute value ≤B.

The map v↦product_j b_j^{v_j} on Z^t is injective: at a prime dividing b_j, only that base has nonzero valuation. Its map into rational prime-valuation space is a Q-linear injection on Q^t, by the same argument. Consequently the sparse-span membership condition in §1 is exactly the same if checked in these t coordinates instead. All actual aggregate coordinates are in this integer lattice, without any need for the primes that divide the bases to be found.

## 3. Listing candidate aggregates without enumerating the high-rank word ball

Let s=omega(N) count distinct primes, D=Omega(N)=sum n_v count them with multiplicities; both ≤L. Along the latent tree c_v is a (signed) product of at most s−1 slope letters. Thus C is represented by a word of length at most (s−1)D<L², even if the full word ball is huge. In gcd coordinates w=v(C) therefore satisfies |w_j|≤R:=L² B. Unlike global low-rank enumeration, we never construct that full ball.

Enumerate each subset of at most h labels; keep independent columns or replace by a basis subcollection. For an independent choice of j columns form the t by j integer matrix A0. If j=0, list w=0. Otherwise choose j pivot rows I such that the square matrix A_I is nonsingular; exact rational elimination finds them. Enumerate every z in the integer box [-R,R]^j. Solve A_I u=z over Q and compute w=A0 u. Keep w when all entries are integers and bounded in absolute value by R. Turn it into the exact positive rational C'=product b_i^w_i; duplicates may be kept. Every integral vector of the chosen rational span in the specified t-dimensional box appears: its pivot coordinates determine it uniquely. The coefficients u need not be integers. No claim that every enumerated w is realized by a slope word or a graph is necessary. Requiring membership only in the subgroup generated by the chosen j labels would in general omit candidates.

The pivot argument in isolation applies to any polynomially enumerable family of rational subspaces of bounded dimension with polynomial-bit bases, intersected with this bounded integer box. Here the family is concretely specified by the actual dictionary, which is essential to the stated promise.

There are at most (1+|S|)^h subcollections times a constant depending on h, and ≤(2R+1)^h pivot tuples per subcollection. The ambient dimension t is polynomial, each input entry has magnitude ≤B, and j≤h is fixed. Pivot coefficients by Cramer's rule, and reconstructed w, have polynomial bit length, even before the integrality/bound checks. The numerator/denominator of C' have bit lengths ≤1+R sum_i log_2 b_i, polynomial by §2. This entire enumeration uses polynomial bit time on all inputs, independently of the dictionary's true multiplicative rank. The true C on a member is among its outputs.

## 4. The minimum is near one of the computable centers

First include as seed candidates all integers in [2,min(N,4LE)]. Suppose for the correctness proof the true minimum P>4LE. On a true edge p<q with its canonical slope m≥1, |q/(mp)−1|≤E/P. Thus |log(q/(mp))|≤2E/P. Summing with signs on paths of length ≤s−1≤L gives
    |log(p_v/(c_v P))| ≤ 2LE/P < 1/2.
Using |exp(u)−1|≤2|u| on this interval yields |p_v/c_v−P|≤4LE. The positive number (N/C)^(1/D) lies between the extreme p_v/c_v since it is their positive multiplicity-weighted geometric mean.

For each candidate C' from §3 and D'=1,...,L compute J=floor((N/C')^(1/D')) by exact integer power comparison after clearing the rational denominator. Doubling and binary search suffice, with comparisons on polynomial-bit integers: the search is bounded by 1+max(1,N/C'), and D'≤L. Also test as seeds the integer candidates from J−4LE through J+1+4LE, clipped to [2,N]. On a member the true minimum occurs in a tested list, whether small or large. The number of seed candidates over all lists is polynomial.

Retain only a seed that is prime and divides N. From each reached prime t and each m enumerate all integer prospective neighbors larger than t in [mt−E,mt+E], and those smaller than t in [(t−E)/m,(t+E)/m]. Exact ceiling, floor, strict ordering and clipping to [2,N] are used; each interval has at most 2E+1 integers because m≥1. Keep new candidates exactly if they are prime divisors of N satisfying the defining inequality. Breadth-first search visits precisely that prime's component by induction on graph distance, of size ≤L. Repeated exact division by the distinct visited primes recovers whole exponents. Require residual 1; this certifies the complete factorization and that the graph is connected.

With that unique prime-power factorization available, recompute the full graph, least witnessing labels, lexicographic spanning tree, rooted scales (equivalently add or subtract their coordinate vectors) and the exact aggregate w. By ordinary rational linear algebra test whether w is in the span of any subset of size ≤h; there are polynomially many tests at fixed h. Accept only after this last condition holds. If it fails on a certified full decomposition, membership is false independently of the seed; otherwise, if no seed path certifies the predicate, reject. A possibly successful seed outside the promise never falsely certifies a member. All arc, arithmetic and primality tests have polynomial bit costs. This establishes recognition as well as factoring, not merely a promise calculation. For comparison, an existential choice of noncanonical tree with sparse aggregate also guarantees the factor-search phase succeeds when all possible witnesses are allowed in the correctness argument; this fact alone does not establish recognition of that different predicate.

## 5. Strict separation, including unbounded separation

Let u>v_1,...,v_j be distinct rational primes. Consider a support with anchor x, a vertex near ux, vertices near v_i x and vertices near (u/v_i)x. When its only edges are x→y with label u, x→a_i with v_i, and b_i→y with v_i, the tree and witnesses are unique. Its rooted scales are 1,u,v_i,u/v_i. With unit multiplicities away from x, C=u^(1+j), no matter how large the multiplicity at x. The rank of labels actually required by the unique spanning tree is j+1. Thus sparsity here is a cancellation between branches and does not mean there is a low-rank spanning subtree. The finite 12-vertex test in RESULTS.txt realizes j=5 for error E=L, with P>4LE, a fixed dictionary of six prime labels and unit multiplicities.

Here is a distinct, unconditional existence statement with *arbitrarily high* necessary edge rank for a single fixed public rule. Take S_L to be all rational primes ≤L, which are uniformly enumerable in polynomial time, c=2 and h=1. For any fixed j select the primes u,v_i above. Let x tend to infinity over primes. For each of the other finitely many distinct scales in T={1,u,v_i,u/v_i} choose a prime at distance O_j(x^0.525) from the target scale times x, using the theorem of Baker, Harman and Pintz on short intervals. The implicit constants can depend on this fixed choice of labels. In particular the vertices are eventually distinct, with least vertex x. Give x multiplicity floor(x^(3/10)/log x), all others multiplicity one, using natural logarithms. Their product has binary length L asymptotic to x^(3/10)/log 2, because the fixed number of other log-prime terms is O_j(log x). Then E=L² is eventually much larger than the O_j(x^0.525) errors on each intended edge, and 4LE=o(x). All intended prime labels are in S_L once x is large.

There can be no further edges for large x, nor an alternative witnessing label on an intended edge. Ratios of the finitely many distinct target scales have only the following integer values greater than one: u (between 1 and u), and each v_i (between 1 and v_i or between u/v_i and u). This follows by uniqueness of factorization into the distinct primes u,v_i. Every other ordered ratio is a fixed positive distance from the set of integers; realized prime ratios converge to their target ratios. Since E/x tends to zero, any witnessing integer multiplier must equal the indicated integer ratio, and a prime multiplier moreover belongs to the prescribed dictionary. Thus the true graph is exactly the displayed tree. This gives rank j+1 at will while the aggregate has a one-label certificate. The use of large multiplicity inflates L: these examples do **not** show a superpolynomial lower bound for all trial-division cutoffs, or existence of unusually short prime gaps beyond the cited short-interval theorem. They separate two mathematical promises. No hardness claim is made.

If the full dictionary has rank at most h, a basis of its span can be chosen from at most h actual dictionary columns. Every canonical aggregate belongs to that span automatically. Thus at the same fixed h the earlier globally low-rank result is contained here; the examples prove that the converse fails, even if one considers the ranks of labels actually necessary for connectivity rather than the full dictionary. The general §4 sparsity count of the earlier low-rank result does not use low rank and applies here as well (fixed c and polynomial-size public dictionary, at least two distinct primes). These remain thin classes; no improvement is asserted for unrestricted integer factorization.
