# Prior methods and the narrower increment

* **Gcd factor refinement.** Daniel J. Bernstein, [*Factoring into coprimes in essentially linear time*](https://cr.yp.to/lineartime/dcba-20040404.pdf), J. Algorithms 54 (2005), 1–30, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgor.2004.04.009), gives fast algorithms for natural coprime bases (notably §7 and Algorithm 13.2); Kousha Etessami, Alistair Stewart and Mihalis Yannakakis, [*A note on the complexity of comparing succinctly represented integers*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.5429), discuss power-product equality and its arithmetic algorithms. Our short gcd-refinement proof does not claim to improve those algorithms, output prime factors of labels, or invent coprime bases. Nor is fixed-dimensional pivot-row enumeration a new linear-algebra algorithm.
* **Sparse structural input in integer factorization.** Manindra Agrawal, Nitin Saxena and Shubham Sahai Srivastava, [*Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), discuss sparse polynomial dependencies between two factors and supplied-relation elimination. Sparsity here is of one multiplicative aggregate in the rational span of dictionary label vectors, not sparse monomials in a two-factor polynomial or knowledge of a numerical dependence. Ford, Konyagin and Luca, [*Prime chains and Pratt trees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0473), analyze the classical p|(q−1) relation. We do not invent prime chains or present a distribution estimate for those chains. [Źrałek](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-09-02262-5) studies deterministic methods involving smooth shifted prime factors and totient reductions; no smoothness of p−1 is assumed here. Weighted-geometric-mean ideas in factoring, including the older two-variable setting, are standard (see [Hales and Hiary](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-00959-7)).
* **Strict improvement over the related support-graph result.** In the [prior globally bounded-multiplicative-rank note](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/a46f95e18045533780f74e03d32626e64f18b4e4/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-unbounded-low-rank-slopes) in this repository, the entire public dictionary had rank ≤h and one enumerated its complete bounded-radius rational word ball. Its geometric-mean seed bound, seed-to-component traversal and sparsity count are reused with attribution, not claimed twice. Here the *complete* rational word ball may be too large. Gcd-free coordinates make it possible to enumerate only projections of a bounded-coordinate cube onto an unknown relevant rational span and then certify the canonical aggregate from the actual factorization. All connected inputs in the earlier rank-h scope have such a certificate, but even the necessary edge-label rank of our examples is unbounded.
* **Short prime intervals for a promise separation only.** R. C. Baker, G. Harman and J. Pintz, [*The difference between consecutive primes, II*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/83.3.532), Proc. Lond. Math. Soc. 83 (2001), 532–562, establish that a sufficiently large x has a prime within O(x^0.525). Apply this separately at each of finitely many targets for each fixed number of branches, not as a conjecture about simultaneous values of a prime tuple with specified constant offsets. It is used solely for the unbounded-rank separation, not in the recognition algorithm. [Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781) supply the standard deterministic primality test in the bit bound.

A bounded search compared small-dependency factoring, prime-chain structure, sparse multiplicative relations and rational power-product/gcd-free methods. No matching arbitrary-dictionary canonical-aggregate theorem was identified. This is not a claim of exhaustive priority or of general sparse-subspace recovery. The connected relation and sparse aggregate are both substantive restrictions.
