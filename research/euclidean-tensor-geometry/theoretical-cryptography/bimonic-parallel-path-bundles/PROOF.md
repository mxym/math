# Public parallel-path bundles on prime-support graphs

## 1. Public catalogue and promise

Let n>=2 and L=bitlength(n). On the length L alone a deterministic public rule outputs a dense catalogue of integer bivariate polynomials, positive in both partial degrees, together with a list of bundles of oriented words in those relations. Each use of a catalogue entry may swap its variables. Both partial leading coefficients of every relation are nonzero integer constants; we call this bimonic. The dense encoding size of the relations and the total word/label encoding size of all the bundles are polynomial in L, as is generation time. Each bundle contains at least two words, possibly of different lengths, and at least one nonempty word. Some words may be empty. Write d>=1 for a public upper bound on partial degree, h for maximum word length, and assume d^h <= (L+1)^C for a fixed constant C. Thus degrees of growing words and their branching remain within a uniform polynomial. All word lengths are also polynomial by explicit encoding. No separate bound on the number of words per bundle is needed beyond total encoding size. In particular, the public rule lists the permitted bundles; the algorithm does not guess subsets of all words of polynomial size.

The graph of n has its distinct prime divisors as vertices, with an undirected edge between distinct primes if one of the two oriented relation equations holds. A prime path of type w follows the specified oriented labels; at every step its vertices must be primes dividing n and consecutive vertices distinct. It may revisit vertices. The empty path has its starting vertex as endpoint.

For a word w let P_w(T,Y) denote its endpoint polynomial, defined explicitly below. For a fixed bundle w_1,...,w_r, write P_i=P_{w_i}. Call the bundle *finite* if the P_i have no common root in an algebraic closure of Q(T), as polynomials in Y. Equivalently their gcd in Q(T)[Y] is one up to units. The structural promise is that n's graph is connected and at least one public finite bundle has an actual common starting prime p and an actual common ending prime q in that graph: every word in the bundle is realizable by a prime path from p to q. The endpoints p and q may coincide, and different paths do not have to agree or be disjoint away from their endpoints. The graph, endpoints, path choices, which bundle works, and all multiplicities are unknown; the sole instance input is n.

**Theorem.** Complete prime factorization on this promise and exact membership recognition both have deterministic bit complexity polynomial in L. No conclusion is made about the difficulty of n outside the promise.

## 2. Exact endpoint computation from a fixed word

For a path of type F_1,...,F_m, start with P_0(T,Y)=Y-T, then set P_{j+1}(T,Y)=Res_U(P_j(T,U),F_{j+1}(U,Y)). Use this unnormalised recursion, including for the empty word. For any fixed complex T=t the leading U-coefficient of P_j(T,U) is a nonzero constant, as is that of the second input. By the resultant root-product formula the result is a nonzero scalar times the product, over all roots u of P_j(t,U) with repetitions, of F_{j+1}(u,Y). Each factor has its full Y-degree and a constant nonzero leading coefficient. This proves the constant-leading induction and exact endpoint sets after every specialization. In particular every actual path from p to q gives P_m(p,q)=0. Repeated roots do not create extra endpoint values.

Here are bit bounds also allowing a growing word. At step j, both the T- and Y-degrees of P_j are <=d^j. In the next Sylvester determinant each term has e<=d entries from P_j, the only entries depending on T, and m_j<=d^j entries from the edge relation, the only entries depending on Y. This proves the degree recursion directly. If b bounds the bit length of one plus the coefficient l1 norm of a catalogue polynomial, the same determinant bounds the norm of the next P by

  ||P_{j+1}||_1 <= (m_j+e)! ||P_j||_1^e ||F_{j+1}||_1^{m_j}.

With B_j=log2(1+||P_j||_1), iterating gives B_j<=d^j poly(j,b,log(d+1)), also when d=1. All are polynomial in L by d^h and the explicit encoding length. One can compute each resultant by evaluating on a grid for the two degree bounds, taking exact integer determinants there and interpolating; entries, determinant bits, grid size and rational interpolation bit work are polynomial by these bounds. This proof is specific to the path transfer, not a generic solver in h variables.

## 3. From many endpoint equations to a finite anchor polynomial

Work first over Q(T), with algebraic closure used for roots. Let M=deg_Y P_1 (at least one). For a nonnegative integer lambda set

  B_lambda(T,Y)=sum_{i=2}^r lambda^{i-2} P_i(T,Y).

The exponent-zero term is taken as one also at lambda=0. If the bundle is finite, for each root xi of P_1 over the algebraic closure at least one P_i(T,xi), i>=2, is nonzero. Hence B_lambda(T,xi) as a polynomial in lambda is nonzero of degree<=r-2. At most M(r-2) integer lambda values can be excluded across the at most M distinct roots of P_1. Thus among 0,...,M(r-2) there is a lambda for which

  A(T)=Res_Y(P_1(T,Y), B_lambda(T,Y))

is not identically zero. The case r=2 uses just lambda=0. If a B_lambda happens to be the zero polynomial, it cannot give a certificate and is skipped; a nonzero B_lambda of Y-degree zero gives its usual constant-input resultant. Conversely if the P_i have a generic common root, every such resultant vanishes identically or has its second polynomial zero. Hence the finite-bundle condition is testable by this finite deterministic search.

Fix the first nonzero A for an accepted public bundle. At any common finite endpoint (p,q), both P_1(p,q) and B_lambda(p,q) vanish, so A(p)=0. The conclusion holds even if some leading coefficients cancel in B_lambda or a specialization drops its degree: the Sylvester determinant with formal degrees vanishes in the presence of a common finite root. Equivalently P_1 has constant nonzero leading coefficient and its specialised root-product formula still has a zero factor when B_lambda(p,q)=0. A may have extra roots. It is sufficient as a finite superset, and one nonzero resultant per bundle is enough.

The number of lambda tests is polynomial in L even if r grows, since M and the explicit bundle size are polynomial. Coefficients lambda^{i-2} have bit length O(r log(2+M r)); all B_lambda norms therefore have polynomial bit size. Both T- and Y-degrees are bounded by d^h, so the resultant determinant for A has polynomial size, degree in T and bit height. Evaluation/interpolation or standard univariate coefficient arithmetic computes it in polynomial time. No r-fold Cartesian enumeration of word-tree choices is involved in the algebraic part.

## 4. Verifying actual prime paths and complete factorization

For each accepted bundle, list the integer roots of its nonzero A. Polynomial-time univariate factorization over Q finds the linear factors, from which integer roots can be read; retain only positive primes dividing n. For each retained p, list the integer roots of P_1(p,Y), whose leading coefficient is a nonzero constant. Keep only positive primes q dividing n. Check each possibility q against the *entire* bundle. To verify one word of length m, start at p and follow its prescribed oriented equations by enumerating roots of univariate specialisations: there are at most d choices per step, and after discarding nonprimes, values not dividing n and self-steps, the word qualifies iff q can occur at its end. The empty word is accepted iff p=q. Each step has polynomial bit cost because visited primes have at most L bits and the dense relation degrees/heights are polynomial. Each word visits at most (h+1)d^h path states before any merging, which is polynomial. Words can be tested separately for the common q, so the cost per candidate endpoint pair is a **sum** over words, not the product of their path state counts.

For a promised n this search includes its actual finite bundle and its actual p,q. Conversely if it returns a pair satisfying all words, the actual prime-path condition is certified, not inferred solely from a resultant. Starting from any one certified p, traverse the actual graph by listing both F_s(v,Y) and F_s(Y,v) integer roots at each discovered prime v, keeping only new primes dividing n. Every visit is to a verified prime divisor, so at most L are visited. The resulting set is exactly the connected component of p; repeated values or different labels on the same pair make no difference. Exact divisions recover the full multiplicity of every visited prime. If the residue is a unit, the factorization and connectivity are certified, and the bundle witness has already been checked. If it is nonunit, the graph is not connected; if no pair was certified, the bundle condition failed. These conclusions establish both directions of exact membership. This is an abstract complexity proof, not an algorithm for an unrestricted target or an input-taking implementation in this package.

## 5. Strict separation from single closed words and fixed bundle width

Three paths of length one suffice for a sharp small comparison. Set f_1=2X+1, f_2=3X-4, f_3=X+1, and have three catalogue relations with root sets in Y

  {f_1,f_2}, {f_2,f_3}, {f_3,f_1},

each encoded by the monic product of the corresponding two linear factors Y-f_i(X). Both leading partial coefficients are nonzero constants. All three relations hold from 5 to 11, but none in that original orientation from 11 to 5. Over Q(T) each pair has a common generic affine branch, yet the three share no generic endpoint. A complex common endpoint can occur only if two of the branches coincide, at T in {0,5/2,5}. The only positive integer prime source in that set is 5, whose common actual endpoint is 11. Taking the first endpoint polynomial as P_1, the choice lambda=1 gives determinant
  -(f_1-f_2)^2 (f_1-f_3)(f_2-f_3).
All its asserted sample identities involve just this one finite example.

In the support {5,11} any actual closed word alternates forward and reverse steps. Each adjacent pair of relations along such a word has a common affine branch that is an invertible map over Q. On an arbitrary complex starting value T, each consecutive pair can be realized by following that shared branch and its inverse, keeping T unchanged after the pair. This constructs a closed complex path for every value of T, regardless of which prime was the true start. Hence every such individual closed word has identically zero diagonal, including words longer than the three catalogue entries. The three-path anchor is not a repackaging of a nonzero diagonal from one actual closed walk.

The arity can also be necessary in a length-uniform catalogue. For each L let r=max(3,L), f_i(X)=i(X-5)+11, 1<=i<=r, and let F_j be the product of Y-f_i(X) over i!=j. List one bundle of all r one-edge forward words. Each F_j has partial degrees r-1 and constant nonzero leading coefficients in both variables; the linear factors have coefficient norms O(r), so norms are <=(O(r))^{r-1}. With O(r^2) possible coefficients of O(r log r) bits per polynomial, the full r-polynomial dense encoding is O(r^4 log r) bits. It can be constructed by repeated polynomial multiplication in polynomial bit time. Here h=1 and d^h=r-1.

Take n of support {5,11}, with any positive multiplicities, so L may grow. Every label holds from 5 to 11; none holds in its original orientation from 11 to 5. The entire bundle has a generic empty endpoint intersection, since at every X other than 5 the f_i are distinct and each is absent from one relation. At X=5 all f_i are 11; the only common affine endpoint pair is (5,11), giving an actual certificate. Every actual single closed word still has identically zero diagonal even if it uses all r labels: every pair of leave-one-out relations shares a branch, so the earlier two-step return argument applies. More strongly, *any* common-endpoint bundle of actual paths that collectively uses fewer than r distinct labels has a generic common endpoint. Indeed choose i absent as a label: its branch f_i is then present at every step. All actual paths from the same source to the same endpoint have the same parity of length and alternate directions. Choosing f_i and its inverse successively gives the same generic endpoint for all of them (T for even length, f_i(T) or f_i^{-1}(T) according to the direction for odd length). Empty paths also have endpoint T. Thus no uniformly bounded total length of such a witness suffices for this public family. This separates certificate types, not computational hardness; these constructed integers themselves are elementary.

If one starts with just a relation alphabet and a fixed r bound, one may take all tuples of at most r words of length <=h as the public list when (2sd)^{rh} is polynomial in L. Variable arity above requires the public bundle list, not an assertion that one can find an arbitrary large subset of words by brute force. The single-word diagonal is recovered at the level of certificates by taking a pair of words (a nonempty word and the empty word). Both observations distinguish the new promise from a stronger unrestricted existence search.
