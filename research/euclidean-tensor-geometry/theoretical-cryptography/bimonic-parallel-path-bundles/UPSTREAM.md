# Primary comparisons and provenance

* [Short bimonic cycle anchors](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/c085586a3b5f3642a312b0ac1e254a135f278d05/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/bimonic-short-cycle-anchors/PROOF.md), §§2-4, supplies the affine endpoint convention (including constant leading coefficients), the growing-word bit bounds and the single-seed support traversal. The classic sources for iterating correspondences by resultants and taking a closed-path diagonal are Patrick Morton, [Solutions of diophantine equations as periodic points of p-adic algebraic functions. I](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2016/22-33v.pdf), NYJM 22 (2016) 715-740, §3 p.727 equations (11)-(12), and Rin Gotou, [Dynamical Systems of Correspondences on the Projective Line I](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.06394#S4.SS2), arXiv:2109.06394v1, §4.2. There is no new claim for the path-transfer eliminants themselves.
* Doug Ierardi and Dexter Kozen, [Parallel Resultant Computation](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~kozen/Papers/resultant.pdf), in J. Reif (ed.), Synthesis of Parallel Algorithms, 1993, pp.679-720, §20.2.4, Lemma 20.2 and Theorem 20.10, Algorithms 20.3 and 20.5. They give the power-weighted combination of several polynomials and the degree bound controlling bad coefficient specializations. The elementary bounded integer search used here is the deterministic application of that fact in characteristic zero, not a new resultant algorithm. The increment is applying it to publicly listed path bundles with a shared actual prime endpoint, verifying each path independently, and separating this structural witness from every single closed word and from bounded label width.
* General integer polynomial factorization over the rationals is standard: A. K. Lenstra, H. W. Lenstra Jr. and L. Lovász, [Factoring polynomials with rational coefficients](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf), Math. Ann. 261 (1982), 515-534. Deterministic primality is standard: M. Agrawal, N. Kayal and N. Saxena, [PRIMES is in P](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781).
* M. Agrawal, N. Saxena and S. S. Srivastava, [Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), MFCS 2016, article 6, pp.6:3-6:4, discuss supplied relations for a prime and its cofactor, already an input for elimination. This note does not receive any actual relations with their endpoints as instance advice; the permitted word bundles are fixed by the bit length. János Kasza, [On the graphs of primes determined by linear functions](https://doi.org/10.71352/ac.43.181), 2014, §1, studies cycles in a graph of prime divisors of linear evaluations, rather than simultaneous exact root correspondences. These comparisons locate the specific increment without claiming universal complexity or exhaustive priority.
