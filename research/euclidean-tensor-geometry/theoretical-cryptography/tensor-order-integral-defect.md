# Integral defects in the canonical tensor product of number fields

The tensor product of the canonical Euclidean spaces is the right real space for a compositum when the degrees multiply. It need not identify the corresponding integral lattices. This note gives an exact criterion prime by prime, and the full list of local elementary divisors whenever at most one factor is wildly ramified there. Two fully worked wild examples show why knowing the individual ramification indices is insufficient without that assumption.

This is an algebraic comparison of rings and lattices, not an analysis of concrete schemes or a computational reduction.

## Set-up and statements

Let $K_1,\ldots,K_t$ be number fields, let $M$ be their compositum and assume that the multiplication map $\bigotimes_{i,\mathbb Q}K_i\to M$ is an isomorphism. Write

$$
A=\mathcal O_{K_1}\cdots\mathcal O_{K_t}\ \subseteq\ B=\mathcal O_M
$$

where $A$ denotes the additive group of finite sums of products of integers from the factors, or equivalently the image of their integral tensor product. For a rational prime $p$, the following hold.

1. **Local equality.** $A\otimes\mathbb Z_p=B\otimes\mathbb Z_p$ if and only if at most one factor $K_i$ is ramified over $p$. Thus global equality is equivalent to pairwise coprimality of the absolute field discriminants. This criterion does not assume tame ramification.
2. **Elementary divisors.** Suppose that at most one factor has a wildly ramified prime above $p$. For each tuple of primes $\mathfrak p_i\mid p$, write $e_i,f_i$ for their ramification indices and residue degrees. Take the multiset

$$
\left\{
   \left\lfloor\sum_{i=1}^t a_i/e_i\right\rfloor:\quad 0\le a_i<e_i
 \right\},
\tag{1}
$$

each entry repeated $\prod_i f_i$ times, and concatenate over all tuples. If the resulting exponents are $s_j$, the local inclusion has Smith entries $p^{s_j}$, including $p^0=1$. In particular its local index valuation is $\sum_j s_j$. For the map on additive groups modulo $p^b$, the kernel and cokernel are each abstractly $\bigoplus_j\mathbb Z/p^{\min(s_j,b)}\mathbb Z$; this is not a claim of a canonical ring decomposition of either one.

For two factors with tame ramification or just one wild factor, the nonzero exponent is always 1. For a given tuple, it occurs $\tfrac12(e_1e_2-e_1-e_2+\gcd(e_1,e_2))\prod_i f_i$ times. This is the count of pairs $a_1/e_1+a_2/e_2\ge1$: among pairs with both entries nonzero, reflection in both coordinates interchanges strict inequalities and fixes the set of $\gcd(e_1,e_2)-1$ equality pairs. For any number of factors satisfying the hypothesis of (1), the defect is killed by $p^{t-1}$.

## The special-fiber criterion

Pass from $\mathbb Z_p$ to the complete unramified DVR $V=W(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$, with uniformizer $p$ and algebraically closed residue field $k$. This is faithfully flat and preserves the multiset of Smith exponents, since an entry $p^s$ stays $p^s$ times a unit. For each local completion of a factor, the unramified part splits into $f_i$ copies. In each copy the remaining ring is a DVR, generated over $V$ by a uniformizer with an Eisenstein polynomial of degree $e_i$ (base change of the Eisenstein presentation over the maximal unramified subfield). The same holds independently for $B$. Thus the integral closure after this base change is exactly the base change of $B$.

Modulo $p$, the resulting DVR of degree $e$ gives $k[z]/(z^e)$. On each tuple of factors the tensor ring modulo $p$ instead has the form

$$
k[z_1,\ldots,z_t]/(z_1^{e_1},\ldots,z_t^{e_t}),
$$

which is local, with cotangent space at its maximal ideal of dimension $\#\{i:e_i>1\}$. For $B/pB$ every local summand has cotangent dimension at most one. If two factor fields are ramified at $p$, choose a tuple with $e_i>1$ for both: the special fibers cannot be isomorphic, so the index is divisible by $p$. If at most one factor field is ramified at $p$, every tensor tuple over $V$ is already a DVR, since its other factors are copies of $V$. Therefore the order is already the full local integral closure, which proves the criterion.

## Computing the Smith entries

Fix one tuple over $V$. Use the possibly wild DVR as the base $D$, with field of fractions $F$, ramification index $e_0$ over $V$, and uniformizer $\varpi$. If there is no wild factor, use $D=V$, $e_0=1$, and $\varpi=p$. Each other factor is tame, has index $e_i$ prime to $p$, and is generated by an $e_i$th root of $p$: an arbitrary uniformizer raised to $e_i$ differs from $p$ by a unit, and all units have $e_i$th roots by Hensel's lemma and the algebraically closed residue field. Consequently the tuple's order can be written over $D$ as

$$
C=D[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/(x_i^{e_i}-\varpi^{e_0})_{i=1}^m.
\tag{2}
$$

In fact $p/\varpi^{e_0}$ is a unit of $D$ with $e_i$th roots, so the scalings of the tame generators that give (2) are changes by units. Degree-one factors cause no change.

Let $\ell=\operatorname{lcm}_i(e_i/\gcd(e_i,e_0))$, and use $\ell=1$ if there are no remaining factors. Put $w_i=e_0\ell/e_i\in\mathbb Z$ and adjoin $z$ with $z^\ell=\varpi$. This is Eisenstein unless $\ell=1$ and its integer ring is $D[z]$; all $\ell$th and $e_i$th roots of unity are in $D$. These roots are distinct mod $\varpi$. The group $\mu_\ell$ embeds in $H=\prod_i\mu_{e_i}$ by $\omega\mapsto(\omega^{w_i})_i$: the orders of the coordinate maps have least common multiple $\ell$. Therefore the possible geometric tuples $(h_i z^{w_i})_i$ split into distinct orbits indexed by the quotient $H/\mu_\ell$, and the generic fiber of (2) is one copy of $F(z)$ per orbit. This can also be seen by taking the evaluation map over $F(z)$, where all these roots are distinct and the Chinese remainder theorem applies. The normalization is consequently one copy of $D[z]$ per orbit.

Take representatives $h$ of these orbits. The monomial $x^a=\prod_i x_i^{a_i}$, $0\le a_i<e_i$, maps to the tuple

$$
\left(\prod_i h_i^{a_i}\right) z^{r(a)} \varpi^{t(a)},\qquad
r(a)=\sum_i a_iw_i\bmod\ell,\quad t(a)=\left\lfloor e_0\sum_i a_i/e_i\right\rfloor .
$$

For a fixed residue $r$, choose one index $a_*$ with $r(a_*)=r$. The residue map on the character group $\prod_i\mathbb Z/e_i$ is surjective (restriction to the embedded $\mu_\ell$), and differences $a-a_*$ in this fiber give exactly the characters of $H/\mu_\ell$. The coefficient matrix $(\prod h_i^{a_i})_{h,a}$ of the $z^r$ components is therefore an invertible character table times unit row factors. Its inverse is given by character orthogonality, with denominator $|H/\mu_\ell|$ prime to $p$. Grouping the different $r$ blocks now gives the $D$-module diagonal entries $\varpi^{t(a)}$. This step computes the elementary divisors, not just their product.

Finally a local quotient $D/\varpi^sD$ has $V$-elementary exponents
$\lfloor(s+b)/e_0\rfloor$ for $b=0,\ldots,e_0-1$. Indeed the Eisenstein basis $1,\varpi,\ldots,\varpi^{e_0-1}$ has distinct valuations mod $e_0$; to obtain valuation at least $s$, its $b$th coefficient must be divisible by $p^{\lceil(s-b)/e_0\rceil}$ when that power is positive. Reverse the index $b$ to get the stated floors. Upon substituting $s=t(a)$, the exponents become precisely $\lfloor b/e_0+\sum_i a_i/e_i\rfloor$. This proves (1) including the possibly wild factor. Restoring the $\prod f_i$ copies and descending the Smith list gives the asserted global count.

For example, if $t$ linearly disjoint quadratic fields are all ramified at an odd prime $p$, (1) gives the exponent $\lfloor j/2\rfloor$ with multiplicity $\binom tj$. At $p=3$, the fields $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3),\mathbb Q(\sqrt{15}),\mathbb Q(\sqrt{21})$ give exponents $0$ with multiplicity 4 and $1$ with multiplicity 4. Their squareclasses are independent, which ensures the hypothesis on the compositum.

## Why there is a wild caveat

At $p=2$, compare two pairs with the same residue and ramification indices in each factor: $e_1=e_2=2$, $f_1=f_2=1$.

- For $\mathbb Q(i)$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, the compositum is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)$. With $\zeta=\zeta_8$, its integer basis is $1,\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3$. The tensor basis is $1,\zeta^2,\zeta-\zeta^3,\zeta+\zeta^3$, and has exponents $(0,0,0,1)$.
- For $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})$, put $a=\sqrt2$, $b=\sqrt5$, $\theta=(1+b)/2$. The field $\mathbb Q_2(b)$ is unramified quadratic because the reduction of $\theta^2-\theta-1$ is irreducible; $a$ then has Eisenstein polynomial $X^2-2$. The local integer basis is $1,a,\theta,a\theta$. The tensor basis $1,a,ab,2b$ becomes $1,a,2a\theta,4\theta$ after integral column additions. Its exponents are $(0,0,1,2)$.

Thus local equality still fails as predicted, but both the index and its annihilator can differ for the same $(e_i,f_i)$. This is not a contradiction to the at-most-one-wild formula.

## Canonical geometry and source comparison

For simple tensors the canonical real inner product is multiplicative: every tuple of complex embeddings occurs once because the degree of the compositum is the product of degrees. Summing the products of embedding pairings with their complex conjugates factors into the product of the sums for the separate fields. By linearity the ambient real map is an isometry for the tensor product of the canonical Euclidean structures. The defect above is purely integral. It cautions against passing from an ambient isometry to an integral basis or additive quotient identification without checking the index.

[Lyubashevsky–Peikert–Regev, A Toolkit for Ring-LWE Cryptography, §1.2](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/fac/cpeikert/pubs/toolkit.pdf) use the prime-power cyclotomic tensor decomposition with distinct conductor primes: those factors have disjoint ramification, so no defect occurs there. [Pedrouzo-Ulloa–Troncoso-Pastoriza–Pérez-González](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.05244) consider tensor products more generally; our hypothesis is specifically that the generic tensor is a *single field*, and the calculation is about equality between two different integral orders in it. The technical precedents are classical tame normalization after a uniformizer extension, for example [Abhyankar's lemma and tame ramification](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EXT), together with the coprime-discriminant integral basis theorem. The scoped contribution here is the character-block calculation of all additive elementary divisors under the stated hypothesis, not the construction of the generic tensor product or of its tame components. This comparison is not an exhaustive priority claim or a statement about RLWE hardness.

The accompanying verifier checks the exact floor counts, character blocks over small residue fields, and the two wild example matrices. It does not test actual cryptographic instances.
