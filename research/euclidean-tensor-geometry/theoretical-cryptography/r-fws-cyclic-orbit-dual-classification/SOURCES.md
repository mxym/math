# Primary sources and scope

- **[CPZ]** Chiara Castello, Olga Polverino and Ferdinando Zullo, [*Full weight spectrum one-orbit cyclic subspace codes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2024.106005), JCTA 212 (2025), 106005; [arXiv:2405.19911v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19911v1). Their Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 4.12 give the ordinary FWS classification and family distinction, Corollary 2.6 gives the generated-field degree as an equivalence invariant, and Proposition 3.3 gives the trace-power identity. These foundations are known, not new here. Section 7's final question defines \(r\)-FWS by trailing zeros and asks for its classification, without making the positive-\(r\) reduction.
- **[SS]** Minjia Shi and Wenhao Song, [*New characterization of full weight spectrum one-orbit cyclic subspace codes*, arXiv:2506.13418v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13418v1). Section 4 Theorem 4.6 gives the printed two-family \(r\)-FWS classification; §5 asks for the corresponding exact linear equivalence. The font/notation variant for the direct sum is resolved by reading Lemma 4.1 and its use in the proof. We show a missing family independently of that notation and independently of any interpretation of how the distance-two multiplier is chosen. Their Theorem 2.4 on the ordinary second-family weight distribution is separate; the present note does not claim a new distribution theorem or audit every formula in their Section 4.
- **[SO]** Minjia Shi and Ferruh Özbudak, [*Linear equivalence of full weight spectrum cyclic orbit codes*, arXiv:2611.04947v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04947v1). Theorem 1.1 classifies ordinary FWS equivalences under the full linear group; Lemma 3.3 gives the trace-power identity and the proof following it uses trace duality *within the generated field* for the ordinary boundary \(t=2h-1\). Section 5 claims resolution of the ordinary FWS equivalence question from [CPZ], not the \(r>0\) classification or equivalence question. Our proof applies ambient trace duality and the exact diameter to transfer their full criteria to \(r>0\).
- **[CGLPZ]** Chiara Castello, Heide Gluesing-Luerssen, Olga Polverino and Ferdinando Zullo, [*Quasi-optimal cyclic orbit codes*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-025-01751-4), §4 Proposition 4.1. The trace-dual isometry of cyclic orbit codes, including preservation of the entire distance distribution, is known. The ratio witness for nonzero intersection is also standard orbit-code geometry, explicitly used in [SO, Lemma 2.1]; the note uses these tools to fix the trailing-zero classification rather than claim new general duality.

We checked the article and latest accessible arXiv records through 2026-11-28, and searched the exact [SS] title and identifier with “r-FWS”, “trace dual”, “Theorem 4.6”, “erratum”, and “equivalence”. A later public seminar announcement uses the same title but supplies no revised theorem. We found no public correction or equivalent positive-\(r\) classification in this bounded search; the correction is expressly pinned to the public v1 text. This is not an absolute priority claim. All statements concern abstract cyclic Grassmann codes.
