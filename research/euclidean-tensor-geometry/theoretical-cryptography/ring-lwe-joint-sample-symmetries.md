# Rigidity of exact single-row relabelings in wrapped Gaussian ring learning

## 1. The statistical experiment and symmetry group

Let \(q\ge3\) be odd, \(d\ge1\), and
\[
 R=(\mathbb Z/q)[X]/(X^d+1),\qquad
 \Omega=R\times\mathbb T^d,\qquad
 f_b(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z} b^{n^2}e^{2\pi i n y},\quad 0<b<1 .
\]
Use coefficient coordinates; \(p_b(y)=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}f_b(y_j)\). For a label \(s\in R\), the row law \(P_s\) on \(\Omega\) consists of uniform \(A\in R\) and \(Y=As/q+E\) on the torus, where \(E\) has the fixed wrapped spherical Gaussian density \(p_b\). We consider one arbitrary Borel Markov kernel \(K:\Omega\to\Omega\) that can change **both** the coefficient and the response, with any dependence on the observed row and added randomness. It cannot depend on \(s\).

**Theorem.** For a permutation \(\psi:R\to R\), the following are equivalent.
1. \(P_sK=P_{\psi(s)}\) for all \(s\), for some single-row kernel \(K\).
2. There exist \(v\in R\), \(u\in R^\times\), and \(l\in(\mathbb Z/2d)^\times\) with
   \[
     \psi(s)=v+u\,\sigma_l(s), \qquad \sigma_l(X)=X^l .
     \tag{1}
   \]
The representation in (1) is unique, so the group of these label symmetries has \(q^d|R^\times|\varphi(2d)\) elements. In particular, the result applies to power-of-two cyclotomic rings, but does not require \(d\) to be a power of two, \(q\) to be prime, or broad noise.

For each permutation outside (1) and each fixed \(q,d,b\), there is a strictly positive lower bound on \(\inf_K\max_s\operatorname{TV}(P_sK,P_{\psi(s)})\), given in Section 2. The bound is not claimed uniform as \(q,d,b\) vary. This result rules out a class of exact same-width sample transformations irrespective of computation time. It is not a claim about cryptographic schemes, multirow reductions, changing widths, or search algorithms.

**Sufficiency.** For each \(l\) as in (1), \(\sigma_l\) is a signed permutation of the coefficient basis, and is a ring automorphism (use the inverse exponent modulo \(2d\)). Thus it acts also on the torus, preserving \(p_b\). The deterministic transform
\[
 A'=\sigma_l(A)u^{-1},\qquad Y'=\sigma_l(Y)+A'v/q
\]
has uniform coefficient and precisely the correct shifted response. Uniqueness follows by evaluating at \(0,1,X\) (for \(d=1\), the exponent group is trivial), since the \(2d\) powers of \(X\) are distinct for odd \(q\).

## 2. Posterior equality and an error gap

Put \(N=q^d\) and \(\mu=N^{-1}\sum_sP_s\). Let \(\eta_s(a,y)=\Pr[S=s\mid A=a,Y=y]\) under the uniform prior, and write \(\eta(x)\) for its vector. The density of \(\mu\) has full support, and all entries of \(\eta\) are positive continuous functions, since \(f_b\) is positive. Put
\[
 \mathcal C=\{\eta(x):x\in\Omega\},\qquad
 \psi^*w=(w_{\psi(s)})_{s\in R}.
\]
The set \(\mathcal C\) is compact. If \(K\) satisfies item 1, then under \(S\to Z\to Z'\), where \(Z\mid S\sim P_S\), the posterior at \(Z'\) is \(\psi^*\eta(Z')\) and \(Z'\sim\mu\). Therefore conditional expectation carries \(\eta(Z)\) to \(\psi^*\eta(Z')\) with equal total squared norm in expectation, forcing equality almost surely. In particular \(\eta(Z)\in\psi^*\mathcal C\) almost surely.

We will show in Sections 3–5 that even the latter inclusion is enough for (1). Already it gives a robust formulation. For any candidate \(K\) with \(\max_s\operatorname{TV}(P_sK,P_{\psi(s)})\le\delta\),
\[
 \delta\ \ge\ \frac12\int
       \operatorname{dist}\bigl(\eta(z),\psi^*\mathcal C\bigr)^2\,d\mu(z).
 \tag{2}
\]
In fact compare the two joint laws of \((S,Z')\), the first generated by \(K\) and the target with \(Z'\mid S\sim P_{\psi(S)}\). They are within \(\delta\) in TV. The squared loss \(\|\mathbf e_S-\psi^*\eta(Z')\|_2^2\) lies in \([0,2]\), so its expectation under the first law is at most the initial Bayes risk \( \mathbb E\|\mathbf e_S-\eta(Z)\|_2^2\) plus \(2\delta\), since the expectation under the target equals that risk. Orthogonal projection given \(Z\) in the Markov chain shows that the risk excess under the first law equals \(\mathbb E\|\eta(Z)-\psi^*\eta(Z')\|^2\), which dominates the integrated distance. For a permutation outside (1), the inclusion we will analyze fails on a set of positive \(\mu\) measure, so (2) is strictly positive.

## 3. Recovering the coordinate subspaces from unit branches

Assume now that \(\eta(Z)\in\psi^*\mathcal C\) almost surely. The log-posterior after subtracting its mean over labels is
\[
 W(a,y)(s)= \log p_b(y-as/q)-\mathbb E_{r\in R}\log p_b(y-ar/q).
 \tag{3}
\]
It has the same matching property \(W(a,y)=\psi^*W(a',y')\), for some output coefficient and response, for Haar-a.e. \(y\) at each \(a\). We work initially on unit input coefficients. At a nonunit \(a'\), at least two labels give equal posterior entries for every \(y'\), since multiplication by \(a'\) is noninjective on the finite ring. At a unit \(a\), for each pair of distinct \(s,r\) the analytic difference \(\log p_b(y-as/q)-\log p_b(y-ar/q)\) is not identically zero: at \(y=as/q\) the first product attains the strict global maximum, while the other does not. The strict maximum follows from monotonicity below, or directly from positivity of all \(b^{n^2}\). Thus for \(a\) a unit the entries are pairwise distinct outside a Haar-null set. Every matching on this set uses a unit \(a'\).

Here are the required one-circle facts. Write \(h=\log f_b\) and
\[
 c(y)_j=h(y-j/q)-q^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}h(y-r/q).
\]
By [Jacobi's triple product, DLMF 20.5.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.5.E9), \(h\) is real analytic, even, and \(h'(y)<0\) for \(0<y<1/2\). Indeed the factors in the product with \(y\)-dependence are \(1+2b^{2k-1}\cos(2\pi y)+b^{4k-2}\), with logarithmic derivatives of the same strict negative sign on this interval. The product also gives the nonzero Fourier coefficients
\[
 \widehat h(n)=\frac{(-1)^{|n|+1}b^{|n|}}{|n|(1-b^{2|n|})}\qquad(n\ne0)
 \tag{4}
\]
by expanding the logarithms of paired conjugate factors. Convergence on a sufficiently small complex strip about the real axis justifies these operations for each fixed \(0<b<1\). Hence \(c(\mathbb T)\) spans the real sum-zero coefficient space: a zero-sum covector annihilating every \(c(y)\) has all of its nontrivial grid Fourier coefficients zero by (4).

For a unit \(a\), let \(V_a\) be the space of functions of the form \(\sum_j F_j((as)_j)\) with each \(F_j:\mathbb Z/q\to\mathbb R\) of mean zero. It has dimension \(d(q-1)\) since the summands are orthogonal under the uniform distribution of \(as\). Equation (3) has its image in \(V_a\), and those images span all of \(V_a\). Indeed integrating over every response coordinate but \(y_j\) kills the other summands, and the remaining curve spans by (4). All analytic image statements concern the compact connected torus. For a fixed unit \(a\), its image lies a.e. in the finite union of \(\psi^*V_{a'}\) over units \(a'\). For any one of those linear subspaces not containing the whole image, some linear functional vanishing on it gives a nonidentically-zero real analytic function of \(y\) and hence a null zero set. Therefore for each unit \(a\) some unit \(a'\) satisfies
\[
 V_a=\psi^* V_{a'} .
 \tag{5}
\]
The equality, not just inclusion, follows from dimension. The finite family of distinct unit subspaces is thus permuted by \(\psi^*\).

Take \(a=1\) in (5). If \(V_1=\psi^* V_{a_0}\), then \(\theta(s)=a_0\psi(s)\) preserves \(V_1\) by pullback. Multiplication by a unit is already an allowed symmetry by Section 1, so the posterior inclusion remains valid for \(\theta\). To see this directly, an exact invertible symmetry acts on the posterior support with equality, and the inclusions are preserved by composition. The reproducing kernel of the orthogonal projection onto \(V_1\) is
\[
 K(s,r)=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\big(q\,\mathbf1_{\{s_j=r_j\}}-1\big).
\]
A permutation preserving \(V_1\) preserves this kernel, hence preserves the Hamming metric. Such an isometry is a permutation of the coordinates followed by independent alphabet permutations \(\pi_j\) of \(\mathbb Z/q\). One elementary proof is to use the coordinate lines, which are the maximal cliques in the Hamming graph, and use the induced four-cycles (opposite edges have the same direction) to transport a line's direction when one other coordinate is changed. All parallel lines are connected by such moves, so the output direction depends only on the input direction, and each output coordinate depends only on its corresponding input coordinate. For \(d=1\) the assertion is immediate. See also [Mirafzal–Ziaee, arXiv:1901.07784](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07784) for the graph automorphism theorem.

We may therefore replace \(\psi\) by \(\theta\) and assume it is a Hamming isometry, keeping the posterior inclusion and (5). Then \(\psi^*V_{a'}=V_1\) holds exactly when \(V_{a'}=V_1\). For a unit \(a'\), the latter means that multiplication by \(a'\) is itself a Hamming isometry. Applied to the vector \(1\) this forces \(a'=c_0 X^j\) with \(c_0\in(\mathbb Z/q)^\times\). Conversely all such multipliers preserve \(V_1\).

## 4. The likelihood curve restricts the alphabet permutations

For input coefficient \(1\) and matching output coefficient \(a'\), almost every input response uses only \(a'\) with \(\psi^*V_{a'}=V_1\), by the same analytic null-set argument in Section 3. Such coefficients are scalar times \(X^j\). Multiplication by \(X^j\) is signed coordinate permutation; the signs can be absorbed into the log curves since \(h\) is even and \(c(-y)_r=c(y)_{-r}\). Thus in \(V_1\), identified with the \(d\)-fold product of sum-zero alphabet spaces by the unique orthogonal decomposition, all output branches with a given scalar \(c_0\) have curve image the product of \(d\) copies of \(c(\mathbb T)\) relabeled by multiplication by \(c_0\).

The curve \(c(\mathbb T)\) is a real-analytic embedded circle. To verify injectivity, on the open interval between adjacent grid labels \(j,j+1\), the two largest coordinates of \(c(y)\) are exactly those labels (as an unordered pair). No third label can tie the second there. The difference \(c(y)_j-c(y)_{j+1}\) is strictly decreasing throughout that interval by strict radial monotonicity. At a grid vertex the largest coordinate is uniquely that vertex, and the second-largest is tied between its two neighbors; hence these endpoints are distinguished from the open intervals and from one another. For immersion, the derivative of the indicated difference is strictly negative on the open interval and at its endpoints using \(h'(1/q)<0\), since \(q\ge3\). A compact injective immersion is an embedding.

Two such connected compact real-analytic embedded curves either coincide or their intersection has measure zero in each curve. In fact an accumulation point of intersections makes the local analytic defining functions of one curve vanish identically along an arc of the other (use a nonvanishing coordinate derivative to write a local analytic graph). The set of matching open arcs is then both open and closed along a curve by the same local identity argument. Thus if there is no matching arc, the intersections are isolated, and compactness also makes them finite. This applies to all the coordinate relabelings of the curve.

For each candidate scalar \(c_0\), matching its whole product of curves after pullback by \(\psi\) means matching each of its coordinate curves, up to the already-known permutation of the coordinates. If any coordinate curve fails to coincide, the corresponding matches have Haar measure zero for that response coordinate. The finite union over scalars covers almost every input response by the posterior inclusion; consequently at least one **single common scalar** \(c_0\) has coincidence in all coordinates. Equivalently, after multiplying the Hamming isometry by this same scalar, each alphabet permutation preserves \(c(\mathbb T)\); multiplying by it is an allowed symmetry. (This follows also by writing the coordinate pullback as \(F(r)\mapsto F(c_0\pi_j(r))\).)

A permutation of the alphabet preserving this curve must preserve the adjacency graph of \(\mathbb Z/q\): an unordered pair of labels occurs as the two largest coordinates with those two positions unambiguously specified if and only if its labels are neighbors. The automorphisms of this cycle are exactly \(r\mapsto\pm r+t\), including \(q=3\) when all six permutations are of this form. Conversely these affine transformations preserve the curve. By also removing the resulting common vector translation (allowed by Section 1), we have proved that \(\psi\), up to composition with already-allowed symmetries, is a signed linear coordinate permutation \(B\).

## 5. The ring frames force a cyclic signed substitution

For a unit \(a\), the nonzero coefficient characters in the complex Fourier support of \(V_a\) are
\[
 \big\{\kappa\,\overline a\,X^j:\ \kappa\in\mathbb Z/q\setminus\{0\},\
                                  0\le j<d\big\},
 \qquad \overline a=a(X^{-1}).
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the coefficient pairing satisfies \(\langle x,as\rangle=\langle\overline a x,s\rangle\); this follows for \(X\) since the cyclic shift is signed orthogonal, and then by linearity. The characters of additive order \(q\) in (6) are precisely the single coset \(\overline a\,H\) of \(H=(\mathbb Z/q)^\times\langle X\rangle\) in \(R^\times\), since multiplication by a unit does not change additive order. By (5), \(B^\top\) permutes this family of cosets: order \(q\) is preserved by a linear permutation. Its inverse is \(B\), so \(B\) also permutes them.

For \(d\ge3\), use \(a=1-X\), which is a unit since \((1-X)\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}X^j=2\) in \(R\). The signed coordinate images of all \(X^j a\) must be in one coset of \(H\). Their supports are pairs of distinct coefficient positions. Because multiplication by a scalar and \(X^k\) keeps the same unordered difference of a pair modulo \(d\), the index permutation \(\rho\) of \(B\) maps every edge \(\{j,j+1\}\) of the cyclic index graph to a pair of fixed difference up to sign, say \(\pm l\). The difference cannot have order two, since the two neighbors of any vertex have distinct images. The graph on indices with edges of difference \(\pm l\) is a union of cycles. The image of the original cyclic graph traverses all \(d\) vertices, so \(\gcd(l,d)=1\), and it must follow the same orientation at each step, since reversing would revisit the previous vertex. Thus \(\rho(j)=r+l j\bmod d\), up to replacing \(l\) by \(-l\).

Choose an odd representative \(l\) prime to \(2d\) of the resulting invertible residue modulo \(d\) (if \(d\) is odd, one of the two lifts modulo \(2d\) is odd). The signed permutation \(s\mapsto X^r\sigma_l(s)\) has the same index permutation and is already allowed. Remove it. The remaining \(B\) is diagonal with signs \(\epsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}\), and likewise preserves the coset family. Since its images of \(X^j(1-X)\) are all in the coset of \(B(1-X)\), their supports force the relative shift to be \(j\bmod d\); no other shift takes the unordered pair \(\{0,1\}\) to \(\{j,j+1\}\) for \(d\ge3\). A shift by \(j+d\) only changes the scalar sign. Comparing ratios of the two nonzero signed coefficients for all \(j\), including the wraparound with \(X^d=-1\), gives
\[
 \epsilon_{j+1}/\epsilon_j=\epsilon_1/\epsilon_0 \quad(\text{indices modulo }d).
\]
The common ratio is \(\pm1\), and its \(d\)-th power is 1. Hence \(B(s)=\epsilon_0 s\) when the ratio is \(1\); when it is \(-1\) necessarily \(d\) is even and \(B(s)=\epsilon_0 s(-X)=\epsilon_0\sigma_{d+1}(s)\). Both are allowed. If \(d=2\), all signed coordinate permutations are generated by multiplication by \(X\) and \(\sigma_3\) (the latter changes the sign of the second coordinate). If \(d=1\), a sign itself is a unit multiplier. This completes (1) in all cases.

## 6. Sources and exact scope

[Lyubashevsky, Peikert and Regev, §5.1 Lemmas 5.5–5.6 and Theorem 5.3](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/ideal-lwe.pdf) already use number-field automorphisms to map samples to samples, and distinguish fixed spherical from more general noise families in their search-to-decision proof. The forward Galois action here is that familiar idea in coefficient coordinates, not a new reduction. [Shani's bounded-sample Ring-LWE presentation](https://crypto.iacr.org/2018/affevents/mathcrypt/medias/14-00_3.pdf) studies limitations of sample-preserving search-to-decision with a restricted distinguisher; that is a different problem from classifying one exact relabeling of an entire fixed experiment. [Blackwell's comparison theorem](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032) is general background for randomized transitions; the posterior quadratic identity (2) is derived directly above. The earlier [fixed-coefficient location result](ring-lwe-single-response-transport-rigidity.md) does not allow changing or mixing the observed coefficient. The present argument treats arbitrary such joint changes by recovering the hidden coordinate geometry from the posterior itself, and then imposing compatibility of all unit branches. No broader claim about hardness, approximation uniformly in the dimensions, or a deployed system is made.

The accompanying exact finite checks concern modular ring frames, signed permutations, and abstract posterior quadratic identities. They are not a solver and do not replace the analytic curve proof.
