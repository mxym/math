# Tame tetrahedral elliptic pencils via the cubic quotient

Scope. Let a separable degree-four map from a smooth geometric genus-one curve to the projective line have geometric monodromy A4. Work in characteristic different from 2 and 3. We count geometric covers above a fixed cyclic cubic quotient map and a fixed set of branch values of double-transposition type, and give a finite-field obstruction/counting rule. This is a statement about algebraic covers, not a discrete-logarithm algorithm. These are specialized elementary consequences of standard Galois/Kummer geometry; global novelty is not claimed.

## Signatures

The tame inertia generators in A4 are double transpositions and 3-cycles, each of index two in degree four. Riemann–Hurwitz gives four distinct geometric branch values. Put r equal to the number of 3-cycle values. Over the three-cycle values, the cubic quotient X = Y/V4 of the Galois closure Y is totally ramified. The quotient X→P1 is cyclic of degree three; it is unramified elsewhere. The labels of the r ramified points in C3 are ±1 and sum to zero. The labels for r=2 are (+,−), for r=3 all the same, and for r=4 two of each; r=1 is impossible by the sum and r=0 by Riemann–Hurwitz for a connected unramified degree-three cover of P1. In each of these three cases X has genus r−2. Conversely the corresponding quotient exists over an algebraically closed field for arbitrary distinct branch values: take a Kummer cubic with valuations congruent to the prescribed labels (their sum is zero modulo three). For a branch value at infinity one may change coordinates. Choose 4−r other distinct values for the double-transposition points. The construction below gives tetrahedral degree-four covers for all three signatures and every such choice.

## Kummer classifier and exact geometric counts

Let B_i be the three points above the i-th double-transposition value and let U=X minus their union B. Let T generate the cubic deck group. In étale cohomology there is an equivariant sequence
```
0 → J_X[2] → H¹(U,F2) → E(B) → 0,
E(B) = { (e_b) in F2^B : sum e_b = 0 }.
```
For empty B the last term is 0. Indeed a quadratic function-field class is represented by a function with valuations even outside B. Its parities have even sum; the 2-divisibility of Pic⁰(X) over the algebraic closure produces a function giving each such pattern; the everywhere-even classes identify with J_X[2]. This also proves all assertions at the ramification points without appealing to an étale cohomology comparison theorem. The sequence splits equivariantly since |C3| is odd. The nontrivial irreducible F2[C3]-module M has dimension two and endomorphism ring F4, with its scalar generator determined by choosing T. The nontrivial part of E(B) consists of one M per triple, namely the even subsets on that triple.

A tetrahedral cover above the fixed quotient and fixed type-two branch values is exactly a two-dimensional irreducible C3-invariant F2 subspace W of H¹(U,F2) whose residue projection to each B_i is nonzero. To justify both directions, adjoining the square roots of a basis of W gives a connected V4 cover Y→X. Invariance makes Y→P1 Galois with order 12 and nontrivial C3 action on V4; the extension splits (a Sylow 3 subgroup maps isomorphically to the quotient), giving A4. Ramification at B_i is by C2 exactly when the projection is nonzero, as a nonzero functional to F2 on square classes gives nontrivial inertia; transitivity along the triple means all three points ramify. Above a three-cycle branch value, Y→X is unramified and the inertia in Y→P1 has order three. All other points are unramified. The quartic is the quotient of Y by a subgroup of order three; all such subgroups are conjugate, and their core is trivial. Thus its closure is Y and the quartic has genus one by Riemann–Hurwitz. Conversely the square classes from any such closure satisfy the stated condition because the action of the cubic deck group on V4 is the unique irreducible action. There is no overcount: the closure of an isomorphism class determines the multiquadratic extension of k(X), with the only possible ambiguity on X a cubic deck transform, which preserves W. We do not mark the generator or a basis of W.

* r=2. J_X[2]=0 and there are two residue triples. The admissible irreducible planes are the F4-lines in M⊕M with both coordinates nonzero, i.e. graphs of the three nonzero scalars. Count **3**.
* r=3. X has genus one; T fixes a point, and with that origin satisfies 1+T+T²=0 in End(X): cancel the nonzero isogeny T−1 in (T−1)(1+T+T²)=0. Thus J_X[2] is M. There is one residue triple, and W is the graph of one of the four maps M→M after an equivariant choice of splitting. Count **4**.
* r=4. For genus-two X the hyperelliptic involution commutes with T. The automorphism induced by T on the hyperelliptic projective line has order 3, because its kernel would have order at most 2. It has only two fixed geometric points; the six Weierstrass points, which map bijectively to hyperelliptic branch values, must therefore constitute two triples. In the standard description of J_X[2] by even subsets of these six points modulo total complement, the direct sum of the even subsets on the two triples maps injectively (complement has odd parity on each triple), hence gives all of J_X[2]. It is M⊕M and all projective lines are admitted. Count **5**.

## Finite-field conditions and exact arithmetic fiber rules

Over F_q (q coprime to 6), the arithmetic monodromy over geometric A4 is either A4 or S4. Write ε=+1 or −1 respectively. Conjugation by Frobenius on A4/V4=C3 is T↦T^ε (normalizers can be read in S4). This action can be obtained without the closure: it is the conjugation action on the geometric deck group of X→P1. Tame local inertia/Frobenius at a closed branch value of degree d satisfies Frob g Frob⁻¹=g^(q^d), after a choice of generator up to conjugacy. In C3 this gives ε^d=q^d mod 3 at every 3-cycle value. More generally under rational Frobenius the class labels of geometric 3-cycle branch points are transported by the factor εq modulo 3 (using inverse Frobenius instead gives the same sign). Thus when εq=−1 every corresponding closed branch value has even degree and the two class counts agree. In particular r=3 forces ε≡q mod 3, without assuming a rational branch value. For r=3 and q≡2 mod 3 the cubic fibers of the quartic have density zero over F_q (as in the arithmetic A4/S4 classification); over the quadratic constant extension that group obstruction disappears. For r=2 or 4, a single odd-degree three-cycle branch value gives the same ε restriction. This is explicitly an application of the Branch Cycle Lemma rather than a new form of that lemma.

For a quotient X→P1 and a double-branch divisor defined over F_q, the geometric covers in a fiber that have F_q models are exactly the Frobenius-stable W above. Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, stability gives an isomorphism over the geometric base P1 between the quartic and its Frobenius conjugate, using the classifier and the given F_q model of X. The quartic has no nonidentity geometric automorphism over P1: its automorphism group equals N_{A4}(C3)/C3=1. The isomorphism is unique and satisfies the finite descent cocycle. In detail choose a finite extension containing the equations and this isomorphism; the full iterate is an automorphism and hence identity. Therefore effectiveness of Galois descent gives a unique F_q model up to F_q isomorphism. The cubic quotient as unmarked cover descends to the specified X: the geometric construction identifies it and any ambiguity is a deck transform, but the canonical quotient of the quartic is formed by permuting the three partitions into two unordered pairs. To remove the possible ambiguity of rational twists of X, the Frobenius identification of Y built from W may be chosen to extend the specified Frobenius on X; after adjusting by V4 it preserves the chosen order-three subgroup. That adjustment is invisible on X. The resulting semilinear map on the quartic is precisely its unique descent map, so the induced Frobenius on the canonical quotient agrees with the specified X.

Explicit count: on the nontrivial part, F is linear over F4 when ε=+1, and semilinear by the squaring automorphism when ε=−1. In r=2 use the two residue triples, in r=4 the two Weierstrass triples. F preserves or exchanges those two coordinates, and in either case acts on nonzero slopes c by one of c↦k c, k c², k/c, k/c², for k∈F4×, as dictated by the coordinate exchange and semilinearity. In order, the numbers of fixed nonzero slopes are 3 if k=1 and 0 otherwise; 1; 1; and 3 if k=1 and 0 otherwise. In r=2 these are the entire counts. In r=4 add two when coordinates are preserved and zero when they are exchanged. For r=3 the admissible sections form an affine F4-line; after choosing one to be the origin the F action is x↦a x^s+b where a≠0 and s=1 or 2 according as ε=+1 or −1. For s=1 there is one fixed point if a≠1, otherwise all four if b=0 and none if b≠0. For s=2, the F2-linear map x↦x−a x² has exactly two elements in its kernel; there are two fixed points if b is in its image and none otherwise. These rules are exact once the action on the indicated finite vector space is supplied; do not infer existence from branch-cycle parity alone.

### Intrinsic halving test in the r=3 fiber

The affine rule need not be implemented by computing cohomology. Choose a geometric point O fixed by T and use it as elliptic origin on X. Choose a point P over the unique double-transposition branch value (necessarily rational as a value); its fiber is {P,TP,T²P}. Then the four geometric lifts are indexed by the four geometric points Q of X satisfying [2]Q=−P. Indeed T+T²=−1, so for each such Q the divisor (TP)+(T²P)−2(Q) is principal. Its representing function has residue the pair omitting P. Distinct halves give distinct square classes, since equality would make (Q₁)−(Q₂) principal. Moreover every such square class lies in the nontrivial C3-isotypic part: its norm under the C3 action has zero residues and lies in the invariant part of J_X[2], which is zero. Hence its orbit spans the unique graph section taking exactly this square class on that pair. This identifies all four.

Write F(P)=T^j P for geometric points under the q-power Frobenius, where j is taken modulo 3. The exact existence test is whether at least one half Q satisfies F(Q)=T^j Q, and the number of F_q quartics over the given quotient equals the number of such halves. The Frobenius transform of the divisor misses F(P) and subtracts twice F(Q); in the section generated by Q the class on the pair missing F(P) subtracts twice T^j Q. They agree precisely when the two geometric points are equal. This argument does not require O rational; the divisor description is independent of the coordinate used to express the half equation. In particular when F(P)=P, the criterion is an actual rational halving test on the X-torsor after the displayed choice of O. This provides an exact genus-one group-law realization of the four-point arithmetic fiber.

For example, for the cubic quotient y:X→P¹ over F13 with X given by y²=x³+5 and T(x,y)=(3x,y), the three 3-cycle locations are infinity and the roots of y²=5. The nonbranch value y=0 and point P=(2,0) give four rational halves of −P, at (7,6), (7,7), (10,2), (10,11). Choosing instead the nonbranch value y=2 and P=(4,2) gives no rational half of −P. Thus two allowed double-transposition locations for the same quotient give respectively four and zero rational quartics, while the branch-cycle parity test holds in both cases. The toy verifier enumerates F13 points and uses the ordinary doubling formula.
