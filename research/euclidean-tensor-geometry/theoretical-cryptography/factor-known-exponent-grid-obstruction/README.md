# Small-base, smooth-stripping obstruction with a known exponent

This note counts balanced semiprimes on which a specified gcd test cannot split the modulus, even when the test receives an exact multiple of the group exponent. It does **not** give a lower bound for integer factorization or for arbitrary algorithms supplied with a totient or a group exponent.

Let \(L>0\) be any exponent for which there is a constant \(C\ge1\) such that every reduced residue class modulo \(M\) contains a prime at most \(CM^L\). One may take \(L=5\) by [Xylouris, Theorem 1](https://m.mathnet.ru/links/fd12fde4668210330e7c02bfda03173f/cheb681.pdf); optimality is not asserted. All logarithms are natural.

**Theorem.** Fix \(0<c<1/(2L)\) and put \(B=\lceil c\log X\rceil\). For every sufficiently large \(X\), at least \(X^{1/L-2c-o(1)}\) distinct odd semiprimes \(N=pq\le X\) with \(p<q<2p\) and \(p,q>B\) have the following property. Let \(\lambda(N)=\operatorname{lcm}(p-1,q-1)\) and let \(H_B\) be the subgroup of \((\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times\) generated by the residue classes of the integers \(1,\ldots,B\). For every positive multiple \(E\) of \(\lambda(N)\), every positive divisor \(D\mid E\) whose prime divisors are at most \(B\), and every \(b\in H_B\),
\[
\gcd(b^{E/D}-1,N)=N.
\]
The gcd is well-defined using any integer representative of \(b\). For \(b\in -H_B\), the gcd is either 1 or \(N\). Thus the same obstruction holds at the cutoff \(c\log N\) for infinitely many balanced semiprimes. With \(L=5\), the quantitative bound holds for every fixed \(c<1/10\).

## Proof

Put \(Q=8\prod_{3\le\ell\le B,\ \ell\text{ prime}}\ell\). First take distinct primes \(p,q\equiv-1\pmod Q\) with \(p,q>B\). For either prime \(s\in\{p,q\}\), the congruence \(s\equiv7\pmod8\) gives \(v_2(s-1)=1\) and \((2/s)=1\). Here \((a/s)\) denotes the Legendre symbol. For an odd prime \(\ell\le B\), quadratic reciprocity gives
\[
(\ell/s)=(s/\ell)(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}=(-1/\ell)(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}=1.
\]
Also \(s-1\equiv-2\pmod\ell\), so \(\ell\nmid s-1\). Thus every generator of \(H_B\) is a square modulo \(s\), as is every product of generators and their inverses. Its order modulo \(s\) divides \((s-1)/2\), which has no prime divisor at most \(B\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives \(\operatorname{ord}_N(b)=\operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{ord}_p(b),\operatorname{ord}_q(b))\) with the same property. This order divides \(E\) and is coprime to \(D\), so it divides \(E/D\). If \(b=-h\) for \(h\in H_B\), then \(b^{E/D}\equiv(-1)^{E/D}\pmod N\), giving the claimed two gcd values.

To count the moduli, the prime number theorem gives \(\log Q=(1+o(1))B\) and hence \(Q=X^{c+o(1)}\). Let \(R\) be the largest power of two with \(RQ\le (\sqrt X/C)^{1/L}\). Then \(R=X^{1/(2L)-c+o(1)}\) grows without bound. The \(R\) classes \(jQ-1\pmod{RQ}\), \(1\le j\le R\), are distinct and reduced: every prime divisor of \(RQ\) divides \(Q\). In particular, for \(R=2\) these are \(Q-1\) and \(2Q-1\) modulo \(2Q\). By the defining property of \(L\), the \(R\) classes give \(R\) distinct primes in \([Q-1,\sqrt X]\), all greater than \(B\) for large \(X\). Partition that interval into its intersections with the dyadic intervals \([2^j,2^{j+1})\). There are \(O(\log X)\) intervals, so one contains \(\gg R/\log X\) of the primes. Each unordered pair in that interval has ratio strictly less than two and gives a distinct semiprime by unique factorization. The number of pairs is \(\gg R^2/(\log X)^2\), which gives the asserted exponent. Their least factor exceeds \(\sqrt{N/2}\), and \(N\ge(Q-1)^2\) ensures the moduli grow.

## Scope and replay

The condition is simultaneous in both cutoffs: an unrestricted base, a removed prime above \(B\), or a different arithmetic use of \(E\) is outside the result. Here the exponent is a multiple of \(\lambda(N)\), not \(N-1\); no strong-pseudoprime assertion is made. For instance, knowing \(\phi(N)\) gives \(p+q=N+1-\phi(N)\) for these semiprimes and factors them by a quadratic equation. The counting argument is existential and does not claim that finding the asserted prime representatives takes polynomial time in their bit length.
