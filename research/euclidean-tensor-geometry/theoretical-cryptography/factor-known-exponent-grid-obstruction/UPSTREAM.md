# Sources and overlap

- T. Xylouris, *Linniks Konstante ist kleiner als 5*, Chebyshevskii Sbornik 19(3) (2018), 80–94, DOI [10.22405/2226-8383-2018-19-3-80-94](https://doi.org/10.22405/2226-8383-2018-19-3-80-94), [Theorem 1 on printed p. 81](https://m.mathnet.ru/links/fd12fde4668210330e7c02bfda03173f/cheb681.pdf). It gives a uniform bound \(P(a,M)<CM^5\) for reduced classes. The counting proof uses this existing theorem, together with the prime number theorem in the form \(\sum_{\ell\le B}\log\ell\sim B\), quadratic reciprocity, and the Chinese remainder theorem. It does not assert an optimal Linnik exponent.
- G. L. Miller, [*Riemann's hypothesis and tests for primality*](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf), J. Comput. System Sci. 13 (1976), 300–317, Lemma 5 and Theorem 3, printed p. 310: the conditional known-period factoring reduction. E. Bach, G. Miller and J. Shallit, [*Sums of divisors, perfect numbers and factoring*](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/BMS86.pdf), SIAM J. Comput. 15 (1986), 1143–1154, §2, Theorem 3 and Lemma A, pp. 1145–1146: randomized splitting from a multiple of a relevant local period. These are antecedents of the gcd mechanism, not claims that a logarithmic initial interval always supplies a splitting base.
- B. Żrałek, [*A deterministic version of Pollard's p-1 algorithm*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102v5), v5, §4, printed pp. 4 and 6–7, definition of a Fermat–Euclid witness via unequal local orders and `Split` step 3. The algorithm in Theorem 4.5 and Corollary 4.6 uses further tests, including a cyclicity check, and is not ruled out. F. Morain, G. Renault and B. Smith, [*Deterministic factoring with oracles*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444v2), v2, §2.3 and Appendix A, discuss known \(\phi\), known \(\lambda\), randomized splitting, and the distinction from a deterministic reduction.
- J. Pomykała and M. Radziejewski, [*Integer factoring and compositeness witnesses*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jmc-2019-0023/html), J. Math. Cryptol. 14(1) (2020), 346–358, DOI [10.1515/jmc-2019-0023](https://doi.org/10.1515/jmc-2019-0023). The closest antecedent is §3, Algorithm 2 steps 2–4 (bounded bases and bounded primary-order primes), Theorem 3.5, and Lemma 3.2 (fixed order and exceptional characters). These describe the test and give success bounds and structural restrictions on exceptions. The quantified assertion here is an explicit lower count for failure of the restricted grid. On the constructed examples there is no nontrivial primary component at a tested prime, so no positive index for the power-difference step. This is not a failure claim about trial division or the full algorithms. Their §4, Algorithm 3 step 3, can use arbitrary prime divisors of an exact order and is outside the bound; the replay gives an example of the distinction. The corresponding locations in the [author conference draft](https://nutmic2019.imj-prg.fr/confpapers/FactoringWitnesses.pdf) are printed pp. 5–6 and 9–10.
- W. R. Alford, A. Granville and C. Pomerance, [*On the difficulty of finding reliable witnesses*](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/reliable.pdf), ANTS I (1994), LNCS 877, 1–16, DOI [10.1007/3-540-58691-1_36](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-58691-1_36), Introduction and least-witness theorem: the exponent is \(n-1\) and the statistic is the least ordinary strong-pseudoprime witness. E. Bach, [*Explicit bounds for primality testing and related problems*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8), Math. Comp. 55 (1990), 355–380, gives related ERH upper bounds for small omitted elements and primality witnesses. Neither type of statement is the bounded known-exponent grid count. K. Durnoga and J. Pomykała, [*Large sieve, Miller–Rabin compositeness witnesses and integer factoring problem*](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2017-1603), Fund. Inform. 156(2) (2017), 179–185, is nearby work on reductions for a large class; only its publisher abstract and its discussion in the later paper were accessible here.
