# Source and overlap audit

1. [Arzaki, Esgin, Sakzad and Steinfeld, *SEC: One-Share Everywhere-Short Secret Sharing from Lossless Expanders*, arXiv:2611.16244v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16244), also [ePrint 2026/2841, verified 2026-11-12 PDF](https://opencryptology.org/2026/2841/versions/20261112T172655Z.pdf). PDF §2.5.2, Definition 7, p.14 requires a common integer-coefficient ambient module and public integer scalar reconstruction. Its dealer syntax (Definitions 5–6) does not impose a linear dealer. §3.1 gives SEC's specific linear Dealer, and §5.1 (Structural Setup Condition) adds integral column containment for private sets; our AR is the weaker no-rational-reconstruction condition for the public equations, not a replacement for their Definition 10 hard-core local leakage experiment. §7.2, p.54 asks about one/constant atomic shares and an exact (non-ramp) threshold or a narrower ramp. Our theorem concerns one atomic share, exact cardinality thresholds with AR, and conventional fixed-view statistical privacy for the explicit alternative. It does not decide the general §7.2 computational model or the multi-share/ramp questions. No SEC parameters or cryptographic security assumption are evaluated here.

2. [Cramer and Fehr, *Optimal Black-Box Secret Sharing over Arbitrary Abelian Groups*, CRYPTO 2002](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~fehr/mypapers/CF02.pdf), Proposition 1, Definition 6 and Lemma 1. [Cramer and Xing, *Blackbox Secret Sharing Revisited: A Coding-Theoretic Approach With Application to Expansionless Near-Threshold Schemes*, EUROCRYPT 2020](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/30154/Cramer-Xing2020_Chapter_BlackboxSecretSharingRevisited.pdf), §1.1 pp.500–501, §1.3 pp.503–506, Definitions 3–4, Lemmas 1–2, Theorem 1, and Remark 3/Eq. (1), p.514. The local/global integer solvability criterion, primitive target normalization, integer span-program language, and the classical reduction-modulo-2 obstruction to tiny expansion for universal black-box near-threshold schemes are prior. In the notation of §1.1, the intersection of the primal share-span V_U with the integral target line V_0 is zero under AR. Our L_U instead generates the image of the dual integral solution lattice under the target coordinate, with the U coordinates set to zero. The formal condition (P2) of Definition 4 requires that image to contain 1, not only that the primal intersection be zero. Thus the dual ideal is a standard lattice quantity; the new assertions are its exact lcm evaluation for these normalized one-share reconstruction tables, the forced divisibility by lcm(1,…,t), and the coefficient-magnitude bound. The fact that multiples of the designated secret may be easier to reconstruct is already explicit in §1.3. Their universal black-box schemes, over all finite Abelian groups, are not claimed to use the one-share sublattice model of this note.

3. [Damgård and Thorbek, *Linear Integer Secret Sharing and Distributed Exponentiation*, PKC 2006](https://iacr.org/archive/pkc2006/39580077/39580077.pdf), §1.1 and §3. They already use finite-interval statistical privacy, integer reconstruction, and statistical masking by translating bounded noise. [Braun, Damgård and Orlandi, *Secure Multiparty Computation from Threshold Encryption Based on Class Groups*, ePrint 2022/1437](https://opencryptology.org/2022/1437/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf), §4.1, Protocol 1 and Eq. (1); Appendix B.1–B.2, use integer-point polynomial sharing, finite-box mask shifts, factorial scaling and recovery of a scaled target. Those principles and the Lagrange/Vandermonde interpolation identity are not new. The moment construction here uses the dual Vandermonde coordinates, reconstructs the actual bounded sublattice secret with integer coefficients, and evaluates precisely the spacing and maximum reconstruction magnitude in that normalization. In codimension one these match both universal lower bounds.

4. [Ball, Cakan and Malkin, *Linear Threshold Secret-Sharing with Binary Reconstruction*, ITC 2021](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITC.2021.12), §2–§5. Their small-coefficient reconstruction results concern the share size of binary-coefficient schemes over finite fields; they are not a magnitude bound for integer reconstructors. [Cascudo, Cramer and Xing, *Bounds on the Threshold Gap in Secret Sharing and its Applications*, IEEE TIT 2013](https://eprint.iacr.org/2012/319.pdf), Theorems 3.1, 3.25–3.29, and [Bogdanov, Guo and Komargodski, *Threshold Secret Sharing Requires a Linear-Size Alphabet*, ToC 2020](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v016a002/v016a002.pdf), Theorems 1.1–1.2, give the relevant fixed finite-alphabet lower bounds. None of these classical reductions are claimed as original here; the divisor bound works even if the secret interval is deliberately very sparse in every small-prime residue.

On 2026-11-28 the arXiv record for SEC listed v1 only. Bounded primary-literature searches for its title and for integer/one-share/coefficient-magnitude lower bounds, integer threshold secret spacing, and divisor-based reconstruction included the sources above; we did not find the threshold coefficient-magnitude/divisor result or the simultaneous codimension-one sharpness. A check of the working repository research tree at 6915817e4f0391001fd454f7fcc5245dee48694c found other threshold/privacy constructions, not this integer-coefficient problem. These are bounded checks, not an absolute priority claim.
