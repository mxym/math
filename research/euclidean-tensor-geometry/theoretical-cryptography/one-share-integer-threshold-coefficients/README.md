# Coefficient and secret-spacing limits for one-share integer thresholds

Arzaki–Esgin–Sakzad–Steinfeld ask in [SEC §7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16244v1) whether short integer-module sharing with one or constantly many atomic shares per participant can realize an exact threshold or a narrower ramp. This note isolates the **one-atomic-share, public scalar integer reconstruction** part of that question.

For a t-out-of-n table (2≤t<n), require that no rational scalar reconstruction on t−1 participants follows from the public identities. This is implied, for example, by conventional pairwise statistical privacy with error less than one for any two distinct allowed secrets. It is also implied by retaining SEC's stated integral column condition in its formal scalar-matrix setting. It is not assumed to follow from an arbitrary computational hard-core privacy definition.

* If B is the largest magnitude of an integer reconstruction coefficient, then B≥max(t,n−t+1). Moreover n−t is at most the number of divisors of some positive integer ≤B²; the proof gives a finite refinement. In particular B grows faster than every fixed power of n−t. This concerns coefficient magnitude, not bit length.
* In the same fixed integer module for secrets and atomic shares, the lcm L of the absolute nonzero t-set reconstruction coefficients is the exact spacing obstruction under pairwise view overlap. Every protected secret difference belongs to L times the module, and secrets restricted to such a lattice coset can be shared with arbitrarily small fixed-view TV error. Universally L is divisible by lcm(1,…,t).
* An explicit moment-table construction has one integer or integer-vector share per party, exact correctness and arbitrarily small pairwise statistical error for bounded **sublattice** secrets. Its reconstruction magnitude and secret spacing have explicit formulas. When t=n−1 it simultaneously attains B=t and L=lcm(1,…,t).

The model fixes the common integer embedding and reconstructs the actual secret with coefficient 1, not a multiple subsequently divided out. This is not a conclusion about SEC's computational guarantee, multiple atomic shares, more general reconstruction maps, or arbitrary unscaled secret intervals.
