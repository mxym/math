# The critical load law of finite-field hashing on uniform product supports

Let \(F\) be a fixed finite field of size \(q\), \(S\subseteq F\) a fixed set of size \(s\ge2\), \(X\) uniform on \(S^m\), and \(A\) a public \(k\)-by-\(m\) matrix of independent uniform \(F\) entries. Here \(X\) is independent of \(A\), \(m\) tends to infinity, and \(k\) may vary with \(m\). Set
\[
 \lambda_m=s^m/q^k,\qquad
 L_A(u)=|\{x\in S^m:Ax=u\}|,\qquad
 \mathcal D_A=d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(AX\mid A), U(F^k)).
\]
Assume first that \(\lambda_m\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\) along a sequence of integer dimensions. This is the constant-offset, zero-dispersion window for the uniform source. The arithmetic of \(s\) and \(q\) need not allow every \(\lambda\) along the entire integer sequence.

**Dichotomy.**
* If \(S\) is **not** a coset of an additive subgroup of \(F\), the empirical load law over the \(q^k\) bins converges in probability in TV to \(\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)\), and
  \[
       \mathcal D_A \ \xrightarrow[L^2]{}\ 
          \Pr(\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor)
          = e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}/
                  \lfloor\lambda\rfloor!.                            \tag{1}
  \]
* If \(S=a+H\) for a nonzero additive subgroup \(H\), let \(F_0=\{b\in F:bH\subseteq H\}\), the scalar stabilizer subfield, and put \(Q=|F_0|\), \(h=\dim_{F_0}H\), \(f=[F:F_0]\). In this case \(\lambda=Q^\ell\) for an integer \(\ell=hm-fk\) eventually constant. The kernel of \(A\) restricted to \(H^m\) has \(F_0\)-dimension \(D_m\). It converges in distribution to the nullity of an asymptotically large uniform \(F_0\) matrix with number of columns minus number of rows equal to \(\ell\). More explicitly its limit \(D\) has law
  \[
    \Pr(D=d)
       =\frac{C_Q\, Q^{-d(d-\ell)}}
           {\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-Q^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{d-\ell}(1-Q^{-i})},
    \quad d\ge \max(0,\ell),\qquad C_Q=\prod_{i\ge1}(1-Q^{-i}).          \tag{2}
  \]
  The empirical load law is exactly
  \((1-Q^{\ell-D_m})\delta_0+Q^{\ell-D_m}\delta_{Q^{D_m}}\); consequently \(\mathcal D_A\) converges in distribution to \(1-Q^{\ell-D}\), a nonconstant limit. The limiting rank law depends on the largest scalar stabilizer, not necessarily on the prime field.

Outside the constant-offset window, \(\mathcal D_A\) tends to 1 uniformly in \(A\) if \(\lambda_m\to0\) (support size at most \(s^m\)), and to 0 in \(L^1\) if \(\lambda_m\to\infty\) (the ordinary two-universal hash bound \(\mathbb E_A\mathcal D_A\le1/(2\sqrt{\lambda_m})\)). Thus the Poisson versus rank distinction concerns exactly the bounded window.

**Prior and scope.** This complements the nonzero-dispersion result [module-information-spectrum.md](module-information-spectrum.md) in a different critical scaling: no side information and a uniform one-letter source on a fixed set in a field. Poisson limits via sparse linear relations have a long history, e.g. [Mikhailov, 2003, random linear subspaces](https://doi.org/10.4213/dm200) and [Kopyttsev–Mikhailov, 2010, random linear inclusions](https://doi.org/10.4213/mvk21). [Kumar, *Linear Hashing Is Poissonesque*, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34750v1) bounds a log-potential and load extremes of random binary linear maps on arbitrary input sets, and notes why subspace loads are not independent Poisson. In our product-support setting the thin-relationship moment argument is classical; the comparison here explicitly classifies when it applies, identifies the public-matrix TV limit, and separates the distinct subfield-stabilized rank law. We do not assert a new arbitrary-set Poisson theorem, a result about sources over all finite rings, or any operational cryptographic claim.

## 1. An exact criterion for thin affine dependencies

Suppose coefficients \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\in F\) have sum \(1\), at least two are nonzero, and
\[
                 \sum_i \alpha_i x_i\in S\quad\text{for every }x\in S^r .
                                                                         \tag{3}
\]
Fix all but two nonzero positions. With \(B_1,B_2\) the two nonzero scalar multiples of \(S\), each \(B_1+b\) for \(b\in B_2\) lies in a fixed translate of \(S\) of the same cardinality \(s\), so all such translates agree. Thus \(B_1\) is invariant under the additive subgroup \(H'\) generated by \(B_2-B_2\). The group has size at least \(s\), because \(B_2\) lies in one \(H'\)-coset, and at most \(s\), because \(B_1\) is a nonempty union of its cosets. Hence \(B_1\) and \(S\) are subgroup cosets. If \(S\) is not a subgroup coset, any such relation discards at least one of the \(s^r\) **one-coordinate** assignments. Consequently for \(m\) independent coordinates it allows at most \((s^r-1)^m\) assignments for the \(r\) free input vectors.

In the subgroup case translate away \(a\); the resulting output is translated by the public offset, which leaves its distance and load histogram unchanged. The set \(F_0\) is a subfield: closure under sums and products and the presence of \(1\) are direct; a nonzero stabilizing scalar maps the finite subgroup bijectively to itself and its inverse then stabilizes too. For arbitrary (not necessarily affine) \(\alpha_i\), all assignments \(x_i\in H\) give \(\sum_i\alpha_i x_i\in H\) exactly when every \(\alpha_i\in F_0\). The nontrivial implication follows by setting all but one \(x_i\) to zero. This is the analogue of (3) used for the rank law.

## 2. Non-coset case: joint moments of randomly sampled bins

Draw any fixed number \(b\) of independent uniform bins \(U_1,\ldots,U_b\in F^k\), all independent of \(A\). We show that their loads converge jointly to independent \(\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)\) variables. For a mixed falling-factorial moment with fixed orders \(t_1,\ldots,t_b\), expand over ordered input vectors, distinct within each group but allowed to coincide across groups. Write \(t=\sum_g t_g\). Append the tag \(e_g\in F^b\) to an input vector in group \(g\). If the augmented vectors have \(F\)-rank \(r\), the probability of all required output equalities is \(q^{-kr}\): each row of \([A\mid -U_1\mid\cdots\mid -U_b]\) is uniform and must annihilate their \(r\)-dimensional span.

For \(r<t\), fix which \(r\) tagged vectors form a basis and the coefficients describing the other vectors. The number of such choices is bounded in terms of \(q,t,b\) only. In a dependent relation expressing a tagged vector from that basis the coefficients sum to one, by summing tag coordinates. At least two coefficients are nonzero. Otherwise a single coefficient must be one, the tag must match, and that input is a repeated vector in the same group, forbidden by the falling factorial. Section 1 therefore gives at most \((s^r-1)^m\) assignments, not counting independence restrictions on the chosen basis. Its expectation contribution is at most a fixed constant times
\[
                q^{-kr}(s^r-1)^m
                  =\lambda_m^r(1-s^{-r})^m=o(1).                       \tag{4}
\]
For full augmented rank \(t\) the number of tuples is
\(\prod_g(s^m)_{t_g}-o(s^{mt})=(1+o(1))s^{mt}\), since the same counts bound all lower ranks. Its contribution tends to \(\lambda^t\). The multivariate moment method applies: ordinary moments are finite linear combinations of the falling factorial moments, and the limiting product Poisson law has a moment generating function finite near zero. This proves the joint claim, and also bounds every fixed mixed ordinary moment uniformly.

For a fixed load level \(j\), the fraction of bins with load \(j\) has first moment tending to the Poisson mass at \(j\), and second moment tending to its square by the two-bin version. Hence it converges in \(L^2\) to that mass. Since the average load is exactly \(\lambda_m\) for every matrix, the empirical fraction above level \(B\) is at most \(\lambda_m/B\), uniformly. Truncating at any fixed \(B\) and then letting \(B\) grow proves the TV convergence of the empirical law.

Finally
\[
 \mathcal D_A=\frac{1}{2\lambda_m}\mathbb E_{U}|L_A(U)-\lambda_m|.
\]
Its expectation and second moment converge to the first power and square of \((2\lambda)^{-1}\mathbb E|P-\lambda|\) for \(P\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\lambda)\), by single-bin and two-bin convergence, respectively. The uniform higher mixed moments just established give uniform integrability for the absolute values and their pair products. For \(j=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor\), noting that \(P\) has mean \(\lambda\),
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E|P-\lambda|}{2\lambda}
     =\frac{\mathbb E(\lambda-P)_+}{\lambda}
     =\Pr(P\le j)-\Pr(P\le j-1)=\Pr(P=j),
\]
using \(i\Pr(P=i)=\lambda\Pr(P=i-1)\). This proves (1).

## 3. Coset case: full stabilizer-field rank universality

The restricted map \(H^m\to F^k\) is \(F_0\)-linear, with domain dimension \(a_m=hm\) and codomain dimension \(b_m=fk\). The convergence \(\lambda_m=Q^{a_m-b_m}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\) implies eventual equality \(a_m-b_m=\ell\). Both dimensions grow, because \(s\ge2\) and \(k\) is in the specified window.

For a fixed \(j\), let
\[
    W_j(D_m)=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(Q^{D_m}-Q^i)
\]
be the number of ordered independent \(j\)-tuples over \(F_0\) in this kernel (zero if \(D_m<j\)). Expand its expectation over the \(j\)-tuples in \(H^m\) independent over \(F_0\). A tuple of \(F\)-rank \(r\) is killed with probability \(q^{-kr}\). If \(r<j\), choose its basis and coefficient descriptions as before. Some dependence has a coefficient outside \(F_0\), because otherwise the tuple was already \(F_0\)-dependent. By section 1 the available coordinate assignments for the \(r\) free vectors are at most \((|H|^r-1)^m\), giving \(o(1)\) in expectation by (4). For rank \(j\) over \(F\), independence over \(F_0\) is automatic and there are \((1-o(1))|H|^{mj}\) choices: count lower ranks without the discard, using at most a fixed multiple of \(\sum_{r<j}|H|^{mr}\). Thus
\[
                       \mathbb E W_j(D_m)\longrightarrow \lambda^j.
                                                                         \tag{5}
\]
The same moment limit holds for the nullity of the truly uniform \(b_m\)-by-\(a_m\) matrix over \(F_0\), by counting its independent \(j\)-tuples and multiplying by \(Q^{-b_m j}\).

For clarity, the moments (5) determine this nullity distribution without an ordinary real moment-determinacy assumption. The first moment gives tightness in integer \(D_m\). For every \(j\), the \((j+1)\)-st moment bound gives uniform integrability of \(W_j(D_m)\), since their ratio on \(D_m\ge B>j\) is \(W_j/W_{j+1}\le(Q^B-Q^j)^{-1}\). Thus each subsequential law has expected Gaussian binomial coefficient
\[
 \mathbb E{D\brack j}_Q
   =\lambda^j/\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(Q^j-Q^i)
     =O_Q(Q^{\ell j-j^2}).
\]
For fixed \(u\ge0\) the Gaussian-binomial inversion identity gives
\[
 1_{\{D=u\}}
   =\sum_{j\ge u}(-1)^{j-u}Q^{\binom{j-u}{2}}
                      {D\brack j}_Q{j\brack u}_Q.                    \tag{6}
\]
For each finite \(D\), it is a finite sum; factor out \({D\brack u}_Q\) and apply the \(q\)-binomial theorem at \(-1\) to the remaining \((D-u)\) factors when \(D\ge u\). For \(D>u\) the result is zero because the factor \(1-Q^0\) vanishes; for \(D=u\) it is one. The expectation of (6) is absolutely summable, because for fixed \(u\), \({j\brack u}_Q=O_{Q,u}(Q^{u(j-u)})\) and the expected coefficients decay as \(Q^{-j^2+O(j)}\). So all atom probabilities are uniquely specified by (5).

The uniform comparison matrix has finite nullity mass
\[
       Q^{-d(d-\ell)}
       \frac{\prod_{i=d+1}^{a_m}(1-Q^{-i})
                 \prod_{i=d-\ell+1}^{b_m}(1-Q^{-i})}
            {\prod_{i=1}^{a_m-d}(1-Q^{-i})}
\]
for \(d\ge\max(0,\ell)\) in its feasible range. This is obtained by factoring a rank-\((a_m-d)\) matrix through its rank space and dividing by changes of its basis. The limit is (2). Tightness and uniqueness via (6) prove the same full law for the restricted map, rather than asserting that its entries are independent over \(F_0\). Each occupied bin of the restricted map has exactly \(Q^{D_m}\) preimages, and the image has fraction \(Q^{\ell-D_m}\) of the target. This yields the claimed load and TV laws.

For example in \(F_{16}\) choose an element \(z\) of degree four over \(\mathbb F_2\), and compare the three four-point supports \(\mathbb F_4\), \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{1,z\}\), and \(\{0,1,z,z^2\}\). Set \(m=2k\), so \(\lambda=1\) for all three. The first two have stabilizer fields of sizes 4 and 2, respectively: for the second, a stabilizer of size 4 would equal the subgroup because \(1\) is in it, contrary to the degree of \(z\). Their limiting probabilities of exact uniformity are \(C_4\) and \(C_2\). The last support contains zero but is not closed under addition, and (1) gives the deterministic limiting TV \(e^{-1}\). Cardinality alone at this entropy offset cannot predict the profile.
