# Constant-depth classical circuits cannot read out broad ring observations

An information-theoretic search threshold does not imply an ordinary polynomial-time algorithm, and a small low-degree correlation need not immediately control a Boolean circuit. Here there is an exact stronger statement for a typical public design: after conditioning on a linear number of specified label coefficients, all sufficiently small sets of response positions are independent with the same marginal laws as in the null experiment. Bounded-independence fooling theorems then give a classical circuit lower bound, even with unrestricted design-dependent preprocessing.

## 1. Model and results

Fix any odd \(q\ge3\), dyadic \(d\), \(R=(\mathbb Z/q)[X]/(X^d+1)\), and a polynomial bound \(1\le k\le d^A\) with \(A>0\) fixed. Let \(S\) be uniform on \(R\) and, independently, \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\) uniform on \(R\). Given the label and design, take \(Y_i=A_iS/q+E_i\) in the coefficient torus, with independent scalar noise coordinates of density \(f_b(y)=\sum_j b^{j^2}e^{2\pi i j y}\), \(b\in(0,1)\). The law \(g_b=q^{-1}\sum_a f_b(\cdot-a/q)\) is the averaged one-coordinate law. In each scalar response coordinate use \(2^p\) bins of equal probability under \(g_b\), and record the bin index as \(p\) bits. Here \(p\ge1\) is arbitrary (we use \(p=O(\log d)\) in the statistical separation). The null law of response bits given the design is independent fair bits.

Let \(I\subseteq\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\) have size \(\ell=\lfloor c_0 d\rfloor\) for a sufficiently small \(c_0>0\) depending on \(q\), and write \(W=(S_i)_{i\in I}\). The conclusion holds simultaneously for all such \(I\), so \(I\) may depend on the design but not on the responses. There is an event of designs of probability at least \(1-e^{-c_1d}\), on which:

1. For **every** \(w\in(\mathbb Z/q)^\ell\), the response bit law conditional on \(W=w\) and the design is exactly \(h_d\)-wise independent, where \(h_d=\lfloor c_2 d/\log d\rfloor\). More intrinsically, the full continuous scalar responses at any \(h_d\) distinct positions are independent with density \(g_b\), even conditional on \(W=w\).
2. Any fixed-depth AND/OR/NOT circuit of response bits of sufficiently small size has acceptance probability almost the same under the actual mixture over \(S\) and the null. The statement holds for **every** such circuit on a good design; the entire circuit may be chosen using arbitrary preprocessing of that design. Quantitatively, for depth \(D\) and size at most \(M\), the difference is at most \(\eta\) whenever
   \[
        C_D(\log(M+2))^{C_D}\log(2/\eta)\le h_d.                 \tag{1}
   \]
3. For a circuit giving a list of at most \(L\) full label candidates, the probability of including the true label is at most \(L/q^\ell+\eta\) conditional on a good design, provided (1) holds with depth \(D+3\) and size \(M+O(Ld\log q)\). The same bound holds for a list of \(W\)-values. Fresh randomized circuits are also covered.

In particular polynomial-size, constant-depth Boolean circuits have success at most \(\exp[-c d/(\log d)^{C'}]\) for polynomial-sized lists, with constants depending on their polynomial size and depth bounds. The theorem also gives a stretched-exponential size/error tradeoff directly from (1). In contrast, with \(b=\beta/d\) and \(k b^2\to t>\log q\), the same experiment quantized with any power-of-two bin count \(\gg\sqrt d\) has optimal statistical success tending to one. Thus the restriction is on classical circuit computation, not a sample shortage. No restriction on how the design is inspected offline is imposed. Circuits with modular counting, threshold gates, arbitrary arithmetic, or unbounded depth are not analyzed; neither a solver nor an implication for deployment is supplied.

There are also two extensions in the broad range \(b=\beta/d\). First, the same circuit bounds, with an exponentially small additional error, hold when the circuit is first given **any fixed collection of linearly independent binary parities** of the response bits instead of the bits themselves. The independent parity collection may be chosen from the entire design; this includes an invertible binary change of coordinates. Second, a learner allowed an adaptive decision tree using at most \(c d\) exact binary parity probes, chosen arbitrarily from the preceding probe replies and the design, has exponentially small probability of a correct label guess even if computation between probes is unrestricted. Section 5 states the precise qualification for each extension. Neither result covers general circuits with unrestricted, possibly dependent modular gates.

## 2. No short coefficient relation modulo the projected label information

Identify \(R\) additively with \((\mathbb Z/q)^d\), with the ordinary coefficient dot pairing. The scalar phase index before adding noise at position \((i,j)\) is the linear functional of \(S\) with coefficient vector
\[
                       u_{ij}=X^j\overline{A_i},\qquad
                  \overline{A_i}=A_i(X^{-1}).
\]
Indeed multiplication by \(X\) is a signed permutation of the coefficient basis and its transpose is multiplication by \(X^{-1}\), proving the adjoint rule by linearity. Write \(V_I\) for the additive subgroup spanned by the coefficient coordinate vectors \(e_i\) for \(i\in I\); it has cardinality \(q^\ell\).

Fix a nonempty set of at most \(h_d\) scalar positions and nonzero weights \(c_{ij}\in\mathbb Z/q\) there. For row \(i\), put \(P_i=\sum_j c_{ij} X^j\). At least one \(P_i\ne0\) because degree-\(<d\) coefficients represent \(R\) faithfully. Since the \(\overline A_i\) are independent uniform, the sum \(\sum_i P_i\overline A_i\) is uniform in the nonzero ideal \(J=\sum_i P_i R\): it is the image of a homomorphism from independent uniform finite abelian groups, which has equally large fibers. The lower bound
\[
                        |J|\ge e^{\alpha_q d}                   \tag{2}
\]
holds with \(\alpha_q>0\) fixed, for every nonzero ideal. A nonzero finite \(R\)-module \(J\) has a simple quotient, which is \(R/\mathfrak m\) for a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) by cyclicity of simple modules. Every such residue is over some odd prime \(r\mid q\). The factor degree of \(X^d+1\) mod \(r\) is \(\operatorname{ord}_{2d}(r)\), since all roots have exact order \(2d\), and it grows proportionally to \(d\) at fixed \(r\) by the successive-squaring formula \(v_2(r^{2^j}-1)=v_2(r^2-1)+j-1\) for \(j\ge1\). The associated residue field therefore has exponential size. This proves (2) even for a composite odd \(q\).

As a result, the probability that the fixed short combination lies in \(V_I\) is at most \(q^\ell e^{-\alpha_q d}\le e^{-3\alpha_q d/4}\) when \(c_0\) is small. The number of possible \(I\)'s is at most \(e^{dH(c_0)}\) up to a subexponential factor, where \(H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)\). Take \(c_0\) small enough that \(H(c_0)<\alpha_q/8\) as well. The number of nontrivial short combinations is at most \((qkd+1)^{h_d+1}\), since each nonzero position is specified by its index and its weight. As \(kd\le d^{A+1}\), choose \(c_2\) sufficiently small to pay both union bounds. On the resulting good event, **no** nontrivial combination of up to \(h_d\) positions lies in any such \(V_I\).

Consider any such selected positions together with all of the \(\ell\) coordinates of \(W\). Their map on \(S\) is onto the product additive group \((\mathbb Z/q)^{\#\mathrm{positions}+\ell}\). Indeed, if it were not onto, some nontrivial character of the target would vanish on its image; this is finite abelian group duality and holds for composite \(q\) as well. The resulting coefficient identity would either nontrivially combine the response positions into \(V_I\), or give a nontrivial relation among distinct coordinate vectors \(e_i\). Neither is possible. Uniform \(S\) therefore makes these phase indices and \(W\) jointly independent uniform. Adding the independent scalar errors gives exact independence of those continuous responses with marginal \(g_b\), after conditioning on any \(W=w\). For any set of up to \(h_d\) response bits, it involves at most \(h_d\) scalar positions. At each such position the equal-reference-probability bin has uniform index, hence independent fair bits, proving part 1. No independence assumption was made on the entire design-induced phase vector.

## 3. From exact limited independence to distinguishing and list lower bounds

[Braverman](https://doi.org/10.1145/1754399.1754401) established that polylogarithmic independence fools \(\mathrm{AC}^0\). Use the improved error dependence of [Harsha–Srinivasan, Corollary 14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.08121), in the form that depth-\(D\), size-\(M\) circuits are \(\eta\)-fooled by \(C_D(\log(M+2))^{C_D}\log(2/\eta)\)-wise independence for a constant \(C_D\) depending on depth. We may count inputs and output wires in \(M\) so that the declared size bounds cover the usual size notion for the theorem. It applies to the response-bit laws in §2 separately for each \(w\) at fixed good design, hence also to their mixture. This yields the acceptance bound in part 2. There is no need to control the circuit as a function of the design, because the independence event was proved before any choice of circuit made from that design.

For a list-producing circuit and a fixed \(w\), append a test \(C_w\) that accepts if at least one listed \(I\)-projection equals \(w\). This uses equality to fixed bit strings by AND of literals and an OR across the list, adding at most three gate layers and \(O(Ld\log q)\) size under the above convention. Use a fixed ordinary injective bit encoding of residues; invalid encodings can be discarded, or simply never counted as a correct projection. The fooling bound for \(\Pr(C_w=1\mid A,W=w)\) differs by at most \(\eta\) from its null value. Average over all \(w\), which are uniform given \(A\):
\[
  \Pr[W\text{ is in the projected list}\mid A]
      \le q^{-\ell}\sum_w \Pr_{\rm null}(C_w=1\mid A)+\eta
      \le L/q^\ell+\eta.
\]
The last inequality is pointwise for any output list, regardless of correlations among its entries. No union factor \(q^\ell\) multiplies the fooling error. Full-label success implies projected success. Fixing and averaging fresh independent random coins permits randomized circuits. With polynomial size, take \(\eta\) an exponential in a constant times \(d/(\log d)^{C'}\) with sufficiently large \(C'\); the added size for a polynomial list is still polynomial. The bad design probability is exponentially small in \(d\) and hence does not change this upper scale in the full experiment.

## 4. Why the finite responses can contain enough information

For \(b=\beta/d\), \(k b^2\to t>\log q\), the [fixed odd-modulus statistical theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/18d24b1b111079badad2799860d77be0c27ec244/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-fixed-odd-modulus-threshold.md) gives exact-label statistical success tending to one under the uniform prior for the original continuous observations. One may see the strict above-threshold regime directly: true log likelihood relative to the full-grid response reference has mean \(t d+o(d)\) and variance \(O(d)\). On typical designs the false-pair integrals against that reference are bounded by a constant for every distinct pair (use the bounded-overlap argument in [the adaptive-prior note](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/27381dbb91194c272fc9d65875df9abd95ad11a8/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-design-adaptive-prior.md)). The total of wrong likelihood weights divided by \(q^d\) then has bounded expectation under the true joint law and cannot overwhelm the true weight, which exceeds \(e^{cd}\) with probability tending to one. This is an unrestricted probability comparison, not a time bound on the optimum.

To transfer sufficient information to our discretization, use the scalar cumulative-distribution coordinate \(u=\int_0^y g_b(v)\,dv\). This makes the reference uniform, and the true density at a shift \(a/q\) is \(f_b(y-a/q)/g_b(y)\) in the \(u\)-coordinate. Its derivative in \(u\) is uniformly \(O(b)\) and its values are \(1+O(b)\), by the Fourier series and positivity of \(g_b\) for small \(b\). The quantile bins become ordinary equal-width intervals in \(u\). Reconstructing inside each reported interval with uniform reference measure, independently, changes the one-coordinate density by \(O(b/2^p)\) uniformly. The product TV loss is at most \(O(\sqrt{kd}\,b/2^p)=O(\sqrt d/2^p)\) by Hellinger tensorization, uniformly in all labels and designs. Thus \(2^p\gg\sqrt d\) suffices to preserve unrestricted success. This reconstruction is only a statistical comparison and is not asserted to be a constant-depth computation.

## 5. Independent parity preprocessing and adaptive parity queries

Now assume \(b=\beta/d\). On a good design and for every fixed \(W=w\), let \(P\) denote the response-bit law. Its bias against every nontrivial parity of the bits is at most
\[
        \Delta_d=(C_q b)^{h_d+1}\le e^{-c_3 d} .                 \tag{2}
\]
To see this, group the parity's selected bits into the distinct scalar positions it touches; in any such block the resulting nontrivial parity of the scalar bin index has zero expectation under the reference fair bits and is bounded by 1 in absolute value. If the number of touched blocks is at most \(h_d\), its expectation under \(P\) is exactly zero by §2. Otherwise, conditional on the full true label, the scalar positions are independent and the one-coordinate density ratio against \(g_b\) differs from 1 by at most \(C_q b\) uniformly over all shifts. Therefore the absolute expectation of this parity is at most \((C_q b)^{\#\text{blocks}}\). Averaging over the full label conditional on \(W=w\) does not increase the bound. Since \(h_d\) is a positive multiple of \(d/\log d\), (2) follows. These calculations again hold for every \(w\) and every allowed projection choice, without requiring the parities to be fixed before seeing the design.

Suppose the response preprocessor computes \(n'\) linearly independent parities over \(\mathbb F_2\), with arbitrary fixed offsets. Under the null their outputs are independent fair bits because the map onto its \(n'\) output bits is surjective. Every nontrivial output parity is a nontrivial input parity, and so has bias at most \(\Delta_d\) under \(P\). To use a bounded-independence theorem with small but nonzero bias, an elementary correction suffices. For the density \(f\) of the output relative to fair bits, remove all its nonzero Fourier coefficients of degrees at most \(a\), obtaining \(g\). If
\[
         \Lambda=\Delta_d\sum_{j=1}^{a}\binom{n'}j,
\]
then \(|g-f|\le\Lambda\) pointwise and \(g\ge-\Lambda\). The density \((g+\Lambda)/(1+\Lambda)\) is nonnegative, integrates to one, and is exactly \(a\)-wise independent. Its TV distance from \(f\) is at most \(3\Lambda/2\), by the displayed sup-norm error and \(E|f-1|\le2\). Apply the circuit theorem with \(a\) at least the left side of (1), or with the amended size and depth for the list event. The acceptance error is then at most \(\eta+3\Lambda/2\) for each \(w\). In particular for \(n'\) and circuit sizes polynomial in \(d\), choose \(a\le c_3 d/(4\log(n'+2))\) and \(\eta=\exp[-c d/(\log d)^{C'}]\) with sufficiently large \(C'\). The correction is exponentially small, and the list success bound averaged over \(w\) is unchanged except for this additive error. The claim requires independent output parities; dependent parity-gate networks need not have independent fair inputs to their outer circuits, so this proof does not treat them.

For the adaptive probe bound, fix the design and the learner's coins. A deterministic tree with at most \(m\) queries asks an arbitrary parity of all response bits at each node. The observation at its leaf determines any terminal output. Each reachable leaf is the conjunction of the parity constraints along its path, hence either empty or an affine subspace of codimension \(r\le m\). The indicator of a feasible leaf is the uniform signed average of its \(2^r\) affine characters; the coefficient of the trivial character gives precisely the null mass and the remaining contribution to its actual probability is at most \(\Delta_d(1-2^{-r})\) by (2). There are at most \(2^m\) leaves, so conditional on \(W=w\) the full leaf law differs from its null law in TV by at most \(2^{m-1}\Delta_d\). The same argument as for circuits (sum the bounded event for a fixed \(w\), then average) bounds list success by \(L/q^\ell+2^{m-1}\Delta_d\). If \(m\le c_3 d/(2\log 2)\) and \(L\) is polynomial, this is exponentially small; independent coins may be averaged. The probes here are noiseless but limited to parity functions. This does not yield a bound on arbitrary \(m\)-bit summaries or general query computations.

## 6. Prior and limitation

Bounded independence as a tool for unconditional restricted-circuit security is also explicit in [Degwekar–Vaikuntanathan–Vasudevan](https://opencryptology.org/2016/580). Here the independence is a direct property of the conditional wrapped ring observation model, not a new pseudorandom generator construction. The earlier [response-coordinate degree obstruction](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/1aaf3b754c946fbefcfadcdee20fd3ae10648a1b/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-coordinate-degree-and-tv.md) treats low-degree correlation and points out that this does not in itself imply hardness for a larger algorithmic class. Here the exact joint short-support relation condition, still valid after conditioning on a fixed linear-sized label projection, permits a rigorous consequence for ordinary bounded-depth Boolean circuits using a separate fooling theorem. In particular the conclusion is not based on applying an \(L^2\) polynomial approximation under the null and silently transferring its error to an exponentially tilted law. It remains a classical restricted computation result and not a statement against unrestricted polynomial-time or other gate classes.
