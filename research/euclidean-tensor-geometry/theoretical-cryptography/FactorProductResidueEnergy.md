# Two-prime products modulo almost all moduli at the support scale

Products generated by two primes cannot look uniform modulo integers much larger than the size of the product family. Below that obstruction, they do look uniform on average over the modulus. We sharpen both of the logarithmic losses in Friedlander–Kurlberg–Shparlinski (2008), Theorem 1: for general integer moduli the main variance term gains two powers of the factor-scale logarithm, and for prime moduli it gains one. The conclusion applies both to the primes up to a cutoff and to a balanced prime interval.

## Statements

Let $\mathcal P$ be either all primes $\le X$ or all primes in $[X,2X]$, and put $M=|\mathcal P|\asymp X/\log X$. Use either of two counting conventions:

- each ordered $(p,q)\in\mathcal P^2$ is counted, including $p=q$;
- each unordered pair of distinct primes in $\mathcal P$ is counted once.

For $m\ge2$ let $C_m(a)$ be the number of such products in the unit class $a\bmod m$, let $T_m=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times}C_m(a)$, and define
$$
V_m=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times}
       \left|C_m(a)-\frac{T_m}{\varphi(m)}\right|^2.
$$
For every fixed $A,C>0$, uniformly for integer $2\le Q\le X^C$,
$$
 \sum_{Q<m\le2Q}V_m
 \ll_{A,C} Q M^2 + X^4(\log X)^{-A}.
 \tag{1}
$$
For prime moduli $q$ in $Q<q\le2Q$, and any fixed $\epsilon>0$, uniformly for $X^{1+\epsilon}\le Q\le X^C$,
$$
 \sum_{\substack{Q<q\le2Q\\q\ {\rm prime}}}V_q
 \ll_{\epsilon,C}\frac{Q M^2}{\log Q}.
 \tag{2}
$$
For the original ordered counting convention these improve the main terms $QX^2$ and $QX^2/(\log X)^2$ in [Friedlander–Kurlberg–Shparlinski, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.1562), respectively. Replacing our center by their center has negligible effect in (1), as checked below.

To state the statistical consequence, choose a product by the corresponding counting convention, conditioned on both prime factors being coprime to $m$. Denote its law on the unit group by $D_m$ and the uniform unit law by $U_m$. With either the uniform integer-modulus or prime-modulus average over $(Q,2Q]$, for $Q\le X^C$,
$$
 \mathbb E_m\,\chi^2(D_m\Vert U_m)
 \ll_{A,C} \frac{Q}{M^2}+(\log X)^{-A},
 \tag{3}
$$
where $\chi^2(D\Vert U)=\sum_a(D(a)-U(a))^2/U(a)$. Therefore the average total variation goes to zero for $Q=o(M^2)$, and for all but an $o(1)$ fraction of the sampled moduli, all but an $o(1)$ fraction of unit classes have the expected count up to relative $o(1)$ error. Conversely, both averages obey
$$
 \mathbb E_m\,d_{\rm TV}(D_m,U_m)\ \ge\ 1-O(M^2/Q),
 \tag{4}
$$
which tends to one for $Q/M^2\to\infty$. This is an average scale statement; it gives neither a limiting law at $Q\asymp M^2$ nor coverage of every residue class or every individual modulus.

## Integer moduli: retain the sequence energy and sum the deletions in mean square

Let $a_n$ be the product sequence with the chosen counting convention. Each coefficient belongs to $\{0,1,2\}$ by unique factorization; the sequence is supported on $n\le N=\lceil 4X^2\rceil$ and
$$
 E:=\sum_n|a_n|^2\asymp M^2.
$$
Extend Dirichlet characters by zero on nonunits. Orthogonality gives the exact identity
$$
 V_m=\frac1{\varphi(m)}\sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}
          \left|\sum_n a_n\chi(n)\right|^2.
 \tag{5}
$$
The principal character term is really $T_m$ in the numerator, since a product with a prime dividing $m$ is not a unit.

Write a nonprincipal $\chi\bmod m$ as induced by a primitive $\psi\bmod f$ with $m=fe$ and $f\ge2$. Put
$$
 S_f(\psi)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\psi(p),\quad
 B_f(\psi)=\sum_n a_n\psi(n),\quad
 T_e(\psi)=\sum_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P\\p\mid e}}\psi(p),
 \qquad d(e)=\#\{p\in\mathcal P:p\mid e\}.
$$
The single-prime sum for $\chi$ is exactly $S_f-T_e$, including when $f,e$ share primes, because $\psi$ already vanishes there. In the ordered convention $B_f=S_f^2$. In the distinct convention $B_f=(S_f^2-\sum_p\psi(p)^2)/2$. The product-sum change on passing from $\psi$ to $\chi$ has squared modulus in either convention at most
$$
 O\big(d(e)^2 |S_f(\psi)|^2+d(e)^4\big).
 \tag{6}
$$
For the distinct convention the change of the squared-prime diagonal is bounded by $d(e)$ as well. This yields (6) also when $d(e)=0$.

By orthogonality over **all** characters modulo $f$,
$$
 \frac1{\varphi(f)}\sum_{\psi\bmod f}|S_f(\psi)|^2
 =\#\{p,p'\in\mathcal P:\ p\equiv p'\bmod f,\ (pp',f)=1\}
 \ll M(1+X/f).
$$
Since $\varphi(fe)\ge\varphi(f)\varphi(e)$, summing the error (6) over the conductor decomposition is bounded by a constant times
$$
 \sum_{e\le2Q}\frac1{\varphi(e)}
     \sum_{2\le f\le2Q/e}\big(d(e)^2 M(1+X/f)+d(e)^4\big)
 \ll_C QM+MX(1+\log X)^4+Q.
 \tag{7}
$$
For this, use $d(e)\le\omega(e)\ll\log(2e)$, $\sum_{e\le Y}1/\varphi(e)\ll\log(2Y)$, and $\sum_{e\ge1}(1+\log e)^4/(e\varphi(e))<\infty$. The totient estimates follow from the bounded mean of $e/\varphi(e)$: expand $\prod_{p\mid e}(1+1/(p-1))$ into a squarefree divisor sum; its mean is bounded by $\prod_p(1+1/(p(p-1)))$. In (7) we used $\sum_{f\le Y}f^{-1}=O(\log(2Y))$ and $\log Q\ll_C\log X$. The error (7) is admissible in (1).

The main remaining sum is bounded by
$$
 \sum_{e\le2Q}\frac1{\varphi(e)}
 \sum_{2\le f\le2Q/e}\frac1{\varphi(f)}
          \sum_{\psi\bmod f}^{*}|B_f(\psi)|^2,
 \tag{8}
$$
where the star denotes primitive characters. Fix $F=(\log X)^B$, with $B$ a sufficiently large constant in terms of $A$. For $f\le F$, Siegel–Walfisz on the prime sums gives $S_f(\psi)=O_{D,B}(X(\log X)^{-D})$ for arbitrary $D$; the primitive character is nonprincipal. In the distinct convention the extra term is bounded by $M$. Thus $|B_f(\psi)|\ll_{D,B} X^2(\log X)^{-D}$ for arbitrary $D$ in either convention. Summing over $f\le F$ and $e$ in (8) gives $O_{A,C}(X^4(\log X)^{-A})$ after choosing $D$.

For $f>F$, the multiplicative large sieve with conductor weight $f/\varphi(f)$ gives on a dyadic block $Y<f\le2Y$
$$
 \sum_{Y<f\le2Y}\frac1{\varphi(f)}
     \sum_{\psi\bmod f}^{*}|B_f(\psi)|^2
 \ll (Y+N/Y) E.
$$
Sum the $Y$ and $N/Y$ terms as two geometric series, instead of multiplying their supremum by the number of blocks. The inner sum in (8) is at most $O((Q/e+N/F)E)$. Consequently its total is $O(QE+NE\log(2Q)/F)$. Choosing $B$ accordingly proves (1), by (7).

For comparison with the 2008 normalization, centering at the full ordered mass $M^2/\varphi(m)$ adds the square of the constant offset times $\varphi(m)$: the cross term with the exactly centered residual vanishes. The extra summed variance is at most
$$
 O\!\left(M^2\sum_{Q<m\le2Q}\frac{d(m)^2}{\varphi(m)}\right)
 \ll_C M^2(1+\log X)^3.
$$
Here $T_m=(M-d(m))^2$. The displayed error is absorbed by $X^4(\log X)^{-A}$, so the older center makes no difference to the improvement of its bound.

## Prime moduli: the rough support saves the extra logarithm

For $X^{1+\epsilon}\le Q\le X^C$, use Iwaniec's prime-moduli large sieve [*The large sieve with prime moduli*, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.4171/RMI/1381). Write $D=Q/\sqrt N$ and choose
$$
 z=\exp((\log\log Q)^5),\qquad P_0=\prod_{\ell\le z}\ell .
$$
The length condition $N\le Q^2(\log Q)^{-8}$ and $z<D$ hold for large $X$, and $\log D\gg_{\epsilon,C}\log Q$. For coefficients supported on $(n,P_0)=1$, Iwaniec's factor is
$$
 1+(\log\log Q)^{5/2}
        \prod_{\ell\le z}(1-1/\ell)^{1/2}=O(1)
$$
by Mertens' product estimate. The dyadic additive large-sieve sum for these coefficients is therefore
$$
 \sum_{\substack{Q<q\le2Q\\q\ {\rm prime}}}
 \sum_{a\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}
       \left|\sum_n a_n e(an/q)\right|^2
 \ll_{\epsilon,C}\frac{Q^2 E}{\log Q}.
 \tag{9}
$$
Here $e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)$. For each prime $q$ the nonprincipal characters are primitive. The Gauss sum identity and character orthogonality over unit $a$ bound their sum of squares by the additive sum over $a$ on the left of (9), since the Gauss sums have squared modulus $q$. Divide by $\varphi(q)\asymp Q$ in (5) to obtain the desired variance for the rough coefficients.

In the balanced prime pool the entire product support is already rough, since $z=X^{o(1)}<X$. In the all-primes pool temporarily delete products with a prime factor $\le z$. For the deleted coefficients the squared norm is $O(M\pi(z))=X^{1+o(1)}$; unique factorization bounds all the weights. The ordinary multiplicative large sieve and (5) bound their summed contribution by $O((Q+N/Q)X^{1+o(1)})$, which is $o(QM^2/\log Q)$ in the range of (2). Splitting the squared sums at a factor of two proves (2) for the original pool.

## Consequences and limits

Uniformly for $m\le2X^C$, $d(m)=O_C(\log X)$, so $T_m\asymp M^2$ in either convention. By direct normalization, $\chi^2(D_m\Vert U_m)=\varphi(m)V_m/T_m^2$ and $d_{\rm TV}(D_m,U_m)\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2(D_m\Vert U_m)}$. Equation (1) proves (3) for all integer moduli after increasing its logarithmic saving. Equation (2), together with the number of primes $\asymp Q/\log Q$ in the averaging interval, proves it for prime moduli in that range. To cover smaller prime moduli, use (2) above $Q=X^{3/2}$; for $(\log X)^{A+5}<Q\le X^{3/2}$ the ordinary large sieve gives averaged $\chi^2\ll (Q+X^2/Q)\log(2Q)/M^2\ll_A(\log X)^{-A}$. For still smaller $Q$ use Siegel–Walfisz on individual characters as in the small-conductor part above. The statement about relative counts follows by applying Markov's inequality to the average of their squared relative errors.

For (4), the support contains at most $M(M+1)/2$ classes even before dropping nonunits. Comparing this support with the uniform law gives $1-d_{\rm TV}\le M(M+1)/(2\varphi(m))$. The average of $1/\varphi(m)$ over integer moduli in the dyadic block is $O(1/Q)$ by the bounded mean proved above, and it is $O(1/Q)$ individually for prime moduli there. This shows why moving the average estimate to the support scale is meaningful. The results do not address worst-case moduli or recover the factors of any integer.
