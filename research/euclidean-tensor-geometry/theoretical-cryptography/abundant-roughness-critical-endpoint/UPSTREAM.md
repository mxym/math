# Sources, prior scope and version fingerprints

1. Joseph M. Shunia, [*Deterministic integer factorization from abundance decisions*, arXiv:2611.34242v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34242v1), 25 November 2026. The last sentence of §5.2, printed p. 15 (PDF page index 14), explicitly leaves c=1 unsettled by the preceding arguments. Theorem 5.2, printed p. 14, proves the existence of suitably rough abundance-oracle queries for 0<c<1, and finiteness of suitably rough abundant integers for c>1. Our result is the arithmetic existence statement at c=1, not the oracle construction at c=1 and not a no-oracle factoring algorithm.
2. Lluis Eriksson, [*Sharp Normalization Exponents, a 19x Rescaled-Vandermonde Contraction, Linear-Exponent Bounded-omega(n) Factoring, and Resolution of the c = 1 Endpoint for Abundance-Oracle Factorization*, AIRR ARR-2026-1ZVBB45HG78WNVDH v1](https://airr.science/papers/ARR-2026-1ZVBB45HG78WNVDH/versions/v1/), [fixed PDF](https://airr.science/papers/ARR-2026-1ZVBB45HG78WNVDH/ARR-2026-1ZVBB45HG78WNVDH-v1.pdf), [archive-recorded canonical PDF SHA-256 c1bcf55a9afe5ac894558ee686acc4fabe430991031ce56c696e5bbe28c7f6d1](https://airr.science/papers/ARR-2026-1ZVBB45HG78WNVDH/versions/v1/#disclosures). Despite the broad title, Theorem 3(b), printed p. 3, explicitly assumes Theta<1, where Theta is the supremum of real parts of nontrivial zeta zeros. Its detailed hydrogen assessment also identifies this scope. Part (a) concerns a smooth-prime model, not actual primes. Our proof removes the hypothesis Theta<1, with a different method. The archive assessment is not used as a mathematical premise or represented as journal peer review.
3. Harold G. Diamond and Janos Pintz, [*Oscillation of Mertens' product formula*](https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_2009__21_3_523_0.pdf), J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 21(3) (2009), 523–533, DOI [10.5802/jtnb.687](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.687). Theorem 1.1 and the equivalent form (2.1) are on printed p. 524 (PDF page index 2). The negative form of (2.1), with log M(x)-gamma-log log x less than -H/(sqrt(x)log x) for arbitrarily large x and any fixed H>0, is used directly. Equation (1.1), printed p. 523, is the ordinary product asymptotic. Only the unconditional statements are used.
4. Douglas E. Iannucci, [*On the smallest abundant number not divisible by the first k primes*](https://doi.org/10.36045/bbms/1113318127), Bull. Belg. Math. Soc. Simon Stevin 12 (2005), 39–44, Theorem 1 gives the logarithmic exponent 2 up to arbitrary exponent slack. Joshua Zelinsky, [*On the small prime factors of a non-deficient number*](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/x13/x13.pdf), Integers 23 (2023), A13, studies distinct prime-factor counts, notably in terms of the second, third and fourth smallest primes. These are prior background on rough abundance, not the critical constant obtained here.

The source comparison is bounded and not a claim of first priority. The manuscript uses natural logs throughout.
