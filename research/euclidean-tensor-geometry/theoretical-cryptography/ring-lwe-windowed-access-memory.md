# A classical memory lower bound with local random access in each scan

A lower bound for repeated scans in exactly the same order does not directly cover learners that seek or reread data locally. We prove that the quadratic persistent-state lower bound for the broad wrapped-Gaussian ring experiment survives bounded backtracking within each scan, even when each requested position and the amount of local rereading are chosen from the data. The essential step is to represent a continuous row by an \(O(d)\)-bit latent symbol, with a once-per-position random interpretation. No precision bound on the original learner is needed.

## 1. Model and results

Fix any odd \(q\ge3\), dyadic \(d\), \(R=(\mathbb Z/q)[X]/(X^d+1)\), and \(0<b\le1/(100d)\). A uniform label \(S\in R\) is drawn once. At each of \(T\) positions draw an independent row conditional on \(S\):
\[
 A\sim U_R,\quad Y=AS/q+E\pmod{\mathbb Z^d},
 \quad E\sim\bigotimes_{p=1}^d f_b,\quad
 f_b(y)=\sum_h b^{h^2}e^{2\pi i h y}.
\]
The actual pair \((A,Y)\) at a position is unchanged on every revisit or restart. A learner may restart its head at most \(P-1\) times. Within a scan, requested position \(i\) is allowed if \(i\) is no more than \(B\) positions behind the largest index previously requested in that scan. Forward skips of any length are allowed. The learner may reread the same positions arbitrarily often within the window, can choose requests from its state, and may stop the scan early. It keeps at most \(m\) bits of state beyond its externally given current scan number and current requested position; in particular no time counter, output tape, or other memory stores information for later use unless it is included in the state. On receiving a row it can use any measurable transition, arbitrary transient computation and precision, and fresh independent randomness. The terminal output is a guess for \(S\). The budget concerns the information retained between operations, not working precision while one row is in hand.

**Theorem.** For each fixed \(P\) there is \(c>0\), depending only on \(q,P\), such that for all sufficiently large \(d\), if \(T\le e^{cd}\), \(m\le cd^2\), and \(B\le cd\), the probability of correctness is at most \(e^{-c d}\) after decreasing \(c\) if necessary. The statement is uniform in the range of \(b\). It also holds for final lists of size \(e^{cd}\), with smaller constants.

More generally, if \(P=o(\log\log d)\), then for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\), for all sufficiently large \(d\), bounds \(m\le d^{2-\epsilon}\), \(B\le d^{1-\epsilon}\), and \(T\le\exp(d^{1-\epsilon})\) imply correctness at most \(\exp(-d^{1-\epsilon})\). If \(P\) is 1, use two scans with one idle scan. List size at most \(\exp(d^{1-\epsilon})\) is also covered (possibly after increasing the lower cutoff on \(d\)).

There is a second access model to which the same budgets apply. In each scan the learner reads each position exactly once, in a possibly data-dependent order. For every possible path, let \(j_1,\ldots,j_t\) be its first \(t\) requests of that scan. Assume the number of skipped but not yet consumed positions behind the furthest request satisfies
\[
                \max_{1\le u\le t}j_u-t\ \le B               \tag{1}
\]
at every prefix. No bound on numerical backtracking is then needed: the small number of outstanding rows replaces the window. The same conclusion holds in this model, even for different orders in different scans. In particular it covers arbitrary local reordering of contiguous blocks of size at most \(B+1\).

Neither version gives a lower bound for general random access, unrestricted running time, or a specific cryptographic implementation. For \(b=\beta/d\) in the stated range, the ordinary idealized experiment is statistically sufficient with \(O_q(d^2)\) rows for an unrestricted decision; see the [fixed-order reference, §6](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/09dead42499780e321e58b7b995a547af89d2dec/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-constant-pass-memory-lower-bound.md). Thus the theorem restricts an access and state model, not just the supplied number of samples.

## 2. Finite reproducing symbols for continuous rows

We use the positive sign-mixture representation of the [fixed-order bound, §§3–4](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/09dead42499780e321e58b7b995a547af89d2dec/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-constant-pass-memory-lower-bound.md). We recall exactly what is needed for the memory accounting.

The reference density \(g(y)=\prod_p f_{b^{q^2}}(q y_p)\) is at least \(1/2\), and a nonconstant mode \(u\in\mathbb Z^d\), \(w=u\bmod q\ne0\), has weight at most \(8 b^{\|u\|^2}\). Conditional on the mode draw independent uniform \(a,\tau\in R\) and a deterministic binary sign \(F_w(as+\tau)\), where \(F_w\) is a balanced sign on character-value fibers. The output conditional on these finite data has density \(g\) plus the sign times a cosine of amplitude \(1/2\) with its public phase. For such a sign, the target Fourier coefficient has magnitude at least \(1/4\); the exact choice of weight recovers the required Fourier term after averaging \(\tau\). Dummy residual weight outputs \(g\) and its binary sign is ignored. These densities are nonnegative and normalized. All mode distributions and auxiliary translations are independent of the hidden label; only the sign depends on it. The representation before truncation is exact.

Here truncate by **total squared coefficient weight** \(\|u\|_2^2\le d\), rather than a per-coordinate bound. The discarded probability is at most
\[
 8 b^{d/2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb Z^d}(\sqrt b)^{\|u\|_2^2}
 \ \le\ 8 b^{d/2}\exp(3d\sqrt b)
 \ =\ \exp(-\Omega(d\log d)).
\]
In the inequality use \(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}v^{j^2}\le1+3v\) for small \(v\). Move discarded weight to the dummy. Also \(\|u\|_1\le\|u\|_2^2\) for integer coefficients. Thus there are at most \(2^d\binom{2d}{d}\le8^d\) retained modes by assigning signs and counting nonnegative vectors of sum at most \(d\). The entire finite input catalogue, mode and \((a,\tau)\), has at most \(1+q^{2d}(8^d+1)\) elements. An index together with its binary sign needs only \(O_q(d)\) bits.

If a uniform index set of size \(2^n\) is desired, approximate the catalogue probabilities by dyadic masses of denominator \(2^n\), and split them by duplicating entries. Choose \(n=C_q d\) large enough that the resulting error in one row is \(e^{-4d}\), including the truncation for large \(d\). The concept set may be uniform of size \(2^n\) by mapping its points to \(R\) with balanced fibers; its projected distribution also differs from uniform pointwise in relative density and in total variation by \(e^{-4d}\) after increasing \(C_q\). Thus the alphabet size in bits is still \(O_q(d)\), uniformly in \(b\). When coupled to the original model, the independent initial rows give total discrepancy \(O((T+1)e^{-4d})\). Repeating a row later does not cause a further coupling charge.

To realize the continuous rows in the resulting finite experiment, attach an independent random seed to each **position**. Given the finite row and its sign, a measurable sampling map turns that seed into one torus response with the prescribed density. Such a map may be obtained, for example, using successive conditional distribution functions, which exist for these bounded Borel densities. The simulator uses the same positional seed every time that row is needed. For the lower-bound argument all these seeds can be fixed in advance, independently of the finite samples and of the concept. The resulting program may be nonuniform. On averaging the seeds, it sees the correct law of an unchanged continuous list. No finite approximation of the original learner's measurable transition on real inputs is required.

The binary extractor still has a linear parameter \(r_0=\lfloor c_q d\rfloor\) with slack under these finite changes. Indeed for a nonnegative density \(h\) on the secret ring, the square over \((a,\tau)\) of normalized correlation with \(F_w(as+\tau)\) is at most
\[
        (q/q^d)^2+e^{-\alpha_q d}\|h\|_2^2
\]
for an absolute \(\alpha_q>0\) at fixed \(q\). Orthogonality in the public translation gives a Fourier sum; each nonzero frequency's character index after the coefficient adjoint is uniform on a nonzero ideal of size at least \(e^{\alpha_q d}\). The zero coefficient is at most \(q/q^d\) due to the balanced fibers. This bound is uniform in the nonconstant mode and also for the sign chosen for the dummy. Averaging over a finite subset of modes therefore does not weaken it. The dyadic row approximation changes the probability of any bad row set by at most \(e^{-4d}\). If a density on the larger binary concept space is projected onto \(R\), conditional Jensen and the fiber relative-density bound increase its normalized \(L^2\) norm by at most \((1+e^{-4d})^{1/2}\). Markov and taking \(c_q\) small enough verify the extractor condition that \(\|h\|_2\le2^{r_0}\) implies correlation of absolute value over \(2^{-r_0}\) on at most \(2^{-r_0}\) of the row space. All constants can be chosen before seeing \(b,P,T\).

## 3. One sequential sweep simulates one local-access scan

For the backtracking version, simulate an original scan using one sweep of the finite input list in order \(1,\ldots,T\). When the learner asks for a new high-water mark, advance the finite sweep until it reaches that position, storing the most recent \(B+1\) row symbols with their signs. Every subsequent requested row at or behind the current mark lies in this buffer by the access restriction. Its position-dependent continuous interpretation is exactly the same as at its first read. Requesting a position before the window is forbidden on all paths. Each buffer position can be stored explicitly (at most \(\log_2(T+1)\) bits per entry); a cyclic buffer would also suffice. For the model (1), instead buffer all skipped but still unconsumed row symbols and remove the symbol once the original program consumes it. Between requests their number is at most \(B\), and including the currently arriving row it is at most \(B+1\). Each adaptive access or early stop is determined by the learner's allowed state, position and fresh coins.

We justify why this is an ordinary finite branching program with exactly \(T\) sequential steps per scan. Between two advances of the sequential reader, all data available to the simulated learner are reconstructible from the current finite state and the fixed positional seeds. Thus its entire local computation until requesting an unread position can be folded into a single transition on that finite state, regardless of how many local raw-row requests it makes. The next unread target index and the current simulated position can both be held in \(O(\log(T+1))\) bits while advancing past intermediate positions. If the scan ends before the last sequential layer, consume the remaining rows with no updates. The same finite list is then reread sequentially in the next scan. The implementation need not compute real values with bounded time or bounded numerical precision because it is defining transition functions on a finite state set, not proposing an efficient simulator. Independent private randomness can be sampled as fresh transition randomness and averaged, or fixed as part of the nonuniform transition tables. The once-per-position seeds for interpreting the samples are separate and reused identically. If the local program has a null set of nonterminating internal loops, fix an arbitrary transition there; the simulation concerns algorithms with a well-defined output. After the last sweep the same finite-state readout produces the guess or list.

The width in bits is therefore at most
\[
                m+C_q(B+1)(d+\log(T+1))+C_q\log(T+1).        \tag{2}
\]
The fixed positional seeds are not counted as writable memory: for each fixing they determine constants in the transition functions, independent of the example list and hidden label. Conversely no rows, response values, or adaptive choices depending on them are free program constants. This distinction is necessary to apply a finite-state lower bound to arbitrary continuous-input programs.

## 4. Apply the known same-order classical lower bound

The input to the simulated program is now the binary extractor experiment just described, uniformly sampled finite row labels and a deterministic sign from each row and hidden concept. It processes exactly the same stream in the same order on all \(P\) sweeps. Apply [Lyu–Tal–Wu–Yang, FOCS 2023, Theorem 6 and Definition 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08070v1) to the simulated program, not directly to the original access order. With \(k_{\rm ext}=\ell_{\rm ext}=r_{\rm ext}=r_0\), the theorem permits a width of at least \(2^{c_q d^2/\kappa_P}\) and per-pass length up to \(2^{c_q d/\kappa_P}\), for \(\kappa_P=\exp(C_1 3^P)\) with a sufficiently large absolute \(C_1\). For detail, the theorem's auxiliary parameter \(\ell\) may be a sufficiently small multiple of \(r_0/(P\,100^{3^{P-1}})\), with its length exponent a small multiple of \(\ell\). Its width exponent is at least a fixed multiple of \(r_0\ell/(P^{4P})\). The bad event is bounded by \(2^{-\ell/2^P}\). All these factors are absorbed in \(\kappa_P\). The final-state conditional concept density on the good event, relative to the uniform concept law, is at most \(2^{100^{3^{P-1}}\ell+1}\le2^{r_0/2}\). For a projected label its uniform prior mass is \((1+o(1))q^{-d}\), so even a list of size \(e^{c_q d/\kappa_P}\) has good-event success exponentially small in \(d\). The harmless padding convention \(W\ge(10\log|\mathcal X|)^2\) in the proof of the cited theorem is met by adding unused vertices, costing \(O(\log d)\) bits since the binary symbol size here is \(n=O_q(d)\).

For fixed \(P\), shrink \(c\) so that (2) falls beneath the permitted width, \(\log T\) beneath its length requirement, and the initial coupling error is negligible versus the asserted bound. For \(P=o(\log\log d)\), \(\kappa_P=d^{o(1)}\). Thus the proposed width in (2) is \(O_q(d^{2-\epsilon})\), well below \(d^{2-o(1)}\); the logarithm of the sample bound is below \(d^{1-o(1)}\) as required. The bad event is \(\exp(-d^{1-o(1)})\), and the allowed list size and initial coupling error do not change the stated upper bound. Averaging the positional seeds and any private coins proves the result.

## 5. Prior and scope

The cited extractor theorem already treats many identical-order sweeps, with both the constant-\(P\) scale and the dependence on growing \(P\). The positive Fourier/sign representation is also from the linked fixed-order derivation. Neither theorem by itself charges for storing continuous rows in a local-access simulator. The distinct additions here are the linear-bit truncation of the reproducing symbols, and the finite-state simulation of adaptive local rereads and out-of-order scans under either explicit buffer condition. This is a lower bound on a class of **classical calculations with restricted access and retained state**, not an unrestricted polynomial-time lower bound, a recovery method, or a statement about deployment parameters.
