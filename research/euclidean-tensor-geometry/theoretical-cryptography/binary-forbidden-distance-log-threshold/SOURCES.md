# Sources and scope

- **[JY]** Jianwei Jiang and Chunhua Yang, [*Binary Codes with a Forbidden Distance*, arXiv:2611.04523v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04523v1). Lemma 3.2 reduces a single even forbidden distance \(2r+2\) to the consecutive distances \(2r+1,2r+2\) in one fewer dimension; Proposition 3.3 lifts the extremal ball examples. Proposition 4.4 and Corollary 4.5 use the exact sufficient condition Eq. (4.10), \(V_N(r)V_N(t-2)>2^N V_N(t-3)\). Proposition 5.3 verifies it at \(n=N+1\ge(t+1)^2\), leading to Theorems 1.1–1.2. We use their extremal theorem conditional on Eq. (4.10). We do not claim new isoperimetry, parity lifting, or a new extremizer family.
- **Context.** Peter Keevash, Noam Lifshitz, Eoin Long and Dor Minzer, [*Forbidden intersections for codes*](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/keevash/papers/ForbidIntCodesJournal.pdf), JLMS 108 (2023), 2037–2083, DOI [10.1112/jlms.12801](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12801). Section 9 explicitly raised the binary alphabet case. Jiang–Yang answer it in a stated dimension range (with a parity distinction), and the present note extends the even-distance dimension range by determining the sharp asymptotic threshold of their existing criterion. It is not a new solution to arbitrary-alphabet forbidden intersections.
- **Numerical check only.** Herbert Robbins, [*A Remark on Stirling’s Formula*](https://doi.org/10.2307/2308012), Amer. Math. Monthly 62 (1955), 26–29, Eq. (2). The independent numeric check evaluates these simple factorial bounds in the moderate-deviation window; the main proof needs only the usual relative Stirling formula.
- **Bounded overlap check.** On 2026-11-28 the public Jiang–Yang arXiv record showed v1. We searched its title/identifier with “threshold”, “logarithmic”, “binomial condition”, and “even forbidden distance” and checked the near-distance and larger-alphabet literature above. No matching asymptotic threshold for Eq. (4.10) was found in these sources. The monotonicity and relative tail estimates are elementary, and the note claims their application and explicit threshold, not a new general binomial large-deviation theorem. The independent-set lower example is the elementary graph degree bound, not a new coding bound.
