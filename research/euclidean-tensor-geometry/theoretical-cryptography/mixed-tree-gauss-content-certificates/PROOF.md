# Prime-content certificates for constant monomial fibres of mixed correspondence trees

## 1. Setting and result

Fix k,d. Given a length L, a deterministic public rule lists in poly(L) bit operations a catalogue of bivariate integer polynomials of total degree at most d. Its dependence is on L alone; the bit lengths of its coefficients are polynomial in L, but their magnitudes need not be. Every edge equation F depends on both variables, and for every complex a neither F(a,Y) nor F(X,a) is identically zero. Equivalently neither one-variable polynomial content (over Q) has positive degree. This permits reducible and genuinely mixed correspondences. Edge equations can be used in either direction. A template is a labelled tree of r<=k vertices with an oriented catalogue equation on each edge. A singleton is allowed, with no edge.

Write V for the affine algebraic set specified by a template. Call a component of a nonzero monomial level positive-dimensional if it has positive complex dimension. There is an explicitly computable positive integer B_T for each template T, of bit length polynomial in L, such that the following hold:

* For any integer exponents e_i, the numerator and denominator of every nonzero rational constant c of the character prod X_i^{e_i} on a positive-dimensional irreducible component of the torus part of V have prime support contained in that of B_T. The same conclusion holds on a positive-dimensional irreducible component of any nonzero character level. We use the torus to ensure all negative powers are defined.
* In particular for n>1 and positive exponents, n has only finite complex affine fibres on T whenever gcd(n,B_T^L)<n, where L is the bit length of n. If B_T=1 all n>1 pass. The same test works if coefficients are very large.
* A sufficient condition that gives B_T=1 for every template is that, modulo every prime, the edge equations retain their two partial degrees, have nonzero trailing polynomials in each variable, and have neither vertical nor horizontal line components over the algebraic closure of the residue field. This sufficient local condition is decidable on the edge alone without factoring its coefficients.

Arithmetic consequence: use the distinct prime divisors of n as the vertices of a graph, with edges available when a catalogue equation holds in one order. On inputs admitting a true spanning tree with gcd(n,B_T^L)<n, and at most k distinct prime divisors, complete factorization from n alone has deterministic polynomial bit complexity. The witness tree and exponents are not supplied. In particular, when every template has B_T=1 this covers the full connected-graph class, no exceptions. An input with no accepted tuple need not be outside the bare connected-graph class, since all its true trees might fail the certificate. Failure of the test does not force a positive-dimensional fibre. No assertion is made about unpromised integers.

## 2. Computing the rooted certificate

Choose one root, with parameter t, of the fixed template. Use raw resultants in this paragraph. For each vertex construct P_v(t,Y) by P_root=Y-t and, for each child v of w, by

  P_v(t,Y) = Res_U(P_w(t,U),F_wv(U,Y)).

The edge equation is written in parent,child order. Over the algebraic closure of C(t), all roots of these polynomials are transcendental over C. At the root this is immediate. Inductively every root u of P_w is transcendental, so F(u,Y) has its full positive Y-degree and is nonzero. It cannot vanish at a constant a since F(U,a) is not identically zero. The root-product formula for the resultant gives a nonzero scalar times the product of these F(u,Y), counted with multiplicity. This proves the induction. The Sylvester determinant identity shows that at every affine point of V the raw P_v vanishes at (t,X_v), even if specialized degrees drop.

For the certificate only, divide the coefficients in Y by their maximal common factor in Q[t]; write the resulting polynomial in primitive integer form. Also remove any common factor Y^m. The latter removal is redundant for the raw recursion under our characteristic-zero hypotheses, but harmless. Denote the resulting polynomial by P'_v; let a_v(t) and z_v(t) be its nonzero leading and constant coefficients in Y. Both are integer polynomials. Define c(g) for nonzero g in Z[t] as the positive gcd of its integer coefficients. For this choice of root define

  b_root = product_v c(a_v)c(z_v).

Take B_T = gcd_root b_root, using all r possible root choices, not a product over the roots.

All degrees in this stage depend only on k,d. Indeed each Sylvester matrix has dimension bounded in terms of the preceding constant degree and d, and the tree has bounded depth. The determinant and all fixed-degree gcd and primitive normalization operations have polynomial bit complexity. Integer contents are just gcds of known coefficients, not their factorization. For a given fixed L one can list all these certificates in polynomial time: there are at most a constant times (1+catalogue size)^(k-1) templates, with the constant accounting for labels, trees and directions.

## 3. Gauss localization, with no smoothness requirement

Fix a positive-dimensional irreducible component C of a nonzero level (or one on which the character is constant). The projection to any single vertex has finite geometric fibres: after fixing a value, there are at most d choices for each adjacent variable, and the argument propagates along the tree. Thus the root t is nonconstant on C for every root. In particular no nonzero polynomial in C[t] (the polynomial ring over the complex field) vanishes at that element of the component's function field. The raw P_v still gives a relation in that field. Removing the common coefficient factor, and any factor Y^m, preserves that relation, because those factors are nonzero there. This argument applies on every component, whether or not its affine closure is singular, and does not assume that an individual integer slice meets it.

Let ell be a rational prime not dividing B_T. There is some root for which ell does not divide b_root. The ring R=Z[t]_(ell), localized at the prime ideal generated by ell (not a localization of Q[t] at ell), is the Gauss discrete valuation ring in Q(t). In fact every polynomial equals ell^a times a polynomial with at least one coefficient prime to ell, which is a unit in this localization. So R is integrally closed, with R intersect Q = Z_(ell). It injects into the component's function field by the root transcendence proved above. Each P'_v has all coefficients in R, and a_v and z_v are units in R by the definition of the root certificate. Dividing by a_v gives a monic relation for X_v; reversing its coefficient list and dividing by z_v gives a monic relation for 1/X_v. Thus both are integral over R. Products and integer powers of these units in the integral closure remain integral, so c and 1/c are integral over R. Since c is rational they are already in Frac(R), and integral closure gives c and 1/c in R intersect Q. Therefore v_ell(c)=0. Since every ell outside the support of B_T has such a root, the claimed support assertion follows. The proof uses no common root choice across all ell, no choice of a prime of definition of the component, and no arithmetic descent of its coefficients.

For the integer corollary, the valuations of n are at most L. Hence gcd(n,B_T^L)<n exactly when at least one prime of n lies outside the support of B_T. Then no positive-dimensional component can have constant character n on that template. We do not need gcd(n,B_T)=1. The previous argument concerns the *entire* level; it rules out positive-dimensional branches also in the presence of other isolated branches. This stronger gcd test is a certificate of zero-dimensionality; the conclusion of full factorization below does not assume that splitting by the gcd itself has factored anything.

## 4. A local all-prime criterion

For a fixed ell assume every edge retains its partial degrees on reduction, retains nonzero trailing polynomials in both variables, and satisfies the two finite-fibre conditions over the algebraic closure of F_ell. Starting with Y-t, run the raw recursion over F_ell(t). The induction from section 2 applies word for word with constant field the algebraic closure of F_ell, not just F_ell: each root is transcendental, so no nonzero leading coefficient vanishes there and no specialized child root is an algebraic constant. Thus the resulting Y-degree is the old parent degree times the edge Y-degree, as in characteristic zero, and its trailing coefficient is nonzero. By induction the degrees used to construct the Sylvester matrices over Z[t,Y] were retained, so reducing each such matrix proves agreement with the residue calculation. In particular the leading and trailing polynomials of each raw resultant are nonzero modulo ell. Integer primitive normalization cannot destroy that property. To see this, raw P_v is Q_v(t) times the primitive P'_v with Q_v integral after a choice of sign: Gauss's lemma clears denominators in the quotient because P'_v has integer content 1. Nonzero reduction of each raw endpoint implies nonzero reduction of each normalized endpoint. Here no Y^m factor occurs. Consequently ell does not divide b_root for any root. If the local condition holds at every prime, all b_root and all B_T equal 1.

For clarity one factorization-free way to certify a sufficient local condition is as follows. Check primitivity separately of the leading and trailing coefficient polynomials in both variables. Regard all coefficients in Y as polynomials in X and homogenize them to their same common maximum degree m in binary variables; do the swapped check as well. Require that the resulting homogeneous forms have no common projective zero over the algebraic closure of F_ell for any ell. Without degree retention this would be stronger than excluding affine zeros. Here at least one coefficient has the retained degree in the homogenized variable, so the point at infinity is already excluded. For m>=1, construct the integer matrix of tuples of homogeneous multipliers of degree m-1 mapped to their sum of products with the forms, in binary degree 2m-1. The matrix has full target rank over a field exactly when the forms have no common projective zero over its algebraic closure. Indeed a common zero obstructs spanning by evaluation. Conversely, over the infinite algebraic closure choose one nonzero form A, and a linear combination B of the list nonvanishing at every root of A. This is possible by avoiding a finite union of proper linear hyperplanes. Then A,B are coprime, and the linear map (U,V)->UA+VB on two degree m-1 multipliers is injective (A cannot divide any such nonzero V), hence an isomorphism by dimension 2m. Descent of matrix rank gives the criterion over the field. Thus the gcd of all full-size minors equals 1 precisely when the target rank is full at every prime. If all maximal minors vanish, the test is rejected. If m=0 the check is just gcd of the constants. These matrix dimensions are fixed by d, so this test costs polynomial bit time; it uses no arithmetic information about the prospective input n. Primitivity of both endpoint polys also ensures the two partial degrees remain as required. The main B_T criterion does not need this sufficient local test.

## 5. Arithmetic consequence and examples

For fixed n enumerate the finite template classes and positive exponent lists with sum <=L. Skip trees that fail their gcd test, and for every surviving template impose the original edge equations and prod X_i^{e_i}=n. Do not impose the divided eliminants; their only purpose was to certify the level has no positive-dimensional branch. Polynomial degrees are O(L), coefficient bits polynomial in L, and the number of real variables is fixed. Standard fixed-dimensional real algebraic decomposition enumerates the finite real solution sets with polynomial bit cost. Test integrality exactly, then primality, distinctness and exact product; any certified tuple is a full factorization. A promised input supplies one true surviving template, so one of these tuples is reached. The one-vertex case may be solved by exact integer single-variable power checks and primality, or by the same finite real algorithm. The number of exponent lists is O((L+1)^k), and even a direct B_T^L has polynomial bit size. This is a statement about abstract deterministic bit complexity. The finite checkers do not implement this procedure.

For strict separation from a numeric-small height requirement, take any integer H and the edge
  F_H(X,Y)=(Y-1)X^2+[H(Y-3)-15]X+Y^2-2.
This quadratic-in-each-variable correspondence has the prime point (7,3) for every H. Both leading/trailing polynomials in either variable are primitive. Over any residue characteristic it has Y-leading coefficient 1; in the other direction, the only possible horizontal line has Y=1, but then its constant-in-X coefficient is -1. Both partial degrees are retained and both trailing polynomials survive. The stronger projective test also passes: at infinity the coefficient of Y is nonzero after X-homogenization and the constant in X is nonzero after Y-homogenization. This permits H=2^L, genuinely outside polynomial magnitudes. The mixed relation cannot be replaced by a polynomial separation identity on all its branches: with X=1/t, its equation multiplied by t^2 admits a complex analytic branch Y(t) -> 1 as t -> 0, by the implicit function theorem at (t,Y)=(0,1). For any nonconstant polynomials U,V, U(1/t) is unbounded while V(Y(t)) is bounded. This is a separation of the forms of hypotheses, not a claim that a particular prime point has no other relation.

The old common slice can give an actual spurious exceptional value even in the presence of a true promised tree. For L>=4 put C_L=3*2^(L-2) and include the two edges Y-X-1 and X Y-(C_L+1)X+1. Each is the graph of a nonconstant fractional linear transformation represented by an integral determinant-one matrix; reductions retain both partial degrees and have no vertical or horizontal lines. For the second equation alone, root X=t has coordinate equation tY-(C_L+1)t+1, and the old choice of the first valid positive slice is t=1. Its point (1,C_L) with powers (1,1) yields C_L as a genuine member of the old explicit exception superset at length L. But B_T=1 on every template of this catalogue by section 4. At n=C_L the true prime support (2,3) has the first edge, with unknown exponents (L-2,1). This separates the old global catalogue guard from the new certificate on eligible instances. It does not show that separately testing only the true old template would fail, and C_L is an elementary integer, not a hard example.

The gcd over possible roots is not redundant. For a single edge with distinct coprime integers p=2^h,q=3^h, take
  (p(X-5)+1)(q(Y-3)+1)-1.
It has point (5,3), and both affine finite-fibre conditions in characteristic zero. In the two root directions the leading contents are respectively q and p; both trailing contents are 1. Indeed the trailing coefficient gcd in the first direction divides p, since its two coefficients (after using gcd with the nonconstant multiplier) are coprime to 1-3q; modulo 2 its constant term is -3q, odd. In the other direction exchange p,q and 5,3, and reduce the resulting constant modulo 3 to -5p, nonzero. There is no polynomial content in the edge or overall scalar to remove. Neither single root has globally primitive endpoints, but B_T=1. For comparison XY-C at any integer C>1 has B_T=C and truly has a constant-product component. Failure of the certificate is not a lower bound: for instance pY-qX with nonzero coprime p,q has normalized certificates depending on p and q but its positive monomial levels can be finite quite independently of such tests.
