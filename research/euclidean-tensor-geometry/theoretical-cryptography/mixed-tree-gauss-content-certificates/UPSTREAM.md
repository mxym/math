# Sources and scope

* Predecessor: [Exceptional constants on a tree of mixed plane correspondences, §§2-4](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/56a55c8a3a7b048a3c483fc52ed3e2fa1c0c08a0/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-bare-n-mixed-tree-constant-fibers/PROOF.md). It used an integer slice of the same recursively eliminated coordinates. Its positive-integer exceptional list is polynomial in length and its unconditional coverage of that list required polynomial coefficient magnitudes. This note uses coefficient contents before specializing; it excludes all nonunit rational constants on certified templates, including arbitrary polynomial bit height if the certificate is 1. The sharp claim is about this factorization-free certificate and its use, not about general factoring. The gcd over different roots and the local criterion give two ways to certify the geometric conclusion.
* Standard nonarchimedean ingredients: Barry Green and Peter Roquette, [An introduction to Deuring's theory of constant reductions](https://www.mathi.uni-heidelberg.de/~roquette/2021conm15398.pdf), DOI [10.1090/conm/767/15398](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/767/15398), Theorem 1 (Gauss extension and its residue field); [Stacks Project §10.36](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00GH) (finite and integral ring extensions), and [§15.125](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0ASF) (extension of valuation rings). The proof here avoids using valuation extension by working directly over an integrally closed localization. Both monic and reciprocal monic equations are classical sufficient tests for a root to be an integral unit. These are not originality claims.
* Good reduction for rational functions is classical; see P. Morton and J. Silverman, [Rational periodic points of rational functions](https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792894000127), IMRN 1994 (2), 97-110, and Silverman's [Arithmetic Dynamics notes](https://swc-math.github.io/aws/2010/2010SilvermanNotes.pdf), §5.1, discussion preceding Proposition 24 on reduction of rational maps. Determinant-one fractional linear maps give an elementary example of both theories. The local proof here is for multivalued mixed correspondences on arbitrary fixed trees and uses retention both at zero and at the top degree, not iteration of a self-map. The maximal-minor test is a standard binary homogeneous matrix argument, included with proof.
* E. Bombieri, D. Masser and U. Zannier, [Anomalous Subvarieties: Structure Theorems and Applications](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnm057), IMRN 2007, rnm057, gives the wider geometric context of intersections with translates of subtori. We do not claim that recognizing a component in a character level, using a valuation, or regarding it as a subtorus-coset phenomenon is new. This note treats rational constant values of a character on prescribed trees and gives explicit integer support certificates, not finiteness for intersections with varying exponents or a height bound on individual points.
* For the arithmetic corollary, the supplied two-variable (or prime and cofactor) relation is already a standard elimination input; see M. Agrawal, N. Saxena and S. S. Srivastava, [Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), MFCS 2016, article 6, pp. 6:3-6:4, Theorems 1.2-1.3. Their separate cyclotomic speed bounds use conjectures. Fixed-dimensional exact real root enumeration is standard (G. E. Collins, [Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-07407-4_17), 1975; J. Renegar, [On the computational complexity and geometry of the first-order theory of the reals, part I](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/305759aa-f544-4006-aa85-f70ddce54e00/content), 1992), as is deterministic primality ([Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781)). Targeted comparisons do not certify global priority.
