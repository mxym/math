# Sources, credits, and limits of the comparison

* Erdős, Luca and Pomerance, *On the proportion of numbers coprime to a given integer*, [author copy, §2, Lemma 1 and Algorithm A](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/gcdnphin14.pdf). Reduction of phi(n)/n to squarefree support and the largest-prime property of the reduced denominator are classical. Algorithm A reconstructs the unique squarefree preimage from the ratio using the largest prime divisor of successive denominators; it is not stated as a polynomial-time algorithm for the promise here.
* B. Źrałek, *A deterministic version of Pollard’s p−1 algorithm*, [author preprint v5, §6, printed pp.13–14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102v5). It gives the generalized two-distinct-prime case with all multiplicities, and states the three-distinct-prime unequal-exponent case from the single numeric totient in deterministic O((ln n)^3) time. Its §6 also distinguishes unconditional randomized, ERH deterministic and almost-all conclusions from unconditional worst-case deterministic ones. These small-support results are not presented as new here. Equal exponents reduce by exact powers, not by new information.
* Morain, Renault and Smith, *Deterministic factoring with oracles*, [§5.1 Theorem 5.1 and §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08444v2). Landau's general equal-order decomposition uses O(omega(N)) oracle calls. The elementary gcd splitting in §5.2 starts with squarefree input. A single supplied numeric value is a different resource from an oracle on arbitrary divisors.
* Hittmeir and Pomykała, *Deterministic Integer Factorization with Oracles for Euler’s Totient Function*, [publisher](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2020-1891). Their factorization results assume the prime-power decomposition of the totient is given, not just its numeric value as here.
* Cohn and Heninger, *Ideal Forms of Coppersmith’s Theorem and Guruswami–Sudan List Decoding*, [Theorem 1.1, printed p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1284). This theorem credits Coppersmith, Howgrave-Graham and May. Its univariate monic theorem, including polynomial dependence on the degree, is the cited lifting tool. Neither the generic algorithm nor its small-root exponent is claimed here as a discovery.

This note isolates a bounded-ratio, clustered-source promise with unknown multiplicities, a bounded terminal affine relation and a maximal-multiplicity balancing argument. It is not a proof of priority over all factoring literature, a general reduction from squareful to squarefree with one value, or a solution to ordinary integer factorization. The pair-plus-rest application is a composition with a separate squarefree result; it is not needed by the radical theorem.
