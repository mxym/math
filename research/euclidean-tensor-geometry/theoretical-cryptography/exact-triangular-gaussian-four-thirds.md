# Exact Gaussian conversion at the four-thirds scale on the triangular lattice

Consider the exact conversion experiment: one observation is \(\theta+g_t\) for an unknown deterministic point \(\theta\) of a fixed lattice \(\Lambda\), and a Markov kernel must output the law \(\theta+D_{\Lambda,s}\), for every \(\theta\in\Lambda\). Here \(g_t(x)=t^{-d}e^{-\pi\|x\|^2/t^2}\) and the centered discrete Gaussian \(D_{\Lambda,s}\) has masses proportional to \(e^{-\pi\|u\|^2/s^2}\) on \(\Lambda\). The center is not supplied to the kernel.

**Theorem.** If \(\Lambda\) is a planar triangular lattice (equivalently its dual is triangular), and \(\lambda=\lambda_1(\Lambda^*)\), there are absolute constants \(C,T>0\) such that for \(t\ge T/\lambda\) such a kernel exists whenever
\[
                       s^2\ge\tfrac43t^2+C/\lambda^2.
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular the leading squared-width ratio \(4/3\) is sharp with a bounded additive gap on this nonorthogonal lattice, by the [all-lattice four-thirds obstruction](exact-gaussian-four-thirds-obstruction.md). The same follows for a fixed orthogonal direct sum of triangular and one-dimensional factors, applying (1) and the [line result](exact-gaussian-four-thirds.md) separately. No assertion is made for general skew lattices, for the exact endpoint \(s^2=\tfrac43t^2\), or about efficient implementation.

The earlier [Voronoi-dependent sufficient bound](exact-lattice-gaussian-gap.md) gave every fixed ratio strictly above \(3/2\) on the triangular lattice; the [line construction](exact-gaussian-four-thirds.md) gives \(4/3+O(t^{-2})\) only for rectangular products. The proof here is not coordinatewise rounding. The two lowest dual shells require different companion frequencies, and a Schur complement controls their spillover into all higher shells. Approximate Gaussian randomized rounding in [Peikert, Theorem 3.1 and §4.1](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/pargauss.pdf) or [LPR Toolkit, §2.4.2](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/toolkit.pdf) addresses statistical closeness rather than this exact conversion law. This is a standalone existence theorem, with no protocol or security implication.

## 1. Triangular shell facts

Scale the dual lattice \(\Gamma=\Lambda^*\) to shortest length 1. It is generated by \(v_1=(1,0)\), \(v_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2)\), whose integral coordinate form is \(Q(a,b)=a^2+ab+b^2\). Its six unit vectors \(U\) are \(\pm v_1,\pm v_2,\pm(v_1-v_2)\). Let \(I\) be the finite union of the two low nonzero shells of squared norms 1 and 3; its complement among nonzero frequencies is \(H\). For each \(j\ne0\), set \(p(j)=\max_{v\in U}j\cdot v\).

The values of \(p(j)\) are half-integers. By the six equally spaced directions, \(p(j)\ge(\sqrt3/2)\|j\|\). The only squared norms below 4 for nonzero \(j\) are 1 and 3, since \(Q(a,b)\equiv(a-b)^2\bmod3\). The length-\(\sqrt3\) vectors are sums \(v+w\) of adjacent unit vectors, and have maximum projection \(3/2\); this can also be seen by solving \(Q(a,b)=3\) for the six integer pairs. Thus \(p(j)\ge2\) on \(H\). Moreover \(p(j)=2\) holds there precisely for \(j=2v\), \(v\in U\). To prove this, take a maximizing direction \(v\) and choose orthonormal coordinates with first axis \(v\). By the lattice's \(60^\circ\) rotation symmetry, the coordinates of a lattice point then have horizontal part \(a+b/2\) and vertical part \(b\sqrt3/2\). Horizontal part 2 forces \(b\) even. If the vertical part is nonzero, one of the directions at \(\pm60^\circ\) from \(v\) has dot product at least \(1+3/2>2\), a contradiction. This classification will bound all high-to-low companion entries.

We also use a uniform shifted Gaussian lattice sum bound
\[
   \sup_{x\in\mathbb R^2}\sum_{u\in\Gamma}e^{-\pi D\|u-x\|^2}\le C_0
                       \qquad(D\ge1).
 \tag{2}
\]
For instance reduce \(x\) to a bounded fundamental region, then compare the tails to a convergent Gaussian lattice sum at \(D=1\). A centered sum with the zero term omitted is at most \(C_0'e^{-\pi D}\), by factoring out the shortest squared length 1. Constants below are absolute in this normalization.

## 2. Companion frequencies

Put \(D=s^2-t^2\), \(A=s^2/D\), and \(\delta=4-A\). For the constructive case assume
\[
       D\ge1,\qquad 0<\delta\le1/4,\qquad D\delta\ge L_0,
 \tag{3}
\]
where the absolute constant \(L_0\) will be chosen large. Set \(\beta=1/A\) and \(\omega=D(1-1/A)=t^2/A\). Completing squares at a prescribed \(u\in\Lambda\) gives
\[
                 g_t(u-x)g_{\sqrt D}(x)
                       =g_s(u)\,g_{\sqrt\omega}(x-\beta u).
 \tag{4}
\]

For \(j\in\Gamma\setminus\{0\}\), choose a unit direction \(v_j\), a primary frequency \(\alpha_j\), and its companion \(\zeta_j=\alpha_j-A v_j\) as follows.
- If \(\|j\|=1\), choose \(v_j=j\), \(\alpha_j=d_1 j\), \(d_1=2-\delta/4\).
- If \(\|j\|=\sqrt3\), write \(j=v_j+w_j\) for adjacent unit directions and set \(\alpha_j=j+e v_j\), \(e=1/2-\delta/4\).
- If \(j\in H\), choose \(v_j\) maximizing \(j\cdot v\) over \(v\in U\) and set \(\alpha_j=j\).

Make choices in opposing pairs so \(v_{-j}=-v_j\) and \(\alpha_{-j}=-\alpha_j\). With
\[
       b_j=e^{-\pi D(A-1)(\,2\alpha_j\cdot v_j-A\,)},\qquad
       U_j(x)=e^{2\pi i\alpha_j\cdot x}-b_j e^{2\pi i\zeta_j\cdot x},
 \tag{5}
\]
all \(b_j\le1\): in each low shell the last parenthetical factor is \(\delta/2\), and on \(H\) it is at least \(\delta\). Also \(\|U_j\|_\infty\le2\). Each \(U_j\) averages to zero against \(g_{\sqrt\omega}(x-\beta u)\) for every \(u\in\Lambda\), since the phases differ by \(v_j\cdot u\in\mathbb Z\) and the difference of their Gaussian damping exponents is
\[
            \omega(\|\alpha_j\|^2-\|\zeta_j\|^2)
                          =D(A-1)(2\alpha_j\cdot v_j-A).
\]

We seek \(c\in\ell^1(\Gamma\setminus\{0\})\) such that \(g_{\sqrt D}(1+\sum_jc_jU_j)\) has all nonzero \(\Gamma\)-Fourier coefficients zero. This is the real linear equation
\[
 Tc=f,\quad f_i=-e^{-\pi D\|i\|^2},\qquad
 T_{ij}=e^{-\pi D\|i-\alpha_j\|^2}-b_je^{-\pi D\|i-\zeta_j\|^2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The matrix is bounded on \(\ell^1\) by (2). We prove that its real solution has \(2\|c\|_1<1\).

## 3. High block and high-to-low row bound

Write the block matrix on \(I\sqcup H\) as
\[
        T=\begin{pmatrix}L&R\\ V&B\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In the high block all primary frequencies are their column indices, so (2) and \(b_j\le e^{-\pi D(A-1)\delta}\) give
\[
        \|B-I_H\|_{1\to1}\le C_1(e^{-\pi D}+e^{-\pi D(A-1)\delta}).
 \tag{7}
\]
Since \(A\ge15/4\), \(D\delta\ge L_0\), and \(D\ge D\delta\), the right-hand side is less than \(1/2\) for large enough \(L_0\), giving \(\|B^{-1}\|\le2\). The unrestricted low-to-high column bound is \(\|V\|\le2C_0\) from (2).

For the high-to-low block a stronger estimate is essential:
\[
                         \|R\|_{1\to1}\le C_2 e^{-\pi D}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Every primary entry in this block has distance at least 1. For a high companion with \(p(j)\ge5/2\), the damping exponent divided by \(\pi D\) is at least \((A-1)(5-A)\ge 11/4>1\). If \(p(j)=2\), we proved \(j=2v_j\); then \(\zeta_j=-(2-\delta)v_j\). A low-shell vector has distance at least \(1-\delta\) from it: for length 1 the closest possibility is \(-v_j\), and for length \(\sqrt3\) its projection along \(-v_j\) is at most \(3/2\), giving squared distance at least \((2-\delta)^2-3(2-\delta)+3\), which exceeds \((1-\delta)^2\). Thus even in this exceptional case the companion entry exponent is at least
\[
         (A-1)\delta+(1-\delta)^2=1+\delta .
\]
This proves the column norm bound (8), since \(I\) has exactly twelve elements.

## 4. Low block and its Schur complement

Let \(\mathsf D\) be the positive diagonal matrix on \(I\) with entry \(e^{-\pi D(d_1-1)^2}\) on the unit shell and \(e^{-\pi D e^2}\) on the \(\sqrt3\) shell. These are the primary diagonal entries of \(L\). We check
\[
                      \|L\mathsf D^{-1}-I_I\|
                                 \le C_3 e^{-\pi D\delta/4}.
 \tag{9}
\]
First take a unit-shell column \(j\). For another unit-shell row, \(i\cdot j\le1/2\), so the primary squared-distance gap over the diagonal is at least \(d_1\). For a row of length \(\sqrt3\), the difference is at least
\[
            (3-3d_1+d_1^2)-(d_1-1)^2=2-d_1=\delta/4.
\]
The companion here is \(-d'_1j\) with \(d'_1=A-d_1=2-3\delta/4\), and its minimum low row squared distance is \((d'_1-1)^2\) (the \(\sqrt3\) shell comparison adds \(2-d'_1>0\) as above). Including damping, its gap over the primary diagonal is at least
\[
       (A-1)(2d_1-A)+(d'_1-1)^2-(d_1-1)^2=2d_1-A=\delta/2 .
\]
Now take a \(\sqrt3\)-shell column. Its off-diagonal primary distances (even to any lattice row \(i\ne j\)) exceed \(e^2\) by at least \(1-2e=\delta/2\), since \(\|i-j\|\ge1\) gives
\(\|i-j\|^2-2e(i-j)\cdot v_j\ge (1-2e)\|i-j\|^2\).
The companion is
\(\zeta_j=w_j-3v_j+(1/2+3\delta/4)v_j\).
A lattice point whose component perpendicular to \(v_j\) differs from that of \(w_j\) has perpendicular distance at least \(\sqrt3/2\). In its own parallel lattice row the nearest distance is \(1/2-3\delta/4\), by reduction modulo the unit basis vector \(v_j\). With the damping from (5), the gap over the primary diagonal is exactly at least
\[
      (A-1)\delta/2+(1/2-3\delta/4)^2-e^2=\delta .
\]
These estimates include the companions on the primary diagonal. Summing over the finite low rows proves (9).

Put \(d_{\max}=\max\{(d_1-1)^2,e^2\}\), so \(1-d_{\max}\ge\delta/4\) throughout (3). Let \(S=L-RB^{-1}V\). By (7)–(9),
\[
 \|(S-L)\mathsf D^{-1}\|
     \le C_4 e^{-\pi D(1-d_{\max})},\qquad
                       \|S\mathsf D^{-1}-I_I\|\le C_5 e^{-\pi D\delta/4}.
 \tag{10}
\]
In particular \(S\mathsf D^{-1}\) is invertible, with inverse norm at most two when \(L_0\) is large. The right-hand side in (6) has \(\|f_I\|_1\le12e^{-\pi D}\) and \(\|f_H\|_1\le C_6e^{-4\pi D}\), by the lowest high squared norm and the same tail factorization as in (2). The Schur solution is
\[
 c_I=\mathsf D^{-1}(S\mathsf D^{-1})^{-1}(f_I-RB^{-1}f_H),
 \qquad
 c_H=B^{-1}(f_H-Vc_I).
\]
It satisfies \(\|c_I\|_1\le C_7 e^{-\pi D(1-d_{\max})}\) and
\(\|c_H\|_1\le C_8(e^{-4\pi D}+\|c_I\|_1)\), from (7), (8), (10). Choose \(L_0\) sufficiently large so their sum is less than \(1/2\). This reasoning solves the infinite high block on \(\ell^1\); a finite numerical truncation is not used. It also shows uniqueness. The real matrix and the sign pairing \(T_{-i,-j}=T_{ij}\), \(f_{-i}=f_i\) give a real solution with \(c_j=c_{-j}\).

## 5. The exact law and the variance scale

Let \(p_0(x)=g_{\sqrt D}(x)(1+\sum_jc_jU_j(x))\). Sign pairing makes the sum real, and \(2\|c\|_1<1\) makes it a positive bounded perturbation of the Gaussian. The periodization of \(p_0\) converges continuously (dominated by Gaussian tails) and is constant, because all its nonzero lattice Fourier coefficients vanish by (6). Normalize \(p=p_0/\int p_0\), whose periodization is the reciprocal of the covolume of \(\Lambda\). Thus \(K(u\mid x)=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)p(u-x)\) is a probability kernel on the lattice. By (4)–(5) and absolute convergence, \((g_t*p_0)(u)=g_s(u)\) for every lattice \(u\). Its normalized masses therefore give exactly \(D_{\Lambda,s}\) at center zero, and lattice translation by any \(\theta\in\Lambda\) gives \(\theta+D_{\Lambda,s}\).

For normalized \(\lambda=1\), take \(s^2=\tfrac43t^2+C\), with \(3C\ge L_0\). Then \(D\delta=4D-s^2=3C\) and for all large enough \(t\) condition (3) holds. For larger \(s^2\), first add independent continuous Gaussian noise so the input width becomes \(\widetilde t=\sqrt{\tfrac34(s^2-C)}\ge t\), and apply the equality case. Scaling the primal lattice by \(\lambda\) normalizes its dual shortest length to 1; this replaces \(s,t\) by \(\lambda s,\lambda t\) in (1). The product extension follows from independence of orthogonal coordinates. The companion checker verifies exact triangular coordinate and exponent identities for rational parameters and finite examples, not the infinite operator bound.
