# Source and priority notes

- **Reverse problem and sharp witness.** Shahram Khazaei, *Reverse Substitution in Secret Sharing: The Critical One-Half Threshold and a Route to Superpolynomial Lower Bounds*, [ePrint 2026/2652](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2652), [fixed November 11, 2026 PDF](https://opencryptology.org/2026/2652/versions/20261111T084015Z.pdf). §§2.2, 2.4 and §3.1 equation (13) define closed convec regions, the substitution, and the factor in (0,1]. §5.1–5.2 Lemma 5.1 and Propositions 5.2–5.3 give the conditioning and grouping reductions; Definition 6.3 and Theorem 7.3 give general capacity and its supporting-halfspace formulation. Corollary 6.8 already gives exact reversal for nearly ideal factors. Theorem 8.1 and Appendix B.2 give the explicit anchored convec and its factor 8/9; the attainment used here is the same construction, dropping the anchor for four parties. This note proves an all-composite lower bound for the explicit two-switch pair class, using the two Shannon telescopes, and a closed factor formula there. It gives neither the universal minimum of §9 nor the depth-uniform simultaneous/recursive Csirmaz value asked there. It does not assume common-secret compatibility in the forward construction.

- **Small factors.** Douglas R. Stinson, *An Explication of Secret Sharing Schemes*, Designs, Codes and Cryptography 2 (1992), 357–390, [PDF](https://www.terpconnect.umd.edu/~abarg/626/Stinson%20Secret%20Sharing%2092.pdf). Table 1 (printed p. 373), Theorem 5.4 and the qualification reduction before Table 1 give the four nonideal types and precisely where disjoint families can be separated. §6 Theorems 6.1–6.2 give classical entropy restrictions; §§7–8 give constructions. This note works with full closed convec regions, not just their optimal maximum coordinate. It gives explicit endpoint constructions and two-switch witnesses to identify the regions used here; the underlying four access structures and their nonideality are not new.

- **King and pawns ingredient.** Jessica Ruth Metcalf-Burton, *Information Rates for Secret Sharing over Various Access Structures*, [thesis, chapter 5](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/63729/jmetcalf_1.pdf?sequence=1), analyzes the king-and-pawns family, including the three-pawn structure. This note does not extrapolate the pair-region characterization to an arbitrary number of pawns.
