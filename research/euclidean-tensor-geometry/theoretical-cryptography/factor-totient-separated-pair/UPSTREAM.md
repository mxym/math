# Sources and bounded comparison

* Henry Cohn and Nadia Heninger, "Ideal forms of Coppersmith's theorem and Guruswami-Sudan list decoding," Theorem 1.1, p. 2: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1284v2#page=2 . The theorem is the monic univariate small-root bound on an unknown divisor, in the formulation attributed there to Coppersmith, Howgrave-Graham and May. This note uses it as a black box twice, only in degree one, with strict fixed exponent margins. Neither this root algorithm nor the general idea of exploiting approximate divisors is claimed as new.
* Francois Morain, Guenael Renault and Benjamin Smith, "Deterministic factoring with oracles," https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08444v2 . Section 4.2, Theorem 4.2, already treats a known shift: D|N and (D-u)|M. Its displayed sufficient size exponent for D is greater than (1+theta)/4 with M=N^theta. In our use M=T is comparable to N, theta tends to 1, and the pair product is N^(2/k+o(1)), below that generic threshold when k>=5. Our extra input to the classical small-root step is a sufficiently accurate *center for D itself*, derived from two clusters. Section 5.4, especially Theorem 5.6, uses prime-size exponents. All exponents in our regime tend to 1/k. For r<=k-1, (r/k)^2 <= ((k-1)/k)^2 < 1-1/k, so Theorem 5.6's stated inequalities do not yield this equal-exponent regime as a direct corollary. The comparison is to those named sufficient tests, not a separation from every combination of previous results. The general deterministic numeric-totient problem remains outside this note.
* Markus Hittmeir and Jacek Pomykala, "Deterministic Integer Factorization with Oracles for Euler's Totient Function," https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2020-1891 . Their stated input includes the prime-power decomposition of phi(N), a stronger oracle than the single integer value used here.
* Michael Sagraloff, "On the Complexity of Real Root Isolation," https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0344 . Fixed-degree real-root isolation for integer polynomials of polynomial bit height is standard. The proof requires just constant absolute precision after removing multiplicities, not a new isolation algorithm.

Scope of priority check: the above primary results and targeted searches on numeric-totient factoring, comparable/close prime factors and approximate block divisors were checked. No claim of a globally first theorem or of an optimal diameter region follows from that bounded search. The shell estimate uses only the ordinary prime number theorem over a fixed proportional interval.
