# Strict Gaussian-coordinate improvement from cyclotomic basis packing

The usual powerful product basis of a cyclotomic integer ring gives a normalized operator norm of one. For conductors with exactly three distinct odd prime factors, we classify when the norm can be made strictly smaller **using only shortest lattice vectors**. It happens precisely for the triples \((3,5,7),(3,5,11),(3,5,13)\). In each of the three base cases we give two disjoint integral bases of normalized roots, and an exact quantitative contraction certificate for the first basis. Tensor extension gives strict improvement for infinitely many conductors.

This is a Euclidean optimization of a continuous Gaussian covariance step. It is not a new Ring-LWE reduction or an assertion about computational hardness or deployed parameters.

## 1. Statement and relevance

Use the canonical inner product \(\langle x,y\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(x\overline y)\), counting both members of complex conjugate pairs. Let \(m\not\equiv2\bmod4\) be the reduced conductor, and let \(\widehat m=m\) for odd \(m\), \(\widehat m=m/2\) for even \(m\). Let \(B\) denote the real matrix of the columns of an integral basis in canonical orthonormal coordinates.

The theorem has two parts.

1. If \(m\) has exactly three distinct odd prime divisors \(p<q<r\), arbitrary exponents, then the minimum of \(\|B\|_{\rm op}\) **over bases consisting only of shortest vectors** is less than \(\sqrt{\widehat m}\) if and only if \((p,q,r)=(3,5,7),(3,5,11),(3,5,13)\). For all other triples this restricted minimum is exactly \(\sqrt{\widehat m}\).
2. If the prime divisors of \(m\) contain one of these three triples, and may contain more odd primes, then there is an integral basis, still made of shortest vectors, with
\[
 \|B\|_{\rm op}^2 < \widehat m\,c_{pqr},
 \qquad c_{105}=953/1000,\quad
        c_{165}=99/100,\quad
        c_{195}=499/500 .                                     \tag{1}
\]
Here the subscript \(pqr\) is the product of the three primes; choose the smallest bound if multiple triples divide \(m\).

These are strict upper bounds for the unrestricted minimum. The theorem neither finds the exact minimum in the three strict cases nor excludes non-shortest-column improvements outside them, except in families where a separate theorem already does so.

To see the covariance relevance, applying the trace coordinates of \(B\) to spherical canonical Gaussian error of width \(s\) gives width-squared matrix \(s^2 B^{\mathsf T}B\). An independent Gaussian can pad it to spherical width \(s'\) exactly when \((s')^2I\succeq s^2 B^{\mathsf T}B\), so the least such width is \(s\|B\|_{\rm op}\). This is the Gaussian covariance step in [Peikert–Pepin, §5.3 and Corollary 5.5](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/structured-lwe.pdf); their full theorem imposes further algebraic requirements, including a power basis. Arbitrary integral bases here are not asserted to meet those requirements.

## 2. Equal-norm Parseval frames and complements

Let \(\mathcal V=(v_e:e\in E)\) be an equal-norm Parseval frame for a real space of dimension \(d\). That is, \(\sum_{e\in E}v_e v_e^{\mathsf T}=I\) and every \(\|v_e\|^2\) is the same. Write \(\delta=d/|E|\) for that common squared norm. For any chosen column set \(S\) its operator norm squared is
\[
       \left\|\sum_{e\in S} v_e v_e^{\mathsf T}\right\|_{\rm op}
       =1-\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\sum_{e\in E\setminus S}
                                             v_e v_e^{\mathsf T}\right).
                                                                  \tag{2}
\]
Hence the norm is strictly below one exactly when the complement spans the space. In particular no \(d\)-column selection can strictly contract when \(|E|<2d\).

There is also an exact converse **for real bases** in any such frame. For \(W\subseteq E\), the restricted frame operator has eigenvalues in \([0,1]\), trace \(\delta|W|\), and rank \(r(W)=\dim\operatorname{span}\{v_e:e\in W\}\). Thus \(r(W)\ge\delta|W|\). By the [Edmonds–Fulkerson base-packing criterion, Theorem 2c](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69B/jresv69Bn3p147_A1b.pdf), \(\ell\) disjoint real bases exist when \(|E\setminus W|\ge\ell(d-r(W))\) for every \(W\). The trace bound verifies this for \(\ell\le1/\delta\), and more than \(\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor\) bases cannot fit by cardinality. Consequently any equal-norm Parseval frame has the maximum possible number \(\lfloor|E|/d\rfloor\) of disjoint real bases. Strict real-basis contraction is therefore equivalent to \(\delta\le1/2\). This argument does not assert any of the packed bases is an **integral** basis of the lattice generated by the frame. In particular combinatorial spanning over \(\mathbb R\) cannot substitute for the determinants below.

## 3. Cyclotomic normalization and the three-prime transition

Write \(P_p=I-J/p\) for projection onto the coordinate-sum-zero subspace of \(\mathbb R^p\), and write \(u_i^{(p)}=P_p e_i\), \(0\le i<p\). They form a Parseval frame there, and \(u_0^{(p)},\ldots,u_{p-2}^{(p)}\) are an integral basis of \(A_{p-1}^*=P_p\mathbb Z^p\), with \(u_{p-1}^{(p)}=-\sum_{i<p-1}u_i^{(p)}\).

For an odd prime power \(p^e\), group its cyclotomic power basis by exponents \(j+p^{e-1}i\) where \(0\le j<p^{e-1}\), \(0\le i<p-1\). The trace Ramanujan sum gives zero across differing \(j\)'s, and each within-\(j\) Gram matrix is \(p^e(I-J/p)\). For powers of two the canonical power basis is orthogonal, of squared length \(2^{a-1}\) at conductor \(2^a\), \(a\ge2\). For coprime conductor factors, their rings tensor integrally: the prime-power roots generate the full ring and the degrees multiply; all combinations of embeddings occur so the Gram factors multiply too. Thus dividing lengths by \(\sqrt{\widehat m}\) gives an orthogonal sum of identical normalized blocks
\[
                           L=\bigotimes_{p\mid m,\ p\ {\rm odd}} A_{p-1}^* .
\]
This normalization is the one developed for the powerful basis in the [LPR toolkit, §4](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/fac/cpeikert/pubs/toolkit.pdf). The tensor projected-coordinate products, indexed by the product of the prime alphabets, form a Parseval frame since the local frames do. Each block has \(N=\prod p\) such columns and rank \(d=\prod(p-1)\), and the frame density is \(\delta=d/N\). Taking any \(p-1\) local columns in each factor and their products gives an integral basis of norm at most one.

These frame vectors, up to signs, are exactly the shortest vectors of each block. In the original lattice every nonzero integer \(x\) has \(\sum_\sigma|\sigma(x)|^2\ge [K:\mathbb Q]|\mathrm{N}(x)|^{2/[K:\mathbb Q]}\ge [K:\mathbb Q]\), with equality only if all conjugates have modulus one, hence \(x\) is a root of unity by Kronecker. Conversely roots attain equality and realize the indicated block column vectors. Explicitly, the product of the distinct prime-power root coordinates gives one root for each indicated cell, and the power-basis blocks ensure those products are distinct up to sign. They exhaust the roots: if an additional root of order \(j\) were in the field, its compositum with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\) would be \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\operatorname{lcm}(m,j)})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\), so equality of totients forces the lcm to be \(m\) or \(2m\) with \(m\) odd. The corresponding total number of sign pairs is exactly the number of cells across blocks. Shortest vectors of the orthogonal sum are supported in individual blocks. Thus a basis of shortest vectors splits into bases of the blocks; reordering or signs do not affect a block frame norm.

For three ordered odd primes \(p<q<r\), the density cannot equal \(1/2\), since \(N\) is odd. If \(p\ge5\) the density is at least \((4/5)(6/7)(10/11)>1/2\). If \(p=3,q\ge7\), it is at least \((2/3)(6/7)(10/11)>1/2\). In the remaining case it is \((8/15)(1-1/r)\), below \(1/2\) exactly when \(r<16\), i.e., \(r=7,11,13\). For the large-density cases, (2) gives norm exactly one for every shortest-column basis. It remains to give integral witnesses in the three low-density cases.

The correspondence of cyclotomic bases to complementary simplicial spanning trees is classical: see [Martin–Reiner](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~reiner/Papers/cyclotomic.pdf), with the integral torsion issue made explicit in the [companion tensor-stability note](cyclotomic-coordinate-tensor-stability.md). The equal-norm packing statement supplies a real complement in these cases, but that torsion issue is precisely why the integral certificates are needed. The prior [two-prime coordinate theorem](cyclotomic-gaussian-coordinate-optima.md) and tensor-stability theorem cover other regimes and do not give an integral contraction for these three triples.

## 4. Exact certificates for 105, 165, and 195

The accompanying verifier contains the two fixed disjoint sets \(S_0,S_1\) of cell labels in each example, in lexicographic encoding \((i,j,k)\mapsto (5i+j)r+k\) for \(0\le i<3,0\le j<5,0\le k<r\). Their matrices are square in the tensor product of the \(p-1\) ordinary projected-coordinate basis vectors. The coordinate column for a cell is the Kronecker product of its local columns (the \(p-1\) standard unit vectors and the single column \(-\boldsymbol1\)). The determinants are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|r|r|r}
    3\cdot5\cdot r & d & \det S_0 & \det S_1\\\hline
    105 &48&+1&-1\\
    165 &80&-1&+1\\
    195 &96&+1&-1
 \end{array}
\]
so both are integral bases. In particular, the complement of either one spans, and both give a strict contraction by (2).

For the quantitative bounds in (1) the verifier also checks every leading principal minor of \(c_m I-G_{S_0}\) by fraction-free elimination, after a positive integer rescaling. This is an exact Sylvester certificate for positive definiteness, not an eigenvalue comparison with numerical tolerance. For cell labels \(e,f\), the normalized Gram entry used there is
\[
          G_{e,f}=\frac{1}{3\cdot5\cdot r}
                      \prod_{p\in\{3,5,r\}}
                           (p\mathbf1_{e_p=f_p}-1).
\]
Tentative sets were sought by basis exchanges that preserve determinant of absolute value one; the mathematical conclusion depends only on the frozen, exactly checked witnesses, not on the search. A selected set containing \(d\) columns is required for basis and covariance statements, and the second certificate independently ensures the complement property.

Finally, tensoring one of these contracting bases with the displayed standard basis in each additional prime factor multiplies operator norms (the singular values of a tensor product are pairwise products) and preserves integral generation. It also preserves use of projected roots, hence shortest columns. Copy the resulting basis in each orthogonal block to account for arbitrary prime powers and the two-power factor. This proves (1) and the strict direction of the three-prime classification.

## Reproduction

Run `python3 verify-cyclotomic-basis-packing.py`, using only the Python standard library. It checks the two determinant signs, disjointness, integer Gram rescaling, and all positive pivots for each example. The companion statements about arbitrary real packing use the trace bound and Edmonds–Fulkerson rather than any finite search.
