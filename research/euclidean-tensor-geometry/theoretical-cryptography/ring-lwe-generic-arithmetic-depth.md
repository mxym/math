# A generic arithmetic depth obstruction for finite dyadic noisy products

The coefficient representation carries information not automatically exposed by arithmetic on opaque ring handles. In a generic ring model, even for information-rich broad-noise data, a classical algorithm cannot learn from algebraic coincidences unless its arithmetic expressions reach large formal degree, it makes many queries, or it obtains enough additional representation information. This is a result about a deliberately restricted oracle model, not the usual representation of Ring-LWE.

## 1. Experiment and oracle model

Fix an odd integer \(q\ge3\), and let \(d\) grow through powers of two. Over \(R=(\mathbb Z/q)[X]/(X^d+1)\), choose \(S,A_1,\ldots,A_m\) independently uniformly. Put \(Y_i=A_i S+E_i\) with independent ring errors, independent of the design and label. Assume the common error law satisfies
\[
   c\le |R|\Pr[E_i=e]\le C\quad(e\in R),\qquad
   0<c\le1\le C<\infty,\qquad M=C/c.
\tag{1}
\]
The bounds can also vary with \(d\); the theorem keeps \(M\) explicit. Let \(Q_{\min}\) be the size of the smallest residue field of \(R\), and \(u\) the number of local factors in the standard CRT decomposition.

Take a uniformly random injective naming of elements of \(R\), independent of the experiment. The algorithm is given opaque handles for \(A_i,Y_i\). It may request handles for **known constants**, add, subtract, multiply, compare handles for equality, and optionally request inversion with a distinguished failure symbol on nonunits. There is no operation that interprets an opaque handle in the coefficient representation, and guessed labels are not accepted as handles; all operands must have been given by the interface. Randomness of the algorithm is independent of the input. The program may otherwise perform arbitrary classical computation, including using the random handle strings. It may output a putative coefficient label for \(S\), or an existing handle that is supposed to name \(S\).

Use the formal straight-line algebra generated by independent indeterminates for each input handle, with constants in \(R\). In every CRT factor, use rational expressions whose denominators reduce to nonzero polynomials modulo its maximal ideal. An inversion fails formally if its numerator reduces to the zero polynomial in any factor. Assume the formal numerator and denominator total degrees at each successful gate are at most \(D\ge1\); this is required uniformly on the program's symbolic executions, not only on a selected planted path. Let \(L\) include all initial handles, requested constants, and oracle gates. We assume the number of formal names needed is at most \(|R|\); when the stated bound is nontrivial this is automatic up to an inessential budget convention.

**Theorem.** There is an absolute constant \(K\) with the following properties.
* The joint law of the view and \(S\), with no additional representation input, is within total variation
  \[
       \delta\le K M (L^2+uL)D/Q_{\min}                         \tag{2}
  \]
  of a law where the view is independent of \(S\). This independent view is the same symbolic simulation if the \(Y_i\)'s instead are independent uniform elements.
* Exact-label success is at most \(|R|^{-1}+\delta\) for coefficient outputs and at most \(|R|^{-1}+\delta+K M D/Q_{\min}\) for opaque-handle outputs. A success probability bound for a list of at most \(J\) output candidates follows by multiplying the displayed one-candidate bounds by \(J\).
* If the algorithm additionally receives at most \(B\) bits of arbitrary passive or adaptive input information, the one-output success bounds above can be multiplied by \(2^B\). The advice may depend on the whole experiment and previous handle transcript, provided the same budgets hold for each fixed reply string; it is not allowed to change the oracle. The independence assertion of the first bullet is not claimed when advice can itself name secret information.

For fixed odd \(q\), \(u=O_q(1)\) and \(Q_{\min}=\exp(\Omega_q(d))\). Thus a nonnegligibly successful polynomial-query decoder with bounded \(M\) and no side information needs formal degree \(\exp(\Omega_q(d))\) in this model. A division-free program of multiplicative depth \(h\) has \(D\le2^h\), since addition and subtraction take the maximum of input degrees and multiplication adds them. It follows that successful division-free generic computation needs multiplicative depth \(\Omega_q(d)\). These are formal degree and depth statements, not a gate-count lower bound for general circuits.

## 2. Algebra and an antidote to dependence

Over every prime \(p\mid q\), the roots of \(X^d+1\) have order \(2d\) and are distinct, so each irreducible factor has degree \({\rm ord}_{2d}(p)=\Theta_q(d)\); the valuation identity \(v_2(p^{2^j}-1)=v_2(p^2-1)+j-1\) for \(j\ge1\) gives eventual doubling of the order. For the prime-power component \(p^e\), use coprime Hensel lifts of these factors and CRT. Each local factor \(A\) is finite free over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\), with residue a field \(K_A\) of the stated degree and maximal ideal \(pA\). Elements reducing to a nonzero field element are units, since the kernel of reduction is nilpotent. The field sizes and number of factors above follow.

We need a version of Schwartz–Zippel without independence. If \(Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)\) ranges in \(K_A^n\) and every \(Z_i\) has conditional maximum point mass at most \(M/|K_A|\) even conditional on all other \(Z_j\), then for any nonzero polynomial \(P\) over \(K_A\),
\[
                       \Pr[P(Z)=0]\le M\deg(P)/|K_A|.          \tag{3}
\]
To prove it, write \(P\) as a univariate polynomial in \(Z_n\) of degree \(k\) with nonzero leading coefficient \(P_k\) in the other variables. The latter has degree at most \(\deg(P)-k\). Induct on the number of variables in the marginal law, where the same point-mass condition holds by averaging; when \(P_k\ne0\), at most \(k\) possible values of \(Z_n\) are roots. The constant base case is immediate. This lops off only the degree assigned to the eliminated variable, so there is no dimension factor in (3).

The required condition holds for the reductions of the input registers into **every** residue field, conditional on any fixed \(S=s\), despite the correlations in a pair. For \(Y_i\) conditional on \(A_i,S\) it follows from (1); for \(A_i\) conditional on \(Y_i,S\), Bayes gives a likelihood between \(c/|R|\) and \(C/|R|\) and a posterior maximum point mass at most \(M/|R|\). Other pairs are conditionally independent given \(S\). Projecting a uniform ring element to any of its residue fields has equally large fibers, so the same maximum bound holds relative to uniform there. Conditioning on the other projected input registers, instead of all other full registers, preserves the bound by averaging.

A nonzero formal polynomial \(P\) over a local factor \(A\) has a minimum \(p\)-adic valuation \(v<e\) among its coefficients. Choose a polynomial \(P'\) with \(P=p^v P'\) and with \(P'\) nonzero modulo \(p\). If \(P\) evaluates to zero in \(A\), the evaluation of \(P'\) lies in \(p^{e-v}A\) and hence is zero modulo \(p\). The implication uses finite freeness over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\). Consequently (3) bounds accidental zeroes of nonzero **formal** polynomials over local factors too. These statements are for the formal polynomials, not for distinct functions once degrees exceed the residue field size.

## 3. Symbolic simulation and adaptivity

Maintain one random name per tuple of formal rational expressions, with two tuples identified precisely when their rational expressions agree in every local polynomial localization. Any denominator whose reduction is a nonzero polynomial is a non-zerodivisor in the corresponding polynomial ring: for a nonzero factor with minimum valuation \(v\), the product modulo the next power \(p^{v+1}\) is the product of nonzero polynomials over the residue field. Thus formal rational equality can be checked by cross multiplication. A formally failed inversion is indeed a failed inversion on every evaluation for which all earlier denominators are units.

Fix the random coins and the random names of the formal simulation. This fixes its whole adaptive branch **before** evaluating the formal indeterminates on the sampled data. A first discrepancy between the formal run and an actual run requires one of the following:
1. A collision of distinct formal expressions (including initial or newly requested constant handles). A nonzero cross-multiplied difference in at least one local factor has degree at most \(2D\).
2. A denominator or numerator required to be a unit becomes a nonunit on evaluation. In one of at most \(u\) residue fields a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(D\) evaluates to zero. This includes actual failed inversions along a formally successful path. Along any path without such a discrepancy, formal evaluations through earlier successful gates exist and agree with the actual operations.

A union bound over at most \(L^2\) relevant pairs and \(O(uL)\) individual polynomials in the second category, using (3) or its valuation extension, proves (2) for each fixed \(s\). The random naming can be coupled throughout: when evaluated forms have not collided, the successive fresh names for their values are uniformly sampled without replacement, just as in the symbolic transcript. Reveal names lazily for unseen values. Thus the symbolic view really is a single common reference for all labels, not one reference chosen after the secret is known. When no collisions occur, the transcript is fixed by its symbolic coins and names; there is no additional data-dependent branching outside the specified oracle, even if the program reads the random names as bit strings.

An ordinary coefficient output derived from the common view has exact success at most \(|R|^{-1}\), since \(S\) is uniform and independent of that view. For an output handle, its symbolic expression may itself evaluate to the hidden \(S\) without the program learning that it did. If the expression is a formal constant independent of the sampled inputs, it matches a uniform \(S\) with probability \(|R|^{-1}\) after fixing the symbolic run. If not, for every \(s\) its difference from \(s\) is nonzero in at least one local factor. Cross-multiplying its denominator and using (3) conditional on \(S=s\) bounds this extra success probability by \(O(MD/Q_{\min})\). The selected expression is fixed by the symbolic run, so the selected-handle bound needs no additional factor \(L\). With \(B\) arbitrary extra bits, run a separate symbolic argument for every possible fixed reply string, ignoring the actual advice, and take a union over those strings. Actual success implies success of at least one such fixed-reply program. This proves the advice bound without any assumption on the distribution of advice or on the information it carries.

## 4. A statistically informative finite example

Use \(E_i\) with independent scalar coordinates distributed as \((1-b)U_q+b\delta_0\), \(b=\beta/d\) for fixed \(\beta>0\). Then \(c=(1-b)^d\) and \(C=(1+(q-1)b)^d\), so \(M=O_{q,\beta}(1)\). For prime \(q\), and \(m b^2\to t\) with \((q-1)t/2>\log q\), the **usual coefficient data** suffice for unrestricted statistical recovery with probability tending to one. This last claim concerns only the existence of a statistical decision, not its computation cost.

Here is a direct verification. Under the true label, the log likelihood relative to independent uniform responses is a sum of \(md\) independent terms of mean \((q-1)b^2/2+O_q(b^3)\) and variance \(O_q(b^2)\), by expanding \((1+x)\log(1+x)\) with \(x=b(q\,1_{\{E=0\}}-1)\) under the uniform law. Thus with probability tending to one its value is \(d((q-1)t/2+o(1))\). For a wrong label, integrate its likelihood ratio against the true law conditional on the design. Each scalar contributes \(1+b^2(q\,1_{\{(A_i v)_j=0\}}-1)\), where \(v\ne0\) is the difference of label values. For \(A_i\) uniform, \(A_i v\) is uniform on a nonzero ideal \(J\). Each coefficient marginal is uniform over \(\mathbb F_q\): if any coordinate functional on \(J\) is nonzero, all are nonzero by the signed cyclic permutation given by multiplication by \(X\), and a nonzero linear functional onto \(\mathbb F_q\) of a uniform vector is uniform.

To control correlations within one row, choose a fixed dyadic base dimension \(d_0\) after the order-doubling regime starts. The prime-field factors at dimension \(d\) are the irreducible substitutions \(F(X^{d/d_0})\) of the factors at \(d_0\). By CRT an ideal extends a base ideal and is a disjoint coefficient block product of \(d/d_0\) copies of it, grouping powers by exponent mod \(d/d_0\) (see [the ideal decomposition proof in the coordinate-degree note, §3](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/3e3d636250444949f79182a2cb9dafbea4a03786/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/ring-lwe-coordinate-degree-frontier.md)). Within each block of fixed length the first-order term in \(b^2\) vanishes by uniform scalar marginals. The expected product is then \(1+O_q(b^4)\) per block. Over all rows the expected wrong likelihood ratio is at most \((1+O_q(b^4))^{md/d_0}=1+o(1)\), uniformly over nonzero \(v\). Therefore the sum of all wrong likelihood ratios divided by \(q^d\) is bounded in expectation. Markov's inequality and the strict inequality for the true log likelihood show that the true posterior mass tends to one. In contrast, the generic theorem still gives exponentially small success for sublinear multiplicative depth and polynomially many gates on the same distribution of ring values.

## 5. Relation to earlier generic models and limitations

The symbolic name simulation follows the standard approach behind generic group and ring lower bounds, for example [Jager and Schwenk, On the Analysis of Cryptographic Assumptions in the Generic Ring Model](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00145-012-9120-y); for other approaches in related generic models see [Hhan, A New Approach to Generic Lower Bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11269). The ingredients particular to this experiment are the two-sided likelihood and Bayes comparison of a dependent input pair, the full-conditional form of the root bound, and its lift to local factors with nilpotents. The comparison to an ordinary coefficient experiment is essential; generic hardness by itself does not transfer to that representation.

High formal degree is not synonymous with many gates. For example \(X^{|K_A|}-X\) as a polynomial in one indeterminate over a residue field is not the zero formal polynomial but vanishes on every field element. Degree \(|K_A|\) is of the scale at which (3) loses content, and repeated squaring reaches that scale with \(O(\log |K_A|)\) multiplication gates. This observation limits the symbolic comparison; it is not a search or recovery construction. The result imposes no bound on unrestricted classical Ring-LWE, actual parameter sets, cryptographic schemes or implementations.
