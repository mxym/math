# Earlier results and scope

* Hui Zhang and Tsuyoshi Takagi, [IEICE Transactions on Fundamentals E97-A (7), 1533–1541 (2014), DOI 10.1587/transfun.E97.A.1533](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/transfun/E97.A/7/E97.A_1533/_article), explicitly obtain efficient factoring for sufficiently close same-scale primes with spread below N^(1/r^2). This is an earlier result in the all-slopes-one, close-factor direction, not counted here as new. Mengce Zheng, Noboru Kunihiro, and Honggang Hu, [ePrint 2015/1137](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/1137), develop further lattice-based work for same-bit-size factors, a different method and hypothesis from a connected graph of hidden slopes. We do not claim the unscaled case as new, including the polylogarithmic inflation of the common close-factor scale available from the general approximation theorem below.
* Alexander May, [*New RSA Vulnerabilities Using Lattice Reduction Methods*, Theorem 7, printed p. 37](https://d-nb.info/972386416/34#page=38), records the general univariate Coppersmith bound at the exponent threshold with an allowable polylogarithmic multiplier. Once the scaled product supplies approximate factor centers, this is an alternative route at the same scale. The present argument is an elementary, lattice-free product-to-polynomial reconstruction; it does not claim that approximation-based polynomial-time factorization was unknown.
* Jonathon Hales and Ghaith Hiary, [*A generalization of Lehman's method*, §2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-00959-7), extend weighted arithmetic/geometric-mean estimates for factoring and power divisors, an earlier close-factor approach. Manindra Agrawal, Nitin Saxena, and Shubham Sahai Srivastava, [*Integer Factoring Using Small Algebraic Dependencies*, introduction](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.6), describe the classical reduction from an exact supplied bivariate relation to univariate integer roots. Here the unknown deviations need not be polynomially enumerable.
* Bartosz Źrałek, [*A deterministic version of Pollard's p−1 algorithm*, v5 Lemma 8.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102v5#page=19), reconstructs elementary symmetric coefficients without carries in base A from exact common congruences p_i=1 mod A and sufficiently large A. That is a closely related coefficient method. Here the center is computed from the normalized product, offsets are signed and need not satisfy a congruence, and carries are handled by finite coefficient windows.
* J. Renegar, [*On the Computational Complexity and Geometry of the First-order Theory of the Reals*, Part I, Theorem 1.1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717110800033), is one standard source for the exact fixed-dimensional decision step. Any deterministic exact univariate real-root isolation routine with a polynomial bit bound also suffices.

The specific result here is an explicit deterministic normalization from a connected graph of near-affine arithmetic relations with hidden bounded slopes, followed by elementary reconstruction at the centered-digit scale. The source comparison is targeted, not an assertion of worldwide priority. The examples are finite number-theory fixtures and the result does not describe any particular cryptographic system, key, or deployment.
