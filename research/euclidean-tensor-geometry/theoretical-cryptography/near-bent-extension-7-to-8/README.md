# Every seven-variable near-bent Boolean function admits a bent extension

**Theorem.** If $p:\mathbb F_2^7\to\mathbb F_2$ has Walsh values in $\{0,16,-16\}$, then there is a Boolean $q$ on the same space such that $p\Vert q$ is bent on eight variables. The given $p$ occupies exactly the original lower hyperplane; no input or output transformation of $p$ is necessary.

## Proof from the eight-variable cubic derivative theorem

Let $T$ denote the unnormalized real Walsh (Hadamard) matrix $T_{u,x}=(-1)^{u\cdot x}$ and $W_p=T((-1)^p)$. Orthogonality gives $T^2=128I$. Let $h(u)$ be $W_p(u)/16$ reduced modulo 2 (including negative integers). Because $p$ is near-bent, this is exactly the indicator of the nonzero spectrum. Parseval gives $|\{h=1\}|=128^2/16^2=64$.

We claim $\deg h\le3$. Denote by $1_U$ the input with support $U$. For any subset $J$ of size $k\ge4$, the algebraic normal form coefficient of the monomial indexed by $J$ is, modulo 2,
$$
\sum_{U\subseteq J}(-1)^{k-|U|}\frac{W_p(1_U)}{16}
=\frac{(-2)^k}{16}\sum_{x:\,x_j=1\ (\forall j\in J)}(-1)^{p(x)}.
$$
Indeed the inner coefficient of $(-1)^{p(x)}$ after interchanging sums is the product of $(-1)^{x_j}-1$ for $j\in J$. For $k=4$ the rightmost sum has eight terms, hence is even; for $k>4$ its multiplier is even. Every coefficient of degree at least four vanishes.

Apply the eight-variable derivative theorem to the balanced cubic-or-lower $h$ lifted along a dummy variable, in that variable's direction. By its two-slice Walsh criterion, the 64-point set $Z=\{h=0\}$ admits a signing $s$, set equal to zero off $Z$, with $(Ts)(x)\in\{-8,8\}$ for all $x$. Define $q$ by $(-1)^{q(x)}=(Ts)(x)/8$. Then $W_q=T^2s/8=16s$. At each frequency exactly one of $W_p,W_q$ is nonzero and its magnitude is 16. Hence the two coefficients $W_p(u)\pm W_q(u)$ of the concatenation both have magnitude 16, as required.

This argument applies to **all** seven-variable near-bent functions, not just to the additional 1,309 examples below. Its only computer-assisted ingredient is the previously certified eight-variable cubic derivative theorem. It does not extend that theorem or the extension conclusion to arbitrary numbers of variables.

## Numerical cross-check against published representatives

The [Gillot–Langevin classification paper](https://langevin.univ-tln.fr/project/agl7/BFA-paper2022.pdf), Section 5.2, Open Problem 1, asks the converse of hyperplane restriction. This result settles its seven-variable instance. The [Gillot–Langevin–Polujan paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2026.3685370) ([arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21779), §5.3) reports that only 30 of its 1,309 EA representatives of abnormal seven-variable near-bent functions extend to bent functions. The [authors' project page](https://langevin.univ-tln.fr/project/indice/indice.html) uses the unrestricted extension definition above. The following direct calculation gives a different result for precisely that intermediate count. The expansion-coverage argument relying on the published intermediate count of 30 cannot stand as stated, but this does not disprove the final normality theorem. Indeed both concrete eight-variable extensions at indices 5 and 12 are weakly normal: on the four-flats with decimal (offset; ordered basis) $(192;161,18,20,8)$ and $(64;161,18,148,8)$ their respective restrictions are the nonconstant affine functions $1+u_1+u_4$ and $u_3+u_4$ in the four basis coordinates.

For each ANF in the [authors' 1,309-record file](https://langevin.univ-tln.fr/project/indice/representatives.txt), we give a 128-bit upper-half $q$ in extensions.tsv. The verifier first evaluates the source ANF. It then checks the lower seven-variable spectrum and all 256 Walsh coefficients of each full eight-variable concatenation. In addition it cross-references the authors' [30-item extension list](https://langevin.univ-tln.fr/project/indice/ab-near-expandable.txt), stripping only the affine output constant from each reported restriction to match the stated representatives. For all 1,309 original ANFs the extension test succeeds. The discrepancy is with the published existence count, not with the definition or an empirical error in random search; no attribution of its cause is intended.

**Individual witness.** With the source ANF at zero-based index 5, beyond the published 30-item subset, the lower truth table equals 6802f20d771d8474bc7c8326a6965a00 and one upper truth table is aee65d8f2b36285fb3a1d9a13384032e. Bit $z$ represents the input with binary expansion $z$ having variable $a$ in the least significant position. Concatenating the tables (upper half as high bits) gives a function with 136 Walsh coefficients $+16$ and 120 coefficients $-16$.

**Reproduction.** Use Python 3.10 or later, no third-party packages. Run verify_extensions.py with the --download switch (author-hosted input read into memory) or supply --representatives PATH --published-30 PATH to work offline. The ANF and published-30 files are independently hash-pinned, not redistributed. The bundled witness table contains only source ANF indices and constructed upper halves, no third-party ANFs.

The calculation measures existence; it does not count all possible extensions. The attribution "1,309 EA representatives" uses the source authors' classification. No conclusion about other dimensions follows.

## Independent checks and bibliographic details

- The proof above uses [the eight-variable derivative certificates](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/131b8c914ec32770e3c01fab8356270fe95289a1) as a lemma. It does not assume the completeness, abnormality, or inequivalence of the 1,309-record list. Conversely, the empirical 1,309/1,309 verification needs neither the derivative theorem nor its source cubic classification.
- Gillot–Langevin, [author-hosted manuscript](https://langevin.univ-tln.fr/project/agl7/BFA-paper2022.pdf), §5.2, p. 7, Open problem 1: “Are all the near bent function a restriction of a bent function ?” Their enumeration in that section concerns seven inputs; the question is not qualified by normality. The statement here is limited to the seven-to-eight-variable case, not all odd-dimensional near-bent functions.
- Gillot–Langevin–Polujan, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 72 (6), 4352–4364 (2026), [final article](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2026.3685370), p. 4362, §V.C after Algorithm 3 and before Theorem 7: “It appears that only 30 of these representatives are bent-expandable.” Its Appendix B lists the 30. The [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.21779), §5.3, p. 20, has the same statement; its Appendix A is the corresponding list. The source authors' definition above their Remark 5.7 (preprint) is the ordinary concatenation condition. They do not require the upper half or extension itself to be abnormal. We do not impute a cause for their numerical result.
- **Equivalence is not a loophole.** If $p'(z)=p(Az+c)+\ell(z)$ is EA-equivalent to $p$, with $A$ invertible and $\ell$ affine, extend it by applying $(z,t)\mapsto(Az+c,t)$ to $p\Vert q$ and adding $\ell(z)$. This remains bent and has lower half exactly $p'$. In the count, a complement is just the particular affine output change $\ell=1$. In the parent derivative calculation a *different* classification is used (input AGL only) and is not being used to infer pairwise EA inequivalence among the 1,309 records.
- **Stronger identified example.** Zero-based ANF index 12 has lower hex dc7c3dc4fc904782e91c520e33fa78e8 and upper hex 9a74eea9677620685fb1e7af6b8a4afd. Every one of the 94,488 affine four-dimensional flats in $\mathbb F_2^7$ is checked: its restriction to none is affine, so the lower half is genuinely abnormal. It is also **not EA-equivalent to any of the 30 printed appendix entries**, independently of the authors' classification. For every nonzero $d$ take the sorted multiset of the 128 absolute Walsh values of $D_d p$, and take the multiset of these 127 multisets. Under an EA change, differentiating eliminates the added affine function up to a constant, and the linear part of the input change permutes the nonzero directions. Thus this invariant is unchanged. Index 12 has a different invariant from each of the 30. These tests need no enumeration of the full affine or EA groups. The verifier also checks that the 30 supplemental ANFs, with their as-published output constants retained, give exactly the 30 literal hexadecimal words in the preprint appendix (hash of their sorted LF-terminated representation).
- The independent verifier uses the subset zeta transform of the ANF coefficients to form each lower truth table, then checks that all 255 nonzero-direction derivatives of the eight-variable concatenation have weight 128. This is sufficient because the Walsh transform of the sign autocorrelation is $W^2$; zero autocorrelation off the origin implies $W^2=256$ everywhere. The 333,795 derivative checks are logically independent of the first implementation's 335,104 Walsh checks. The source indices in the witness table are zero-based ANF indices, not literal text-file line numbers.
- [Potapov (2023), Proposition 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04244), already relates the support of a bent derivative to the complement of the Walsh support of a near-bent hyperplane restriction; that relationship is not claimed as new. The older [complementary-spectrum criterion](https://doi.org/10.1007/10719994_6) and [Leander–McGuire constructions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2008.12.004) are relevant background, not claims first proved here. As of the 2026-11-27 search, we found neither an earlier proof covering all seven-variable inputs nor a correction of the cited numerical count. The universal seven-to-eight conclusion is a corollary of the preceding derivative result, not a second unrelated discovery.
