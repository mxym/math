# Polynomial exceptional-source sets for two-terminal AND networks

## 1. Model and result

A relation label is a nonconstant dense polynomial F(X,Y) in Z[X,Y], of positive degree in each variable, whose leading coefficient with respect to either variable is a nonzero **constant**. Call it bimonic; the constants need not equal 1. Its reverse F(Y,X) is allowed. A two-terminal formula is a leaf (one oriented label), a series composition (identify only the left sink and right source), or a parallel AND composition (identify only the two sources and the two sinks). All other child vertices are disjoint. A witness assigns values to all vertices and satisfies all leaf equations simultaneously. Repeated labels are allowed. The encoding is an explicit formula, not a compressed graph of reused gates. Define h(g) by 1 at a leaf, addition at series, and maximum at parallel. Every directed path internal to the gate has length at most h(g) if measured as part of a full source-to-sink path.

**Exceptional-source theorem.** Suppose formula size, alphabet dense bit size and leaf coefficient bit length are polynomial in a size parameter L, and D=d^h is polynomial in L, where d is the maximum partial leaf degree (at least 1) and h is the root path-length bound. Deterministically in polynomial bit time, construct for every gate g an integer bivariate polynomial G_g(T,Y), primitive, of squarefree generic Y-root set, and a nonzero integer polynomial E_g(T). For every complex t with E_g(t) nonzero, the sink values of all complex witnesses with source t are **exactly** the roots of G_g(t,Y). The endpoint set is finite. G_g=1 signifies no witness for a transcendental source; E_g is a sufficient, not necessarily minimal, exceptional polynomial.

**Arithmetic consequence.** Fix a uniform public rule, depending only on L=bit length of n, for producing a catalogue of such labelled formulas and their alphabet, of total dense encoding length and generation time polynomial in L, with d^max(h) polynomial in L. On the prime support of n, put an undirected edge between different primes if a label holds in one orientation. A prime witness for a formula uses only support primes and has distinct prime values at each leaf's two endpoints; nonadjacent vertices may repeat. Define membership (n>=2) by (i) connectedness of the support graph, and (ii) existence of a prime witness for some listed formula with G_root=1. There is a deterministic bit-polynomial decision algorithm for this promise class, which also returns the complete prime factorization on members. Neither support size nor multiplicities are fixed. This is a public-structure theorem, not an algorithmic claim for unrestricted inputs; it supplies no prevalence assertion. A singleton support has no witness with the distinct-adjacent convention.

## 2. Path calculations

For a directed oriented word, denote its full endpoint polynomial by P_pi(T,Y). The empty path has Y-T. A nonempty path is built by the raw Sylvester recursion
```
P_{pi F}(T,Y) = Res_U(P_pi(T,U), F(U,Y)).
```
Its Y-leading coefficient is a nonzero constant, its partial degrees are at most d^length(pi), and for every complex t its roots are precisely the endpoints of paths from t (allowing all complex intermediate values). For the induction, the first polynomial in U retains degree on specializing t; the product formula for the resultant then gives, up to a nonzero constant, a product of F(u,Y) over its roots u with multiplicity. Every factor has the same nonzero constant Y-leading coefficient. The separate degree bounds follow from Sylvester homogeneity in the two blocks of coefficients. In particular, the root product is not use of specialized degree in the second polynomial as a variable convention: the identity with the original Sylvester degree still holds when that degree drops at an individual Y. These statements also hold in any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

For a transcendental start T, each intermediate vertex or endpoint of any path is transcendental over Q. Otherwise a first nontranscendental endpoint forces its predecessor algebraic over Q, since F(X,u) still has positive X-degree for each u, and backward iteration contradicts transcendence of T. The same applies to the canonical path within a genuine complex witness, measured over a function field at the generic start. In particular P_pi(T,U) has no generic root that is a constant algebraic number. Choice of an orientation does not change either bimonicity property.

Quantitatively, if the leaf coefficient lengths are at most b, use coefficient 1-norm for the determinant bounds. At a length-j step one block has size at most d, the other at most d^j; the next log norm is bounded by d times the preceding log norm plus O(d^j b+d^j log(d^j+d+1)+d log(d^j+d+1)), after including dense degree factors in those logarithms. More explicitly, the sum of products has at most (d^j+d)! terms and each next row uses coefficients from one of the two polynomials. Iterating gives a bound polynomial in h,D,b and the input sizes, including d=1. The determinants can be computed by exact polynomial matrix arithmetic, or by an integer interpolation grid using these degree bounds and polynomial-height evaluation. There are only two free variables in each resultant.

For each gate g choose a canonical full directed path pi_g: concatenate at series, choose the first child at parallel. For each descendant v of g (including g), choose a prefix pi_{g,v} to the source of v. For v=g it is empty. In a parallel child and in a left series child reuse that child's prefix. For v in the right series child first take pi_left, then the prefix within the right child. All chosen prefixes have length at most h; crucially they are constrained paths in the corresponding witness, not algorithmically guessed paths.

## 3. Local algebra and specialization

All generic computations are over K=Q(T) in the sink variable. Normalize a nonzero polynomial by taking its primitive, squarefree representative in Z[T,Y], clearing rational-function denominators, deleting all factors depending only on T, and making the integer coefficient content 1. For constants use 1. This operation preserves generic roots. For an integer polynomial, once its T-content is removed, squarefree normalization also preserves the specialized root set at every t where this primitive polynomial and its normalized representative do not vanish identically: both are products of the same nonvertical irreducible integer factors up to nonzero constants, with different positive multiplicities. At any t the leading coefficient of the normalized product being nonzero prevents any factor specializing identically to zero.

Construct each G_g and a single *local* operation polynomial B_g(T) as follows. B_g is not an accumulated child exceptional set.

* Leaf. Normalize F to G_g and use B_g=1. The constant Y-leading coefficient of F precludes any T-only factor and forces each nonconstant factor to have constant Y-leading coefficient. Therefore removing powers preserves every specialization.
* Parallel. Take G_g to be the normalized gcd H of G_A,G_B over K[Y]. Take two divisibility identities and one Bezout identity
  ```
  G_A=H Q_A,    G_B=H Q_B,    A_0 G_A+B_0 G_B=H
  ```
  with coefficients in K. Use for B_g the product of their nonzero polynomial denominators and the leading Y-coefficient of H. At t with B_g(t) nonzero, joint vanishing of the two child polynomials is equivalent to vanishing of H, including H=1.
* Series. If either child has generic G=1, set G_g=1 and B_g=1. If both nonempty, form R(T,Y)=Res_U(G_A(T,U),G_B(U,Y)), remove its nonzero T-content c(T), and normalize the remainder to G_g. Use B_g=c(T) times the Y-leading coefficients of G_A and G_g, writing the former as the U-leading coefficient when needed. The generic intermediary u is transcendental over Q, by containment in the left canonical path: substituting it for the source in the right polynomial neither kills its leading coefficient nor its valid generic witness equivalence. Thus R is nonzero and the resultant root product describes exactly the generic series endpoints. At t with B_g(t) nonzero, provided the left child specializes exactly at t and the right child specializes exactly at *each* actual left endpoint u, the same conclusion holds by the root product and normalization. For a generic-empty child, the same proviso implies empty specialized composition, without forming a resultant.

The generic statements here follow simultaneously by induction, or by applying the previous paragraph in an algebraic closure of Q(T); at a transcendental argument all nonzero polynomials in that argument remain nonzero. For parallel children their interiors are disjoint, hence witnesses with matching terminals can be combined. For series they share only the intermediate value, so generic composition has exactly the stated existential semantics. A gate's actual generic endpoints are a subset of its canonical path's endpoints. This proves by induction the containment used to exclude constants for the next series gate.

## 4. A single layer of pullbacks

For every gate define
```
E_g(T) = product over descendants v of g of
         Res_U(P_{pi_{g,v}}(T,U), B_v(U)).
```
The empty-prefix factor is B_g(T). A nonzero constant B contributes a nonzero constant factor. Otherwise its roots are algebraic constants, and no generic path endpoint can be among them, so every pulled-back factor is nonzero. For every complex t the path calculation shows exactly that such a factor vanishes iff some endpoint of the specified canonical prefix is a zero of B_v. There is no assumption that G itself is bimonic. Bimonicity is used on the **public raw paths**; this is why a constant upstream branch cannot make a pullback identically zero.

Inductively suppose E_g(t) is nonzero. At a parallel gate each child's raw prefix factors occur unchanged; exact child specialization and the local identities give the result. At a series gate the left child's factors likewise occur unchanged, so every left endpoint u is an actual left witness endpoint and is reachable by the left canonical full path. If E_right(u) vanished, some descendant's local B_v would vanish at the end of a right-child canonical prefix from u. Concatenating the canonical left path and this prefix witnesses a vanishing factor of E_g(t), a contradiction. Thus every possible u is an exact specialization for the right child. Together with the local operation polynomial, this proves both directions of the endpoint equality, including empty children. Finiteness follows for every t (even exceptional t) from containment in the full canonical path. The key point is to pull back **local** operation factors along raw paths, not to feed a large accumulated exception polynomial recursively through each composition.

## 5. Bit bounds for the full construction

Every nonconstant G_g divides P_{pi_g} in K[Y] since it is squarefree there and its roots are a subset of the raw path roots. The normalized G_g is also primitive as a polynomial in Y over Z[T]; Gauss's lemma therefore gives integer bivariate divisibility. Both its partial degrees are at most D. To bound its coefficient height independently of recursive algebraic simplification, substitute T=z and Y=z^(D+1) in both polynomials. This substitution preserves coefficients distinctly on each of their supports and gives univariate integer divisibility of degree O((D+1)^2). Every root of the factor is a root of the raw path image and thus has modulus at most one plus the raw maximum coefficient; the absolute leading coefficient divides the raw integral leading coefficient and is at most its absolute value. Elementary symmetric coefficient bounds now give polynomial log height for G_g in D and the already bounded raw path height. The same uniform bound includes the case G_g=1.

For arithmetic over Q(T), polynomial resultants may be computed by Sylvester determinants; bounded polynomial gcds, normalization and Bezout data admit deterministic polynomial bit cost. Here is one explicit justification for the last point. For a pair of univariates of Y-degree at most m with bounded coefficient polynomials in T, try k=0,...,m and solve the coefficient equations for A P+B Q=Y^k+sum_{i<k} c_i Y^i with unknown A,B of degrees at most 2m. The first feasible degree gives the monic gcd and bounded Bezout data by Euclidean degree bounds. Over Q(T), coefficient-matrix ranks and a solved consistent subsystem may be calculated by fraction-free elimination and ratios of minors (there are only polynomially many pivots tested). With polynomial matrix dimension and polynomial degree/height of the entries, all needed minors have polynomial degree and coefficient bit length by the determinant norm bound. Constant and zero inputs are trivial separately; for rational function entries clear the polynomially many denominators first. Division to obtain quotient identities is another bounded linear coefficient solve. Squarefree normalization uses the gcd with the derivative, quotient, univariate T-content gcd and scalar primitive normalization. No multivariate irreducible factorization is required to compute this normalization. These operations also apply to an immediate series resultant before taking any quotient, whose size is polynomial by its uniformly bounded child inputs. Therefore all individual B_v have polynomial degree/height. Each factor of E_g uses a raw path P of the path bound and one such B_v, and is one further polynomial-size resultant. The number of factors is bounded by explicit gate count; multiplication adds degrees and log 1-norms. Computing all E_g separately is still polynomial. This bounds degrees, intermediate sizes, and running time, not just count of generic endpoints.

## 6. From exceptional sources to arithmetic membership

For each listed root with G_root=1, any true prime witness must start at an integer root of E_root. List them by exact univariate rational factorization or real-root isolation and integer testing, retaining only positive prime divisors of n. Standard deterministic polynomial bit algorithms for dense integer univariate factoring and primality suffice. For each candidate p verify an actual prime witness, rather than accepting an exceptional complex witness:

* Memoize the set of feasible sinks for a gate and a supplied support prime. At a leaf, evaluate the relation at the given source, list its integer roots, and retain only different prime divisors of the original n. Bimonicity ensures every specialized polynomial has fixed positive degree. At a parallel gate intersect child sink sets. At a series gate union the right child's sink sets over the left child's sinks. The set recursion is exact because the only shared vertices are the displayed terminals. It allows repeated values across nonadjacent vertices.
* Every encountered state argument or returned sink is a true prime divisor of n, hence there are at most L possible values per gate. The gate/prime memoization and set computations have polynomial cost regardless of the number of raw witness assignments. Coefficients evaluated at L-bit inputs still have polynomial bit lengths.
* From a certified p traverse the undirected support component by solving each specialized public label in both orientations, keeping only distinct prime divisors of n. This has at most L vertices. Remove every multiplicity of each visited prime by exact division. Residue 1 is equivalent to complete connectivity of the support; the certificate already supplies the other membership condition. If the algorithm accepts, both conditions thus hold. Conversely the true witness on a member has its source in the exceptional root list, passes exact verification and reaches all support vertices. Reject if no candidate passes both tests.

This gives the claimed recognition and full factorization of the stated class without assuming a supplied relation template, source prime, factor count or exponent list beyond the public L-only catalogue. No outside-class runtime claim is needed: even rejection uses only polynomially many algebraic candidates and actual divisors when exploring.

## 7. Shared variable is not an independent bundle

Let f1(X)=2X+1, f2(X)=3X-4, f3(X)=X+6, and h(X)=4X+3. The two left relations A=(U-f1)(U-f2)=0 and B=(U-f1)(U-f3)=0 must both hold at a shared U. Append in series one bimonic relation
```
C(U,Y)=(3Y-4U-25)(Y-4U+21)=0.
```
Finally impose the direct edge Y=h(X) in parallel AND. All four labels are bimonic. The two branches on the appended edge are h composed with the inverse of f2 and h composed with the inverse of f3 respectively. Accordingly the three constituent independent paths A-C, B-C, and the direct path each contain the generic endpoint h(X); any bundle of these paths with only common terminals fails to provide a finite-source certificate. In this network, however, generically the two left edges force the same U=f1(X). More explicitly, f2=f3 only at X=5 and then both also equal f1. Substitution of U=f1(X),Y=h(X) into the appended edge gives two nonzero scalar multiples of X-5 as its two factors. Thus the entire network has exactly one complex witness, (X,U,Y)=(5,11,23), including its unique source. All three values are prime and the support graph is connected. This separates this network certificate from all independent bundles of its constituent paths; it is not a claim about every other word constructible from the same alphabet.

A distinct specialization example is A=(Y-T)(Y-2T-1), B=(Y-T)(Y-3T+1). Their generic gcd is Y-T; at T=2 the extra common endpoint 5 appears. A prefix U=2T+1 pulls this exceptional source back to T=1/2, rather than 2. It illustrates why it is not enough just to reuse a generic gcd at all sources, or to union child exception roots without a pullback.
