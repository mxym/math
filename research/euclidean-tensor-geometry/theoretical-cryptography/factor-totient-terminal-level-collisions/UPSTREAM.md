# Sources and bounded scope

* [Three-component companion, fixed Git commit, complete proof](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/e36b5afe51724bc4e0cba333b5be5c4d21a6b545/research/euclidean-tensor-geometry/theoretical-cryptography/factor-totient-prime-graph-three-components/PROOF.md). The reduced D,V,B invariants, the two-tree resultant, and the three-coordinate projective method are prior within this line. The new step separates leaves by multiplicity and uses **two** pair-product equations. The proof in the present package is independent of access to the companion.
* Erdős, Luca and Pomerance, [*On the proportion of numbers coprime to a given integer*, §2](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/gcdnphin14.pdf): reduced support of the ratio phi(n)/n and Algorithm A. Its reconstruction uses the largest prime factor of successive denominators. The elementary gcd layer identity for a known squarefree divisor is a valuation calculation, not a new primality or integer-factorization primitive.
* Ford, Konyagin and Luca, [*Prime chains and Pratt trees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.0473): the underlying p | q-1 relation/chains are classical. The present graph uses only primes dividing N and the orientation toward larger primes.
* Źrałek, [*A deterministic version of Pollard's p-1 algorithm*, §6, printed p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102v5), records the two-prime case with multiplicities and a three-distinct-prime unequal-exponent result from a single totient. Morain, Renault and Smith, [*Deterministic factoring with oracles*, §5.1, Thm 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444v2), give the Landau equal-order result using an oracle on multiple inputs. It is not assumed here that a totient of each newly found unfactored block is available. Hittmeir and Pomykała, [publisher](https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2020-1891), have a different resource: the factorization of phi(N).
* The [Stacks Project](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01WC) gives the properness/closed-projection fact for a projective space. The resultant criterion and affine Bézout bound for isolated solutions are standard. Renegar, [*On the Computational Complexity and Geometry of the First-Order Theory of the Reals*, Part I](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/305759aa-f544-4006-aa85-f70ddce54e00/content), supplies the fixed-variable existential decision complexity used for listing bounded integer points.

The limited priority comparison concerns these named results; no assertion of global priority, positive density or an infinite supply of examples is made. The theorem needs one exact numeric totient and all component/multiplicity promises, not merely four factors or the existence of any prime chain.
