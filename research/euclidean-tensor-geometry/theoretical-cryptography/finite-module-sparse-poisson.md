# Poisson critical window for a sparse signed-monomial random-module shell

Let d≥1 be a power of two, S=Z[Y]/(Y^d+1), q an odd prime, S_q=∏_{j=1}^J F_{q^{f_j}}, and M_1,…,M_B independent uniform in S_q^t, with D=dt. No restriction on how q splits. For each nonempty support of size w use signed monomials ±Y^k, 0≤k<d, normalized so the first nonzero entry is 1. There are N=binom(B,w)(2d)^{w−1} such candidates. Let Z count candidates satisfying Σ_i c_i M_i=0, and put λ=N q^{−D}. Scalar signed unit lines are the d=1 case. This theorem concerns the random count, not finding a relation or solving a cryptographic problem.

## Finite factorial moment estimate

For k≥2 and 2≤r<k let
    ρ_r = min(1, binom(r,2) 2^w (w/B)^{ceil(w/(2(r−1)))}) .
Then
    0 ≤ E[(Z)_k] − (N)_k q^(−Dk)
      ≤ d Σ_{r=2}^{k−1} binom(k,r) λ^r ρ_r (2d+1)^{r(k−r)}.
For k=1, equality E Z=λ is exact.

To prove the upper bound, evaluate a tuple of k distinct normalized candidates in each CRT component, and let r be the minimum of its component row ranks. The joint event probability is q^{−t Σ_j f_j r_j}, hence lies between q^{−Dk} and q^{−Dr}, and equals the former if r=k. Distinct candidate rows are never proportional in any one component: their supports and normalized first nonzero value survive, and the 2d signed monomial values at a root of Y^d+1 are distinct because every root has multiplicative order 2d. Thus r≥2 for k≥2.

For r<k choose a component attaining r and r of the tuple's positions furnishing an independent basis there. Call an ordered r-tuple good if each basis support has strictly more than w/2 positions outside the union of other basis supports. If the basis is good, any nonzero linear combination involving at least two basis vectors has more than w nonzero positions: each of its participating basis rows contributes its own exclusive positions. A combination using just one basis vector cannot give another normalized candidate (the scalar must be 1). Hence a deficient k-tuple must have a bad chosen basis.

For r independently uniformly selected shell candidates their supports are iid uniform w-subsets. If a basis row has at most w/2 exclusive positions, it overlaps some other basis row at least ceil(w/[2(r−1)]) times. For two iid uniform w-subsets A,B0, Pr(|A∩B0|≥u)≤binom(w,u)(w/B)^u by the union bound over u-element subsets of A. This gives the stated ρ_r, so the number of bad ordered r-tuples is at most N^r ρ_r (counting repeats only enlarges it). In the chosen factor, r pivot coordinate values determine a vector in an r-dimensional span. Each such coordinate is either zero or one of 2d allowed values, so the span contains at most (2d+1)^r candidates, using injectivity of the normalized ring candidate into its reduction in that factor. The number of ordered deficient k-tuples is therefore at most d binom(k,r) N^r ρ_r (2d+1)^{r(k−r)} for any rank r, using J≤d. Multiply by q^{−Dr} to get the estimate. This counts even deficiency in a single CRT factor, and does not replace all component ranks by one equality.

## Limit and critical minimum

If B/w→∞ and log d=o(w log(B/w)), with λ→λ_*∈[0,∞), then Z converges in total variation to Pois(λ_*). Indeed ρ_r≤exp(−Ω_r(w log(B/w))) for fixed r, so every moment error term tends to zero; N→∞ and (N)_k q^(−Dk)→λ_*^k. The factorial moment conclusion implies the Poisson probability mass by elementary Bonferroni: for fixed l, expand binom(Z,l)(1−1)^(Z−l), take even/odd truncation bounds, and take limits of the fixed factorial moments to squeeze Pr(Z=l) between partial sums for λ_*^l exp(−λ_*)/l!. Bounded means imply tightness, turning pointwise masses into total variation convergence. The same reasoning on subsequences shows TV(Z,Pois(λ))→0 when λ is merely bounded and need not converge.

If λ_*∈(0,∞), the minimum energy among nonzero signed-monomial tuples is w with probability 1−exp(−λ_*)+o(1), and w+1 with probability exp(−λ_*)+o(1). Each successive lower-shell candidate-count ratio is at most w/[2d(B−w+1)], so their total expected count is o(1). The next shell mean tends to infinity as its upward ratio is 2d(B−w)/(w+1). Its pairwise independence follows either by the k=2 formula or by the unit-minor argument; variance is at most its mean, so it is nonempty with high probability. This is conditional on a sequence with λ_w converging to a strictly positive finite value rather than a claim that every natural size sequence lands in that window. Such sequences can be produced, for example, with d=1, fixed odd q, w→∞, t=w², and choose B as the smallest size making λ≥λ_*. There B/w→∞ and adjacent-B count ratios tend to 1 uniformly near the threshold, so λ→λ_*.

For d=1 the same two-point law applies to the actual integer minimum when additionally w<q and w(w/B)^3→0. In the scalar sparse count (see [the scalar note](finite-module-scalar-energy.md)), all primitive sign lines of energy ≤w number between N and N(1+H(x))^w/(1−x) with x=w/[2(B−w+1)], H(x)=Σ_{m≥2}x^(m²−1)=O(x³). Thus all lines other than the signed-unit exact shell number o(N), and each has probability q^{−t} because its reduction is nonzero, so they have vanishing expected count. Nonprimitive vectors of energy below w cannot introduce a smaller minimum without their primitive division being feasible, because w<q. The upper w+1 signed-unit shell remains available. No analog for the entire ring-valued integer minimum is asserted, where extra nonunit coefficients can change marginals.

## Exact finite certificate and comparator literature

Pairwise independence does not imply exact finite Poisson. Three scalar signed lines e1−e2, e2−e3, e1−e3 have rank 2. For a genuinely mixed CRT example take d=2, q=5, B=w=3, t=1: amongst 560 unordered triples there are 48 of ranks (2,2), 480 of ranks (3,3), 16 of ranks (2,3), and 16 of ranks (3,2). Direct enumeration of all 15,625 toy input matrices gives summed moments (Z)_1,(Z)_2,(Z)_3 of 10,000; 6,000; 11,040 respectively. [The exact checker](verify-finite-module-sparse-poisson.js) verifies these by exact CRT-rank probability formula, and checks two additional small parameter sets. The theorem makes these higher-order dependencies negligible only in its sparse growing regime.

Hao–Huang–Livshyts–Tikhomirov, https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12833, prove a strong comparison with independent projective weights, especially for conventional random linear codes in fixed-rate regimes. Their work already treats the broad theme of dependencies negligibly affecting minimal weight. This note concerns an explicitly sparse fixed signed-monomial alphabet with possibly growing degree, and controls mixed CRT ranks by exclusive supports; no priority claim for factorial moments or a general coding theorem is made. Linial–Mosheiff discuss moment contributions from low-rank configurations for random binary linear codes (https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.21214).
