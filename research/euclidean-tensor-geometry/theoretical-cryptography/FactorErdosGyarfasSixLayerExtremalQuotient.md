# The only six-layer bipartite abelian quotient attaining dyadic minimum 32

Let G be a nonempty finite simple bipartite graph of minimum degree at least three. Suppose a free abelian group A acts preserving both parts and has at most six vertex orbits. If G has none of the cycle lengths 4, 8 and 16, the quotient on vertex orbits is exactly the simple graph K3,3, with one matching for each base edge. In particular G is cubic. Each component is a connected regular abelian cover of K3,3. Consequently G has a 32-cycle through each vertex. Its minimum possible order is 798, with exactly the three already classified graphs at equality. The conclusion about the exact order now ranges over all such graphs and actions, not just the previously specified base. This does not apply when the group can interchange the two parts, and not to the unrestricted Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture.

## Reduction to three cubic quotients

Use the previously proved five-orbit theorem to assume six layers. There are no within-layer edges because the action preserves the parts. Between two layers the edge orbits are translation matchings. Three matchings on one pair give 4, 8 or 16 by the two-layer theorem. If two distinct doubled pairs have a common layer, two nonzero differences x and y are available as length-two paths in that layer. If x = ±y, the two paths with common endpoints give a square. Otherwise the rectangle 0, x, x+y, y is simple and expands to an 8-cycle. The two different intermediate layers separate the path types, and the two passes of one type differ by the other nonzero displacement. Thus quotient multiplicities are at most 2 and doubled pairs are disjoint. A 1+5 split cannot reach minimum degree three on the size-five side. In a 2+4 split the four layers would each need a doubled pair, contradicting disjointness. The split is 3+3.

In the 3-by-3 matrix, each row or column with sum greater than three has all entries positive, since at most one can be 2. If an excess row exists, total row and column sums give an excess column. Decrement their intersection to remove a matching and preserve all minimum sums. Repeat to obtain a spanning cubic quotient. In a cubic quotient the zero entries and doubled entries occupy identical sets of rows and columns, and each set has at most one of each. Up to independent permutations of the sides, there are just three patterns. The number of doubled entries is zero, two, or three. A single double would require the zero in its own cell. At two doubles the relative permutation between the zero and double matchings is a transposition, and at three it is a three-cycle. The zero-double pattern is the all-ones matrix K3,3.

The other two cubic quotient matrices, in the replay order, are
```
0 1 2       0 1 2
1 1 1       1 2 0
2 1 0       2 0 1
```
The shift certificates below force a 4-, 8-, or 16-cycle in every simple lift of either pattern. Thus the cubic subgraph has quotient K3,3.

## An extra matching to K3,3 forces a short cycle

Suppose K3,3 has a doubled pair AB, with A on the left and B on the right. Let x be the nonzero shift of a two-edge walk from A to itself through two different matchings on AB. Choose its orientation so repeated traversals move by x. Excluding 4-cycles gives 2x nonzero.

Write C,D for the two right layers different from B and R,S for the two left layers different from A. Switch shifts to zero on AC and AD. Consider the length-four walks A-C-R-D-A and A-C-S-D-A with displacements y and z. If y or z is zero, the corresponding walk is a 4-cycle. If either is ±2x, its path and two consecutive AB steps of displacement ±x have the same endpoints and disjoint interiors, giving a simple 8-cycle. If y=z, the paths C-R-D and C-S-D have common endpoints and give a square. If y=-z and nonzero, use the two A-based length-four paths, reversing the second and starting both at sheet zero. Their C and D vertices occur at sheets zero and y interchanged. The R and S vertices lie in different layers, so the paths form a simple 8-cycle. It follows that one of y and z lies outside {0, ±x, ±2x}. Call it y and call its length-four path Q.

Let P be two consecutive AB steps, of length four and displacement 2x. Concatenate P, Q, P reversed, Q reversed from their successive endpoints. The four displacements sum to zero because A is abelian. The visited sheets in the A layer, excluding the repeated endpoint, are 0, x, 2x, 2x+y, x+y, y. They are distinct by the preceding exclusions. In layer B the first two and second two sheets are the same pair translated by y, and the first pair differs by x. These four sheets are distinct because y is different from 0 and ±x. In the three internal layers of Q there is one forward and one backward visit, at sheets differing by 2x. Those layers are distinct from A and B. Thus the 16-edge walk is simple, a contradiction.

Returning to the spanning K3,3 quotient, any omitted matching makes a doubled pair there, which this argument excludes. There was therefore no omitted edge, and G itself is cubic with simple quotient. Switch the tree shifts to zero. The chord shifts generate a subgroup H of A; each component consists of one coset of H in each fiber. The group H acts freely and transitively in each layer of the component, giving the asserted connected regular abelian covers. The known rooted theorem and fixed-base census now apply to each component.

## Frozen certificates and independent checks

The replay verifies both doubled cubic patterns and independently corroborates the extra-matching lemma using a third certificate for the matrix ((1,1,2),(1,1,1),(1,1,1)). Each edge is a signed unit coordinate corresponding to its cotree edge, or zero on the spanning tree. At a lattice R of relations known to vanish in A, a witness is a closed base word whose total chord vector is in R, with every same-layer difference between distinct prefix vectors outside R. If a difference vanishes after evaluation into A, the certificate has a child for the lattice generated by R and exactly that difference. The checker verifies all such differences. Every finite branch ends at a surviving 4-, 8-, or 16-cycle, or a length-two witness in two distinct edge orbits. The latter means two matchings coincide, impossible in a simple graph. A root with no relations covers arbitrary abelian groups, including all torsion patterns. A failure of simplicity adds a vector outside the current lattice, so each recursive step strictly increases the hypothesized relation lattice. The checker validates every collision mechanism; in the finite tree the branch must terminate at a true short cycle or an impossible duplicated matching.

The three certificate sizes are 6, 31 and 6 distinct relation states, with depths 2, 4 and 2. The [primary Node replay](verify_erdos_gyarfas_six_layer_extremal.mjs) recomputes lattice membership by the rank and largest-minor gcd: when a vector is appended, unchanged rank gives the same rational span and saturation, and unchanged divisor gives the same finite index in that saturation. The [independent Python replay](verify_erdos_gyarfas_six_layer_extremal_independent.py) instead computes the integer row Hermite form by Euclidean row operations. It independently validates the edge data, every occurrence in each tree (7, 57, and 7), exact closure, all collision successors, and fails if any root branch is removed. It also enumerates all 190 possible 3-by-3 minimum-sum matrices after the disjointness reduction and recovers the same three cubic patterns. The finite proofs use only the complete relation trees, not searches over sampled finite groups.

Run `node verify_erdos_gyarfas_six_layer_extremal.mjs` and then `python3 verify_erdos_gyarfas_six_layer_extremal_independent.py` in this directory.
