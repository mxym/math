# Sharp low-degree CRT observation bounds

This is a model-specific classical lower bound for obtaining factor-sensitive observations, not an ordinary bit-complexity lower bound or a factoring algorithm.

## Experiment

Fix distinct primes p,q and N=pq. A CRT representation is never supplied to an observer. Its hidden data are vectors X_p∈F_p^m and X_q∈F_q^m, independent and uniformly distributed, representing one hidden vector over Z/NZ. A query is an explicitly modeled polynomial f in m variables with coefficients in Z/NZ. The same data can be reused arbitrarily; renaming variables accommodates fresh independent coordinates. In the global experiment the only response is 1 when f vanishes on both components and 0 otherwise. In a stronger mathematical comparison experiment, the response is the pair of local zero indicators. This second transcript is used to upper-bound detectable component separation; it is not a real interface supplying local tests.

Identically zero polynomials modulo N are allowed, with fixed answers 1 or (1,1) in both experiments. For each remaining query, require its reduction to be a nonzero *formal polynomial* modulo both primes; this is equivalent to coefficient content gcd(N,c_1,...,c_k)=1. For example a nonzero integer coefficient vector with all entries of magnitude less than min(p,q) automatically qualifies. No restriction to constant-free, linear, or nonadaptive queries is imposed. Queries may depend on prior responses and on private coins, but no other information about the hidden vectors may enter the query selection. N and any other data fixed independently of those vectors are unrestricted side information; the theorem makes no claim about computation using that data without queries.

A shadow transcript answers the known identically zero tests exactly and answers every remaining query as nonzero, in both fields in the tagged version. Fix the observer's coins and run it against the shadow. Let d_i be the total degrees of the remaining polynomials on that path, and D=Σ d_i. Assume D≤min(p,q). Then for complete transcripts (including the observer's coins, if randomized),
```
TV(global, shadow) ≤ (Σ_i d_i²)/(pq).
TV(tagged, shadow) ≤ D/p + D/q − D²/(pq).
```
For randomized observers, these right hand sides can be averaged over the coins with their path profiles. A one-sided CRT split at any time in the tagged experiment entails shadow departure, so it is bounded by the tagged formula too. This is an upper bound on such a split, not an equality for split probability.

More precisely, each inequality is sharp for **every** degree profile in the stated regime if only two-sided primitivity is imposed and at least one hidden coordinate exists. The same nonadaptive test family attains both transcript-distance bounds. A budget just on D gives Σ d_i²≤D², attained with a single test allocating the entire degree budget there. A cap d imposes Σ d_i²≤dD, displaying the difference between many capped tests and a single high-degree test. For balanced k-bit primes, polynomial-in-k total degree makes both distances exponentially small, without requiring independent query answers.

## Proof and sharpness

For fixed coins the shadow path is deterministic. Real and shadow transcripts first disagree precisely when some shadow-path query, evaluated at the real hidden vector, would return a nonshadow answer. This equivalence follows by running the same deterministic observer up to the first disagreement. Later behavior after disagreement is irrelevant. Thus the TV distance to the single shadow transcript is the probability of this union of events. Averaging gives the randomized statement, and forgetting coins can only decrease TV.

For the global transcript put A_i={f_i(X_p)=0} and B_i={f_i(X_q)=0}. These events for the same i depend on independent components. The polynomial zero bound gives P(A_i)≤d_i/p and P(B_i)≤d_i/q, hence P(union_i (A_i∩B_i))≤Σ_i d_i²/(pq). Higher powers of a repeated root do not by themselves make this bound tight; the extremizer below uses distinct roots.

For the tagged transcript let A=union_i A_i, B=union_i B_i. At either prime the product of the f_i is nonzero formally, since the polynomial ring over a field has no zero divisors. Its zero event is the union of the local zero events and its degree is at most D. Thus P(A)≤D/p, P(B)≤D/q. The two unions themselves are independent, including when the same sample is reused for every query. Therefore P(A∪B)=a+b−ab with a=P(A), b=P(B), an increasing function in both arguments on [0,1]. The tagged bound follows. Equivalently the same estimate on each union follows from a union bound at that prime, but keeping CRT independence recovers the full product correction.

To certify simultaneous sharpness, partition D distinct small integer test points in {0,1,...,D−1} into blocks of sizes d_i. For block i use a monic univariate polynomial with those d_i simple roots in one coordinate (empty block means f_i=1). Points remain distinct modulo both primes. The local unions have measures precisely D/p and D/q, and the events A_i∩B_i for different i are disjoint. This is an abstract extremal construction for the distances, not a procedure for factoring an ordinary input.

Single-query check. If local zero probabilities are a,b then the probability of an actually asymmetric local response is a+b−2ab. For d≤min(p,q)/2 it is bounded sharply by d/p+d/q−2d²/(pq), attained by the same monic-polynomial construction with one block. Indeed the expression is increasing throughout the rectangle 0≤a≤d/p, 0≤b≤d/q in that degree range. Without that range, do not assert monotonicity.

## Exceptional fibers and refinements

Three formal cases occur for coefficient content c=gcd(N,c_1,...,c_k): c=1 (two-sided primitivity), c=N (identically zero on both sides), or c∈{p,q} (identically zero on exactly one side). This trichotomy concerns coefficients, not values on field points. With arbitrary coefficients one can stop the comparison at the first one-sided coefficient event on the frozen shadow path, apply the formulas to the preceding good queries, and charge an additional probability at most η that the frozen path ever issues a one-sided-content query. The event defining η depends only on observer coins and the fixed side data, not on hidden samples. Without excluding or accounting for this exceptional case, an inverse-prime bound is false: an asymmetric-content constant has one local zero bit deterministically. Two-sided primitive high-degree polynomials can also give function identities, another reason the degree bound matters.

The explicit total degree can often be replaced by any sharper field-specific upper bound on each zero probability in the global transcript, or on each component union in the tagged transcript. For instance if a nonzero reduction has lexicographic leading exponent vector e at a prime ℓ and each e_j<ℓ, its nonzero probability is at least ∏_j (1−e_j/ℓ): induct on variables by taking the highest-degree coefficient in the first variable, restricting to assignments where that coefficient is nonzero, then applying the univariate root bound. The exponents should be computed after reduction on each side. This gives sharper footprint-style bounds for some fixed multivariate profiles.

## Scope and prior art

The proof uses the standard polynomial zero bound, adaptive shadow transcript, and exact CRT independence; related point-counting and symbolic execution are not claimed new. The specific quantitative result is the sharp distinction between the two experiments and the full correction under aggregate degree, not an extension to unrestricted generic rings. In particular circuit size need not bound formal degree polynomially; divisions are not modeled; hidden samples need not model adversarially conditioned distributions; and ordinary access to the integer encodings can leak structure that is absent from these transcripts. Since public N is fixed in the comparison, the shadow is not an alternate distribution of N or its factorization; the theorem bounds distinguishing a transcript from the shadow, and does not bound an observer's implicit knowledge or offline computation of the primes from N.

The closest primary reference is [Aggarwal–Maurer, Breaking RSA Generically is Equivalent to Factoring](https://crypto.ethz.ch/publications/AggMau09.html), which studies a larger generic-ring setting with straight-line operations, equality, and divisions, and proves a reduction, not this restricted transcript bound as stated. [Maurer, On the Oracle Complexity of Factoring Integers](https://crypto.ethz.ch/publications/Maurer96.html) allows arbitrary yes/no questions to an all-powerful oracle and is a different model.
