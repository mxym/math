# A strict entropy gap for HSOP cyclotomic generating functions

Let $H_n$ count the basic degree-$n$ cyclotomic generating functions that are Hilbert series of weighted polynomial rings modulo a homogeneous system of parameters (HSOP). This is $\Phi_n^{\mathrm{alg}}$ in [Billey–Swanson, Problem 62](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07620v3#S6.SS3). Write
\[
 A=\frac{\zeta(2)^2\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{9}{4096000}.
\]

**Theorem.**
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log H_n}{\sqrt n}
 \ \le\ 2\sqrt{A-\gamma}\ <\ 2\sqrt A
 \ =\frac{\sqrt{105\zeta(3)}}{\pi}.
\]
In particular, the unrestricted cyclotomic-partition entropy constant is **not** the HSOP entropy constant. The previously constructed [line-by-divisor-tree submonoid](CGFHSOPDivisorTreeGrowth.md) gives the independent lower bound $3.32315\ldots$ for the liminf. Neither the existence nor the exact value of the limit for the full HSOP monoid is claimed here.

The last constant is the benchmark for unrestricted cyclotomic products with no $\Phi_1$ factor (Boyd–Montgomery, *Cyclotomic partitions*, 1990; the [preceding note](CGFHSOPDivisorTreeGrowth.md#3-upper-benchmark-for-all-hsops) includes an elementary derivation of the free generating series asymptotic). [Gatzweiler–Levicán-Santibáñez–Yoshida, Proposition 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22226v1) give an existence criterion for empty weighted complete intersections. We use only the necessary subset inequality, and prove that the violation is prevalent enough to produce an exponential entropy loss. This is not a bound for the larger monoid of all nonnegative cyclotomic generating functions.

## 1. A local condition in cyclotomic coordinates

For a product $f=\prod_{m\ge2}\Phi_m(q)^{e_m}$, let $d_j$ be its canonical signed exponents in the $q$-integers $[j]_q=(1-q^j)/(1-q)$. For $j\ge2$, Möbius inversion gives
\[
 d_j=\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)e_{jk}, \qquad d_1=-\sum_{j\ge2}d_j.
\]
In the canonical degree multisets $U,V$, positive $d_j$ contribute numerator degrees and negative $d_j$ contribute denominator weights. The adjustment at 1 makes the cardinalities equal. For any HSOP presentation of $f$, these multisets are obtained by canceling all common entries from its degrees and weights.

For a numerator and denominator pair $(U,V)$ the necessary subset condition is: for every submultiset $I$ of $V$, at least $|I|$ entries of $U$ lie in the additive semigroup generated by the weights in $I$, counted with multiplicity. Indeed, on the subspace using only those coordinates, the other homogeneous equations vanish identically, and fewer than $|I|$ equations cannot cut the affine cone to the origin by the height bound. This is the necessary direction of the cited criterion. It persists after canceling one common value $a$ from $U,V$: if $a$ is not in the semigroup generated by a retained $I$, the removed degree did not count; if it is, apply the old bound to $I$ together with the removed copy of $a$, which generates the same semigroup. Induction gives the necessary condition on the canonical pair.

Now fix $r\ge2$. The following **isolated pattern is impossible** for an HSOP product:
\[
 e_{6r}=1,\qquad e_{jr}=0\quad\text{for all positive }j\ne6.
\tag{1}
\]
In fact inversion restricted to multiples of $r$ gives positive degrees $r,6r$ and negative weights $2r,3r$, each with multiplicity one. The semigroup generated by the latter pair cannot contain $r$; it contains $6r$, and there is no other eligible numerator degree because every semigroup element is a multiple of $r$. Thus exactly one numerator degree is available for the two chosen weights, a contradiction. No assumption on the coordinates not divisible by $r$ is needed.

## 2. Independent short tests

Take the free product model at parameter $t=1/X$, with $X\to\infty$. Independently for each $m\ge2$, set
\[
 \Pr(e_m=h)=(1-u_m)u_m^h,\qquad u_m=e^{-\varphi(m)/X},\quad h=0,1,\ldots .
\]
The total weight of a product is $e^{-t\deg f}$ divided by
$F(e^{-t})=\prod_{m\ge2}(1-e^{-t\varphi(m)})^{-1}$.
The law has finite support in the index variable almost surely, because
$\sum_{m\ge2}u_m<\infty$; for example, $\varphi(m)\ge m^{2/3}/2$ by the prime-power formula. For that latter bound, every prime-power factor of $\varphi(m)/m^{2/3}$ is at least 1 except at powers of 2 and 3; their combined worst factor is $6^{1/3}/3>1/2$.

Put $K=96$ and let $R$ contain the integers $r\in[X,2X]$ with no prime divisor at most $K$ and with $\varphi(r)\ge 9r/10$. Then $|R|\ge X/10$ for sufficiently large $X$. To see this, let $P=\prod_{p\le K}p$ and $h_K=\prod_{p\le K}(1-1/p)$. There are $h_K X+O_K(1)$ integers in the interval coprime to $P$. On these integers the conditional mean of $\sum_{p\mid r}1/p$ is at most $1/K+o(1)$: for each $p>K$ the count in this set divisible by $p$ is $h_K X/p+O_K(1)$ by the Chinese remainder theorem. Sum after weighting by $1/p$, using $\sum_{p>K}p^{-2}\le1/K$ and that the total error is $O_K(\log X)$. Markov's inequality and $\varphi(r)/r\ge1-\sum_{p\mid r}1/p$ give a limiting lower density $h_K(1-10/K)>1/10$. The last comparison is an exact finite prime product.

For $r\in R$, let $I_r$ be the event that (1) holds for $1\le j\le K$. These events are independent: an equality $jr=j'r'$ with $j,j'\le K$ and $r,r'\in R$ would give coprime reduced factors dividing the two roots, with prime factors at most $K$, so both reduced factors must be 1 and $r=r'$. The exact event probability is
\[
 \Pr(I_r)=u_{6r}\prod_{j=1}^K(1-u_{jr}).
\]
Here $u_{6r}\ge e^{-4}$ since $(6,r)=1$ and $\varphi(6r)=2\varphi(r)\le4X$; and $\varphi(jr)\ge\varphi(j)\varphi(r)\ge(9/10)X\varphi(j)$, by the prime-power formula. Thus
\[
 \Pr(I_r)\ge e^{-4}\prod_{j\ge1}(1-e^{-9\varphi(j)/10})
 >p_0:=1/320.
\tag{2}
\]
The finite constants are certified in Section 4. For $Z=\sum_{r\in R}\boldsymbol 1_{I_r}$, the independent Bernoulli lower-tail bound gives
\[
 \Pr(Z<p_0|R|/2)\le e^{-p_0|R|/8}.
\tag{3}
\]

## 3. Large multiples cannot usually repair the failures

For a cyclotomic index $m$, put
\[
 a_m=\#\{r\in R:r\mid m,\ m/r>K\},\qquad
 T=\sum_{m\ge2}a_m\boldsymbol 1_{e_m>0}.
\]
If the product belongs to the HSOP monoid, then $T\ge Z$. In fact every root for which the short pattern occurs must be repaired by at least one nonzero large-multiple coordinate; $T$ counts the possible repairs with multiplicity even when one coordinate repairs many roots.

We use the uniform bound
\[
 \#\{j\ge1:\varphi(j)\le Y\}\le16Y\qquad(Y>0).
\tag{4}
\]
To check it, expand $\bigl(j/\varphi(j)\bigr)^2$ as a sum over squarefree divisors of $j$ with positive coefficients. Its sum for $j\le L$ is at most $B L$, where
\[
 B=\prod_p\left(1+\frac{2p-1}{p(p-1)^2}\right)
 \le \frac52\frac{17}{12}\frac{89}{80}e^{1/2}<7.
\]
The tail bound uses $(2p-1)/(p(p-1)^2)\le 2/(p-1)^2$ and summation over $p>5$. There are at most $2Y$ indices up to $2Y$; on the dyadic block $(2^kY,2^{k+1}Y]$, $k\ge1$, the counted indices have ratio at least $2^k$, giving the additional bound $2BY$ summed over those blocks. This proves (4).

Each root counted by $a_m$ has a distinct quotient $j=m/r$. By multiplicativity with the inclusion of common factors, $\varphi(j)\le10\varphi(m)/(9X)$, so (4) gives $a_m\le160\varphi(m)/(9X)$. With $s=9/320$, this means $s a_m\le\varphi(m)/(2X)$. Independence of the cyclotomic coordinates then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \log\mathbb E e^{sT}
 &\le \sum_{m:a_m>0}u_m(e^{s a_m}-1)\\
 &\le \sum_{m:a_m>0}e^{-\varphi(m)/(2X)}
 \le |R|\sum_{j>K}e^{-9\varphi(j)/20}
 < |R|\,s p_0/4 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
In the third bound, charge each index to all its representations $jr$, with $r\in R,\ j>K$. This step does not assume independence of repairs, nor independence between $Z$ and $T$; its purpose is to account for overlaps of arbitrarily large shared multiples. Markov's inequality bounds $\Pr(T\ge p_0|R|/2)$ by $e^{-s p_0 |R|/4}$. Equations (3) and (5) therefore give
\[
 \Pr(f\text{ is HSOP})\le 2e^{-\gamma X},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{s p_0}{40}=\frac9{4096000}.
\]

Let $G(e^{-t})=\sum_{n\ge0}H_n e^{-tn}$. This probability is $G(e^{-t})/F(e^{-t})$. The unrestricted series satisfies $\log F(e^{-t})=(A+o(1))/t$: the counting density of inverse totients is $\zeta(2)\zeta(3)/\zeta(6)$ and the logarithm of the partition product multiplies it by $\zeta(2)$. It follows that $\log G(e^{-t})\le(A-\gamma+o(1))/t$. Bounding $H_n e^{-tn}$ by $G(e^{-t})$ and taking $t=\sqrt{(A-\gamma)/n}$ proves the theorem.

## 4. Exact numerical certificates used in the proof

Run `python3 -O verify_cgf_hsop_strict_entropy_upper.py`. The small rational constants are as follows:
\[
 e^{-9/10}<407/1000,\quad e^{-9/20}<638/1000,\quad
 e^4<55,\quad e^{1/2}<5/3,\quad h_K(1-10/K)>1/10.
\]
All exponential comparisons follow from positive Taylor terms and a geometric bound on the remainder. To verify (2), use the rational upper powers of $407/1000$ through $j<4096$, rounded outwards, then use $\prod(1-a_j)\ge1-\sum a_j$ for the remainder. For (5), use the analogous upper powers of $638/1000$. For either $v=407/1000$ or $638/1000$, the common tail is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{j\ge4096}v^{\varphi(j)}
 \le \frac{4096\,v^{128}}{1-2v^{64}}.
\]
Indeed, on the $i$-th dyadic block from $4096$, $\varphi(j)\ge128\,2^{2i/3}\ge128+64i$. The checker proves the resulting rational strict inequalities against $1/320$ and $s p_0/4=9/409600$. It also runs independent local controls for canonical inversion, disjoint short coordinates, and the forbidden subset.
