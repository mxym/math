# Boundary q²≈w: deterministic annihilators and a clipped critical law

Let d be a power of two, S=Z[Y]/(Y^d+1), and q an odd prime. A tuple c∈S^B has integer coefficient energy E(c), the sum of squares over its Bd integer coefficients. The modulus-q random experiment uses iid uniform rows M_i in S_q^t, t≥1, and calls c feasible if Σ_i c_i M_i=0. The monomial group µ={±Y^j:0≤j<d} acts freely on nonzero integral tuples, preserving energy and feasibility. All results are finite/random algebra, not witness-finding algorithms.

## Deterministic universal relations, including fixed prime powers

For any fixed q and any k≥1, the tuples which annihilate every possible set of unrestricted rows modulo q^k are exactly (q^k S)^B. To see necessity, set one row equal to a vector with a single 1 and all other rows to zero, in turn. Sufficiency is immediate. The shortest nonzero energy is exactly q^{2k}: its attaining tuples have a signed monomial times q^k in one row and all others zero. There are B such µ-orbits. For any integer cutoff W, the exact orbit count of nonzero universal tuples is
    (1/(2d)) Σ_{j=1}^{floor(W/q^{2k})} [z^j] (Σ_{m∈Z} z^{m²})^{Bd}.
In particular only the B shortest orbits occur in the window q^{2k}≤W<2q^{2k}.

More generally if rows may vary in I^t where I=∏_j p_j^{h_j}/q^k S for the primes p_j above q and 0≤h_j≤k, the universal coefficient tuples are exactly A^B with A=∏_j p_j^{k−h_j}. Here q is unramified in the power-of-two cyclotomic ring. Testing one row isolates the annihilator, and local valuations in S/p_j^k give that annihilator componentwise. The exact minimum energy is min_{0≠u∈A}||u||²_coeff, attained by one row; the algebraic norm gives lower bound N(A)^{2/d}. For the principal constant ideal q^k S this is the explicit sharp q^{2k}. We do not replace that minimum by a norm identity for arbitrary ideals.

## Random crossover statement and its hypotheses

Let w→∞, assume B/w²→∞, B≤w^C for a fixed C, and q²/w→1. For the signed-monomial shell with exactly w occupied rows, assume
    λ_w=binom(B,w)(2d)^{w−1} q^(−D)→λ∈(0,∞), D=dt.
The unrestricted nonzero minimum energy, up to o(1) total variation, has the law of min(q², w+X), where X is 0 with probability 1−e^(−λ) and 1 with probability e^(−λ). At energies ≤w, equivalently subtract the deterministic universal orbit count; the remaining count converges to Pois(λ). This replaces q²/w→∞ in [the prior critical-window note](finite-module-unrestricted-critical-window.md) at the transition, not throughout all intermediate or low-sparsity regimes. No particular splitting type or relation between q and t is required.

The inference d=O(w), D=Θ(w) follows directly: criticality first gives the exact upper bound D log q≤w log B+w log(2d)+O(1). Since log q∼(1/2)log w, D≥d and B is polynomial, this yields d≤O(w)(1+log d/log w). It first forces d≤w² for all large w (the ratio d/(1+log d/log w) is increasing for d≥1 and at w² is much larger than w), then d=O(w). Now binomial estimates in the exact criticality identity yield D log q=(log B/log w+log d/log w−1+o(1))w log w, which gives D=Θ(w). Thus the earlier sparse-shell Poisson conditions hold. Fully CRT-active exceptional vectors of energy ≤w remain negligible by the earlier unweighted array-count argument: in Bd slots nearly all such arrays are exactly w signed unit coordinates and, as B≫w², distinct rows with high proportion. All fully active orbits have probability q^(−D). The genuine new issue is nonuniversal inactive tuples close to the q² floor.

## Cyclotomic factor descent and weighted suppression

Let f=ord_{2d}(q), a power of two, the common irreducible factor degree. Put h=max(1,f/2), d'=d/h. Then d'≤q+1. Indeed if f=1, 2d≤q−1. Otherwise LTE gives v₂(q^f−1)=v₂(q−1)+v₂(q+1)+log₂(f)−1. One of q−1,q+1 has valuation 1, so 2d≤(q+1)f and d'≤q+1. For f≥2, the subgroup generated by q² modulo 2d has order h and is precisely the subgroup of exponents congruent 1 modulo 2d/h. If h=1 this is trivial. Otherwise apply the same valuation identity to powers of q²: starting valuation v₂(q²−1)≥3 rises by log₂ h at exactly the modulus 2d, showing inclusion in this congruence subgroup, and the equal orders show equality. Frobenius root orbits of Y^d+1 are thus invariant under multiplying roots by h-th roots of unity. It follows each irreducible factor has form G_j(Y^h), where G_j is a monic irreducible in F_q[Z], of degree f/h≤2. The degree-1 case h=1 is automatic.

Set T=Z[Z]/(Z^{d'}+1) embedded via Z=Y^h. Write each coefficient as c_i=Σ_{ℓ=0}^{h−1}Y^ℓ c_{iℓ}(Z); the power coefficient energy adds over these independent blocks. Divisibility of c_i by G_j(Y^h) modulo q is equivalent to divisibility of every c_{iℓ}(Z) by G_j(Z) modulo q. This is simply polynomial coefficient-block division; it follows also from the direct module sum with basis 1,Y,…,Y^{h−1}. In particular if the CRT dormant proportion is g/d, the common dormant proportion in all blocks over T is the same g/d (degree sum g/h in degree d/h). For a tuple with L nonzero blocks, there is one of energy ≤w/L; the norm-in-an-ideal bound applied to this block implies g/d≤(1/2)log_q(w/L). Consequently relation probability is bounded by
    q^(−D)(w/L)^{D/2} = exp[−(D/2)log(q² L/w)].
All inactive tuples have L≤w/2 because any energy-1 block is a unit monomial at every factor. There are B'=Bh block positions and d'=O(√w) coefficients per block; B'd'=Bd.

Exclude universal c∈qS^B. If L=1, c has only one occupied original row and at least one active factor; that random row must vanish there in all t entries. The union probability is at most Bd q^(−tf). The LTE bound gives tf≥2D/(q+1)=Ω(√w) (also for f=1), so this is o(1). More generally, this bound applies to every nonuniversal single-row feasible tuple regardless of L.

For 2≤L≤w^{1/4}, selecting blocks and their coefficient arrays costs exp(o(w)) uniformly: there are binom(B',L) choices, and at most 2^{min(C',w)}binom(C'+w,w) arrays on C'=Ld'=O(w^{3/4}) slots, by the support/energy composition count. The last log is O(C' log(1+w/C')+C')=o(w). Since q²/w→1 and L≥2, relation probability is exp(−Ω(w)), enough after summing.

For w^{1/4}≤L≤w/2, use subsequences with log L/log w→β∈[1/4,1], log B'/log w→b'≥2 finite and log d'/log w→a'∈[0,1/2]. Along such a subsequence D/(2w)→b'+a'−1 by criticality and B'd'=Bd. For β<1 the support-selector log is o(w log w); the log array count divided by w log w is ≤(β+a'−1)_++o(1). The weighted exponent is therefore at most (β+a'−1)_+−(b'+a'−1)β+o(1), strictly negative. (On the positive branch this equals a'−1−(b'+a'−2)β.) For β=1 the selector contributes ≤(b'−1)/2 from L≤w/2, arrays ≤a', so the weighted exponent is at most −(b'−1)/2+o(1). Compactness and the ≤w possible L complete the weighted first moment. This proves no inactive nonuniversal exceptional vector of energy ≤w with probability 1−o(1), including d=Θ(w).

The previous factorial moment theorem gives Pois(λ) for the main shell, and the w+1 shell is nonempty with high probability by its diverging mean and pairwise independence. Thus below w the only possible minima are universal, and at w the random extra is the Poisson shell together with the deterministic universal relation if present. The claimed clipped law follows in all integer placements of q² relative to w, including equality. It does not say the count at q² is itself Poisson before removing the universal part.

## Exact one-row formula and toy checks

For one random input row let J be the set of CRT fields in which all its t entries vanish, independently with rates q^(−t f_j). Define A_J=∏_{j∉J} p_j and ℓ_q(A_J)=min_{u∈A_J\qS}||u||²_coeff (∞ for empty). The exact probability of a nonuniversal one-row relation of energy ≤W is Σ_J 1[ℓ_q(A_J)≤W] Pr(J); over B independent rows the probability of at least one is 1−(1−p)^B. This distinguishes occasional random annihilators from the deterministic ideal qS. It is not necessary to assume this expression is small in every setting; the union bound above proves it in our crossover.

[The exact verifier](verify-finite-module-q-square-crossover.js) tests all residues for (q,d,h)=(3,8,2) and (5,4,1), comparing every factor-vanishing test with all lower-block tests and computing exact shortest ideal representatives outside qS via centered lifts. For q=3,d=8, the factors are (Z²+Z+2)(Z²−Z+2) with Z=Y². The two proper ideals each have ℓ_q=3, while full ideal S has ℓ_q=1 and qS has ℓ_q=∞; random row zero-component counts are 6400,80,80,1 out of 6561. For t=1, the exact one-row probability changes from 1/6561 at W≤2 (W≥1) to 161/6561 at W=3; universal shortest energy stays q²=9. At q=5,d=4, proper ideal minima are 5, zero masks have counts 576,24,24,1, and at W=5 the one-row probability is 49/625; deterministic universal energy 25. These are nearby formal checks of CRT and ideals, not asymptotic numerical evidence.
