# Primary sources and boundaries

1. **Eight-symbol question.** Amos Beimel, Aner Ben-Efraim, Oriol Farràs, Adriana Moya, *Ideal Secret Sharing Schemes over Small Domains*, [ePrint 2026/1794](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1794), [fixed September 7 PDF](https://opencryptology.org/2026/1794/versions/20260907T011348Z.pdf), §1.4 (discussion immediately following the related-work paragraph for k=5) and §2.2 Definition 2.5 (matroid ports). They prove the F₄ and size-six classifications and explicitly distinguish the unresolved existence of an 8-ideal access structure without an ideal F₈-linear realization. Our result is affirmative scalar representability for one **restricted binary three-fold rank-three class**. It neither supposes arbitrary 8-entropic matroids are three-fold nor settles the general eight-symbol question.

2. **Partial-spread input, already known.** Ron Shaw, *Double-fives and partial spreads in PG(5, 2)*, in *Geometry, Combinatorial Designs and Related Structures*, Cambridge University Press, 1997, pp. 201–216, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526114.018), [author text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266710886_Double-fives_and_partial_spreads_in_PG52). §4.1, Lemmas 4.1–4.2 and the classification following displays (13)–(16) already give the common field spread versus maximal five partial spread dichotomy, including the seven noncommuting choices for the fifth direction once four have been normalized. §2 of our proof supplies an elementary linearized-map derivation and does **not** claim the spread classification as new. The additional conclusion is the extension from a line to arbitrary rank-three point incidences and its same-matroid port corollary. The closely related uniqueness of full order-eight translation planes is also prior; see Corollary 2.5 and reference [32] of [Monzillo–Penttila–Siciliano, *Classifying pseudo-ovals, translation generalized quadrangles, and elation Laguerre planes of small order*](https://airus.unisalento.it/retrieve/323dc5b0-1eab-4fd2-90e6-fcc87bd683c8/DCC2024Monzillo.pdf). That full-spread result alone does not handle the maximal five-set branch.

3. **MDS/arc results not this incidence claim.** Simeon Ball, Guillermo Gamboa, Michel Lavrauw, *On additive MDS codes over small fields*, [arXiv:2012.06183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06183), classify additive MDS codes at small alphabets including eight. Janne I. Kokkala and Patric R. J. Östergård, [arXiv:1504.06982](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06982), classify the relevant seven- and eight-symbol MDS codes with distance at least three. Their uniform rank-three configurations require every triple to be independent; the present statement instead requires a rank-two flat with at least five distinct points. The proof uses neither their search nor pseudo-oval classification, and does not assert a new MDS bound.

4. **Known surrounding classifications.** Emmanuel Abbe and Sophie Spirkl, [*Entropic matroids and their representation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12175), give the matroid/entropy and almost-affine context. Mohammad Hossein Kalantari and Shahram Khazaei, [*Five-Entropic Matroids Are Quinary*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11970), settle the five-symbol case after the original paper; it is not claimed as open here. Analytic field and matroid statements in this note have separate hypotheses.

5. **Bounded duplicate check.** Queries on binary three-fold rank-three matroids, long lines, octary ideal secret sharing, partial plane spreads in PG(5,2), and extension classifications were compared with the primary sources above. The complete/partial spread input is known. We did not find the exact same-matroid long-line extension in the reviewed sources. The unified repository at `f3a70752f88b1d91e2415d7e65809afad5bef15f` contains partial-spread notes for Gorodilova and Dempwolff, concerning parity in binary two-spreads and semibent partial-spread extension respectively; neither gives this rank-three binary three-fold port statement. This is a bounded search, not an absolute priority claim.
