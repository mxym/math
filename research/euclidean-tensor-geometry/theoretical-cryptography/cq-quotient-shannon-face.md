# Classically Gaussian fluctuations on a quantum conditional quotient entropy face

A finite-module quotient may have noncommuting conditional log-density operators because the side information is quantum. Nevertheless all quotients that simultaneously attain a feasible Shannon-type bound have a classical joint Gaussian fluctuation. Their operator commutators need not vanish, even in operator norm after averaging many copies; their state-weighted commutators do vanish. This describes the input-side statistics, not an operational second-order privacy-amplification theorem.

## 1. Setup

Let \(M\) be a fixed finite left module (only its additive group and submodule lattice will be used). More generally one may take a finite abelian group and a collection of subgroups closed under intersections and sums. Let \(X\in M\) be a classical register with a finite-dimensional quantum system \(E\), and write its state as
\(\omega=\sum_x |x\rangle\langle x|\otimes R_x\), where \(R_x\succeq0\), \(\sum_x\operatorname{Tr}R_x=1\), and \(R=\sum_x R_x\). For a submodule \(J\), define \(U_J=X+J\), \(R^J_u=\sum_{x+J=u}R_x\), and the pullback conditional surprise operator
\[
 S_J=\bigoplus_x\big(\log R-\log R^J_{x+J}\big),\qquad
 H_J=\operatorname{Tr}\omega S_J=H(U_J\mid E).
 \tag{1}
\]
Logs are natural and extended by zero on their own kernels. These extensions leave the conditional entropy unchanged; no full-rank hypothesis is made for a fixed source. In particular \(S_M=0\).

For \(\beta\ge0\) such that
\[
        e_J:=H_J-\beta\log|M/J|\ge0\quad\text{for all }J ,
 \tag{2}
\]
call a quotient active if \(e_J=0\). For \(n\) independent copies, set
\[
 B_{J,n}=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n B_J^{(i)},\qquad B_J=S_J-H_J I,\qquad
 \Sigma_{J,K}=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\omega B_J B_K).
 \tag{3}
\]
This is a feasible entropy face, defined by (2), not a claim about the available rates of any particular hash family.

**Result.** The set of active submodules is closed under meet and join. On this set \((B_{J,n})_J\) converges to a centered **classical** Gaussian vector with covariance \(\Sigma\), in the sense that expectations of ordered products of unitaries generated by these observables, and fixed ordered sequential spectral threshold tests at continuity thresholds, converge to the corresponding classical expectations. Indices can be repeated and ordered arbitrarily. On the active lattice the limit obeys, almost surely,
\[
       G_J+G_K=G_{J\cap K}+G_{J+K}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The commutator of each active pair tends to zero in the state-dependent right-GNS norm, at rate \(O(n^{-1/2})\). But the operator-norm commutator can stay strictly positive at every \(n\). There is a two-binary-label, one-qubit example with two noncommuting nontrivial active quotients and nonsingular joint covariance (§4).

A stable version applies when the state and \(\beta\) may vary with \(n\) on the same finite group and quantum dimension, (2) holds in each row, the selected quotients' deficits tend to zero, their covariance matrix converges, and the maximum of their centered log-operator norms, together with 1, is \(L_n=o(n^{1/6})\). Under these conditions the same classical Gaussian limit holds for those selected quotients. The finite meet/join closure of those quotients is also asymptotically active. Every such meet/join relation remains asymptotically exact in the state norm, and in the Gaussian limit if all four coordinates are included.

## 2. Why the quotient face has zero symplectic form

For \(J,K\) write \(U=X+J\), \(V=X+K\), \(W=X+(J+K)\), and \(T=X+(J\cap K)\). The pair \((U,V)\) is in bijection with \(T\) on its image: equality of both labels is exactly equality modulo the intersection. Moreover \(W\) is a deterministic function of both \(U\) and \(V\). Thus the conditional mutual information identity is
\[
 H_J+H_K-H_{J\cap K}-H_{J+K}=I(U:V\mid WE).
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(H(U\mid WE)=H(U\mid E)-H(W\mid E)\), and the same for \(V\) and the joint label, by the chain rule. The additive-group identity \(|J+K||J\cap K|=|J||K|\) follows by counting fibers of \((j,k)\mapsto j+k\). Hence the log quotient cardinalities in (2) cancel from (5). Strong subadditivity and feasibility give
\[
 0\le I(U:V\mid WE)\le e_J+e_K,\qquad
 e_{J\cap K}+e_{J+K}=e_J+e_K-I(U:V\mid WE).
 \tag{6}
\]
This proves closure and zero conditional mutual information for active pairs.

For two self-adjoint operators \(A,B\) define their symplectic form in state \(\omega\) as \((2i)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\omega[A,B]\), the imaginary part of their mixed second moment. In the commutator expectation for \(S_J,S_K\), the terms involving \(\log R\) vanish. For instance
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\omega[\log R^J_{U},\log R]
      =\sum_{u}\operatorname{Tr}R^J_u[\log R^J_u,\log R]=0,
\]
by trace cyclicity. The remaining expectation is \(\operatorname{Tr}\omega[\log R^J_U,\log R^K_V]\). It is the usual modular commutator, up to the factor \(i\), for the tripartite state with systems \(U\mid WE\mid V\): adjoining \(W\) to \(U\) or \(V\) duplicates a deterministic label and changes none of their nonzero blocks. Thus [Kim, Shi, Kato and Albert (2022), §II](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.10400v1) already shows that this expectation vanishes when the right-hand side of (5) is zero. More quantitatively, [Yang (2026), Lemma 2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09019v1) bounds its modulus by
\[
 C_{\dim}\sqrt{\eta(1+\log(1/\eta))},\qquad \eta=I(U:V\mid WE),\quad 0<\eta\le1/2,
 \tag{7}
\]
with the constant depending only on the fixed system dimensions. His bound includes nonfaithful states, with the usual zero-on-kernel convention. His Theorem 2.1 also gives the exact equality
\[
       ( S_J+S_K-S_{J\cap K}-S_{J+K})\sqrt{\omega}=0
              \quad\text{when }\eta=0.
 \tag{8}
\]
Indeed the corresponding modular Markov defect for \(U\mid WE\mid V\) has zero right-GNS norm; pulling back along \(X\mapsto(U,V,W)\) preserves that norm, since the expectation of its square is unchanged by collecting equal blocks. The common \(\log R\) cancels from (8). This recovers the support-restricted modular identity, which is also a consequence of [the quantum Markov equality structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304007). More generally put \(D=S_J+S_K-S_{J\cap K}-S_{J+K}\) and note \(\operatorname{Tr}\omega D=\eta\). The same theorem gives \(\|(D-\eta I)\sqrt\omega\|_2\le C_{\dim}\sqrt{\eta(1+\log(1/\eta))}\) in the range of (7): centering can only decrease that norm. Independent tensor copies preserve this centered second moment under the normalization \(n^{-1/2}\sum_i\), so the near-active meet/join relation converges to zero in the state norm even for the triangular version. Repeatedly applying (6) gives near-activeness of the entire finite meet/join closure. These identities do not claim that the operators commute.

For a complementary elementary estimate when the nonzero-probability \(U,V\) blocks are faithful, put \(A=\log R^J_U\), \(D=\log R^K_V\) as lifted operators, \(a=\|A\|_\infty\), \(b=\|D\|_\infty\), ignoring zero-probability blocks. Rotate each \(U,V\) block by \(e^{itA_u}\), writing \(\omega(t)\) for the new state. This keeps \(UE\) fixed, hence also \(WE\); it preserves the entropy of \(U,V,E\) as a blockwise unitary. Consequently its conditional mutual information differs from \(\eta\) only by the change of entropy of \(VE\). The cross-entropy bound \(H(VE)_{\omega(t)}-H(VE)_\omega\le -\operatorname{Tr}[(\omega(t)-\omega)D]\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}[(\omega(t)-\omega)D]\le\eta\quad(t\in\mathbb R).
 \tag{9}
\]
Here the entropy upper bound is just nonnegativity of relative entropy, with the support condition ensured by faithfulness in every used \(V\) block. The Taylor expansion of conjugation yields
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Tr}[(\omega(t)-\omega)D]
                   -it\operatorname{Tr}\omega[D,A]\right|\le 2a^2b\,t^2 .
\]
Indeed the second derivative of \(e^{-itA}De^{itA}\) has norm at most \(4a^2b\). Optimizing either sign of \(t\) in (9), and then interchanging \(U,V\), shows
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Tr}\omega[S_J,S_K]\right|
       \le2\sqrt{2\,\eta\,\min(a^2b,b^2a)} .
 \tag{10}
\]
Zero values of \(a\) or \(b\) mean the commutator is zero. This fixed-norm estimate follows from the modular entropy-drift method; (7) is useful when eigenvalue bounds are unavailable. The contribution here is applying the modular commutator to the whole feasible quotient lattice via (5)–(6), not the general Markov no-go or the general spectral-free bound.

## 3. Classical joint limit and what "commuting" means

For a fixed ordered list \(J_1,\ldots,J_s\) and real \(t_\ell\), tensor factorization gives the exact identity
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\omega^{\otimes n}\prod_{\ell=1}^s e^{it_\ell B_{J_\ell,n}}
      =
 \left(\operatorname{Tr}\omega
               \prod_{\ell=1}^s e^{it_\ell B_{J_\ell}/\sqrt n}\right)^n .
 \tag{11}
\]
Even operators on a single copy need not commute; operators on different copies do. Centering cancels the linear term. The quadratic term in the bracket is
\[
 -\frac1{2n}\sum_{\ell} t_\ell^2\operatorname{Tr}\omega B_{J_\ell}^2
 -\frac1n\sum_{\ell<j}t_\ell t_j\operatorname{Tr}\omega B_{J_\ell} B_{J_j}.
 \tag{12}
\]
The remainder is \(O(n^{-3/2}L_n^3)\) when \(s,t_\ell\) are fixed and the indicated norm bound holds, by Taylor expansion of finitely many bounded operator exponentials. For active indices, (6)–(7) make all the terms in (12) real. In the triangular version their imaginary parts are \(o(1/n)\); their real parts converge by the assumed covariance convergence. Since \(\Sigma\) is positive semidefinite (it is the real Gram matrix of \(B_J\sqrt\omega\)), the \(n\)-th power converges to the ordered-characteristic expression \(\exp(-\sum_{\ell,j}t_\ell t_j\Sigma_{J_\ell,J_j}/2)\). This is independent of the order; it is the classical joint Gaussian expression. For a fixed law this is the classical-symplectic-zero specialization of [Jakšić, Pautrat and Pillet, Theorem 4.1](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/mp_arc/c/09/09-187.pdf); (11)–(12) also give the row-varying form used here. Equation (8) and the exact entropy relation from (5) give variance zero for \(G_J+G_K-G_{J\cap K}-G_{J+K}\), proving (4).

Here is a verification for sequential spectral tests that also applies to the triangular version. A tested projector \(P_\ell\) is \(\mathbf 1_{\{B_{J_\ell,n}\le c_\ell\}}\) or its complement. Require that the limiting Gaussian marginal gives zero mass to \(c_\ell\) (this includes zero variance when \(c_\ell\ne0\)). The probability of the given sequence of outcomes is
\[
       \|P_s\cdots P_1\sqrt{\omega^{\otimes n}}\|_2^2 .
\]
For fixed \(R_0\) and \(\delta>0\), replace each \(P_\ell\) by a smooth compactly supported contraction \(F_\ell=f_\ell(B_{J_\ell,n})\) agreeing with that threshold away from its \(\delta\)-neighborhood on \([-R_0,R_0]\). Choose a smooth bounded function \(h_\ell\ge|\mathbf 1-f_\ell|^2\), where \(\mathbf 1\) here is the tested event function, such that \(h_\ell\) vanishes on \([-R_0+1,R_0-1]\) outside a \(2\delta\)-neighborhood of the threshold, and equals 1 outside a larger compact interval. Then \(h_\ell\) is constant plus a smooth compactly supported function. Products of the \(f\)'s and \(h\)'s converge in their given order by Fourier inversion, (11)–(12), and dominated convergence: each transform is a finite measure and all ordered exponentials have expectation bounded by 1. Telescoping from the left in the ordered operator product in the state norm bounds the total difference by
\[
 \sum_{\ell=1}^s
 \big\|(P_\ell-F_\ell)F_{\ell-1}\cdots F_1\sqrt{\omega^{\otimes n}}\big\|_2.
\]
The square of each summand is at most the expectation of the ordered product \(F_1\cdots F_{\ell-1}h_\ell(B_{J_\ell,n})F_{\ell-1}\cdots F_1\); its limiting value is bounded by \(\mathbb E h_\ell(G_{J_\ell})\). First let \(R_0\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\to0\); the Gaussian marginal continuity makes every such bound tend to zero. The limiting squared norm of the smooth product converges to the event probability by dominated convergence. This proves the joint sequential threshold statement without treating noncommuting finite-\(n\) projectors as an actual joint spectral measure.

There is also an exact commutator-norm calculation. For \(C=[B_J,B_K]\) and \(\nu=\operatorname{Tr}\omega C\), the tensor-average identity gives
\[
 \big\|[B_{J,n},B_{K,n}]\sqrt{\omega^{\otimes n}}\big\|_2^2
     =|\nu|^2+\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\omega C^\dagger C)-|\nu|^2}{n}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The \(n\) diagonal tensor terms give the second moment, and all the cross terms factor. With \(\nu=0\) the rate is \(n^{-1/2}\) in norm. In contrast,
\(\|[B_{J,n},B_{K,n}]\|_\infty=\|C\|_\infty\) for every \(n\). The operators \(iC^{(i)}\) are Hermitian and commute across copies, so a tensor power of any maximal absolute-eigenvalue vector attains the norm of their average; the opposite inequality is the triangle inequality. This is an exact distinction between state-weighted and operator-norm commutation.

## 4. A noncommuting critical example with genuine two-dimensional noise

Let \(M=\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2\) as a module over itself, with \(X=(x,y)\) uniform on its four labels. Distinguish \(x,y\in\{0,1\}\) from the qubit Pauli matrices \(P_z,P_x\). For \(a=1/2\) and \(0<b<a\), the conditional density is
\[
  \rho_{xy}=\tfrac12\left(I+a(-1)^x P_z+b(-1)^y P_x\right),\qquad R_{xy}=\rho_{xy}/4.
\]
All these densities are positive definite, and \(R=I/2\). Let \(h(v)\) be the binary entropy in nats of \((1+v)/2\), and put \(r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\). The conditional entropies for the first, second, and joint labels are \(h(a)\), \(h(b)\), and \(\log2+h(r)\), respectively. Choose \(b\) to solve
\[
                 \log2+h(\sqrt{a^2+b^2})=2h(a).
 \tag{14}
\]
This has a unique solution in \((0,a)\); the companion script certifies \(19/40<b<12/25\) by rational series bounds. The left-hand side decreases strictly with \(b\), is larger than \(2h(a)\) at zero, and smaller at \(a\) because \(z\mapsto h(\sqrt z)\) is strictly concave on \([0,1)\). For the latter claim use its convergent power series
\(h(v)=\log2-\sum_{j\ge1}v^{2j}/[(2j)(2j-1)]\); the terms of degrees \(j\ge2\) give strict concavity. At \(\beta=h(a)/\log2\), the first label and the joint label are exactly active, the second is strictly slack since \(b<a\), and the trivial quotient is active with variance zero.

Conjugating by Pauli signs within each fiber reduces the covariance and commutator calculation to \(x=y=0\). Ignoring scalar terms, the two active operators there are \(-\alpha P_z\) and \(-\gamma(a P_z+bP_x)/r\), with \(\alpha=\operatorname{atanh}a\), \(\gamma=\operatorname{atanh}r\). Thus their commutator is nonzero, with norm \(2\alpha\gamma b/r\). The pairwise covariance calculations use the conditional expectations \(a\) and \(r\) of the two unit Pauli directions, and their symmetrized product \(a/r\). The covariance entries are
\[
 \Sigma_{11}=\alpha^2(1-a^2),\quad
 \Sigma_{22}=\gamma^2(1-r^2),\quad
 \Sigma_{12}=\alpha\gamma a(1-r^2)/r.
\]
The determinant equals \(\alpha^2\gamma^2(1-r^2)b^2/r^2>0\), so the Gaussian vector is neither degenerate nor independent. The norm distinction in (13) is nonvacuous even on this tiny exact entropy face.

## 5. Scope

The earlier [finite-module information spectrum note](module-information-spectrum.md) treats an operational hashing problem with **classical side information**. The result here is an input-side structural and joint fluctuation statement, valid for noncommuting side information. It does not identify a joint quantum likelihood-ratio test after hashing, an optimal operational second-order trace-distance profile, or a general simultaneous smoothing theorem. For those separate issues, see [Drescher–Fawzi on simultaneous smoothing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.7642), [Colomer–Winter on multi-user decoupling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12114), and the [single-terminal second-order privacy amplification result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11590). No cryptosystem, deployment, or hardness consequence is asserted. The companion script verifies finite subgroup/coset identities, a rational bracket for the example's entropy equation, Pauli algebra, and the tensor commutator formula; it is not used to assume the entropy or CLT statements.
