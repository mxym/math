# Prior results and scope

1. G. Tenenbaum, [*Normal largest gap between prime factors*](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.1107), J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 31 (2019). Its Erdős normal-order statistic takes logarithms of ratios of logarithms of successive prime factors, for almost all integers. The exceptional-set exponent for a maximum *ordinary additive* support gap bounded by a power of bit length is a different question.
2. J.-M. De Koninck and I. Kátai, [*Consecutive neighbour spacings between the prime divisors of an integer*](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/colloquium-mathematicum/online/114711/consecutive-neighbour-spacings-between-the-prime-divisors-of-an-integer), Colloq. Math. 170 (2022). Their relative log-spacings/typical distribution are related background, not a bound for arbitrary length-specific bounded-degree graphs on primes. Work on ratios of *all divisors* of dense-divisor integers is also distinct.
3. C. Borgs, J. Chayes, J. Kahn and L. Lovász, [*Left and right convergence of graphs with bounded degree*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0115), Lemma 2.1. Exponential-in-size bounds for connected sets at a distinguished vertex in a graph of bounded degree are standard. Our elementary plane-forest majorization is not a new unweighted connected-set enumeration principle. The use here concerns the arithmetic weights, multiple small roots, and two charged large roots.
4. R. C. Baker, G. Harman and J. Pintz, [*The difference between consecutive primes, II*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/83.3.532), Proc. London Math. Soc. 83 (2001), 532–562, Theorem 1. The existence of a prime in every sufficiently large interval [v−v^0.525,v] is the published input in §4. We do not establish a short-interval theorem or claim the exponent 0.525 is optimal. The remaining analytic inputs are the classical prime number theorem, Chebyshev's bound, partial summation and Rankin's weighted-counting inequality.
5. The [earlier repository sparsity bound](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/a46f95e18045533780f74e03d32626e64f18b4e4) treated the bounded-slope graph setting and gave an upper estimate X/(log X)^J for every fixed J. This counting paper does not rely on its algorithmic material: it works for arbitrary graphs of bounded degree without any computational access assumptions. For unit slope the matching additive-gap count does not follow from density zero alone.

Bibliography searches for consecutive-prime-divisor spacings, bounded additive support gaps, and connected supports in graphs of bounded degree returned the nearby results above; this does not certify global publication priority. We make no claim about arbitrary factoring, prime-gap conjectures, deployment, or the leading multiplicative constant in any of the X^(α+o(1)) results.
