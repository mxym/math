# Upper bound and ordinary Koszul lower certificate

For the exact 27-dimensional rational symmetric cubic \(T\) in [Lovitz, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28292v1#S4.SS1), the following bounds hold over \(\mathbb C\):
\[
29\leq \underline R(T)\leq\underline R_{\rm sym}(T)\leq 30 .
\]
The companion [symmetric lower bound](lower.md) determines the symmetric border rank exactly. The Koszul certificate below on its own gives 29; the [ordinary lower bound](ordinary-lower.md) now gives 30.

**Lower certificate.** The companion lovitz-koszul.py builds the exact integer tensor \(T\). In each of the two copies of \(G\), drop the sixth coordinate using its linear dependence; split along \(c\) using the first coordinate (equal to 1 on \(c\)). This is a rational choice of complement allowed by Lovitz. The grid contribution is the sum of the three placements of \(e_\nu\otimes w_\nu\otimes w_\nu\) with weights \(\alpha_r\alpha_s\), plus \(a_1^3+a_2^3+c^3\). Project the first leg to dimension six by the integer matrix specified in the script. The Koszul map \(\Lambda^2\mathbb C^6\otimes(\mathbb C^{27})^*\to\Lambda^3\mathbb C^6\otimes\mathbb C^{27}\) has exact rank 286 modulo 101 (re-run verified), so it has rank at least 286 over \(\mathbb C\). For a simple tensor its rank is at most \(\binom 52=10\); minors give the same upper bound multiplied by the number of summands for limits. Hence \(\underline R(T)\ge29\).

**Upper certificate.** Work in the symmetric algebra on the final 27-dimensional vector space and write a power \(l^3\) for a cube of a linear generator \(l\). In each copy \(i\) embed \(G=\{(u,v):2\sum u+\sum v=0\}\) with the indicated common \(c\). For describing combinations, let \(u_r,v_s\) stand for ambient basis generators before embedding and let \(\alpha=(1,1,-2)\). Let \(A_i=\sum_r\alpha_r u_r\) and \(B_i=\sum_s\alpha_s v_s\); each belongs to \(G_i\), \(A_i+B_i=c\), and \(A_i-B_i=a_i\). Write \(\beta_r=1/3+t\alpha_r,\ \gamma_s=1/3-t\alpha_s/2\), of sum one. Put \(U_i=\sum\beta_r u_r,\ V_i=\sum\gamma_s v_s\), \(w_{rs}=u_r-2v_s\), \(R_{ir}=u_r-2V_i\), \(C_{is}=U_i-2v_s\), and \(M_i=U_i-2V_i\). Each of the last four types lies in \(G_i\); the auxiliary \(U_i,V_i\) separately need not. Expanding the two sides gives
\[
 \sum_{r,s}\beta_r\gamma_s w_{rs}^3-\sum_r\beta_r R_{ir}^3-\sum_s\gamma_s C_{is}^3=-M_i^3 .
\]
One way to see this is that for two independent variables with means \(U_i,-2V_i\) the degree-three terms not involving both centered variables survive, and the two centered means are zero. Interpret signed weights algebraically, so no positivity is required.

Put \(s_1=1,\ s_2=-1\). Use only the fifteen displayed cubes from each copy, with their coefficients multiplied by \(s_i/t^2\). The unperturbed combination tends to \(-3c^2(h_1-h_2)\), since \(M_i=tc+h_i\) with \(h_i\) constant. We next compute freedoms in the limit:
- Shift all three \(R_{ir}\) by the same constant vector \(D_i\). Since \(R_{ir}=tB_i+r_{ir}\), \(\sum\beta_r=1\) and \(t^{-1}\sum\beta_r r_{ir}\to A_i\), the resulting change tends to \(-3s_i(B_i^2+2A_iB_i)D_i\). The quadratic and cubic terms in \(D_i\) vanish because \(\sum\beta_r R_{ir}=O(t)\) and \(\sum\beta_r=1\).
- Shift one such row, in addition, by \(E_i/t\). The limit change is \(-3s_i\alpha_r B_i^2 E_i\). The added constant \(D_i\) has no effect on this leading term. Shift one column by \(F_i/t\); it contributes \((3/2)s_i\alpha_s A_i^2 F_i\). In each case the higher terms vanish.
- In grid position \((r,s)\), the scaled coefficient tends to \(s_i(-\alpha_r\alpha_s/2)\ne0\). Replacing \(w_{rs}\) by \(w_{rs}+\epsilon d_{irs}\) realizes any desired linear-in-\(\epsilon\) coefficient in \(w_{rs}^2\) times an arbitrary vector, plus \(O(\epsilon^2)\) in the limit in \(t\).

The first two freedoms span \(A_i^2,B_i^2,A_iB_i\) times arbitrary vectors. Thus for fixed \(\epsilon\) choose these shifts to cancel the entire constant \(-3c^2(h_1-h_2)\) and leave exactly \(\epsilon(a_1^3+a_2^3+c^3)\). The grid shifts realize \(\epsilon\) times the desired grid adjoins up to \(O(\epsilon^2)\). At every finite nonzero \(t\) there are at most 30 weighted cubes. Their limit for each fixed \(\epsilon\) lies in the closed affine 30th secant cone and equals \(\epsilon T+O(\epsilon^2)\). Scaling by \(1/\epsilon\) and taking \(\epsilon\to0\) stays in the same cone, giving the claimed upper bound. This is a two-stage limiting argument; no claimed convergence along \(t\) with \(\epsilon\) fixed before the indicated cancellation.
