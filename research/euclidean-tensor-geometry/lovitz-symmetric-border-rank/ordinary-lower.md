# Ordinary border rank lower bound

We prove that the polarized tensor of \(F=\sum_i w_i^2 e_i\) from [the grid degeneration](lower.md) has ordinary border rank at least 30 over \(\mathbb C\). This suffices for the original tensor by closedness of the secant cone.

## Fixed pair and its first prolongation

Write \(W=V\oplus Z\). In the three labeled copies of \(W\), the polarized \(F\) is proportional to the sum of the three placements of \(w_i,w_i,e_i\), summed over the 18 labels. If its ordinary border rank were at most 29, [border apolarity, Theorem 3.15 and Corollary 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.01944v2#S4.SS2) would give a nonempty projective variety of apolar ideals in the component of limits of 29 general points of \((\mathbb P W)^3\). The connected solvable group generated by common \(Z\)-scaling and the shears \(e_i^{(s)}\mapsto e_i^{(s)}+\lambda_{i,s}w_i^{(s)}\), with \(\sum_s\lambda_{i,s}=0\), fixes the projective point \([F]\). The shears commute and the torus normalizes them, so the Borel fixed-point theorem gives a fixed ideal in this projective variety.

Its inverse system in degree \((1,1,0)\) has dimension 29. Write it as \(A\oplus B\oplus C\) by total \(Z\)-degree, and put \(K=\langle w_i\otimes w_i\rangle\), \(D_0=\{\sum_i w_i(x)w_i\otimes e_i:x\in V^*\}\). Then \(A\) contains \(K\) of dimension 18, and \(B\) contains the 9-dimensional \(D=\{(d,d):d\in D_0\}\) where we identify the two mixed orientations by transposition. Every nonzero \(d\in D_0\) has rank at least 3, as proved in [the grid calculation](lower.md).

On this pair, one-sided shears on either factor are available by compensating in the third factor. If \(C\ne0\), take a nonzero member and an active row and column. Shearing those labels separately gives two nonzero mixed tensors, one in each orientation, each of rank one. Their classes are independent modulo \(D\): any combination lying in \(D\) has both component ranks at most one, hence is zero. There would be at least three extra dimensions beyond \(K\oplus D\), counting \(C\), a contradiction. Thus \(C=0\). Set \(t=\dim A-18\), \(b=\dim B-9\), so \(t+b=2\). When \(t=0\), one-sided shearing any mixed column makes it a rank-one tensor with one factor \(w_i\) inside the symmetric space \(K\). Thus each mixed column in either orientation is parallel to its \(w_i\).

The degree \((2,1,0)\) inverse system is a 29-dimensional subspace of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 W\otimes W\) whose polars in the repeated first slot land in \(A\oplus B\). We bound the dimension of the entire space of such polars separately by total \(Z\)-degree \(k\), writing \(h_k\) for the dimensions.

## Pure part

Put \(X=V^*\) and \(J_{\mathrm{pair}}=K^\perp\subset X\otimes X\). The annihilator \(A^\perp\) is a codimension-\(t\) subspace of \(J_{\mathrm{pair}}\). Write \(\ell:J_{\mathrm{pair}}\to U=J_{\mathrm{pair}}/A^\perp\). In \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\otimes X\), multiplication \(X\otimes J_{\mathrm{pair}}\to\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\otimes X\) symmetrizes the repeated first slot. Its image has codimension 18: a tensor in its annihilator is symmetric in the first two slots and all its first-slot polars are in \(K\), so it is symmetric in all three, and [the exact grid calculation](lower.md) gives dimension 18. If \(S\) is the image under \(\mathrm{id}\otimes\ell\) of the kernel of this multiplication, then \(h_0=18+9t-\dim S\).

When \(A\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2 V\), the same argument identifies this prolongation with the symmetric one, so the [grid syzygy bounds](lower.md#pure-v-prolongations-via-syzygies) give \(h_0\le18,23,27\) for \(t=0,1,2\). For nonsymmetric \(A\), let \(a>0\) be the rank of \(\ell\) on \(\Lambda^2 X\). Skew Jacobi syzygies give in \(S\) the image of
\[
x\wedge y\wedge z\ \longmapsto\
x\otimes\ell(y\wedge z)+y\otimes\ell(z\wedge x)+z\otimes\ell(x\wedge y).
\]
A nonzero scalar two-form \(\omega\) obtained by projecting \(\ell\) gives rank at least seven: the dual map is \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda\wedge\omega\), whose kernel has dimension at most two, and vanishes if \(\omega\) has rank at least four. Indeed \(\lambda\wedge\omega=0\) for nonzero \(\lambda\) forces \(\omega\) divisible by \(\lambda\), and a nonzero decomposable two-form has wedge kernel exactly its two-dimensional support. It follows that \(h_0\le20\) when \(t=1,a=1\).

If \(t=2,a=2\) and some form in the resulting two-plane has rank at least four, the same projected rank is nine. Otherwise two independent decomposable generators have decomposable sum, so their supports meet in a line. In a suitable dual basis they are \(x_1^*\wedge x_2^*,x_1^*\wedge x_3^*\). The triples \((x_1,x_2,x_j),(x_1,x_3,x_j)\), \(4\le j\le9\), already give 12 independent projected syzygies. Hence \(h_0\le27\). If \(t=2,a=1\), Jacobi syzygies supply seven dimensions in \(X\otimes\ell(\Lambda^2X)\). After quotienting by that line, the scalar map kills \(\Lambda^2 X\), and the symmetric grid syzygies supply at least four more: every symmetric syzygy lifts to a left syzygy after correction by \(X\otimes\Lambda^2 X\), since the latter's partial multiplication image equals the kernel of full symmetrization \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 X\otimes X\to\operatorname{Sym}^3X\). This equality follows by swapping the last two tensor factors before full symmetrization; the differences generate the kernel because the first two factors are already symmetrized. This gives \(h_0\le25\), in particular at most 27.

## Mixed parts

The grade-one components have forms \(P\in (V\otimes Z)\otimes V\) and \(Q\in\operatorname{Sym}^2 V\otimes Z\), according to whether \(Z\) occurs in the repeated or single slot. In the component \(P\), the two \(V\) entries are symmetric when \(A\) is symmetric, by polarizing in the repeated \(Z\) slot. The polar in a first \(V\)-direction gives a pair of mixed orientations in \(B\). Up to the harmless choice of factors in symmetric polarization, write it as
\[
(D_0(Lx),D_0(Lx))+\sum_{j=1}^b \beta_j(x)(R_j,S_j)
\]
where \(D_0(Lx)\) denotes the evaluated element of the core and \(\beta_j\in V\). When all \(\beta_j=0\), symmetry of the \(Q\) columns implies \(L^*w_i\) is proportional to \(w_i\) for every \(i\). The grid rectangle relations and common line force \(L\) to be scalar, yielding at most one dimension.

Suppose \(A\) is symmetric. For \(b=1\), if some column in either orientation is not parallel to \(w_i\), alternation of that column's two \(V\) entries, modulo \(w_i\), puts \(\beta_1\) in a two-plane. This gives dimension at most three. Otherwise columns of both extra orientations are parallel to their \(w_i\); this also always holds for \(b=2\), by the shear observation for \(t=0\). In this diagonal case symmetry forces the resulting column vectors of both polars to equal \(m_i w_i\) and \(n_i w_i\). If \(r_{ij},s_{ij}\) are the extra coefficients along \(w_i\), subtraction gives
\[
(m_i-n_i)w_i=\sum_j(r_{ij}-s_{ij})\beta_j .
\]
For \(b=1\) a nonzero difference matrix places \(\beta_1\) on a line, giving at most two dimensions. For \(b=2\), rank two of the difference matrix constrains two independent combinations of \(\beta_1,\beta_2\) to lines, giving dimension at most three with the scalar kernel. Rank one constrains one parameter to a line, and the kernel of that parameter reduces to a linked one-extra case. Rank zero is the linked case itself. In the linked case the two orientations agree and the problem is the one-orientation diagonal calculation in [the symmetric proof](lower.md#mixed-prolongations), whose bounds are four for one extra and seven for two. Thus \(h_1\le1,4,7\) for symmetric \(A\) and \(b=0,1,2\), respectively.

For arbitrary \(A\), when \(b=0\) the \(Q\) projection alone determines both mixed polars (and hence \(P,Q\)), and the column symmetry of \(Q\) gives \(h_1\le1\). When \(A\) is nonsymmetric and \(b=1\), the \(Q\) projection alone has dimension at most four using the same one-orientation mixed bound, with zero or one extra beyond \(D_0\). Its kernel has \(Q=0\), so the remaining polars land on an orientation axis meeting \(B\) in dimension at most one (because the axis is disjoint from \(D\)). If that intersection is nonzero, \(P\) is then determined by \(\beta\in V\) times a fixed nonzero mixed matrix. Fix a nonzero column \(u\) of this matrix. The \(Z\)-polar constraint in \(A\) requires \(\beta\otimes u\in A\) (up to swapping factors). The skew projection of \(A\) has dimension one since \(t=1\). As \(\beta\mapsto\beta\wedge u\) has one-dimensional kernel, there are at most two dimensions for \(\beta\). Hence \(h_1\le6\).

For grade two, first-slot \(V\)-polars kill the component with one repeated \(Z\) and \(Z\) in the single slot, since \(C=0\). The remaining tensor lies in \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 Z\otimes V\), and its repeated \(Z\)-polars lie on a single orientation axis of \(B\), whose intersection has dimension at most \(b\). The resulting prolongation has dimension at most \(0,1,3\) for \(b=0,1,2\). To see the last bound, denote the mixed polar space by \(E\subset Z\otimes V\) of dimension at most two. If contraction by some \(V^*\)-direction is injective on \(E\), it embeds this prolongation into the symmetric square of an at-most-two-dimensional space. If \(\dim E=2\) and every contraction is noninjective, its two independent matrices viewed as maps from \(V^*\) to \(Z\) have dependent values at every argument. If one map has rank at least two, evaluation at two arguments with independent images and their sums forces proportionality of the maps. Otherwise both are rank one and their image lines must coincide, since their two nonzero linear forms are simultaneously nonzero at some argument. Consequently \(E\subset \mathbb C z\otimes V\), and symmetry forces the prolongation into \(z^2\otimes V\), with allowable \(V\) dimension at most two. Grade three vanishes as its repeated-slot polars would lie in \(C\).

## Conclusion

The possible totals are at most \(18+7+3=28\) for \(t=0\); \(23+4+1=28\) for symmetric \(A\) with \(t=1\); \(20+6+1=27\) for nonsymmetric \(A\) with \(t=1\); and \(27+1=28\) for \(t=2\). Each contradicts the required dimension 29 in degree \((2,1,0)\). This proves the ordinary lower bound 30, and the [30-cube upper bound](upper.md) proves that both border ranks of Lovitz's tensor equal 30.
