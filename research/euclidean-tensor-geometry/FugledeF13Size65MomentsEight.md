# Size 65 in F_13^3: every difference set is five-blocking and moments through degree eight vanish

Use the [preceding size-65 results](FugledeF13Size65MomentsSeven.md): both members have mixed moments through degree seven zero, affine plane cap thirteen, and affine line cap five; neither member is layered. These are still necessary conditions only, not a nonexistence claim.

The [equianharmonic Radon obstruction](FugledeF13Size65NoParallelFiveLines.md) gives that no two five-point affine lines are parallel and every projective line meets each difference set at least four times.

## A low-direction stack of thirteen slices is impossible

Suppose a projective line contains at most four directions from \(D(B)\). Every parallel slice has at most five sites (a six-site slice would be noncollinear by line cap five, and the degree-seven planar checker rules it out with four directions), so all thirteen slices have size five. Each slice is collinear or a five-site cross: the exact triangle-normalized census in [verify-fuglede-f13-size65-four-directions.js](verify-fuglede-f13-size65-four-directions.js) checks all noncollinear possibilities with at most four directions. Since there are no two parallel five-site lines, there are at most four collinear slices, with distinct directions. There is at least one cross, so choose its coordinates to make the four directions \(0,\infty,1,-1\). The common directions then allow precisely two cross orientations, along the axes or along the two diagonals. For example choosing adjacent directions \(0,1\) as the two arms would require the remaining directions to be vertical and \(1/2\), not vertical and \(-1\); symmetries of the fixed harmonic quadruple handle the other adjacent choices.

Put \(Z=X+iY, W=X-iY\) for \(i^2=-1\) in \(\mathbb F_{13}\). Let \(\mu,\nu\) be their slice means as functions of the level \(s\), and use "centered" moments in each slice after subtracting these means. A raw planar moment of degree \(j\) is a function of \(s\) with reduced polynomial degree at most \(4+j\) from global moment vanishing through seven and power-sum orthogonality. In particular \(\mu,\nu\) have degree at most five. At a cross slice, the centered pure second and third moments are zero, so the raw moments are \(5\mu^2,5\mu^3\) and similarly for \(\nu\). At a collinear slice the centered variables satisfy \(W-\nu=\kappa(Z-\mu)\) with \(\kappa\) one of \(1,-1,i,-i\), all distinct across such slices.

The exact checker proves \(\deg\mu,\deg\nu\le2\). Here is its full constraint basis. Translate/shear their affine parts away, enumerate the affine level orbits of the exceptional set of collinear levels and try each reduced candidate mean \(s^2(a+bs+cs^2+ds^3)\). On all cross levels the raw pure second/third moments must interpolate degree-six/seven polynomials, determined by the first seven/eight cross levels and checked on the rest. For at most three exceptional levels these scalar tests already force degree at most two. For four exceptional levels check pairs of mean candidates and the 24 possible distinct assignments of the slopes \(\kappa\) at those levels. The centered pure second/third moments must be related by \(\kappa^2,\kappa^3\), respectively. Only degree ≤2 in both components survives, with \(24+288+288+3456=4056\) pairs with assigned slopes in each of the seven level orbits.

Now centered pure second profiles have degree ≤6 and vanish on at least nine cross levels, hence are identically zero. Then centered pure third profiles are raw cubic minus \(5\mu^3\) or \(5\nu^3\), have degree ≤7 and likewise vanish identically. Consequently all centered third moments vanish (at crosses by symmetry and at collinear levels by proportionality). Let \(t(s)\) be the centered mixed second moment \(ZW\), with degree ≤6; if either mean is quadratic, the raw mixed cubic \(5\mu^2\nu+2\mu t\) or \(5\mu\nu^2+2\nu t\) has degree ≤7, forcing \(\deg t\le5\). It follows that the centered mixed quartics
\[
 U(s)=\sum (Z-\mu)^3(W-\nu),\qquad U'(s)=\sum (Z-\mu)(W-\nu)^3
\]
have degree ≤9: for example \(U=\operatorname{raw}(Z^3 W)-5\mu^3\nu-3\mu^2t\), with all degrees ≤9 given the bounds. Both vanish at every cross level, by cancellation between the two arms. At a collinear level \(U\ne0\): otherwise the 5 distinct centered \(Z\)-values would have moments 1 through 4 zero, hence their monic polynomial would be \(X^5-c\) by Newton’s identities, impossible since fifth power is injective on \(\mathbb F_{13}\). If there are one to three collinear slices, at least ten zeros force \(U=0\) identically. If there are four, \(U,U'\) are both nonzero multiples of the same monic polynomial with the nine good roots, so their ratio on bad slices is constant, whereas \(U'/U=\kappa^2\) takes both \(1\) and \(-1\). All collinear cases fail.

## Closing the all-cross case

For the all-cross case, let \(P\) be the pure fourth centered \(Z\)-moment. The raw pure fourth, fifth and sixth profiles are \(5\mu^4+P\), \(5\mu^5+5\mu P\), \(5\mu^6+15\mu^2 P\), respectively (centered pure second, third, fifth and sixth vanish). If \(\mu\) were quadratic, the degree-eight/nine bounds on raw4/raw5 force the degree-eight leading coefficient of \(P\) to be the negative fourth power of that of \(\mu\). The raw sixth then has a nonzero term in degree twelve, contradicting its degree-ten bound. Likewise \(\nu\) is affine. Remove the means by a common affine shear. Now let \(t=\sum ZW\); the mixed fourth and sixth centered profiles \(t^2/4,t^3/16\) have degrees ≤8,10 and force \(\deg t\le3\) as \(\deg t\le6\) originally and no wrap occurs. Also \(t\ne0\) on all levels. Its character determines axis versus diagonal orientation (a factor 2 for the diagonal \(ZW\)-values, and 2 nonsquare), so the pure fourth is \(P=t^8/4\) and the mixed \(Z^5W\) moment is \(tP/4=t^9/16\). The exact checker enumerates all reduced cubics or lower, finding only the 12 nonzero constants satisfy the nowhere-zero condition and degrees \(\deg(t^8)\le8,\deg(t^9)\le10\). Then the common orientation and centered scale are constant, and the centered slices are all the same five-point set. This gives full thirteen-point lines, impossible. Thus the difference sets are both five-blocking.

## Moment-eight deduction

A nonzero homogeneous degree-eight form vanishing on \(D(B)\) has all nonfactor lines with \(5\le n_L=|D(B)\cap L|\le8\); every factor has \(n_L\le10\) by no layers in the partner. Then
\[
 N^2-169N+7320=\sum_L(n_L-5)(n_L-8)\le8\cdot(10-5)(10-8)=80.
\]
The minimum over integer \(N\) is 180. Thus no such form exists. Apply this to the eighth moment of each member, which vanishes on the partner's differences. All mixed moments through degree eight therefore vanish.
