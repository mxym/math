# Kahn 1994 cover conjectures and edge-count-sensitive fractional bounds: limited screen

Screen date: 2026-10-07. This is a finite literature check, not a proof review or a priority claim. It distinguishes primary text read from abstract/metadata checks and from search non-hits.

## Kahn 1994, Section 5

Primary source read: Jeff Kahn, “On a Problem of Erdős and Lovász. II: n(r)=O(r),” *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* 7(1) (1994), 125–143, DOI [10.1090/S0894-0347-1994-1224593-5](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-1994-1224593-5). Full text was read from the AMS PDF; a page-extracted local copy is `/tmp/kahn-jina-pdf`.

Kahn’s Conjecture 5.5 (pp. 140–141) concerns an r-regular auxiliary hypergraph on at most c r vertices, with c fixed, every pair of distinct vertices lying in some common edge, and maximum triple codegree o(r). It predicts an edge-cover number at most (c/(c+1)+o(1))r. The conjecture is presented as relaxing the pair-codegree condition in Corollary 5.3 to a triple-codegree condition.

Conjecture 5.6 (p. 142) instead fixes a rank bound k and assumes a fractional tiling t on a k-bounded hypergraph. It defines b(t) by requiring every restriction t|X, for |X|≤b(t), to lie in MP(X), the matching polytope of the hypergraph whose edges are all subsets of X of size at least two. Thus the local coordinates include subsets of size three and larger; this is not in general the matching polytope of the complete graph on X. Only when all coordinates of size at least three vanish does it reduce to the graph matching polytope. The conjecture says the integral edge-cover number is asymptotically at most t(H) when the maximum triple weight α3(t) tends to zero and b(t) tends to infinity.

## Direct follow-up: Kayll’s theorem proves Conjecture 5.6

The strongest directly matched source found is P. Mark Kayll, *Asymptotically Good Covers in Hypergraphs: Extended Abstract of the Dissertation*, DIMACS Technical Report 95-55 (December 1995), [public PostScript gzip](https://archive.dimacs.rutgers.edu/archive/TechnicalReports/TechReports/1995/95-55.ps.gz). I downloaded and read the 11-page report; local extracted files are `/tmp/kayll95-55.ps.gz`, `/tmp/kayll95-55.pdf`, and `/tmp/kayll95-55.txt`.

Kayll’s Theorem 2.1 (p. 5) states: for fixed k≥2, if H is k-bounded and t is a fractional cover, then ρ(H)≤(1+o(1))t(H) as α3(t)→0 and b(t)→∞. The report explicitly identifies this as the main theorem of Kayll’s October 1994 Rutgers Ph.D. dissertation and says its proof will appear in reference [20], “J. Kahn and P. M. Kayll, Fractional v. integral covers in hypergraphs of bounded edge size” (then manuscript, 1995). The matching journal publication is Jeff Kahn and P. Mark Kayll, *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* 78(2) (1997), 199–235, DOI [10.1006/jcta.1997.2761](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1997.2761), verified against Crossref.

Thus Conjecture 5.6 is proved by the dissertation theorem, in a stronger form that allows a fractional cover rather than requiring a fractional tiling. This theorem is also the main bounded-rank fractional-to-integral rounding result relevant to the question. The DIMACS extended abstract gives the theorem and proof strategy; I have not accessed the full 1997 journal text, so detailed theorem-number or wording comparisons with that final version remain unchecked.

This does **not** by itself establish Conjecture 5.5. Kahn says on p. 142 that Conjecture 5.6 might imply 5.5, but that he does not see the implication; he also notes that b(t) holds in many natural cases that appear difficult for 5.5. The stated Kayll theorem has the extra b(t)→∞ hypothesis and a fixed edge-size bound. A finite scan of the Kahn paper’s citing records and the accessible Kayll abstract surfaced no later source directly proving the full 5.5 statement or giving a counterexample. This is a search-limited status, not a claim that 5.5 remains open.

## Edge-count-sensitive bounds for intersecting uniform hypergraphs

The closest explicitly edge-count-sensitive result found in primary text is still Kahn 1994, Corollary 5.4 (p. 140): if H is an intersecting r-uniform hypergraph with at most c r edges, c fixed, and maximum pairwise edge intersection o(r), then the **integral** cover number satisfies τ(H)≤(c/(c+1)+o(1))r. The same upper bound follows for the fractional cover number from τ*(H)≤τ(H), under those same small-pair-intersection assumptions. It is not an unconditional fractional bound in terms of m/r.

Füredi’s “Maximum degree and fractional matchings in uniform hypergraphs,” *Combinatorica* 1(2) (1981), 155–162, DOI [10.1007/BF02579271](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579271), was checked at the Springer abstract level. For an intersecting r-uniform family, it gives a maximum-degree alternative: either the family is a projective plane of order r−1, with maximum degree m/(r−1+1/r), or maximum degree is at least m/(r−1). This is a degree-versus-edge-count statement, not the piecewise fractional-cover envelope in m/r.

Füredi, Kahn, and Seymour, “On the fractional matching polytope of a hypergraph,” *Combinatorica* 13(2) (1993), 167–180, DOI [10.1007/BF01303202](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01303202), was checked at the Springer abstract level. The abstract says their weighted matching conjecture holds when the hypergraph is uniform or intersecting (and also when the weight function is constant). This is the source of the familiar rank-dependent, edge-count-independent fractional matching bound for intersecting uniform hypergraphs; the abstract gives no m/r piecewise curve.

Füredi’s fractional matching results and projective-plane examples explain the universal endpoint scale r−1+1/r and a classical design extremizer. Kahn’s conditional bound explains c/(c+1) at a fixed edge/vertex ratio under low pairwise intersections. In the primary text and abstracts checked here, I found no theorem stating the unconditional chord envelope h(c) with integer values c/(c+1), nor the newer nonlinear fractional envelope φ(c). This is a limited non-hit, not evidence of nonexistence. Standard Steiner 2-design duals are relevant equality examples for the new manuscript’s finite statement, but their existence/classification is not itself a prior theorem giving its general inequality.

## Additional screen: fixed-ratio fractional-deficit stability

The candidate under comparison fixes k and considers an intersecting r-uniform H with m=(k−1)r+1 and fractional deficit d=m/k−τ*(H). It proposes deleting at most 4k²(k+1)d edges to obtain a core with maximum degree at most k and controlled aggregate intersection excess. Its asymptotic version has m/r→a for integer a, τ*/r→a/(a+1), and deletes o(r) edges to leave a near-Steiner core. This statement is recorded as a candidate supplied for comparison; no proof review is performed here.

The closest exact structural precedent is the equality case of the universal finite envelope in the repository’s `fractional-intersecting-cover-envelope/paper.md`: at m=(k−1)r+1, equality τ*=m/k holds exactly for a linear H with every active vertex of degree k, equivalently the incidence dual of a Steiner 2-(m,k,1) design with replication r. This gives the exact extremal objects but not a quantitative deletion statement for positive deficit.

The classical projective-plane extremizer is at a different scale. Füredi’s 1981 paper and Füredi–Kahn–Seymour’s 1993 fractional-matching-polytope paper concern the global fractional-matching bound for intersecting uniform hypergraphs; projective planes give the exceptional equality case at m=r²−r+1=Θ(r²). The candidate instead has m=(k−1)r+1=Θ(r) for fixed k. The maximum-degree dichotomy in the 1981 Springer abstract is not a deletion-distance theorem for this fixed-ratio regime. No quantitative stability result converting small fractional deficit into deletion of O_k(d) edges was found in the limited searches for fractional-matching stability, near-Steiner systems, and near-projective-plane extremizers.

Kahn’s 1994 Corollary 5.4 and Theorem 5.2 are relevant after a core has been constructed, but do not supply this stability step: Corollary 5.4 assumes small maximum pairwise intersections in the original intersecting uniform family, while Theorem 5.2 is a fixed-rank weighted cover-rounding theorem with a small weighted pair-codegree condition. Neither states the candidate’s deletion bound. Existing existence theorems for Steiner designs likewise concern exact designs, not a stability conclusion from fractional deficit. These are limited comparisons; non-hits do not establish novelty or priority.
