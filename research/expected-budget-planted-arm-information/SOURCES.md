# Question, neighboring results, and search record

**Original question.** Yuzhou Gu, Yanjun Han and Jian Qian, [*Evolution of Information in Interactive Decision Making: A Case Study for Multi-Armed Bandits*, NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/71cdf0b117bb85329523fdd26ff195a7-Paper-Conference.pdf), also [arXiv:2503.00273v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00273v2). Their (1) specifies independent Bernoulli observations with one uniform exceptional location. Their (2) takes the supremum over adaptive policies and includes both actions and rewards in the history. Their Theorem 1.1 studies fixed horizons in the gap regime bounded away from 1. In §4.3, Proposition 4.4 allows an expected-budget stopping time: for t≤n/Δ² the authors determine the order of optimal identification probability and give an information lower bound of order tΔ² log(n)/n. Appendix C.4 obtains the latter by probabilistically running a longer informative experiment or taking no samples. Appendix C.5 conjectures a matching information **upper bound**, and explains why the original deterministic-horizon dummy-law proof fails for some valid policies. The present note proves this upper bound; the sharp constant at nonzero noise is not determined. It is not a new fixed-horizon theorem, success-probability theorem or lower bound in the original Bernoulli regime.

**Prior ingredients.** Gu–Han–Qian Theorem 3.1(a) already controls fixed-horizon information via the KL-to-Hellinger comparison, Hellinger convexity and an all-low reference. The density-ratio inequality is [Yang and Barron, *An asymptotic property of model selection criteria* (1998), Lemma 4](https://www.stat.yale.edu/~arb4/publications_files/AnAsymptoticPropertyOfModelSelectionCriteriaIEEEIT.pdf). Stopped adaptive experiment change-of-measure with expected arm counts is standard: see [Kaufmann, Cappé and Garivier (2016), Lemma 1 and its proof](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume17/kaufman16a/kaufman16a.pdf). The new application compares **two** admissible hypotheses through their common, two-planted auxiliary experiment; summing the resulting off-diagonal expected counts charges the original prior budget. Alternatively, [the second proof](BARRIER.md) controls the all-low cost until a likelihood barrier and charges only the subsequent information on the exceptional event.

**Arbitrary known observation laws.** The uniform upper uses the one-sided sequential Hellinger chain inequality, *not* a new inequality here. A directly applicable statement is [Foster, Han, Qian and Rakhlin, NeurIPS 2024, Lemma F.2](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/4b6908c6695009dd84846e273d6c2607-Paper-Conference.pdf), with constant 7; their proof uses an earlier cut-and-paste property. [Wu's notes following T.S. Jayram, Theorem 1](https://www.stat.yale.edu/~yw562/teaching/SDS677/chainrule-H2.pdf) give the same type of chain bound with constant 4. [Zhuo and Nagarajan, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24446) likewise apply adaptive Hellinger stability to semi-bandit observations; their objective is regret in monotone stochastic optimization. [A separate 2026 semi-bandit paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17320) records the sequential inequality in Lemma 1.5. The lower bound uses known-law likelihood tests, a geometrically shrinking tournament and the original expected-budget dilution idea. Staged elimination is classical (e.g. [Even-Dar, Mannor and Mansour, Theorem 10](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume7/evendar06a/evendar06a.pdf)); for noisy tournaments see [Feige, Raghavan, Peleg and Upfal](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539791195877). The present counterpart gives the order of the **full stopped-transcript information** in terms of H²(U,V). It does not claim an exact numerical constant, a new tournament design, or an identification guarantee in a more general bandit model.

**Other comparisons.** [Cohen and Zhao (2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1023) study active identification of anomalous processes under known normal and anomalous observation laws. Their performance criterion is expected detection time against an error constraint/asymptotic Bayes risk; that is different from the information about a uniform single location achievable at *every* expected budget. [Mannor and Tsitsiklis (2004)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume5/mannor04b/mannor04b.pdf) establish sample-complexity bounds for exploration and consider Bayesian variants; reliable identification is not the stopped-information objective. [Kaufmann and Koolen (2021)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume22/18-798/18-798.pdf) develop mixture martingales for adaptive sequential tests, not this budget-information curve. For the noiseless appendix, guessing by singletons and ordered sequential search are classical; see [Massey, *Guessing and entropy* (1994)](https://www.isiweb.ee.ethz.ch/archive/massey_pub/pdf/BI633.pdf). Counting guesses until a correct answer is confirmed is different from information in a stopped transcript, which can infer the final unqueried label.
