# A separate likelihood-barrier proof of the converse

**Statement.** Let the secret J be uniform on {1,…,n}, n≥2. Conditional on J, a pull of arm a gives a fresh Bernoulli reward with probability (1+Δ)/2 if a=J and (1−Δ)/2 otherwise. Each successive action and the decision whether to continue can depend on all past actions and rewards and independent private randomness. If the number of pulls τ has expectation at most t under the **uniform planted prior**, then, with natural logarithms, for 0<Δ<1,
    
    I(J;H_τ) ≤ min{log n, 4(8+5 log n) κ t/n},
    κ = Δ log((1+Δ)/(1−Δ)).

Here the entire transcript H_τ includes its length. The expected budget need not hold in the dummy experiment in which all arms give the low reward. A direct noiseless comparison (separate note) strengthens this to
    
    I(J;H_τ) ≤ min{log n, 192 t Δ² log(n)/n},  0<Δ≤1.

Gu–Han–Qian, *Evolution of Information in Interactive Decision Making: A Case Study for Multi-Armed Bandits*, NeurIPS 2025, (1), §4.3, Proposition 4.4 and Appendix C.5, ask for this order of upper bound. Their Proposition 4.4 gives the matching lower bound for t≤n/Δ² under their model, in their noise regime bounded away from the noiseless endpoint. Thus in that regime the optimal mutual information has order min{1,tΔ²/n} log n (use monotonicity for larger t). Their fixed-horizon bounds for information, stopped achievability and stopped success-probability bounds are prior; none are claimed as new. The threshold argument below addresses the obstacle explicitly recorded in Appendix C.5.

## 1. Bounded auxiliary stopping time

Represent all randomization of the algorithm by an independent seed R, revealed to the analyst. Let P_i be the conditional law of the seeded history when J=i; P=n⁻¹∑P_i its planted predictive law, Q its all-low law. In particular R has the same distribution under every law. Let F_k be generated by R and all actions/rewards through min(k,τ), including visible stop decisions, using a cemetery symbol subsequently. Whether a pull k+1 is made, and which arm is to be queried if so, are F_k-measurable. No access to the index J is granted to this filtration. Write Y_V for the full seeded history, including the value of a stopping time V, up through V. All comparisons here can equivalently be stated on the stopped sigma-algebra F_V. The cost is the number of actual pulls, not padded steps.

The likelihoods are positive on any finite horizon for 0<Δ<1. Specifically L_{i,0}=1; each pull of arm i changes L_{i,k} by the high/low likelihood ratio of that reward, and every other pull and cemetery step leave it unchanged. Thus
    
    L_k = (1/n)∑_i L_{i,k} = dP|F_k / dQ|F_k.

Fix an integer m≥1. Put T=τ∧m, θ=1/4, and S=T∧inf{k≥0:L_k≤θ}. The barrier uses no unknown planted index; it is a function of the observed seed and prefix. For all k<m, {S>k} is in F_k and L_k>θ on that event. Hence
    
    E_Q S = ∑_{k<m} Q(S>k) ≤ 4∑_{k<m} P(S>k) = 4 E_P S ≤ 4t.        (1)

Let P_i^S,P^S,Q^S denote the induced laws on Y_S. For bounded stopping times the restriction of each likelihood to F_S is its stopped value: decompose any event by {S=k}. This applies even on paths where the policy or an auxiliary rule decides to stop. In particular, for reverse KL, taking expected negative log-likelihood increments under Q,
    
    K := (1/n)∑_i D(Q^S || P_i^S) = (κ/n) E_Q S ≤ 4κt/n.               (2)

Indeed, on {S≥k} the kth arm is known from F_{k−1}. Conditional expected negative log-likelihood increment under Q is κ for its corresponding planted hypothesis, zero for the n−1 others. This is an exact finite sum and does not use optional stopping at an unbounded time.

## 2. Reverse divergence, information and the lost continuation

Use H²(A,B)=∫(√dA−√dB)², with no half. For A≤nM, let u=√(dA/dM). The normalized KL integrand is f(u)=2u² log u−u²+1 (continuous at zero), with f(1)=f′(1)=0 and f″(u)=4+4 log u≤4+2 log n for 0<u≤√n. Taylor's inequality on either side of 1 gives D(A||M)≤(2+log n)H²(A,M). Apply it to A=P_i^S, M=P^S. By the triangle inequality for H, convexity of H² in the second argument and the elementary H²(A,B)≤D(A||B) (pointwise −log x≥2(1−√x) integrated under A), we have
    
    (1/n)∑ H²(P_i^S,P^S)
       ≤ (2/n)∑ H²(P_i^S,Q^S) + 2H²(Q^S,P^S)
       ≤ (4/n)∑ H²(Q^S,P_i^S) ≤ 4K.

Consequently I(J;Y_S)≤4(2+log n)K. This is the fixed-horizon comparison principle used by Gu–Han–Qian in their proof of Theorem 3.1(a), here applied to an auxiliary stopped transcript, with the density-bounded KL comparison proved above.

Let B={L_S≤1/4}, measurable from Y_S. Because dP^S/dQ^S=L_S, P^S(B)≤Q^S(B)/4. Moreover (1−√L_S)²≥L_S on B. Thus
    
    P(B) ≤ H²(Q^S,P^S) ≤ K.                                             (3)

Y_S is determined by Y_T: compute the first crossing on its prefix, stopping at T if absent. On Bᶜ, Y_T equals Y_S. Conditional on Y_S, continuation can contribute at most log n nats on B, and zero on Bᶜ. It follows by the chain rule, without an extra binary-entropy term (the hit is already visible), that
    
    I(J;Y_T) ≤ I(J;Y_S)+P(B) log n ≤ (8+5 log n)K.                     (4)

Finally σ(Y_{τ∧m}) increases in m: prefix truncation is deterministic, with seed retained. The join generates the full seeded stopped transcript up to P-null sets, since E_Pτ<∞. The coordinates of the posterior of finite J are bounded martingales, converging to the posterior conditional on the join; bounded continuity of entropy gives convergence of the mutual informations. (These are finite-horizon sigma-algebras, not necessarily finite-cardinality when R is nonatomic.) Data processing removes the extra seed. Equations (2)–(4) and I≤log n prove the first bound. If t=0 then τ=0 almost surely under P and the result is immediate. At Δ=0 no information can be obtained.

## 3. Constants and boundary

For 0<Δ≤1/2, log((1+Δ)/(1−Δ))≤(8/3)Δ by integrating 2/(1−x²). For n≥2, ln n≥ln 2>2/3, so 8+5 ln n≤17 ln n. Equations (2)–(4) give the uniform factor 192 (indeed 544/3<192). For Δ≥1/2 the noiseless simulation inequality I≤2(1+log n)t/n from ENDPOINT.md yields a smaller factor (use 1+log n≤(5/2)log n and 1≤4Δ²). This includes Δ=1, where the dummy reverse KL formula cannot be used. We do not need any claim about noisy experiments being Blackwell comparable after conditioning on a hidden, data-dependent random seed: domination is via an explicit simulated transcript with enlarged independent seeds.
