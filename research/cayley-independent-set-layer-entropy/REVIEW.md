# Independent mathematical audit: sparse coefficient loss and typical fixed-density entropy

**Verdict: PASS for both theorems.** The independent audit was performed on the frozen proof versions before editorial adjustments to the surrounding scope text. It is not external peer review. In the sparse claim n^(5/6) ≪ k = o(n); the conclusion is the rate of the *log likelihood* log(i_k/E i_k) in probability, not the rate of an exceptional probability and not a fixed-density formula. At fixed density 0<a<ρ, s_Q is a *typical* or in-probability fixed-layer entropy. In particular, for a>1/2 a labeled path has i_k=0 for all large n and has positive (though very small) probability, so the untruncated expectation of log i_k is −∞. No average-of-log conclusion is valid there. The fixed-density result does not cover the independence endpoint a=ρ.

## Uniform pressure expansion

The real bound on the root error after depth six follows by six applications of |∏(1+uᵢ)⁻¹−∏(1+vᵢ)⁻¹|≤∑|uᵢ−vᵢ|. Boundary odds differ by at most z; summing N₆ over vertices counts each descendant with an ancestor six above exactly once. Every fixed-tree truncation therefore has the same first six Taylor terms. A depth-six tree of maximum offspring M has a complex disk of radius r=1/(8(M+1)): |R|≤2|z| by induction since −M log(1−2r)≤4Mr<log 2. The log toll is analytic there and bounded by 4r. At real z≤r/2 use the Cauchy geometric tail; at z>r/2 bound the toll by 1 and each of its six Taylor terms by const·(z/r)^7. This is a different r per truncated tree, never a uniform complex zero-free disk for the full environment. The sums of the resulting seventh-moment factors have O(n) expectation: root-to-seven marked paths of length ≤6 have a connected union on ≤43 vertices. There are finitely many patterns, including repetitions, and each size-h pattern has O(n^h) possible injections each present with probability h/n^(h−1).

Möbius inversion makes each order-j log coefficient a linear combination of connected induced subtrees of ≤j vertices, since all monomials of log(1+w) use ≤j distinct labels. A fixed subtree count of order h has expectation a constant multiple of (n)_h/n^(h−1) and variance O(n); disjoint copies have independent presence indicators by the forest formula, while positive overlaps have connected acyclic union and O(n) expected joint counts; cyclic unions contribute no positive covariance. Cauchy–Schwarz handles mixed counts. The first two coefficients are deterministic. The remaining finitely many fluctuations of size O_P(√n), the O(1) biases, and the common positive O_P(n) bound on the Taylor remainder imply (4) uniformly on [a/2,2a]. No per-fugacity O_P statement is evaluated at a random fugacity without such a common bound.

The Poisson moment recursion has the correct independent offspring interpretation; multiplication by zʲ in the outer equation improves the order of the simultaneous real-moment approximation by ≥1 each iteration. Independently, independent.py enumerates *all whole* labeled trees of orders 1–7 as connected edge sets and counts subsets directly. For each j≤6 it solves the triangular linear system in n=1,…,j for coefficients b_h in E[zʲ]log Z = ∑ b_h(n)_h/n^(h−1); hence the slope per vertex is ∑b_h. All n up to 7 not used to fit the column are exact holdouts. This is not an enumeration of induced-subtree Möbius coefficients or a simulation. For the annealed expansion Lagrange inversion of z=b(1+b)e^b and a=b/(1+b) gives j²[zʲ]p_A=[u^(j−1)]e^(−ju)(1+u)^(−j−2). Recovered coefficients agree through order five; order six gives −3741/20 vs −934/5, difference exactly +1/4 for annealed minus quenched.

## Saddle without a residual log k term

For q=z/(1+z), each occupation is at most q and at least q(1−d_v q) by conditioning on the rest. Mean density lies in [q(1−2q),q]. The inverse-mean saddle exists when k=o(n) and k>0: every tree has independence number ≥n/2 and its nondegenerate tilted law has positive variance. The bounds imply z_T=a+O(a²) uniformly with deterministic constants. Along a length-d path, integrating off-path branches leaves an inhomogeneous binary Markov chain; an occupied predecessor forces zero, and an absent predecessor gives successor occupation probability ≤q. Thus absolute covariance ≤z^(d+1) for z<1. Expected counts of ordered d-paths are (n)_(d+1)(d+1)/n^d≤n(d+1), including d growing with n. The expected off-diagonal *upper bound* at z=2a is O(na²); Markov gives w.h.p. ≤na simultaneously at every z≤2a. Diagonal sum ≤2na.

Disjoint exact two-vertex fringe trees occur with limiting frequency e^(−2), and no slot contains another. Condition on the entire remaining core: the pendant slots then become independent, even if several have the same parent. Each contributes size zero or one, with odds z or 2z according to the parent's fixed spin. At z_T and on the high-probability environmental variance/slot event, the total conditional mean is within O(√k) of k for a proportion ≥3/4 of cores (total variance inequality). At least one of the two slot types has Ω(n) trials; the other type fluctuates by O(√k) with conditional probability ≥3/4. At the needed integer the large binomial has mean and variance Θ(k), with distance O(√k) from its mean; the integer is within its support for all large k. Uniform Stirling bounds give an atom of order k^(−1/2); for any core the upper bound follows directly from the maximum atom of its larger binomial. No limiting local Gaussian constant was claimed or needed. This supplies −(log k)/2+O_P(1) rather than an error of order log n. The same term appears in Bedrosian's exact expectation by Stirling. The two-sided comparison of minima uses (4) at z_T and z_A; their sixth powers are a⁶+O(a⁷). Therefore the total error O_P(1+√n a³+na⁷) is o_P(na⁶) whenever na⁶→∞ and a→0, however slowly either condition holds. In this range i_k>0 deterministically.

## Fixed density and the leaf test

The repository's earlier pressure P(t) is differentiable for every fixed real t: the derivative of the root toll has integrable bound ∑ q_*^d N_d for q_*<1 on compact t-intervals (E N_d=1); the fixed-error exponential environmental estimate is at fixed t. The tail bounds on P(t) in the companion force P′(−∞)=0 and P′(+∞)=ρ by convexity, using the classical limiting maximum independent fraction. Differentiability gives continuity of the monotone slope; a slope plateau is harmless. Compact subintervals of (0,ρ) have their minimizing t's bounded by taking a strictly smaller and a strictly larger derivative on either side. The Legendre minima are attained and vary continuously in a. The upper bound on one coefficient is immediate. For the lower bound tilt to b=a+d with d fixed small, apply three fixed-t environmental bounds and two conditional Chernoff bounds, and take an atom of weight Ω(1/n) within d/3 of b. It lies ≥k_n eventually; deleting j−k_n vertices from its independent sets costs at most exp(nH(2d)) after the elementary binomial double count. The t error is bounded by const·d, then d is fixed as a function of the requested accuracy. This proves convergence with exponential-in-n tails at each interior target, not a uniform estimate over moving targets or an endpoint result.

Given the planted vertex set, excess degrees are empty-bin occupancies from n−2 independent draws split into m−1 unrestricted and k−1 outside-only draws (degree-weighted split graph tree enumeration). Thus the stated expected empty-bin fractions are exact, including m=1; changing one draw changes the empty-bin *count* by ≤1, because gain and loss have opposite signs. At a≤4/5, use the two-point B with E B=1 and Var B=a³/(1−a); curvature e^(−x)≥e^(−5) on both rates and throughout their convex hull. This proves the strictly positive lower bound on the limiting mean gap, and Taylor near 1 gives e^(−1)a³/2 as its first term. The O(1/n) error in expected fractions is uniform on this range, since m≥n/5. Above 4/5 the planted labels alone give a uniformly positive difference, also at k=n−1. At the midpoint event H the two one-sided McDiarmid errors are exp(−Ω(na⁶)) whenever na⁶→∞. On H and Y>exp(−cna⁶), use E[Y1_H]=Q(H) and then absorb constants from H^c. This is a one-sided, nonsharp bound on both probability and magnitude, vs a sharp *typical log* only at sublinear densities. Along k/n→a∈(0,ρ), strict entropy gap follows by the two convergences. Comparing at the two minimizing fugacities separately gives s_A−s_Q=a⁶/4+O(a⁷): their parameters are a+O(a²) from the deterministic occupation-slope bounds passed to the differentiable limit by secants. No diagonal exchange was used. The independent checker verifies both expected leaf counts and expected layer counts for every admissible k≤n−1, 2≤n≤7.
