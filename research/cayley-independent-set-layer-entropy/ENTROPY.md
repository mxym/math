# The entropy gap at fixed independent-set densities, and a uniform leaf test

**Scope.** This is a typical fixed-density result, not a claim about the untruncated expectation of the logarithm. For $k/n\to a\in(0,\rho)$, where $\rho e^\rho=1$ is the limiting independence ratio of a uniform labeled tree, let $X=i_k(T_n)$. Then $n^{-1}\log X\to s_Q(a)$ in probability, with $\log0=-\infty$, where
$$s_Q(a)=\min_{t\in\mathbb R}\big[P(t)-at\big],\qquad
 P(t)=\mathbb E\log(1+R_{\rm root}(e^t)).$$
Here the odds are from the ordinary unconditioned Poisson(1) Galton–Watson fringe recursion. For each fixed error $\eta>0$ the exceptional probability is $\le e^{-c(a,\eta)n}$ for all sufficiently large $n$ along the given sequence. The annealed entropy from Bedrosian's exact formula is $s_A(a)=-a\log a-(1-2a)\log(1-a)$. Then $s_Q(a)<s_A(a)$ for every $0<a<\rho$ and
$$s_A(a)-s_Q(a)\sim a^6/4\quad (a\downarrow0).$$
The latter statement is a sequential limit ($n\to\infty$ at fixed density first), not a proof of the separate moving sparse-window theorem. No value at $a=\rho$ is asserted.

## From canonical pressure to an exact layer

For each fixed $t$, $P_n(t)=n^{-1}\log\sum_j i_j(T_n)e^{tj}$ converges to $P(t)$ with fixed-error probability $1-e^{-\Omega(n)}$. Also $P$ is finite, convex, and differentiable: see the preceding Cayley modes note, which derives the fringe formula and differentiation by the integrable critical-GW level-size envelope on every compact interval. These are existing inputs, not new results of this argument. The derivative of a differentiable convex function on an open interval is continuous and nondecreasing. For $t<0$, $0\le P(t)\le\log(1+e^t)$ by counting all subsets, so $P'(-\infty)=0$. For $t>0$, $t\alpha(T_n)/n\le P_n(t)\le t\alpha(T_n)/n+\log2$. By the classical $\alpha(T_n)/n\to\rho$, passage to the limit gives $t\rho\le P(t)\le t\rho+\log2$, hence $P'(+\infty)=\rho$. The derivative covers every intermediate density, without any strict-convexity assumption. This also puts all minimizers of $P(t)-bt$ in a common finite interval for $b$ in each fixed compact subset of $(0,\rho)$. The minimum is continuous there by minimization over this compact interval.

For an upper bound at any specified $k_n/n\to a$, fix $t$ such that $P'(t)=a$. The single coefficient obeys $n^{-1}\log X\le P_n(t)-tk_n/n$. This has the stated fixed-error upper tail. For the reverse bound choose a small $d>0$ and set $b=a+d<\rho$, $P'(t_b)=b$. Let $\zeta=d/3$. Choose $h>0$ such that $P(t_b\pm h)-P(t_b)\mp hb\le h\zeta/2$ by differentiability. With probability $1-e^{-c n}$ all three pressures at $t_b,t_b\pm h$ are within $h\zeta/8$ of their limits. Conditional Chernoff bounds then put all but $2e^{-h\zeta n/4}$ of the size distribution tilted by $e^{t_b j}$ into $|j/n-b|\le\zeta$. There is such a $j$ with atom at least $1/(2(n+1))$. Consequently
$$n^{-1}\log i_j(T_n)\ge P_n(t_b)-t_b b-|t_b|\zeta-o(1).$$
For large $n$ every such $j$ is $\ge k_n$ and $0\le j-k_n\le2dn$. Counting pairs of independent sets related by inclusion,
$$i_{k_n}(T_n)\binom{n-k_n}{j-k_n}\ge i_j(T_n)\binom{j}{k_n}.$$
Using the upper entropy bound for $\binom n{j-k_n}$ with $2d<1/2$, the log cost after division by $n$ is at most the binary entropy $H(2d)$. For any target error, first take $d$ small so that $s_Q(b)\to s_Q(a)$, $H(2d)\to0$ and $|t_b|d\to0$ (bounded minimizers), and then take $n$ large. If needed shrink the three pressure tolerances to ensure they also fit the target error. This proves the exact-layer lower tail as stated; it does not just give a sum over a macroscopic interval of sizes. This use of ensemble tilting/convex duality is standard.

## A uniform separation by the number of leaves

Let $Y=X/\mathbb EX$; the size-biased law $Q$ obtained by planting a uniform independent $k$-set has likelihood ratio $Y$ under the original law $P_0$. As recalled and verified in the sparse-count paper, the excess degree $(\deg i-1)_i$ under $P_0$ has the occupancy law from $n-2$ independent unrestricted labels. Under $Q$ and a fixed planted set, it has the counts from $m-1$ such draws plus $k-1$ independent draws restricted to the $m=n-k$ outside labels (Martin–Reiner, Theorem 4). Only this unordered count law is needed. For the graph-invariant leaf count $L$ both moments and tails may be analyzed conditional on a fixed planted set.

Put $A=(1-1/n)^{m-1}$. The two exact expected leaf fractions are
$$\ell_0=(1-1/n)^{n-2},\qquad \ell_1=A\left[a+(1-a)(1-1/m)^{k-1}\right].$$
A leaf is simply an empty bin, including $m=1$ using $0^0=1$ when applicable. Uniformly for $0\le a\le4/5$, $m\ge n/5$ and log expansion gives
$$\ell_0=e^{-1}+O(n^{-1}),\quad
\ell_1=L(a)+O(n^{-1}),\quad
L(a)=e^{-(1-a)}\big[a+(1-a)e^{-a/(1-a)}\big].$$
Indeed $(m-1)\log(1-1/n)=-(1-a)+O(1/n)$ and $(k-1)\log(1-1/m)=-a/(1-a)+O(1/n)$ there. Interpret $L(a)$ as $\mathbb E e^{-B}$ for $B$ taking values $1-a$ and $1-a+a/(1-a)$ with probabilities $a,1-a$. The mean is 1 and variance $a^3/(1-a)$. On this range $0\le B\le5$; the quadratic Taylor lower bound for $e^{-x}$ about 1 thus gives $L(a)-e^{-1}\ge e^{-5}a^3/2$. Moreover the rates are within $O(a)$ of 1 as $a\downarrow0$, so $L(a)-e^{-1}\sim e^{-1}a^3/2$. For $4n/5\le k\le n-1$, the fraction of empty bins inside the planted set alone gives $\ell_1\ge (4/5)(1-1/n)^{n/5}$, bounded above $\ell_0$ by an absolute positive constant eventually. Thus for some universal $c,C>0$, the difference $D_n=\mathbb E_Q L-\mathbb E_{P_0}L$ is at least $c n a^3-C$ throughout $1\le k\le n-1$.

Changing one draw changes the number of empty bins by at most one: a source can become empty and a target become nonempty, which have opposite signs. Each law has $n-2$ independent draws (for $Q$, conditional on the planted set; the distribution is invariant under a relabeling of that set). Consequently when $na^6\to\infty$, bounded differences at the midpoint event $H=\{L\le(\mathbb E_{P_0}L+\mathbb E_QL)/2\}$ gives $P_0(H^c),Q(H)\le e^{-c' n a^6}$. Since $Q(H)=\mathbb E_{P_0}[Y\mathbf1_H]$, decreasing an absolute $c''>0$ gives the simultaneous magnitude/probability bound
$$P_0\{Y>e^{-c'' n a^6}\}\le e^{-c'' n a^6}$$
for all sufficiently large $n$ along each such sequence, including $k=n-1$. This is a one-sided, nonsharp rate, not equality at positive density. Applying it at fixed $0<a<\rho$ proves $s_A(a)-s_Q(a)\ge c'' a^6>0$. It does not imply any sharp moving-window constant by itself.

## Sixth-order coefficient in the limiting entropies

Write $p_Q(z)=P(\log z)$. The separate small-activity coefficient calculation gives $p_A(z)-p_Q(z)=z^6/4+O(z^7)$; it is also a direct consequence of the Poisson moment recursion in §2 of MOBILE.md without needing that note's finite-tree estimates. A minimizer $z_Q$ for $s_Q(a)$ can be taken with $a/(1+Ca)\le z_Q\le a(1+Ca)$ for small $a$. Indeed the deterministic occupation-mean bounds $q(1-2q)\le n^{-1}\mathbb E_{T,z}K\le q$ with $q=z/(1+z)$ pass to the differentiable pressure slope: a convex function's pointwise limits of difference quotients bound every subsequential slope (use fixed secant intervals then shrink). Alternatively, convexity and $P(t)=e^t+O(e^{2t})$ at $-\infty$ yield $P'(t)/e^t\to1$ from multiplicative secants with factor tending to one, which suffices. The first bound at $z=a/2$ and $2a$ brackets a minimizer in that interval; inversion then gives $z_Q=a+O(a^2)$. The annealed minimizer $z_A$ also equals $a+O(a^2)$. Comparing the two minima at $z_Q$ and $z_A$ sandwiches their difference between $p_A(z_A)-p_Q(z_A)$ and $p_A(z_Q)-p_Q(z_Q)$ (in either order as appropriate). More explicitly, $s_Q(a)\le s_A(a)-[p_A(z_A)-p_Q(z_A)]$ and $s_A(a)\le s_Q(a)+[p_A(z_Q)-p_Q(z_Q)]$. Thus $s_A(a)-s_Q(a)=a^6/4+O(a^7)$. This conclusion is not an exchange of the fixed-density limit with sparse finite-$n$ estimates.
