# Exact leading loss above the sparse concentration window

**Scope.** Let $T_n$ be uniform on the labeled trees on $[n]$, $X=i_k(T_n)$ and $a=k/n$. For every deterministic sequence $n^{5/6}\ll k=o(n)$,
$$\frac{\log(X/\mathbb E X)}{k^6/n^5}\ \longrightarrow\ -\tfrac14\qquad\text{in probability}.\tag{1}$$
In particular this covers arbitrarily slow divergence of $k/n^{5/6}$; no logarithmic separation is required. All logarithms are natural. In fact the proof gives for $k\to\infty$, $k=o(n)$,
$$\log(X/\mathbb EX)=-na^6/4+O_{\mathbb P}(1+\sqrt n a^3+na^7).\tag{2}$$
For these sequences $k<n/2$ eventually; every tree then has an independent $k$-set. This is different from the critical lognormal and full supercritical convergence-in-probability-to-zero result in the preceding repository note. We do not assert a matching exceptional probability exponent, an exact decay formula at positive fixed density, or a joint random process limit.

## 1. A finite uniform expansion of the tree pressure

Write $F_T(z)=\log\sum_j i_j(T)z^j$ for its germ at zero (and the real log for real nonnegative $z$). For a rooted tree define the subtree odds recursively by $R_v=z\prod_w(1+R_w)^{-1}$ over the children. Then $F_T(z)=\sum_v\log(1+R_v)$. For $z\ge0$, $0\le R_v\le z$. Truncate each descendant tree at depth 6 with all depth-six vertices made leaves. Relative to any legal boundary odds in $[0,z]$, its root odds change by at most $z^7 N_6(v)$, where $N_d(v)$ counts descendants at distance $d$: at each level the real product has Lipschitz difference at most $z\sum_w|R_w-R'_w|$, and the initial boundary uncertainty is $z$. This also bounds the real log-toll error, and $\sum_vN_6(v)\le n$. For a fixed finite tree the difference is $O_T(z^7)$ at zero, so the true and truncated tolls have the same first six Taylor coefficients.

For each truncated tree let $D_v$ be its maximum offspring number. On the complex disk $|z|\le r=1/[8(D_v+1)]$, all its odds satisfy $|R|\le2|z|$ by induction: $(1-2r)^{-D_v}\le\exp(4rD_v)<2$. Thus the analytic log toll at zero has absolute value at most $4r$ on the circle, and Taylor coefficients of order $h$ have magnitude at most $4r^{1-h}$. For real $0\le z\le r/2$, Cauchy's geometric tail bounds the remainder after degree six by $C(z/r)^7$. For $r/2<z\le1$, its actual log toll is at most 1, and each of the first six terms is at most $4r(z/r)^h\le C(z/r)^7$ since $z/r>1/2$. Consequently with $c_j(T)=[z^j]F_T(z)$,
$$\left|F_T(z)-\sum_{j=1}^6 c_j(T)z^j\right|\le C z^7\big[n+\sum_v(D_v+1)^7\big],\qquad 0\le z\le1.\tag{3}$$

The expectation of the bracket in (3) is $O(n)$ for $T=T_n$ rooted at 1. Indeed $D_v+1$ is at most one plus the size of the unrooted radius-six ball at $v$. Ordered 7-tuples of vertices from such a ball, repetitions allowed, have a connected union of their paths from $v$ of at most 43 vertices with finitely many possible rooted, tuple-marked abstract tree types. For any connected fixed $h$-vertex tree shape $H$, a specified injection of it into $[n]$ has probability $h/n^{h-1}$ of being contained in $T_n$ by the classical Cayley forest-extension formula. This also follows by contracting the specified forest and applying weighted Cayley to its component sizes. Thus the expected number of embeddings of each marked type is $O(n)$. This bounds all terms of the expanded seventh power, including lower powers.

For each fixed $j\le6$, $c_j(T)$ is a fixed linear combination of the counts $N_H(T)$ of unrooted subtrees of shapes $H$ of orders $h\le j$. Here is an elementary justification. For an arbitrary graph $G$ put
$$b_j(G)=\sum_{A\subset V(G)}(-1)^{|V(G)|-|A|}[z^j]\log Z_{G[A]}(z).$$
By Möbius inversion $c_j(G)=\sum_A b_j(G[A])$. Additivity of the log on disconnected graphs makes $b_j$ vanish there. Also the Taylor expansion of the log at zero uses products of independent-set counts in layers of total size $j$; each product enumerates tuples using at most $j$ distinct vertices. Inclusion–exclusion annihilates these products if $|V(G)|>j$. Since a connected induced subgraph of a tree is a subtree, the claim follows. The expected $N_H$ is $(n)_h h/(|\mathrm{Aut}(H)|n^{h-1})$. Its variance is $O_H(n)$: fixed vertex-disjoint copies' indicators are independent by the same forest formula; for overlapping copies, one sums a finite number of connected union types, each either cyclic (joint probability zero) or realized $O(n)$ times in expectation. For a variance upper bound negative covariances can be dropped. Cauchy–Schwarz handles signed finite linear combinations. Hence for deterministic constants $c_j$, $\mathbb E c_j(T_n)=n c_j+O(1)$ and $\operatorname{Var}(c_j(T_n))=O(n)$. In the first two orders there is no fluctuation: $c_1(T_n)=n$ and $c_2(T_n)=-n/2-(n-1)$.

The limiting constants can be computed by the Poisson(1) fringe rule. From the classical conditioned-tree fringe convergence and the bounded log toll, as in the repository's Cayley modes paper, $n^{-1}F_T(z)\to p_Q(z)=\mathbb E\log(1+R)$ in probability and expectation, with $R$ evaluated on an unconditioned finite-a.s. Poisson(1) Galton–Watson tree. Taking expectations in (3) shows $p_Q(z)=\sum_{j\le6}c_jz^j+O(z^7)$ as $z\downarrow0$. No uniform complex zero-free disk for the whole random tree has been used.

## 2. The first noncancelling coefficient

Write $m_j=\mathbb E R^j$. The Poisson offspring generating function gives the exact identities
$$m_j=z^j\exp\{\mathbb E(1+R)^{-j}-1\},\qquad 0\le m_j\le z^j.$$
For any fixed order $N$ use the Taylor polynomial of $(1+R)^{-j}$ through order $N$ (for $j\le N$, the remainder is $O_N(z^{N+1})$ on $0\le R\le z\le1/2$). Coefficient recursion is triangular in *total power of $z$*: the outer factor $z^j$ means every term at order $l$ only needs moment coefficients through $l-j$. The formula can equally be iterated $N+1$ times modulo $z^{N+1}$ starting from zero. To see that this formal procedure gives a real asymptotic expansion, if previous moments approximate the true ones with error $O(z^l)$, truncating inside the exponential as above incurs $O(z^{N+1})$ and its outer factor supplies at least one extra power, uniformly for $1\le j\le N$. Apply $\mathbb E\log(1+R)=\sum_{j\le N}(-1)^{j-1}m_j/j+O_N(z^{N+1})$.

The exact Bedrosian expectation gives the annealed entropy $s_A(a)=-a\log a-(1-2a)\log(1-a)$ on $0<a<1$. Write $p_A(z)=\sup_{0<b<1}(s_A(b)+b\log z)$. The maximizer $a_A(z)$ is unique because its stationarity relation is
$$z=\frac{a_A}{(1-a_A)^2}\exp[a_A/(1-a_A)],$$
strictly increasing from 0 to infinity. Equivalently, putting $b=a_A/(1-a_A)$ gives $z=b(1+b)e^b$, $a_A=b/(1+b)$, and $z p'_A(z)=a_A(z)$ with $p_A(0)=0$. These identities follow by ordinary differentiation; when needed at $b\to1$ in the supremum, $s_A(b)\to-\infty$.

The two expansions have coefficients
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
j&1&2&3&4&5&6\\\hline
[z^j]p_Q&1&-3/2&23/6&-149/12&1843/40&-3741/20\\
[z^j]p_A&1&-3/2&23/6&-149/12&1843/40&-934/5
\end{array}
$$
so $p_A(z)-p_Q(z)=z^6/4+O(z^7)$. The short rational program computes these coefficients from the two recurrences. A second derivation for the quenched row is to sum $h b_j(H)/|\mathrm{Aut}(H)|$ over all unrooted tree types through order $j$, by §1. Combining the first two deterministic coefficients with (3), for every deterministic $a=o(1)$ we have **uniformly** for $a/2\le z\le2a$,
$$F_T(z)=n p_A(z)-n z^6/4+O_{\mathbb P}(1+\sqrt n a^3+na^7).\tag{4}$$
Indeed the finitely many centered coefficients of order at least 3 are $O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt n)$ and the single random remainder bound has $O(n)$ expectation.

## 3. Extracting precisely the $k$-th coefficient without a logarithmic loss

For every tree and real fugacity $z>0$ write $K$ for hard-core cardinality. Its mean is strictly increasing in $\log z$ with derivative $\operatorname{Var}(K)>0$, from 0 up to the independence number (at least $n/2$). Thus for $k=o(n)$ positive there is a unique $z_T$ with conditional mean $k$. Put $q=z/(1+z)$. The conditional probability of occupation of a vertex given every other spin is $q$ if no neighbor is occupied and otherwise 0. Therefore every vertex has probability at most $q$ and at least $q(1-d_v q)$ by the union bound. The expected fraction lies between $q(1-2q)$ and $q$, uniformly in the tree. Applying these bounds at $z=a/2,2a$ and then at the saddle gives $z_T=a+O(a^2)$ uniformly.

Here is a variance bound that remains valid at the tree-dependent saddle. At vertices $v,w$ at distance $d$, the hard-core occupation indicators have absolute covariance at most $z^{d+1}$ for $0<z<1$. Along the unique path, integrate out side branches. The path is then a (possibly time-inhomogeneous) Markov chain; one possible previous occupied state forces the next to be zero, and if the predecessor is absent the next is occupied with probability at most $q\le z$, since its forward-subtree odds are at most $z$. The contraction of conditional occupation differences is at most $z$ on each of $d$ edges. Multiply by the start indicator's variance, at most $z$. The expected number of ordered vertex pairs at distance $d$ is $(n)_{d+1}(d+1)/n^d\le n(d+1)$ by the Cayley forest formula even for $d$ growing. Hence the expected sum over off-diagonal absolute covariance *bounds at $2a$* is $O(na^2)$. Markov's inequality shows that with probability tending to one it is at most $na$; diagonal terms sum to at most $2na$. In particular $\operatorname{Var}(K)\le 3na$ simultaneously for $z\in[a/2,2a]$ on this event. No uniformity of fixed-fugacity limit theorems is needed.

With probability tending to one $T_n$, rooted at 1, contains at least $c n$ disjoint proper fringe trees of size two (a child and its unique leaf child). They have equal order and are disjoint. The conditioned Poisson fringe theorem gives limiting density $e^{-2}$; no independent-slot occurrence assertion is needed. Delete all such slots and condition under the hard-core law on an independent set in the remaining core, at $z=z_T$. The contributions of the slots to $K$ are then independent Bernoulli random variables: if a slot's parent is occupied its odds of size one are $z$, and otherwise its odds are $2z$. They have size at most one since their two vertices are joined. Thus $K$ conditional on the core is the core size plus two independent binomials, with total trial count at least $cn$ and individual success probabilities comparable to $a$. Their sum has variance $O(na)$. Also $\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[K\mid\mathrm{core}])\le\operatorname{Var}K\le3na$. With core probability at least $3/4$, the conditional mean is within $\sqrt{12na}$ of the saddle mean $k$. Choose the larger trial group (at least $cn/2$ trials). With probability at least $3/4$ the other binomial lies within $C\sqrt{na}$ of its mean, uniformly in the core (also if this binomial has no trials). The requested integer for the larger binomial is then within $C'\sqrt{na}$ of its own mean. By Stirling, for $L\ge cn/2$, $L\le n$ and success probability comparable to $a$, all such atoms are at least $c'/\sqrt{na}$, and every atom is at most $C'/\sqrt{na}$, since $na=k\to\infty$. Indeed the relative entropy cost at bounded standardized distance is bounded, and the Stirling prefactor is comparable to that inverse standard deviation. Averaging over the core and the other binomial yields
$$c'/\sqrt k\le\Pr_{T,z_T}(K=k)\le C'/\sqrt k\tag{5}$$
on the above events, with constants independent of $a\to0$. This local bound is essential if $na^6$ diverges more slowly than $\log n$.

Since $z_T$ minimizes $F_T(z)-k\log z$, (5) gives
$$\log X=\min_z[F_T(z)-k\log z]-\tfrac12\log k+O_{\mathbb P}(1).$$
Let $z_A$ minimize $p_A(z)-a\log z$; the value is $s_A(a)$ and $z_A=a+O(a^2)$ by §2. For a lower bound in the last minimum use (4) at $z_T$ and $p_A(z_T)-a\log z_T\ge s_A(a)$; for an upper bound evaluate at $z_A$. In both cases $nz^6=na^6+O(na^7)$. Stirling in Bedrosian's exact positive $\mathbb EX=\binom nk(1-a)^{k-1}$ gives $\log\mathbb EX=ns_A(a)-\tfrac12\log k+O(1)$. Subtraction proves (2), and if $na^6\to\infty$ the errors are $o_{\mathbb P}(na^6)$, proving (1).

The leading constant and moving-window conclusion are separate from a sequential small-density limit of an entropy curve.
