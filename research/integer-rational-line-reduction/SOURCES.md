# Sources and scope

1. Jack Doerner, Iftach Haitner, Yuval Ishai and Nikolaos Makriyannis, [From OT to OLE with Subquadratic Communication](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1722), IACR ePrint 2025/1722; [ACM CCS 2025 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719027.3765225). The ePrint landing page lists January 5, 2026 as its revision. The [archived full PDF](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1722/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf), also available via the [mirror's paper page](https://opencryptology.org/2025/1722), displays the date September 22, 2025 on its cover. Its §3.1 (printed p.10) defines (n)={0,…,n} and calls N the natural numbers without explicitly listing whether zero is included; §3.3 (printed p.11) defines the bit-length prime sets. The conclusions about the original inequality do not require choosing a convention for N.
2. In that full version, §5.3.5 on printed p.39 (PDF p.42, counting from 1) introduces integer g^(a,b)=ax+b for a,b in N, Definition 5.36 prints the maximum of αhat−α, and Conjecture 5.37 gives the domain-size and bit-length bounds. The source also states its deterministic restriction there. Theorem 5.39 and Remark 5.40 on printed p.40 explain the intended context. Our note concerns only the stated finite combinatorial hypothesis, not any evaluation of the cited protocol, a variant or an implementation.
3. [AICR c/0074](https://aicr.info/c/0074/) separately indexes the problem and explicitly reverses the sign in its AI-written formalization. It already points out why the printed direction is vacuous. Our independent counterexample concerns exactly the corrected direction while leaving the original integer coefficient domain unchanged. The rational-affine theorems change that domain and are not asserted as a solution of the corrected integer conjecture.
4. [Vu, M. M. Wood and P. M. Wood, Mapping Incidences](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4407), J. London Math. Soc. 84 (2011), Theorems 1.1 and 2.3, is an antecedent for preservation of finite incidence patterns under reduction to appropriately chosen prime fields. [Göös, Harms, Richter and Sofronova, No Constant-Cost Protocol for Point–Line Incidence](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03805v1), and [Goh and Hatami, Communication complexity of point-line incidences over the reals](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25192v1), §1.2, use random-prime checking of a fixed incidence as background (the first credits Chattopadhyay, Lovett and Vinyals, STOC 2019). None is a theorem about optimal rational line agreement under a prime-dependent maximizing modular line and an arbitrary fixed point law. Determinants and prime-divisor counting are standard ingredients; the stated sharp grid threshold and the weighted-average bounds are the particular consequences proved here. For the prime-count asymptotic only, see the [Prime Number Theorem, DLMF 27.2.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.2.E3).
5. A bounded search (2026-11-27 UTC) covered the title, ePrint identifier, Conjecture 5.37, corrected sign, and combinations of random-prime reduction, rational affine agreement, line incidences, and determinant counting. It found the foregoing nearby methods, no prior explicit refutation of the corrected integer reading, and no match for the weighted rational maximum comparison. This is not a priority claim or an exhaustive survey.
