# No 81-point binary Sidon set in twelve dimensions

**Theorem.** The maximum size of a Sidon set in $V_{12}=\mathbb F_2^{12}$ lies between **66 and 80**, inclusive. The upper bound is unrestricted, not just for graph sets or invariant sets. Exact determination remains open. We rule out size 81; supersets would contain an 81-subset. We use the standard 66-point lower example only as a control.

Write $\Delta A=\{x+y:x,y\in A, x\ne y\}$; Sidon means that distinct unordered pairs have distinct sums. For a set $A$ its Walsh coefficient at $u$ is $W_A(u)=\sum_{x\in A}(-1)^{u\cdot x}$. A maximum half of an affine system means its largest intersection with an affine hyperplane of its own ambient space.

## 1. Four quarter profiles, then one

The prerequisite [classification of the 48-point extremizers](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/a78febb46b89666125b6fc483fd14322b5f593bc) and its opposite-layer corollary imply that a 12-dimensional Sidon set of size at least 65 has no 48-point section and that no section can be larger; thus our maximal section $m$ is at most 47.

For $n$ points in binary dimension $d$ let $P_2=2^dn-n^2$ and $P_4=2^d(3n^2-2n)-n^4$ be the off-zero second and fourth Walsh moments. These follow by character orthogonality, counting diagonal pairs and ordered zero-sum quadruples. If the maximal half is at most $m$, all nontrivial $W$ obey $l\le W^2\le H$ for $l=n\bmod2,H=(2m-n)^2$. Hence $P_4-(l+H)P_2+lH(2^d-1)\le0$. At $n=81,d=12,m=45$ the left side is **10,575,360**, so $m\ge46$. Among maximum $m$-sections choose one with its own maximum half $a$ greatest. Applying the same slab to this $m$-set in dimension 11 excludes $\max a\le27$ when $m=46$ (gap 2,440,944), and $\max a\le28$ for $m=47$ (953,856). Writing the quarter sizes $n_i$ as $(a,m-a,c,81-m-c)$, with $c$ the larger side, every pair has sum at most $m$. These constraints and the previously established $s(10)=34$ give exactly four shapes: $(28,18,18,17)$ for 46; $(29,18,17,17),(29,18,18,16),(30,17,17,17)$ for 47. The small coverage checker regenerates all four.

For four quarter layers in $U=\mathbb F_2^{10}$ let $w_i(u)$ be their Walsh coefficients. At $u\ne0$ all four quotient-signed sums have magnitude at most $2m-81$. Moreover $|w_i|+|w_j|\le2a-m$ when $n_i+n_j=m$, by the chosen maximum internal section across **all** maximum halves. With $Q=1024$ the exact total rows are
- $\sum w_i^2=Q n_i$ and $\sum w_i^4=Q(3n_i^2-2n_i)$ by Sidon;
- $\sum w_i^2w_j^2=Qn_i n_j$ because same-layer distinct pair differences never recur in another layer;
- $\sum\prod_i w_i=0$ since four distinct layers cannot supply a zero-sum quadruple.

For the single $m=46$ shape, an explicit 16-entry integer combination in coverage.py has nonnegative value on all 1,296 signed, parity-admissible nonzero profiles and residual RHS $-135720$. (Its count row is included.) This rules the shape out.

For each of the two remaining nonhard $m=47$ shapes, independently translate any three quotient layers in $U$ by an affine shear plus common translation; the fourth translation is their XOR. Center every odd layer's XOR at zero, then force the first $e-1$ of the $e$ even layers to contain zero. Exactly three translations are specified. Even-layer XORs $g_i$ are translation-invariant. Up to changes of basis in their span there are 2 configurations for one even layer and 16 for three (introduce a new independent vector as the next basis element, otherwise take any previous span element). For a frequency $u$, the tag $b$ is its restriction to this span. Odd layers have $w_i\equiv n_i\bmod4$; even layers have $w_i\equiv n_i-2\langle b,g_i\rangle\bmod4$. The sum of each tag count is $Q/|\langle g_i\rangle|$; a rooted layer has $\sum_u w_i(u)=Q$.

The indicator signals on $U$ for the signed certificates are:
- $P_i=1_{S_i}$, and its complement $p_i$, with masses $n_i,Q-n_i$.
- $D_i=1_{\Delta S_i}$ for within-layer sums of distinct pairs, mass $\binom{n_i}{2}$. Its Walsh value is $(w_i^2-n_i)/2$. The four $D_i$ have disjoint supports; $d$ is the indicator of the complement of their union.
- $C_{ij}=1_{S_i+S_j}$, mass $n_i n_j$ and Walsh $w_iw_j$, because all these cross-sums are distinct. For each partition $0j|kl$, $C_{0j},C_{kl}$ have disjoint supports (no four-layer collision); $c_j$ is the complement of their union.

At $u\ne0$ the Fourier transform of a complement is the negative of the given signal or disjoint signal sum. **At zero instead use its full mass.** For any above indicator $T$ and any $h$ in the XOR span, $\sum_u(-1)^{u\cdot h}\widehat T(u)$ lies between 0 and $Q$ by Fourier inversion. For any two indicators $T,S$, the analogous Fourier sum with product $\widehat T\widehat S$ is $Q$ times their translate intersection cardinality, between 0 and $Q\min(|T|,|S|)$. These bounds also apply to a signal paired with itself.

Subtract the $u=0$ profile from all RHSs. Each stored certificate is an integer linear combination of equality and upper-bound rows, with nonnegative multipliers on inequalities, nonnegative value on every admissible tagged column and strictly negative residual RHS. The checker independently enumerates every required label configuration and every tagged column. A positive indicator/control replay on two rank-full subsamples of the explicit 66-Sidon example checks every active bound or equality; this also exercises nonconstant character signs and both the one-even-layer and three-even-layers normalizations. No floating-point output is used.

Henceforth the maximum section size is 47 and some 47-section has a maximum half of 30. **Every** 47-section has all its halves of size at most 30: if one has size $a'$, the complementary 34 points split into two layers each of size at most $47-a'$, so $34\le2(47-a')$. Indeed any pair of the four cosets of a codimension-two space forms an affine hyperplane.

## 2. The last quarter, of size 30

Among all 30-halves of all 47-sections choose one, $A\subset V_{10}$, whose own largest half $q$ is maximal. Write $A=C\sqcup(U+e)$, where $C,U\subset V_9$, $|C|=q$ and $e$ is the new coordinate. The other three quarters of the full 81-set have size 17 each by the preceding 34-point argument.

We have $q\ge20$. Suppose all halves of a Sidon 30-set in $V_{10}$ had size at most 19. Then $|W(u)|\le8$ off zero. For $g=\bigoplus_{x\in A}x$, each $u\in H=g^\perp$ counts an even number of negative terms, so $W(u)\equiv2\pmod4$ and $|W(u)|\le6$ off zero in $H$. Orthogonality on $H$ gives $\sum_{u\in H}W(u)^2=|H|\#\{(x,y):x+y\in H^\perp\}\ge512\cdot30$, also when $g=0$. The off-zero fourth sum is at most $64(1024\cdot30-30^2)-28(512\cdot30-30^2)=1,503,600$, less than its exact $1024(3\cdot30^2-2\cdot30)-30^4=1,893,360$.

Pick any of the three 17-quarters, $B$. Its union with $A$ is a maximum 47-section. Split $B$ parallel to $C,U$, with $V$ its larger piece ($k\ge9$) and $W$ its other piece. The 47-section has cosets of sizes $(q,30-q,k,17-k)$ over $V_9$. In particular $q+k\le30$. Thus $q=20$ or 21 and $9\le k\le30-q$. We may orient the two halves of $B$ independently of the two halves of $A$, by an affine shear with the layer indicator as input. We write $A=C\sqcup(U+e)$ and $B=V\sqcup(W+e)$ in common $V_{10}$ coordinates. Within the affine 47-section, any three of the four $V_9$ pieces may receive arbitrary translations; the fourth follows. This freedom allows us to set $C$ to a catalog representative and $U,V$ each to a representative modulo translations. The induced changes on $W$ impose no search restriction. In particular $W$ is **not** itself normalized by a translation during the search.

For each nonzero $u\in V_9$, $|W_C|+|W_U|\le2q-30$, as $A$ has maximum half $q$. Likewise $|W_C|+|W_V|\le2q-30$ if $q+k=30$: this other size-30 half is itself a half of a maximum 47-section, so its own maximum is at most the globally selected $q$. If $q+k<30$ we retain the weaker valid bound 13: the four possible quotient-signed sums over the 47-section have absolute value at most $2\cdot30-47=13$; averaging appropriate two sums cancels $U,W$ and gives $|W_C\pm W_V|\le13$. Finally for every nonzero $t\in V_{10}$ we must have $|W_A(t)|+|W_B(t)|\le13$, exactly the nonvertical character cap on $A\cup(B+f)$.

### Centering and the $q=21$ peak test

Let the four quarters of 81 be indexed by $V_2$, with the 30-quarter labeled 0. Center each of the three odd quarters by translating it by its own XOR. These three translations extend to an affine function of the quotient label (any three of the four quotient points are affinely independent), so this is a single affine coordinate change of $V_{12}$; let $t$ denote the resulting translation of $A$. At each $u\ne0$ each of the three centered 17-sets has Walsh coefficient $1\bmod4$. Each makes a 47-section with $A$, so if $|W_A(u)|=12$, its coefficient has absolute value at most 1 and hence is **+1**. The quotient character giving all three plus signs exists. If $(-1)^{u\cdot t}W_A(u)=12$, it gives coefficient 15 in the full 81-set, contradicting the global cap $2\cdot47-81=13$. Thus **one** translate of $A$ makes all its peaks of magnitude 12 negative simultaneously. For $q=20$ there are no such peaks.

When $q=21$, necessarily $k=9$ and $|W|=8$. More importantly $C\cup(V+e)$ is a 30-quarter in its own right. It is a half of the same 47-section; the other 34 points of the 81-set, split along its two other parallel quarters, are each bounded by $47-30=17$ and hence have sizes 17 and 17. The remaining quarter within the 47-section also has size 17. This new decomposition is eligible for **fresh** centering of its own three odd layers by the same affine argument. Thus **both** $C+U$ and $C+V$ (here meaning two-layer unions, not sumsets) must satisfy the peak test. No agreement between their two centering translations is assumed. Failing to demand agreement is a safe relaxation.

## 3. Complete finite endpoints

The [nine-dimensional affine Sidon census](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/8022fae01a2c79356c0cb3c9123a778bac0fb986) yields **1,681** orbits of 20-sets and **738** of 21-sets. Its exact source catalog is included; the linked mass checks certify completeness independently of the present sieves. Affine changes of $V_9$ extend to the larger spaces. Generate all normalized $U$ of size $30-q$ whose internal differences are distinct, avoid $\Delta C$, and obey $|W_C|+|W_U|\le2q-30$. These conditions suffice to make $A$ Sidon. Independent generators use different orderings of differences. A Sidon $U$ has a unique unordered pair realizing its smallest difference in the chosen order; put these endpoints at $0,d$ and fix their possible swap lexicographically. Every translation class occurs; the two generator lists are compared **exactly**, after renormalizing by taking the lexicographically first of all its translations containing zero. The first generator uses coloring/clique branching and a bit-sliced Walsh test; the second uses residual stable classes and direct signed sums. They yield the same 60,338 marked $20+10$ rows; at $21+9$ they each generate 34,115,749 marked rows before peaks and retain precisely the same 787,461 after peaks. One checks peak compatibility by binary elimination and the other intersects explicit subsets of all 1,024 possible translations.

**$q=20$.** The 60,338 rows are covered by 475 displayed representatives under affine transformations on $V_{10}$. One explicit nonsingular matrix and translation per input row are provided and checked directly. We need only coverage, not a proof that the representatives are pairwise different up to affine equivalence. The hyperplane $C$ need not be preserved by a map: after transporting the putative entire 81-set to the representative, its displayed 20+10 split is again maximal and the argument of section 2 applies to that very split.

For each representative, enumerate the larger piece $V$ of a possible 17-layer, for both $k=9,10$, up to translations of $V_9$, with internal differences distinct and disjoint from $\Delta C\cup\Delta U$ and with the section-2 Walsh cap. The smaller piece $W$ must belong to
$$V_9\setminus(C+U+V),$$
where now $+$ means the ordinary sumset; otherwise a four-piece collision exists. Its own pair differences must be distinct and disjoint from $\Delta C\cup\Delta U\cup\Delta V$. These conditions are sufficient for the 47 points to be Sidon: a four-point relation has either four points in one piece, two each in two pieces, or one in every piece. Enumerate $17-k$-subsets of the available holes, then test the final $V_{10}$ cap 13. Two independent search routines have per-representative equal counts: **7,345,317** normalized prefixes, **13,891** with enough holes, **no** 17-layer passing. The search does not need to fit three opposite 17-sets simultaneously.

**$q=21$.** Here $U$ and $V$ are both 9-sets from the *same* per-source peak-eligible list. Test every **ordered** pair with disjoint internal differences, without any affine quotient. The same holes and forbidden internal differences determine each possible size-eight $W$. For each of the 738 sources two independent cut-off-free batch sweeps agree on every count:

|marked eligible rows|ordered disjoint pairs|at least eight holes|largest hole set|47-point Sidon joins before final cap|pass final cap|
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|787,461|10,369,060|48,820|14|126|0|

One implementation uses a forbidden-difference inverted index, bit-permutation hole tables and a final Walsh transform. The other uses a direct pair scan, byte-permuted hole sets and checks each accepted 47-join by all 1,081 pair sums and all 2,047 hyperplane directions in $V_{11}$. All **126** displayed marked coordinate sets are distinct as labeled sets; **every one has largest hyperplane section 32 inside that 47-point restriction**, not the required at most 30. We make no assertion about their number of affine classes. The corresponding seven anchor keys yield 18 each. The first solver's final $|W_A|+|W_B|$ check covers all nonvertical directions; the vertical split is 30 versus 17.

Both possibilities for $q$ are impossible, completing the proof. In binary code terminology the optimal minimum distance of a linear $[80,68]$ code is 4: a check matrix of an alleged distance-at-least-5 code would have 80 columns forming with 0 an 81-Sidon set. Conversely 80 distinct check columns in the affine coset $x_0=1$ can include an affine basis and have no dependency of size at most three.

## Reproduction

Requires Python 3.10+, a C++17 compiler named `c++` and a little over 0.2 GB of disposable working space; no network or third-party Python packages. Use an ordinary little-endian machine for the compact matrix ledger. Compiler assertions are required (guarded at compile time); Python also works under `-O`. All times depend on hardware; use fewer jobs if sharing the machine.

    python3 -O replay.py --stage audit --jobs 2
    python3 -O replay.py --stage full --jobs 3

Audit checks the moment/indicator certificates, controls, ledgers and 126 explicit 47-point joins. Full also regenerates both catalogs independently for all 2,419 anchors, checks their exact normalized lists, rechecks all 60,338 affine matrices, repeats both $q=20$ endpoints and both full $q=21$ sweeps, and compares every per-source counter and positive 47-join to the frozen ledgers. Full is the certified computational proof; audit alone does not re-enumerate negative searches. Stages `generators`, `q20`, `q21` are available for splitting a replay. The mark algorithms' only changed constant in the real $q=20$ positive test is the final $V_{10}$ cap from 13 to 17 (i.e. on the 47-set the maximum nonvertical half from 30 to 32). All original prefix and Sidon constraints stay active, and both return the same 18 relaxed layers, including the supplied planted one. The strict cap rejects that planted layer. The original indicator tests also use known 66-set controls with nontrivial parity tags.

The file `quarter/README.md` explains only the analytic reduction component and intentionally does not claim the final endpoint there. No speculative estimate, affine fingerprint, numerical LP solver status or assumed symmetry of an 81-set is used as an exclusion.
