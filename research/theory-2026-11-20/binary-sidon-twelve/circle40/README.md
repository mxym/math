# Rigidity of maximum hyperplane sections of the 66-point norm-circle Sidon set

Work in the additive group of E = GF(4096); identify its twelve-dimensional binary vector space with V. Write K = GF(64), and let S consist of 0 together with the 65 solutions of N(E/K)(z) = z^65 = 1. A binary Sidon set means a set whose unordered pairs of different points have distinct sums.

**Theorem.** The maximum size of a hyperplane section of S is 40, achieved in 585 affine hyperplanes. Let A be any one of these sections. Every Sidon set of size at least 66 in V containing an affine copy of A is an affine copy of S. More precisely, after fixing the embedded A in its hyperplane, the opposite 26 points are forced up to a translation in the direction space of that hyperplane. No affine automorphism of A is needed.

This says nothing about a purported 67-point set avoiding these sections, and does not classify all 66-point sets. In particular it does not determine the maximum in dimension 12. Ordinary completeness of the full conic plus nucleus is older (Nagy, *Sidon sets, thin sets, and the nonlinearity of vectorial Boolean functions*, [JCTA 212 (2025), 106001, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2024.106001)). Here only 40 of the 66 points are prescribed.

## Proof

Represent K by the polynomial X^6+X+1; encode its elements in the polynomial bit basis. In the quadratic extension write an element as a+bY (encoded a+64b), where Y²=Y+delta and delta=32 has absolute trace one. The verifier constructs S directly by the norm equation and checks the 2145 different pair sums. Multiplication by the order-65 norm-one element 180 and squaring are invertible binary linear maps preserving S. Their transposes partition the 4095 nonzero character directions. For a character u let W(u) be the sum of (-1)^(u·x) on S. The section sizes in its two affine hyperplanes are (66±W(u))/2. The complete signed distribution is:

|W|−14|−10|−6|−2|2|6|10|14|
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|number of directions|195|520|585|390|780|780|455|390|

The 390 positive and 195 negative extremal directions are each a *single orbit*, represented by u=6 and u=17, respectively. We shift coordinates so the large section is in the zero layer. Drop the least set coordinate of u to identify each coset with V11, using u·x (shifted by the constant choosing the 40 side) as last coordinate. On both representatives the resulting set A has affine rank 11. Also A together with all sums of three distinct points of A equals V11. Consequently a Sidon set containing A cannot contain any further point of its affine span. All these finite linear-algebra and triple-coverage statements are rederived by run.py before either search starts.

Let B in V11 be an opposite-layer set. Write D(U) for all x+y with x,y distinct in U. Since A is Sidon, the two-layer union is Sidon iff B is Sidon and D(B) avoids D(A). To see sufficiency, any additive collision of four distinct points has even parity of layer labels; a two-and-two collision is exactly an intersection of the two internal difference sets.

**Exact subproblem.** For each of the two A, the translation classes of compatible B of size 26 consist of exactly one class, namely the known opposite layer of S. The verifier recovers this explicit class by two separately implemented searches using different internal splits. The following establishes coverage of both algorithms.

* Pick an internal character of the 11-dimensional layer, so A splits into C,T in V10. Local side labels are chosen to put the larger part in C; each selected split is 27+13. Write B as L on side 0 and R on side 1 of the same split. Translating the *whole B* in V11 preserves all its differences and may interchange L,R, so assume k=|L|≥|R|=26−k. Both searches traverse every k from 13 through 26 inclusive; no external Sidon maximum is needed.
* In the two internal sides the necessary and sufficient conditions are: L and R are Sidon; D(L), D(R) both avoid D(C)∪D(T) and each other; and no member of L+R belongs to C+T. The last condition includes the collisions in all four quarter labels and follows by rearrangement. Sufficiency follows either by counting the four labels or by using D(A) and D(B) above.
* Root L by a least unordered difference d in L in a fixed order on the nonzero V10 vectors. Translation by an endpoint of that pair makes 0,d belong to L. This translation acts on R as well, and R's actual position is enumerated. The degree-ordered search sorts the eligible nonzero vectors by compatibility degree (ties numeric), progressively suppressing edges that use earlier differences; the numeric search simply compares raw XORs with d when constructing its graph. Choosing either endpoint works, so repeated possible representations are removed **only after** a complete labelled B is recovered. A translation class is represented by the least lexicographic sorted list among its translates that contain 0 (necessarily the least of all translates).
* In the L recursion, disallow fixed differences in D(C)∪D(T), and on adjoining x remove the triples x+l+l' of the previously chosen points, since each would cause four distinct L points to sum to zero. Other old triple exclusions are inherited. At every stage R must lie in the complement of C+T+L. Organize compatible L vertices in a graph. Greedily proper-coloring a candidate subgraph gives an upper bound on its clique number; each prefix ending with color c uses at most c colors. Pruning by any of these bounds is therefore exact. Similarly an upper bound for R may ignore the extra forbidden differences of L. Neither implementation assumes a greedy lower bound is optimal.
* At a complete L, enumerate all (26−k)-subsets of the remaining pool for R satisfying the difference restrictions, adding R-pair differences to the forbidden set as each point is taken. This also handles empty R. Every terminal is lifted to the original V11 coordinates and checked independently against A by a direct all-pair calculation in V12 before normalization. The code never stops on the first acceptable opposite layer.
* The degree search chooses the **first** maximizing internal direction: 284 or 37. The numeric search rebuilds the graph for each numeric d from scratch and takes the **last** maximizing direction: 1743 or 2041. In each of the four exhaustive runs only k=13 gives a translate class. Each final reconstructed class is compared for equality with the explicitly computed planted 26-set, not merely counted. See the source and transcript.

If an opposite 27-set existed, any two of its 26-point deletions would lie in this one translation class. These distinct sets overlap in 25 points. Two distinct translates of a Sidon set have intersection at most two, since every member of U∩(U+t) with t≠0 comes from an unordered pair of difference t, and at most one such pair exists. Contradiction. This rules out all larger opposite sets by taking subsets. Thus a Sidon set of size ≥66 through A has 40 points in the anchored hyperplane and exactly the planted opposite 26 up to translation t. The affine shear (x,s)↦(x+s t,s) fixes the anchored hyperplane pointwise while applying precisely this translation on the other layer. This proves the theorem.

## Independent replay

Run `python3 -O run.py` in this directory. Python 3.10+, a C++17 compiler named g++ (or `CXX`), and an x86-64 Linux host with the GNU-compatible builtins are sufficient. Source C++ rejects NDEBUG. The wrapper builds fresh executables in a temporary directory, constructs the finite field and all sections from scratch, verifies the human-readable copies in sections.txt, and checks both complete search outputs. No network, code tables, prior bound or external optimization library is used. sections.txt alternates A then the planted opposite B, first u=6, then u=17. The final line must read PASS.
