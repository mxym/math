## Additional affine symmetry check

The additional `check_types.py` (outside the unchanged ZIP) tabulates the full absolute Walsh distributions of the 40-point anchors: they have 222 and 252 zero nontrivial characters, respectively, so the two types are not affinely equivalent. Since one affine type gives the 390 sections all containing the nucleus, every affine automorphism of the full 66-point set preserves that family. Each nonzero circle point belongs to exactly 390·39/65 = 234 family members by norm-one multiplication transitivity. Thus the nucleus is the unique point in every family member and must be fixed.

Weight each pair of circle points by the number of those 390 sections containing both. The script independently reconstructs the circle in an absolute polynomial basis and exhausts all automorphisms of the resulting weighted graph fixing the point 1 (781 backtracking nodes, 12 permutations). Every affine automorphism of the 66 set acts faithfully on the circle since the circle spans the space. The 65 norm-one multiplications and twelve Frobenius powers already give 780 distinct elements and a transitive action on the circle. Hence the full affine group is C65 ⋊ C12. An affine stabilizer of either 40-point anchor can be extended to the ambient space; the unique opposite translate lets one correct the extension by an affine shear. That correction is unique because a nonzero translation meets the original 26-set in at most two points. Therefore the two full affine stabilizers of the anchors have orders 2 and 4 respectively.

## Prior work and version check

Nagy's [Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2024.106001) already treats completeness of the entire conic-plus-nucleus; it does not force the other points from just its 40-point section. The circle's linear group is treated here as an independent check, not as a new coding-theory group: compare the [binary Zetterberg code description](https://reneschoof.github.io/vdvhecke.pdf), the [cyclic-code group census through length 70](https://docserv.uni-duesseldorf.de/servlets/DocumentServlet?id=4837) and [Feng–Hollmann–Li–Xiang, arXiv:2603.01904v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01904v2), revised 9 November 2026. The dedicated July–21 November search checked [Thornburgh's bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27731v1), [the Dobbertin Walsh-spectrum paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18907v1), and [the partition regularity preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11840), which concern different results. This repository's section and opposite-layer searches found no copy of the specific 40-point rigidity statement. This check is bounded and does not establish global priority.
