# There is no 80-point Sidon set in twelve binary dimensions

**Theorem.** Every Sidon subset of $\mathbb F_2^{12}$ has size at most 79. This packet proves the claim by ruling out two types of 11-dimensional sections with independently executed and matched finite searches. The known lower bound is 66.

Let $V_r=\mathbb F_2^r$, and let a half mean an affine hyperplane section. Four distinct elements of a Sidon set never sum to zero. The published [eleven-dimensional extremizer classification](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/a78febb46b89666125b6fc483fd14322b5f593bc) ensures the largest half $m$ of an 80-point set in $V_{12}$ is at most 47. For a Sidon $n$-set in $V_r$, the off-zero second/fourth Walsh moments are $2^rn-n^2$ and $2^r(3n^2-2n)-n^4$ by orthogonality. With $H=(2m-n)^2,l=n\bmod2$, each off-zero term $(W^2-H)(W^2-l)$ would be nonpositive. At $n=80,r=12,m=45$, their sum is instead 4,899,840, so $m\ge46$.

Among all $m$-halves choose one whose largest internal half $a$ is as large as possible. The other $80-m$ points occupy two parallel quarters each of size at most $m-a$: in the quotient $V_2$ any two distinct cosets are a hyperplane. Consequently $a\le29$ at $m=46$ and $a\le30$ at $m=47$.

Apply the same off-zero second/fourth moment slab in dimension 11 to the $m$-half. The six pair bounds and $s(10)=34$ leave exactly the following six size profiles (verified by analytic/n80shapes.py):
* $m=46$: $(28,18,17,17),(28,18,18,16),(29,17,17,17)$;
* $m=47$: $(29,18,17,16),(29,18,18,15),(30,17,17,16)$.
The exact signed-Walsh route in [analytic/WALSH.md](analytic/WALSH.md), with all 82 integer separators, eliminates the first two profiles on each line. Thus an ambient 80-set would supply either a 46-point section with largest half 29, or a 47-point section with largest half 30. No general code-classification theorem is needed.

Write (I) for the assertion that no 46-point Sidon set in $V_{11}$ has maximum half 29, and (II) for the assertion that no 47-point Sidon set has maximum half 30. The two finite assertions suffice for the preceding reduction.

To check (I), select among all its 29-halves $A$ one with the largest internal maximum $q$. The [seven-term block-moment argument](analytic/SIDON29.md) proves that every 29-set in $V_{10}$ which is Sidon has a half of size at least 20; its separate integer checker gives a sum of $-88$ for a nonnegative quantity under the contrary assumption. Thus $q\ge20$. Split $A=C\sqcup(U+e)$, $|C|=q$. The opposite 17-set splits into $V,W$ of sizes $k,17-k$, with $k\ge9$. Because $C,V$ form a half of the 46-set, $q+k\le29$. It follows that $(|C|,|U|,|V|,|W|)=(20,9,9,8)$. Both layers $C\cup U$ and $C\cup V$ (as two-coset sets) have 29 points, and each has largest internal half at most $q=20$. Therefore at each $u\in V_9^*\setminus0$ we must have $|W_C(u)|+|W_U(u)|\le11$, and the identical inequality for $V$. The final 46-set has off-zero Walsh cap $2\cdot29-46=12$. No tests comparing its individual peak signs are imposed.

For (II), choose $A$ of size 30 and $q$ analogously. A 29-point subset of $A$ already forces $q\ge20$ by that same lemma. Since $q+k\le30$ for the split opposite 17-set with $k\ge9$, $q=20$ or 21. If $q=20$, the earlier proof's entire 60,338-marked-row / 475-representative search already tests every $20+10$ quarter against all eligible 17-layers. No 81-specific sign conditions were imposed in that branch; the required cap for another 30-half is valid by maximizing $q$ over the present standalone 47-set's 30-halves. The search returned no opposite 17. For $q=21$, the four sizes are $(21,9,9,8)$, both $C+U$ and $C+V$ two-coset 30-sets have internal maximum $\le21$, giving both within-pair Walsh caps 12. The final amplitude cap is 13. Unlike the earlier 81-point proof this time **there is no requirement that either marked 30-set admit a simultaneous translation sign at its peaks**. All feasible 9-sets are included.

Use the complete [nine-dimensional orbit census](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/8022fae01a2c79356c0cb3c9123a778bac0fb986) for $C$, with 1,681 input 20-sets or 738 input 21-sets. Any affine chart-taking of $C$ extends to the full $V_{11}$. Two of the other independent $V_9$ layer translations may be specified freely while keeping $C$ fixed, since a quotient-dependent affine translation on $V_2$ is prescribed by any three quotient labels. Normalize $U,V$ independently modulo translations; leave $W$ unrestricted (its induced translation is immaterial). Generate Sidon 9-sets whose internally distinct differences avoid $\Delta C$ and satisfy the indicated Walsh bound. For each unordered pair with $\Delta U\cap\Delta V=\varnothing$ require $W$ to consist of eight positions in $V_9\setminus(C+U+V)$, with actual sumsets in this expression. Then test its Sidon differences avoid $\Delta C\cup\Delta U\cup\Delta V$. The listed conditions suffice for Sidon: the multiplicities of a zero-sum four-tuple in the four quotient layers must be 4, 2+2, or 1+1+1+1. Swapping $U,V$ permutes affine quotient labels and is a genuine symmetry since both pieces have size nine and exactly the same internal Walsh cap. Each full legal fourth layer is tested against the full $V_{11}$ maximum-half cap.

Both searches use the unique pair realizing the least nonzero difference in a Sidon 9-set to root it at zero and remove its residual two orientations lexicographically. Their least-difference orders, graph-color branching methods, Walsh arithmetic, bitset pair filters, hole-set encoding and final tests are implemented independently. They make no group-invariance assumption on the putative 80-set or 11D sections. The only selected-set symmetries are exact changes of coordinates or translations. ## Independent compression through a classical code theorem

A useful elementary code constraint supplies the corresponding lower bound $a\ge29$ in both cases. An affinely spanning $n$-set in $V_d$ gives an injective evaluation of all affine linear functions, an $[n,d+1,\ge n-a]$ code (a nonspanning set has a half containing all of it). Jaffe, [Optimal binary linear codes of length ≤30](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(99)00135-1), proves there is no binary $[29,11,10]$ code. The same formulation with distance at least ten follows by puncturing $d-10$ positions in a minimum word then appending zero columns; the dimension is preserved. Thus there is no $[28,11,\ge9]$ code, by parity extension. If a binary $[n,k,d]$ code has a minimum word of weight $d$, puncturing its support gives $[n-d,k-1,\ge\lceil d/2\rceil]$: only that word and zero vanish on the complement (two other words with the same projection would sum to it, and could not both be supported on its $d$ positions); the two preimages of a nonzero residual word of weight $b$ have weight sum $d+2b\ge2d$. In general to invoke an obstruction for a lower target distance, first lower any stronger distance by puncturing within a minimum word and append zero positions to restore length. The residual bounds therefore exclude $[46,12,\ge18]$ and $[47,12,\ge19]$ (the latter has a $[28,11,\ge10]$ residual), giving the stated lower bound. The same argument excludes $[45,12,\ge17]$.

Together with the pair-size inequalities this says that an ambient 80-set would yield (I), (II), or a 47-subset with largest half 29. The latter gives a 46-subset of the type in (I) by deleting outside the chosen 29-half. Also Jaffe implies directly that each 29-set in $V_{10}$ has a half of at least 20 by affine evaluation or failure to span. This route establishes the same reduction without any of the 82 signed certificates or the special Sidon 29-set lemma.

## Corollaries and scope

The result implies $66\le s(12)\le79$, not its exact value. The 66-point lower bound is explicit in analytic/control66.txt and is also given by [Nagy’s ellipse-plus-nucleus construction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0097316524001407); the Sidon/code correspondence is discussed by [Czerwinski–Pott](https://doi.org/10.3934/amc.2023054). No 80-point Sidon set means no binary $[79,67,\ge5]$ code: zero plus the parity-check columns of any such code would be an 80-set in $V_{12}$ with pairwise distinct pair sums. For a matching distance-four code take any 79 distinct parity-check columns spanning $V_{12}$ in the affine coset $x_0=1$ (include $e_0$ and $e_0+e_j$, $1\le j<12$). No sum of at most three columns vanishes, so its check-kernel has dimension 67 and distance at least four. Thus $d_2(79,67)=4$. Puncturing a putative $[80,67,\ge6]$ code would violate this result, whereas a [known $[80,67,5]$ construction](https://www.codetables.de/BKLC/BKLC.php?q=2&n=80&k=67) supplies the matching lower bound. As a direct positive certificate, columns80_67.txt gives 80 nonzero 13-bit parity-check columns extracted from the stored $[82,68,6]$ construction by puncturing once then omitting a final column; the checker verifies rank 13 and all 3,240 pair sums of the augmented 81-set independently. Hence $d_2(80,67)=5$. Before this exclusion [Grassl's table for the first pair](https://www.codetables.de/BKLC/BKLC.php?q=2&n=79&k=67) gives 4–5, and the companion page 5–6. This theorem does not decide size 79; in particular this packet does not exclude size-47 maximal sections with inner maximum 31 or classify all 47-sets.
