# The intrinsic projective groups of qutrit error bases

**Main result.** For any qutrit unitary error basis S, its projective left differences either preserve three orthogonal coordinate lines or generate a dense subgroup of PU(3). Both possibilities occur. In the monomial branch the closure is finite, of order 9N with N a nonzero Eisenstein norm, or the full projective diagonal torus extended by a cyclic coordinate permutation. Every listed order is realized. The explicit nonmonomial example is a universal single-qutrit gate set modulo phase; no multi-qudit or fault-tolerant universality is asserted.

**Files:** dichotomy.pdf and dichotomy.tex (complete three-page manuscript); primitive.py, standard-library exact finite subgroup check; replay.txt, its expected output. Run **python3 primitive.py**. No numerical root or matrix optimization is used. The explicit dense example is elementary and independent of the finite subgroup classification and the computer check. It is the same basis as the separate bipartite two-copy discrimination paper, which establishes a different operational property.

**Provenance:** [Blichfeldt's primitive projective plane group list, stated as Dolgachev's Theorem 14](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/cheltsov/ed/pdf/asia.pdf) supplies the four containing abstract groups used for the exact finite comparison. The computation sets a necessary combinatorial condition, not an assertion that all order-three elements in every qutrit representation have trace zero. [Werner](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0003070) gives the standard monomial construction; [Nayak–Yuen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01672) prove existence of inequivalent bases in dimension three and higher. [Svegborn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16396) give a one-parameter SIC-derived family of inequivalent qutrit maximally entangled measurements. The universal example is different. Searches for qutrit nonmonomial unitary bases, projectively universal error bases, and finite qutrit UEB spectra and the local mxym/math tree revealed no directly matching theorem (20 November 2026). No priority guarantee. We do not claim a complete equivalence classification among the universal bases.
