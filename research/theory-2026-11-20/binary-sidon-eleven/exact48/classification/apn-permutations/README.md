# Linearity of a six-variable APN permutation is at most 28

**Corollary of the completed optimal 48-Sidon-set classification.** If $F:\mathbb F_2^6\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^6$ is an APN permutation, its Walsh linearity is at most 28 (nonlinearity at least 18). No example with linearity 28 or full classification of six-bit APN permutations is asserted.

The prerequisite classification, in [the archived proof](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/a78febb46b89666125b6fc483fd14322b5f593bc) (**Sidon_extremizers_11D_48_v1.zip**), SHA-256 **54cc84b038f8336c78a781e1b5a47ac736477036574f712656e76a939ec43474**, proves without a quadraticity assumption that the maximum linearity 32 among all six-bit APN functions belongs to the CCZ class of $D_7$ only. For permutations every nontrivial output component is balanced. Comparing it to an input linear form gives Walsh value divisible by four: two balanced truth vectors have even Hamming distance, and the zero input form gives correlation zero. It remains to exclude $D_7$ up to CCZ from permutations.

This last, **class-specific fact is prior work**, not a new impossibility by itself: [Testing CCZ-equivalence to permutations via projections of Walsh zero spaces, §5 and Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.10269v1#S5) found just the Kim class ($D_5$) permutation-equivalent among the thirteen quadratic six-dimensional classes. The contribution of the preceding unrestricted extremal theorem is ruling out linearity 32 for *all* APN permutations, without presuming the list of all six-bit APNs is complete. See [the explicit polynomials, D5 and D7 in Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22394v2#A1.T1). The small checker verifies both truth tables from those polynomials. It reproves the needed class-specific exclusion in two ways.

Let $G$ be a 64-point subset of $V=\mathbb F_2^{12}$. It can be a permutation graph in some affine coordinates if and only if there are complementary six-dimensional kernels $K,L$ each avoiding $\Delta G$ (the distinct unordered pair sums). Indeed projecting $G$ modulo either kernel is then injective and hence bijective, and the two quotient maps identify $V$ with $(V/K)\oplus(V/L)$. Conversely the two coordinate projections of any permutation graph have these properties. Translation of $G$ leaves the kernel criterion unchanged. Equivalently the annihilator $K^\perp$ must consist, except for zero, of zero Walsh coefficients of the indicator $1_G$. This follows by Fourier inversion on $V/K$: the number of points over every quotient word is one exactly when all nonconstant quotient coefficients vanish. For pairwise complementary dimension-six subspaces, complementary annihilators give precisely the same condition; indeed the intersection dimensions of the two kernels and their annihilators agree.

**First enumeration (C++):** Enumerate the six-subspaces in the complement of the nonzero distinct-pair sums by reduced bases, with highest-bit pivots in increasing order and old pivot bits zero on each later vector. Every subspace has exactly one such binary basis. At a prefix with span $T$, eligible higher representatives $y$ have $y+T$ allowed. After adding $x$, retain exactly the larger-pivot $y$ with the pivot bit of $x$ zero and $x+y$ also in the old eligible list; then $y+(T+\langle x\rangle)$ is allowed. Stop if fewer than $2^{6-\dim T}-1$ nonzero admissible cosets remain: a target subspace would require that many. A new pivot at depth $k$ is at most $6+k$ (numbering bits 0–11), leaving space for the remaining higher pivots. The program checks all output subspaces are distinct and all points avoid the forbidden difference set.

**Independent enumeration (Python):** Work instead in the zero-Walsh set and compute admissible $k$-spaces in the first $d$ binary coordinates. Such a space either lies in the first $d-1$ coordinates, or has a unique admissible $(k-1)$-dimensional intersection $T$ there. In the latter case its other half is exactly one high-bit coset of $T$, and *every* such coset contained in the zero-Walsh set is tried. No RREF or difference-set code from the first implementation is reused. The Python calculation also checks closure under addition and reports intersection cardinalities directly.

Both return:
- $D_7$: 94 six-spaces, with pair intersections of sizes 4 (180 pairs), 8 (1,740), 16 (2,145), 32 (306). No pair has intersection $\{0\}$.
- Known Kim positive control $D_5$: 222 six-spaces; 256 unordered pairs have intersection $\{0\}$. Other intersection cardinalities are checked in the script.

Run from any directory with Python 3.10+ and C++17: **python3 -O check.py** (specify its path if not in the current directory). No network or dependencies beyond the standard library and compiler.
