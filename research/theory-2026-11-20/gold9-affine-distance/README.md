# Exact affine distances and nine-flat sections of the nine-bit Gold graphs

Work over the binary field K of order 512. For d=3,5,17 let G_d={(x,x^d):x∈K}. The Hamming distance below counts unequal *vectors*, not unequal component bits.

**Theorem.** The maximum intersection of G_d with an arbitrary affine 9-space in K² is **19**, for each of these three exponents. For the more restrictive graphs of affine maps K→K the maxima, in that order, are **19,18,18**: the distances to the nearest vectorial affine map are respectively **493,494,494**. For the three inverse permutations (exponents 341,409,481) the maxima are all **19**, giving distances **493,493,493**. For d=5 or 17 every 19-point nine-flat has input projection rank 8, though some have output projection rank 9.

Ambient nine-flat intersection is unchanged under any affine graph equivalence (in particular, CCZ-equivalence); distance to affine *graphs* is not. In fact x^5 and its compositional inverse in this theorem illustrate the difference. Squaring the output is linear and invertible; its iterations identify all genuine Gold exponents 1+2^i, gcd(i,9)=1, with one of d=3,5,17 modulo 511. Output Frobenius also gives the corresponding inverse-exponent rotations, for example 171 and 103. We make no APN claim about x^9 on K: gcd(3,9)≠1.

**Prior work.** G. P. Nagy, [On the minimum Hamming distance between vectorial Boolean and affine functions](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-025-00808-4), Theorem 18(iv), gave the bound 491 on the affine distance in dimension nine in the indicated Gold cases. Our finite reduction follows his use of centred triples (called gerbera leaves in his Definition 19) but requires only three such leaves, completes the quotient-space extension exactly, and also drops the graph restriction. This result is **not** a claim about the maximum unrestricted 12-dimensional binary Sidon set.

## Proof and complete reduction

Call a finite set Sidon if all sums of two distinct members are distinct as the unordered pair varies. For the Gold function f=x^(1+2^i), the derivative at a≠0 has linear part a^(2^i)x+a x^(2^i), of kernel {0,a} since gcd(i,9)=1. Thus G_d and every intersection in it are Sidon.

**A centre with three leaves.** Let S be any 19-element Sidon set of a 9-dimensional affine binary space. A sum of three distinct elements cannot be in S. The r(u) triples with a fixed sum u are pairwise disjoint (otherwise their other pair sums coincide). Suppose r≤2. There are choose(19,3)=969 triples and at most 512−19=493 centres, so at least 476 centres have r=2. Their two triples yield a zero-sum six-set; every such six-set accounts for exactly ten unordered partitions into triples and hence ten such centres (distinct by the r≤2 hypothesis). Thus there are at least 48 zero-sum six-sets. If two distinct such blocks share ≥3 points, a shared triple has its own triple sum and one complementary triple from each block, contrary to r≤2. A pair of points is in ≤floor(17/4)=4 blocks, since the four remaining points of each block through it are disjoint. A point is in at most floor(18·4/5)=14 blocks; altogether there are at most floor(19·14/6)=44 blocks, a contradiction. The same three-leaf condition therefore applies to a set of any size ≥19 by taking a subset.

The centre of the three triples is in the affine span of their points. It lies outside G_d by the Sidon property. Translate the graph by the affine automorphism (x,y)↦(x+a,y+f(a)+a^(2^i)x+a x^(2^i)), making the centre's input zero. The new centre is (0,c), c≠0; scale by (x,y)↦(t x,t^d y) to get c=1, possible since gcd(d,511)=1. These maps preserve affine 9-spaces, input-projection ranks, and the property of being a graph over the input; they need not preserve output-projection ranks. We require invertibility on the output only for explicit inverse lower witnesses, not as a global invariant of this normalization.

From now on subtract (0,1), so we enumerate linear spaces through zero in the translated graph G'={(x,f(x)+1)}. Enumerate its zero-sum triples directly: input triple {a,b,a+b} with 0<a<b<a+b and f(a)+f(b)+f(a+b)=1. There are 85 leaves. Every three distinct leaves span a 6-space U: dimension ≤6 from the leaf relations; their nine points cannot lie in dimension ≤5, as they have 36 distinct nonzero pair sums and 2^5−1=31. This does not assert full rank of their input projections. U contains exactly nine points of G', and each of its nonzero cosets contains at most three: both facts are explicitly verified for every raw triple in the arithmetic replay, independently of any prior Sidon census.

**Unrestricted nine-flats.** A 9-space containing U is the inverse image of a 3-space in V/U, where dim(V/U)=12. At quotient element v put h(v)=|G'∩(v+U)|. A 3-space with basis i,j,k has total intersection
    9+h(i)+h(j)+h(i+j)+h(k)+h(k+i)+h(k+j)+h(k+i+j).
The search materializes these actual seven values simultaneously; none are chosen independently. To search threshold n=19 or 20 put s=n−9. The first method chooses the largest-weight nonzero i, then the next largest j, then largest k outside span(i,j), with ties broken by index. Since every h≤3, any space attaining the threshold has positive maximum outside every plane (a plane contributes ≤9 to s≥10). Necessarily 7h(i)≥s, h(i)+6h(j)≥s and h(i)+h(j)+h(i+j)+4h(k)≥s. These are only pruning inequalities. All candidate spaces are checked by exact sums. Frobenius z↦z² permutes the 85 leaves in the normalized graph, so a simultaneous Frobenius orbit representative of the **unordered triple of leaves** suffices. There are 10,982 representatives of all 98,770 triples.

Independent method: each point of a seven-point binary projective plane belongs to three of its seven lines. Thus in any desired 3-space there is a 2-subspace of weight ≥ceil(3s/7), counting its three nonzero vectors. In that 2-space order a basis to give its first, second and third weights in descending order; the third vector must be the sum of the first two. Test every such plane, then each exterior k of weight at least ceil((s−plane_weight)/4). At least one exterior k qualifies in any solution, as one may maximize its weight among the four. The complete seven weights are tested for each. The independent implementation uses polynomial X^9+X^4+X^3+X+1, least-bit elimination and log/antilog field arithmetic, checks all 98,770 triples without orbit reduction, and checks the claimed input projection rank for every high frame encountered. The first implementation uses X^9+X^4+1, ordinary polynomial multiplication and highest-bit elimination. For x³ neither found an intersection ≥20. For x⁵ and x¹⁷ neither found any ≥20; 175 and 156 **raw triple frames**, respectively, have some 19-flat in the independent coordinates. For each, the input-projection rank of every such flat is 8. The unweighted numbers of qualifying (Frobenius-representative triple, 9-flat) pairs in the first coordinates are 21 and 18. These are algorithm diagnostics, not counts of all distinct affine spaces.

**Restricted graph replays.** Rank-nine input projection suffices to recognize an affine graph. Independently we discard every three-leaf frame whose input projection has rank <6. In the remaining 93,436 raw frames U projects isomorphically onto an input 6-space. Choose three complement vectors; the unknown linear map into the output is determined by three output values. For input coset c∈F₂³ and candidate complement image v∈K compute h_c(v) by direct comparison on all 64 entries. For i,j,k a quotient basis and images a,b,e the seven weights are h_i(a),h_j(b),h_(i+j)(a+b),h_k(e),h_(i+k)(a+e),h_(j+k)(b+e),h_(i+j+k)(a+b+e). The first graph engine uses greedy heaviest basis and a Frobenius reduction (10,388 representative frames). The second graph engine checks all 93,436 using plane averaging, including zero-weight plane coordinates when needed; it enumerates every output value on the complementary basis vector. It also detects a witness at each of the three lower adjacent cutoffs.

**Attainment and inverse powers.** The standalone Python witness checker evaluates all 512 arguments of each stated function and affine map. In addition to the direct maps with 19,18,18 roots, it verifies a 19-flat in each of the three graphs. Each explicitly selected flat projects bijectively onto the output, hence its transposed flat is an affine graph attaining 19 agreements for the inverse function. Swapping the coordinates cannot change the unrestricted 9-flat upper bound. This proves the inverse conclusions without imposing invertibility on arbitrary nearest affine maps. The code prints every affine intercept and nine columns using the first polynomial basis. For x³ the witnessing direct affine map is itself invertible; for the two other functions the transposed witnesses arise from nongraph flats.

## Replay

From this directory run `python3 -O run.py` with a C++17 compiler (`CXX` or g++). No downloads, external database, third-party library, or floating arithmetic are required. The runner independently builds all four searches, compares tested frame totals, thresholds and outcomes, then checks all six explicit maps. Assertions must stay enabled in the C++ builds; passing -DNDEBUG is rejected. All reported figures are exact. The compact proof and six witnesses can be read without running a search; `python3 -O witness.py` checks the witnesses alone.

## Literature comparison, checked through 20 November 2026

The dated July–November 2026 pass searched recent APN/Gold affine-distance and gerbera results, together with the repository. The [published Nagy Theorem 18(iv)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12095-025-00808-4) supplies a lower distance bound of 491 for the listed exponents, not the six exact distances here. [Rodríguez-Aldama et al., arXiv:2609.22394v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22394v3) (revised 27 October) treats related breaking and balance properties, not these distance or nine-flat extrema. No matching theorem was located in this bounded check; it is not a priority claim.
