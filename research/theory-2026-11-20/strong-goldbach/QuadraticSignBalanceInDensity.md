# Goldbach summands have balanced quadratic signs in natural density

Let $N$ be even. In the representations $r+s=N$ by odd primes, give the oriented pair the sign $(s/r)$. For relative density one of the even integers the proportions of the two signs tend to $1/2$. The same holds when each unordered pair is oriented by its **smaller** summand $r$. More precisely, for either convention and every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the proportion of centers in $[X,2X]$ for which the absolute signed count exceeds $\varepsilon N/\log^2N$ tends to zero. The diagonal, assigned sign zero, is negligible.

This is a density version of the [July 2022 question of Dr. Pi](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/426188/quadratic-patterns-in-summands-of-goldbachs-conjecture), not Goldbach's conjecture. In fact the proposed unrestricted pointwise balance is false: at an even square $N=4m^2>4$ each genuine prime pair has $(s/r)=(N/r)=+1$. A nonunit endpoint would force $r=s=2m^2$, which is composite. [Perelli's unconditional polynomial exceptional-set estimate, Corollary 1](https://content.ems.press/assets/public/full-texts/serials/rmi/12/2/5197/online/10.4171-rmi-205.pdf), applied to $4m^2$, shows that almost all of these square targets in the parameter $m$ are represented. Their signed proportion is exactly $+1$, consistent with the density result because the square targets have density zero. [Rakhmonov](https://www.mathnet.ru/links/13cd2c21c2297b7c601242f022e55bde/rm1994_eng.pdf) investigates characters at shifted primes and the least represented number in a progression, different averages. The genuine prime input below is [Bienvenu, *A higher-dimensional Siegel–Walfisz theorem*, Acta Arith. 179 (2017), Theorem 1.3](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/92171). Compare the [natural-scale lower bounds for both signs](NaturalMovingQuadraticSectors.md). Here we average the **full** switches in both directions and show asymptotic balance, including $N=2\pmod4$.

## 1. A signed reversal and its proposed row sum

Fix $\eta=1$ or $1/2$. Work on $X\le N<2X$ and, initially, the eligible even $N$ for which $(N/g)=-1$ with a **fixed** prime $g\equiv1\pmod{12}$. Put $L=\log X$, $T=L^8$, and take all $J\asymp T/\log T$ primes $t\in[T,2T]$. Write $A_t=g t^2$. Work in $\mathbb Z/(4N)$ with least residues. Define
$$Z_N(r)=\vartheta(r)\vartheta(N-r)\,1_{0<r<\eta N},\qquad
\vartheta(r)=(\log r)1_{r\text{ odd prime}}$$
(with zero off the support). The even real Dirichlet character $\chi_N=(4N/\cdot)$ on units, extended by zero, has a modulus $4N$; for a prime $r$ on the support $\chi_N(r)=((N-r)/r)$. This includes the zero value on the diagonal. Each $t\nmid N$ gives inverse permutations $v_t^+(r)=[A_t r]_{4N}$ and $v_t^-(r)=[A_t^{-1}r]_{4N}$. As $\chi_N(A_t)=-1$, the change of variable $r\mapsto A_t r$ gives **exactly**
$$\sum_r\chi_N(r) Z_N(r)(Z_N(v_t^+(r))+Z_N(v_t^-(r)))=0.$$

For either orientation, write $P^{\pm}Z=J^{-1}\sum_{t\nmid N} Z_N(v_t^{\pm}(r))$ and similarly for other functions. Our averaging measure is
$$\int h=X^{-2}\sum_{N\text{ eligible}}\sum_{r} Z_N(r)h(N,r).$$
It has bounded mass absolutely, by the prime number theorem or Chebyshev applied to two unconstrained primes. For fixed $w\ge\max(13,g)$ put $W=\prod_{\ell\le w}\ell$ (all such products over primes), $H=4gW$, $c_w=W/\phi(W)$, and replace the partner by the model
$$Z_N^0(v)=c_w^2 1_{(v(N-v),W)=1}1_{0<v<\eta N}.$$
The comparison function is
$$K_w(N)=\frac{\eta}{4} c_w^2\prod_{\ell\le w,\ \ell\nmid N}(1-2/\ell).$$
Here $\ell\mid N$ costs **no** partner condition at a physical endpoint: multiplication or inverse multiplication by a unit preserves being a unit at $\ell$. For every even $N$, $K_w(N)\ge c_\eta>0$, since $\prod_{\ell>2}(1-2/\ell)(1-1/\ell)^{-2}>0$. We prove
$$\lim_{w\to\infty}\limsup_{X\to\infty}\int|P^\pm(Z-Z^0)|^2=0,\qquad
\lim_{X\to\infty}\int|P^\pm Z^0-K_w|^2=0 \quad(w\text{ fixed}).\tag{1}$$
The paired assertions in the two orientations are essential.

## 2. Uniform prime replacement in mean square

The diagonal $t=u$ in any squared average costs $O_w(L^6/J)=o(1)$ even by trivial weights. For distinct $t,u$ put $A=gt^2$, $B=gu^2$, $M=gt^2u^2$. In the expansion of the centered product $(Z-Z^0)(v_t)(Z-Z^0)(v_u)$, all four terms have the **same** real window and the same six small-prime masks after taking their prime main terms. We give the details and control their large-prime discrepancies uniformly as $w\to\infty$.

**Outgoing charts.** Write the common endpoint as $r=\theta N$, with $0<\theta<\eta$. For the two marked outputs write $A r-4bN$ and $B r-4dN$; their ratios to $N$ must lie in $(0,\eta)$. The chart's real width $\lambda$ in $\theta$ is at most $1/A$ and, if positive, $\gg1/(AB)$, by its rational endpoints. There are $O(A)$ charts, with $O(1)$ choices of $d$ for a given $b$ and conversely. The rational slopes of the six forms in the free variable are
$$0,1,4b/A,(4b+1)/A,4d/B,(4d+1)/B.$$
The free-variable coefficients are units away from $2gtu$. Thus primes outside $2gtu$ at which slopes can coincide divide the product $\Delta$ of
$$4b,4b+1,A-4b,A-4b-1,\quad4d,4d+1,B-4d,B-4d-1,$$
$$t^2(4d+j)-u^2(4b+i)\quad(0\le i,j\le1).$$
Omit slope duplications: each zero one-variable factor occurs for $O(1)$ overlapping charts; each zero cross factor occurs $O(g+1)$ times since $t^2d-u^2b$ specifies $b\bmod t^2$. Their normalized total real area is $O_g(T^{-2})$ per ordered prime pair. Also omit $t\mid 4b(4b+1)$ or $u\mid4d(4d+1)$, total area $O_g(T^{-1})$. Afterward no form is identically zero at $t,u$. Actual conditions $tu\nmid N$ here and below mean $t\nmid N$ and $u\nmid N$; in the forward sum one may drop these restrictions at cost $O_w(L^6/T)$ by counting all endpoints on such centers.

**Incoming charts.** Write the common endpoint as $y\in(0,\eta N)$. Its two marked preimages are $(y+4bN)/A,(y+4dN)/B$ in $(0,\eta N)$. Here $0\le b<A\eta/4$, $0\le d<B\eta/4$ and $b\equiv d\pmod g$. Boundary offsets where either input's upper bound truncates the $y$ interval have total relative area $O_g(T^{-2})$: at most two boundary values on either side. They can be discarded, leaving full $y$ width $\eta N$. The CRT gives a unique $e\bmod M$ with $e\equiv b\pmod A$, $e\equiv d\pmod B$, and $y=Mz-4eN$. The real width in $z/N$ is thus $\lambda=\eta/M$. There are $O(M)$ such charts. On the CRT lattice the six free-variable coefficients are among $\pm M,\pm M/A,\pm M/B$. Outside $2gtu$ the six slopes can be compared in the original $y/N$ coordinate as
$$0,1,-4b,A-4b,-4d,B-4d.$$
Take $\Delta$ to comprise the same eight one-variable factors above and the four cross terms $4(d-b)+c$, $c\in\{0,A,-B,A-B\}$. A zero factor has $O(A+B)$ possible offsets, relative area $O_g(T^{-2})$. A form identically zero modulo $t$ or $u$ occurs only in an $O_g(1/T)$ fraction of the charts: for $y,N-y$, this requires $4b$ or $4b+1$ divisible by $t$ (analogously $4d,4d+1$ by $u$). For the first marked preimage its free coefficient is $u^2$ and its $N$ coefficient is $4(b-e)/A$, so modulo $u$ the two excluded possibilities are $4(b-d)\equiv0,A\pmod u$; interchange $t,u$ for the other preimage. Counting residue classes among $b\equiv d\bmod g$ gives the claim. Finally, on the CRT lattice a center with $t\mid N$ forces $t\mid y$ and $t\mid N-y$, incompatible with the base prime pair unless $N=2t$, outside range; similarly for $u$. Thus dropping the coprimality restriction here introduces no mass, even though multiple offsets could otherwise describe a residue.

All discards have $o_{g,w}(1)$ effect with the crude bound $O_w(L^6)$: the lattice count in a chart is its area plus $O_g(X\log^{O(1)}X)$ (sum of the explicit intervals over integer $N$), and there are only polylogarithmically many charts. A prime argument equal to any fixed $\ell\le w$ or any proper prime power in a true-prime slot can likewise be removed when applying the prime theorem, at cost $O_{g,w}(X^{3/2}\log^{O(1)}X)$ per chart before division by $X^2$. For instance each form has nonzero free-variable coefficient and the number of candidate powers $\le2X$ is $O(\sqrt X\log X)$. All kernels/coordinates, including $z$, lie in a fixed multiple of the box $[-X,X]^2$.

**Cancellation of local factors.** For each nondegenerate chart, its main real volume divided by $X^2$ is $3\lambda/2$. Partition its lattice variables $(N,z)$ (or $(N,r)$) modulo $H$, impose the target eligibility and model masks, and apply Bienvenu's Theorem 1.3 to the 2, 4 or 6 genuine forms. They have pairwise nonparallel linear parts. The coefficients are polylogarithmic and each fixed-progression subchart has volume $\gg_{g,w}X^2/\log^C X$; boundary translations do not change its real area. Nonstrict-boundary conventions differ by $O(X\log^{O(1)}X)$. If a form is zero modulo a small prime on that progression then the associated six-mask main term is zero and large prime points are absent; $\ell\le w$ outside a model slot is handled by the same exclusion. Beyond $w$ the large-prime factors are positive by the union bound below. The partial weights at primes $\ell\le w$ are exactly a factor $(1-1/\ell)^{-1}$ per actual slot surviving there and the same factor per replacement slot from $c_w$. After summing the fixed residue classes, the common small factor is therefore
$$S_{\le w}=\mathbb E_{n,z\bmod H}1_{n\text{ eligible mod }H}
  \prod_{\ell\le w}(1-1/\ell)^{-6}
   1_{\text{all six forms are nonzero mod }\ell}.$$
Only the local small-prime eligibility conditions are meant in this average. For any subset $I$ of actual slots in the expansion (always including the base pair), the remaining product is $\prod_{\ell>w}\beta_{\ell,I}$ for the linear forms in those slots, **unconditioned** at $\ell>w$ since the progression change is invertible there. In two variables, each nonzero homogeneous form vanishes on fraction $1/\ell$ and distinct lines intersect only at the origin. Consequently, for $k=|I|\le6$, $\beta_{\ell,I}\ge(1-k/\ell)(1-1/\ell)^{-k}=1-O(\ell^{-2})$; away from $gtu\Delta$ it is also $1+O(\ell^{-2})$. At other primes above $w$ it is at most $1+O(1/\ell)$. Write $F_D(h)=\prod_{\ell\mid |h|,\ \ell\in D}(1+C/\ell)$ for $h\ne0$ and sufficiently large absolute $C$, with $F=F_{\text{all}}$. These estimates give
$$S_{\le w}\ll_g F(\Delta),\qquad
\left|\prod_{\ell>w}\beta_{\ell,I}-1\right|
 \ll (1/w+1/T)F_{>w}(\Delta)+F_{>w}(\Delta)-1.\tag{2}$$
For the former simply drop eligibility and average all six lines locally. Fixed $2,g$ cost $O_g(1)$ even where a small local obstruction is present, and other exceptional lines are covered by $\Delta$. The prime asymptotic has uniform **relative** error $o_{g,w}(1)$; the following mean bounds ensure its summed error remains $o_{g,w}(1)$, not a product with an unbounded coefficient factor.

**Determinant tails on the actual chart measure.** For each fixed integer $h_0\ge1$, squarefree expansion and $\lfloor Z/d\rfloor\le Z/d$ give
$$\sum_{1\le n\le Z}F(n)^{h_0}\ll_{h_0}Z,\qquad
\sum_{1\le n\le Z}(F_{>w}(n)^{h_0}-1)\ll_{h_0} Z\sum_{\ell>w}\ell^{-2}.$$
These apply to *each* listed nonzero factor on chart measure $\lambda$, uniformly in the prime pair: forward, a one-variable factor has size $O(A)$ and bounded multiplicity; every shifted cross term has size $O(T^2)$ by interval overlap, multiplicity at most $g+1$, and $\lambda\ll1/A$. Inverse, each one-variable argument has $O(A)$ possible values with $O(B/g)$ or $O(A/g)$ multiplicity, normalized by $1/M$. Each cross difference is divisible by $g$ and after dropping that fixed factor has size $O(T^2)$ with $O(A)$ multiplicity, again normalized by $1/M$. Thus by Hölder all fixed $F(\Delta)$ moments on this measure are bounded by $O_g(1)$ (constant depends also on the moment). Furthermore $F_{>w}(\Delta)-1$ tends to zero in every fixed moment uniformly: telescope the product upper bound over its finitely many listed factors and apply Hölder, using $(x-1)^j\le x^j-1$ for $x\ge1$. In particular the chart average of $F(\Delta)(F_{>w}(\Delta)-1)$ tends to zero. Therefore (2), summed in each pair of switches, permits replacing the large-prime product in **each** expansion term by 1 with averaged error $o_{w\to\infty;g}(1)+o_{X\to\infty;g,w}(1)$. The common small-prime factors then cancel with coefficients $1,-1,-1,1$. This proves the first half of (1).

## 3. Mixing the finite partner model

Now keep $w$ fixed. Condition on the classes of $N$ and the physical base endpoint $r$ modulo $H=4gW$ (call the latter $y$ in the inverse calculation). Classes with either base leg $0\bmod\ell$ for some $\ell\mid W$ may be omitted by the small-prime estimate. In each remaining class the two-form prime density in $(N,r)$ is a fixed positive constant, independent of the real angle $\theta=r/N\in(0,\eta)$: after the residue substitution the two local forms outside $W$ are independent linear coordinates, so all their factors equal 1. The constant per unit area includes the $H^{-2}$ covolume. Below, expectation means divide by the mass of this fixed class (or use its positive constant times the full rectangular-angle area); $K_w(N)$ is constant there.

**Forward.** The one-partner weight is $c_w^2$ precisely when $b=\lfloor A\theta/4\rfloor$ satisfies
$$\{A\theta/4\}<\eta/4,\qquad
(A r-4bN)(N-A r+4bN)\ne0\pmod\ell\quad(\ell\mid W).$$
Thus it is a bounded step function of $A\theta/(4H)\pmod1$, with its finite list of shapes depending only on the fixed classes and $A\bmod H$. Its Haar mean on the circle is $K_w(N)$: the fractional-window factor is $\eta/4$, the digit $b\bmod H$ is uniform and independent of the fractional part, primes $\ell\mid N$ impose no restriction, and at every $\ell\nmid N$ there are two distinct excluded residues of $b$ ($\ell$ is odd). Here $A$ is a unit at every $\ell\mid N$ in $W$, since these primes include neither $g$ nor $t$.

For one or two partner marks the physical base pair can be replaced by its constant class density before integrating these step functions on the uniform angle $0<\theta<\eta$. Indeed cutting at both offset and acceptance boundaries gives $O(A+B)$ polygons with widths either zero or $\gg1/(AB)$, on each of which the two true base forms have the same local factors; apply Bienvenu with uniform relative error. For a single mark use just its own boundaries. Dropping $t,u\mid N$ exclusions in the physical averages costs $o_w(1)$ as above. For two distinct primes, the two circle phases with frequencies $gt^2/(4H),gu^2/(4H)$ are jointly equidistributed on average over $t,u$: for each fixed pair of integral Fourier indices $(h,k)\ne(0,0)$, the pairs with $|h t^2+k u^2|\le\sqrt T$ are $O_{h,k}(J)$ or absent for large $T$, since successive $|k|u^2$ are separated by $\gg_k T$ when $k\ne0$. Else the normalized angular Fourier integral is $O_{g,H,\eta}(T^{-1/2})$. For one phase every nonzero frequency tends to infinity. Approximation of the finitely many interval-step rectangles by upper and lower trigonometric polynomials (equivalently continuous majorants/minorants followed by uniform Fourier approximation) yields the area products. The bound on bad pairs is valid even after selecting the shapes by $t,u\bmod H$; no distribution theorem for those residues is needed. Hence the averaged first and off-diagonal second model moments are $K_w$ and $K_w^2$ up to $o_{g,w}(1)$.

**Inverse.** Conditional on $N,y\bmod H$, each offset in an inverse chart satisfies $4bN\equiv-y\pmod g$, fixing one class $b_0\bmod g$. A second offset $d$ has the same fixed class. Apart from the boundary loss $O_g(1/A+1/B)$ in chart area, the two may range **independently** over
$$0\le b<A\eta/4,\ b\equiv b_0\pmod g;\qquad
0\le d<B\eta/4,\ d\equiv b_0\pmod g .$$
In the fixed endpoint class the additional lattice conditions on $y$ are $y\equiv-4bN\pmod{t^2}$ and $y\equiv-4dN\pmod{u^2}$. For each $N$ they give an exact covolume multiplier $t^2u^2$ in the free endpoint; the real $y$-window is the original one. More explicitly write $N=n_0+Hn$ and $y=y_0+H(M'z+e'n+f')$, $M'=t^2u^2$, solving the two congruences for the coefficients $e',f'\bmod M'$. One can keep $z$ in $[-C X,C X]$; all coefficients remain polylogarithmic. The two base forms are nonparallel, the area is that of the full fixed class divided by $M'$, and the local factors away from $Htu$ are unchanged by this invertible affine substitution. At $t$ both forms reduce to unit multiples of $N$ unless $t\mid4b(4b+1)$, a proportion $O(1/t)$ of the offsets; for the good ones the factor is $(1-1/t)^{-1}=1+O(1/t)$. At $u$ use $d$ instead. Bad offsets make a base leg a multiple of the corresponding prime, and have negligible total by the same coarse bound as before. Thus every remaining offset pair has the same base mass divided by $t^2u^2$ up to a uniform relative $o_{g,w}(1)$, by Bienvenu. With just one mark divide by $t^2$ and use the single congruence. As before actual $t,u$ dividing $N$ cannot contribute on these inverse lattices.

At the marked input $(y+4bN)/A$, each small-prime test is determined by $y,N\bmod H$ and $b=b_0+g j\bmod gW$ (since $gW\mid H$). Incrementing $j$ by 1 changes the input mod $\ell\mid W$ by $4N/t^2$. At $\ell\mid N$ the input is a unit because $A$ is, and its complement is too. At each $\ell\nmid N$ this increment is a unit, **including $\ell=g$**: division by $g$ used one extra digit of $y,N$. The joint small-prime acceptance proportion as $j$ ranges mod $W$ is $\prod_{\ell\le w,\ell\nmid N}(1-2/\ell)$. The interval of $j$ has length $t^2\eta/4+O(1)$, so its measure after division by $t^2$ tends to the archimedean factor $\eta/4$, with residue-class errors $O_{w}(1/t^2)$. Since $b,d$ independently range over their intervals in the same $g$-class the product calculation factorizes. Restoring $c_w^2$ per mark yields the same averaged first and second moments $K_w$ and $K_w^2$. This handles the inverse without assuming that the modular inverse multipliers are angularly equidistributed. In either orientation the diagonal costs $o_w(1)$. Expanding the square and summing over finitely many endpoint classes proves the second half of (1).

## 4. From reversal to the two signs

Triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz in the bounded-mass measure in (1) yield
$$\lim_{w\to\infty}\limsup_{X\to\infty}\int |P^\pm Z-K_w|=0.$$
Sum the signed reversal over $t$, use $|\chi_N|\le1$ and the triangle inequality **center by center**. The two copies of $K_w$ thus give
$$X^{-2}\sum_{N\text{ eligible}}\left|\sum_r\chi_N(r)Z_N(r)\right|=o_g(1)$$
because $\inf_{N,w}K_w(N)>0$. For an arbitrary fixed finite menu of primes $g\equiv1\pmod{12}$, Markov's inequality gives the same convergence **in density** on its union of eligible targets. By CRT the relative density among even integers missed by the entire menu is $\prod_g(g+1)/(2g)$. Enlarge the menu *after* the dyadic limit to make this arbitrarily small. In particular the signed weighted sum divided by $N$ tends to zero in density.

For clarity the needed ordinary unsigned lower bound follows from the same prime theorem, with no hypothesis on the binary problem. Fix $W$ as above and an even target class. In a dyadic slab of relative width $1/\log X$, normalize $\sum_{0<r<\eta N}\vartheta(r)\vartheta(N-r)$ by $\eta$ times the left edge of the slab; using $\eta N$ instead changes it by $1+o(1)$. Its conditional mean is $(1+o_w(1))\mu$, where $\mu=\prod_{\ell\mid W} (1-1/\ell)^{-2}(1-h_\ell/\ell)$ and $h_\ell=1$ or 2 according as $\ell\mid N$ or not. Indeed beyond $w$ the averages of the fiber factors at $n\bmod\ell$ are exactly 1. Its conditional second moment is $(1+o_w(1))\mu^2\prod_{\ell>w}(1+O(\ell^{-3}))$: replicate the two forms at independent $r,r'$ over the same $N$; the local variance comes from $n=0$ of probability $1/\ell$ differing from the other residues by $O(1/\ell)$. All four directions are distinct in three variables, and the theorem applies also on these slabs. Since $\mu\gg1$, Chebyshev and then $w\to\infty$ give a density-one lower bound $\gg N$ on the ordinary true prime-log mass, for both windows. Its ratio to $N$ is also bounded in probability, by the unconditional first-moment bound for the two unrestricted prime legs. Discarding endpoints within $N/\log^4 N$ of either end costs $O(N/\log^4N)$ unweighted and $O(N/\log^2N)$ weighted at **every** center; on the remaining endpoints the two logarithms are each $\log N+O(\log\log N)$. Consequently the signed unweighted count divided by $N/\log^2 N$ tends to zero in density, while the ordinary unweighted count is $\gg N/\log^2N$ in density one. This yields the asserted proportions (and the negligible diagonal does not affect them).

**Where averaging is essential.** Both prime comparisons use two independent lattice variables, the *target* and the base endpoint. Freezing a prescribed $N$ makes the required prime forms parallel in the remaining variable. Even more simply, if an individual $N$ has no pair, the signed identity and this entire pair-weighted measure at that $N$ are identically zero. The argument supplies neither a representation nor a sign constraint for an individual possible exceptional center.
