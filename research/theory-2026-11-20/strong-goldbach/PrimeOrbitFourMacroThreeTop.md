# The four-macro maximum limiting weight has multiplicity at least three

Use the six-label fixed-seed near-smooth hypotheses of [the four-intermediate theorem](PrimeOrbitFourMacroNearSmoothIntermediate.md), and fix a subsequence where the six logarithmic weights converge and exactly four are positive. The maximum W of those four satisfies W≤1/2 by [the above-half exclusion](PrimeOrbitFourMacroNoAboveHalf.md). **At least three of the four have weight W.** This is about equality of *logarithmic weights*, not of primes or their ratios; the branches with three or four top weights remain open.

The fixed left-null balance already forces a second comparable macro. If exactly two are of maximal weight, name them M>K (fixing indices after extraction); the other four labels are o(K) with a power gap. The [top-ratio result](PrimeOrbitFourMacroTwoLines.md) establishes, also at W≤1/2, a fixed odd integer r=p^k with M/K→r and e=M−rK=O(M²/N). Since e is a nonzero even integer (distinct primes when r=1, divisibility otherwise), W<1/2 is impossible. At W=1/2 the same bound forces M,K≫sqrt(N), with an implied positive constant. We exclude this last case.

The fixed left-null vector l has both l_M and l_K nonzero. M has a unique parent because no two smaller labels can differ by a nonzero multiple of M. Thus K cannot be its parent by lA=0, regardless of the parent's multiplicity. If M were K's only parent it would similarly force l_M=0. If M and a smaller label s both parent K, (M−s)/K would be an even integer tending to the odd integer r, impossible as s=o(K). Hence the two tops each have a single lower parent, i for M and j for K. Let m=A_iM,n=A_jK be their positive multiplicities, both at most two since M,K≫sqrt(N). From CA=C, g_M=m g_i and g_K=n g_j for the fixed 1-eigen Gale columns. These columns are nonzero, and no three are proportional, so i,j are distinct and the two pair directions distinct. Killing each direction in turn shows i↔M, j↔K and U↔V for the remaining lower labels, exactly as in [the matching argument](PrimeOrbitFourMacroMatchedOffset.md): the surviving pair has entries of the same sign, while the remaining two must be of the opposite sign; both two-element sign supports must contain cycles. This uses proportionality, not equality of entries.

If m=1 or n=1, apply the single-pair congruence from [the above-half exclusion](PrimeOrbitFourMacroNoAboveHalf.md) to that particular pair: there is a left eigenrow with equal positive entries on the multiplicity-one pair, zero entries at both members of the other top pair (regardless of that pair's multiplicity), and fixed negative entries on U,V. Hence U,V→∞. The proof uses only U,V=o(K), M/K→r and M=o(N), all still valid here. If m=n=1 it further gives a common multiplier for all three pairs and contradicts the presence of a unique fixed seed, by exactly the same proof.

If m=2, then N−i=P M² where P is a bounded positive integer because M≫sqrt(N); thus by fixing a subsequence P is constant. The factorization would force P to be a power of p, possibly one, but boundedness suffices. Likewise for n=2 write N−j=Q K² with fixed Q. Each squared row also gives M²/N=O(1), hence the nonzero e is fixed after another extraction.

If m=n=2, then Q=P r² by dividing both factorizations by K² and using M/K→r. Thus j−i=P(M−rK)(M+rK) has size ≫K, contrary to i,j=o(K).

If m=2,n=1, then N≡j mod K and M≡e mod K, so j−i≡P e² mod K. Since i,j=o(K) and P,e are fixed, j−i=P e² exactly. If m=1,n=2, then N≡i mod M and rK≡−e mod M. Reducing r²(N−j)=Q(rK)² modulo M gives r²(i−j)=Q e² exactly by the same size bound. In both cases the one multiplicity-one pair forced U,V→∞, so the fixed seed p is one of i,j. Their now-fixed difference contradicts divergence of each nonseed. All multiplicity cases are exhausted.
