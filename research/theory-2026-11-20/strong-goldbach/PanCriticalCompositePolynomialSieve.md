# A polynomial physical sieve for the complete both-composite row at the original Pan cutoff

Let \(P\) be even, \(I=[P/3,2P/3]\), \(X=P/3\), \(L=\log P\), \(r=P-n\), and \(Q=\sqrt P/L^{20}\) be the original fixed square-root split. Keep \(A(n),A^*(r),B(n),B^*(r),S(P)\) from the [critical roughness proof](PanCriticalPhysicalRoughness.md); in particular the starred coefficients exclude divisors sharing a prime with \(P\). On the **physical** row where both \(n,r\) are composite, write \(C\) for the sum of \(A(n)A^*(r)\), and \(C_z\) for its restriction to both \(n,r\) free of prime factors \(\le z\). Fix \(\eta=1/1000\). For \(z=P^\epsilon\) put \(s=\eta/\epsilon\), \(u=1/(2\epsilon)\), and \(b(u)=e^{-\gamma}\int_0^{u-1}\rho(t)\,dt\) for Dickman's function.

**Theorem.** For sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
 C=XS(P)+O(P L^{-1/5}),\qquad
 \frac{C_z}{XS(P)}=b(u)^2+O(u^4s^{-s/2})+o_\epsilon(1).       \tag{1}
\]
The constant in the displayed big O is absolute. In particular for every tolerance \(\delta>0\), one can fix a **positive exponent** \(\epsilon\) and then for every sufficiently large even target preserve this whole signed row to error at most \(\delta XS(P)\) while requiring *both* inputs to be \(P^\epsilon\)-rough. This is the actual composite–composite part of the original raw remainder, not just factored terms in its divisor sum. There is no claim that the same polynomial restriction loses little in either true prime–composite orientation, that the absolute value inside the deleted physical sum is small, or that the Goldbach count is positive.

**Pass to short–short.** On true central primes the applicable short coefficient vanishes. Composite prime powers on either input, or \(r_c\) a higher prime power for the target-unit part \(r_c\) of \(r\), have negligible cost as proved in the critical roughness paper. On the rough inputs, any remaining nonunit \((r,P)>1\) is divisible by some \(\ell\mid P,\ell>z\); the number of such inputs is \(O(PL/z)\), and any individual divisor weight is \(P^{o(1)}\). Thus \(C_z\) is the rough sum of \(B(n)B^*(r)\) up to a power saving, even with the asymmetric original coefficient. Without roughness, use §2 of the critical roughness proof with \(\mathcal E=\varnothing\): the extra \(B(n)\Lambda(r_c)\) with \(r=g p\) composite has \(g>1\) and its total is \(O(PL^{-1/5})\). Indeed the \(g\le P^{.05}\) principal vanishes by \(\sum_{e\mid\operatorname{rad}g}\mu(e)=0\), and the same error proof permits keeping the \(g>1\) restriction (in the large-\(t\) blocks it is automatic). The unconditional first equality of (1) follows from the unmasked short–short calculation there.

**A signed-discrepancy sieve for the bilinear count.** Smooth the sharp physical \(I\) first with a nonnegative bump \(W(n/P)\) supported on \(I/P\), equal to one except within \(L^{-K}\) of the endpoints, with its fixed-order derivatives bounded by powers of \(L\). The error against the *actual* rough weights is \(O_H(PL^{-H})\) on choosing \(K\) large in terms of \(H\): use \(|B B^*|\le L^2\tau(n)\tau(r)\) and two applications of Cauchy–Schwarz with \(\sum_{n\le P}\tau(n)^4\ll PL^{15}\). No smoothing error is paid separately on each of polynomially many sieve divisors. The corresponding smooth length is \(X'=P\widehat W(0)=X+O(PL^{-K})\).

In the short sums take \(d,e\le Q\), both \(z\)-rough, right \((e,P)=1\). A compatible pair requires \((d,e)=1\). On its CRT progression sieve \(nr\) against all small primes using upper/lower beta weights \(\lambda_w^\pm\), level \(D=P^\eta\), supported on squarefree small-prime products, coefficients bounded by one. The local excluded density \(g(\ell)\) is \(2/\ell\) at \(\ell\nmid P\) and \(1/\ell\) at \(\ell\mid P\). The product \(V_2=\prod_{\ell\le z}(1-g(\ell))\) satisfies the uniform dimension-two condition by Mertens (the bad-prime factor only decreases dimension). The [fundamental lemma as stated in Kurlberg–Lester–Rosenzweig, Theorem 2.2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/superscars-for-arithmetic-point-scatters-ii/3CCB969E058792C1803466AE0EF29136) yields
\(\sum_w\lambda_w^\pm g(w)=V_2(1+O(s^{-s/2}))\) for sufficiently large \(s\), uniformly in the target.

A crucial distinction is that the beyond-square-root dispersion theorem used next is **signed**, not a sum of absolute discrepancies for each pair \((d,e)\). If the two beta estimates on the positive progression count are \(N^-_{d,e}\le N_{d,e}\le N^+_{d,e}\), the signed sum with coefficient \(c_{d,e}=\mu(d)\log d\,\mu(e)\log e\) differs from \(\sum c_{d,e}N^+_{d,e}\) by at most \(\sum |c_{d,e}|(N^+_{d,e}-N^-_{d,e})\). **Both** the signed sequence and its absolute value factor in the two variables. Thus it suffices to compute the signed majorant and the unsigned upper-minus-lower sums separately by bilinear dispersion; it is not necessary or legitimate to replace an absolute discrepancy on every \((d,e)\) by that theorem.

More precisely, for one \(w\le D\) assign each prime of \(w\) not dividing \(P\) to either the left or right input; the options are disjoint since that prime cannot divide both \(n,r\). At \(\ell\mid w,\ell\mid P\) assign it **only to the left**, since the two conditions coincide. This gives \(w=w_1w_2\) with \((w_2,P)=1\). The new divisors are \(b=d w_1,c=e w_2\). They still carry independent coefficients supported on multiples of \(w_1,w_2\), bounded by \(L\) each; for fixed \(w_1,w_2\) division recovers \(d,e\) uniquely. The right modulus is coprime to \(P\), and incompatible \((b,c)>1\) are automatically absent. On each smoothed sum the Poisson zero frequency is exactly \(X'/(bc)\) on the compatible pairs, times their coefficients.

For completeness, the signed smoothed discrepancy from those zero modes is \(O(P^{1-.007})\) per fixed allocation for all sufficiently large \(P\) (constants may depend on \(K\)). Dyadize \(b\sim B,c\sim C\), with upper lengths at most \(2P^{.501}\). If \(BC<P^{.99}\), each smooth CRT count differs from its integral by \(O(1)\) for a bump of bounded total variation (choose one monotone transition at each endpoint), so the error over this dyad is \(\ll L^2 BC\). If \(BC\ge P^{.99}\) but \(H=BC/P<1\), Poisson decay may simply be used to bound by the estimate at \(H=1\): the prefactor \(H^{-1}\) is canceled by taking sufficiently many Fourier derivatives, since the nonzero frequency scale starts at \(F\ge1\); derivatives of \(W\) cost only fixed logs. In this case both lengths are at least \(P^{.489-o(1)}\). Directly from the trilinear bound below, at \(F=1\) with \(H^{-1}(1+1/H)^{1/2}\) absorbed by Fourier decay, its two exponents before small slack are at most \(.85(1)+.25(.502)=.9755\) and \(.875(1)+.125(.502)=.93775\) respectively. For \(H\ge1\), the uniform nonzero-mode bound from [§3, equations (4)–(5) of the double-divisor proof](PanSupercriticalShoulder.md) is \(P^{1+o(1)}\) times the larger of
\[
 P^{[14(u_1+v_1)+5\max(u_1,v_1)-17]/20},
 \qquad P^{[8\max(u_1,v_1)+7\min(u_1,v_1)-8]/8},
\]
where \(u_1=\log B/L,v_1=\log C/L\); the \(o(1)\) here can be replaced by any fixed positive slack from the imported inequality. Both exponents are below \(-.022\) since \(u_1,v_1\le.501+o(1)\). The input is the published [Bettin–Chandee, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.00769), applied with numerator \(\pm P\) at *both* signs; the arbitrary single-variable sequences allow the constrained multiples here. The Mellin separation and frequency summation do not use Möbius structure or lower bounds on coefficient norms. The cited proof at \(H\ge1\) therefore applies unchanged. Sum the \(O(L^2)\) dyads. Finally there are at most \(\sum_{w\le D}2^{\omega(w)}\ll D(1+\log D)\) allocations, so their total signed discrepancies, including those for \(|c|\) and either choice of beta weights, save a power of \(P\). This power room is why it is legitimate to insert a *polynomial* physical sieve here rather than a fixed polylog sieve at the critical cutoff. No analogous beyond-square-root estimate for true primes in all these moduli is being used.

**Main terms with their actual signs.** Summing the zero modes over the small-prime allocations gives exactly \(X'g(w)/(de)\) for each original compatible \((d,e)\). The preceding sandwich therefore yields
\[
 C_z=X' V_2\!\sum_{\substack{d,e\le Q,\ P^-(de)>z\\(d,e)=1,\ (e,P)=1}}
       \frac{\mu(d)\log d\,\mu(e)\log e}{de}
 +O\left(X' V_2 s^{-s/2}\left[\sum_{d\le Q,P^-(d)>z}\frac{\mu^2(d)\log d}{d}\right]^2\right)
 +O_H(PL^{-H}).
\]
At fixed \(\epsilon\), \(u_P=\log Q/\log z\to u\). By Mertens the unsigned bracket is \(O(u^2\log z)\); denominator coprimality and target omissions in the signed term cost \(o_\epsilon((\log z)^2)\), by marking a common prime (sum of reciprocal squares above \(z\)) or one of \(P\)'s primes above \(z\) (harmonic sum \(O(L/z)\)). The exact signed rough-divisor integral, proved by prime-factor simplex expansion in [the global rough transfer §3](PanGlobalRoughTransfer.md), gives
\[
 -\frac1{\log z}\sum_{d\le Q,P^-(d)>z}\frac{\mu(d)\log d}d
       \longrightarrow \int_0^{u-1}\rho(t)\,dt.
\]
The moving endpoint \(u_P\to u\) is harmless by continuity of the finite signed simplex integrals, or by enclosing the last thin strip in an unsigned simplex integral. Direct local multiplication gives \(V_2=(1+o_\epsilon(1))S(P)e^{-2\gamma}/(\log z)^2\) uniformly in the even target. As \(b(u)\to1\) and \(u^4s^{-s/2}\to0\) for \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), these calculations prove (1).

**Limit.** The remaining issue for polynomial roughness of the *whole* Pan remainder now lies in the two actual prime–composite rows, which coincide by reflection on central true prime pairs with composite partners (aside from negligible prime powers and target-prime exceptions). The true prime–prime atom itself is never deleted. No comparison of its size against this positive composite base has been shown.
