# Two-step counting at a fixed Goldbach target, and a genuine-prime parity obstruction to its lower bound

Let \(N\) be even and large. On the odd prime vertices \(V_N=\{3\le p\le N-3:(p,N)=1\}\) put an edge \(p\to q\) for each prime \(q\mid N-p\). Every such \(q\) is again a vertex. This is the reverse orientation of [Bożek's factorization graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900). Write \(U_j(N,S)\) for vertices with no path of length at most \(j\) to \(S\), including length zero. Let \(R(N)\) count ordered prime starts \(p\) with \(N-p\) prime, counting a diagonal once.

## 1. Two steps with a subpower menu (all targets)

Put \(L=\log N,\ L_2=\log L,\ F=\log L_2,\ Z=\exp(L/F)\), \(S=V_N\cap[1,Z]\). Then
\[
 |\#U_2(N,S)-R(N)|\ll E(N):=\frac{NL_2^3 F^4(1+\log F)}{L^4}.
\]
No lower bound for \(\#U_2\) is assumed or obtained. Compared with the [three-step estimator](PrimeOrbitThreeStepPairEstimator.md) this needs a much larger menu.

Let \(H=U_1(N,S)\). Its primes \(q>Z\) have \(N-q\) free of prime factors up to \(Z\). For \(Z\le Y\le N\), sieve \(q\le Y\) with the primality root 0 for \(5\le t\le Y^{.01}\), and the additional root \(N\bmod t\) for \(t\le z=\min(Z,Y^{.01})\), with prime \(t\) throughout. Primes \(q\le Y^{.01}\) cost at most \(Y^{.01}\). The elementary uniform Selberg upper bound proved in the linked note (at most three roots, error \(O(Y^{1/2})\)) and Mertens give
\[
 H(Y)\ll Y L_2/(\log Y\log Z).
\]
Indeed \(\log z\ge .01\log Z\), and two-root collisions at \(t\mid N\) cost \(O(L_2)\): for any \(1\le b\le N^2\), \(\prod_{t\mid b}(1-1/t)^{-1}\ll L_2\) by splitting at \(L^2\). The sieve and small-prime errors are absorbed uniformly. Partial summation over \([Z,N]\) gives \(\sum_{q\in H}1/q\ll L_2(1+\log F)/\log Z=o(1)\). The semigroup generated by \(H\), **excluding 1**, has reciprocal mass at most \(\prod_{q\in H}(1-1/q)^{-1}-1\ll L_2(1+\log F)/\log Z\).

A false survivor \(x\in U_2\) has composite \(N-x=mq\) with \(q\in H\) and \(m>1\) in that semigroup. For each \(m\), set \(Y=N/m>Z\). The two primes \(q,x=N-mq\) exceed \(Z\), and \(N-q\) is \(Z\)-rough. To \(z=\min(Z,Y^{.01})\) the three forbidden residues for \(q\) are \(0,N/m,N\bmod t\); all \(t\le z\) are coprime to \(m\). There is one root when \(t\mid N\), two when \(t\nmid N,\ t\mid m-1\), and otherwise three. Relative to a generic three-root product the collision loss is \(O(L_2^2)\) from \(N(m-1)\ne0\); local ratios after removal of \((1-1/t)^{-2}\) at bad primes contribute at most an absolutely convergent product. Thus each \(m\) costs \(\ll (N/m)L_2^2/\log^3 Z\). Sum the reciprocal bound. Every ordered endpoint whose pair avoids \(S\) survives, except the nonunit diagonal; at most \(2|S|+1=o(E(N))\) endpoints are lost. This proves the assertion, including the upper bound \(O(E(N))\) on false survivors alone.

## 2. A parity mark invisible to near-full divisor tests (almost all targets)

Keep **all** primes, edges, factorizations and seed tests as they are. Only when summing over *starting* vertices assign multiplicity
\[
 w_N^\pm(p)=1\pm\lambda(N-p),\qquad \lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}.
\]
These nonnegative weights are 0 or 2. In particular \(w_N^+\) gives every genuine prime endpoint weight zero, at every target. It follows that the \(w_N^+\)-weighted two-step count is \(O(E(N))\). The \(w_N^-\)-weighted count is at least \(2(R(N)-2|S|-1)\). The analogous assertions hold for the earlier three-step menus with their respective false-positive bounds.

Fix \(0<a<1\) and \(A,B,C,H_0>0\). For dyadic \(X\) write \(D=X/\exp((\log X)^a),\ T=(\log X)^{H_0}\). Except for \(O(X/\log^A X)\) integers \(N\in[X,2X]\), the following holds for every even \(N\) in the remaining set:
\[
 \sum_{d\le D}\tau(d)^C
 \max_{\substack{h\le T,\ c\bmod h\\ I\ {\rm interval}}}
 \left|\sum_{\substack{p\in V_N,\ d\mid N-p\\(N-p)/d\in I,\ (N-p)/d\equiv c\bmod h}}
                (w_N^+(p)-1)\right| \ll X/\log^B X. \tag{1}
\]
The same absolute discrepancy holds for \(w_N^-\). All constants may depend on the five fixed parameters. Intervals can also localize \(p\), since \(p=N-dk\). The modulus, cofactor class, interval, and signed coefficient for each \(d\) may depend on the target and its entire true graph. Tests for several divisors on the same row amount to their least common multiple; after combining duplicates the total coefficient per \(d\le D\) must obey the stated divisor bound (fixed additional log powers can be absorbed).

**Proof.** Davenport's [uniform Möbius exponential-sum bound](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/os-8.1.313) implies for every fixed \(J\) that
\(\sup_\theta|\sum_{k\le K}\lambda(k)e(k\theta)|\ll_J K/\log^J(2K)\), where \(e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)\). Use \(\lambda(k)=\sum_{b^2\mid k}\mu(k/b^2)\), treating \(b>K^{1/4}\) trivially. Take \(K=\lfloor2X/d\rfloor\). Temporarily use every prime \(p\le2X\). Unrestricted in \(N\), the signed sum with \(p+dk=N,\ 1\le k\le K\), is a convolution since \(\lambda(dk)=\lambda(d)\lambda(k)\). For each fixed phase \(e(\beta k)\) in the cofactor sum, Parseval bounds its \(\ell^2(N)\) norm by
\[
 \|\mathbf1_{\mathrm{prime}\ p\le2X}\|_2\ 
 \sup_\alpha\left|\sum_{1\le k\le K}\lambda(k)e((d\alpha+\beta)k)\right|
 \ll_J X^{1/2}K/\log^J(2K).
\]
For fixed \(h,c\), insert \(1_{k=c\bmod h}\) by averaging \(h\) phases, at no loss by the triangle inequality. To take the maximum over all intervals, Fourier-expand \(1_{I\cap[1,K]}\) on \(\mathbb Z/K\): the modulus of normalized coefficient \(j\) is at most \(O(1/(1+\min(j,K-j)))\), **simultaneously for every interval** by the geometric-sum formula. Sum the \(\ell^2\) bounds for the resulting modulations, losing only \(O(\log(2K))\). The maximum over \(h,c\) costs at most \(O(1+T^2)\) by summing their norms. Minkowski over \(d\), \(\log(2K)\gg(\log X)^a\), and \(\sum_{d\le D}\tau(d)^C/d\ll_C(\log(2D))^{O_C(1)}\) now give an \(\ell^2\) bound \(O_M(X^{3/2}/\log^M X)\) for every fixed \(M\). The divisor bound follows by the Euler product to \(D\), whose prime term is \(2^C/p\) and higher terms sum to \(O_C(1)\) in its logarithm. Removing \(p=2,\ p\mid N,\ p=N-1\) changes the weighted sum by at most
\(\sum_{p\ {\rm removed}}\sum_{d\mid N-p}\tau(d)^C=X^{o(1)}\) per even target: there are \(O(\log X)\) removed terms, and \(1\le N-p\le 2X\). No \(p\ge N\) occurred. Chebyshev with \(2M\ge A+2B+2\) proves (1).

## 3. The exact limitation

Consider a rootwise minorant \(b_N(p)\le1_{p\in U_2}\) assembled as a sum over \(d\le D\) of the cells in (1), with coefficient of magnitude \(\le\tau(d)^C\) per divisor (or a linear combination of choices within each \(d\) with that total absolute weight). For each good target of (1), by nonnegativity,
\[
 \sum_{p\in V_N} b_N(p)
 \le \sum_{p\in V_N} w_N^+(p)1_{p\in U_2}+O(X/\log^B X)
 \ll E(N)+X/\log^B X.
\]
For a rootwise minorant of the prime-endpoint indicator itself the first term is exactly zero. These are inequalities for the **ordinary unweighted** minorant sum on the actual primes. Divisor tests of this form, even informed by downstream graph data, therefore cannot give an \(\omega(E(N))\) two-step lower bound on all large targets or recover the genuine pair-scale count. This is not a no-go theorem for a favorable constant *within* \(O(E)\), exact primality, unrestricted nonlinear or Type II information, or proofs using exceptional-target structure.

For contrast there is a positive proportion of even \(N\in[X,2X]\) with \(R(N)\gg X/\log^2 X\) on which (1) holds. The PNT counts pairs of primes in \((.55X,.70X)\), giving first moment \(\gg X^2/\log^2 X\). The ordinary two-root upper sieve used above, keeping the exact target collision product, gives
\(R(N)\ll (X/\log^2X)\prod_{t\mid N}(1-1/t)^{-1}\) for even \(N\) (the two possible small prime legs cost \(O(X^{.01})\)). The squared product has bounded mean, by expansion with \(h(t)=(1-1/t)^{-2}-1=O(1/t)\) and \(\sum_{d\ {\rm squarefree}}\prod_{t\mid d}h(t)/d<\infty\). Cauchy--Schwarz proves the positive proportion; discarding the exceptional set in (1) does not affect it. Thus the two nonnegative marked counts really differ by order \(X/\log^2X\) there, despite (1).

The classical parity principle and the weights \(1\pm\lambda\) are not new: compare [Friedlander–Iwaniec, Asymptotic sieve for primes](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811186), their [Goldbach parity discussion](https://msp.org/ent/2022/1-1/ent-v1-n1-p02-p.pdf), the [arithmetic two-weight model](ArithmeticTwoWeightParityWitness.md), and [genuine-prime thinning](GenuinePrimeThinning.md). The distinction here is an almost-all-target localized Type I comparison directly on the genuine prime starts, leaving the graph and all partner factorizations unchanged, and its application to a pointwise estimator. It supplies neither a new even target represented nor a counterexample to Goldbach.
