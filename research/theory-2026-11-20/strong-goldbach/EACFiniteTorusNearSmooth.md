# A finite-torus obstruction at near-smooth centers, including every fixed signature on up to five labels

Use the signed prime-factor equations of a genuine all-composite [Bożek terminal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900):
\[
 N-x_i=\prod_j x_j^{A_{ij}},
 \qquad B=I-A .
\]
The distinct \(x_i\) are odd unit primes at most \(N/3\); \(A\) is irreducible and nonnegative integral with zero diagonal and row sums at least two. A sequence is *near-smooth* if \(N\to\infty\) and \(N/N_F=N^{o(1)}\) for a fixed finite prime set \(F\).

**Theorem 1.** Fix a signature with \(\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb Q}B=1\), and take primitive integer nullvectors \(Bv=0,\ c^TB=0\). Consider over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) the torus equations
\[
 X^{B_i}=-1\ (1\le i\le k),\qquad g(X)=\sum_i c_i/X_i=0 .
 \tag{1}
\]
If this locus is finite (including empty), there is no near-smooth sequence of realizations, with no restriction on individual label sizes. A sufficient test is that some \(v_i\) occurs only once on the support of \(c\). No invertibility of \(A\) or a priori one-dimensionality of the physical variety is assumed.

**Theorem 2.** No **fixed** signature on at most five labels can recur along a near-smooth sequence. In particular, if all-composite terminals of size at most five do occur on an unbounded near-smooth sequence, their maximum row multiplicity (equivalently the maximum row sum for bounded size) must tend to infinity and \(\log(\min_i x_i)/\log N\to0\). This does not eliminate such growing-multiplicity terminals, assert that the smallest label is bounded, or control larger terminal sizes.

## 1. The dimension gate uses actual unit-prime specializations

In a fixed-signature hypothetical sequence choose a fixed \(\ell\in F\) for which \(v_\ell(N)\to\infty\) along a subsequence. At all these centers the labels are \(\ell\)-adic units. The rational points satisfy
\[
 X^{B_i}(1-N/X_i)=-1.
\]
Taking the product with exponents \(c_i\) and passing to any convergent \(\ell\)-adic subsequence gives \((-1)^{\sum c_i}=1\), even at \(\ell=2\) (this is equality in characteristic zero). This product therefore equals one. In a neighborhood of \(N=0\) with unit coordinates the regular function
\[
 H(N,X)=\bigg[\prod_i(1-N/X_i)^{c_i}-1\bigg]/N
\]
is defined including negative exponents (clear the factors nonzero there), and \(H(0,X)=-g(X)\). Every physical point with \(N\ne0\) satisfies \(H=0\).

Choose a geometric irreducible component of the Zariski closure of infinitely many of the physical points along this subsequence, on which an infinite subsequence is dense; it is defined over \(\mathbb Q\) since the dense points are rational. By compactness of the finite Cartesian product of \(\ell\)-adic units, a further subsequence converges to \((0,X^*)\) on its base change to \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\). The coordinates are units and (1) holds. If the locus is empty this is already a contradiction. If finite, the points are algebraic and finiteness is unchanged under this extension of algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields.

Locally at that point impose only \(k-1\) independent monomial rows with their deformations. The Jacobian in the torus variables at \(N=0\) has rank \(k-1\) by logarithmic differentiation; these equations cut a smooth surface in the \(k+1\) variables, with smooth one-dimensional slice \(N=0\). The slice is locally a translate of the primitive subtorus \(X_j=\zeta_j u^{v_j}\). The omitted dependent row holds on its entire component, since its monomial is constant there and has the prescribed value at the chosen point. By finiteness of (1), \(g\) is not identically zero on this component. Thus the intersection of \(H=0\) with \(N=0\) on the surface is isolated; in particular \(H=0\) is locally at most one dimensional. Our actual component passing through the point must be a curve. The unique-value test for (1) follows from this same torus parametrization, or integral Smith reduction: a unique \(v_i\) on the support contributes a Laurent monomial that cannot cancel on any translate.

We will also need the local intersection **length** at any torus point in (1): it is at most \(k-1\). Indeed \(H|_{N=0}\) is a nonzero sum of at most \(k\) distinct Laurent monomials in \(u\). A nonzero root has multiplicity at most one less than the number of terms, by applying \((u\,d/du)^j\) and the Vandermonde determinant. This length bounds the vanishing order of \(N\) on every branch of our actual normalized curve through that point. For detail, in completed surface coordinates \(\overline{\mathbb Q}[[u-u_0,N]]\), intersecting \(H\) with \(N=0\) gives the order of \(H(u,0)\). If \(J\) is the irreducible local plane-branch factor defining the chosen branch, \(J\mid H\); its own intersection multiplicity with \(N=0\) is the order of \(N\) on its primitive normalized uniformizer. This is the usual local plane-curve intersection formula (or the degree of its distinguished Weierstrass polynomial in \(u-u_0\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}[[N]]\)). Multiplicities of other factors can only increase the total length.

## 2. Integral curves and the center polynomial

By [Siegel’s theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2097), the normalized integral curve above has genus zero and one or two geometric points at infinity. There are infinitely many nonsingular rational points, so its smooth completion is a rational projective line. We give the two-puncture exclusion even when neither puncture is rational. Over a fixed field \(K\) of degree at most two splitting both, choose a parameter \(h\) with divisor their difference. Both \(h,h^{-1}\) are regular on the affine normalization, so integral over the original \(K\)-coordinate ring. Fixed multiples of both are algebraic integers at the rational integral physical tuples. Consequently all their finite valuations are bounded and zero outside a fixed finite set. The archimedean product of \(|h|_v\), using normalized places, is bounded by the product formula. Their projective heights are \(\ll N^{C_0}\) for some constant: both archimedean conjugates satisfy fixed monic equations with polynomial coefficients in the integral coordinates of magnitude \(\le N\), and the finite denominators are bounded.

Now \(N=Q(h)\) is a fixed nonconstant Laurent polynomial over \(K\); remove its monomial factor and let \(d\) be the degree of the remaining polynomial. At places over \(F\), \(h\) is bounded and bounded away from zero. If \(d=0\), the \(F\)-part of \(Q(h)\) is bounded. Otherwise choose nearest roots \(\alpha_v\) in the local algebraic closures; factoring the polynomial yields
\[
 \prod_{v\mid F}|h-\alpha_v|_v\ll N_F^{-1/d}.
\]
There are finitely many root choices. Apply the number-field [Subspace Theorem](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/98-absolute.pdf) to \((h,1)\), with \(X-\alpha_vY,Y\) at those places, and coordinate forms at the archimedean and other fixed bad places. Since the other places have max norm one, the product of these forms is \(\ll N_F^{-1/d}\), less than a fixed negative power of the projective height even if \(N_F\ge N^\eta\) for any fixed \(\eta>0\). (When finitely many \(h\) have bounded height there is nothing to show.) Each exceptional \(K\)-line gives at most one \(h\). Thus the two-puncture case cannot realize the sequence.

With one rational puncture take a rational parameter \(z\) with a single simple pole there. Clearing the denominator of \(z\) as in the [interior rough-part theorem](EACInteriorTrinomialRoughPart.md), its physical values are integers and \(N=Q(z),x_i=G_i(z)\) are rational polynomials. Write \(D=\deg Q\), choose \(z\to+\infty\) and hence positive leading coefficient. At near-smooth centers \(Q\) can have only one distinct root: the nearest-root \(p\)-adic Subspace Theorem calculation in the linked proof gives \(N_F\ll (1+|z|)^{m_0+\epsilon}\) for any \(\epsilon>0\), where \(m_0\) is its largest root multiplicity. Since \(|Q(z)|\asymp |z|^D\), one must have \(m_0=D\). Thus \(Q=c(z-\beta)^D\), \(c\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\beta\in\mathbb Q\). Write \(c=C\lambda^D\) with \(C\) a positive \(D\)-th-power-free integer and \(\lambda\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), and set \(T=\lambda(z-\beta)\). Since \(N=C T^D\) is integral, a rational \(T\) must be integral: a negative valuation cannot be offset by a valuation of \(C\) in \([0,D-1]\).

We have \(x_i=P_i(T)\) with rational polynomials. **None is constant.** A constant vertex would be a fixed odd prime \(p\) in a bounded factor-closed set of unit primes whose complements are all composite. These are precisely the hypotheses of the [fixed-and-moving seed independence theorem](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md), since near-smoothness gives \(N_F\ge N^{1/2+1/4}\) eventually and \(p\nmid N\). That theorem gives \(\operatorname{nullity}(B)\ge2\), contradicting the hypothesis in Theorem 1. Thus all degrees \(d_i\) are positive. Each label occurs in a polynomial product with at least two factors, while \(P_i(T)=x_i\le N/3\) on the physical sequence; the degree of every product \(N-P_i\) is \(D\) (even at equal degree its leading term cannot cancel by this bound). It follows that all \(d_i<D\). In fact each \(P_i\) is primitive integral. At any rational prime the coefficient Gauss norm \(h_i\) cannot be less than one, since then its values at integers would be divisible by that prime, contrary to their being unbounded prime values. Since \(C\) is integral and \(d_i<D\), the Gauss norm of \(CT^D-P_i\) is \(h_i\). Multiplicativity in the factor equations gives \(A\log h=\log h\), with \(\log h\ge0\). Irreducibility and Perron root at least two force this vector to be zero. No constant vanishes, since \(T\mid P_i(T)\) would contradict coprimality with \(N\). The same nonnegative-eigenvector reasoning on \(\log |P_i(0)|\) gives \(P_i(0)=\sigma_i\in\{1,-1\}\). Every \(P_i\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) by Gauss’s lemma and its unbounded prime values, all leading coefficients are positive, and the polynomials are distinct.

At \(T=0\) the point of the actual affine curve is therefore in the unit torus. The normalized parameter \(T\) is primitive because it differs by a rational linear change from \(z\). The local intersection bound in section 1, now applied at this point, gives
\[
                  2\le D=\operatorname{ord}_{T=0}N\le k-1.
\]

## 3. The missing coefficients force impossible binomial polynomials

In \(\mathbb Q[T]/(T^D)\) take the formal logarithms of \(P_i/\sigma_i\), each with constant one. The deformed monomial equations, divided by their values at zero, give \(B\log(P/\sigma)=0\) in this quotient. Coefficient by coefficient, then,
\[
 P_i(T)/\sigma_i=U(T)^{v_i}\pmod {T^D}
\]
for a single formal unit \(U(0)=1\) (exponentiate the common scalar logarithm). No \(v_i\) can vanish, since \(\deg P_i<D\) and every \(P_i\) is nonconstant. No two \(v_i\) coincide: equality would make the normalized polynomials identical; their leading coefficients have the same positive sign, so the original polynomials would be identical, contrary to distinct labels. Write \(h=\operatorname{ord}_0(U-1)<D\), choosing a formal lift. Each \(P_i-\sigma_i\) first changes in degree \(h\), so \(h\le m=\min_i d_i\). Every label occurs in some product of total degree \(D\) with at least two occurrences. Hence \(d_i\le D-m\) for every \(i\).

For \(n=rh<D\), \(r\ge1\), the coefficient of \(T^n\) in \(U^v\) is a polynomial in the exponent \(v\) of degree exactly \(r\); its leading coefficient is \(([T^h](U-1))^r/r!\). No multiple of \(h\) lies strictly between \(D-m\) and \(D\): that coefficient would vanish for all \(k\) distinct \(v_i\) but its degree would be \(r\le D-1<k\). Since \(h\le m\), the interval exclusion forces \(h=m\) and \(m\mid D\) (if \(h<m\) use the last multiple of \(h\) strictly below \(D\); if \(h=m\) use the remainder of \(D\) modulo \(m\)). Pick a minimum label with
\[
 P_0/\sigma_0=1+\gamma T^m,\qquad \gamma\ne0.
\]
All the other normalized polynomials are the truncations of \((1+\gamma T^m)^{v_i/v_0}\) modulo \(T^D\), hence polynomials in \(T^m\). Set \(d=D/m\ge2\). If \(d_i<D-m\) the coefficient of \(T^{D-m}\) vanishes, forcing \(v_i/v_0\in\{0,\ldots,d-2\}\). Zero is impossible; any value at least two makes \(P_i\) a reducible power, and value one requires \(i=0\). Thus if \(d>2\), the unique minimum is the only polynomial below degree \(D-m\); every output of its own row is a different vertex (no self-loop), hence has degree \(D-m>D/2\). At least two such factors cannot fit in degree \(D\).

If \(d=2\), all polynomials are linear in \(Z=T^m\), with positive leading coefficients \(l_i\) and constant signs \(\sigma_i\); each complement has two child factors counting multiplicity. There must be a negative constant since \(-\sigma_i\) is the product of child signs. In a negative-constant row the child signs coincide. They cannot both be positive, since the coefficient of \(Z\) in their product would be positive whereas the coefficient in \(C Z^2-P_i\) is negative. Thus both children are negative. Strong connectivity makes every constant negative. Comparing the linear coefficients then gives \(l_i=l_j+l_h\) at every row, impossible in a row of smallest positive \(l_i\). This proves Theorem 1.

## 4. No bounded-order label in a bounded terminal: a path argument

On every near-smooth sequence of all-composite terminals with at most \(k\) vertices, the minimum **signed order** in \((\mathbb Z/N)^\times/\{\pm1\}\) of any vertex tends to infinity. The exponents in the factor graph need not be bounded in this observation.

Suppose to the contrary an endpoint \(x\) has bounded order on a subsequence, so \(x^E\equiv1\bmod N\) for a fixed positive even \(E\). There is a prime vertex \(\le\sqrt N\), by factoring a composite complement. Choose a simple directed path from it to \(x\), of bounded length. Pass to a subsequence on which this length is fixed and each path vertex's logarithm divided by \(\log N\) has a limit. The source has limit at most \(1/2\). Choose the **last** path vertex \(y\) whose limit is less than one, possibly \(y=x\). It satisfies \(y\le N^{1-\eta}\) with some fixed \(\eta>0\) on this subsequence; no such gap is imposed uniformly beforehand. Every subsequent directed edge \(z\to z'\) has \(z'=N^{1-o(1)}\), so its integer cofactor \((N-z)/z'\) is \(N^{o(1)}\). Their product \(W\) (one for an empty suffix) is \(N^{o(1)}\), a unit mod \(N\). Iterating the actual row identities gives \(y/W\equiv\pm x\bmod N\), hence \((y/W)^E\equiv1\bmod N\).

Reduce the positive ratio \(y/W\) to the primitive pair \((Y,Z)\). At each \(\ell\mid N,\ell\in F\), both are units. Choosing a nearest \(E\)-th root of unity \(\zeta_\ell\) gives \(|Y-\zeta_\ell Z|_\ell\ll_E |N|_\ell\); this follows by factoring the separable polynomial, with constants depending also on the fixed prime. At other \(\ell\in F\) the integral coordinates and unit roots give a bound one. With forms \(X-\zeta_\ell Y,Y\) there and coordinate forms at infinity, the product is \(O_F(yW/N_F)\le N^{-\eta+o(1)}\). Its height is at most \(N^{O(1)}\). The [Subspace Theorem](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf) confines all ratios to a fixed finite set; bounded primitive heights cause no problem. A fixed reduced ratio \(a/b>0\) on an unbounded subsequence would satisfy \(N\mid a^E-b^E\), hence \(a=b\). This makes \(x\equiv\pm1\bmod N\), impossible since \(3\le x\le N/3\).

## 5. All fixed signatures of up to five labels

Let \(k\le5\), fix the signature, and retain strong connectivity. The upward arcs between increasing labels form an undirected forest: at its largest vertex any undirected cycle would give two distinct smaller parents, impossible since a smaller parent of \(z\) must equal \(N\bmod z\). The smaller color class is a directed feedback set of size at most \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor\). Eliminating the complementary nilpotent block gives an effective matrix on that set with row sums at least two, so the nullity of its identity minus adjacency is at most the set size minus one. See the exact [first-return proof](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md). Thus \(\operatorname{nullity}B\le1\) without a condition on \(A\).

If \(B\) is invertible, the signed multiplicative relations and its adjugate force each label to have signed order dividing the fixed \(|\det B|\), excluded by section 4. Suppose instead \(B\) has corank one. Let \(v,c\) be its primitive right and left nullvectors. Each vector has both signs by Perron–Frobenius for \(A\) or \(A^T\) (their spectral radius is at least two). On either sign support every vertex must lead to one of the same sign in the eigenvector equation. The zero diagonal means each sign support has at least two labels; thus \(k=4\) or 5. If \(v_z=0\), the rational row space of \(B\) contains the coordinate basis vector \(e_z\). A fixed positive multiple is in the integral row lattice, forcing bounded signed order of \(z\), contrary to section 4.

With no zero in \(v\), at least one of its sign groups has exactly two labels. If any \(v_i\) occurs only once on the support of \(c\), use Theorem 1. Otherwise both \(c\)-entries on that size-two group are nonzero: \(c\) has at most one zero, and just one nonzero entry in the group would be unique. Their two \(v\)-values are equal. Orient them positive and call the two prime labels \(p,q\). The only possible positive output in the \(p\)-row is \(q\); its coefficient cannot be one, since there are no zero \(v_i\) and the row would then have total multiplicity one. Both mate coefficients are therefore at least two. In particular \(p,q<\sqrt N\).

Also \(v_p=v_q\), so a fixed positive multiple of \(e_p-e_q\) is in the integral row lattice. For a fixed positive even \(E\) one has \((p/q)^E\equiv1\bmod N\). The primitive-vector nearest-root argument of section 4 gives product \(O(pq/N_F)\) for \((p,q)\). If on some subsequence \(pq\le N^{1-\eta}\) for any fixed \(\eta>0\), the Subspace Theorem forces a fixed ratio. A reduced ratio of distinct primes determines both, which is also incompatible with \(N\mid p^E-q^E\) at arbitrarily large \(N\). Thus \(pq=N^{1-o(1)}\) and both \(p,q=N^{1/2-o(1)}\). Define positive integers \(a,b\) by
\[
 N-p=a q^2,\qquad N-q=b p^2 .
\]
They are units modulo \(N\) and \(a,b=N^{o(1)}\), without needing to identify their prime factors. Modulo \(N\), division gives \((p/q)^3\equiv a/b\), whence \(N\mid a^E-b^E\). Both powers are subpolynomial, so \(a=b\) eventually. Subtraction of the two physical rows would now give \(q-p=a(q-p)(q+p)\), impossible. This proves the fixed-signature assertion. For bounded \(k\), bounded row multiplicities give only finitely many matrices, while a lower bound \(x_i\ge N^\delta\) bounds every row sum by \(1/\delta\). The two stated sequence consequences follow.

The sign-support reduction and first-order torus are also used, for an arithmetic counting theorem, in the [five-label sparsity proof](EACFiveSquareRoot.md). These results neither prove that an exceptional center admits a small terminal nor rule out unbounded exponents in the small-terminal cases.
