# A fixed power threshold for corank-one prime-factor signatures

This note strengthens the [finite-torus theorem](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md) and the [cyclotomic phase obstruction](EACCyclotomicPhaseClasses.md). The new inputs are the [effective near-full divisor lifting theorem](EACNearFullLinearLift.md), a weighted count of local multiplicities on the resulting curve, and a restriction on three simultaneous phase columns. No computational enumeration or uniform-in-signature form of Siegel's theorem is used.

In the Bożek factor graph, fix an irreducible \(k\)-vertex all-composite terminal exponent matrix \(A\) as in the linked papers. For the actual distinct odd unit primes \(x_i\le N/3\), \(N-x_i=\prod_j x_j^{A_{ij}}\), with \(A_{ii}=0\) and all row sums at least two. Assume \(B=I-A\) has rational corank one; \(v,c\) are primitive right/left nullvectors, and \(d\) is its last nonzero Smith invariant. Write \(F\) for a fixed finite set of rational primes, \(N_F\) for the full \(F\)-part of \(N\), and \(s=2^{-k}\).

**Theorem.** The fixed signature occurs at most finitely often in the following regimes, for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\).
* **Finite torus, arbitrary \(F\):** if \(\sum_i c_i X_i^{-1}\) is nonzero as a Laurent polynomial on every translate of the torus \(X^{B_i}=-1\), it suffices that \(N_F\ge N^{1-1/k+\epsilon}\). This includes the previous unique-exponent test.
* **Arbitrary \(F\), \(k\le9\):** no further assumption on the signature is needed when \(N_F\ge N^{1-s/k+\epsilon}\).
* **Joint signed bases, arbitrary fixed \(k\):** the same \(N_F\ge N^{1-s/k+\epsilon}\) suffices whenever \(F\) consists of at most two primes whose local roots of unity of order dividing \(2d\) are only signs. In particular \(F=\{2,3\}\) works for every \(d\), with no assumed balance between its two valuations.
* **One prescribed base:** suppose \(F=\{\ell\}\) and \(N_F\ge N^{1-s+\epsilon}\). For \(\ell=2\) the fixed signature is excluded. For odd \(\ell\), an unbounded sequence requires two distinct odd primes dividing \(\gcd(d,\ell-1)\), and if \(P<Q\) are the two least ones then \(k\ge 2(P+Q)-2\). In particular \(k\le13\) is excluded at every single fixed base.

The strict exponent gaps are not optimality claims. For example \(k=9\) in the second conclusion asks for a smooth fraction exponent exceeding \(1-1/4608\), not merely an arbitrary positive exponent. It does include all near-smooth sequences \(N_F=N^{1-o(1)}\), without a bounded-cofactor assumption. No conclusion rules out arbitrary growing exponents, larger corank, or all graphs at a center.

### 1. Good local translates and weighted root multiplicity

Argue by contradiction along an unbounded realizing sequence. Pass to a subsequence on which \(\lambda_\ell=\lim\log N_{\{\ell\}}/\log N\) and \(w_i=\lim\log x_i/\log N\) exist; set \(\rho=\sum_{\ell\in F}\lambda_\ell\), \(\eta=1-\rho\). One may take an irreducible component of the physical Zariski closure containing a dense subsequence; the rational points make that geometric component rationally defined. Choose simultaneous local unit accumulation at the finitely many places with \(\lambda_\ell>0\), hence \(v_\ell(N)\to\infty\). Only positive-mass places are needed. Since all the regimes have \(\rho>1/2\), the [bounded-orbit fixed-seed theorem](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) gives \(\min x_i\to\infty\): a bounded label would fix a prime on a subsequence and force corank at least two.

As in the [local phase argument](EACCyclotomicPhaseClasses.md), integral Smith coordinates at each place give a translate \(X_i=\xi_{i\ell}U^{v_i}\) through that actual unit accumulation. The local phases have order dividing \(2d\) and belong to \(\mathbb Q_\ell\); the same formula holds at physical unit coordinates modulo \(\ell^{v_\ell(N)-O_{A,\ell}(1)}\), with a common varying unit parameter. Call a positive-mass place **good** if
\[
                  g_\ell(U)=\sum_i c_i\xi_{i\ell}^{-1}U^{-v_i}
\]
is not identically zero on this translate. Set \(\gamma=\sum_{\ell\ {\rm good}}\lambda_\ell\). If even one is good, the physical component is a curve. Indeed, \(k-1\) independent deformed rows cut a smooth surface, and on its one-dimensional \(N=0\) slice the remaining regular equation \([\prod(1-N/X_i)^{c_i}-1]/N=0\) restricts to \(-g_\ell=0\). This also bounds the vanishing order \(e_\ell\) of \(N\) on the approached branch of the normalized physical curve by \(k-1\): on the slice a nonzero sum of at most \(k\) distinct Laurent monomials has a nonzero-root multiplicity at most \(k-1\), by the Vandermonde matrix. The local intersection length bounds that of each branch factor. See [the detailed local length proof](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md). At a bad place we do not use this length bound.

With \(\gamma>0\), the integral points on this fixed curve imply genus zero and one or two geometric punctures by [Siegel's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2097), and its rational points identify the completion with \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Q}\). The two-puncture case is already excluded as soon as \(\rho>0\): in the quadratic splitting field a parameter and its inverse have bounded finite denominators, \(N\) is a Laurent polynomial in that parameter, and proximity to its roots at the places over \(F\) contradicts the Subspace Theorem. The earlier [finite-torus proof, section 2](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md), explicitly gives this positive-power scope, not only near-smoothness.

With one puncture take the same primitive rational parameter, rescaled so physical \(z\) are integers of unbounded height; \(N=Q(z)\), \(x_i=G_i(z)\) are fixed rational polynomials, \(D=\deg Q\ge1\) and \(\log |z|=(1/D+o(1))\log N\). At \(\ell\in F\) of positive mass pass to a convergent integer subsequence with limit \(\alpha_\ell\in\mathbb Z_\ell\), a root of \(Q\). Its multiplicity \(1\le e_\ell\le D\) is precisely the vanishing order on the normalized parameter. Its coordinates \(G_i(\alpha_\ell)\) are the unit limits of the \(x_i\). In particular at a good place \(e_\ell\le k-1\). Factoring at that root gives
\[
        |z-\alpha_\ell|_\ell \ll_{Q,\ell} N_{\{\ell\}}^{-1/e_\ell}.
\]
On \((z,1)\) use coordinate forms at infinity and \((X-\alpha_\ell Y,Y)\) at those places. The [Subspace Theorem for algebraic local forms, (2.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2268#page=5) gives
\[
               \sum_{\ell:\lambda_\ell>0}\frac{\lambda_\ell}{e_\ell}
                        \ \le\ \frac{1}{D}.                           \tag{1}
\]
Otherwise the product of the form values would be a fixed negative power of \(|z|\), and finitely many rational lines cannot contain unbounded distinct \((z,1)\). In particular
\[
             \frac{\gamma}{k-1}+\frac{\rho-\gamma}{D}
                    \le \frac1D,\qquad
             D\le(k-1)(1+\eta/\gamma).                               \tag{2}
\]
If every place is good, (1) immediately gives \(D\le(k-1)/\rho<k\) when \(\rho>1-1/k\). More generally \(D<k\) follows from (2) if \(\gamma>(k-1)\eta\).

When \(D\le k-1\) and \(\rho>1-1/k\), \(Q\) must have a single distinct root: for \(D\ge2\) otherwise all \(e_\ell\le D-1\), and (1) gives \(\rho\le1-1/D<1-1/k\). For \(D=1\) the statement is automatic. A rational affine change gives \(N=C T^D\) with positive \(D\)-th-power-free integral \(C\), and the physical \(T\) are integers. At this point the [corank-one differential-pencil contradiction](EACReciprocalNearSmooth.md), or the [finite-torus polynomial argument](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md), applies without another smoothness hypothesis. Explicitly, no coordinate polynomial is constant because the fixed-seed theorem already applies at \(\rho>1/2\); the primitive-polynomial, signed-constant and missing-coefficient arguments use the factor equations, nonconstant prime values, and \(D\le k-1\). This rules out the sequence. It proves the first theorem bullet, where \(\gamma=\rho\).

### 2. Exact lifting on the bad places

Let \(F_b\) consist of the positive-mass places not good. On every nonempty active class \(S_b=\{i:v_i=b,\ c_i\ne0\}\) (here \(b\) is an eigenvector coordinate, unrelated to the subscript of \(F_b\)), the identity \(g_\ell\equiv0\) gives \(\sum_{i\in S_b}c_i\xi_{i\ell}^{-1}=0\); cyclotomic conjugation gives \(\sum_{i\in S_b}c_i\xi_{i\ell}=0\) too.

Suppose first that the bad mass \(\rho-\gamma>1-s\). Every rational-integer relation between the simultaneous columns \((\xi_{i\ell})_{\ell\in F_b}\), \(i\in S_b\), gives
\[
             \sum_{i\in S_b}a_i x_i\equiv0\bmod D_b,\qquad
       D_b=\prod_{\ell\in F_b}\ell^{v_\ell(N)-O_{A,\ell}(1)}\mid N.
\]
Choose nonnegative fixed integral local loss constants as in the signed Smith calculation. The coefficients \(a_i\) here are fixed along the frozen subsequence; the common factor \(u_\ell^b\) does not affect a relation. Now \(\log D_b/\log N\to\rho-\gamma>1-2^{-k}\). Thus the [pointwise lifting theorem](EACNearFullLinearLift.md) upgrades the congruence to divisibility by the *full* \(N\); since \(x_i<N/\min x_j=o(N)\), every such fixed linear relation lifts to an exact zero. In particular no distinct columns in an active group may agree up to one common sign, and any primitive integral relation must have even coefficient sum (all labels odd). One must use the *same* coefficients at all bad places.

* **At most two signed places.** The non-antipodal sign columns in \(\mathbb Q^2\) lie on the two independent rays represented by \((1,1),(1,-1)\), at most one per ray. In dimension one there is just one ray. Thus no nonempty active class can support the required dependence.
* **Arbitrary \(F_b\).** Every active class must have at least four indices. Size one or two is impossible by nonzero phases and exclusion of global antipodal columns. For size three take a rational dependence with primitive nonzero integer coefficients. At a single place, either all three phases are proportional up to sign, or all are pairwise nonproportional and form a nondegenerate three-term cyclotomic relation. Exactly two proportional classes cannot satisfy a relation with three nonzero coefficients unless all three are proportional. In the nondegenerate case [Conway–Jones, Theorem 5, with squarefree reduced exponent](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-30-3-229-240) gives only the complete signed triangle: its ratio exponent has no primes besides \(2,3\); after normalizing the three phases are the three signed third roots and all primitive coefficients have magnitude 1. This contradicts the even-sum constraint. If instead at every place all three phases are proportional, normalize each coordinate by the first phase there. The columns become sign vectors. Change column signs so the first entry is \(+1\). Any three distinct such sign vectors are linearly independent: linear dependence would put three distinct vertices of a hypercube on a single affine line, impossible by looking at a varying coordinate. Either case precludes size three.
* **A single arbitrary prime \(\ell\).** With all the mass bad, the same exact lift and the circuit computation of [the single-base phase obstruction](EACCyclotomicPhaseClasses.md) apply. At \(\ell=2\) the phases are signed. At an odd \(\ell\), each active class needs at least \(n=P+Q-2\) elements, with \(P,Q\) as in the theorem, or no active class at all if these primes do not both exist. Indeed an irredundant circuit whose ratio exponent has only one odd factor is an entire signed regular odd polygon, impossible after lifting by parity. For any remaining circuit the Conway–Jones length bound gives \(n\).

### 3. Class count at a fixed smooth fraction

We need two active classes and four classes overall at \(\rho>2/3\), not just in the prior near-smooth regime. For distinct \(i,j\) with \(v_i=v_j\), the fixed saturated-row relation gives \((x_i/x_j)^{2d}\equiv1\bmod N\). At unbounded positive-mass places pass to fixed local roots with valuation loss \(O_{d,\ell}(1)\). On the primitive coordinate pair \((x_i,x_j)\), the product of forms \((X-\zeta_\ell Y,Y)\) at those places and coordinate forms at infinity is \(O(x_i x_j/N^{\rho+o(1)})\). If \(w_i+w_j<\rho\), the Subspace Theorem puts the pair on finitely many rational lines; its height is at most \(N\), it is unbounded, and distinct unbounded prime coordinates cannot have a fixed ratio. Consequently \(w_i+w_j\ge\rho\).

On an edge \(i\to j\) with \(v_i=v_j\), the cofactor \(z=(N-x_i)/x_j\ge3\) satisfies \(z^{2d}\equiv1\bmod N\). It cannot be bounded on a subsequence. The same local forms on \((z,1)\), with \(z\le N/x_j\), force \(w_j\le1-\rho=\eta\). A directed cycle with one common \(v\)-value would have at least two distinct vertices, each of weight \(\le\eta\), but a same-value pair has combined weight at least \(\rho>2\eta\). No such cycle exists.

The right and left eigenvectors \(v,c\) for eigenvalue 1 have both signs by Perron–Frobenius (\(\rho(A)\ge2\)). In the positive set of \(v\) choose a positive child from each row; similarly in its negative set. The two resulting same-*sign* cycles must each use at least two distinct \(v\)-values, giving four classes overall. In the positive left support of \(c\) each vertex has a positive parent, giving a cycle entirely inside \(\operatorname{supp} c\). It uses at least two active \(v\)-classes. Thus if each active class needs \(n\) elements, then \(k\ge2n+2\). In the arbitrary-\(F_b\) case \(n=4\), so \(k\le9\) excludes a bad mass greater than \(1-s\).

For either of the second and third bullets, we have now proved \(\rho-\gamma\le1-s\), i.e. \(\gamma\ge s-\eta\). Since \(\eta<s/k\), one has \(\gamma>(k-1)\eta\); the good mass is positive and (2) gives \(D<k\), finishing the contradiction by section 1. For the final single-base bullet, if its circuit/cardinality requirement fails, the one place cannot be bad since its mass exceeds \(1-s\). Then \(\gamma=\rho>1-1/k\) and section 1 again applies. Throughout the prime alphabet and signature are fixed before taking limits.
