# Conditional prime pairs with a polylogarithmic multiplier at every even target

Let $P$ be a prescribed sufficiently large even integer, $L=\log P$ and $I=[P/3,2P/3]$. The following is a conditional theorem for actual primes, not an assertion about modeled almost-prime weights.

**Theorem.** Suppose a fixed $\Theta<1$ bounds the real parts of the nontrivial zeros of all primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet $L$-functions. There exists $K_0$ such that for each fixed $K\ge K_0$ one can choose positive constants $b_1,\ldots,b_K$, with $2b_i<b_{i+1}$, as follows. Let
$$
 \mathcal P_i=\{\ell\text{ prime}:L^{b_i}\le\ell\le L^{2b_i},\ 
              \ell\equiv1\pmod4,\ \ell\nmid P\},\quad
 V_i=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_i}1/\ell,\quad \mathcal V=\prod_i V_i .
$$
Write $\mathcal A$ for the squarefree products selecting one prime from each bank. Then
$$
 \#\{P=p+a q:p\in I,\ p,q\text{ prime},\ a\in\mathcal A\}
  =(1+o(1))\mathfrak S(P)\mathcal V\,|I|/L^2,                       \tag{1}
$$
where $\mathfrak S(P)=\prod_{r\mid P}(1-1/r)^{-1}
\prod_{r\nmid P}(1-(r-1)^{-2})$ (products over primes). In particular $a\le L^B$, $B=2\sum_i b_i$, and $q\ge P/(3L^B)$. Each physical $p$ is counted once: $q$ is the unique prime factor above $L^B$ and the other $K$ primes of $P-p$ lie in disjoint banks. The count is unweighted; $\mathcal V\to(\tfrac12\log 2)^K>0$.

The constant $B$ can be very large. The earlier [fixed-rank statement, §4](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b262263b99802e5445835d22ebd99fe673ef7875/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/near_full_cofactor/Proof.md) allowed $a\le\exp(O_K(L^{1/5}))$. The improvement to a *fixed power of the logarithm* is not an application of the stated hypotheses on label sizes in [Jiang–Lau–Lü, Theorem 4.1, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2#S4), nor does that theorem concern the moving target $P$. We use their line-model proof at amended scales, the [full-target minor comparison's geometry](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/f22749a7/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/target_slope_switch/Proof.md), and the [projected major calculation](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b262263b99802e5445835d22ebd99fe673ef7875/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/near_full_cofactor/Proof.md#1-a-truly-projected-major-bound). The fixed half-plane is used for **prime sums of polylogarithmic length**; the previous weaker local zero-free strip approaching 1 does not suffice for that argument. The wider external half-plane assertion [OpenAI, September 30, Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf) would supply the stated nonprincipal hypothesis. Its proof is not audited here.

## 1. The mean square and the order of choices

Use $z=\exp(L^{.24})$ and the full rough, target-unit bilinear boxes of the two in-repository comparisons: $M,N\ge P^\delta$, $MN\asymp P$, $mn<P$ and a box of ratio at most $1.1$. For an admissible smooth fixed shell bump $\eta$, put
$$
 Q_Y=\sum_{h>0}\left|\mathcal V^{-1}\sum_{P-mn=ah,\,a\in\mathcal A}
                 \eta(a/Y)\alpha_m\beta_n\right|^2.
$$
The coefficients are bounded by $L^{C}$, the first being $\mu(m)$ on the complete interval with just the rough/unit restrictions (a bounded polynomial-time twist is also permitted). For the minor comparison no Möbius condition is needed. For any fixed accuracy $R$ we establish, after the choices below, $Q_Y\ll PY L^{-R}$ uniformly. We only require one finite accuracy for (1). We henceforth write $X=MN\asymp P$.

Fix $\delta,C,R$, the shell bump and exterior mesh accuracies *before* choosing $K$. In the minor proof take $E=R+4C+6$, $G=E+4$, $A_0=G+3$ and $K_0>200(G+1)$, as in JLL's [proof of Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2#S6.SS5). Increase $K_0$ if needed for these fixed losses. Fix any $K\ge K_0$. Determine the finite additional log saving $D_{\rm maj}$ needed on a single complete prime label in the major estimate of §3 by fixing the auxiliary integration-by-parts and truncation precisions there. None of these exponents depends on $b_i$: sizes appear only as coordinates normalized to their current shell. Constants may depend on $K$. Choose
$$
 J>1000(1+R+C+E+G+A_0+K+D_{\rm maj}),\qquad b_1>100J,
 \qquad b_s>100(J+K+\sum_{i<s} b_i)\quad(s\ge2).                      \tag{2}
$$
Also increase $b_1$ and all subsequent $b_s$ so $(1-\Theta)b_1$ exceeds the exponent required from the explicit formula and time twists in §3. An admissible shell then has $L^{b_1}/2\le Y\le L^B$, and all its labels exceed all the denominators $d\le L^{A_0}$. Prime PNT in the class 1 modulo 4 gives the stated $V_i$ limit by partial summation; deletion of target primes costs $O(L^{1-b_1})$. Thus $1/4\le V_i\le1/2$ for sufficiently large $P$. Fixed factors such as $4^K$ are not log exponents. There is no growing collection of label grids.

## 2. Minor arcs with the compressed labels

Work with the same major cut $\|\alpha-c/d\|\le2L^{A_0}/Y$, $d\le L^{A_0}$, and the complementary minor comparison. For clarity, its major kernel still carries the *full* condition $P\mid cmn-ars$ and the centered integer determinant $(cmn-ars)/P$, where $a,c$ are the two label products. The slope-lattice shear and all reductions of the [projected comparison](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/f22749a7/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/target_slope_switch/Proof.md) apply. Here is the audit of label-size dependencies, including hypotheses in the original lemmas that cannot simply be cited verbatim.

* **Upper sizes, traces and floors.** Keep the ordered shared count $J_0=\lfloor L^{.01}\rfloor$, $m_0=J_0+1$ and even trace length $T\asymp L^{1/2}$. Write $D$ for the shared product, $d_0\asymp D$, and $H_*=d_0Y$ for the geometric scale. Then $\log H_*\ll_{\mathbf b,K}L^{.01}\log L=o(T)$, so the reach factor $(1+TH_*)^{1/T}$ stays bounded. On one physical or comparison edge $0<|(cmn-ars)/P|\le5Y<z$ for squarefree distinct labels: zero would imply $a\mid mn$, impossible. Thus the primitive and single-slope steps and the coefficient/fiber interpretations are unchanged. The number of possible real-domain boundaries and paths in JLL (5.19)–(5.23), Lemma 6.2, and Proposition 6.5 need only *upper* label sizes. In fact $\log|\Gamma|\ll_{\mathbf b,K}T m_0\log L=O_{\mathbf b,K}(L^{.51}\log L)$, counting the $O(TH_*)$ coordinate bounds and tuples separately at each step; $\log|q_\gamma|\ll_{\mathbf b,K}(T+m_0)\log L$ for the squarefree path modulus. Boundary counts remain $\exp(O_K(L^{.21}))$ for large $P$: in JLL Lemma 6.2 the endpoints of tests involve products of a shared-product choice and at most $O(K)$ free labels, bounded by $\exp(O_{\mathbf b,K}(L^{.01}\log L))$; the number of tests and integer intervals has the same upper bound per vertex, and there are $O(T)$ vertices. Both the global congruence-frame remainder and the $O(1)$ integer rounding terms for one edge are thus still covered by the original $P^{-c_\delta+o(1)}$ after averaging. In particular this remains valid after direct sum over all physical unit slopes, **not** an assumed estimate at each slope.
* **Separation that uses the lower sizes.** Write $Z_s=L^{b_s}$ and replace $e^{-L^{\vartheta_s}}$ by $Z_s^{-1}$ throughout JLL (5.11), (6.12) and Lemma 6.13. For a fixed tuple of distinct primes its possible shared products form a set of size $\le m_0^K$. The number $N_s$ of its distinct shared products times the possible free labels with indices $i<s$ is at most $m_0^K\prod_{i<s} Z_i^2$. Thus $N_s\le Z_s^{1/16}$ for large $P$ by (2). The first separation test fails on measure $\le2m_0^K Y^{-1/2}$. For each higher-label product $F$ the second test compares distinct integers $n,n'$ in this $N_s$-element set: $\|(n-n')Fr\|\le100/Z_s$ has circular measure $\le200/Z_s$. Summing pairs and averaging $F$ by its reciprocal bank probability gives $O(N_s^2/Z_s)$. Markov at threshold $Z_s^{-1/4}$ costs at most $O(Z_s^{-5/8})$ in exceptional measure. Therefore total failed measure is $O_{K}(L^{-10J})$. The working threshold $Z_s^{-1/4}$ on good states **also** remains below $L^{-10J}$.
* **All-fresh operator block.** The common integral endpoint basis and orthogonal blocking in Lemmas 6.7 and 6.9 are unchanged. In Lemma 6.8 the stated $\log Y\gg L^{\upsilon}$ is replaced using its proof: for $|s|\le L^{A_0}/2$ additive Dirichlet approximation of $\alpha+s/Y$ off the major arcs yields $L^{A_0}<q\le Y/L^{A_0}$, with the integer floor harmless since $Y/L^{2A_0}\to\infty$. The normalized tensor large-sieve norm (its last displayed inequality) is $O(L^{-A_0}+Y^{-1/20})=O(L^{-G})$ by (2). The complementary $s$-tail of the fixed Fourier transform is $O_G(L^{-G-1})$ by choosing its differentiation order. This proves exactly the bound used after (6.11); no uniform positive $\upsilon$ at polylogarithmic $Y$ is assumed. Extending to coincident entering/leaving prime labels costs $O_K((1+L^{3A_0})/Z_1)$ in Schur norm.
* **Remembered-label blocks and bad edges.** The equality case in Lemma 6.11 has exceptional parameter $B_*/m_0^K+B_*^K Z_1^{-1/4}$, with $B_*\ll_K L^{1/2}$. The first term after the square root is $O_K(L^{1/4-K/200})=O(L^{-G})$ and the second is negligible at this finite accuracy. For the major-kernel part of these same blocks, the index-$p$ lattice bound $O(1+Y/p)$ gives a one-sided row/column gain $\ll_K L^{3A_0}(1+B_*)^K(Z_1^{-1}+Y^{-1})$, against the other side's polynomial bound from Lemma 6.10. Its geometric mean is $O(L^{-G})$ by (2). The Fejér moment identity (6.13) then gives the same $L^{-ET}$ trace bound: $2^{Km_0}(8^K C_{K,G} L^{-G})^T\ll L^{-ET}$. The polynomial frame-averaging error is smaller. The single-edge nonseparation term of Proposition 6.15, *outside* the $T$-th moment, costs $\ll_K XYL^{4C+3A_0+2} L^{-10J}$ using the exceptional measure above. The repeated-label term costs $\ll_K XY L^{3A_0+4C+3} J_0^2/Z_1$, by its unchanged pair count and projected divisor-square bound. All these estimates require only the chosen finite accuracy, not arbitrary-log decay for an already fixed $Z_1$. Proposition 5.5 with the projected full-slope norm thus gives the claimed $O(XYL^{-R})$ difference.

These are the places where the lower, separated powers $0.1<\vartheta_i<0.2$ in JLL §§5–6 were used. Finite-order error exponents in the separation or repeated-label steps are not raised to a $T$-th power by fiat: the one-step operator bound and the separate exterior exceptional-edge subtraction play different roles.

## 3. The projected major and the size of a short prime sum

We recall the estimates here to track the finite order of choices. For a major arc with reduced denominator $d\le L^{A_0}$ all physical variables and labels are units modulo $Pd$ (the $z$-rough factors exceed $d$ and $Z_1>d$). Use the full product-mod-$Pd$ character expansion on $P\mid cmn-ars$ from the cited major calculation. Each Fourier coefficient of the rational numerator on the kernel of reduction modulo $P$ is at most $1/\phi(P)$, including when $(P,d)>1$. The two endpoint bilinear polynomials $B_\chi$ after separating variables satisfy
$$
 \frac1{\phi(P)}\sum_{\chi\bmod Pd}|B_\chi|^2\ll d P L^{O(C+1)}
$$
by orthogonality since each input product lies in a length $O(P)$ interval and the divisor-square sum is $O(PL^3)$. At most fixed powers of $d$ are spent on the small rational twists. The smooth remaining factor is a function of six normalized log variables and has $j$-th derivatives $O_j((1+L^{A_0})^j)$ because $|\xi|Y\le 2L^{A_0}$ on the arc. A smooth fixed enclosing box and Fourier separation therefore have an $L^1$ transform norm at most a fixed power of $L$; truncation to frequencies $\le L^{C_1}$ costs any prescribed fixed log error by raising $C_1$ and the differentiability used. Before character expansion this error costs $O(PY^2 L^{O(C+1)})$ at each frequency cut: the exact projector $cu\equiv av\bmod P$ makes $O(1)$ possible $v$ for each $u$ and the converse, and there are $O_K(Y^2)$ label pairs. The arc measure is $O(L^{3A_0}/Y)$. These powers depend on the original chosen precisions, not the future exponents $b_i$.

If the inducing conductor of $\chi$ does not divide $\mathrm{lcm}(4,d)$, both its twist by any $d$-character and the further twist by $\chi_4$ are nonprincipal. Hold all but one of the tag primes fixed with product $A$. The remaining complete tag ranges over a subinterval of $[Y/A,1.1Y/A]$ in its original bank, so $x\asymp Y/A\ge Z_1/O(1)$. The twisted prime sum over any such interval is $\ll (Y/A)L^{-D_{\rm maj}}$, after the choice in (2). Indeed for each primitive nonprincipal twist with $f\le4Pd$ the standard explicit formula gives $\sum_{m\le x}\Lambda(m)\chi^*(m)\ll_{\Theta} x^{\Theta'}\log^{O(1)}(2fx)$, for a fixed $\max(1/2,\Theta)<\Theta'<1$ (enlarging $\Theta$ slightly only costs a fixed factor in $b_1$). One can truncate at height $\asymp x$ at half-integer endpoints; functional equation also bounds the real parts away from zero. Using the estimate on prefixes, partial summation removes the log on primes and pays at most a fixed $L^{C_1+O(1)}$ for the bounded-frequency twists. Prime powers contribute $O(\sqrt x\log^2 x)$ before this operation, and imprimitive or target-unit omissions cost $O(L)$ primes. In particular require $(1-\Theta')b_1>D_{\rm maj}+C_1+C_2+10$, where $C_2$ is the absolute log exponent in the explicit formula. The primitivity reduction, including the split at residue 1 modulo 4, has not been assumed to preserve cancellation for an arbitrary subset of prime labels. Summing $1/A$ over the other slots costs $O_K(1)$, giving $O_K(YL^{-D_{\rm maj}})$ on either tag endpoint. Cauchy on the two $B_\chi$ now saves more than all fixed log losses. This choice does not rely on applying Siegel–Walfisz at length $x$ to a modulus of size $L^{A_0}$.

Only $O(d)$ characters with conductor dividing $\mathrm{lcm}(4,d)$ remain. Their full rough/unit Möbius factor has $O_D(ML^{-D})$ for each fixed $D$ by classical Möbius Siegel–Walfisz, the smooth-convolution identity $\mu1_{P^->z}=\mu*1_{P^+\le z}$, and Rankin for the smooth harmonic tail above $\sqrt M$, exactly as in the previous major proof. Here $M\ge P^\delta$ **is polynomial**, so the moduli $\le4d$ and time factors are legitimate. The $O(L)$ omitted target primes above $z$ cost $O(L(M/z+1))$. Both tag endpoints may use $O_K(Y)$, and the $1/\phi(P)$ pays the population of exceptional low modes with the $P/\phi(P)\ll\log L$ loss. Multiplication by total arc measure now gives $O_R(PY L^{-R})$ for all major arcs. With §2 this proves the asserted bound for $Q_Y$.

## 4. Count on the actual equation

Use the true ordered source
$$
 \sum_{n\in I}\Lambda(n)\!\sum_{a\in\mathcal A,\ a\mid P-n}
       {\bf1}_{q=(P-n)/a\ {\rm prime}}\frac{\log q}L .                 \tag{3}
$$
It suffices to take the first coefficient rough and a unit modulo $P$: on actual prime rows only divisors of $P$ or proper powers are excluded, at cost $P^{1/2+o(1)}$. The logarithms normalize (3) as $L(1+o(1))$ times the **unweighted** count after that deletion, since both actual primes have logs $L+O_{\mathbf b}(\log L)$ uniformly. The giant prime in (3) is unique even when $n$ is not prime. Crucially the residual test after tag extraction is simply $1_{h\,\mathrm{prime}}\log h/L$, bounded on $1\le h\le P$, on *each* shell. No replacement of a sparse event by a mean-one bank weight is needed.

Expand Vaughan's identity at $D_0=P^{1/5}$ on the source $n$, with the genuine rough and unit masks from above. The short isolated $\Lambda_{\le D_0}$ term vanishes. In the term $\mu_{>D_0}*(\Lambda_{>D_0}*1)$ each factor is polynomially long; the first has the required full rough/unit Möbius coefficient, the second is bounded by $L$. Packetize the region by log-mesh widths $L^{-B_0}$ as in the earlier fixed-rank proof, with $B_0$ fixed large independently of $K,\mathbf b$. Boundary integers cost $O(PL^{-B_0/2+O(1)})$ by Hölder and fixed divisor moments. Indeed the fourth moment of the normalized tag divisor weight is $O_K(P)$: expand the lcms of up to $4K$ labels; reciprocal lcm sums factor into $O_K(1)$ and total CRT floor errors are $\ll L^{4B}=P^{o(1)}$, hence absorbed. Ordinary fourth moments of the divisor function and $|\beta|\le L$ give a log-loss exponent independent of $K,\mathbf b$. There are only log-power many packets with exponent independent of these constants and $O_{\mathbf b}(\log L)$ label shells. Cauchy in the residual $h\ll P/Y$ gives $\ll (P/Y)^{1/2} Q_Y^{1/2}$ per packet and shell before restoring $\mathcal V$. Thus choosing $R$ large **first**, and then all constants above, makes the Type II error $O(PL^{-3})$.

The two Type I terms are exactly those in [the fixed-multiplier calculation, §2 and §4](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b262263b99802e5445835d22ebd99fe673ef7875/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/near_full_cofactor/Proof.md). For precision here, at fixed $a$ the short rough variable $d\le D_0^2$ and the squarefree small-prime variable $e$ opened to depth $\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor+1$ have product $d e\le P^{.4+o(1)}$, with each $d,e$ determined by their product. We use ordinary maximal-prefix prime Bombieri–Vinogradov at $x=P/a\ge P/L^B$, including for the nonnegative last Bonferroni term; there is a fixed power margin. The reduced residue is $P\bar a\bmod de$; moduli sharing a prime with $P$ or $a$ cannot contribute on eligible unit rows. A possible $q\mid P$ is negligible even on the artificially opened source: at most $O(L)$ values, $a\le L^B$ and all divisor multiplicities $P^{o(1)}$. At firm $a$, replacing prime-power parts in BV costs at most $O(x^{1/2}P^{.4+o(1)})$ with harmless logs; summing all $a\le L^B$ remains power-small. The signed coefficients are $\mu_{\le D_0}$ times $\log(n/d)$ in one term and a short $\mu*\Lambda$ in the second, whose absolute value is at most $L$. The $e$-tail principal costs $\ll_A L^{-A}$ since its harmonic factorial moment is $O((C\log L)^{\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor}/\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor!)$. The remaining sums in rough $d$ and the small-prime local product are exactly those evaluated in the cited §2; applied before removing the $a$-sum they give
$$
 \text{(3)}=\frac{|I|}L\mathfrak S(P)\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac1a
                       \prod_{\ell\mid a}\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}
                +o_K\big(\mathfrak S(P) P/L\big).                       \tag{4}
$$
Specifically the $e$-product omits primes dividing $Pa$, while the completed rough $d$-functional is $\prod_{r\le z}(1-1/r)^{-1}$ up to arbitrary-log error. Their ratio is $\mathfrak S(P)\prod_{\ell\mid a}(\ell-1)/(\ell-2)$ up to relative $O(L/z)$; summing the BV errors costs only $\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}1/a=\mathcal V$. The initial rank factor is $1+O_K(Z_1^{-1})$ uniformly. This proves (1).

There is also a literal multiplier interpretation. The classical two-linear-form upper-bound sieve, applied to $q$ and $P-aq$ at length comparable to $P/a$, gives at most $C\mathfrak S(P)P/(aL^2)$ representations for any one of these multipliers, with an absolute $C$ for $P$ sufficiently large in terms of the fixed constants. There are two roots away from primes dividing $Pa$ and one there, and the extra coefficient correction $\prod_{\ell\mid a}(\ell-1)/(\ell-2)$ tends uniformly to 1. Combining with (1) proves that a positive proportion in the probability $\mathcal V^{-1}/a$ of the chosen multipliers actually occurs. No individual predetermined $a$ is asserted to work.

## What the bound changes, and what it does not

This is finite-rank true-prime partner arithmetic on a shrinking boundary: $\log a/\log P=O_{K}(\log\log P/\log P)$, compared with $O_K((\log P)^{-4/5})$ in the previous fixed-rank construction. It is not a corollary of the conditional Poisson–Dirichlet law for a random complement, which assigns zero limiting mass to every bounded rank, nor of a result averaged in the target. In another direction [Chen's theorem](https://www.ams.org/bookstore/pspdf/gsm-134-prev.pdf) uses only a prime or a semiprime on the opposite leg, with no polylogarithmic bound of the present kind on the cofactor. The [2026 fixed-rank-2 unbalanced Goldbach work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05224) considers $P=p+rq$ with $r$ either 1 or a prime, $r\le q^{a-1}$ for a fixed $a>1$, rather than a multiplier of fixed large rank and polylogarithmic size. These statements are not interchangeable. No claim is made about priority or the truth of the external zero-free result. The mandatory $K$ distinct label factors here prevent $a=1$ and do not cover the needed unguarded rank-2/3/4 composite debit. Letting the construction constants depend on $P$ to claim a bounded multiplier, or taking $K=1$ in §2, is not justified.
