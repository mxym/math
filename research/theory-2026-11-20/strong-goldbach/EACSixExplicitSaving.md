# An explicit (very small) six-label counting exponent from degree-bounded CAD

Supplement to [the six-label strict power saving](EACSixFullStrictPowerSaving.md). This computes a valid positive exponent, not an optimal or practical one. Set
\[
 D=30^{64},\qquad \epsilon=(8D)^{-1}.
\]
Then the full count of actual six-label EAC centers is at most \(X^{3/4-\epsilon+o(1)}\), using the previously proved geometry and counting inputs of the compactness proof. No CAD is executed; the number follows from the degree recurrence proved below. This does not compute an effective numerical cutoff for all the imported counting lemmas, and does not count ordinary Goldbach exceptions.

## 1. A real relaxation to which the stationary proof genuinely applies

It suffices to consider \(\det A=0,\ \operatorname{corank}(I-A)=2,\ p>N^{1/5}\), with a fixed valid chart \(A\) (positive exponent matrix entries are nonnegative integers, row sums 2–4, some row sum 4, no loops, irreducible). Other branches retain stronger previous counts: for \(q\le N^{1/16}\) the three-label supported-minor bound is \(5/8\); otherwise \(p\le N^{1/5}\) uses the older tangent count \(1/5+1/2=7/10\). A prior counted arity≤3 obstruction guarantees a quartic row for the actual doubly singular corank-two class. Since we fix its chart rather than generate numbers, all its graph and rank constraints are exact Boolean selection rules on a finite set of matrices, not polynomial conditions in additional variables.

Take real variables \(N,x_0<\cdots<x_5\) with \(p=x_0,q=x_1,M=x_5\), impose:
 
1. \(N>3,\ p\ge3,\ p^5>N,\ p^4<N,\ Mp<N,\ 3M<N\), and \((pq)^7\le N^5\). The last is eventually valid for actual doubly singular tuples by the earlier \(7/10+o(1)\) product theorem, because \(5/7>7/10\). The other initial inequalities follow from positive bounded composites and the chosen arity class. Include the exact equations \(x_i+\prod_jx_j^{A_{ij}}=N\).
2. **Oddness/integer spacings replaced:** For every pair of monomials \(U,V\) in the six variables of total degrees between 1 and 4, require \(U=V\), \(U-V\ge2\), or \(V-U\ge2\). Require the successive true labels to differ by at least 2 as well (redundant but convenient). Actual odd integer labels satisfy these. Among uses in the stationary proof: opposite factors on a cofactor 4-cycle differ by ≥2; changing a \(p\) to a \(q\) in an arity-four or scaled monomial gives a gap ≥\(2p^3\) or ≥\(2pv\); distinct squares and their mixed product have gaps ≥twice the lesser factor; the mixed axis triple uses a prime \(v\) and virtual quadratic monomial \(m\), and the pure triple uses virtual \(s^2\) versus \(v\). The latter virtual values are not labeled inputs. In the real relaxation we do not assert that a virtual product is composite and a raw is prime: if the virtual and raw were equal all three displayed products would coincide, impossible for distinct inputs, and otherwise the explicit monomial-spacing disjunction supplies the ≥2 gap. The same argument covers \(p^4\) versus a different quartic; those values are ordered by exponents because the base-label order is strict.
3. **Shared-factor divisibility replaced:** Whenever \(A_{ij}>0,A_{kj}>0\) with \(i\ne k\), impose \(|x_i-x_k|\ge2x_j\). In the actual odd setting the difference is a nonzero multiple of the odd prime \(x_j\) and is even. This is the only prime-divisibility inference in the stationary proof (used to make a unique maximum factor occur in at most one row, and when two leading high factors are asymptotic). Chart-known signs of \(i-k\) can replace absolute values by linear forms.
4. **Factor closure, primality, and sign support:** The input/output membership, no self-factor, closure of each selected all-low set when all its rows use such factors, occurrence of every factor, and strong connectivity are chart properties. The nonexistence of a closed proper low set is precisely irreducibility of \(A\), not a primality assumption. The two nonzero sign-cycle support argument and all kernel/rank decompositions use only this finite nonnegative matrix. No other integer, prime, congruence, or oddness inference is used in the full stationary contradiction. In particular the pure-\(s^4\) divisor count, which **does** use integer divisors, is left outside this real relaxation; all charts qualifying for it are handled directly for actual labels.

Choose the frozen seed by chart as in the compact proof: a pure-fourth base \(s\) if present, otherwise \(p\). Throw out all charts having the exact divisor-lane shape for this chosen seed. The actual divisor count over thrown-out charts is at most \(X^{13/20+o(1)}\): \(s<X^{1/4}\), and \(u^2p<N\) with \(p>N^{1/5}\) gives \(u<X^{2/5}\), and \(r\mid(s^4-u)\ne0\). Restricting \(p\) still closer to \(N^{1/4}\) recovers the smaller previously stated exponent for that local lane, but is unnecessary here. No integer-boundary assumptions on this lane enter the real stationary proof.

Thus any unbounded sequence of **these real relaxed tuples** with \(p=N^{1/4+o(1)}\) and with a nonzero frozen tangent whose moving coordinates all have weight at least \(1/2\) falls into the same contradiction as in the full strict-saving note. More precisely, one can assume for every fixed limiting weight \(<1/2\) the corresponding derivative is *identically zero for large terms* in the sequence. All quartet factors then have derivative exactly zero, so the extra \(n\to0\) argument holds over reals. The only numerical input used to make \(q\) strictly below weight \(1/2\) is the explicit \((pq)^7\le N^5\), giving \(\omega_q\le5/7-1/4=13/28<1/2\). The factor-gap uses of the main proof are all itemized above, including that a maximum factor cannot occur twice because its two inputs would have distance \(\ge2M\), yet \(\le M\). The two-support, three-small-label exact tangent argument uses positivity of \(H+e_H(N-H)\), distinct bounded integer **exponents** in a fixed chart, and irreducibility to rule out closed low labels; it does not require primality of their values. This checks the real asymptotic, not just the true-prime subset.

## 2. Eight variables and degree at most 15

For each chart and each candidate least moving position \(i\) not equal to the frozen seed, write \(J\) for the \(6\times7\) Jacobian of the six equations, with respect to the raw labels and \(N\). Delete columns of the frozen seed and all raw indices \(<i\), leaving \(J_i\); let \(J_i^-\) also delete the raw column at \(i\). Existence of a frozen tangent with this earliest nonzero raw coordinate is equivalent to 
\[
 \operatorname{rank}J_i=\operatorname{rank}J_i^-:
\]
the distinguished column must lie in the span of the others, and after scaling the coefficient at it can be chosen equal to 1. This criterion already handles all auxiliary derivatives; there are no differential variables in the polynomial formula. Encode by a finite disjunction on rank \(r\): all \((r+1)\)-minors of \(J_i\) vanish, and some \(r\)-minor of \(J_i^-\) is nonzero (rank zero uses empty minor 1). Entries have degree at most 3. Any 6-minor necessarily uses the \(N\)-column, constant, because one raw column is already deleted; thus the degrees of all minors are at most \(5\cdot3=15\), not 18. Nondegenerate actual frozen tangents have some moving raw coordinate; an all-zero raw differential would force \(\delta N=0\).

Add free \(y\) with \(0<y\le1,\ yN\le p^4,\ yN\le x_i^2\). Let \(E\subset\mathbb R^2_{N,y}\) be the union over permissible charts and indices \(i\) of the projections of these formulas. All use exactly these two free variables and six existential labels, i.e. eight variables total; charts/indices are finite union, not variables. The \(p,q\) power restriction has total degree 14, and the monomial gaps, shared-label gaps, product equations, and cutoff constraints have degrees at most 5. The whole input family has maximum total degree at most 15.

Let \(R(N)=\sup\{y:(N,y)\in E\}\), zero when empty. Thus \(0\le R(N)\le1\). In fact \(R(N)\to0\). Otherwise some unbounded sequence of such real-relaxed witnesses has \(y\ge c>0\), hence \(p\ge c^{1/4}N^{1/4}\) and \(Y:=x_i\ge c^{1/2}\sqrt N\). Only indices \(\ge i\) may have nonzero derivatives, so every raw label of limiting weight \(<1/2\) has exact derivative zero eventually. Apply §1 to that real sequence, fixing a chart, index, and limiting weights. It is impossible. Suprema need not be achieved: an approximate positive witness at \(R(N)/2\) suffices.

## 3. Elementary CAD degree recurrence up to the two-dimensional base

Use the full coefficient-and-Sylvester-minor projection for sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition. Here is a degree-accounted justification, to avoid relying on an unspecified constant in a complexity theorem. For a family of polynomials of total degree at most \(d\), regard them as univariate in the last coordinate. Include in the projection every nonzero coefficient, and all minors of Sylvester matrices for every pair of truncated polynomials from the family (with all possible degrees), also the pairs of each such truncation with its derivative. Constant or identically zero members need only their coefficients. Every determinant has order at most \(2d\) and entries of total degree at most \(d\) in the retained variables, so all projected degrees are at most \(2d^2\).

On a connected base cell where the projected signs are fixed, coefficient signs make each specialization either identically zero throughout, or of a fixed degree. Select its actual degree truncation. Sylvester-matrix ranks are constant by the fixed zero/nonzero minor pattern, so gcd degrees with each other and with the derivative are constant. The latter gives constant number of distinct complex roots. Locally separate the distinct roots into small disjoint disks and use continuity of roots with multiplicities (leading coefficient nonzero). Since no disk can become empty and the distinct count is fixed, each contains exactly one distinct root locally, of fixed multiplicity; a disk centered on a real root retains a real root by conjugation. Thus ordered real roots vary continuously with no internal collisions. Across two polynomials, overlaps cannot be gained locally at a point where the roots were distinct (disjoint neighborhoods), and if any existing overlap disappeared, the gcd degree would fall because multiplicities are locally fixed. Therefore relative order and equality of their root sections are constant locally, hence on the connected base cell. Lift to sections and sectors between these roots; signs are invariant, and the lifted cells are connected and project surjectively to the base cell. The case of a polynomial specializing identically to zero throughout a cell just gives sign zero on its entire cylinder, so no unresolved nullification issue remains. Starting in one real dimension with points and root-separated intervals and applying this lift establishes the required CAD. This is in the classical [Collins projection tradition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02494); the explicit recurrence above is the needed bound.

Starting from \(d=15\) in eight variables, stopping at two variables after six projections gives degrees \(D_2\le (2\cdot15)^{2^6}/2\le D=30^{64}\). We can then project once more to construct the final one-dimensional base intervals in \(N\); that extra degree is irrelevant for the two-dimensional section polynomials. Use all polynomials from all chart formulas in the initial family, so \(E\) is a union of whole cells of the two-dimensional base: original sign truth is constant on each full cell, each witness cell projects through the cylinder onto the entire base cell. For all sufficiently large \(N\) we lie in one unbounded \(N\)-interval. Since \(E\) is downward closed in \(y\) within \((0,1]\), either its fibers are all empty there (then \(R=0\)) or their upper boundary is one fixed CAD section (whether or not attained). The formula includes \(y\le1\), so there is no unbounded upper sector. The section satisfies \(P(N,R(N))=0\) for a fixed nonzero polynomial \(P\) of total degree at most \(D\), by the lift definition.

Write \(P(N,Y)=\sum_{\ell=0}^I N^\ell P_\ell(Y)\) with \(P_I\ne0\), and let \(j\le D\) be the order of vanishing of \(P_I\) at \(Y=0\). As \(R(N)\to0\) and \(R>0\) on the positive-section lane, the equation implies
\[
 R(N)^j\ll |P_I(R(N))|\ll N^{-1}.
\]
Here all lower \(P_\ell\) are bounded for \(R\in[0,1]\). If \(j=0\) this positive section cannot persist; otherwise \(R(N)=O(N^{-1/j})=O(N^{-1/D})\). In all cases \(R(N)\le N^{-1/(2D)}\) eventually. This calculation uses no Puiseux data and no execution of CAD, only the projection degree bound.

## 4. Substituting the explicit saving into the actual count

An actual non-divisor tuple with nonzero frozen tangent has \(Y\) its least moving raw coordinate; it satisfies the rank criterion and hence
\[
 \min(p^4/N,\ Y^2/N)\le R(N)\le N^{-1/(2D)}.
\]
Thus either \(p\le N^{1/4-1/(8D)}\) or \(Y\le N^{1/2-1/(4D)}\). In the former apply the older least-\(p\) tangent count (or small-\(q\) supported-minor branch) fixing \(p\) and one label of value at most \(\sqrt N\). It costs \(X^{3/4-1/(8D)+o(1)}\). In the latter freeze the designated seed (pure-fourth \(s\) replacing \(p\), or \(p\) itself) and \(Y\); the compactness proof's direct rank argument for \(s\) ensures legitimate at-most-one tangent, and the selected \(Y\) kills it. It costs \(X^{3/4-1/(4D)+o(1)}\). Vanishing frozen tangent costs only \(X^{1/4+o(1)}\) by the zero-tangent bound. Other prior branches have exponent \(\le7/10\) or \(13/20\), comfortably below \(3/4-1/(8D)\).

This explicitly yields \(\epsilon=1/(8\cdot30^{64})\) (approximately \(3.64\cdot10^{-96}\)). It is a deliberately loose number: the degree estimate involves all minors and every chart and could be improved by tailored elimination. What is calculated here is the exponent, not CAD tables, evaluated ranges, a count of six-label examples, or a threshold for binary Goldbach.
