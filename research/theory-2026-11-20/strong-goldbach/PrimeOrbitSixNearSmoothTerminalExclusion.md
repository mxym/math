# A fixed prime cannot lie in a six-label terminal all-composite orbit along near-smooth centers

**Theorem.** Fix a finite set of primes F and an odd prime p. There is no unbounded sequence of even N coprime to p with log(N/N_F)=o(log N) whose six-prime, strongly connected, closed factor orbit contains p and has all complements composite. Here N_F is the full F-part. The conclusion assumes a fixed seed, the stated near-smoothness, and strong connectivity. This does not yet exclude a six-label *nonterminal forward orbit* from p, an arbitrary six-prime component not containing p, or a Goldbach exception.

On a hypothetical sequence extract logarithmic-weight limits w_i=lim log x_i/log N. The [fixed-seed independence bound](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) and feedback bound give nullity(I−A)=2 for the complete six-by-six multiplicity matrix A. There are at least three positive weights by [the macroscopic-rank theorem](PrimeOrbitMacroscopicRank.md) and at most five since p is fixed. The [five-macro](PrimeOrbitFiveMacroExclusion.md) and [four-macro](PrimeOrbitFourMacroNearSmoothExclusion.md) results rule out those two counts. Thus we have three small labels S={p,q,r} (q<r and q→∞ by [multiple-seed minimality](PrimeOrbitMultiSeedMinimal.md)), and three macro labels with positive weights. At least one macro is high, meaning its weight equals one, by [the no-high exclusion](PrimeOrbitThreeMacroNoHighExclusion.md). The proof below eliminates the remaining weight patterns.

**A singleton exists.** Write P for the limiting stochastic mass matrix and W for the diagonal weight matrix as in the no-high proof. Every distinct small input's row has disjoint support from the others, has no p-mass, and rank(P−W)≤4. These statements use near-smoothness at p but require no bound w<1 on the macros: each actual complementary input is ≤(N−3)/3, hence each complement is of size comparable to N. The combinatorial argument in that paper's section “The only remaining stochastic pattern” likewise does not use w<1: on the at-most-one-dimensional quotient by the three disjoint small rows, unused macro coordinates or pairs in a common block give the stated orientation contradictions. In particular, absent a singleton small row e_J at a macro J, the three small supports must be {t}, {s,A}, {B,C} with {s,t}={q,r} and A,B,C the macros. This would force *all* three weights below one, because a positive exponent at A appears alongside positive s mass, and B and C both have positive mass in their common row. This contradicts the high label. Thus there is a small-direct singleton.

**Its alternatives.** If P_s=e_J for small s, then N−s=v J^d with d=1/w_J fixed integral and v=N^o(1). For each small x, gcd(N−J,N−x) divides the positive subpower integer v x^d−x+s, so P_J is disjoint from the three small rows. The four disjoint stochastic rows (the three small rows and P_J) belong to and span row(P−W). Each macro must occupy a singleton block: its adjusted row has one strictly negative entry at itself and all other entries nonnegative, so a negatively weighted block in this disjoint basis can contain only that one coordinate.

If all three macros R_i are such direct singleton children of the three smalls s_i, write N−s_i=v_i R_i^{d_i}. Their own rows are disjoint from the three macro singleton blocks, hence N−R_i factors on the smalls only. The exact first-return rank-one calculation in “Excluding a small singleton macro block” of the no-high paper gives s_i Z^{a_i}=v_i(N−R_i)^{d_i}, a primitive positive integer vector a, Z²≡1 mod N and Z≥sqrt(N+1). Consequently a_i are bounded and a_i=d_i/gcd_j(d_j) after fixing them; the s_i valuation then shows a_i divides 1, so all d_i are equal. These deductions use only v_i,s_i subpower, 2N/3≤N−R_i<N, and the presence of actual singleton arcs; the later assumption R_i/N=O(N^(−η)) from the no-high paper is **not** needed or invoked here. As some w_i=1, all three are then high.

Otherwise exactly two macros X,Y are directly singleton-parented by distinct small inputs s_X,s_Y, and the fourth basis row must be the singleton e_H for the third macro. Both P_X and P_Y equal e_H: each is disjoint from the small rows, and each belongs to the four-row span because e_X,e_Y do. The remaining small input t has no macro factor since its row is disjoint from these singleton blocks (including P_X). Thus

    N−s_X=a_X X^{d_X},  N−s_Y=a_Y Y^{d_Y},
    N−X=u H^f,          N−Y=v H^f,

where d_X,d_Y,f are fixed positive integers, equal to the reciprocals of the respective weights, and all four coefficients are positive and N^o(1).

* Exactly one high: if it is X or Y this violates [the unique-high/small-parent theorem](PrimeOrbitSixOneHighResultants.md), which explicitly assumes no smoothness. If it is H, then the last two complements share H^f of weight one (indeed f=1), although their gcd divides the difference of the two intermediate inputs X,Y, of weight strictly below one. Both possibilities are impossible.
* Two highs, one of them H: relabel so X,H are high and Y intermediate. Then d_X=f=1, and a_X X=N−s_X gives a_X u H=(a_X−1)N+s_X. Substituting vH=N−Y yields

      a_X u Y+v s_X = [a_X u−v(a_X−1)] N.

  The positive left side is o(N) because all coefficients and s_X are subpower and Y has fixed intermediate weight. It cannot equal an integer multiple of N.
* Two highs, both direct: X,Y are high and H intermediate. Neither X nor Y can divide N−H: if N−H=z X, then z=N^o(1), and a_X H−z s_X=(a_X−z)N. The left side is strictly positive for sufficiently large N (H has a fixed positive weight and z,s_X are subpower) and o(N), impossible. The loop at H is absent, so P_H is supported on small columns only. Since e_H belongs to the four-row span, P_H does too; it must equal the sole basis block supported only on smalls, namely P_t. On the other hand a_X u H^f=(a_X−1)N+s_X, so N−H divides F(N), F(T)=a_X u T^f−(a_X−1)T−s_X. The small prime t differs from s_X; hence F(t)≡−s_X mod t is nonzero and |F(t)|=N^o(1). This bounds gcd(N−H,N−t), contradicting P_H=P_t. There is no need to identify t with p.

Thus any remaining sequence has three highs. All three are eventually strictly above sqrt(N), and the three smalls below it. By [the half-above-square-root theorem](PrimeOrbitHalfAboveRoot.md) for this irreducible six-label matrix of nullity two, there is an even h>0 and a positive W₀ with rad(W₀)=pqr such that every 1+hs for s∈{p,q,r} divides W₀. However, the **multiplier lemma** in [the six-label smooth-center theorem](PrimeOrbitSixSmoothExclusion.md), section “The multiplier lemma and the right choice of scale,” is independent of N: with p fixed and q<r, q→∞ there is no sequence of positive even h for which all three 1+hs factor over {p,q,r}. We use that lemma, not the smooth-center hypothesis of its surrounding theorem. This eliminates the final high pattern and proves the terminal assertion.

The [six-label fork](PrimeOrbitSixLabelFork.md) gives the precise alternative still requiring separate work for a general six-prime all-composite forward orbit from p on this scale: a unit-multiplicity two-cycle containing p feeds a four-label terminal component not containing p. The present theorem does not move that terminal component's small primes to fixed p.
