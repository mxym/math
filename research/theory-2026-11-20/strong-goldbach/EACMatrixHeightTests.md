# Two effective height tests for components with several mixed rows

Take Bożek's actual factor-closed strongly connected component with k>=2 distinct odd prime labels x_i, all coprime to N, all complementary rows composite. Fix the nonnegative integral exponent matrix A in the orientation N-x_i=product_j x_j^(A_ij), with A_ii=0. Each x_i<=N/3, since it occurs as a factor of an odd composite complement. Assume A is invertible over Q and put w=A^(-1)1. The log equations imply
    x_i=N^(w_i) exp(E_i),   |E_i| <= log(3/2) sum_j |(A^(-1))_ij|.
This exact uniform bound needs no Diophantine approximation. For an unbounded family the weights necessarily lie in [0,1]. The tests below are local to a component and do not presume failure of Goldbach.

## A criterion using the fiber at N=0

Let D be the least common denominator of w_1,...,w_k. Suppose det(I-A) is nonzero and
    D > |det(I-A)|.
Then the fixed matrix A admits only finitely many centers, with an effective bound obtained by finite rational polynomial arithmetic. This extends the integral projector to certain matrices with every row mixed and no prime-power pivot. For example take rows (0,0,1,2), (1,0,2,0), (0,2,0,2), (2,1,0,0). The directed graph is strongly connected, det A=14, det(I-A)=-5, and w=(6,2,4,5)/14, giving D=14 and the criterion. This is a test signature and does not assert it has a prime solution.

Proof. In the Q-algebra R on n,X_1,...,X_k given by the complement equations, examine the special fiber n=0. If any coordinate is zero, all its predecessor coordinates are zero, so strong connectivity forces the origin. This point is reduced, since the derivative of the system with respect to the X_i is the identity at the origin (all complementary monomials have total degree >=2). If all coordinates are nonzero the special fiber is the Laurent binomial system X^(e_i-A_i)=-1, where e_i is the coordinate vector and A_i is the corresponding row. Smith normal form of I-A gives exactly s=|det(I-A)| distinct solutions over the algebraic closure in the torus. All are simple in characteristic 0, as can also be seen from the invertible logarithmic Jacobian. Thus dim_Q R/nR=s+1. This statement refers to exact equations, including the origin and its multiplicity.

Choose a rational basis of R/nR including the class of 1. All polynomials reduce into the span of n^h times those basis representatives, 0<=h<m, modulo n^m. One exact implementation computes the fiber Groebner basis, lifts its polynomial division identities through the stated relations after subtracting the fiber remainders, divides the resulting multiple of n in the presentation, and repeats m times. This uses rational operations and can track identities. Equivalently a Groebner computation for (I,n^m) can recover the relation after the dimension argument. The span has (s+1)m coordinates, which need not be independent. The formal solution X_i(n)=n+O(n^2) exists in Q[[n]] by iterating the system X_i=n minus its monomial starting at zero. Evaluation of the coordinates at this solution modulo n^m has rank m because n^h is included. For any polynomial U, the reduced column of X_1^m U maps to zero, hence all such columns lie in an at most sm-dimensional space.

The group generated by w_i modulo 1 has order D. Pick D nonnegative exponent vectors nu with distinct weights v_nu=w·nu modulo 1 (take each coefficient in 0,...,D-1). Choose all U=n^j X^nu with integer j>=0 and weight H=j+v_nu<m. The weights are distinct and differ by at least 1/D. With H_0=max v_nu, the number T of such U is >=D(m-H_0-1)>sm once m is sufficiently large, since D>s. Thus a nonzero integer linear combination P of these distinct monomials has X_1^m P=0 in R/(n^m). Rational ideal membership gives a computable positive integer C clearing the finite identity denominators. At a physical tuple N^m divides C x_1^m P(N,x), hence divides C P(N,x), using gcd(x_1,N)=1. No restriction on prime divisors of C or N is required.

Each selected monomial has positive size between computable fixed constants times N^H by the log equations. Its largest possible weight is m-1/D. Thus |C P|<N^m for N past a computable threshold. The unique maximal-weight term dominates all lower terms past another computable threshold, so P(N,x) is nonzero. This contradicts divisibility, completing the effective proof. This reproduces the pure cycle numerical inequality D>s (the pure cycle has s=D-1), while covering other signatures under the same numerical test. It does not furnish a uniform numerical constant as A varies.

## A short signed congruence that even works when det(I-A)=0

If some nonzero vector u=c^t(I-A) with integer c has both positive and negative weights
    h_+=w·u_+ <1,     h_-=w·u_- <1,
the fixed signature has an effective height bound without any assumption on det(I-A). The rows give X^(e_i-A_i)=-1 modulo N in the group of units, hence x^u=(-1)^(sum c_i) modulo N. Clearing negative exponents gives x^(u_+) congruent to plus or minus x^(u_-) modulo N. Both monomials have size O_A,u(N^h) with h=max(h_+,h_-)<1. Their signed difference cannot be exactly zero, by unique factorization of the distinct prime labels if the sign is positive and positivity if it is negative. Past a computable N it has magnitude <N, contradicting the congruence. One may check existence by enumerating the finitely many u satisfying the two strict mass bounds whenever all w_i>0, with lattice membership by Smith normal form. If c lies in the left integer kernel of I-A and its sum is odd, the original congruence is already incompatible with N>2.

The weight and congruence computations below are checked using exact integer and rational arithmetic in [the companion program](check_eac_matrix_height.py). This test gives effective height for the following actual all-height signatures, where the row names identify the original factorization and the new signature can have different prime values.
- N=128 signature, k=8. Use c_3=1, c_13=c_23=-1. Then u has positive monomial x_3^2 x_7 and negative monomial x_5 x_13, weights 11/12 and 10/12. Here det(I-A)=0. The weighted absolute inverse row-sums on the two supports are 43/12 and 34/12, so N<=2^12(3/2)^43 suffices.
- N=1718 signature, k=28. Use c_3=1, c_31=c_241=-1. The two monomials are x_3 x_211 and x_5 x_7 x_31, both weights 6/7. The two row-sum costs are 112/42 and 292/42, so N<=2^7(3/2)^(146/3) suffices. Again det(I-A)=0.
- N=1928 signature, k=14. Use c_3=1, c_5=c_641=-1. The two monomials are x_3^4 x_13 and x_5^3 x_7, both weights 8/9. The row-sum costs are 288/9 and 62/9, giving N<=2^9(3/2)^288. Again det(I-A)=0.
The 1862 and 6142 signatures also have det(I-A)=0. A bounded check of signed combinations of up to four distinct rows found no strict mass witness in those two. This does not rule out other witnesses. The 2200 pure cycle is covered by the preceding projector (D=21, s=20). The six represented centers are historical controls, not supposed Goldbach counterexamples.

## Sources and scope

The component notion and represented examples come from [Bożek’s original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900v1). The algebraic argument uses the standard torus binomial calculation, as in [Eisenbud–Sturmfels, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9401001), or directly by Smith diagonalization as above. The determinant argument extends [the pure-cycle integer projector](EACPureCycleHeight.md), while [the one-mixed proof](EACOneMixedProjector.md) uses a different local fiber condition. The related [Kelanemer record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460) describes exponent matrices under a global counterexample hypothesis; the latest full text was not accessible. The criteria here are sufficient restrictions on fixed matrices and apply also to represented centers. No arbitrary-signature or Goldbach finiteness result is claimed. The three historical congruences serve as controls; the [all-height historical classification](EACHistoricalSignatureRigidity.md) is much stronger on those matrices.
