# All binary relation fibers can be connected despite one binary hole

This is an abstract counterexample to deducing saturation of degree two from affine Freiman rigidity, toricity, or even full saturation in degree three. It is **not** a counterexample concerning the actual primes.

[Michael Nelson, *Additive Relations and Betti Growth in Goldbach Rings*, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14580v2) studies the ring with generators indexed by odd primes and quadratic relations identifying equal two-prime sums, with a bidegree given by the sum and the number of factors. His affine rigidity is a statement about already available relations, and is not a degree-two existence statement. The following example works even if one strengthens it to connectedness of every binary-move fiber, so that the quadratic ring itself equals its toric quotient.

## Proposition

For any integer a≥4 put k=a−2 and

    A={0,1,…,k} ∪ {a} ∪ {2a,2a+1,…}.

Then \(2A=\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\setminus\{2a-1\}\) and \(3A=\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Every map \(g:A\to G\), to any abelian group, for which \(g(u)+g(v)\) depends only on \(u+v\), is affine. Moreover for every sum e and cardinality d, all unordered lists of d members of A totaling e are connected by moves replacing two entries by two entries with the same sum. In particular the quadratic-relation ring over any field is a domain.

## Proof

**Sums.** Two small elements give [0,2a−4]; a plus a small element gives [a,2a−2]. These cover [0,2a−2] because a≥4. The value a+a=2a is already in the tail, and adding 0 to a tail element gives every e≥2a. No pair gives 2a−1. Finally 2a−1=a+k+1 and every other sum extends to degree three by 0.

**Affine map.** On the small block, the relations (j+1)+0=j+1 with the right-hand side considered as the pair (j,1) give \(g(j+1)-g(j)=g(1)-g(0)\). Put \(b=g(1)-g(0)\). The relation \(a+0=k+2\), with right-hand pair (k,2), gives \(g(a)=g(0)+ab\); here \(k≥2\). In the tail the same adjacent recurrence from 0 and 1 gives \(g(j)=g(0)+jb+c\) for some c independent of j≥2a. The relation \(2a+0=a+a\) gives \(c=0\). Thus the affine rigidity holds over arbitrary G, including finite torsion groups.

**Fiber connectivity.** Among entries in [0,k], unit transfers connect all bounded occupancies with the same total and number of slots: if two labeled occupancy vectors disagree, transfer one unit from a coordinate above its target to a coordinate below its target, both staying in [0,k]. We can then forget labels.

If a list has a tail entry T≥2a, that entry absorbs all others using (T,u)→(T+u,0). Thus all lists with a tail are connected to (e,0,…,0). If e≥2a but no tail, then two a entries immediately create a tail by (a,a)→(2a,0). With at most one a, the sum of the small entries is at least a. Redistribute the small entries to fill one slot to k and a second as far as possible. Their sum s is ≥a because after filling k there are at least 2 units left and k≥2; also s≤2k. Replace this pair by (a,s−a), where 0≤s−a≤a−4≤k. Repeat if necessary: when only one a has been created, remaining small weight e−a≥a still allows the same move. This produces a tail, so the fiber with e≥2a is connected.

If e<2a, no tail can occur and there is at most one a. Lists with a fixed number of a entries connect by the bounded transfers. To connect the two possibilities when they both exist, feasibility of a small-only list gives e≤dk. For a list with one a the remaining weight is e−a. Among its d−1 small slots, use bounded transfers to make one equal to \(b_0=\max(0,e-a-(d-2)k)\), filling other slots first. Since e≤dk, \(b_0≤2k-a=a−4=k−2\), hence replace (a,b₀) by the small pair (k,a+b₀−k). This connects the two classes. The cases d=0 or 1 are trivial.

For the ring assertion, map its generator \(x_u\) to \(t z^u\) in the polynomial ring over the field. The kernel of the polynomial-ring map is spanned by differences of monomials of matching sum and degree: distinct monomials in \(t,z\) are linearly independent. Each difference is telescoped along binary moves, so the kernel is exactly the ideal generated by the stated quadratics. Its quotient is a subring of a polynomial domain. ∎

## Prime-ring scope

For odd prime labels a convenient reindexing is \(u=(p-3)/2\). Its additive semigroup lies in \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2}\) and has generators \((u,1)\) with 0 and 1 present. Even if the actual quadratic ring equaled the toric quotient, full normality of that toric quotient is impossible: for a missing degree-one index u>0 the monomial \(t z^u\) is in the fraction field, and for an available index j≥u its j-th power equals \((tz^j)^u t^{j-u}\), which is in the ring. Thus the degree-one holes already prevent full normality. Binary Goldbach asks only for degree-two saturation. The example above has one arbitrarily large degree-two hole, complete coverage in degree three and higher, and even maximal relation-fiber connectivity. Inferring that the prime ring has no degree-two holes would require prime-specific structure beyond these algebraic invariants.
