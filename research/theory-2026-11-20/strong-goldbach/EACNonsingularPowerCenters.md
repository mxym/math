# Nonsingular terminal signatures: arbitrary centers and power-free valuations

Let \(A=(A_{ij})\) be a **fixed** strongly connected, loopless nonnegative integral \(k\times k\) matrix with every row sum at least two. An actual all-composite Bożek terminal component at an even center \(N>2\) consists of distinct odd primes \(x_i\), units modulo \(N\), with \(N-x_i=\prod_jx_j^{A_{ij}}\). Every \(x_i\le N/3\), by occurrence as a factor in a composite row. Assume throughout the main theorem that \(B=I-A\) is nonsingular over \(\mathbb Q\); this does **not** require \(A\) to be nonsingular.

**Theorem.** The number of centers \(N\le X\) realizing this fixed signature is \(O_A(1+X^{1/4})\), with no smoothness restriction. For every fixed finite set \(F\) of rational primes, all sufficiently large realizing centers have
\[
         \gcd\{v_l(N):\,l\notin F,\ v_l(N)>0\}=1,
\]
where the empty gcd is zero. Thus the part outside \(F\) is neither \(1\) nor any proper power, **including exponents varying with \(N\)**. Also \(N_F<N^{1/4+\epsilon}\) eventually for each \(\epsilon>0\), where \(N_F\) is the full \(F\)-part. These statements concern occurrences of one fixed composite component, not ordinary Goldbach exceptions. They do not bound all growing signatures.

### 1. Global dimension from the full modulus

Work over characteristic zero. The signed torus root equations at center variable \(T=0\) are \(X^{B_i}=-1\). They have exactly \(b=|\det B|\) points over the algebraic closure, by Smith normal form, all with root-of-unity coordinates of orders dividing \(2b\). Each such point is smooth on the full chart \(T=X_i+X^{A_i}\), with \(T\) an unramified local parameter: the Jacobian in logarithmic \(X\)-coordinates is \(\operatorname{diag}(X_i)B\) up to overall sign. Also every actual \(x_i^{2b}\equiv1\bmod N\), by the same adjugate calculation in the group of units. Consequently no bounded prime label can recur as \(N\to\infty\).

Consider the geometric Zariski closure of all actual tuples in the affine torus open (all labels inverted). It has finitely many components; on each component containing a dense set of rational tuples off the pairwise intersections, Galois invariance implies definition over \(\mathbb Q\). Discard components with bounded center (only finitely many physical tuples). Each remaining component must meet \(T=0\) **in this open**: otherwise the Nullstellensatz on its affine coordinate ring gives \(1=T H\) with rational coefficients (descending a linear ideal membership from \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) if needed). Clearing rational and Laurent denominators gives a nonzero integer \(c\) and, at every physical point on it, \(N\mid c\prod_i x_i^h\) for some fixed \(h\). As all labels are units mod \(N\), this would give \(N\mid c\). Since that nonempty special fiber is finite, the principal ideal theorem bounds the dimension by one. The dimension is exactly one because the center is nonconstant. This does not assume a fixed prime power dividing the centers.

More generally the same dimension argument works in any fixed corank if the augmented torus fiber
\[
       X^{B_i}=-1,\qquad \sum_i c_i/X_i=0\quad(c\in\ker B^\top)
\]
is finite. Use integral left-kernel generators \(c\). Actual points have \(\prod_i(1-T/X_i)^{c_i}=(-1)^{\sum_i c_i}=1\), the last sign following by reduction mod \(N>2\); the divided equation obtained after subtracting 1 and dividing by \(T\) specializes to \(-\sum_i c_i/X_i=0\). Invert all \(X_i\) and \(T-X_i\) to write this regularly. These extra denominators remain units mod \(N\) on physical points. This remark establishes dimension, not transversality or the main theorem for general corank.

Take the affine closure of each curve in the original integer coordinates \(T,X_i\), with no reciprocal coordinates required to be integral. The rational smooth projective model has an infinite integral sequence only if it has genus zero and at most two geometric points at infinity, by [Siegel's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2097). Normalization preserves integrality outside fixed denominators: a regular function on the affine normalization is integral over the affine coordinate ring, and clearing fixed coefficients in its monic equation bounds denominators at physical integral points. Omit the finitely many singular exceptional points. There is a rational point, so the projective genus-zero model is a rational line over \(\mathbb Q\). At least one puncture is needed since \(T\) is nonconstant.

### 2. A circle obstruction at a linear label

With a single puncture, it is rational. Take a parameter \(t\) whose only pole is there; a fixed rational rescaling makes it integral on the physical sequence. Then \(T=Q(t),\ X_i=P_i(t)\) are rational polynomials. Orient the sequence to \(t\to+\infty\); their leading coefficients are positive. Put \(D=\deg Q\). Each \(P_i\) is nonconstant, is irreducible up to rational constants, and no two are proportional. Indeed the product of two fixed nonconstant polynomial factors at integer \(t\), after clearing fixed denominators, cannot be a fixed multiple of an unbounded prime: a factor not divisible by the prime would have to divide that fixed multiple. A fixed ratio of two distinct growing primes is likewise impossible. In particular the \(P_i\) are pairwise coprime.

All zeros of \(Q\) have all \(P_i\ne0\): one zero label at \(T=0\) forces its parents, then by connectivity all labels, to vanish. Hence all zeros land in the signed torus. The nonsingular Jacobian in section 1 implies all zeros are simple and no two distinct parameter points give the same torus tuple, since there is only one nonsingular branch there and normalization is an isomorphism. In particular \(D\le b\), and at every zero \(\alpha\) of \(Q\), every \(P_i(\alpha)\) has complex absolute value one. Each row has \(\deg(Q-P_i)=D\), by \(N-x_i\ge2N/3\); every factor has positive degree and occurs somewhere, so \(\deg P_i\le D-1\).

Suppose some label is linear and change variable to \(z=P_i(t)\); the coefficients stay real rational. All \(D\) distinct zeros of \(Q(z)\) now lie on \(|z|=1\). Choose any individual factor \(F=P_j\) of this label's row, write \(Q(z)-z=F(z)G(z)\), \(d=\deg F\), and put \(F^*(z)=z^dF(1/z)\). At any of the zeros, \(F^*(z)=z^d/F(z)\) since \(|F(z)|=1\). Therefore
\[
                     z^{d-1}G(z)+F^*(z)=0
\]
there. The left side has degree at most \(D-1\), so vanishes identically. Its first summand has degree \(D-1\), hence \(d\ge D-1\). This holds for **every** factor, counted with multiplicity; at least two occur, and their degrees total \(D\). Thus \(D\le2\). Degree one is impossible since every row has at least two positive-degree factors. At \(D=2\) all labels are linear. At the least real root of any label (their roots are distinct, since proportionality is impossible) the other labels are strictly negative. The row of the vanishing label consists of two other factors counted with multiplicity, and gives \(Q>0\). A parent row gives \(Q\) equal to its nonzero negative label. Contradiction.

Consequently there is **no linear label in any one-puncture branch**, and \(D\ge4\) by the row degree sum. The integer parameter satisfies \(|t|=O_A(1+X^{1/D})\) for centers up to \(X\) on each fixed curve. This gives the one-puncture count.

### 3. Two punctures and the smooth fraction

Over a field splitting the punctures choose a parameter \(t\) with them at zero and infinity; \(t,t^{-1}\) have fixed bounded denominators at integral points, by normalization. Rational punctures thus give finitely many rational parameter values. For conjugate quadratic punctures choose \(t\) so its conjugate under the quadratic field automorphism is \(c/t\), fixed \(c\in\mathbb Q^\times\), at rational points. Both fractional ideals \((t),(t^{-1})\) are bounded in denominator and hence range over finitely many ideals. Thus \(t\) ranges over finitely many fixed multiples of algebraic integer units. The imaginary quadratic case is finite; in the real quadratic case there are finitely many geometric progressions under powers of a fundamental unit. The rational center is a Laurent polynomial with poles of the same positive order at the two conjugate punctures (otherwise it is constant). Thus as \(|t|\to\infty\) or \(|t|\to0\), the center magnitude grows like a fixed power of \(\max(|t|,|t|^{-1})\). There are \(O_A(1+\log X)\) possibilities of size at most \(X\). Together with section 2 and the finite number of curves this proves the arbitrary-center count.

A two-puncture sequence has \(N_F=N^{o(1)}\) for each fixed \(F\): [the earlier Subspace Theorem argument, section 2](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md), excludes \(N_F\ge N^\eta\) infinitely often for any fixed \(\eta>0\). It uses only the Laurent presentation and the parameter's bounded finite valuations, which hold here without any assumption on the original center sequence. On the polynomial branch the weighted \(p\)-adic Subspace Theorem calculation in [the transverse theorem](EACTransverseKernel.md) gives \(\limsup\log N_F/\log N\le1/D\): all approached roots of \(Q\) are simple. Explicitly at positive-mass \(l\) one can pass to a root \(\alpha_l\) with \(|t-\alpha_l|_l\ll N_{\{l\}}^{-1}\); multiplying local forms on the primitive \((t,1)\) alongside the archimedean coordinate forms gives a contradiction if \(N_F>|t|^{1+\eta}\) infinitely often. Thus \(D\ge4\) proves the stated cutoff.

### 4. No proper powers outside a prescribed finite alphabet

Suppose \(N=b_0y^m\) where the integer \(b_0>0\) is supported on the fixed \(F\), \(y\) is a positive integer, and \(m\ge2\) can vary. This includes all centers forbidden by the gcd statement. On the one-puncture branch use \(f(t)=Q(t)\), with at least four simple roots. On a two-puncture branch over a fixed splitting number field \(K\), choose a finite set of places \(S\) containing the archimedean places, the primes above \(F\), and the finitely many needed for polynomial coefficients and parameter units. Then \(t\in\mathcal O_S^\times\); write \(T=t^{-d}g(t)\), \(d\) the pole order at zero and \(g(0)\ne0\). At least one nonzero zero of \(g\) is simple: the global torus-intersection argument in section 1 supplies a zero on the original affine torus, and the full Jacobian there is nonsingular. Since \(\mathcal O_S^\times/(\mathcal O_S^\times)^3\) is finite, write \(t=\delta u^3\) with \(\delta\) fixed on one of finitely many classes and \(u\) an \(S\)-unit. The polynomial \(f(u)=g(\delta u^3)\) therefore has at least three **distinct simple** zeros over \(\overline K\); the multiplier \(t^d b_0\) is an \(S\)-unit. For the one-puncture case enlarge \(S\) similarly and use \(u=t\). In both cases we have one of finitely many fixed polynomials \(f\in\mathcal O_S[U]\) and
\[
                        f(u)=b' y^m,\qquad b'\in\mathcal O_S^\times .
\]

Apply [Bérczes, Bugeaud, Győry, Mello, Ostafe and Sha, Theorems 2.2 and 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09704). When \(y\notin\mathcal O_S^\times\), Theorem 2.2 bounds \(m\) in terms of \(K,S,f\) and the \(S\)-norm of \(b'\), which equals 1 independent of its height. For each remaining \(m\), the three simple roots have LeVeque indices \(m,m,m\), so Theorem 2.1(i) for \(m=2\), or (ii) for \(m\ge3\), bounds \(h(u)\) also independently of the unit multiplier. If \(y\) is an \(S\)-unit, absorb \(y^m\) in the multiplier and apply Theorem 2.1(i) with exponent 2 and new right-side variable 1. Bounded height in the fixed number field yields only finitely many \(u\). This also deals with \(N\) itself \(F\)-smooth. We do not assert an effective global height bound after the qualitative Siegel component reduction.
