# Conditional laws for the reflected prime-factor process, unbiased and divisor-weighted

Write $\lambda(m)=(-1)^{\Omega(m)}$. Let $P\to\infty$ through prescribed positive integers, and write $m=P-p$, where $p$ is a **genuine prime**. Rank the logarithms of the prime factors of $m$ in decreasing order, **with multiplicity**, divide by $\log m$, and pad by zeros. Write $V(m)$ for this sequence (use all zeros at $m=1$).

**Conditional theorem.** The following are consequences of the fixed-target projected estimate specified below.

1. For $p$ uniform on the primes of any fixed macroscopic interior interval $I=[uP,vP]$, $0<u<v<1$, the finite-dimensional distributions of $(V(P-p),\lambda(P-p))$ tend to those of $\mathrm{PD}(1)$ and an **independent fair sign**. In particular, for each fixed $0<\delta<1$ and either sign, the number of genuine representations in this interval with $P^+(P-p)\le P^\delta$ and that sign is
   $$
   \left(\tfrac12\rho(1/\delta)+o(1)\right)\,|I|/\log P,
   $$
   where $\rho$ is Dickman's function. The same process limit holds on all $p<P$.
2. Give each prime $p<P$ weight $2^{\omega(P-p)}$, normalized by the sum of these weights. Then $(V(P-p),\lambda(P-p))$ tends to $\mathrm{PD}(2)$ and an **independent fair sign**. This establishes, under the indicated input, convergence for the precise full-target process in [Gorodetsky–Grimmelt, Corollary 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13585v3#S1): their published theorem states that **if** the limit is a Poisson–Dirichlet law then its parameter must be 2. Their Erdős–Kac theorem is already unconditional; this is a different question. We use two unrestricted theorems and a restricted-support calculation of theirs. We do not assert the second result conditional on *only* a parity estimate for $P-p$.

All laws here concern each prescribed target with the hypothesis, not averaging over targets. For ordinary primes in the first conclusion, even for arbitrarily small fixed $\delta>0$ a positive proportion of complements are genuinely smooth and have either factor-count parity. The represented complements here are smooth, not asserted to be prime. Neither law detects a complement with one, two, three or four prime factors at its natural density. The Poisson–Dirichlet process has infinitely many parts almost surely; a partition with finitely many nonzero parts has limit probability zero. There is no Goldbach assertion.

**Input and status.** Use [the full-modulus projected comparison, including the major calculation in §1, at the fixed commit](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b262263b99802e5445835d22ebd99fe673ef7875/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/near_full_cofactor/Proof.md). The [minor comparison itself](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/f22749a7/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/target_slope_switch/Proof.md) is stated for every positive integer target. Although the later near-full-cofactor application is stated for even targets, its §1 major estimate is unchanged for odd ones: reduction of unit groups modulo $Pd$ to modulo $P$ is onto, the possible primitive twists have conductors dividing $4Pd$, and coprime tag primes are taken in the class 1 modulo 4 in either case. The even-target restriction used in its subsequent §2 binary Euler factors is not used here; our Type I computation is given below. The minor calculation uses deterministic estimates of [Jiang–Lau–Lü, §§5–6, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2). This in-repository projected minor comparison is a separate dependency: JLL Theorem 4.1 itself does not give the claimed comparison for a growing target $P$. The pointwise major estimate, not a consequence here of classical mean-value theorems, requires the following local zero-free hypothesis. Write $L=\log P, H=\log L$. With suitable fixed $A_0,A_1$ and any fixed $B_0>0$, for every $d\le L^{A_0}$, each primitive character of conductor $f>L^{B_0}$ dividing $4Pd$ must have no zero with
$$
\Re s>1-H^2/L^{1/10},\qquad |\Im s|\le L^{A_1}.
$$
It suffices, in particular, to require this for every fixed choice of analytical constants along the sequence of targets in the theorem; only one finite accuracy is needed in the projected estimate below, since its tag extraction is against divisor-bounded tests and all modulus-distribution accuracies are classical. The external all-Dirichlet zero-free half-plane $\Re s>7/8$, as asserted in [OpenAI, Sept. 30, Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf), suffices at all large targets. We do not independently verify those external claims or assume full GRH. Everything from the end of the marked Type II comparison onwards below is unconditional.

## 1. Compact tests, extraction, and the actual primes' tag variance

Let $\theta=1$ or 2, $w(m)=\theta^{\omega(m)}$, and let $N_{\theta,P}=\sum_{p<P}w(P-p)$. Thus $N_{1,P}\sim P/L$ by PNT and $N_{2,P}\sim\phi(P)$ by the unconditional 1966 theorem of P. D. T. A. Elliott and H. Halberstam on this divisor-weighted sum (not the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture), as quoted in [Gorodetsky–Grimmelt (1.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13585v3#S1). In the second equivalence use $P/\phi(P)\ll H$. Our normalized physical expectation is $\mathbb E_{\theta,P}Z=N_{\theta,P}^{-1}\sum_{p<P}w(P-p)Z$. Insert a fixed smooth $0\le j\le1$ compactly supported in $0<p/P<1$. It can approximate an interval, or be one on $[2\epsilon,1-2\epsilon]$ and zero off $[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]$.

Fix disjoint prime-exponent intervals $I_i=[a_i,b_i]\subset(0,1)$, $1\le i\le k$, $k\ge1$, $\sum b_i<1$. Write $R=r_1\cdots r_k$ with primes $P^{a_i}\le r_i\le P^{b_i}$, and
$$
 F(m)=\sum_{R\mid m}1,\qquad S_0=\sum_R R^{-1},\qquad S_1=\sum_R (\log R)/(LR).
$$
All three are $O_{\mathcal I}(1)$, where $\mathcal I$ is the fixed collection of intervals. Choose $\kappa>0$ below both $\min a_i$ and $1-\sum b_i$. We prove the necessary moment estimates for both $F$ and $\lambda F$. Smooth weights in the actual $p$ variable, not in $R$, are enough.

Use the $J\asymp_K H$ disjoint families from [JLL (2.1)–(2.5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2#S2) and the projected comparison. Each product $a\in\mathcal A_j$ draws one true prime not dividing $P$, congruent to 1 modulo 4, from each of $K$ disjoint prescribed subpower intervals. Across the $J$ families the prime banks are disjoint. To avoid confusing the smooth function $j$ with a family index write $i_0$ for the latter. Put
$$
W_{i_0}(m)=\mathcal V_{i_0}^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{i_0}}1_{a\mid m},\quad
\mathcal V_{i_0}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{i_0}}1/a,\qquad
\bar W=J^{-1}\sum_{i_0}W_{i_0}.
$$
All tag primes exceed $E=\exp(L^{.1})/O(1)$, all tag products are $\exp(O_K(L^{.2}))$, and the reciprocal mass in each individual bank stays between positive absolute constants.

For the **actual prime probability above**, not a random-integer surrogate,
$$
\mathbb E_{\theta,P}|\bar W-\theta^K|^2=o_K(1).                       \tag{1}
$$
For $\theta=1$, ordinary prime BV over subpower moduli gives each first moment $1+o(1)$, each cross-family product moment $1+o(1)$, and the diagonal second moments $O_K(1)$, uniformly in the family indices: $1/\phi(a)=(1+O_K(E^{-1}))/a$, and coincident labels in a diagonal moment are counted by their lcm. For $\theta=2$, apply [Gorodetsky–Grimmelt Proposition 1.2, (1.4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13585v3#S1) directly to the full prime probability for each such support of lcms. All moduli are squarefree, coprime to $P$, have at most $2K$ prime factors and are $\le\exp(O_K(L^{.2}))<P^{1/100}$. The sum of absolute errors in replacing a divisibility probability by $2^{\omega(a)}/a$ is $O_K((H^3+L^{.2})/L)+O_K(L^{-2})$, since the harmonic lcm mass is bounded. A particular lcm has bounded multiplicity in a fixed pair of banks: at most $2^K$ choices when a family is repeated. The first and cross-family means are consequently $2^K+o_K(1)$ and $2^{2K}+o_K(1)$. For either weight, within a single bank the harmonic lcm sum for two labels is at most $V+V^2$, where $V$ is its harmonic mass, so all diagonal means are bounded. This proves (1). Cauchy allows insertion of $\bar W/\theta^K$ in a bounded probe at normalized cost $o(1)$, even after the fixed spatial window, without assuming that any small-rank prime partner exists. This is also why the weight on the true primes cannot silently be changed from $2^\omega$ to 1.

## 2. The marked comparison

Take either $b=1$ or $b=\lambda$. Consider the physical sum
$$
\sum_n j(n/P)\Lambda(n)\,1_{(P-n,P)=1}\,w(P-n)b(P-n)F(P-n) W_{i_0}(P-n).       \tag{2}
$$
We will divide by $L N_{\theta,P}\theta^K$ and average in $i_0$. The unit restriction on a true prime omits only prime divisors of $P$, and conversion of $\log p$ to $L$ on the fixed interior loses $O_j(1/L)$ relatively in the absolute upper bound from (1). The actual prime source is not a prime-power source. Removing the proper powers $n=p^j$, $j\ge2$, from the von Mangoldt sum costs $P^{1/2+o(1)}$: there are $O(\sqrt P\log P)$ such integers and every other physical weight is $P^{o(1)}$. On the other leg we remove only repeated **selected** large prime factors as below; arbitrary unselected prime powers of $P-n$ remain in the genuine $2^\omega$ and $\lambda$ weights. Simultaneous exceptional factors, including one on each leg, lie in the union of these two bounds. Insert $P^-(n)>z=\exp(L^{.24})$ and Vaughan's exact identity with $D=P^{1/5}$:
$$
\Lambda=\mu_{\le D}*\log-\mu_{\le D}*\Lambda_{\le D}*1+\Lambda_{\le D}
       +\mu_{>D}*(\Lambda_{>D}*1).                                      \tag{3}
$$
The third slot vanishes. Both masks on the factors of $n$ in the last slot separate, since $(P-n,P)=1\iff(n,P)=1$. Tags are taken out of the opposite number $y=P-n=ah$.

Here and below occurrences of a selected tag prime or macro prime dividing its cofactor can be discarded and restored in the extracted formula at cost $O_A(PL^{-A})$. Indeed $y$ must then be divisible by the square of a prime $\ell\ge E$. Counting multiples gives at most $O(P/E+\sqrt P)$ such $y\le P$. All physical sums of absolute terms before or after these replacements are bounded pointwise by $O_{K,\mathcal I}(L^{O(1)}\tau(n)^C\tau(y)^C)$ for a fixed $C$ independent of $K$ except for the prefactor (each unnormalized tag tuple is uniquely a divisor of $y$ and $1/\mathcal V_{i_0}=O_K(1)$); an inclusion–exclusion over divisors costs at most another divisor function. Hölder and the standard fixed divisor moments turn this exceptional-set bound into an arbitrary log saving. In particular $w(ah)=\theta^K w(h)$ outside this error, $b(ah)=b(a)b(h)$ always, and $F(ah)=F(h)$ exactly because all tags are below the probe. In applying the projected estimate use the same source and shell normalization as [the prime–cofactor calculation, §§1–2](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b262263b99802e5445835d22ebd99fe673ef7875/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/near_full_cofactor/Proof.md): in the last convolution of (3) the Möbius slot has its full rough/unit interval coefficient, the second is bounded by $L$, and both have polynomial length. The residual on $h$ is now bounded by $O_{\mathcal I}(\tau(h))$ instead of 1; on each shell $\sum_{h\ll P/Y}\tau(h)^2\ll(P/Y)L^3$, costing only a fixed log power in Cauchy against $Q\ll_R PY L^{-R}$. The sign $b(a)$ is constant for a family. A fixed interior $j(n/P)$ is separable on log boxes by Fourier, or by the same relative-width $L^{-B}$ partition: with $j$ frozen on each box, the absolute error is bounded by its Lipschitz modulus times the physical mixed divisor moments. For this step the tag fourth and higher fixed moments are $O_{K,c}(1)$ as integer averages (expand at most $cK$ labels and count their lcms); this keeps the log-loss exponents independent of $K$. A fixed sufficiently high precision precedes choosing $K$. Thus the true Type II term is $O_{j,K,\mathcal I}(PL^{-3})$. This is the only step requiring the projected major estimate and its zero-free input. The smoothing involves only a fixed compact interval with $mn\asymp P$, $mn<P$; no comparable comparison at $mn=o(P)$ is being assumed.

## 3. The ordinary cofactor and the common two-dimensional principal

In the first two convolutions of (3) the short known divisor $d\le D^2$ is $z$-rough. Its (combined, in the second sum) coefficient has absolute value $\le L$, since $\sum_{d_2\mid d}\Lambda(d_2)=\log d$. The rest of its weight is either 1 or $\log(n/d)$. Open roughness of $n/d$ over squarefree $e$ with primes at most $z$ by inclusion–exclusion stopped at $h_0=\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor$. The pointwise error is bounded by a divisor weight on integers with at least $h_0$ such prime factors. The number of those integers up to $P$ is at most
$$ P\,(O(H))^{h_0}/h_0!+ z^{h_0}=O_A(PL^{-A}) $$
by counting products of $h_0$ distinct small primes. The preceding mixed-moment argument handles all physical coefficients of this remainder. In particular the growing depth is genuine; a fixed-depth unsigned estimate would not work here.

Write $q=de\le P^{.4+o(1)}$. Its $z$-smooth part $e$ and rough part $d$ are uniquely determined. On the unit-supported physical sequence discard $q$ not coprime to $P$ since $q\mid n$; if $a$ shares a prime with $q$ then $a\mid P-n$ and $q\mid n$ would force that prime to divide $P$, also impossible. After extraction $y=aRs$, the remaining condition on $Rs$ is the **reduced class** $P\bar a\bmod q$, and the exact source unit mask is $(Rs,P)=1$. The square-overlap argument from §2 permits $w(aRs)b(aRs)$ to be replaced by $\theta^{K+k} b(a)b(R)w(s)b(s)$, where $b(R)=1$ for $b=1$ and $b(R)=(-1)^k$ for $b=\lambda$. No sign of an individual Type I summand is presumed.

For each fixed $a$, bilinear BV replaces all such reduced progressions by their actual unit sums divided by $\phi(q)$, with total error $O_A((P/a)L^{-A})$ (before the harmless log coefficient). Here is a precise application of [Bombieri–Friedlander–Iwaniec, Theorem 0(b), (1.5)](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6385-11511_2006_Article_BF02399204.pdf). Take one designated macro-prime slot as the Siegel–Walfisz sequence and group the other slots and $s$ as the arbitrary divisor-bounded sequence, including their masks of being units modulo $P$. Both lengths exceed a fixed power since $s\gg_j P/(aR)\ge P^{\kappa-o(1)}$. The free prime slot also excludes primes dividing $P$, at most $O(L)$ individual omissions; in the auxiliary coprimality of (A2) deleting divisors of any integer $v$ costs $O(\omega(v))$, absorbed by its allowed divisor power. Prime Siegel–Walfisz and trivial bounds for large class modulus give (A2); for a truncated prime subinterval of length less than a full dyad one may interpolate its trivial count with the ambient arbitrary-log estimate to obtain the required norm-scaled version. Split grouped factors into dyads (only log powers). The smooth functions $j((P-aRs)/P)$ and $j((P-aRs)/P)\log((P-aRs)/d)$, in $\log(aRs/P)$, have fixed compact support and derivatives $O_j(1+L)$. Mellin separation on each dyad, truncated at a fixed power of $L$, gives independent polynomial-time twists, preserving prime Siegel–Walfisz by partial summation. The discarded transform can be made arbitrarily log-small: its physical absolute mass is bounded by the same divisor moments and, on the averaged side, by $O((P/a)L^{O(1)}\sum_{q\le P^{.41}}1/\phi(q))$. This last bound is obtained by dropping unit masks and summing $w(s)\le\tau(s)$ on $s\asymp P/(aR)$. There are no different prefix endpoints to maximize over separately for different $q$. The $-\log d$ is merely an outside scalar. Since $a=P^{o(1)}$, the displayed modulus bound has a power margin from $\sqrt{P/a}$. The fixed tag-normalized harmonic sum $\mathcal V_{i_0}^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{i_0}}1/a=1$ pays the $a$-sum without any limit exchange or exceptional residue assumption.

Write $C=Pq$ (here and in (4), $C$ denotes a modulus rather than a generic exponent). The ordinary unit sums needed, with $v\gg P^{\kappa/2}$ and $C\ll P^2$, are as follows, uniformly:
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\sum_{s\le v,(s,C)=1}1=v\phi(C)/C+O(P^{o(1)}),\\
\sum_{s\le v,(s,C)=1}2^{\omega(s)}=v[c_C\log v+d_C]+O(v^{.85}P^{o(1)}),\\
\sum_{s\le v,(s,C)=1} w(s)\lambda(s)\ll_A v L^{-A}.
\end{array}                                                        \tag{4}
$$
The first follows by inclusion–exclusion, $\tau(C)=P^{o(1)}$. For the second the Dirichlet series is $\zeta(t)^2 G_C(t)$, where $G_C(t)=\zeta(2t)^{-1}\prod_{r\mid C}(1-r^{-t})/(1+r^{-t})$. Its absolute coefficient norm at $3/4$ is $P^{o(1)}$ by $\sum_{r\mid C}r^{-3/4}=o(L)$, and its norm and first logarithmic moment at 1 are bounded by fixed powers of $L$. Convolution with the elementary divisor summatory $v\log v+(2\gamma-1)v+O(\sqrt v)$ gives (4); tails of the log moment cost at most $v^{.85}P^{o(1)}$. In particular $c_C,d_C\ll L^{O(1)}$. For the last line, before the unit mask the Dirichlet series is $\zeta(2t)/\zeta(t)^\theta$. Removing local factors costs a Dirichlet convolution whose absolute reciprocal norm is $L^{O(1)}$ and $3/4$-norm $P^{o(1)}$ (include the absolutely convergent square factor in these norms). For $\theta=1$ use the Mertens bound on $\mu$, for $\theta=2$ the same arbitrary-log bound for $\mu*\mu$ by the Dirichlet hyperbola formula. Split the extra convolution at $\sqrt v$; the reciprocal tail is power-small. This proves the last line at the specified polynomial scale.

In each principal term only $(R,C)=1$ occurs. On the harmonic sums $S_0,S_1$ removing this restriction costs $O_{\mathcal I}(L P^{-\kappa})$: each individual large excluded prime has $1/r\le P^{-\kappa}$, there are $O(L)$ divisors of $C$, and all other slots have bounded harmonic mass. Thus exact physical coprimalities have not been replaced by statistical independence.

For $b=\lambda$, (4) and partial summation already give an arbitrary-log-small total principal, using $\sum_{q\le P^{.41}}1/\phi(q)\ll L$ and the harmonic sums over $a,R$. Consequently all signed normalized factorial moments with $k\ge1$ vanish. For $b=1$, set $t=aRs/P$, in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,1)$ thanks to $j$. Apply (4) by partial summation with the **same** $j(1-t)$ (and the one possible factor $\log(P(1-t)/d)$) in the bracket. In the second line of (4) the density in $t$ is exactly
$$
\frac P{aR}\,[\ c_C\log(Pt/a)+d_C+c_C\ - c_C\log R\ ]\,dt.          \tag{5}
$$
For $\theta=1$ it is $(P/(aR))(\phi(C)/C)\,dt$ with no linear part. No $O(1)$ lower-order term in (5) has been dropped before paying the $d,e$ sums. Indeed each rank-$k$ sum has the same signed linear functional of $S_0,S_1$, with all target, tag and modulus derivatives, rough cutoffs, and both Vaughan signs included in its two coefficients. The errors from (4) are power-small on $s\gg P^{\kappa/2}$, so all harmonic sums and log coefficients above are harmless. The $R$-restriction does not enter these common coefficients. Dividing by $LN_{\theta,P}\theta^K$ and averaging the same families, (1)–(5) prove
$$
\mathbb E_{\theta,P}\big[j(p/P) F(P-p)\big]
  =\theta^k(A_{j,P}S_0-B_{j,P}S_1)+o(1).                              \tag{6}
$$
Here $B_{j,P}=0$ for $\theta=1$. The constants may depend on $j,P,\theta$ and the chosen spectral grid, **not on the macro-prime slots**. Use a common fixed precision/grid for any finite list of probes, including the calibration probes. In (6) the $S_i$ are the exact finite prime sums; replacing them by limiting integrals before calibrating possibly unbounded coefficients would be unjustified.


## 4. Determining the principal from true prime data

When $\theta=1$, choose any single fixed interval of prime exponents strictly inside $(0,1/2)$. Ordinary prime BV in the actual class $P\bmod r$ for $r\nmid P$ gives its spatially weighted normalized mean
$$
  \left(\int_0^1 j(u)\,du\right) S_0+o(1).
$$
The harmonic omission for $r\mid P$ is power-small and $1/\phi(r)-1/r$ is summable with a power saving here. Since this particular $S_0$ has positive limiting value, (6) implies $A_{j,P}=\int j+o(1)$. Consequently every admissible box has the predicted unsigned $(\int j)\prod_i\log(b_i/a_i)$ limit. Spatially localized signed moments are zero by §3.

For $\theta=2$ use the full probability. Uniformly for each fixed small $\epsilon$, the two endpoint intervals of length $2\epsilon P$ each have total **unmarked** weight $O(\epsilon\phi(P))+P^{o(1)}$, with an absolute implied constant for $P$ sufficiently large depending on $\epsilon$. Exclude the prime divisors of $P$ first. Bound $2^{\omega(P-p)}\le\tau(P-p)\le2\sum_{d\mid(P-p),\,d\le\sqrt P}1$; all such $d$ are target units. The ordinary interval Brun–Titchmarsh theorem on the primes $p\equiv P\bmod d$ bounds their count by $O(\epsilon P/(\phi(d)L))$ as $d\le\sqrt P$. Also
$$
 \sum_{d\le\sqrt P,(d,P)=1}\frac1{\phi(d)}
 \le \prod_{r\le\sqrt P,r\nmid P}(1+r/(r-1)^2)
 \ll L\frac{\phi(P)}P.
$$
This uses Mertens and the $1+O(1/r^2)$ comparison, including the harmless at most one omitted target prime $>\sqrt P$. Therefore replacing $j=j_\epsilon$ by 1 in a normalized bounded physical probe costs $O_{\mathcal I}(\epsilon)+o(1)$ for either sign. No bound on the *marked* endpoint is needed: (1) first removes the marks on the interior.

Let $\eta>0$ be the absolute constant from [Gorodetsky–Grimmelt Proposition 1.3 and its explicit deduction (2.3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13585v3#S2). For a single fixed prime slot $[a,b]\subset(0,\eta)$ they prove on the **full true weighted probability** the limit
$$ 2\int_a^b(1-t)\frac{dt}{t}. $$
Pick two such disjoint intervals, the first strictly below the second. The two rows $(S_0,-S_1)$ tend to $(\int dt/t,-\int dt)$ there by prime Mertens and partial summation. Their limiting matrix is invertible because the weighted mean $S_1/S_0$ lies in each respective interval. From (6), the endpoint bound, and these **two independent calibrations**, we obtain
$$ |A_{j_\epsilon,P}-1|+|B_{j_\epsilon,P}-1| \le O(\epsilon)+o_{\epsilon}(1). $$
Here the constant in $O(\epsilon)$ depends on the two fixed reference intervals, not on $\epsilon$. This calibrates the exact finite matrix before sending its entries to their limits. Use the same two probes beside each new fixed box. First let $P\to\infty$ and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The full, positive weighted probability satisfies the factorial moment law
$$
 \lim\mathbb E_{2,P}F(P-p)
 =2^k\int_{\prod I_i}\left(1-\sum_i t_i\right)\prod_i\frac{dt_i}{t_i},
 \qquad \lim\mathbb E_{2,P}\lambda(P-p) F(P-p)=0.                    \tag{7}
$$
Indeed the same endpoint estimate bounds absolute signed probes and the interior signed term vanishes independently of the calibration. This two-parameter argument never requires each modulus's constant $d_C$ to be negligible. For $\theta=1$ the counterpart of (7) has integrand $\prod dt_i/t_i$, also when conditioned on a fixed macroscopic prime interval (smooth approximation, ordinary PNT and bounded probes).

## 5. The sign and the probability limit

First work with factor sizes divided by $L$, whose sum is at most one. Repeated macro-prime factors with exponent at least $\kappa>0$ have negligible normalized physical mass: use the same square count as in §2, $\Lambda\le L$ and the integer divisor moments. In fact for the lower-bound convergence criteria multiplicities can only increase the unsigned counts. Total signs have equal limiting probabilities: approximate the function $t$ by step functions on $[\xi,1-\xi]$ in the single-slot formulas for each fixed $\xi>0$. The selected sum of log factor sizes $Z_\xi$ lies in $[0,1]$ and has vanishing signed expectation. Its unsigned limiting expectation is $\int_\xi^{1-\xi}\theta(1-t)^{\theta-1}dt$, tending to 1. Thus $|\mathbb E\lambda|\le |\mathbb E\lambda Z_\xi|+1-\mathbb E Z_\xi\to0$ in the iterated limits. The same holds for the spatially conditioned $\theta=1$ probability.

In either conditional sign probability each disjoint box with $\sum b_i<1$ consequently has the same limiting moment as its unsigned counterpart. With total log mass at most one, apply [Arratia–Kochman–Miller, Lemmas 2 and 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1401.1555#S3) to each conditional law. In the $\theta=2$ case (7) is the integral of the actual PD(2) multi-intensity and dominates the lower bound with $(1-\sum b_i)$ in their lemma. This proves the two asserted factor-size laws simultaneously with independence of the sign. Replacing $L$ by $\log(P-p)$ changes coordinates by $o(1)$ uniformly on each fixed interior window; for the full probability the endpoint bound above, or ordinary PNT when $\theta=1$, permits the replacement in probability. The threshold on the largest part in PD(1) is continuous with distribution $\rho(1/\delta)$, yielding the first statement's additive corollary.

For example, on the central interval the conditional proportion of genuine primes $p$ with $P^+(P-p)\le P^{1/2}$ and either *specified* total parity is $(1-\log2)/2+o(1)$, a positive count on the scale $P/\log P$. This does not supply a bound on the cubic rank-2/3/4 prime partner statistic: its finite-factor boundary is invisible to convergence for fixed tests. More explicitly, here each fixed probe requires a cofactor $s\gg P/(aR)\ge P^{\kappa-o(1)}$. If the labels exhaust $P-p$ itself there is no such cofactor. Neither the two-volume calibration nor the decay of the ordinary signed cofactor in (4) can be continued to $s=1$.

[Elboim–Gorodetsky](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00601) give PD behavior for multiplicatively biased ordinary integers without the reflected-prime support. Neither the new process limit nor the external half-plane input supplies an unweighted binary-prime asymptotic. No computer search or numerical evidence enters the proof.
