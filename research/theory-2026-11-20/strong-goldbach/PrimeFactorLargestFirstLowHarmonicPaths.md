# Positive harmonic low entries force polynomially many disjoint two-step paths

Written research note. Assumes the actual low-label harmonic branch of the [planted \(P_4\) quarter-plus entry result](PrimeOppositePlantedLargestLabelSupply.md), not the high-label alternative. Work with an arbitrary sufficiently large even N and the genuine distinct prime label set \(Q\subset(N^a,N^b]\), \(a=.2502,\ b=.4\), generated by the actual retained prime roots \(p(q)\asymp\sqrt N\). Let \(L=\log N\) and assume \(\sum_{q\in Q}1/q\ge .02/1300=1/65000\) as in that branch. The conclusions concern these many sources, not a single root or an SCC.

**Result.** A fixed positive reciprocal mass of actual q have a genuine prime \(r>N^{.05}\) dividing \(N-q\). Consequently there are \(\gg N^{.05}\) pairwise vertex-disjoint actual paths \(p(q)\to q\to r\) in the forward divisor convention. If one insists the second row also be composite, either a fixed positive reciprocal mass of q already yields genuine Goldbach pairs, or these polynomially many paths can be taken with composite second rows. The first row is automatically composite for these low entries since \(p(q)+q=o(N)\).

## 1. Genuine shifted-prime smooth upper at the needed scale

Set \(\beta=.05\), \(Y=N^\beta\). We prove uniformly in even N that among true unit primes \(q\in(N^a,N^b]\) the subfamily whose actual \(N-q\) is Y-smooth has harmonic mass less than \(1.12\cdot10^{-5}+o(1)\). The only progression theorem used is [Montgomery–Vaughan, The large sieve, Theorem 2, (1.10)](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/large_sieve.pdf): in an actual interval of length \(X>D\) and one reduced residue class mod \(D\), the prime upper is \(2X/(\varphi(D)\log(X/D))\). We apply it only at real \(q\)-intervals, not at the much larger \(N-q\) location.

Cover the q interval by whole dyadic boxes \((X,2X]\); the inclusion of the two end boxes costs no extra leading term in the final integral. Their lower endpoints obey \(X\ge N^a/2\). Unit q implies every prime factor of \(N-q\) is coprime to \(N\), so progression tests \(q\equiv N\bmod D\) in all actual rows are reduced.

First discard rows on which repeated powers contribute log weight at least \(.001L\), where
\[
 E(q)=\sum_{\ell\le Y,\ \ell\ {\rm prime}}\sum_{e\ge2,\ \ell^e\mid N-q}\log \ell.
\]
In one box the sum of \(E(q)\) over actual unit prime q is \(O(X/L)\). For \(\ell^e\le N^{.2}\), apply Brun–Titchmarsh with \(\log(X/\ell^e)\gg L\); the cumulative coefficient is bounded by
\(\sum_\ell\log\ell\sum_{e\ge2}\varphi(\ell^e)^{-1}=\sum_\ell\log\ell/(\ell-1)^2<\infty\).
For \(\ell^e>N^{.2}\), the literal class count is \(O(X/\ell^e+1)\) even with primality dropped. There are \(O(YL)\) power labels \((\ell,e)\) through \(N\), each logarithm at most \(L\), so their total is \(O(X N^{\beta-.2}L^2+N^\beta L^2)=o(X/L)\) uniformly. Markov now discards \(O(X/L^2)\) q per box, hence only \(O(1/L)\) total reciprocal mass.

On a remaining smooth row the true distinct-prime log sum \(S=\sum_{\ell\mid N-q}\log\ell\) is at least \(.998L\) for large N: \(\log(N-q)=L+o(1)\), and \(\log(N-q)=S+E(q)\). Each summand is \(\le.05L\). The sum of \(\prod_{i=1}^{5}\log\ell_i\) over ordered distinct genuine divisors \(\ell_i\) belonging to this row is therefore at least
\[
 \prod_{j=0}^4(S-.05jL)
 \ge L^5\prod_{j=0}^4(.998-.05j)>.57L^5.
\]
To see the first bound, append one prime at a time; the total weight of unused choices in each extension is at least the total S minus \(j(.05L)\).

In the dyadic q box write \(s=\log X/L\). On every genuine selected tuple the modulus \(D=\ell_1\cdots \ell_5\) is squarefree, at most \(Y^5=N^{.25}<X\) for sufficiently large N. The exact progression is \(q=N\bmod D\), and \(\log(X/D)\ge(s-.25)L\). Summing its Brun–Titchmarsh upper over genuine tuple weights gives the bound
\[
 \sum_{\substack{X<q\le2X\\q\ {\rm prime,unit}}}
 \ \sum_{\substack{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_5\le Y\ {\rm distinct\ primes}\\
                     \ell_i\mid N-q}}
      \prod_i\log\ell_i
 \le \frac{2X}{(s-.25)L}
       \left(\sum_{\ell\le Y}\frac{\log\ell}{\ell-1}\right)^5
 = \frac{2X}{(s-.25)L}(\beta+o(1))^5 L^5 .
\]
Dropping distinctness in the final upper product only enlarges it; the true CRT term used distinct primes. Weighted Mertens gives the last asymptotic. Divide by \(.57L^5\) and by X for reciprocals \(1/q\). Summing over dyadic boxes produces a genuine Riemann sum:
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\ \sum_{\substack{q\ {\rm unit\ prime},\,N^a<q\le N^b\\
                                P^+(N-q)\le Y}}\frac1q
 \ \le\ \frac{2\beta^5}{.57\log 2}
                 \log\frac{b-.25}{a-.25}
 <1.12\cdot10^{-5}<\frac1{65000}.
\]
The step is \(\log2/L\) in s, and \(a-.25=.0002\) is fixed and positive, so the two end boxes and all uniform errors are \(o(1)\). The numerical comparison uses merely \((b-.25)/(a-.25)=750\), \(\log750<7\), \(\log2>.69\), and \(\prod_{j=0}^4(.998-.05j)>.57\) (five rational factors), so it does not depend on noncertified quadrature. The difference from the supplied mass gives a fixed positive harmonic submass having actual r>Y.

## 2. Turning that submass into actual polynomially many disjoint paths

Fix one actual witnessing prime root \(p(q)\) per q. They are injective since each root has just one largest label on the fixed profile. A mutual pair \(q\mid N-p,\ p\mid N-q\) has \(N=p+q+c p q\). Here \(c=0\) is impossible because \(p\asymp\sqrt N,\ q\le N^.4\) make \(p+q=o(N)\). For \(c\ge1\), necessarily \(c\ll N^{1/2-a}\), and \((cp+1)(cq+1)=cN+1\) gives at most \(N^{1/2-a+o(1)}\) mutual pairs in total by the divisor bound. Their reciprocal q mass is \(O(N^{1/2-2a+o(1)})=o(1)\). Delete them. The remaining true \(r\mid N-q\) cannot equal p(q) or q (the latter is a unit modulo N).

For any prime \(r>Y\), all true parents using r lie in the reduced class \(q=N\bmod r\) whenever any such parent exists. Uniformly over these r,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{N^a<q\le N^b\\q\ {\rm unit\ prime}\\r\mid N-q}}\frac1q
 \ll N^{-\beta}.
\]
For \(r\le N^{a/2}\), actual dyadic Brun–Titchmarsh gives \(O(1/(rL))\) reciprocal mass per box, since \(\log(X/r)\gg L\); summing O(L) boxes gives \(O(1/r)\). For larger r, drop primality and sum the harmonic progression: \(O(N^{-a}+L/r)=o(N^{-\beta})\), with the first term allowing a residue near the lower endpoint. No unproved distribution result at r near q is used.

Designate one real \(r(q)>Y\) for each remaining q. If at most \(3R\) vertices have already been used, the total discarded reciprocal mass from q or p(q) lying among them is at most \(6R/N^a\), by injectivity. The discarded mass from r(q) lying among them is \(O(RN^{-\beta})\), by the true multiplicity cap. For \(R\le cN^\beta\) with sufficiently small fixed c, this remains less than the surviving positive mass, so a fresh disjoint path exists. Repeat. Even if the roles q and r can overlap in the entire population, the greedy selection excludes every previously used vertex from all three roles. If prime second rows make up at least half the mass then they supply \(\gg N^a\) actual distinct Goldbach pairs (each q is below \(N^b\ll\sqrt N\), so no duplicate orientation occurs); otherwise delete those rows and perform the same greedy selection on the composite second subfamily.

No conclusion is claimed about the high-label branch, a lone root, full component sizes, or the conjecture itself.
