# The four-macroscopic-label branch is impossible in a near-smooth six-prime terminal orbit

**Theorem.** Fix an odd prime p and a finite set F of primes. There is no sequence of even N→∞ coprime to p, with log(N/N_F)=o(log N), carrying a six-prime strongly connected closed all-composite factor orbit containing p in which exactly four labels have positive limiting logarithmic weight. Here N_F is the full F-part and weights are limits of log(x)/log(N) after subsequence extraction. A label's row records the complete prime factorization of N−x. This theorem excludes a specific terminal branch, not every six-prime orbit, not larger orbits, and not a Goldbach exception. In particular it does not dispose of the three-macroscopic-label case.

**Inputs and order.** The [four-intermediate theorem](PrimeOrbitFourMacroNearSmoothIntermediate.md), [square-root theorem](PrimeOrbitFourMacroSquareRoot.md), and [equal-half exclusion](PrimeOrbitFourMacroEqualHalfExclusion.md) reduce the asserted branch to the following order of six distinct prime labels: p<q<V<U<K<M, with q→∞ subpower, V of weight γ strictly between 0 and 1/2, and U,K,M of weight exactly 1/2. M/K is bounded above by the fixed left-null balance and below by one. Set k=1/(2γ)>1, initially any real number. All charts, including the multiplicities at V, are fixed after passing to a subsequence, because γ is fixed and positive. We do **not** presume a uniform bound on k or on those multiplicities.

In row order p,q,M,K,U,V, let Z be the three-column exponent matrix at M,K,U and d the six-entry nonnegative integer exponent column at V. Put R=[Z,d]. The two remaining exponent columns are at p,q. Write E_3 for the M,K,U coordinate columns of I_6 and e for the V coordinate column. The fixed left 1-eigenspace of the complete exponent matrix has basis C of size 2×6. It is exactly the left nullspace of [F_3,d−e], where F_3=Z−E_3, a rank-four matrix after adjoining the last column. The p,q Gale columns of C are independent; all six are nonzero, no three are proportional, and each nonzero left 1-eigenrow has both signs, with a cycle in each sign support. In particular the (unique up to scale) eigenrow vanishing at a prescribed coordinate has at least two strictly positive and two strictly negative entries. The left nullspace of [R,1] is a line generated by l with l_M≠0, l·1=0, lA=0 for the complete exponent matrix A. These are established inputs; in particular rank F_3=3. We have exactly ∑l_x log(N−x)=0, hence ∑l_x x=O(M²/N)=N^o(1) in absolute size. For any fixed positive rational ratio of macros approached with power-saving accuracy, the [algebraic-ratio lift](PrimeOrbitFourMacroThirdThreshold.md) says that ratio is a rational power of the fixed odd p.

## The linear integer problem, including unbounded k

1. Every row of Z has total at most two. There are no self-loops and at most one smaller parent of any target, since that target divides any difference of parents. In particular d_V=0 and p,q cannot both have positive V exponents. Put z_i=2−∑Z_ij. Then 0≤d_i≤k z_i. The q row has total macro weight exactly one by the Subspace Theorem argument in the four-intermediate theorem, so d_q=k z_q. The p row has at least one of the four macros by that theorem. If z_p=2 then d_p≥1, and z_q>0 gives d_p=0, so these two conditions cannot coincide. Distinct rows of R are required: otherwise their complement ratio is a p,q-unit 1+O(M/N); the normalized log argument in the four-intermediate theorem forces equal complements. Equal rows of Z alone are allowed.
2. The gcd of two distinct complements divides their nonzero input difference, at most M=N^(1/2+o(1)). If the total componentwise overlap of their Z rows is ≥2 they are impossible. If it is one, at least one of the two d's must vanish. If it is zero their smaller d is at most k. These are non-strict limiting weight bounds.
3. The left-null l is recovered, up to scaling, as the signed five-row minors of [Z,d,1]; requiring its M coefficient nonzero ensures rank five. Its running sums in decreasing physical order M,K,U,V,q,p must have both signs by summation by parts of the exact log identity. There is also a top-prefix test: if L_1=l_M,L_2=l_M+l_K,L_3=l_M+l_K+l_U, we cannot have L_1,L_2≥0 and L_3>0, or their negations. Indeed the top contribution is L_1(M−K)+L_2(K−U)+L_3 U, the other inputs are power-smaller than U, and ∑l_x x is subpower. All these are linear tests on d, since that column occurs just once in each minor.
4. Let v=(2,2,1,1,1,2)^T. The exact weight law is C(k v−e)=0: multiply the row log identities by C and divide by log N; the p and q contributions vanish because their exponent columns map to C e_p and C e_q and their own weights are zero. The three top weights are 1/2, the V weight is 1/(2k), and CZ=CE_3, Cd=Ce. This proves the law without small-prime exponent caps.

Take an integral three-row basis H of the rational left kernel of F_3. The nonzero V Gale column implies H e≠0. As C is two-dimensional, H(d−e)≠0 and this is proportional to k H v−H e whenever the latter is nonzero. If H v and H e are dependent, then a nonzero kHv−He would be proportional to He, forcing Ce=0. Thus a dependent chart must have kHv=He, a single exact positive scalar if one exists. The generators explicitly test this alternative; none admits k>1. They do not divide by a vanishing vector.

If Hv and He are independent we must have

    H d = s H v + t H e,          s=k(1−t),         t≠1.

Here t=1 would contradict H(d−e)≠0. Membership in the plane is one integer linear equation Jd=0. Choose integral rows ψ,φ in row(H) with ψe=0, ψv>0, φv=0, φe>0. Then s=ψd/(ψv), t=φd/(φe). The integer T=φd has **finitely many** values per Z regardless of k, not merely bounded logarithmic growth: if ψ_q z_q plus summed negative and positive ψ_i z_i across allowed i∈{p,M,K,U} give respective integers lo,hi (omit p if z_q>0), then

    lo ≤ (ψv)s/k ≤ hi,
    ceil((φe)(ψv−hi)/(ψv)) ≤ T ≤ floor((φe)(ψv−lo)/(ψv)).

Fix each such T≠φe. Set P/Q, reduced with Q>0, to (φe)/((ψv)(φe−T)); the integer variable j=Pψd then equals Qk. Thus Jd=0, φd=T, and the j relation express the pencil using only linear integer conditions. All capacities and gcd/disjunction tests above are linear after multiplying by Q. The remaining sign/rank checks of C depend only on k on this slice: its oriented pair minors are, up to common scale, signed four-row minors of [F_3,kv−e], each linear in k. Thus exact zero and sign tests are linear as well; sign changes require separately checking their rational roots. No p/q integrality, primality or further nonlinear condition is used in this finite necessary-condition sieve.

It checks all pencil signed minors, including rank-degenerate alternatives: the only sign breakpoints above one are 4/3,3/2,2,3, and it tests both the open intervals and each root. The [independent Python/Z3 check](top3_z3.py) regenerates the 7,290 matrices column-first and computes H by integer Gaussian elimination, uses triple scalar products in the three-dimensional quotient to recover Gale signs, finds its own local breakpoints with exact fractions, obtains ψ,φ from coordinate pairs of H, and reconstructs the left-null minors by Leibniz expansion. It checks 891 separately generated integer-linear noncoverage queries, all UNSAT (local open and closed intervals are combined by disjunction, so this is not the ISL slice count). ## Elimination of all eleven families

**1–3.** Directly multiplying [R,1] in these three families gives respective null rows (−1,−1,−1,2,0,1), (−2,−1,−1,2,0,2), (0,1,1,−2,0,0), up to sign. The first moment gives M/K=2+O(N^(−η)) for fixed η>0 since V/K is power-small. The rational-ratio lift would make 2 a rational power of the fixed odd p, impossible.

**4–5.** The three input rows at M,K,U have zero Z-projection, with V-exponents 0,t,k or k,t,0, respectively, t=(2k+1)/3 between zero and k and different from k/2. The three complete rows need not be identical: the actual fixed left null l of [R,1], equivalently of A, is proportional to the rational affine relation on those three exponent positions. Hence the middle complement is a weighted geometric mean of the endpoints. Precisely, for the three complements X,Y,Z with Y the middle, X^a Z^b=Y^m with a,b positive coprime, m=a+b=k/gcd(k,t)≥3. Let G=gcd(X,Z). Every prime valuation of Y lies between those of X and Z, so G|Y. As X/G,Z/G are coprime and a,b are prime to m, each of X/G,Z/G is an exact m-th power by valuations. The distinct endpoints therefore differ by at least G(max(X,Z)/G)^(1−1/m), which is ≫N^(1−1/m), whereas all complement differences are at most M=N^(1/2+o(1)). Contradiction.

**B,D,F.** Each has M→K and a second smaller parent s of K which is p in B,D and q in F. Then K|(M−s), and the positive quotient is even as all three primes are odd. It is bounded by M/K≪1, hence fixed along a subsequence. As s is subpower, M/K approaches that even integer with power-saving accuracy, contradicting the rational-ratio lift.

**A,C,E.** The top primes M,K have distinct unique parents, giving Gale columns positively proportional within each top-parent pair. No third column lies in either direction. Killing one such direction forces both vertices in the other pair to have the same sign and both remaining vertices to have the opposite sign: each sign support contains a directed cycle and therefore must have size at least two. Thus all three pairs are mutual (for the two top pairs use both killed directions). In A the top-parent pairs are (M,V) and (K,p), leaving U,q. In C they are (M,q) and (K,p), leaving U,V. In E they are (M,p) and (K,q), leaving U,V.

In each such chart M has a single parent s of multiplicity **one**, so the associated two Gale columns are equal. Write Y,Z for the residual mutual pair, and N=M+s+h_s Ms=Y+Z+h_0 YZ with h_s,h_0 positive even integers. Choose an integral left 1-eigenrow zero on the K pair, positive and equal to D_0 on M,s, and equal to −B,−C_0 on Y,Z, with B,C_0>0 fixed. Exactly as in [the matched-pair congruence proof](PrimeOrbitFourMacroNoAboveHalf.md), its multiplicative relation is

    (1+h_s N)^D_0=((N−Y)/Y)^B ((N−Z)/Z)^C_0.

Squaring and expanding modulo N² (the labels are units) shows that

    n=2(D_0 h_s YZ+B Z+C_0 Y)/N

is a positive integer. Since N≡Z mod Y and N≡Y mod Z, we have n≡2B mod Y and n≡2C_0 mod Z. In all three charts Y and Z tend to infinity. For A,C the parent s tends to infinity, hence n/Y,n/Z→0 by h_s≤N/(sM) and Y,Z≤M. In E the parent p is fixed but take Y=U, Z=V=o(M): the same inequality gives n/Y→0. In every case the first congruence therefore gives n=2B eventually, and then Z→∞ gives n=2C_0 as well without any extra smallness estimate. Substitution gives D_0 h_s=B h_0. The multiplicative identity now says (1+h_s N)^D_0=(1+h_0 N)^B, which forces h_s=h_0 by strict decrease of log(1+x)/x for x>0. It follows from Ms=(N−M−s)/h_0 and YZ=(N−Y−Z)/h_0 that their products have equal limiting logarithmic weights.

In A those weights would be 1/2+γ and 1/2 (s=V, residual U,q). In C and E they would be 1/2 and 1/2+γ (s=q or p, residual U,V). All contradict γ>0. This eliminates the last families and finishes the proof.
