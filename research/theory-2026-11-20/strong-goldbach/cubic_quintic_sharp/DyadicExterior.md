# A dyadic obstruction to a target-independent bound on the exact residual

Use the unchanged sharp own-log score \(H=V-\Pi\) in [the cubic–quintic detector](CubicQuinticSharp.md), and the actual ordered pairs \(x+y=N,\ x/N\in J,\ (x,N)=1\). Write \(M_N=|J|N\mathfrak S(N)\), \(R_J=\langle\Pi,\Pi\rangle\). Let \(P\) be the actual positive score on the floor-\(.123\) squarefree ranks 2 and 3 in [the widened positive block](WiderPositiveBlock.md). Let \(A\) be the actual *magnitude* of the negative score on least factor \(>N^{.105}\) in [the signed negative-arm proof](NegativeArmUnion.md). Define \(E_N\) as the signed sum of \(H(x)H(y)\) outside the literal two paid sets: neither the \(P\)-by-\(P\) set nor the union where at least one arm is an \(A\)-arm. These two removed sets are disjoint.

**Proposition.**
\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\sum_{X<N\le2X,\ 2\mid N}E_N}
      {\sum_{X<N\le2X,\ 2\mid N}M_N}
 =1-m(P)^2+2m(A)+m(A)^2<-2.975 .
 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(m(\cdot)\) is the ordinary log-weighted density in units of the full spatial interval length, not a conditional arithmetic distribution of the opposing arm at any fixed target. Consequently a target-independent lower \(E_N\ge-(.804+o(1))M_N\) on this **whole exact exterior** is false. This says nothing against a bound coupled to the actual size of the paid positive block, a restriction to targets with small \(R_J\), or using a different score.

For clarity about the ordinary normalization, \(H\) itself is a function of the physical integer \(n\) with its own log, without a target-dependent angle. On odd macroscopic integers its ordinary signed mass is \(m(H)=1\). This can be seen from the same short taper as in §2 of the sharp detector: use a fixed spatial interval of integers at scale \(X\), put \(L=\log X\), and replace the fixed smooth own-log taper by its target-log version by the dilation bound; replace its outside \(\log n\) by \(L\). Open the literal squarefree condition by \([d,e^2]\) with the same power-saving tail. In the one-arm integer principal the local factor at \(p>2\) is \(1-p^{-2}-(1-1/p)p^{-s}\), \(s\) near one. At \(s=1\) the regular factor after extracting the inverse zeta is one. Excluding the prime \(2\) contributes \(2\) to that regular factor and an exactly canceling outside odd density \(1/2\). The zeroth completed log moment vanishes and the first has sign \(-1\), so the raw weighted mass is \(-F'(0)=2\). The PNT prime weight on odd integers has mass \(1\). The one-arm sharp return is valid absolutely: the one-form version of the corrected-Henriot phase bound has initial product \(O(1/L)\), factor \((2+|\xi|)^c\) for any fixed \(c>4/\pi\), and the same uniform scaled-cusp Fourier difference \(O(h^{3-c})\). It also gives an ordinary absolute \(O(X)\) upper on the signed score at this scale. Thus \(m(H)=1\) over odd integers, normalized by the *full* interval length; there is no additional \(1/2\) in its use below.

For \(P,A\), all factors exceed fixed powers, the true ranks are bounded, and the score is a bounded continuous function of own prime angles times \(\log n\), restricted by its actual rank, least-prime floor and sign. Ordinary PNT in the genuine largest slot, with reciprocal prime harmonics in the other slots and genuine sorted angular cells, gives their ordinary densities over any fixed macroscopic interval. These integers are themselves odd for large \(X\). Replacing their moving \(N^\alpha\) floors by \(X^\alpha\) while averaging \(X<N\le2X\) changes only a prime angle collar of width \(O(1/\log X)\) at a fixed positive \(\alpha\). On these fixed compact bounded-rank sources such a collar has ordinary weighted mass \(o(X)\), by genuine ordinary angular cells and then shrinking the collar. This replacement may be done in products with the unrestricted \(H\): use its just-stated ordinary absolute \(O(X)\) bound on the other independent axis. Thus moving floors do not change any of the stated dyadic product limits.

At even \(N\) the genuine target units are precisely \((x,y)=1\), so both arms must be odd. After restricting them both to odds, removing \((x,y)=1\) costs \(o(X^2)\) in every product used below: one arm \(P\), \(A\), or \(\Pi\) has minimum prime factor \(\gg X^\eta\) for a fixed \(\eta>0\), so all sharing a prime are at most \(O(X^2\sum_{j\ge X^\eta}j^{-2})\) pairs. Each actual weight is at most \(X^{o(1)}\). The dyadic wedge \(X<x+y\le2X,\ x/(x+y)\in J\) has area \(\frac32|J|X^2\), by the change of variables \((N,x/N)\) with Jacobian \(N\). Finite rectangle approximation of this wedge, applying the genuine one-arm ordinary asymptotics on each odd axis, gives the corresponding ordinary product density times that area. For signed weights, errors at the wedge boundary are controlled by positive one-arm ordinary upper measures: \(|H|\) admits the same envelope on any fixed subinterval with its interval length times a constant in the limiting bound, or one can first truncate true rank/floor at small absolute one-arm cost and use bounded angular densities for the truncated weights. The remaining \(P,A,\Pi\) measures already have their compact or prime PNT bounds. This use of independent axes applies only **after summing over targets**, not at any individual target.

The mean of the actual binary singular series over even integers is 2: expand \(2C_2\prod_{p\mid N,\ p>2}(1+1/(p-2))\) over odd squarefree divisors; the absolutely convergent reciprocal expansion has product \(1/C_2\). Divisibility of even targets by each fixed odd divisor has limiting density \(1/d\), and the tail is dominated by the summable reciprocal coefficients (the divisors dividing a target below \(2X\) have at most \(O(X/d)\) possible multiples). Partial summation then gives \(\sum_{X<N\le2X,\,2\mid N}M_N\sim\frac32|J|X^2\). Similarly the true prime product on the wedge gives \(\sum R_J\sim\frac32|J|X^2\) by ordinary weighted PNT on its two odd axes, deleting their negligible units exception. The identity \(\langle H,H\rangle=R_J+o(M_N)\) is uniform, so the full \(H\)-pair mean is 1.

The actual removed positive pair has mean \(m(P)^2\). The actual removed negative union is \(-\langle A,H\rangle-\langle H,A\rangle-\langle A,A\rangle\) and has mean \(-2m(A)-m(A)^2\). This gives the equality in (1) with the two supports subtracted once. By the actual angular layers in the widened positive proof \(m(P)\) exceeds their ordinary average \(200^{-1}\sum_j\underline m_j>2.0377\). The arithmetic in the negative-arm proof gives \(m(A)<.077983212+.00672821<.08472\). Hence the limit is strictly below
\[
  1-(2.0377)^2+2(.08472)+(.08472)^2
  =-2.9756038116.
\]
No conclusion is drawn about the sign at a particular fixed even target.
