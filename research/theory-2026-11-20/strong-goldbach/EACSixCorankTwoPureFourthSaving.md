# A strict power saving below three quarters in the corank-two pure-fourth lane

Consider actual six-label exceptional autonomous components (EACs) at even \(N\), with exponent matrix \(A\), \(\det A=0\), \(\dim\ker(I-A)=2\), and an actual reflected row \(N-r=p^4\) for \(p\) the least prime label. There is an absolute \(\epsilon>0\) such that the number of centers \(N\le X\) in this lane is at most \(X^{3/4-\epsilon+o(1)}\). The saving is not claimed effective. If a special two-vertex support occurs, the direct divisor bound there is \(X^{5/8+o(1)}\). This result does **not** cover corank-two components lacking a pure \(p^4\) row, does not improve the all-six \(3/4\) bound by itself, and does not bound Goldbach failures.

## 1. Exact counting method and limiting bad case

Let \(q\) be the second label. Strong connectivity and compositeness give the usual \(M:=\max x_i<N/p\). From the actual pure row we have \(p^4=N-r\sim N\), hence \(p\sim N^{1/4}\). All row arities are at most 4 at large \(N\). The [two-thirds least-product bound](EACSixTwoThirdsProduct.md) for doubly singular centers gives \(q\le N^{5/12+o(1)}\); only its strict separation from \(N^{1/2}\) is used.

For a fixed exponent chart use the six polynomial equations \(x_i+\prod_jx_j^{A_{ij}}=N\). Freeze \(p\). The earlier tangent lemma in [the all-centers note, §2](EACSixAllCenters.md) applies (here \(q\ge p>N^\eta\) for any fixed \(\eta<1/4\) and corank is two): the remaining tangent has dimension at most one. If it is zero, there are \(O(1)\) isolated roots for each \(p\) and chart, by the full-rank Jacobian and affine Bézout. If it is one, freezing a moving coordinate \(x_i\) makes the restricted Jacobian full rank (choose five independent equations on the five remaining variables), again \(O(1)\) roots per fixed pair and chart. There are only finitely many arity≤4 charts. Thus it suffices to show that either some moving label is at most \(N^{1/2-\delta}\) for one uniform \(\delta>0\), or a separate exact divisor lane applies.

Define the **divisor lane** as actual charts with \(N-r=p^4,\ N-u=ra\), and \(N-a\) containing \(u\) at least twice and also another factor besides these two occurrences. Since any factor is at least \(p\), \(u^2p<N\), hence \(u\ll N^{3/8}\). For fixed \(p,u\),
\[
 r(a-1)=p^4-u>0.
\]
Each positive divisor \(r\) of this nonzero integer fixes \(N=p^4+r\). Enumeration of \(p\ll X^{1/4}\), \(u\ll X^{3/8}\) and the elementary \(X^{o(1)}\) divisor bound yields \(X^{5/8+o(1)}\) centers here. No tangent or primality search is used in this lane.

Suppose the desired uniform \(\delta\) fails outside that divisor lane. Then choose an unbounded sequence of one-dimensional tangents, with no nonzero derivative on any coordinate \(\le N^{1/2-\delta_j}\), where \(\delta_j\downarrow0\). Fix the arity chart and let all logarithmic weights \(\omega_i=\lim\log x_i/\log N\) exist by subsequence. Normalize a tangent vector in \(h_i=\delta x_i/x_i,n=\delta N/N\) variables with \(h_p=0\). The pure row forces \(n=(r/N)h_r\to0\), and the linearized equations are \(Ah-n\mathbf1=C(n\mathbf1-h)\) for diagonal \(C_{ii}=x_i/(N-x_i)=o(1)\). Thus a further limiting normalized tangent is a nonzero vector in \(\ker A\). This kernel has dimension 1: rank \(A\le4\) would give eigenvalues \(0,0,1,1,\rho,-2-\rho\) by trace zero, violating the Perron spectral radius \(\rho\); rank 5 follows by singularity. We may choose its real representative \(k\) (a limiting complex vector is a nonzero scalar multiple). Let \(S=\{i:k_i\ne0\}\). Every such derivative remains nonzero eventually, so \(\omega_i\ge1/2\) for \(i\in S\). In particular \(p,q\notin S\).

Any row containing a factor in \(S\) must contain a factor of opposite nonzero \(k\)-sign, since \(Ak=0\). With support weights at least \(1/2\), and all labels of weight at least \(1/4\), such a row has exactly two factors, both in \(S\), of opposite sign, multiplicity one. Every \(S\) label appears in one such row and therefore has weight exactly \(1/2\). The resulting undirected cofactor graph on \(S\) is bipartite, connected (each additional component would give another vector in \(\ker A\)), and has no isolated vertices. Let \(t=|S|\); since \(p,q\notin S\), \(2\le t\le4\). All other rows use only \(Z=S^c\). The \(S\)-pair incidence matrix has rank \(t-1\), so the disjoint-output \(Z\) block must have rank \(6-t\) to reach rank 5 overall. Thus there are \(t-1\) or \(t\) distinct \(S\)-pair rows: a tree, or a four-cycle if \(t=4\). This deduction uses only exact charts and existence of the limiting small-label stationary sequence.

## 2. Four support vertices are impossible

For the four-cycle let its shores be \(a<a'\) and \(b<b'\). The input of the smallest product \(ab\) is larger than both \(2a'\) and \(2b'\): start at the positive input of \(a'b'\), then compare with \(a'b\) and \(ab'\); distinct odd factors on a shore differ by at least 2. This input exceeds every support factor, but the only other labels \(p,q\) have lower limiting weights. Impossible.

In the four-vertex tree case the \(Z\) factors are only \(p,q\) and there must be three distinct \(Z\)-only products. Let \(\beta=\omega_q<1/2\). If \(1/4<\beta<1/2\), a product \(p^a q^b\) with \(b>0\) requires \(a/4+b\beta=1,\ a+b\le4\), with only these possible solutions: \(p q^2\) at \(\beta=3/8\), or \(q^3\) at \(\beta=1/3\). So apart from \(p^4\) at most one product is available. If \(\beta=1/4\), all \(Z\)-only monomials have arity 4, and any other exceeds \(p^4\) by at least \(2p^3=N^{3/4+o(1)}\). But all input labels now have weight at most \(1/2\) so \(M=N^{1/2+o(1)}\), a contradiction to the size of the input difference.

## 3. Two mutual pairs force the pure row input into the tree

For the remaining trees \(t=2,3\), choose a nonzero real eigenvector \(w\in\ker(I-A)\) with \(w_p=0\); this is possible by dimension two. The pure row also forces \(w_r=0\). Each sign of an eigenvalue-1 vector of this irreducible nonnegative matrix (Perron root \(\ge2\)) is present and contains a directed cycle; at any vertex of either sign its row needs a successor of the same sign. No loops are allowed. Therefore the other four labels are all nonzero and have signs split two-two; each sign pair is mutual. In particular any further zero is impossible. It also follows that some eigenvector of eigenvalue 1 has \(p\) coordinate nonzero: otherwise all vectors vanish at \(p,r\) and a nontrivial linear combination can vanish at another coordinate.

Each vertex of \(S\) other than \(r\) needs its distinct sign-mate as an input to an \(S\)-pair row, since the mate's row contains it. If \(r\notin S\) this takes \(t\) \(S\)-pair rows when the tree supplies only \(t-1\). Thus \(r\in S\), and these distinct sign-mates give exactly the \(S\)-pair inputs.

## 4. Three-vertex tree cannot occur

Write \(S=\{r,a,b\}\), and \(Z=\{p,u,v\}\) with \(u<v\), so \(u=q\) eventually because \(q\) has weight \(<1/2\) and all \(S\) weights are \(1/2\). If \(a,b\) are mutual mates, their rows must be the two \(S\)-pair rows. The cofactor star cannot have center \(a\) or \(b\), because the row with that input would have a self-factor. If its center is \(r\), the pair products must be \(br,ar\) in the \(a,b\) rows respectively, giving \(b-a=r(b-a)\), impossible. Thus each of \(a,b\) is paired with one of \(u,v\). The two rows with input \(u,v\) are precisely the \(S\)-pairs; the rows at \(a,b\) use only \(Z\) and each contains its own mutual mate \(u\) or \(v\). The other two \(Z\)-only rows are the pure row at \(r\) and the \(p\)-row, which cannot contain \(p\).

The weight of \(v\) cannot be \(<1/2\), as the difference of the \(u,v\) products is the star center times the nonzero even difference of two leaves in \(S\), of size at least \(N^{1/2+o(1)}\), whereas \(|u-v|\) would have strictly smaller weight. If the weight of \(v\) exceeds \(1/2\), \(v=M\) eventually and occurs as a factor in only one row (any two input labels containing it would differ by a nonzero multiple of \(v\), although less than \(v\)). That row is its mutual mate in \(S\). The \(p\)-row must then consist of \(u^j\) alone, with \(j=3\) or 4 since \(\omega_u<1/2\) and arities are at most four. The fourth-power choice is impossible: \(u^4-p^4\ge 2p^3\) while \(M<N/p=(1+o(1))p^3\). Thus \(j=3\), \(\omega_u=1/3\). The mutual mate row of \(u\) uses only \(p,u\), contains \(u\), and the weight equation \(i/4+j/3=1\) forces that same product \(u^3\). Distinct row inputs cannot have identical products. So \(\omega_v=1/2\).

If \(\omega_u>1/4\), the weight equation for the four \(Z\)-only rows leaves just \(p^4\), \(v^2\), \(p^2v\), and at most one \(u\)-containing monomial. For the last statement, without \(v\) the two-variable calculation in §2 applies; with one \(v\), \(i/4+j\omega_u=1/2\) has no solution with \(j\ge1\) in the open range \(1/4<\omega_u<1/2\). Thus \(v^2,p^2v\) must occur and \(v\sim\sqrt N\). Write \(s\) for the cofactor star center in \(S\). From the two input rows \(u,v\), the distance of the star leaves is \((v-u)/s=\!N^{o(1)}\); also \(2s\le v-u\), so \(s\le(1/2+o(1))\sqrt N\). The three \(Z\)-products \(p^4,p^2v,v^2\) have distinct pairwise gaps at least \(2\min(p^2,v)\sim2\sqrt N\). Among their potential row inputs, \(p,u,v,s\) are at most \((1+o(1))\sqrt N\), allowing at most one of those four. The two remaining star leaves have diameter \(o(\sqrt N)\), allowing at most one of them. Three distinct row inputs are impossible.

If \(\omega_u=1/4\), any \(Z\)-only product omitting \(v\) other than \(p^4\) is a quartic in \(p,u\), differs from \(p^4\) by at least \(2p^3\), and is impossible because \(M=N^{1/2+o(1)}\). The choices containing \(v\) are \(v^2\) and \(v p^2,vpu,vu^2\). At most one of the latter three can occur since their gaps are at least \(2pv=N^{3/4+o(1)}\), again too large. Thus fewer than four distinct \(Z\)-only rows exist in this case as well.

## 5. Two-vertex tree gives the divisor lane

Now \(S=\{r,a\}\). In the sign pattern of §3, the mate \(u\) of \(a\) belongs to \(Z\), has product \(ra\) in its row (the sole \(S\)-pair), and is a factor in the \(a\)-row. The two other nonzero sign-mates, call them \(v,w'\), have the same opposite sign to \(a,u\). The \(a\)-row uses only \(p,u,v,w'\). For the selected eigenvector with \(p=r=0\), its values at \(a,u\) are equal by the \(u\)-row. If \(a\)'s row used \(u\) only once, the remaining nonzero terms would come only from \(v,w'\), of the same opposite sign, hence neither can occur. That row would be \(p^\ell u\) for some \(\ell\ge1\). But for *any* eigenvalue-1 vector we have \(r=4p,\ u=r+a,\ a=\ell p+u\) as linear coordinate equations, implying \(p=0\) identically, contrary to §3. Thus \(u\) is used at least twice; equality of the \(a,u\) values when \(p=r=0\) now requires some opposite-sign term \(v\) or \(w'\) to occur. Therefore all exact divisor-lane conditions of §1 hold, contradicting the starting choice of sequence outside it.

The contradiction establishes a uniform \(\delta>0\) in §1. The nonsingular-subsystem count costs \(X^{1/4}X^{1/2-\delta}\) and the divisor lane costs \(X^{5/8+o(1)}\). Taking the smaller of \(\delta\) and \(1/8\) proves the advertised strict improvement within the pure-\(p^4\) lane. No new finite enumeration is used.
