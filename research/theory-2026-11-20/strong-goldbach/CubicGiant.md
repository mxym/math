# The giant binary-move components of every large ternary prime fiber

Put X=s, a sufficiently large odd integer, and I=[1/8,5/8]. On the primes p∈XI put a hyperedge for each triple of primes in XI, with repetitions allowed, of sum s. Two vertices lie in the same component if they can be joined through hyperedges; isolated labels are counted as components here. Two triples of a fixed weight are connected by binary prime moves exactly when they are connected through common labels: any two triples sharing a label differ by one legitimate pair move.

**Pointwise theorem.** If 3∤s there is one macroscopic central component; if 3|s there is one in EACH of the two nonzero residue classes modulo 3, and none of the central triples mixes the two classes. In each sector all but O_D(X/log^D X) prime labels belong to its one giant, for every fixed D>0. The constants are uniform in s. Every giant contains a positive absolute fraction of X/log X labels. These are statements about genuine primes and UNSTABILIZED moves at the fixed total s, not averaged in total s.

In the full fiber on all odd primes, the giant components may merge but cannot split. Apart from the at most two full components containing these giants, the total number of components is O_D(X/log^D X), and the total number of unordered triples in them is O_D(X²/log^D X), for every D. In particular the degree-(s,3) Hilbert function of the full binary Goldbach ring = number of nonempty components is O_D(s/log^D s). No uniform connectedness of the full fiber, and no binary Goldbach positivity, is asserted.

## 1. There are no intermediate-size central components

Uniformly for all but O_D(X/log^D X) even integers m in [3X/8,X], there are ≫X/log²X ordered prime-pair representations of m with both primes in XI. Use the classical balanced almost-all Goldbach asymptotic as stated by Bazzanella–Languasco in their [equation (2), H=1](https://www.research.unipd.it/bitstream/11577/122136/4/B-L.pdf), also applied in [Nelson §6.2, before Lemma 6.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14580v2#S6.SS2). For dyadic supports (M,2M] it supplies the assertion on a fixed inner subinterval of (5M/2,7M/2], since the archimedean count there is ≫M, the even singular series is ≫1, and proper powers contribute O(M^(1/2) log³M). A finite covering with M/X in [1/8,5/16] is possible: e.g. M/X=1/8,1/6,3/14,4/15,3/10 covers the claimed interval with overlap and all integer-rounding endpoint discrepancies harmless. Use the interiors of these target intervals, with sufficiently small fixed margins if desired. Call a label p≤5X/8 **good** if m=s−p has this many central partners. All but O_D(X/log^D X) such prime labels are good, as p↦s−p is injective.

We use the discrete extension estimate of Green–Tao, [Restriction theory of the Selberg sieve, Prop. 4.2, (4.7)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.538.pdf). Take a cyclic group of order M₀ comparable to X and >4X (e.g. a prime between 5X and 10X). For C⊂{primes in XI}, f_C(n)=(log n)1_C(n), set α_C=|C|log X/X and define Fourier coefficients by the normalized discrete transform on that group. For every fixed h>2,
    ||f̂_C||_{ℓ^h} ≪_h α_C^(1/2).
Indeed in (4.7) take k=1, F(n)=n, R=M₀^(1/11) and enveloping β_R, with β_R(n)≫log R on primes n∈XI by their Prop. 3.1(i). Take a_n=f_C(n)/β_R(n) on C, zero elsewhere. The weighted L² norm on the right is O(α_C^(1/2)).

Let C be a central component and k=|C|. All central triples through one of its labels are internal to C. Their ordered weighted count is at most M₀² Σ_b |f̂_C(b)|³ ≪X² α_C^(3/2). Normalization is exact because the chosen cyclic modulus is greater than 3X and cannot produce a wrap. Thus
    |C∩Good| X/log²X ≪ k^(3/2) X^(1/2)/log^(3/2)X.
There is an absolute η>0 such that k<ηX/log X implies |C∩Good|≤k/2. Summing over ALL these components bounds the totality of their labels by twice the central bad-label count. The rest are macroscopic of size ≥ηX/log X, with an absolute bound on their number. This uses the strong L²→L³ restriction estimate, not merely an unweighted exponential-sum bound.

## 2. Uniform approximation of cuts by the small-prime modulus model

Let w≥5 be fixed for now, W=∏_{ℓ<w}ℓ and λ_W(z)=(W/φ(W))1_{(z,W)=1}. For arbitrary disjoint central prime sets C,D the difference between the normalized mixed-triple counts with third weight (log z)1_{z prime}1_{XI}(z) and with λ_W(z)1_{XI}(z) is
    O((w^(−c)+o_w(1)) √(α_C α_D)).
The two other weights remain f_C, f_D, and a normalized count divides the ordered weighted count by M₀² (one may equivalently divide by X², at the cost of an absolute constant). Here and below all constants in the main O(w^(−c)) are independent of w. To verify, the Fourier ℓ⁴ norm of the difference of third weights is ≪w^(−c)+o_w(1), by the U² identity and [Tao–Teräväinen, Theorem 1.3(ii), (1.4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.02158v4#S1.Thm3). Apply (1.4) to each of the two ORIGIN-based prefixes ending at 5X/8 and just before X/8 and subtract by the triangle inequality. Zero-extending to M₀>4X creates no false additive parallelograms; its normalization is comparable to the interval normalization. Replacing Λ by actual log-weighted primes changes the ℓ⁴ norm by o(1), for example using ||f̂||₄≤||f̂||∞^(1/2)||f̂||₂^(1/2) and O(√X log X) proper powers. Fourier inversion and Hölder with exponents 8/3,8/3,4, and the restriction estimate for C,D give the assertion. In particular a partition into components has zero ACTUAL mixed-triple count, hence a model cut of mass ≪w^(−c)+o_w(1).

## 3. The finite local model has only the asserted sectors, uniformly in W

For a sector (all reduced classes if 3∤s, or one allowed sign at 3 if 3|s), use uniform measure on the reduced residue labels of that sector, independently of Lebesgue measure on I. The model kernel on two such labels (x,r),(y,u) is the product of the archimedean kernel 1_{1−x−y∈I} and the finite kernel (W/φ(W)) 1_{(s−r−u,W)=1}. If 3|s the other-sign cross kernel vanishes. We prove that each sector has absolute total edge mass bounded below, its stationary vertex measure dominates an absolute constant times the uniform measure, and its normalized random walk has an absolute spectral gap in absolute value.

At ℓ≥5 the neighbors of r in the ℓ−1 nonzero classes are all classes except possibly s−r (when this is nonzero). The symmetric kernel gives the walk choosing one of the neighbors uniformly, with stationary law proportional to its degree. Any two rows of its stochastic matrix have overlap mass ≥(ℓ−3)/(ℓ−1)≥1/2. The difference between the maximum and minimum values of Pf is thus at most half that of f, so every nonconstant eigenvalue has absolute value ≤1/2. At ℓ=3, s≠0, the unnormalized matrix up to a permutation is [[1,1],[1,0]] with walk eigenvalues 1,−1/2. At ℓ=3, s=0, each sign is a one-state sector. At 2 there is a single state. By Chinese remaindering the finite walk in each sector is the tensor product of these walks, so has the same absolute nonconstant eigenvalue bound.

For ℓ≥3, if ℓ|s then individual degrees are constant; if ℓ∤s the min-to-average degree ratio is (ℓ−2)/(ℓ−2+1/(ℓ−1)). The product of these lower bounds, each 1−O(ℓ^(−2)), is bounded below absolutely. This also proves stationary domination after conditioning on the sign at 3. The total local edge factor, including the weight ℓ/(ℓ−1), is 1−1/(ℓ−1)² for ℓ|s odd and 1+1/(ℓ−1)³ if ℓ∤s odd, by counting the forbidden pairs r+u=s. The factor at 2 is 2. The conditioned-at-3 factors are harmless nonzero absolute constants: if the original endpoint measures give a single sign mass 1/2, its mixed model inside that sign contributes an additional 1/4 versus conditioning both endpoints. Hence the normalized finite total mass is bounded above and below uniformly.

The archimedean walk at x chooses y uniformly in J(x)=I∩[3/8−x,7/8−x]. Its degree len(J(x)) is bounded above and below by positive absolutes on I, hence so is its stationary density. For K=[7/32,9/32], every J(x) meets K in length at least 1/32, and J(z)⊃[1/4,1/2] for all z∈K. Thus the two-step walk dominates a fixed positive multiple of the same uniform law on [1/4,1/2] from every x. This gives an absolute bound ρ_I<1 for nonconstant eigenvalues in absolute value: P² contracts oscillations of bounded functions by a factor <1; the reversible kernel is compact (bounded density) on its stationary L² space, with every nonzero-eigenvalue eigenfunction bounded since P maps L² to bounded functions. Tensoring the archimedean and finite walks leaves an absolute ρ<1. Stationary domination and absolute lower total edge mass also survive the product.

Thus for any fractional 0≤f≤1 on one sector, its model cross mass against 1−f is bounded below by a fixed positive constant times m(1−m), with m the stationary mean of f. Indeed ⟨f,P(1−f)⟩ ≥m(1−m)−ρ Var(f)≥(1−ρ)m(1−m). In particular the lower bound is uniform and positive if f and 1−f both have fixed positive UNIFORM means.

## 4. Passing from the model to true components and cubes

If two macroscopic central components occur in the same sector for arbitrarily large s, take a sequence of such s, C one of these components and D all other central primes in its sector. Both endpoint sets then have α bounded below absolutely by §1. Fix w. Pass to a subsequence with s constant modulo W and with weak limits of the measures X^(−1)Σ_{p∈C}(log p)δ_(p/X,p mod W), and likewise D. PNT in fixed progressions on subintervals shows that their sum is exactly Lebesgue measure times uniform reduced residue measure, restricted to the sector. The two limits therefore have relative densities f,1−f with 0≤f≤1, each of integral bounded below. The model mixed count divided by X² tends to the integral of the model kernel against these two limiting measures; the archimedean boundary x+y=1−inf I or 1−sup I has zero product-limit mass, since both marginal measures have bounded Lebesgue densities in each residue. By §3 the integral is bounded BELOW by an absolute constant independent of w, contradicting §2 for w sufficiently large. This proves at most one macroscopic component per sector for every large s. There is at least one in every sector by PNT and the negligible total of small components (§1). The super-logarithmic bound on the complement now follows from §1, not from a purported effective rate in the compactness step.

For the full fiber, a good label p≤5X/8 has ≫X/log²X prime neighbors in XI via all-central partners (call this two-partner condition, which allows p itself to be outside XI). Thus it attaches to a central giant as the total number of exceptional central labels is O_D(X/log^D X). When 3|s and p>3 the attached sign must be that of p, by reduction modulo 3, since every central label exceeds 3. At most one label of a sum-s triple exceeds X/2, so every triple missing both central giants has at least two bad labels from the O_D(X/log^D X) set in [3,5X/8]. Fixing the pair determines the third. Different nonempty components have disjoint label sets, so their number is bounded by the number of bad labels. This proves both bounds in the theorem.

A directly usable criterion for Nelson's [Conjecture B.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14580v2#A2.SS3): put s=q+t+1 for primes q,t≥5. If each of the two even integers q+1 and t+1 has >X/log^K X distinct ordered prime-pair partners within XI (both summands in XI), for a fixed K and sufficiently large X, the original cubic identity x_q y_t=x_t y_q holds without a multiplier. Indeed q and t themselves then attach to central giants. If 3|s, reducing q+t+1 gives q≡t≡1 mod3, so both attach to the same sign. More generally it suffices that each of the endpoint triples (q,c,d), (t,a,b), c+d=t+1, a+b=q+1, contains SOME label v with this many central partners for s−v, provided the two giant sectors reached coincide. With 3∤s there is only one. If 3|s then two rich nonzero labels of the same residue suffice, or a rich label 3 attaches to BOTH signs (its partners occur in opposite pairs, and all but a super-log-sparse set are in the giants). Without the same-sector condition the more-general criterion would be unjustified if a triple contains 3 and only its opposite-sign label is rich. No individual quantitative lower bound on q+1,t+1 of the required size for every pair is known.

**Decisive remaining exception.** If an even N divisible by 6 were unrepresented, then at s=N+3 the label 3 occurs in no triple at all, and EVERY triple has all three labels in just one nonzero sign modulo 3. The two central macroscopic components proven above would remain distinct even in the full prime fiber. Thus full-fiber connectivity here, unlike the existence of a giant in each legitimate local sector, would rule out these exceptions. A representation of N with endpoints attached to the two central giants would suffice to join them; their merger itself implies existence of some representation of N, because only a triple containing 3 can cross signs. We do not assume or prove such a merger.
