# Removing genuine bounded even ranks from the symmetric reflected correlation

Fix an integer $R\ge3$ and an accuracy $\epsilon>0$. There is an explicit one-parameter score below for which, for all sufficiently large prescribed even $P$,
$$ \left|G(P)-X_P-\sum_{x\in I,\,(x,P)=1}K_R(x)K_R(P-x)\right|<\epsilon X_P.       \tag{1} $$
Here $I=[P/3,2P/3]$, $L=\log P$, $X_P=|I|\mathfrak S(P)$, and $G(P)$ is the true central two-prime count weighted by the two logarithms. The remaining sum has **no prime or semiprime input and no even rank at most $R$ on either side**. A triple contributes a *nonnegative* score. More generally every retained rank $k\le R$ has an exact signed indicator formula depending on its physical divisors, not a modeled density. Consequently a negative contribution of two ranks at most $R$ requires at least one odd rank $\ge5$. The unrestricted higher-rank sum can still have either sign; (1) proves no prime lower bound.

Choose $0<\theta<1/20$ sufficiently small depending on $R,\epsilon$, then let $P$ grow. Put $\eta=\theta^2$, $W=P^\eta$, $A=P^\theta$, $g=1/2-\theta$, $D=P^g$, and $Y(x)=\mathbf1_{P^-(x)>W}$. Set
$$
 F(x)=Y(x)\sum_{d|x,\,d\le D}\mu(d)(\log x-\mathbf1_{d\le A}\log d),\qquad
 K(x)=Y(x)\Lambda(x)-F(x).
$$
The original $K$ is zero at central primes. Define $K_R$ to be zero outside squarefree $W$-rough composites. On those composites put
$$ K_R(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 -\log x\displaystyle\sum_{d|x,\,d<\sqrt x}\mu(d),&2\le\Omega(x)\le R,\\
 K(x),&\Omega(x)>R.
 \end{cases}                                                            \tag{2}
$$
Squarefree composites are never perfect squares. At even rank, the signs of complementary divisors agree, so exactly half of the zero total Möbius divisor sum occurs below the square root. At rank three, if the largest prime is above $\sqrt x$ the sum is $1-2+1=0$; otherwise each singleton, no pair, occurs below it and the sum is $1-3=-2$. The two possible scores are thus $0,2\log x$. If desired, at every bounded odd rank the multiplier of $\log x$ is an even integer: $2^{k-1}$ divisors occur in the half sum.

**Order of quantifiers matters.** We neither take $R$ comparable to $1/\eta$ nor claim one fixed score eliminates all even ranks. The small bound below is on the *signed* change of the reflected correlation; we do not discard the absolute value of all the original mixed terms. This extends the [symmetric four-rough-variable calculation](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/a1209af1e8624ead8f8a9a82da4b97125e2f61df) with a deliberately flattened medium-divisor coefficient.

## 1. Recalibrating after flattening only the medium divisors

Write $\langle B,C\rangle_P=\sum_{x\in I,(x,P)=1}B(x)C(P-x)$, and let $\Lambda_W=Y\Lambda$. Proper powers and central primes dividing $P$ cost $P^{1/2+o(1)}$ at fixed parameters, also with our fixed rough divisor and logarithm bounds. Discarding all squareful $W$-rough inputs later costs $P^{1-c(\eta)}$: their number is $\ll P/W+\sqrt P$ and the scores on such rows and their partners are bounded by $O_\eta(L)$ when the partner is rough. Using $\Lambda_W=F+K$ exactly gives
$$ G=\langle\Lambda_W,F\rangle_P+\langle F,K\rangle_P+\langle K,K\rangle_P+o(X_P).      \tag{3} $$
There is no prime term hidden inside a large-rank approximation.

Use the same positive upper/lower one-dimensional sieves as in the [four-slot proof, §§1,4](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/19b9b6ee7b1602538b24364ee544007d0d93a1aa), now with level $E=P^{\theta/2}$, so $DE=P^{1/2-\theta/2}$ and $s=\log E/\log W=1/(2\theta)$. Their relative error about the Euler model is bounded, in limsup, by $\Delta(s)=2e^{\gamma_E}\rho(s-1)/s$. In a progression requiring $d|P-x$ the signed short rough divisor $d\le D$ is coprime to every sieving modulus supported on primes $\le W$ not dividing $P$. The actual principal condition is $(d,Px)=1$; the additional excluded primes dividing a rough $x$ are all $>W$. Uniformly in such $x\le P$, and likewise when only the target mask is present, the $d$-model multiplier relative to $\mathfrak S(P)$ tends to
$$ c_\theta=e^{-\gamma_E}\left[\eta^{-1}\rho(g/\eta)
       -(\theta/\eta)\rho(\theta/\eta)+\int_0^{\theta/\eta}\rho(v)dv\right].       \tag{4} $$
In fact the rough signed harmonic $\mu(d)/\phi(d)$ has zeroth moment $\rho(g/\eta)+o(1)$ up to $D$, while the first $\log d/\log W$ moment is taken **only up to $A$**, giving $(\theta/\eta)\rho(\theta/\eta)-\int_0^{\theta/\eta}\rho+o(1)$. These finite harmonic limits follow by the fixed finite-prime expansion and Mertens. Multiplication by $V_P(W)\sim e^{-\gamma_E}\mathfrak S(P)/\log W$ pays every factor of $L$. Primes $>W$ in the mask $Px$ have total reciprocal mass $o(1)$, uniformly, as there are at most $2/\eta$ of them. The archimedean $\log(P-x)$ differs from $L$ by $O(1)$ in the central interval. In particular $c_\theta=1+O_B(\theta^B)$ for every fixed $B>0$, by $\int_0^\infty\rho=e^{\gamma_E}$ and $\rho(j)\le1/j!$.

For later use we give the actual source transfer behind (3). If $C$ is one of the fixed convolutions below supported on $W$-rough target units, put $m_C=|I|^{-1}\sum_{x\in I,(x,P)=1}C(x)$ and $m_{|C|}$ similarly. Whenever its sign-split coefficient has centered convolution distribution with arbitrary log saving to $DE$ as detailed below, one has
$$ \langle F,C\rangle_P/X_P
       =c_\theta m_C+O\big(\eta^{-2}\Delta(s)m_{|C|}\big)+o_{\theta,R}(1).           \tag{5}$$
Here and below a sieve limsup error may be enlarged by an arbitrarily small $o(1)$ at fixed parameters. To justify (5), open $d|P-x$ and bound source roughness in its free factor with the linear sieve; choose the bounds separately according to the signs of $\mu(d)$ and $C(x)$. The physical factor $\log(P-x)-\mathbf1_{d\le A}\log d$ is nonnegative. The discrepancy in the actual class $P\bmod de$ sums with fixed divisor multiplicity, leaving the principal over $(x,de)=1$. Since $x$ is rough, the sieve part is independent of $x$ and equals $V_P(W)$ up to its upper/lower error. The absolute normalized budget per unit $m_{|C|}$ is at most $(1+o(1))\eta^{-2}$ by $\sum_{Y(d)=1,d\le D}\mu^2(d)/\phi(d)\le g/\eta+o(1)$ and $V_P(W)L/\mathfrak S(P)\le\eta^{-1}$. For a signed sum of a fixed number of convolutions one may instead pay the sum of their absolute masses. For a prime source, ordinary maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov at $DE<P^{1/2}$ gives $\langle\Lambda_W,F\rangle_P/X_P=c_\theta+O(\eta^{-2}\Delta(s))+o_\theta(1)$ by the same computation, inserting the two signs of the short divisor before bounding roughness. In this prime-source case the prime is *not* required to have a polynomial factorization; its proper powers are negligible and target-unit removal is harmless.

For $K$ use its actual polynomial-slot decomposition
$$ K(x)=Y(x)\left[\sum_{tb=x,\,t>D}\mu(t)\log b
                       -\sum_{db=x,\,A<d\le D}\mu(d)\log d\right].                 \tag{6} $$
In the first term $b>W$ when its log is nonzero, and in the second $b\gg P^{1-g}$. Both convolutions thus have two polynomial slots for fixed $\theta$. Their combined unsigned ordinary mean on $I$ is $O(\eta^{-2})$ with an absolute implied constant: the rough harmonic sum of the marked divisor is $\le(1+o(1))\eta^{-1}$ by its Euler upper bound, and its free rough cofactor count in the full central interval is $\ll P/(t\log W)$ uniformly when that cofactor can exceed $W$ (use the prime upper bound below $W^2$). Their **signed** mean is bounded in limsup by $2\eta^{-1}\rho(\theta/\eta)$: in $u=\log t/\log W$, the first term has density $\rho'(u)(1/\eta-u)\omega(1/\eta-u)$ from $g/\eta$ to $1/\eta-1$, and the second has $\rho'(u)u\omega(1/\eta-u)$ from $\theta/\eta$ to $g/\eta$. Here $0\le\omega\le1$ and $\rho'\le0$. Uniform convergence at the free endpoint follows by first excluding $1\le1/\eta-u\le1+\zeta$, then sending $\zeta\downarrow0$ using positive rough harmonic masses; see [the symmetric computation](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/a1209af1e8624ead8f8a9a82da4b97125e2f61df). Further target-unit restrictions cost $o(P)$.

The usual large sieve for two independent polynomial slots, with finite-factor Siegel–Walfisz for small nonprincipal conductors, proves centered complete distribution up to any fixed $P^{1/2-c}$ for each sign-split slot convolution in (6); see the linked four-slot proof §1. Thus (5), applied to the two pieces of (6), proves from (3) that
$$ \limsup_{P\to\infty}|G/X_P-1-\langle K,K\rangle_P/X_P|
       \ll_B \theta^B                                                     \tag{7} $$
for each fixed $B$, since both $\eta^{-4}\Delta(1/(2\theta))$ and the displayed Dickman tails decay faster than every power of $\theta$. The error in a fixed-$W$ linear sieve was never called an arbitrary-log saving in $P$.

## 2. Where the bounded-rank score is changed, on actual integers

Set $S=K_R-K$, ignoring the already negligible squareful rows. This may have either sign. It vanishes on primes and outside squarefree ranks $2\le k\le R$. On such rows $K=-F$. Apart from physical rows having a divisor in $(D,\sqrt x)$, the short alternating sum $\sum_{d|x,d\le D}\mu(d)$ is the exact half sum. Thus outside this exceptional set $|S(x)|\le 2^R\theta L$, since each of the logarithmic corrections $d\le A$ is at most $\theta L$. Everywhere $|S(x)|\ll_R L$.

Uniformly in $0<\theta<1/20$, with $P$ tending to infinity first, we have
$$ \limsup m_{|S|}\ll_R\theta\,[1+\log(1/\eta)]^R.                         \tag{8} $$
Here is a direct natural-density proof; in particular no shrinking factor window requires a short-interval prime theorem. On rank $k$ select the largest prime of $x$, necessarily $\gg_R P^{1/k}$. The other $k-1$ prime harmonic sums over $(W,P]$ cost at most $(1+\log(1/\eta))^{k-1}$ in limsup, and the terminal prime count is $\ll_R P/(uL)$ after fixing their product $u$. This pays the $\theta L$ bound off the exceptional set.

On that set, split the factors into the two nonempty subproducts $d,x/d$ with $P^{1/2-\theta}<d<\sqrt x$. Both lie between $P^{.44}$ and $P^{.56}$ for large $P$. Select one largest prime in each, written $r,s$; each is $\ge P^{.44/k}$. For each choice of the remaining $k-2$ factors, of product $u$, the $r$ harmonic sum is over an interval with log-width at most $\theta L+O(1)$, since its accompanying partial product in $d$ is fixed. Truncated to $r\ge P^{.44/k}$ this reciprocal sum is $O_R(\theta)+o_{\theta,R}(1)$ by uniform prime Mertens on a fixed positive exponent range (differences of prefixes, with an $o(1)$, suffice). For the remaining prime $s$, the central condition gives $\ll_R P/(urL)$ possibilities when any $s\ge P^{.44/k}$ is eligible, by the ordinary prime upper bound. The remaining harmonic selections cost $O_R((1+\log(1/\eta))^{k-2})$. Bounded label permutations and choices of the subset cost only in $R$. This proves (8) also at the actual central square-root boundary. The target-unit restriction can only reduce these positive counts.

For orientation, for a squarefree semiprime $x=rs$ with $r<s$, the exact original $K$ is $-\log r$ for $r\le A$, zero for $A<r\le D$, and $-\log x$ for $r>D$, since eventually $s>D$. Its whole ordinary negative mass in units of $|I|$ tends to
$$ \int_\eta^\theta\frac{dt}{1-t}+
       \int_g^{1/2}\frac{dt}{t(1-t)}
   =\log\frac{1-\eta}{1-\theta}+\log\frac{1-g}{g}.                    \tag{9}$$
The lower cut $r>W$ is understood here. This small marginal alone does not imply small reflected interaction; the following section is needed.

## 3. The fixed-target transfer needed for deleting both sides

The finite-rank sequences $S_+,S_-$ and $|S|$ admit centered convolution distribution on unit classes up to every fixed $P^{1/2-c}$ with any fixed log saving, at fixed $R,\theta$. We detail the regularity needed for this assertion; an arbitrary mask on a product of primes would not suffice. Expand into $k$ ordered distinct primes $>W$. The decisions in (2), $F$, and (6) are finitely many comparisons of partial products with $A,D,\sqrt x$, and of individual factors for ordering. Between their boundaries each value is an affine combination of the prime logs with bounded-$R$ coefficients. The operations positive part, negative part, and absolute value are Lipschitz there. Partition every prime slot into rectangles with relative mesh $1+L^{-B}$. Off boundary boxes the weight is approximated to $O_R(L^{-B})$ by a constant, including for the sign-split weights; the number of boxes is a fixed power of $L$.

For the boundary boxes use the positive full-rectangle envelope. Each jumped comparison fixes at least one slot, once the others are fixed, to a multiplicative interval of relative width $O_R(L^{-B})$ (for the half cut the coefficients of the logs in the comparison are $+1$ or $-1$). The same holds for a clipped central endpoint or a tie boundary; the constant divisor $1$ causes no jump. Every slot has length-scale $\ge P^\eta$. Dropping primality on that narrowed slot, the number of affected tuples is $O_R(P L^{-B+C_R}+P^{1-\eta}L^{C_R})$ by elementary integer counting and harmonic sums in the other slots. Thus its ordinary total can pay any fixed log factor by choosing $B$ large. To bound the progression contribution of the positive envelope, the large sieve applies separately on each full rectangle with only a polylog number of boxes: take one prime as the first slot and the product of the others as the second (both polynomially long). A primitive small conductor has Siegel–Walfisz on that first prime interval, uniformly after the additional $lP$ unit mask; it removes at most $O(L)$ primes. The large-conductor bound from the referenced proof §1 is $L^{O_R(1)}[P/R_0+R_0\sqrt P+P/\sqrt T+P/\sqrt B_0]$ in primitive conductor dyads $R_0$ and slot lengths $T,B_0$. It applies to the fixed prime convolutions in the second slot; their pointwise multiplicity is $\ll_R1$ for squarefree inputs and remains $\ll_R1$ for a fixed number of possibly repeated labels. This gives arbitrary log saving per box, including bounded extra divisor powers of the modulus. Bound the principal on all boundary envelopes by their just-estimated positive ordinary mass times $\sum_{k\le Q}\tau(k)^C/\phi(k)\ll L^{C'}$. Box summation and choosing the exponents in this order proves the assertion, including for the actual $x$-dependent half test and not merely a dyadic signed rank sum.

We record two additional positive upper bounds, with absolute or $R$-dependent constants uniform in the outer limit $\theta\downarrow0$. First,
$$ |\langle\Lambda_W,S\rangle_P|/X_P\ll m_{|S|}+o_{\theta,R}(1).           \tag{10} $$
Indeed upper-sieve the true reflected prime by excluding all primes $\le P^{\alpha}$ for $\alpha=1/(10R)$ with level $P^{.22}$ (for $R\ge3$, level parameter $.22/\alpha>2$). The tested source is the nonnegative genuine rank sequence $|S|$ for which we just proved distribution. For a fixed $x$ in its principal, exclude $r|Px$ from the sieve and use $1/(r-1)$ otherwise. The resulting product is $(1+o(1))e^{-\gamma_E}\mathfrak S(P)/(\alpha L)$ uniformly on rank-$R$ rough $x$; the at most $R$ extra prime factors are all $>W$. A true central prime has weight at most $L$. Proper powers can be removed separately. The implied constant in (10) may depend on $R$ but not on $\theta$.

Second, for the self term,
$$ |\langle S,S\rangle_P|/X_P
    \ll_R (1+\log(1/\eta))^{R-1}m_{|S|}+o_{\theta,R}(1).                  \tag{11} $$
Use $|S(y)|\ll_R L$ on squarefree $W$-rough rank-$\le R$ inputs. Open **all** of its prime factors $W<r\le P^\alpha$: there are $j\le R-1$, since the largest factor of $y\in I$ is $\gg_R P^{1/R}>P^\alpha$. Their product $b$ is $\le P^{(R-1)\alpha}<P^{.1}$. Majorize by the positive sum over these distinct possible labels with $b|y$ and the cofactor $y/b$ sieved free of primes $\le P^\alpha$. Every qualifying row is covered and no claim is made that all rows of the majorant have the specified rank. If $W>P^\alpha$ the small list is empty; alternatively take $\theta$ small enough that $W<P^\alpha$. Sieve at the same level $E'=P^{.22}$; the composite tested modulus $be$ is below $P^{.32}$, of divisor-bounded multiplicity even when sieve primes divide $b$. In the genuine class $x\equiv P\pmod{be}$ retain $(Px,b)=1$. At $r|b$ the conditional density for $r|y/b$ is $1/r$ by $\phi(b)/\phi(br)$, not $1/(r-1)$; the ratio of that local rough factor to the latter is $1+O(W^{-2})$. Excluding target divisors and the at most $R$ large primes dividing the source $x$ changes the normalized product only by the same uniform singular factor as in (10). Now $\sum_b1/\phi(b)\ll_R(1+\log(1/\eta))^{R-1}$ via the $j$ harmonic sums on $(W,P^\alpha]$. Multiplying the prime-free density by $L$, and using finite-rank distribution to pay all progression errors, proves (11). All source approximations are taken at fixed $\theta,R$ first.

Finally, (5) applies separately to $S_+,S_-$, so $|\langle F,S\rangle_P|/X_P\ll_R m_{|S|}+o_{\theta,R}(1)$ for all sufficiently small $\theta$; here the crucial short-divisor principal is evaluated **signed**, not bounded in absolute value before calibration. Together with (10) this gives the same upper bound for $|\langle K,S\rangle_P|/X_P$. By symmetry of the interval and target-unit condition we have exactly, for $K_R=K+S$,
$$ \langle K,K\rangle_P=\langle K_R,K_R\rangle_P-2\langle K,S\rangle_P-\langle S,S\rangle_P. $$
Combine (7), (8) and (11). The total normalized limsup error in (1) is $O_R(\theta[1+\log(1/\eta)]^{2R-1})+O_B(\theta^B)$, which tends to zero with $\eta=\theta^2$. Choose $\theta$ for a margin strictly below $\epsilon$ and then $P$ sufficiently large. This deletes actual interacting bounded-rank rows, not merely their unsifted arithmetic marginals. It does **not** bound the still-present interaction of the higher and odd ranks in (2).
