# A three-step factor-graph statistic estimates the binary Goldbach count at the same center

Let \(N\) be a sufficiently large even integer, \(L=\log N\), \(L_2=\log L\), and \(R(N)=\#\{p\text{ prime}:N-p\text{ prime}\}\) (ordered, counting a diagonal once). On the odd primes \(3\le x\le N-3\) coprime to \(N\) use the directed edge \(x\to q\) for every distinct prime divisor \(q\mid N-x\). These are the reverse of [Bożek's factorization-graph arrows](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900), restricted to units. Every prime divisor in such a row is again a vertex. Write \(U_j(N,S)\) for the set of vertices with **no** directed path of length at most \(j\) to the specified vertex set \(S\) (length zero allowed).

**Fixed-pivot theorem.** For each fixed odd prime \(\ell\), uniformly over sufficiently large even \(N\) coprime to \(\ell\),
\[
 \big|\,|U_3(N,\{\ell\})|-R(N)\,\big|
 \ \ll_\ell\ \frac{N(\log\log N)^{C_\ell}}{(\log N)^{2+1/(\ell-1)}}.
\]
In particular the absolute error is \(o_\ell(N/\log^2 N)\), without a bound on the singular series, any Goldbach assumption, or averaging over centers. For example the estimate at pivot 3 holds for *every* sufficiently large even \(N\not\equiv0\bmod3\). If the avoidance statistic exceeds this error bound by an arbitrarily large constant factor, the original center itself is represented. No lower bound on the avoidance statistic is asserted.

### A general sieve estimate

For a seed set \(S\) put
\[
 T=\{t\le N-3:t\text{ an odd prime},\ (t,N)=1,\ t\equiv N\bmod s\text{ for some }s\in S\}.
\]
Every \(t\in T\) has an actual edge to \(S\). Suppose there are fixed \(0<\delta\le1,\ C>0\) such that, uniformly for \(3\le Y\le N-3\),
\[
 \sum_{t\le Y,\ t\in T}1/t\ \ge\ \delta\log\log Y-C\log L_2-O(1). \tag{1}
\]
We prove that
\[
 \big|\,|U_3(N,S)|-R(N)\,\big|
 \ \ll_{\delta,C}\ 1+|S|+NL_2^{C'}/L^{2+\delta}, \tag{2}
\]
with a constant also allowed to depend on the uniform absolute constant in (1). A prime-pair vertex \(x\) has only its prime partner \(N-x\) as successor, and that partner only \(x\); a pair not meeting \(S\) therefore always survives. At most \(2|S|\) ordered endpoints are lost this way. The only nonunit pair is the diagonal when \(N/2\) is prime, losing at most one. The remaining issue is false positives with \(N-x\) composite.

Here is the elementary uniform sieve estimate used twice. For integers \(u\le Y\), forbid at most three specified residues modulo each prime \(5\le t\le z=Y^\gamma\). For any sufficiently small fixed \(\gamma>0\) the survivor count is
\[
 \ll Y\prod_{5\le t\le z}(1-\nu(t)/t)+Y^{1/2},
\]
where \(\nu(t)\le3\) is the number of distinct forbidden residues; products here run over primes. Indeed Selberg's upper-bound sieve with divisor support \(D=Y^{1/8}\) gives main upper \(Y/G\), where \(G=\sum_{d\le D}\prod_{t\mid d}\nu(t)/(t-\nu(t))\) over squarefree \(d\) supported on the sieving primes. The unrestricted Euler product is the reciprocal of the displayed product. In its probability normalization each \(t\) occurs with probability \(\nu(t)/t\), so the expected \(\log d\) is at most \(3\sum_{t\le z}(\log t)/t\le 3\log z+O(1)\). For \(\gamma\le1/100\) Markov gives at least half the mass on \(d\le D\) for all sufficiently large \(Y\). The remainder in the Selberg quadratic upper bound is \(O(D^2(\log(2D))^{O(1)})\): the usual minimizing coefficients have modulus at most one, and each pair \(d,d'\le D\) has CRT interval-count error at most \(3^{\omega(d)}3^{\omega(d')}\); summing uses \(3^{\omega(d)}\le\tau_3(d)\). Small \(Y\) are absorbed into the constant. See [Ford, *Sieve Methods*](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf) for the standard Selberg upper bound. Throughout, ignoring all sieving primes dividing a nonzero integer \(b\) of size at most \(N^3\) costs at most \(O(L_2^{O(1)})\) in these product bounds: primes up to \(L^2\) have harmonic sum \(\log L_2+O(1)\), and the remaining prime divisors contribute \(O(L/L^2)\). This also covers all bad primes of the center, not merely finitely many fixed exceptions.

Let \(A=U_2(N,S)\). If \(q\in A\), then no \(t\in T\) divides \(N-q\). Count such primes \(q\le Y\) by forbidding \(q=0\bmod t\) for all sieving primes and \(q=N\bmod t\) additionally for \(t\in T\). The roots are distinct as \((t,N)=1\); primes \(q\le z\) cost at most \(z\). By Mertens, (1), and the displayed sieve estimate,
\[
 A(Y):=\#\{q\in A:q\le Y\}\ll E\,Y/(\log(2Y))^{1+\delta},\qquad E=L_2^{C_1}, \tag{3}
\]
for \(2\le Y\le N\), enlarging a fixed \(C_1\) and the implied constant where necessary. The \(N-3\) endpoint causes no issue since \(z\le N-3\). Write \(\mathcal H\) for the multiplicative semigroup generated by \(A\), including 1. We need its *uniform* harmonic mass. Take \(Y_*=\exp(L_2^{(2C_1+2)/\delta})\). Primes up to \(Y_*\) have harmonic sum \(O(\log L_2)\) by Mertens; by (3) and partial summation the remaining \(A\)-primes have harmonic sum \(O(E(\log Y_*)^{-\delta})=O(1)\). Thus
\[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal H}1/m=\prod_{q\in A}(1-1/q)^{-1}\ll L_2^{C_2}. \tag{4}
\]
An additional uniform consequence is a smooth-part bound. Fix \(\kappa=4,\ 0<\theta<\delta/(2\kappa)\), and \(\beta=\kappa L_2/L\). For \(q\le\exp(L/L_2)\) one has \(q^\beta\le e^\kappa\), so \(\sum_{q\in A}q^{\beta-1}\) restricted there is \(O(\log L_2)\). For \(\exp(L/L_2)<q\le N^\theta\), (3) gives an upper bound \(O(L^{\kappa\theta} E (L/L_2)^{-\delta})=o(1)\). All higher Euler-product powers contribute \(O(1)\) since \(\beta<1/4\) eventually. Rankin's inequality now proves
\[
 \#\{m\in\mathcal H:m\le N,\ P^+(m)\le N^\theta\}
 \ \le N^{1-\beta}\prod_{q\in A,\ q\le N^\theta}(1-q^{\beta-1})^{-1}
 \ \ll NL_2^{C_3}/L^4. \tag{5}
\]
This step has not assumed that the small prime factors of \(N\) have bounded harmonic mass.

For any false three-step survivor all prime factors of \(N-x\) belong to \(A\). Use (5) if its largest prime factor is \(<N^\theta\). Otherwise write \(N-x=mq\) with \(q\ge N^\theta\) its largest prime factor, \(m\in\mathcal H,\ 2\le m\le N^{1-\theta}\). For fixed \(m\) take \(Y=N/m\), so \(\log Y\ge\theta L\). The sieve variable is \(q\le Y\); both \(q\) and \(N-mq=x\) are prime, and no \(t\in T\) divides \(N-q\). Outside prime divisors of \(m(m-1)N\), forbid residues
\[
 0,\quad N/m\pmod t,\qquad\text{and additionally }N\pmod t\text{ if }t\in T.
\]
These are distinct. This is precisely where \(m\ne1\) is essential: the two reflected roots agree identically at a genuine pair. The discarded integer is positive and \(\le N^3\); its factor cost is as above. Choose fixed \(\gamma\le\min(1/100,\theta/2)\) so \(q>Y^\gamma\); discard \(x\le N^\gamma\) **once globally**, at cost \(O(N^\gamma)\). Then the two prime tests are legitimate sieves without exceptions. By (1) the count for this \(m\) is
\[
 \ll Y L_2^{C_4}/(\log Y)^{2+\delta}\ll_\theta (N/m)L_2^{C_4}/L^{2+\delta}.
\]
Sum using (4), and combine with (5) and the true endpoint accounting to obtain (2).

For \(S=\{\ell\}\), the fixed-modulus prime number theorem with its standard de la Vallée Poussin error, or Mertens' theorem in arithmetic progressions, gives \(\sum_{t\le Y,\ t\equiv N(\ell)}1/t=\log\log Y/(\ell-1)+O_\ell(1)\) over primes. Excluding \(t\mid N\), the prime 2 and the last three endpoints costs \(O(\log L_2+1)\) as above. This proves (1), uniformly over the finitely many reduced classes, and proves the fixed-pivot theorem. It does **not** require \(\ell\mid N-2\).

In a related but different setting, [Ford, *Sieving very thin sets of primes, and Pratt trees with missing primes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3498) treats infinite sets closed under the factors of \(p-1\); here the center \(N\) moves, only three steps of \(N-p\) are tested, and the prime-pair two-cycles must remain as the explicit main term. This comparison is not a priority claim.
