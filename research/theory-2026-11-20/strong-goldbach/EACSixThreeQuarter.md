# A three-quarter count for six-label exceptional autonomous components

This concerns centers of actual Bożek EACs, **not** ordinary Goldbach exceptions. A center supporting an EAC may have a representation as the sum of two primes. All proof inputs are separately stated below; no primality search is part of the counting argument.

**Theorem.** The number of even \(N\le X\) which support a six-label EAC is at most \(X^{3/4+o(1)}\). In fact, if both \(A\) and \(I-A\) are singular, the product of the two least labels is \(O(N^{3/4})\). The previous all-six bound stated on main is \(X^{5/6+o(1)}\). The already stronger \(X^{1/2+o(1)}\) bounds for a subpower seed, invertible \(A\), or invertible \(I-A\) are unaffected. With the earlier ≤5 result, the new count also applies to centers supporting at least one EAC of at most six labels, or a nonempty closed all-composite component/orbit of at most six labels (take a terminal SCC in the orientation below).

## Setup and exact inputs

Write the six labels \(p=x_0<q=x_1<x_2<\cdots<x_5=M\), distinct odd primes coprime to \(N\). For every row \(i\), \(N-x_i\) is composite and has all factors among the labels. Orient arcs \(i\to j\) when \(x_j\mid N-x_i\); the component is strongly connected and there are no loops. Put \(A_{ij}=v_{x_j}(N-x_i)\), \(B=I-A\), and \(\nu=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\ker B\). Every label is a factor of some odd composite row, so \(M\le(N-3)/3\), and every complementary product lies in \((2N/3,N)\). Also \(q<\sqrt N\): the \(p\)-row is composite, uses no \(p\), and every factor is at least \(q\).

The inputs below are from this repository's strong-Goldbach directory:
 
1. **Nonsingular counts:** [EACJacobianSquareRoot](EACJacobianSquareRoot.md) (fixed \(k=6\), det \(A\ne0\)) and [EACNonsingularSquareRoot](EACNonsingularSquareRoot.md) (det \(B\ne0\)); each gives \(X^{1/2+o(1)}\) for actual centers.
2. **Supported minors:** [EACSupportedMinorFive](EACSupportedMinorFive.md), general fixed-size lemma: if a chosen set \(S\) of labels has product at most \(X^\alpha\) and deletion of those columns lowers the rational rank of \(B\), the number of centers is \(X^{\alpha+o(1)}\). In particular, any \(\nu+1\) indices suffice for the rank condition. The fixed constant in an \(O(X^\alpha)\) product can be absorbed by \(X^\epsilon\), then \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).
3. **Rank and small labels:** [PrimeOrbitMovingSeed](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) §4 gives \(\nu\le2\) from the upward-edge forest on six vertices, without smoothness; [EACTwoRetained](EACTwoRetained.md), final section (rank), says \(\nu=2\) forces at least three labels \(\le\sqrt N\).
4. **Arity and homogeneous cases:** [EACSixThreeFactorRank](EACSixThreeFactorRank.md), its **unconditional finite rank theorem only**, excludes \(\det A=0,\ \nu\ge2\) when every row has at most three factors, multiplicities counted. [EACSemiprimeQuarter](EACSemiprimeQuarter.md), its six-label corollary, bounds \(p\ll N^{1/4}\) when every row has exactly two factors.
5. **Corank-two tangent geometry:** [EACSixAllCenters](EACSixAllCenters.md) §2, in the range \(\nu=2,\ q>N^\eta\) for fixed \(\eta>0\). This uses the first (geometric) section of [PrimeOrbitSixTerminalFiveMacroscopic](PrimeOrbitSixTerminalFiveMacroscopic.md) but not the fixed-seed smoothness theorem; the high-parent argument is uniform for a varying \(p\). We spell out precisely how the tangent is used and review its assumptions below.

The preceding are results about actual prime components, except for the stated finite obstruction valid for all allowed six-vertex multiplicity patterns. We do not assume any conjecture or infer that a missing Goldbach representation would force a component of size at most six.

## 1. Corank two: actual minimum \(p<N^{1/4}\), hence a cheaper tangent count

The nonsingular counts dispose of \(\det A\ne0\) or \(\det B\ne0\). If \(\det A=0\) and \(\nu=2\), item 4 forces some row to have at least four prime factors; hence \(p^4\le N-x_i<N\). It follows already that \(p q<N^{3/4}\).

Fix e.g. \(\eta=1/16\). When \(q\le N^\eta\), the three least labels have product at most \(X^{1/2+2\eta}\), from item 3. Their columns meet item 2, giving \(X^{5/8+o(1)}\). When \(q>N^\eta\) use the tangent statement. Write the exact equations
\[
 x_i+\prod_{j=0}^5x_j^{A_{ij}}=N,\qquad i=0,\dots,5.
\]
For every sufficiently large actual tuple in this range, after freezing \(p\) the complex tangent dimension is at most one; if it has dimension one then some **other** label \(\le\sqrt N\) moves. Thus freezing two labels kills the tangent, one costing at most \(X^{1/4}\) possibilities and the other at most \(\sqrt X\).

Here is the audit of that imported tangent criterion. Let \(A'\) be the five nonseed columns, bounded by \(1/\eta\). Rank \(A\ge5\): two zero eigenvalues with two unit eigenvalues and Perron root \(\rho\) would force the remaining eigenvalue to be \(-\rho-2\) by trace zero, contrary to \(\rho\) being the spectral radius. If rank \([A'\mid\mathbf1]\le4\) on an unbounded sequence then rank \(A'=4\), \(\mathbf1\in\operatorname{col} A'\), and the \(p\)-column increases rank. Fix \(A'\) by subsequence. Choose one fixed integral left-null vector \(c\) of \(A'\) with \(D=c^\top A_p\ne0\) on the subsequence (one possible vector from a fixed basis). Then \(c^\top\mathbf1=0\), and the exact logarithmic equations give
\[
 D\log p=\sum_i c_i\log(1-x_i/N).
\]
The right side is bounded, so \(p\) can be fixed, and then the nonzero integer \(D\) can be fixed too. On a further weight-convergent subsequence, the cited geometric lemma supplies at most one weight-one label \(y\), whose sole parent \(x\) is nonhigh and has \(N-x=p^a y\), \(a\ge1\). This lemma uses exactly strong connectivity, corank two, the five lower weights \(\ge\eta\), and the factor identities: a high row output can have only the seed as cofactor, two parents are impossible because the product ratio is in \((2/3,3/2)\), and two highs would give a chain in the three-vertex sign group of an eigenvector with zero seed coordinate followed by a return to its first vertex. The resulting nonseed \(x\) would divide \(p^{a+b}-p^a+1=N^{o(1)}\), impossible for \(x\ge N^\eta\). A closed cycle of highs is likewise impossible by composing the same affine equations to give a nonzero subpower multiple of \(N\). These arguments also cover a moving \(p\): any high forces its weight zero, and all cofactor powers are subpower. No smoothness argument is needed. If all fractions \(x_i/N\) tend to zero the displayed log identity contradicts \(D\ne0\). Otherwise \(y/N\) has a nonzero limit after passing to a subsequence, \(a\) is bounded and fixed, and the same identity yields \(p^D=(1-p^{-a})^{c_y}\). A prime factor of \(p^a-1>1\) forces \(c_y=0\), hence \(D=0\), contradiction. Therefore \([A'\mid\mathbf1]\) has rank at least five.

For tangent directions use \(h_i=\delta x_i/x_i\), \(n=\delta N/N\), \(h_0=0\), \(C_i=x_i/(N-x_i)\), so \((A'h)_i-n=C_i(n-h_i)\). If tangent dimension were at least two along a contrary sequence, fix \(A'\), take limiting weights and an orthonormal pair of tangent directions. The right sides tend to zero at all nonhigh indices. If there is a high \(y\), its parent's only nonseed factor is \(y\) to exponent one; at that parent the differential equation forces \(h_y-n\to0\), so the high right side also tends to zero. The limiting orthonormal vectors thus lie in a kernel of dimension at most one, contradiction.

For the small-moving-label claim, suppose every \(x_i\le\sqrt N\) has derivative zero. A row containing an output \(y>\sqrt N\) has the form \(N-x_i=u y\) with \(u\ge3\) a product of small labels and fixed to first order; there is at most one such output. If \(n=0\), the differentiated equality gives \(|\delta y|\le |\delta x_i|/3\); every large label has a parent and the maximum derivative must be zero. If \(n\ne0\), set \(z_i=\delta x_i-x_i n\). Follow the unique large successor starting at any small label (start \(|z_i|=x_i|n|>0\)). Every step obeys \(z_y=-z_i/u\). A repeated vertex would contradict strict nonzero contraction. At an ending row with no large successor the product is fixed to first order, so \(|z_i|=(N-x_i)|n|>\sqrt N|n|\), also impossible. Thus a nonzero direction moves a small label distinct from the already frozen seed.

After freezing it, five variables remain including \(N\); some five of the six equations have full-rank Jacobian on those variables, so the tuple is an isolated nonsingular zero of that square subsystem. Choose the frozen second coordinate and the five equations in finitely many ways. The affine Bézout bound on isolated nonsingular zeros of the chosen subsystem is at most \((1+\log X/\log3)^5\), even if other components have positive dimension (alternatively perturb generically, preserving any finite set of nonsingular zeros). Each exponent is at most \(\log X/\log3\), so all ordered matrices together contribute only a fixed polylogarithmic factor. The parameter cost is therefore \(X^{1/4}\sqrt X\) times a fixed polylogarithm, not the \(X^{1/3}\sqrt X\) cost used for the earlier general bound. This proves the desired count in this rank.

## 2. Corank one: reducing \(pq>N^{3/4}\) to a unique least-label cube

Suppose \(\det A=\det B=0\) and \(\nu=1\). The all-semiprime case already has \(pq\ll N^{3/4}\) by item 4. For any other case with \(pq>N^{3/4}\), the inequality \(q<\sqrt N\) forces \(p>N^{1/4}\). Thus every row has two or three factors, and a triple involving any non-\(p\) factor has product at least \(p^2q>N\), impossible. Hence the only triple factorization is \(p^3\), occurring at exactly one row (distinct inputs imply distinct complementary products). Write \(r=x_j\) for that row's input. It differs from \(p\), and \(N=p^3+r\). In particular \(p<N^{1/3}\) and \(q>N^{5/12}\).

The following elementary geometry sharply restricts the five remaining semiprime rows.

* **Universal pair tests.** Sort each pair of factor indices increasingly. For input indices \(i<j\), their semiprime pair tuples cannot be componentwise nondecreasing (including equality): their actual products are strictly *decreasing* in the input. Every target index has at most one smaller parent, since a smaller input dividing the residue equation is the unique least residue \(N\bmod x_j\). Self-factors are prohibited. The largest label \(M\) occurs in at most one row, since any two different odd input primes differ by an even multiple of \(M\) if \(M\) divides both complements. It cannot occur as \(M^2\): every other semiprime row would have product \(\le M(M-2)\), yet the difference of row inputs has magnitude \(<M\). Strong connectivity is an explicit further test.
* **If any semiprime row contains \(p\):** it is \(pg\) with \(g\ne p\) since \(p^2<2N/3\) (use \(N>p^3\)). Each such output \(g>2N/(3p)>(2/3)N^{2/3}\). For all sufficiently large \(N\), \(gq>N\) since \(q>N^{5/12}\); thus any row using \(g\) must be a \(p\)-pair. Every other non-\(p\) output occurs in a pair omitting \(p\) and is \(<N/q<N^{7/12}\), so the \(g\)'s are exactly a contiguous tail of largest labels. Their diameter is \(<M/p<2p\): \(Mp<N=p^3+r\le p^3+M\), so \(M/p<p^2/(p-1)<2p\). All inputs of \(p\)-pairs and of the cube are congruent to \(r\) mod \(p\). As the labels are odd, at most one tail label can be input of any such row (two would differ by at least \(2p\)).
* **If no semiprime row contains \(p\):** the cofactor graph on the other five labels, with a pair an undirected edge and squares represented by loops, has a star as the component containing \(M\). Indeed every label in that graph appears as a factor by strong connectivity. Let \(s\) be the unique mate of \(M\). Any other edge at \(s\) has mate \(z>(2/3)M\) by comparing its product with \(sM\) in \((2N/3,N)\). Such a \(z\) occurs in only one row because a shared factor \(>M/2\) would divide the nonzero even input difference of magnitude \(<M\). In particular there is no loop at \(s\) (it would make \(s\) a mate \(>M/2\) with two occurrences). All its other neighbors are leaves, proving the star restriction.

## 3. Exact small chart table and two genuine algebraic contradictions

Index the labels by 0,…,5. For each cube row \(j=1,\dots,5\), choose a weakly increasing pair from the other five labels for each remaining row. The universal tests above apply just to the semiprime rows; the cube only has output 0, introducing no upward parent. Test accessibility from every vertex, then exact \(\det A=0\) and exact \(\det(I-A)=0\), then the two geometry lanes from §2. Here is the entire table:

| 2 | 3,955 | 1,590 | 753 | 12 | 1 |

The standalone replay [check_eac_six_three_quarter.js](check_eac_six_three_quarter.js) independently traverses the finite choices in increasing and decreasing input order. It uses exact integer determinant expansion by subset recurrence: append a new selected column \(j\) to partial column set \(S\), with sign \((-1)^{|\{s\in S:s>j\}|}\). The tiny signed sums fit exact JS integers. No polynomial solving, sampling, prime selection, fixed alphabet, or finite search over \(N\) is used. The table and two charts agree in both orders; run `node check_eac_six_three_quarter.js`. The full candidates, in row order 0,…,5, are:

1. \(33,\ 000,\ 15,\ 14,\ 23,\ 22\). Write labels \(p<b<c<d<e<f\). The rows \(c,d\) give \(d-c=b(f-e)\), while rows \(e,f\) give \(f-e=c(d-c)\). Hence \(d-c=bc(d-c)\), impossible.
2. \(33,\ 25,\ 000,\ 24,\ 13,\ 11\). Rows \(b,d\) give \(d-b=c(f-e)\), while rows \(e,f\) give \(f-e=b(d-b)\). Hence \(d-b=bc(d-b)\), also impossible.

Thus no actual mixed-row, doubly singular six-label EAC can have \(pq>N^{3/4}\) at large \(N\). Together with the homogeneous two-factor class and rank-two case, this proves the stated \(O(N^{3/4})\) pointwise product restriction for all doubly singular components. For corank one, use the two least labels in item 2 to obtain the \(X^{3/4+o(1)}\) count. The nonsingular and corank-two branches have already been covered. The only new finite component of the proof is the small one-cube chart table; the contradictions and reduction are exact paper identities.

## Comparison and limits

The exact bounded search located [Bożek's original factorization-graph paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900) and [Kelanemer's revised SSRN manuscript](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460), which advertises a related terminal-matrix framework under a Goldbach-counterexample hypothesis. The closest directly verified prior result is the repository's [EACSixAllCenters](EACSixAllCenters.md), fixed blob 4b3a52c5da3a2d77efd8220b70be71b93ff186a9, with general \(X^{5/6+o(1)}\). The query did not locate the same all-center six-label \(3/4\) bound; no exhaustive priority claim and no reliance on Kelanemer's claimed Goldbach proof. The hypotheses do not constrain terminal components of larger cardinality forced by a hypothetical Goldbach failure. In particular neither the absence of six-label EACs nor a counterexample to Goldbach is asserted.
