# Positive signed credit from the genuine negative one-tag arm against all three tags

Keep the fixed even \(N\), the central interval \(J\), \(L\),
\(M=|J|N\mathfrak S(N)\), the original sextic \(H\) and the
same actual compensated \(S=G_b+W,\ b=.003\), of the
[three-by-three comparison](ThreeByThreeTagCredit.md).
Keep \(x+y=N,\ x\in NJ,\ (x,N)=1\) on every physical row.

Let \(\mathcal A\) require both coordinates to be squarefree,
with exactly one small prime on the left and three on the
right. Here small means actual angle in \((.003,.080]\),
and every other factor on either arm must actually exceed
\(N^{.080}\). Impose the additional **literal** condition
\(H(x)<0\) on the left. There is no rank restriction on
either arm, and no restriction on the sign of \(H(y)\).
Include also its reflection \(\mathcal A^{\rm op}\).

**Proposition.** At each sufficiently large even target the
original paired contribution satisfies
\[
          \sum_{\mathcal A\cup\mathcal A^{\rm op}}
                       S(x)S(y)>.014 M+o(M).
\]
It is disjoint from all previous priced regions. In particular the
[true semiprime one-by-three debit](SmallSemiprimeThreeTagAdverse.md)
does not overlap: every such semiprime has positive \(H\).
This leaves the positive-score nonsemiprime one-tag rows against
three tags unpriced. Nothing is asserted about the sign of that
remaining contribution or the full exterior.

## 1. Actual support and the conditional inequalities

On each of these arms the .003 gate passes and \(Q=0\).
On the one-tag arm \(W=0\) because the second least prime
has angle \(>.080\), so the first two cannot have sum
\(\le.065\); on the three-tag arm \(W=0\) because the third
factor \(\le N^{.080}\) contradicts its two-tag quotient
floor \(>(.555-.065)/3>.163\). Thus \(S=H\), without any
change to the original sign condition. Central coordinates
having these named primes are composite. The exact .080
counts \(1,3\) exclude the earlier one-by-two, two-by-three,
three-by-three, zero-tag, and at-least-four-tag chambers.
The .1251 rough and mixed Q-boundary supports and the
older two-tag ambient are also disjoint, as one arm has
just one .080 tag and the other has three with third
\(\le N^{.080}\). In the older two-tag ambient the first
two actual tags can in principle both occur on the one-tag
arm at its larger cutoff, but would leave the other arm
incompatible with the required .110 quotient floor.
The proved positive sign of \(H\) on all rank-two rows
makes the prescribed \(H(x)<0\) strictly disjoint from
the earlier actual one-by-three semiprime debit.

Write \(X=[-H]_+\) on the literal one-tag support, with
\(X_\ell\) its rank-at-most-seven part and \(X_h=X-X_\ell\).
Write \(Y_\pm=[\pm H]_+\) on the whole exact three-tag
support, zero elsewhere. No rank-one prime atom is
introduced; only \(X\) has an imposed sign.
On the right use .004 bins and a final shortened bin,
with the same ordered-volume factors \(v_i\), and exact variable upper
and lower coefficients \(U_i,k_i\) from the
[three-tag transfer](ThreeByThreeTagCredit.md#1-physical-support-and-the-genuine-paired-measure).
For a right cell use the physical weight
\[
 C_i(y)= L u_1u_2u_3\,1_{\text{cell }i}(y)
\]
with all its exact quotient roughness and squarefree gates.
The label weights here and in the receivers are target-log
angles. In actual scores the own-log formulas can be used
for their limiting angular integrands, with uniform \(o(1)\)
normalized errors after the fixed floor and at most 15
factors. Their positive weighted counts are bounded by
the same true receivers. No cutoff is moved in this step.

Put \(B_-=Y_-/C_i\) inside the right cell. For any
\(d_i>0\) define the true nonnegative deficit
\(D_i=C_i(d_i-B_-)_+\). Pointwise \(Y_-\) on this
cell is at least \(d_i C_i-D_i\). For a finite-floor
ordinary one-arm score \(Z\), write \(m(Z)\) for its
normalization as in the prior comparisons.
The genuine linear **lower** sieve in the rightward
receiver gives
\(\langle X_\ell,C_i\rangle/M\ge k_i v_i m(X_\ell)+o(1)\).
If \(U_X\) is a valid receiver upper for \(X_\ell\), then
the actual reverse upper is
\(\langle X_\ell,D_i\rangle/M\le U_X m(D_i)+o(1)\).
These use ordinary product BV for their **actual** positive
sources at the literal residue \(N\), as in the
three-by-three proof. The positive global \(\langle X_h,Y_-\rangle\)
can be dropped in a lower. Conversely the actual negative
term satisfies
\[
  \langle X,Y_+\rangle/M
           \le U_X m(Y_+)+U_{Y_+}m(X_h)+o(1),                    \tag{1}
\]
using the two genuine receiver orientations. Hence the
desired one-orientation lower is at least
\[
 \sum_i v_i\{d_i k_i m(X_\ell)- U_X D_i^*\}
             -U_X m(Y_+)-U_{Y_+}m(X_h)+o(1),                    \tag{2}
\]
where \(D_i^*\ge m(D_i)/v_i\) is an ordinary deficit upper.
The same bound with the physical reflected interval has
the same limiting constants; this is not an assumption
that \(J\) is symmetric.

## 2. Actual pointwise estimates and the ordinary inputs

Write \(t\) for the one actual small angle on the left,
\(T=1-t\), and normalize the score by \(Lt\).
We first bound \(X_\ell/(Lt)\) by \(15.4+o(1)\) throughout
its true support. Use the exact sextic slice identities of
[the original proof](Proof.md#2-the-whole-rough-negative-arm-comparison).
At rank three the first divided difference of \(F\) is
nonnegative and at most \(\sup(-F')<4.4\);
two negative terms suffice, and the complete two-factor
quotient sum lies beyond its support. For rank four, if
\(z\) is the largest of the quotient angles, then
\(q=\sum u_j^2\le .0064+z\) and the other two angles
give product at most \((1-z)^2/4\). Thus the negative
magnitude divided by \(Lt\) is at most
\[
           (1680(.0064+z)-528)_+ z(1-z)^2/4<11.8 .
\]
For rank five use the exact bound
\((10080\sqrt q-1056/\sqrt q)\prod u_j\); the product
after removing the first angle is at most \(z(1-z)^3/27\)
and \(q\le .0064+z\) again. The resulting positive upper
is \(<15.4\) on \([0,1]\). The two one-dimensional bounds
are verified outward in the checker. Rank six gives
\(40320(T/5)^5<13\).
At rank seven, differentiate the two largest interval
factors in \(b_7\) to get
\(|b_7''(0)|\le2b_5(0)/(u_6u_7)\), where \(b_5\)
uses the five remaining intervals. Put \(e=\sum_{j=2}^5u_j\).
By the same cube-slicing bound as in the original sextic
proof, \(b_5(0)\le 2\sqrt2/e\); use \(e\le 2T/3\)
and \(\prod_{j=2}^5u_j\le(e/4)^4\). The second term
uses \(b_7(0)\le\sqrt{12}/T\).
Together these give normalized upper
\[
 \frac{2112\sqrt2}{128}(2T/3)^3
            +\frac{80640\sqrt{12}}{6^6}T^5<13 .
\]
Rank two is positive and cannot contribute. Thus opening
the one actual tag and upper-sifting the quotient with
ratio 4 gives, in the literal below-root product-BV
range, the genuine receiver
\(U_X=15.4(.575/.080)(.080-.003)\).
The true modulus is at most \(N^{.080+.320}\), even
at the largest one-tag cell.

For the high odd ranks use the following sharper
absolute version of the standard slice estimate.
For a positive order \(j\) differentiate the \(j\)
largest interval factors, leaving a nonnegative
convolution density \(b_{\rm rem}\). The derivative is
\((\prod_{\rm diff}u_i)^{-1}\) times a signed sum
of \(2^j\) translated values of \(b_{\rm rem}\);
there are exactly \(2^{j-1}\) values of each sign,
all nonnegative before taking those signs. Therefore its
absolute value is at most
\(2^{j-1}\sup b_{\rm rem}/\prod_{\rm diff}u_i\).
The remaining convolution has supremum no greater
than the inverse largest undifferentiated length, by
convolution with probability densities.
At rank \(r\ge9\) both derivative orders \(r-5,r-7\)
are positive, so this **halves** both terms in the
usual absolute high odd envelope. The required remaining
convolutions are continuous, and the distributional
derivative identities thus evaluate their values at zero.
For the one-tag arm the high-rank normalized pointwise
cap at quotient rank \(k=r-1=8,10,12\) is consequently
\[
           1056\,2^{k-5}(T/k)^3
            +80640\,2^{k-7}(T/k)^5 .
\]
For the three-tag arm it is, at quotient rank
\(k=r-3=6,8,10,12\),
\[
           1056\,2^{k-3}(T/k)
            +80640\,2^{k-5}(T/k)^3 ,
\]
where now \(T\) is the total remaining quotient angle.
The smallest-product bounds use the genuine least tags
and arithmetic--geometric mean on the smallest remaining
quotient lengths. Right ranks four through seven are
negative or zero, and higher even ranks are zero,
as in the original three-by-three support.
Only these four high odd ranks can contribute to \(Y_+\).
Multiply the caps by the exact ordinary rank densities
\(b_{k,.080}(T)\) for \(m(X_h)\) and \(m(Y_+)\).
For \(U_{Y_+}\) use the maximum of the four available
caps at each right cell times its actual \(U_i v_i\).
This is just a real positive receiver, not a statement
about the sign of the high ranks.

We need only a lower for \(m(X_\ell)\).
In the ordinary one-integer moment of the one-tag
normalized score \(A\), use
\[
 m(A_- \text{ at rank}\le7)\ \ge\
 m(A_+\text{ at ranks }2,3,4)-m(A)-m(|A|\text{ at rank}\ge9).
\]
The three explicit positive terms are legitimate lower
moments: rank two gives \(g_1(0)/T\); rank three uses
\([g_1(0)-g_1(s)-g_1(T-s)]_+\) on the quotient two-factor
harmonic; rank four uses \([528-1680q]_+\)
integrated against unweighted quotient simplex volume
divided by \(3!\), since its quotient product cancels all
three reciprocal factors. Rank-five through seven are
nonpositive, but that is not needed for the inequality.
The signed \(m(A)\) uses the exact one-integer first
moment of the one-tag divided difference. All formulas
include zero-score ranks without displacing the actual
count of rough factors.

## 3. Positive deficit bounds and exact replay

Use shifts \(d\in\{125,150,175,200,225\}\).
Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_k\) be the actual right quotient
angles with \(z_i\ge .080\) and sum \(T\), where
\(.760\le T\le .991\) in the limiting target-angle model.
At rank four the negative magnitude divided by the
tag product and \(L\) is at least
\((1680T^2-528)T>225\), so it has no deficit.
For ranks five, six and seven the following lower
amplitudes suffice, respectively:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 k=2:&(10080\max z_i-1056/\max z_i)\prod z_i,\\
 k=3:&40320\prod z_i,\\
 k=4:&80640\prod z_i/
             \sqrt{.080(1-T)+\sum z_i^2}.
 \end{array}
\]
In rank five, conditioning on the largest interval
gives \(\mathbb E|Y_5|\ge\max z_i/4\) by Jensen and
\(b_5(0)\le1/\max z_i\). In rank seven use
\(b_7''(0)\le0\) and \(b_7(0)\ge1/\sqrt q\);
the three actual tags have sum of squares
\(\le .080(1-T)\). These exact lower amplitudes apply
before taking a positive part of any deficit.
For larger \(k\) the deficit is at most \(d\), including
all zero-score and all possibly positive ranks.

For the ordinary deficit, integrate \((d-\text{lower
amplitude})_+\) on each of these three quotient simplices
with density \(\prod dz_i/z_i\) and the usual \(1/k!\).
The checker's sorted separators cover each simplex
with \((T-.080k)^{k-1}\) Jacobian and the same within-bin
factorials as the earlier four-class cube. This orders the
separator integration coordinates, not the quotient
prime labels. The tail uses \(d\sum_{k=5}^{12}b_{k,.080}(T)\).
Whole-interval product, square and clipped lower bounds
are used before the volume is assigned.
