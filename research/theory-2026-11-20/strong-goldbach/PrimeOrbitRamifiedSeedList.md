# Only finitely many prescribed seed primes can sustain five polynomial-size moving labels

Fix δ∈(0,1/2], and put M=⌊1/δ⌋. There is an effectively enumerable finite list of possibly admissible odd seed primes depending on δ **alone**, not on a smoothness alphabet. For any fixed odd prime p outside this list and any fixed finite prime set F, no unbounded sequence of even N with (N,p)=1 and log(N/N_F)=o(log N) has a strongly connected set of six distinct odd unit primes below N−1, consisting of p and five labels x_j≥N^δ, all six complements composite and factored in that set. Here N_F is the full F-part. There is no restriction on the power class of the outside cofactor or the number of repeated p factors. In particular every potentially admissible seed in this statement satisfies the explicit bound

    log p ≤ 2 log 2 + 2(720 M⁴+1) log(2·24⁹ M³²).

The numerical bound is an easy upper bound for a usually much smaller chart-wise algebraic test, not a claim that the surviving candidates are realized. The asymptotic threshold in N is not made effective. Equivalently, as the **fixed** seed p grows, the least positive limiting nonseed exponent in any such macroscopic configuration on an unbounded sequence is O((log log p/log p)^(1/4)); a subpower nonseed is not excluded. By [the six-label fork](PrimeOrbitSixLabelFork.md), a nonterminal six-label forward orbit must already contain two subpower nonseed labels on such a sequence. Thus the five-macroscopic exclusion also applies if one starts with a size-six forward orbit from p instead of postulating strong connectivity. It does not handle the remaining three/four-macroscopic possibilities, bound the size of an arbitrary orbit, guarantee that an arbitrarily preselected p is coprime to every N, or prove binary Goldbach for these centers.

## The two equations at a new ramified prime

We use [the full-degree étale residue theorem](PrimeOrbitSlowCurveEtaleFiber.md) and its linked prerequisites. On a hypothetical infinite sequence fix the projected six-by-five exponent matrix A′, with entries ≤M, and the nonseed order. Its rank is five; the unique real weights solve A′w=1 with 0<w_j<1. Write B′=E−A′, E the last five columns of I₆. It has rank four; let C be a fixed integral two-by-six row basis for its left kernel. This is also the full left eigenspace of the actual factor matrix A at eigenvalue one. Let v∈Z⁵ be the primitive right kernel of B′, v₀=0; all six entries are distinct. Let l be a nonzero integral generator up to rational scaling of the one-dimensional left kernel of A′. It obeys l·1=0 and annihilates the full A, since its seed column a belongs to col A′. The affine residue algebra has rational coordinates X_i=λ_i Z^(v_i) modulo one irreducible monic g(Z) of degree d≥2, λ₀=p, λ_j=±p^(b_j) for integral b_j. For each of the two rows c of C,

    g(Z) | Σ_i c_i λ_i^(−1) Z^(−v_i)     (in Q[Z,Z⁻¹]).

The splitting fields of the g cannot belong to a fixed number field on an infinite subsequence. As their degrees are bounded by d! (d fixed), Hermite–Minkowski plus bounded local differents implies that their collective ramified primes are unbounded. Discard any prescribed finite collection of primes, including p and the fixed chart constants/minors. For infinitely many distinct remaining primes ℓ, choose a corresponding term whose splitting field ramifies at ℓ. As in the étale proof, g is ℓ-integral monic with unit constant (it divides a Laurent polynomial with unit outer coefficients). Its reduction must have a repeated nonzero root z over Fbar_ℓ, since squarefree reduction implies unramified splitting field. The local residue h_i=λ_i^(−1) z^(−v_i) then satisfies

    C h=0,                 C diag(v₀,…,v₅) h=0,

by reduction and the first Euler derivative. All h_i are nonzero and h₀=p^−1 in the residue field. Two more *exponent-independent* relations hold there. Indeed on the characteristic-zero fiber T=0 the six row identities are −X_i=p^(a_i)∏X_j^(A′_ij). Taking their l-product gives ∏h_i^(l_i)=1 since l·a=l·1=0. The input-p row gives ∏_{j>0}h_j^(A′_0j)=−1/p. These identities remain true mod g and at z mod ℓ: insert the signed p-power expressions for λ_i, clear Laurent monomials/scalars, and use monic integral division before reducing. Put r_j=h_j/h₀ and k=Σ_j A′_0j; k≥2 because N−p is composite, cannot contain p, and all its prime factors are in the set. We obtain

    ∏_{i=0}^5 r_i^(l_i)=1,           ∏_{j>0} r_j^(A′_0j)=−p^(k−1),     r₀=1.        (1)

Neither relation involves the unbounded seed-exponent column of the full diagram.

## The projective line and its nonconstant product

Let j denote the index of the largest actual prime (a nonseed eventually), and i its unique parent: every smaller prime parent must equal N mod x_j in [1,x_j−1], and there is a parent by strong connectivity. Thus A′'s j-column has its only nonzero entry a*=A_ij at i; i may be the seed. Consequently l_i=0 and c_j=a*c_i for every c in the row span C.

These two coordinates do not vanish on the whole two-dimensional span. Otherwise deleting i,j leaves a nonnegative zero-diagonal four-by-four principal submatrix with two independent left eigenvectors at 1, every nonzero element of that space of mixed sign (extend by zero and use orthogonality to the full strictly positive right Perron vector of eigenvalue ≥2). A spectral radius ρ>1 would require eigenvalues ρ,1,1,−ρ−2; for ρ=1 a nonnegative Perron vector is independent of the mixed space and forces 1,1,1,−3. Both are impossible, as is ρ<1. Choose a nonzero rational left vector c with those two coordinates zero and another c′ with them both nonzero. On the four indices S₀ other than i,j, every c_t is nonzero: each of its signs needs at least two indices. Indeed columnwise cA=c gives every positive index a positive predecessor, forcing a directed cycle on positive indices; similarly for negative indices, and no self-loops occur.

The solutions of Ch=CVh=0, V=diag(v), form a two-dimensional linear plane, hence one projective line. To verify both its dimension and the needed coordinate structure, project onto the four indices S₀. There the constraints for c are Σ c_t h_t=Σ c_t v_t h_t=0, giving exactly two degrees of freedom. The constraints for c′ determine h_i,h_j uniquely from these other entries: the two-by-two determinant is c′_i c′_j(v_j−v_i)≠0. More explicitly with s,t homogeneous parameters, the four coordinates at u∈S₀ can be written

    h_u(s,t)=(s+t v_u)/[c_u ∏_{k∈S₀, k≠u}(v_u−v_k)].

This follows by the leading coefficient in interpolation of polynomials of degree ≤2 on the four distinct v-values. Thus these four coordinates are pairwise nonproportional nonzero linear forms, in *any* linear parametrization of the plane. The full stacked four-by-six matrix has rank four, a fact also available by the projection argument. One may instead choose integer linear forms H_u(s,t) via Gaussian elimination on the full stacked matrix. If any is identically zero, no residue point with all entries nonzero exists at any sufficiently large good prime and this chart is impossible.

The rational function ∏ H_u^(l_u) on this projective line (total exponent zero) is nonconstant. For a constant, the sum of l-exponents on every proportionality class of nonzero linear forms must vanish, by taking its order at the zero of each class. The four forms on S₀ are pairwise nonproportional; l_i=0 so only H_j can cancel any one of those four. It would follow that at most two l_u are nonzero. But the six rows of A′ have weight one under w and have distinct values under v, namely v₀,…,v₅ (B′v=0). No two are proportional and no row is zero. Their nonzero dependence l must involve at least three rows. This contradiction proves nonconstancy, including when the seed 0 is one of S₀ or the omitted pair.

## Exact test, rational lift and a uniform bound

Define the nonzero homogeneous polynomial of equal product degrees

    F(s,t)=∏ H_i^(l_i^+)−∏ H_i^(l_i^-).

On the complement of the zeros of all six H_i its vanishing is precisely the first equation of (1). Since the four-row integer system defining the line has rank four, its chosen basis remains a parametrization mod ℓ outside fixed minors. The necessary two equalities (1) have a simultaneous solution with all H_i≠0 over Qbar: otherwise on each of the two standard affine charts adjoin a variable enforcing invertibility of ∏H_i and apply the weak Nullstellensatz to F and the cleared second equation. Clear all denominators in the two certificates to exclude compatible points at all sufficiently large primes, contradicting the infinitely many distinct ramified ℓ above. This detail prevents extraneous common zeros at poles in a naïve resultant.

Thus for each projective algebraic root θ of F with all H_i(θ)≠0, evaluate the algebraic number ∏_{j>0}(H_j/H₀)(θ)^(A′_0j). Only if it is a negative rational integer of the form −p^(k−1) is that prime a candidate. Algebraic root isolation, equality tests and integer power/primality tests constitute a finite exact procedure. Conversely no realization is asserted by membership. For fixed M only finitely many integer A′ with entries ≤M and index choices exist. To list a safe superset, enumerate them, keeping only those with the stated ranks, consistent positive weights, distinct primitive indices and nonzero normalized rows, a single positive entry in the maximum's column, and the line/proportionality properties just proved. All are rational checks and are necessary for an actual countersequence; alternatively one can skip any chart that fails these tests. The surviving polynomial enumerations do not require knowing p or F in advance.

For explicit size bounds, by four-by-four minors of B′ its primitive v can be taken with |v_j|≤24 M⁴, and a basis C with entries of magnitude ≤24 M⁴: use four independent rows and columns for Cramer, choosing the two remaining coordinates successively as the free ones. Similarly l can be obtained from the five-by-five minors using the column 1 and any four columns of A′: these span col A′ because w has all entries nonzero. Expanding the determinants gives |l_i|≤120 M⁴. The integer line forms via four-by-four minors of [C;CV] have coefficients bounded by H=24(24M⁴)^8=24⁹ M³². The homogeneous binary polynomial F has degree K=Σl_i^+≤720 M⁴ and coefficient 1-norm at most 2(2H)^K. The height of each projective algebraic root θ is at most log 2+K log(2H) by Mahler measure (a root at infinity has height zero). Each nonzero ratio H_j(θ)/H₀(θ) has absolute logarithmic height at most h(θ)+log(2H) by the height inequality for two integral linear forms. From (1) and k/(k−1)≤2 this yields the bound displayed at the start. For the exponent reformulation take subsequences realizing the limiting minimum, and approach it from smaller positive δ; the bound log p≪ δ^−4 log(2/δ) gives the stated inverse estimate, with absolute implied constant for large p. No uniform lower bound on the minimum moving exponent is used or implied.
