# Exact exclusion of the four-macroscopic six-label branch

Fix a finite prime alphabet F and an odd seed p. Consider any unbounded sequence of even F-smooth centers N coprime to p in which the all-composite forward factor orbit from p has six labels. Pass to any subsequence with all six weights lim(log x/log N) convergent. **Four positive weights are impossible.** Consequently the six-label orbit must have three subpower labels (p and two moving primes), three weight-one labels, and the [common-multiplier balanced parametrization](PrimeOrbitBalancedReturn.md). This is not an exclusion of that remaining family, of six-label orbits in general, or of Goldbach exceptions.

The finite claims below have a standard-library replay. Place [check_four_macro_closed.py](check_four_macro_closed.py) and [four_macro_boundary_ledger.json](four_macro_boundary_ledger.json) together and run `python -O check_four_macro_closed.py`. It regenerates the initial support set in two different ways, uses explicit failures rather than optimization-removable assertions, evaluates all rational ranks and parameter intervals exactly, and compares the terminal chart list with the frozen ledger. No LP, SAT or floating-point optimization is involved.

## Reduction and conventions

By the [half-weight reduction](PrimeOrbitSixHalfWeights.md), a four-macro sequence would have sorted labels (p,q,X,Y,Z,H), with limiting weights

    w=(0,0,1/2,1/2,γ,1),             1/2≤γ<1.

Here q→∞ by the [multiple-seed bound](PrimeOrbitMultiSeedMinimal.md), so the physical ordering indicated really holds eventually; sort the three equal-weight intermediates by actual magnitude also when γ=1/2. By the [smooth six-label reduction](PrimeOrbitSixSmoothTerminal.md), the full exponent matrix A is irreducible, with ν(I−A)=2 and rank(I−A)=4. For each input vertex i a *row chart* records only the prime factors among X,Y,Z,H of N−x_i, with repeated letters for multiplicities; the small factors p,q are omitted. All these multiplicities are bounded and may be fixed on a subsequence. The six ordered rows are never quotiented by permuting the labels.

The p row has a macro factor: if absent N−p=q^a; the normalized real logarithm bound on a log q−log N with a≤L/log q, L=log N, gives log absolute value ≥−O(log(2q)log(2L/log q))=−o(L), contrary to log(1−p/N)=O(1/N). See [the normalized logarithm argument](PrimeOrbitSixTerminalExtremeLabels.md) for expanding the F-smooth base N. In the q row the total macro weight is exactly one: q cannot occur in its own complement, and the p-power there is subpower by [the small-row Subspace Theorem argument](PrimeOrbitSixTripleMacro.md). In every row the total macro weight is at most one. Thus an interior row is empty, a singleton X,Y,Z,H, or a pair (allowing repetition) drawn from X,Y; on the boundary pairs may be drawn from X,Y,Z. For generating *interior* row lists and capacities it is enough to use the test weight γ=3/4 since these lists do not change throughout the open interval. No individual feasibility decision for γ is made using only this test value.

No row contains its own input. Every macro occurs somewhere by strong connectivity, and each target t has at most one smaller predecessor: it otherwise divides the positive difference of two input primes below it. At the boundary we also use, for any distinct i,j and chart multiplicities m_{it},

    Σ_t min(m_{it},m_{jt}) w_t ≤ max(w_i,w_j),

since their common macro prime powers divide |x_i−x_j|. This inequality is not imposed in the interior enumeration. These rules give exactly 3,065 interior and 12,500 boundary ordered supports. The verifier compares a row-first Cartesian generation with a column-first recursion preselecting a unique smaller predecessor or none and choosing exponents independently at larger predecessors, subject to remaining row capacities.

## First-return tests, including branch weights

For any set R={p,q,V} with outside O the other three macros, check if the outside support digraph is acyclic. In the *boundary* case use each V∈{X,Y,Z,H} but skip this particular return test when its complementary graph has a cycle (never reject merely due to this cycle). In the interior use V=X,Y. For either of these two choices all outside cycles are impossible, even though the outside half U different from V may occur with exponent 2. A cycle avoiding U would be linear, with all target cofactors multiplying to at most N^{1−γ+o(1)}, impossible by the usual affine cycle divisibility at N. On a cycle containing U, start at U and continue until the last node before returning to U. Every traversed target except this last return is H or Z of exponent one; writing its last value as (aN±U)/v gives 0≤a<v by the [affine path recurrence](PrimeOrbitSixFourMacro.md). The product v of the preceding cofactors satisfies v≤N^{1−γ+o(1)}<U. Since the final complement is divisible by U (whether once or twice), reduction mod U gives U|(v−a)N, contradicting (U,N)=1 and 0<v−a<U. Thus an interior outside cycle excludes that chart.

For every applicable acyclic O let K=A_RR+A_RO(I−A_OO)^(-1)A_OR be the first-return matrix, irreducible with every row sum ≥2. The first-return nullity is two, so K−I=abᵀ with strictly positive integer vectors a,b and gcd(a_i)=1, as in the [rank-one return lemma](PrimeOrbitBalancedReturn.md). Although the full small-prime exponents are not enumerated, the column (K−I)_{*,V} is computable entirely from macro arcs. For instance, start with one visit at each specified row s∈R, add one to the count of V for every arc ending at V, and expand arcs into O until they first return to R, respecting all multiplicities. Subtract 1 at s=V. All three resulting column entries must be strictly positive. Divide by their gcd to obtain precisely the primitive a. If a retained s has no outside successor, its return monomial (N−s)/s is not integral, impossible since K−I is nonnegative; this also holds if the row contains retained macro V only.

Let n_{si} be the multiplicity of visits to i in this expansion before first return, including its initial visit at s. The exact monomial identity, with W=∏_{j∈R} x_j^{b_j}, is

    W^{a_s}=∏_i ((N−x_i)/x_i)^{n_si}.

This follows by multiplying row monomials over the DAG with their visit counts: all interior incoming factors cancel the outgoing input denominators, leaving precisely the first-return monomial divided by s. Since each factor is −1 mod N and all labels are units, W^{a_s}≡(−1)^{Σn_si} mod N. Primitivity implies W equals a common choice of sign modulo N; in particular W≥N−1 since W>1. At the boundary reject any chart whose three sign equations cannot use the same sign. In both branches take logarithms divided by log N. Set β_s=Σ_i n_si(1−w_i), using N−x_i between 2N/3 and N. Thus β_s/a_s has the *same value* for all three s, and this value must be ≥1. Each return test can in principle have its own value and common sign. At interior γ all requirements from both choices of V must hold at the *same γ*: each equality and inequality is affine in γ after multiplication by positive integer denominators. The program evaluates every interior root of these affine functions and one rational midpoint in every complementary interval, without omitting isolated boundary-of-cell possibilities.

Of 3,065 interior charts, 1,355 have at least one prohibited outside cycle. Of the remainder 144 pass the return-column and no-empty-path tests, but none satisfies the exact simultaneous return-mass constraints. Of 12,500 boundary charts, 505 pass the applicable structural/column/sign tests and 230 pass the return-mass tests; boundary charts with a cyclic outside choice are retained if all other applicable tests pass. This completes the whole open interval, but not yet the rational boundary.

## The two missing small columns and the odd-cofactor obstruction

Let A_M be the six-by-four matrix of visible exponents and B_M the four corresponding columns of I−A. The classes of the p and q unit coordinates in Q^6/row(I−A) are independent by [the moving-seed theorem](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md), since q is the least nonseed. Hence B_M has rank four: otherwise there would be a nonzero row combination of I−A supported on p,q (its full row rank is four). Consequently the columns of I−A, including the small columns not yet known, all belong to col(B_M). The vector with entries log((N−x_i)/x_i) is in this same real column space, by the actual factorizations. Divide by log N and take limits: (1−w_i)_i lies in col(B_M), a fixed space on each chart. These two rank tests leave exactly 33 boundary charts.

In every remaining chart, the macro weight deficit 1−Σ_t m_{it}w_t is either zero or 1/2. Denote by d the zero-one indicator of the latter rows. On 22 charts d=0. On one chart only the p row has d_i=1; on ten the p row and exactly one other row L have d_i=1. For each of those ten, the verifier checks e_L∉col(B_M). Therefore throughout the 33 charts **the entire p-column has zero limiting mass**: its material prime-power logarithm divided by log N can be positive only on a deficit row, not at p itself; but any limit of this normalized column is in col(B_M) (the identity-column difference is negligible). No multiples of e_L other than zero belong there. This reasoning can be applied on every convergent mass subsequence.

Let m_i be the true small-prime cofactor of row i after removing its recorded macros. If d≠0, put z=m_p=q^A, since p∤N−p; put z=1 if d=0. Set ξ_i=m_i/z^{d_i}. These are odd positive rationals with absolute logarithmic heights o(log N), not just absolute real sizes N^{o(1)}: at a zero-deficit row the cofactor is subpower. At the one possible additional deficit row, the p-power is subpower as just shown and its q-exponent times log q is (1/2+o(1))log N, just as at p. Thus subtracting A gives a signed q-exponent of absolute logarithmic weight o(log N).

All 33 charts have an integral fixed vector c annihilating A_M and d on the left, with c_H≠0. The verifier checks exactly the equivalent condition e_H∉col([A_M,d]). The identities for rows now give

    ∏_i(N−x_i)^{c_i}=∏_i ξ_i^{c_i},           Σ_i c_i=0.

For the second equality take logs divided by log N in the first, since the right side is subpower and c is fixed. There is exactly one high, so its parent s is nonhigh of size at most N^{1/2+o(1)} on this boundary. Write N−s=uH; u≥3 is odd and subpower. All other x_i/N are O(N^(−1/2+o(1))) and

    (N−H)/N = 1−1/u + s/(uN).

After cancelling N^{Σc_i}, it follows that the difference between ∏ ξ_i^{c_i} and ((u−1)/u)^{c_H} is O(N^(−1/2+o(1))); this holds for negative c_i since all nonzero real factors are bounded away from zero. Both expressions are rational of height o(log N), so any *nonzero* difference is at least N^(−o(1)) by integer numerator after clearing denominators. They must be equal. But the left side has zero 2-adic valuation, while the right's is c_H v₂(u−1)≠0. This contradiction completes the boundary and the four-macro exclusion.

## Scope

At a six-label single-seed orbit on fixed-smooth centers, there are at least three positive weights on every limiting subsequence by [macroscopic rank](PrimeOrbitMacroscopicRank.md), and not all five nonseed weights are positive by the [interior Eisenstein theorem](PrimeOrbitInteriorEisenstein.md). Four have now been excluded and the [three-macro result](PrimeOrbitSixTripleMacro.md) forces the remaining three all to have weight one. The integer-factor and Schur-complement conclusions in the balanced proof apply eventually on every such subsequence; by compactness, all sufficiently large six-label orbits in the stated scope have the exact balanced parametrization. Whether it can actually furnish an unbounded orbit in this setting is unresolved here. No claim about binary Goldbach or prime sums follows from the finite replay alone.

It independently matches the entire 33-chart ledger and both limiting-case exclusions. [Kelanemer, August 12, revised September 6, 2026](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460) treats neighboring terminal exponent matrices; both landing and delivery access to the full text failed, so no priority or comprehensive nonoverlap claim is made. The exact balanced residual retains no proof of a Goldbach representation.
