# Cancellation of a bounded union of actual cofactor populations in the high rough Möbius residual

Let $P$ be a prescribed large even integer, $L=\log P$, $H=\log L$, $I=[P/3,2P/3]$, and $X_P=\mathfrak S(P)|I|$. Write $q=P-p$ on actual source primes $p\in I$. Use $z=\exp(L^{.05})$ and $y=P^{.3}$. Put
$$
 B(b)=\begin{cases}\log b,&P^-(b)>z,\\ \log r,&r\text{ is the unique distinct prime }\le z\text{ dividing }b,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
$$
Use $P^-(1)=\infty$, $B(1)=0$, $\mu_z(t)=\mu(t)1_{P^-(t)>z}$. There is **no** coprimality requirement on $t,b$ in the residual
$$
 R_{\mathcal B}=\sum_{p\in I}(\log p)
       \sum_{tb=P-p,\ t>y}\mu_z(t) B(b)1_{b\in\mathcal B}.
$$
This is a signed subpopulation with a genuinely bounded $0/1$ selector on the cofactor $b$; the selector is not a count of tag factorizations. A prime $q$ has zero high weight even if $\mathcal B$ is unrestricted.

**Tag population.** Choose a sufficiently large fixed $K$ as below and $J\asymp_K H$ *disjoint* families from the grid of [Jiang–Lau–Lü, §§2,4, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2). Within family $j$ there are $K$ banks $\mathcal P_{ji}$: each is the full set of target-unit primes $\ell\equiv1\bmod4$ in an interval $[\exp(L^{\theta}),\exp(2L^{\theta})]$, with $.1<\theta<.2$, and the within-family separations of that comparison. Each reciprocal mass is in $[1/4,1/2]$. A cofactor is in $\mathcal B$ if at least one entire family is hit, meaning at least one prime from each of its banks divides $b$.

**Results and dependencies.** Unconditionally the full prime-source identity recalibrates at the moving $z$:
$$
 \sum_{p\in I}(\log p)\Lambda(P-p)=X_P+
 R_{\mathcal B}+R_{\mathcal B^c}+O(P/L^2).                         \tag{1}
$$
Furthermore,
$$ R_{\mathcal B}=O(P/L^2)                                           \tag{2} $$
under the **same external Dirichlet zero-free input**, or the same local alternative with sufficiently large fixed constants, as in [the full-target near-full-cofactor comparison, §1](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/b262263b). Precisely, for fixed sufficiently large $A_0,A_1$, one may use the local hypothesis that for relevant primitive characters with conductor $f\mid4Pd$, $d\le L^{A_0}$, $f>L^{B_0}$ (a fixed $B_0>0$), there is no zero with $\Re\rho>1-H^2/L^{.1}$ and $|\Im\rho|\le L^{A_1}$. Alternatively the asserted **all-Dirichlet** fixed zero-free half-plane $\Re s>7/8$ in [OpenAI, September 30, Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf) suffices at every sufficiently large $P$. Neither that external proof nor the JLL theorem is independently proved here. Ordinary zeta RH alone is not the hypothesis. No fixed-target prime lower bound follows: the signed, genuinely tag-poor $R_{\mathcal B^c}$ is not estimated.

## 1. Moving-cutoff calibration without a variation loss

Pooling all squarefree $z$-smooth divisors gives $\Lambda(q)=\sum_{tb=q}\mu_z(t)B(b)$ exactly. For $t$ $z$-rough, the zero- and singleton-label weights of $b$ use the same small labels as $q$. Restrict $t\le P^{.3}$; discard prime rows $p|P$ at a total divisor-bounded cost $P^{o(1)}$. Conditional on $t|q$, the small prime densities are $1/(r-1)$ at $r\le z$, $r\nmid P$, disjoint from $t$. A singleton label $r$ is marked as a divisor, omitted from the other sieving primes, and its model multiplier relative to the rough product is $1/(r-2)$, including all repeated valuations. Put
$$ V=\prod_{r\le z,r\nmid P}(1-1/(r-1)),\qquad U=\sum_{r\le z,r\nmid P}\log r/(r-2). $$
The upper/lower dimension-one fundamental lemma at level $P^{.10}$ has relative error smaller than $L^{-A}$ for every $A$ ($s=.10L^{.95}$; Friedlander–Iwaniec, *Opera de Cribro*, Lemma 6.11). Maximal BV pays all summed progression errors before signs: moduli $t r d\le P^{.4+o(1)}$, each with divisor-bounded multiplicity; Cauchy absorbs any fixed divisor powers using the trivial progression bound. The total unsigned main loss is polynomial in $L$ since $\sum_{t\le P}1/\phi(t)\ll L$, $U\ll L$. Keep the physical $\log(P-p)$ in partial summation, rather than approximating it by $L$ against individual absolute rows. Thus the actual low sum is
$$ V\int_I\sum_{t\le y} c(t)[\log(P-u)+U-\log t]du+O_A(PL^{-A}),
 \qquad c(t)=\mu_z(t)1_{(t,P)=1}/\phi(t).                           \tag{3}
$$
Indeed one may integrate the two sieve bounds for log weights using maximal prefixes (positive $\log((P-p)/t)$, bounded by $L$, with total variation $O(1)$). No treatment of the physical log shift by unsigned cancellation is used.

For $c_P=\mu/\phi$ on target units, zero otherwise, the full moments of orders zero and one are $0,-\mathfrak S(P)$ with uniform superlog tails beyond $\exp(L^{.1})$, by [the signed Euler calculation, §2](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/488c8f84). We have $c=c_P*h$, where $h$ is nonnegative with local values $h(r^j)=(r-1)^{-j}$ for $r\le z$, $r\nmid P$, and vanishes on other prime powers. Thus its total mass is $V^{-1}$. Rankin at $1/\log z$ gives $\sum_nh(n)n^{1/\log z}\ll L^{O(1)}$: at $r\ge3$ the local ratios $r^{1/\log z}/(r-1)$ stay away from 1 for large $P$, and their sum is $O(\log\log z)$. This gives superlog tails for $n>\sqrt y$, with a log moment too (use half the shift). Splitting the convolution at $n=\sqrt y$, the uniform $c_P$ tails and partial harmonic absolute bounds up to $y$ show that $\sum_{t\le y}c(t)=O_A(L^{-A})$ and $\sum_{t\le y}c(t)\log t=-\mathfrak S(P)V^{-1}+O_A(L^{-A})$. For $n>\sqrt y$, both full moments and the truncated inner sums have at most $L^{O(1)}(1+\log n)$ cost. Substitution into (3) proves (1).

## 2. Exact bounded union and the Type II major/minor input

Write $\omega_{ji}(n)=\#\{\ell\in\mathcal P_{ji}:\ell|n\}$. Discard for now rows with a repeated bank prime in $q$. In $q\le P$ there are $\ll P\sum_{\ell\ge\exp(L^{.1})}\ell^{-2}+\exp(2L^{.2})\ll_A PL^{-A}$ such integers for every $A$, with a superlog saving. By Hölder and ordinary divisor moments, they still cost $O_A(PL^{-A})$ with any fixed powers of $\tau(q),\tau(P-q),L$. This same bound applies when correcting a decomposition on nonprime source rows.

For squarefree bank parts, take the first hit family $j$. Extract $a=\prod_{i=1}^K\ell_i|b$, one label from each of its banks, and write $b=av$. Assign the coefficient
$$ D_j(v)=1_{\text{no family before }j\text{ is hit by }v}
          \prod_{i=1}^K(1+\omega_{ji}(v))^{-1}. $$
Earlier hits are unchanged because the banks are disjoint. Summing $D_j(b/a)$ over all such $j,a|b$ gives **exactly** $1_{b\in\mathcal B}$; the reciprocal counts pay all choices of labels. On repeated-label rows any erroneous identity has divisor-bounded cost as above. All tags exceed $z$, so $B(av)=B(v)+(\log a)1_{P^-(v)>z}$ exactly. In particular, after extracting one full bank product the residual on $h=tv$ has the form
$$ F_j(h)=\sum_{tv=h,\ t>y}\mu_z(t)\,c_j(v), $$
where $c_j(v)$ is either $D_j(v)B(v)$ or $D_j(v)1_{P^-(v)>z}$; the latter has the separate multiplier $\log a$. These functions satisfy $|c_j(v)|\le L$ and $|F_j(h)|\le L\tau(h)$. We can impose $(h,P)=1$ (i.e. target units on both factors) since the excluded actual prime rows have $p|P$. All tags are target units. Thus $P-ah$ is automatically a target unit. The selector depends on the actual factor $b$ rather than only $q$.

Replace the source prime-log weight by $\Lambda$ and impose least factor $>w=\exp(L^{.24})$ there. Actual prime exceptions and proper powers cost $P^{1/2+o(1)}$. Expand with $D=P^{1/5}$:
$$ \Lambda=\mu_{\le D}*\log-\mu_{\le D}*\Lambda_{\le D}*1
                   +\Lambda_{\le D}+\mu_{>D}*(\Lambda_{>D}*1).        \tag{4}
$$
The isolated third term is zero on $I$. In the last convolution the two source slots are $w$-rough target units, both of length $\gg P^{1/5}$ on retained dyads (the second contains a divisor $>D$); the first is the actual Möbius weight, the second bounded by $L$. Their product lies in $I$. The full-target projected comparison under the zero hypothesis above applies with these exact support and coefficient conditions: on a fixed ratio packet of tags $a\asymp Y$ its normalized squared $h$-sum is $\ll_R PY L^{-R}$ for any required fixed $R$ by taking fixed $K$ sufficiently large. See §1 of the linked proof for the exact projector, including imprimitive characters. Cauchy costs $\sum_{h\ll P/Y}|F_j(h)|^2\ll (P/Y)L^5$, not a pointwise divisor maximum. Multiplication by the fixed reciprocal mass to pass to the unnormalized sum is bounded. The extra $\log a$ is $\log Y+\log(a/Y)$, so the same two fixed smooth packet tests apply, with $O(L)$ cost. No truncation of $t$ depends on $a$.

For the sharp source interval use source-slot boxes of relative width $L^{-B_0'}$, keeping only interior products, as in the cited comparison. Boundary products lie within $O(PL^{-B_0'})$ of the central endpoints. Their absolute divisor-weighted cost before applying the comparison is $O(P L^{C-B_0'/2})$ by Hölder and divisor moments, for an absolute $C$ independent of $K$: off repeated bank squares the sum of the nonnegative Ramaré coefficients over all families and tags is exactly at most 1 per original $b$, not $\tau(b)^K$. The separate nonnegative $B(v)$ and $\log(a)$ components are individually bounded by the original $O(L)$ weight per divisor. Repeated bank squares had superlog savings for any fixed $K$. Each narrow source slot can be handled by keeping its own true interval in the projected comparison (the major Möbius estimate is uniform in interval prefixes); arbitrary subsets of the Möbius slot are not asserted. The packet count is a fixed power of $L$ independent of $K$, as are $J\ll_K H$ and the $O_K(L)$ intervals for $a$. Thus one can choose $B_0',R$ and then $K$, with no circular need for $K$ exceeding its own moment loss. The entire last term of (4) is $O(P/L^3)$, allowing a larger fixed $R$ if desired. This is the only place the projected comparison/zero hypothesis is needed.

## 3. Both Type I terms, including short cofactor and induced characters

In the first term of (4) fix the $w$-rough divisor $d\le P^{.2}$ of the source; in the second $d=d_1d_2\le P^{.4}$ with the two **separate** truncations and source roughness retained. The coefficients and their multiplicity are $\ll L\tau(d)$. Sieve the remaining free input for no prime factor $\le w$ by inclusion–exclusion through $j=\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor$ labels. The retained squarefree $e\le w^j=P^{o(1)}$ are coprime to $d$. The omitted absolute terms cost $O_A(PL^{-A})$: source inputs requiring at least $j$ such labels number at most $P(\sum_{r\le w}1/r)^j/j!$, and the absolute pointwise error after all divisor and physical tag convolutions is divisor-bounded in $n,P-n$, times fixed powers of $L$. Hölder applies. This enumeration counts multiples at most $P/e$ without adding a rounding error. The resulting source divisor modulus is $k=de\le P^{.4+o(1)}$, with divisor-bounded multiplicity. Since $n=P-atv$ is a target unit, $(k,P)=1$ for any contribution; $(k,a)=1$ is also forced. The remaining congruence is
$$ tv\equiv P\bar a\pmod k. $$
All source-side factors other than their physical optional $\log(n/d)$ have now been fixed. We need an estimate for this **unit** progression, not for an arbitrary nonunit class.

For fixed $a$, the product scale for $tv$ is $X'\asymp P/a=P^{1-o(1)}$, with $tv$ in a central interval of this scale. Cut $t,v$ separately into dyadic/fine intervals, retaining $t>y$ exactly. Rectangles of relative width $L^{-B}$ separate the target interval and replace the bounded-variable part of $\log(n/d)=\log(P/d)+\log(1-atv/P)$ by a constant with error $O(L^{-B})$ on interior boxes. Across all $a$, boxes meeting a physical endpoint have $n$ in $O(PL^{-B})$ integers. Their cost, after the opened divisors, is bounded by Hölder with fixed divisor powers of $n,P-n$ and a power of $L$, even if one uses the crude number of tags dividing $P-n$. $B$ can be taken large here **after** $K$; only unconditional arbitrary-log estimates are applied in this section. For each $a$ all packet/weight losses are at most fixed powers of $L$, and $\sum_a1/a\ll_K1$ in each family. It suffices to obtain $O_A(X'L^{-A})$ for the progression sums over all $k$ with a fixed divisor-power weight. The following details give that estimate (in the short-$v$ case one exceptional smooth tail is paid globally).

**Balanced $v\ge P^{.02}$.** On a rectangle of dyadic lengths $T,V$ (fine boxes may be subintervals), $T\gg P^{.3}, V\gg P^{.02}$ and $TV\asymp X'$. Put $\alpha(t)=\mu_z(t)1_{(t,P)=1}$ and $\beta(v)=c_j(v)1_{(v,P)=1}$. The second coefficient is *arbitrary* of size $\le L$, including the dynamic no-earlier-family conditions. It need not satisfy Siegel–Walfisz. We claim that the sum over $k\le Q=P^{.4+o(1)}$ of the maximum absolute unit-residue sums of $\alpha(t)\beta(v)$ is $O_A(X'L^{-A})$, without subtracting a principal. By multiplicative orthogonality take absolute values of factorized character sums with weight $1/\phi(k)$. Write $k=r l$ for inducing conductor $r$, so the two primitive character polynomials include the additional masks $(t,l)=(v,l)=1$. For $r\sim R\ge L^D$, freeze $l$ and apply the multiplicative large sieve to both polynomials and Cauchy; using $1/\phi(rl)\ll H/(R\phi(l))$, the bound after summing $1/\phi(l)\ll L$ in aggregate is
$$ L^{O(1)}\bigl[X'/R+R\sqrt{X'}+X'/\sqrt T+X'/\sqrt V\bigr]. $$
The conductor and $l$ truncations can be enlarged in the nonnegative large-sieve sum. This saves arbitrary logs on summing dyadic $R$ after $D$ is chosen; the strict gaps on all polynomial exponents are essential. For $r\le L^D$, including 1, the $t$-polynomial in any subinterval is $O_{D,A}(T L^{-A})$ uniformly in $l$: small-conductor Möbius Siegel–Walfisz and
$$ \mu_z1_{(\cdot,P l)=1}=\mu*1_{\mathcal S_{P l}},\qquad
 \mathcal S_M=\{d:\text{each prime divisor of }d\text{ is }\le z\text{ or divides }M\} $$
prove this by splitting the right-hand divisor at $\sqrt T$. The harmonic mass up to infinity of this semigroup is interpreted with all prime powers and is $\ll L^{O(1)}$ at weight $d^{-1+1/\log z}$: the primes $\le z$ give this by Mertens, and the $O(L)$ primes $>z$ dividing $Pl$ give $O(L z^{-1+1/\log z})$ in its log. The large-divisor absolute harmonic tail is exponentially small by Rankin. The small-divisor free Möbius character sum has length $\gg \sqrt T$ at the dyadic upper endpoints (use prefixes and differences), so Siegel–Walfisz pays any log loss. Summing the low conductors uses at most $L^{O(D+1)}$ after the $1/\phi(k)$ weights. These arguments are absolute in the residue class. Fixed divisor weights on $k$ are recovered by Cauchy using the arbitrary-log saving and the crude complementary weighted bound $O(X'L^{O(1)})$: for an interval of products, the coefficient has absolute value $\le L\tau(n')$. To see the latter bound even for the *maximum* over unit residues, apply Cauchy within each class to $\sum_{n'\equiv b(k)}\tau(n')$, or use the elementary hyperbola bound
$$
 \sum_{n'\ll X',\ n'\equiv b(k)}\tau(n')\ll
 \sum_{s\ll\sqrt{X'},(s,k)=1}(X'/(ks)+1)
 \ll X'L/k+\sqrt{X'} .
$$
Summing $\tau(k)^{O(1)}$ costs only log powers since $Q<\sqrt{X'}$ by a fixed power. This proves the balanced estimate.

**Short $v<P^{.02}$**, including $v=1$. Fix $a,v$. Then $T\asymp P/(av)\gg P^{.97}$ and any nonzero progression has $(v,k)=1$. Expand $\alpha(t)=\mu*1_{\mathcal S_P}(t)$, with divisor $s$ from the semigroup. For $s\le P^{.04}$, $(s,k)=1$ is forced and the free Möbius slot is of length $T/s\gg P^{.92}$. Ordinary maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov for $\mu$ (e.g. the classical statement quoted in [Lü–Wang–Wu, (3.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20242v2#S3)) applies at every prefix of the dyadic interval, in the unit residue $P\overline{avs}\bmod k$, uniformly up to $Q$, with arbitrary-log bound $O_A((T/s)L^{-A})$. If expressed with the principal term removed, its principal sums are negligible by Möbius Siegel–Walfisz/smooth removal for primes dividing $k$: here one may instead use the quoted absolute unit-residue formulation. Cauchy absorbs divisor weights using the trivial progression count. Summing over $s,v,a$ costs respectively $\sum1/s\ll L^{O(1)}, \sum_{v\le P^{.02}}1/v\ll L,\sum_a1/a\ll_K1$ against the respective lengths. This requires no distribution from $c_j(v)$.

The part $s>P^{.04}$ is not paid by a per-modulus rounding error. In the original physical sum it forces $q=atv$ to have a divisor $s>P^{.04}$ in $\mathcal S_P$. There are at most $P\sum_{s>P^{.04},s\in\mathcal S_P}1/s\ll_A PL^{-A}$ such $q$ for every $A$ by the same shifted Rankin product. The pointwise number of all opened divisors/tags is at most $\tau(q)^{O_K(1)}\tau(P-q)^{O(1)}$ times $L^{O(1)}$, so Hölder removes this physical tail with arbitrary log saving. This finishes both Type I signs separately; no replacement by an unverified distribution theorem for the data-dependent bank-free sequence is involved. Together with §2 it proves (2).

## 4. What remains, and the limitation of an integer rarity bound

For any fixed $\delta>0$ and dyadic $B_0\ge P^\delta$ up to $P$, the *integer* proportion of cofactors failing all families is
$$ \ll_K (1-c_K)^J+O_{A,\delta,K}(L^{-A}),\quad c_K>0.                \tag{5}$$
For an elementary proof, the indicator that family $j$ fails is inclusion–exclusion over the $K$ events that one bank has no divisor. In each union of banks use a fixed-degree upper Bonferroni bound for a positive term in this expression and a lower bound for a negative term. This gives a polynomial $U_j$ in prime-divisibility indicators with $U_j\ge1_{\rm fail}\ge0$ pointwise. In the independent model $\Pr(\ell|b)=1/\ell$, the success probability is at least $(1-e^{-1/4})^K$; the errors of the finite Bonferroni truncations are bounded by $2^K(K/2)^{d+1}/(d+1)!$ at degree $d$, up to using consecutive parities. Choose fixed degrees in terms of $K$ so that $\mathbb E U_j\le1-c_K$. The disjoint banks are independent in this model. On the integer interval each term in $\prod_j U_j$ averages to its harmonic lcm weight with total rounding error $P^{o(1)}/B_0$: the absolute coefficient count is at most $\exp(O_K(J L^{.2}))=P^{o(1)}$, and repeated indicators are counted once in the squarefree lcm. Positivity permits multiplication of the upper bounds. This proves (5). It gives no absolute $o(X_P)$ after arbitrary prime/divisor weight losses.

In fact $J$ cannot be increased to pay arbitrary powers of $1/L$. The total reciprocal mass of all admissible primes in the full available interval is $.05H+O(1)$ by Mertens for the split class $1\bmod4$ (excluding target primes only decreases it), while each disjoint complete family uses at least $K/4$. Integers with **no** prime in that full split interval certainly fail. On every polynomial dyad they have density $\gg L^{-.05}$ by the same Mertens product and the elementary fundamental lemma for the ordinary integer sequence (sieving primes are $\le\exp(2L^{.2})$, leaving ample level). Thus polynomial-sized tag-poor cofactors, as well as subpolynomial ones, cannot be discarded by an arbitrary-log integer rarity claim. The prime case for $b$ fails all complete families when $K\ge2$. Dropping primality or signs in (1) does not control their true weighted residual. The zero-free condition in (2) has not been removed, and (1)–(2) are not a proof of binary Goldbach.
