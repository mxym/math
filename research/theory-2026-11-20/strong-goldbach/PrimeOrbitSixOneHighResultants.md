# A unique weight-one label cannot have a subpower parent in the three-macroscopic six-label branch

Consider a sequence N→∞ of even centers with a strongly connected factor-closed set of six distinct unit odd primes, every complement composite. Let A be its exponent matrix. Suppose nullity(I−A)=2. Pass to a subsequence on which all logarithmic weights w_i=lim log x_i/log N exist. Assume there are three weight-zero labels, three positive-weight labels, of which exactly one, called M, has weight one. Then **no weight-zero row can use M as a factor infinitely often**. Thus after discarding finitely many terms on this subsequence every parent of M is one of the two positive but non-high labels. No smoothness hypothesis is needed for this structural statement. The [six-label extremes](PrimeOrbitSixTerminalExtremeLabels.md) explain its relevance at smooth centers; multiple high labels and high labels with macroscopic parents are not excluded here.

**Four disjoint blocks.** Suppose N−s=uM for a weight-zero label s. Here u≥3 is integral and N^{o(1)}, and M occurs to the first power. Pass to a subsequence fixing the indices and letting every bounded M_{ij}=A_{ij} log x_j/log N converge to P_{ij}; M_{ij} here denotes a mass entry, not the prime M. Every row of P sums to one, because all primes occur in a composite and hence are ≤(N−3)/3. For W=diag(w_i) the limiting matrix P−W has rank at most four. For the three small labels t the supports of P_t are disjoint, since gcd(N−t,N−t') divides t−t' and is N^{o(1)}. Also P_s=e_M. For any small t,

    u(N−M)−(u−1)(N−t)=s+(u−1)t>0,

which is N^{o(1)}. Therefore P_M is disjoint from every small-row support, as a shared positive-mass coordinate would give a common prime power ≥N^η for a fixed η>0. Since the small rows of P−W are their rows of P, and its M row is P_M−e_M, its row span contains the four nonzero disjoint stochastic rows P_s, P_t (the two other small t), and P_M. They are independent and consequently span its row space.

For either remaining macroscopic input i, the i-coordinate of (P−W)_i is −w_i<0, every other coordinate nonnegative (there are no loops). Express that row in the above disjoint basis. The coordinate i must lie in a block, and the coefficient of this block is strictly negative; thus the block has support {i} alone, with mass one there. The two macros have distinct such parent rows g_i among the three small labels and M, distinct also from s. With the positive lower macroscopic weight we may fix the bounded exponent d_i=A_{g_i,i}; it satisfies d_i w_i=1. Thus N−x_{g_i}=v_i x_i^{d_i} with v_i=N^{o(1)}. Since these two weights are below one, d_i≥2.

The five rows on all small labels and the two non-high macroscopic labels are dependent because rank(I−A)=4. By the [product inequality for dependent rows](PrimeOrbitMacroscopicRank.md), removing their smallest input leaves product at least N/(log N)^{O(1)}. Consequently the two weights add to at least one. Both are reciprocal integers ≤1/2, forcing d_i=2 and both weights equal to 1/2.

**Six pairwise coprime polynomials.** Associate each row with a polynomial F(T) such that N−x divides F(N), up to no denominators. For a small label t use T−t; for M use (u−1)T+s (its value is u(N−M)); for a square label i parented by a small t use v_i T²−T+t (its value is v_i(N²−x_i²)); and, for the possible square label parented by M, use u v_i T²−(u−1)T−s (its value is u v_i(N²−x_i²)). In each case divisibility follows by substitution. At most one square can have parent M. The degrees are bounded and all integer coefficients are N^{o(1)}.

The small linear roots are distinct positive primes; that of M is negative. Any square with a small parent gives an irreducible quadratic over Q (discriminant 1−4v_i t<0); two such quadratics have different constant terms from different parents and cannot be proportional since both linear coefficients are −1. A square with parent M has positive discriminant, so cannot share a root with such a negative-discriminant quadratic. Its value at −s/(u−1) is strictly positive. At a small prime t vanishing would force t|s and hence t=s, but substituting s gives us(v_i s−1)>0. These checks establish pairwise coprimality for all six polynomials, separately at every physical term.

For two such polynomials, their nonzero integer resultant is N^{o(1)} by the bounded-size Sylvester determinant. It is an integer-polynomial combination of the two input polynomials, so the gcd of their values at N divides it. Thus the gcd of any two of the six complementary rows is N^{o(1)}, making all six supports of P disjoint. Each is nonempty; in six coordinates P must therefore be a permutation matrix. But P−W is singular, so at least one permutation cycle has all its weights one: its block is singular exactly when the product of its w_i equals one, and every weight is in [0,1]. Such a cycle cannot be a singleton because P has zero diagonal, and the unique weight-one label is M. Contradiction.
