# Opposite half-weight intermediates in the four-macroscopic branch

Use the [four-macro smooth-center theorem](PrimeOrbitSixFourMacro.md), with its notation and hypotheses. On any surviving weight-convergent subsequence there are small p,q, the unique high H, and three intermediate primes X≤Y≤Z with weights 0<α≤β≤γ<1. Then **α=β=1/2**. Furthermore if γ>1/2, both X² and Y² must divide some complementary rows for all sufficiently large terms. This does not assert X or Y more precise than N^(1/2+o(1)), nor that their squares occur in distinct components.

## Pairwise sum lower bound

Retain all five nonhigh labels and eliminate H, by §1 of the [four-macro proof](PrimeOrbitSixFourMacro.md). Here C=K−I has rank three, each entry O(log N), and ρ_x=D_xN/x+σ_x with σ_x=±1 and 0<D_x=N^{o(1)}; height exponents are on a convergent subsequence. The sole high H has exactly one incoming parent (apart from the lack of loops, every other label is smaller than H, and two parents would give a positive difference divisible by H). Thus at most one retained input has a path of positive length; all others have D_x=1. The two rows C_p,C_q are independent: otherwise by irreducibility the proportional nonzero rows share a positive column among the three intermediates. Such a shared macroscopic divisor forces D_p σ_q q−D_q σ_p p=0, as in the cited proof; this implies p|D_p and q|D_q, impossible since at least one of these D's equals one.

For any intermediate U, the triple C_p,C_q,C_U is independent. A putative dependence has a nonzero coefficient at U and integer coefficients (log N)^{O(1)} via minors. Expanding the product of ρ's modulo N² gives N|Σ c_i σ_i D_i/x_i as in that proof. On multiplying by p q U, the numerator is nonzero modulo U (its coefficient c_U D_U is nonzero and subpower), but has size N^{w_U+o(1)}<N. Conversely the four rows of p,q,U,V for two different intermediates are dependent; the same argument uses a relation with nonzero coefficients at both U,V by the preceding independence. The numerator after multiplying by p q U V is again nonzero mod U, but now has size at most N^{w_U+w_V+o(1)}. It follows that w_U+w_V≥1 for every pair, in particular α+β≥1.

## A three-retained, one-multiplicity obstruction

If β>1/2, retain p,q,X and take H,Y,Z as outside labels. Each row can use at most one outside factor *with multiplicity*, since all three exceed √N. For such an outside arc with target R, its positive odd cofactor v=(N−source)/R is ≥3, with log v/log N→1−w_R. A cycle on outside vertices would give N|∏v−(−1)^j by affine composition; since the sum of the three available deficits is (1−β)+(1−γ)<1, this is an impossibility. Thus all outside successor paths are acyclic. Their positive D_s from the affine construction satisfy

    0<D_s≤V_s=∏_{arcs on the path}v ≤ N^{2−β−γ+o(1)}.

The first-return K is irreducible, each row sum ≥2, and K−I has rank one as in the [balanced return lemma](PrimeOrbitBalancedReturn.md). With a,b positive integral, gcd_i a_i=1, and W=∏s_i^{b_i}, obtain W^{a_s}=D_s N/s+σ_s, with σ_s=(-1)^{j+1} for path length j. Thus D_s/s is a positive integer and W^{a_s}≡σ_s mod N. By Bézout on the a_s, W≡1 or −1 mod N (the sign may depend on the term), and W>1 gives W≥N−1. On the other hand 2−β−γ<1 gives every W^{a_s}<N^{2−η} for a fixed η>0 eventually. Therefore **all a_s=1**. The D_s/s must all equal some integer h≥1 and all signs are the same: subtract each pair of equations and use N>2 to exclude unequal signs (all D_s/s integral).

Every retained row starts a nonempty outside path (otherwise D_s=1 not divisible by s). The three first targets are distinct because the retained inputs p,q,X are all smaller than the outside labels (ordered X≤Y and β>1/2 with X<Y in the physical ordering), and their positive differences cannot be divisible by such a target. Thus all outside labels are direct first targets. If there were an outside-to-outside arc, the lengths of the paths starting with its source and its target would differ by one, since every outside row has at most one outside successor. Both are actual starts from retained rows, in conflict with their common sign. Therefore all paths have length one. It follows that the three direct cofactors are v_s=1+hs. The partner H is high, so its cofactor is subpower, forcing h subpower as well. This makes the cofactors at both p and q subpower, giving two distinct high partners, a contradiction. Hence β≤1/2; α+β≥1 with α≤β now yields α=β=1/2.

If γ>1/2 and the square of one half-weight prime, say U, is absent along an infinite subsequence, take as outside H,Z,U and retain the other half-weight prime V with p,q. Multiple distinct outside factors are ruled out by their limiting weights; H²,Z² exceed N, and U² was excluded by hypothesis. The same deficit sum is 1/2+(1−γ)<1, so the rank-one argument again gives all a_s=1 and a common h and sign. The distinct-first-target assertion now needs care if V>U. All targets except U still exceed every retained input; U can be common first target of V and a small t only if U divides V−t. Here V and U have comparable logarithmic weight and this divisibility is NOT automatically ruled out. However no small input can share a first-target path with another retained row, regardless of sizes when all a_s=1: if distinct s,t first share high target R with cofactors v_s,v_t and common tail length, the return monomials on R are the same after that first edge, so W=v_s A/s=v_t A/t with A>0. Hence t v_s=s v_t, impossible since (v_s,s)=1 and s,t are distinct primes. This proves disjointness for either choice. Subsequent parity and cofactor arguments give the same contradiction. This forces both squares to occur.

This is a prerequisite of the [exact four-macro exclusion](PrimeOrbitFourMacroExclusion.md); its dated literature comparison applies to the combined result.
