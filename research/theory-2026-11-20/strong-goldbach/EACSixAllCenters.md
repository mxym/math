# Six-label reflected-prime components: a square-root count with a small seed, and a power saving without one

For a [Bożek exceptional autonomous component](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900), use the orientation x→y when the prime y divides the composite N−x. All labels are distinct odd primes coprime to the even center N; the component is strongly connected, has no loops, and every prime factor in each row belongs to the component. In this orientation a nontrivial factor-closed strongly connected component is a terminal component; Bożek uses the reverse direction for arcs.

1. At most **X^(5/6+o(1))** centers N≤X have an EAC on six labels, with *no* smoothness, rank, multiplicity, fixed-label or size assumption.
2. For any fixed function P(X)=X^{o(1)}, the count is **X^(1/2+o(1))** if some label is ≤P(X). More generally the latter bound holds for centers having an all-composite forward factor orbit on at most six labels from a prescribed or freely chosen odd prime seed ≤P(X) coprime to N, even if the orbit is not itself strongly connected. In particular it applies to a fixed seed p. These are upper bounds on centers, not numbers of components; they do not bound ordinary Goldbach failures with arbitrarily large forward orbits. A counted center can have a Goldbach representation.

Write the six labels as p=x₀<x₁=q<x₂<⋯<x₅, A_ij=v_{x_j}(N−x_i), B=I−A, ν=corank B. The [upward-forest feedback bound](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) gives ν≤2 without a smoothness hypothesis. The [retained-set theorem](EACTwoRetained.md) gives at least ν+1 labels ≤√N, and in fact there are always at least two since N−p has a different prime factor ≤√N.

## 1. Reductions using a supported relation

Use the [supported-minor lemma](EACSupportedMinorFive.md), proved for every fixed component size: if outside an index subset the columns of B have rank strictly smaller than rank B and its label product is ≤X^α, the number of possible centers is ≤X^(α+o(1)). The rank inequality holds for any subset of ν+1 indices.

For ν≤1 choose the least two labels. Their product is ≤p√X. This settles the small-seed assertion in this case and also gives ≤X^(5/6+o(1)) whenever p≤N^(1/3). For ν=2 fix a constant η>0. When q≤N^η, the three least labels have product ≤X^(1/2+2η); the supported-minor lemma handles these centers. We take η arbitrarily small only *after* the fixed-η estimates. Centers N≤√X can always be counted trivially.

## 2. A one-dimensional tangent bound after fixing p

Assume ν=2 and q>N^η, and denote by A′ the six-by-five matrix consisting of all columns except the one indexed by p. Its entries are bounded by 1/η. We claim, for all sufficiently large N (uniformly in the indicated labels), that the six-by-six matrix [A′ | 1] has rank at least five. First rank A≥5: otherwise A has at least two eigenvalues zero and two equal to one, counted with algebraic multiplicity. Its Perron root ρ≥2. The sixth eigenvalue would be −2−ρ by the zero diagonal, violating the spectral radius.

For the claim, take any contrary unbounded sequence, fixing A′, all logarithmic weight limits w_i=lim log x_i/log N, and passing to convergent subsequences wherever needed. If [A′ | 1] has rank ≤4, rank A≥5 implies rank A′=4, 1∈col A′, and the p-column increases rank. Choose a fixed integral vector c in the left nullspace of A′ with D=cᵀA_p nonzero: one fixed basis vector works on an infinite subsequence. Then Σc_i=0 and exact factorization gives

    D log p = Σ_i c_i log(1−x_i/N).                                    (1)

Every label divides an odd composite complement and is ≤(N−3)/3; hence the right side is bounded. If p→∞ this is already impossible. We may thus take p fixed.

The [at-most-one-high geometric lemma](PrimeOrbitSixTerminalFiveMacroscopic.md), which uses only strong connectivity, ν=2, and five nonseed weights ≥η, gives at most one weight-one label. To recall why: choose a nonzero vector Af=f with f_p=0. Its two signs each contain a directed cycle. For a high label y its uniquely determined parent x has N−x=p^a y, a≥1, and f_x=f_y≠0 (two nonseed zero entries would leave too few vertices for the sign cycles). The parent and y cannot constitute a sign pair since a mutual pair has product <N. Thus both occupy a three-member sign group. Two highs would force a chain x→y→z in that group: the intermediate cofactors are powers of p, each high has just one parent because possible cofactor ratios lie in [2/3,3/2], and an all-high cycle would give N|∏p^{a_i}−(−1)^j, nonzero subpower. The third vertex z needs a same-sign successor x. For N−x=p^a y and N−y=p^b z, x|N−z implies x|p^{a+b}−p^a+1, subpower, whereas x≥N^η. This is impossible.

If all x_i/N→0 then (1) contradicts |D|log p≥log 3. Otherwise the unique high y has a positive limiting ratio; its nonhigh parent x≠p has N−x=p^a y with fixed a≥1 on a subsequence. Passing to a fixed D (bounded by (1)) yields p^D=(1−p^(−a))^{c_y}. Since p^a−1>1 is prime to p, unique factorization forces c_y=0 and D=0. This proves the rank claim. The same geometry just recalled applies to a variable least prime: for a high label its cofactor is necessarily a power of that least prime, whose weight is then zero; all bounds use only that power's subpolynomial size, the five other lower bounds, and coprimality.

Consider the complex tangent to the equations x_i+∏ x_j^(A_ij)=N at the physical point, *fixing p* but not N. For n=δN/N, h_i=δx_i/x_i and h_p=0 the six differential equations are

    (A′h)_i−n = C_i(n−h_i),            C_i=x_i/(N−x_i).                 (2)

The tangent dimension is at most one for all sufficiently large N. Indeed, if not, take a sequence fixing A′ and limiting weights, and choose on each tangent an orthonormal pair in the six variables (five free h_i,n). For nonhigh i, C_i→0 and the right sides tend to zero. If there is a high y, the same structural argument applies with varying p: any cofactor of its incoming row contains only the smaller-than-polynomial seed (if p itself has positive weight there can be no high labels). Its parent is unique and nonhigh by the sign argument above, which works for a varying seed of weight zero as well. Equation (2) at that parent x gives h_y−n=C_x(n−h_x)→0, so the y right side also tends to zero since C_y≤1/2. Passing to a limit of the orthonormal pair would give two independent elements of ker[A′ | −1], contrary to rank at least five.

If the tangent is nonzero, its direction moves at least one label ≤√N. Here are the details for either value of n. Suppose δx_i=0 for all such labels. When n=0, each large label y occurs in some row N−x=u y where u≥3 consists only of small labels, fixed to first order. Thus |δy|=|δx|/u≤max_i|δx_i|/3. The maximum cannot be positive, since small labels have zero derivative. When n≠0 put z_i=δx_i−x_i n and start at any small label, with 0<|z_i|≤√N |n|. Follow the unique large output y whenever present; differentiating N−x_i=u y gives z_y=−z_i/u. A cycle contradicts the strict contraction from a nonzero start. If a row has no large output, its whole complementary product is fixed to first order, so δx_i=δN and |z_i|=(N−x_i)|n|≥(2N/3)|n|, impossible along the contracting path. This proves the assertion. If the tangent is already zero choose any nonseed label ≤√N, as exists from the p row.

Consequently one may fix p and an additional label ≤√N so the resulting physical point has **zero tangent**, and therefore is an isolated nonsingular zero of a square *subsystem*. More explicitly, after fixing the two coordinates, five free variables remain including N. Select any five of the six equations with independent gradients there. For fixed exponent chart, fixed two label values and selected equations, affine Bézout bounds the number of such isolated points by D_max^5, where D_max≤1+log X/log 3 bounds all polynomial degrees. The bound does not require other components of that system to be zero-dimensional: a small generic perturbation retains each of any chosen finite set of nonsingular points by the inverse function theorem, and has at most the Bézout number of roots. Enumerating fixed indices, subsystems and integer exponent entries costs at most (1+log X)^{O(1)}. The two integer labels cost at most P(X)√X in the small-seed theorem and X^(5/6) when p≤N^(1/3). Combining with §1 and taking η arbitrarily small proves the desired bounds in these ranges.

## 3. All larger minima: clustered semiprime factors

If p>N^(1/3), every complement has exactly two prime factors with multiplicity, and the largest label M is <N^(2/3). Use the undirected cofactor graph H: each row gives an edge uv (a loop allowed when u=v) with N−x=uv. Edges are distinct since row inputs are distinct. All six labels occur. The [semiprime star lemma](EACSemiprimeStar.md) gives a component S containing M that is a star with m≥2 vertices, m−1 edges, no loops. For reference M cannot be a loop since any other product has gap ≥2M; if M is paired with s, any other mate of s exceeds M/2 by comparison of the complements in [2N/3,N], and such a label occurs on a unique edge since a difference between two distinct odd row inputs divisible by it would exceed M. The same argument prevents a loop at s.

Every component outside S is nonbipartite. Otherwise a second connected bipartite component has at least two vertices (none is isolated). If it or S has at least three vertices, at most one vertex remains outside these two components. Such a second bipartite component on ≤4 vertices has at most as many edges as vertices, and a remaining isolated vertex contributes at most one loop, so S's deficit cannot be made up. If both bipartite components have two vertices, both have just one edge; the remaining two vertices supply at most three distinct edges even with loops, again insufficient. Thus outside S every vertex is connected to an odd cycle (a loop counts). Writing t_u=log(u/√N), each cofactor edge obeys t_u+t_v=O(M/N) from uv=N−x; summing along a bounded-length odd closed walk at u gives t_u=O(M/N). Hence every outside label is

    √N+O(M/√N)=√N+O(X^(1/6)).

If m≤3 there are 6−(m−1)≥4 edges outside S, hence at least one of their distinct row inputs is also outside S. More generally whenever such a row exists it gives N=z+uv for three clustered outside labels. Choose one of the three in O(√X) ways, the other two within O(X^(1/6)) of it in O(X^(1/3)) ways. This counts at most O(X^(5/6)) centers, also if u=v.

Only m=4 can remain: with m≥5 at most one outside vertex could not cover the star edge deficit. For m=4 the outside vertices u,v give all three edges u²,uv,v². Suppose each of these has a star input; at least two of their input vertices are different leaves y_i=N−t_i, t_i∈{u²,uv,v²}. Each such leaf gives a different star edge s y_i=N−z_i at the star center s; the inputs z_i are distinct. There is at most one leaf not yet known in the form N−t_j, so for at least one chosen leaf z_i is u,v, s, or a known leaf N−t_j. In the first case (s−1)N=s t_i−z_i, giving s−1|t_i−z_i. That difference is nonzero for large N by clustering. In the second case (s−1)N=s(t_i−1), giving s−1|t_i−1. Either case permits X^{o(1)} values of s per pair u,v by divisor counting, and then determines N. The last case would force s y_i=t_j, impossible since the prime s is neither u nor v. There are O(X^(2/3)) choices of the clustered pair u,v; this terminates the count for m=4 and so proves (1).

Finally if a full all-composite forward orbit on at most six labels is not a six-label EAC, it has a terminal EAC with ≤5 labels, which occurs at no more than [X^(1/2+o(1)) centers](EACFiveSquareRoot.md) by that theorem and its smaller-size prerequisites. This proves the asserted forward-orbit version of (2).

The [finite replay](check_eac_six_all_centers.py) checks the purely combinatorial possible residual cofactor graphs in §3 and the counting split by their row inputs. It is not used for numerical searches over centers.

## Comparison

[Bożek's original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900) lays out the graph framework and classifies two-label components in Theorem 3.7. The existing [five-label result](EACFiveSquareRoot.md), [invertible-exponent-matrix Jacobian result](EACJacobianSquareRoot.md), and [six-label fixed-smooth exclusions](PrimeOrbitSixSmoothExclusion.md) have different hypotheses. The result here bounds a structural subset of even centers, not the extensively studied ordinary Goldbach exceptional set. [Kelanemer, SSRN 7274460, August 2026 with September revision](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460) studies the nearby terminal exponent-matrix framework; only the abstract was accessible in this check, so comprehensive nonoverlap is not asserted. [Nelson, November 2026 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14580v2) studies additive relations in a prime-indexed ring, not the reflected factor equations used here. No priority claim is made.
