# A moving large-prime shape in the signed reflected divisor band

This extends the signed estimate in [PrimeSupportedSmoothNeckCancellation](PrimeSupportedSmoothNeckCancellation.md), equation (1), to a broader genuine raw-modulus family. It includes moduli with one prime of exponent up to .305–.385 depending on the *actual* prime \(s\), and also admits a much smaller smooth core whenever an independent macro prefix supplies the first factor. The complete even-rank band and its odd-orientation interactions remain unresolved.

Keep the notation, centered physical window, ordinary and reflected rough cutoffs, \(b_q=.501\), and \(b_m=.5+T^{-20}\) of that note. For squarefree \(d\) in the literal band \([m/B_m,B_m]\), put \(d_0=\prod_{p\mid d,\,p\le x^{.010}}p\), \(d_1=d/d_0\). Write \(p=P^+(d)\). Replace the family \(\mathcal D\) in that estimate by the following enlarged \(\mathcal V_s\): require
\[
                      p\le x^{.585}/s^2,                            \tag{1}
\]
and one of two **disjoint** choices for the first factor:

* If \(d_0\ge x^{.088}\), the first factor \(A\) is the first increasing-prime prefix of the whole \(d\) reaching \(x^{.088}\). It uses only primes \(\le x^{.010}\), hence \(A\le x^{.098}\).
* If \(x^{.035}\le d_0<x^{.088}\), the first factor \(A\) is the first increasing-prime prefix of **\(d_1\)** reaching \(x^{.088}\); require that this prefix is \(\le x^{.098}\). This eligibility condition depends exclusively on \(d_1\), not on the varying smooth core.

For the enlarged literal family the conclusion is still \(O(T^{-12}M_N)+O_A(xL^{-A})\) on the **signed fixed-target sum** of the earlier equation (1). The source of its nontrivial progression term is still the actual \(s q\) with both primes, not a smooth-number surrogate for either. We verify the needed new factorization and local means below.

## 1. Three actual slots, including the large prime

In both close ranges \(A\) has exponent \(a\in[.088,.098]\); the roughness divisor \(e\) has prime factors \(\le z_m\), whereas every actual factor of \(A\) is \(>z_m\). Thus \(Ae\) is an unambiguous first slot. Let \(t=\log_x d\in[.4999,.5001]\), \(\ell=\log_x e\le .0001\), and \(\sigma=\log_x s\in(.10,.14)\). These endpoint reserves hold eventually for the actual moving support.

If \(p\le x^{.236}\), look at the prime factors remaining after \(A\). If any lies in \([x^{.116},x^{.236}]\), take \(D\) to be the least such prime. Otherwise take \(D\) to be the first increasing prefix of those remaining factors reaching \(x^{.116}\); in this case all increments are \(<.116\) and \(D<x^{.232}\). Either way \(b=\log_x D\in[.116,.236]\); \(C=d/(AD)\) is polynomially long. The four Lichtman rails for \((Ae,D,C)\) are exactly the bounds already checked in Section 3 of the earlier note, including correlated \(C\) and \(D\) endpoints.

If \(p>x^{.236}\), then it cannot be in \(A\) and is the only such prime: two of them together with \(A\ge x^{.088}\) would exceed the band. Take \(C=p\), and **all** its remaining complementary product as \(D=d/(A p)\). If \(c=\log_x p\), then \(.236<c\le .585-2\sigma\le .385\) by (1), and \(b=t-a-c\ge .0169\). The second rail of [Lichtman, Proposition 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08522v1) is
\[
        2\sigma+b+2c=2\sigma+t-a+c\le .9971 .
\]
The first rail is \(a+\ell\le .0981<.10\). With \(\vartheta=7/32\) the third and fourth left exponents respectively are
\[
 \vartheta+\sigma+5t-4a-3c+\ell\le1.79935,
 \qquad\sigma+5t-4a-3c+\ell+2\vartheta(\sigma+2c+a-t)
       \le1.66805625 .
\]
The ratio bracket is positive throughout this high-prime chart, so using the largest \(\vartheta\) only weakens the bound. The latter affine expression is increasing in \(\sigma,t,\ell\) and decreasing in \(a,c\); the displayed upper is attained in this bounding rectangular box at \(c=.236\) (with the original open endpoint understood). The rails have fixed slack for spectral \(\epsilon=.0002\) (including the 8 and 15 multipliers); dyadic scaling constants contribute only \(o(1)\). The moving residue is the true \(2x\), within \(|2x|<x^{1+\epsilon}\) eventually, and the true SW slot remains \(s\) throughout.

### Canonical independence and genuine boundary costs

Within each slot enforce its own squarefree Möbius sign, fixed prime-size restrictions, and its prefix condition \(A/P^+(A)<x^{.088}\), or \(D/P^+(D)<x^{.116}\) where the latter is the prefix case. The first/core-rich case requires \(P^+(A)<P^-(D),P^-(C)\); the macro-only case requires \(P^+(A)\) smaller than **the least prime \(>x^{.010}\)** in either \(D,C\), with the no-candidate sentinel. The small prime factors of those slots are not compared to the macro \(A\). When \(D\) is a singleton, compare it to the least competing prime in \(C\) in its fixed band. When \(D\) is a prefix, enforce all remaining primes \(<x^{.116}\), and \(P^+(D)<P^-(C)\). When \(C=p\) is the unique giant, no counterpart comparison is imposed other than \(P^+(D)\le x^{.236}\). These conditions are equivalent to the definitions on the actual integers; every possible recombined modulus is squarefree and has precisely the stated chosen orientation. The exact finite Fourier comparisons of integer prime labels in the earlier theorem separate all cross-slot ordering. They do not add a rough assumption on \(D,C\), create colliding primes, or twist \(s\) at high frequency.

For the smaller-core case, the actual smooth cores of \(D,C\) multiply to \(d_0\). Use log-relative meshes of spacing \(L^{-F}\) for those two individual core labels, which are part of their own slot, and round the gate at \(x^{.035},x^{.088}\). Terms changed by rounding have actual \(d_0\) in one of the resulting relative \(O(L^{-F})\) strips around a polynomial endpoint. The harmonic sum of fixed divisor powers over such a strip is \(O(L^{-F+O(1)})\) by Shiu and \(1/\phi(v)\ll L^{O(1)}/v\); the absolute macro harmonic over primes of exponents \(.010\) to \(.385\) is bounded, even with fixed divisor powers. Grouping into moduli \(d e\) then costs only the harmonic rough-label multiplicity and the progression upper \(O(xL^{O(1)}/\phi(d e))\), as in the earlier proof. The analogous centered model obeys the same upper. This removes only the artificial rounding error: the literal core inequalities are restored in the principal. Coefficients on independent slots are 1-bounded after harmless separated log powers.

For (1) in the giant chart, use log-relative meshes of the two **actual** \(s,p\) variables. At fixed \(d,e\) and its giant \(p\), any changed \(s\) lies in an \(O(S L^{-F})\) interval about the actual threshold \((x^{.585}/p)^{1/2}\) on an \(s\)-dyad \(S\). On the physical unit support \((s,d e)=1\), (q) lies in one class modulo \(d e\), with count \(O(x/(s d e)+1)\) even if its primality is dropped for this upper. Summing over this short \(s\) range and moduli gives arbitrary log reserve plus at most \(x^{.65+o(1)}\) from the floors (\(s<x^{.14},de<x^{.51}\)); the centered model has the same log-small harmonic upper. This separates the coupled boundary while keeping a true SW prime \(s\) on each surviving independent interval. The other band/product/smooth boundaries are treated exactly as before. There are only log-power many pieces and fixed Fourier factors, so the absolute error reserves survive. Thus Proposition 5.2 restores the coprime principal on precisely \(\mathcal V_s\), rather than treating its weights as arbitrary over the whole near-square band.

## 2. Signed mean and unchanged error scale

The all-integer \(q\) part vanishes by the same CRT below the total product hyperbola, holding \(s\) and \(d\) fixed for the inner masked \(T_q\) harmonic. In particular dependence of (1) on \(s\) is no obstruction there, and the tuple floors are unchanged. The reflected rough indicators are opened and restored with the exact same factorial/short-label error bounds as in the earlier theorem; this part needs only a fixed divisor bound for the number of oriented labels.

On the genuine prime-\(q\) side, after the distribution step, fix \(s,q\) and the macro \(d_1\). Eligibility in (1) depends only on \(d_1\), since the largest factor is macro in any nonempty qualifying band (otherwise \(p\le x^{.010}\) is automatically allowed). In the second case the macro prefix availability also depends only on \(d_1\). Thus the smooth core \(d_0\) still ranges independently over the exact moved neck interval intersected with a fixed interval, either \([x^{.088},\infty)\) or \([x^{.035},x^{.088})\). In particular the sign cancellation may be applied **after** the three-slot factorization has been undone; no extra eligibility of an individual \(d_0\) remains beyond its smoothness, roughness, and these size constraints. True coprimality \((d_1,Ns q)=1\) is kept, and \((d_0,sq)=1\) is automatic. The macro harmonic remains bounded by the Euler sum over primes between \(x^{.010}\) and \(x^{.385}\).

The uniform smooth Möbius bound used in the earlier equation (4) holds on the additional endpoints \(x^{.035}\) and above: in its proof take both convolution split levels to be \(x^{.005}\). The inverse-Euler Rankin tail then still saves \(\exp(-.005T^{23})\); the analytic \(u/\phi(u)\) convolution still saves a power. The actual arguments of the base friable-Möbius sum remain \(\ge x^{.025}\); that proof uses Buchstab in a compact \(u\)-range bounded away from 1 since \(y=x^{.010}\), and masked coprime Mertens at \(U=\exp(T^4)\). These bounds and the bound \(XT^8/L^2\) are unchanged. Therefore the signed \(d_0\) harmonic is \(O((\log z_m)T^8L^{-2}(1+2T^{-20}L))\). Multiplication by the true \(O(\mathfrak S(N)/\log z_m)\) Bonferroni principal for the rough guard and summing the actual prime source again gives \(O(T^{-12}M_N)\) as claimed.

This contains the previously proved raw family, but no claim is made for a modulus with a larger atom than (1), a short core below \(x^{.035}\), or the intermediately small core without a usable macro prefix. In particular the large-modulus support is still selected by literal independent factors. As before the raw partition is not symmetric under \(d\mapsto m/d\), so this is not a separately ranked occupation bound or a solution to the full even-rank signed term.
