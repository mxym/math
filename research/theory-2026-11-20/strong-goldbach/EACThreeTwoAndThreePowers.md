# No three-label exceptional component at any power of two or twice a power of three

At every center N=2^a and at every N=2·3^a, there is **no** three-label exceptional autonomous component (EAC) in [Bożek's prime-factor graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900). The three odd prime labels and all exponents are unrestricted. This does not say whether large numbers in these sequences are Goldbach numbers; other sizes of component are not excluded.

Let m be the smallest label, D the maximum row multiplicity, and e the largest Smith invariant of I−A. It exists and is finite: singularity would supply a nonzero real f with Af=f, of both signs because the component is strongly connected and every row sum is at least 2. Each of its sign supports contains a directed cycle, but three labels without self-loops cannot accommodate two disjoint cycles. As in the [dyadic Smith calculation](EACDyadicSmith.md), m<√N, e≤(1+D²)^{3/2}<(1+D)^3, and m^e≡±1 modulo N. This last congruence comes from quotienting units by signs, not from assuming m^e≡1.

* At N=2^a, the exact dyadic lifting calculation gives e≥2^a/(m+1)>2^a/(2^{a/2}+1). Since D≤log_3 N<2a/3, for a≥26 the lower bound is at least (4/5)2^{a/2}>(1+2a/3)^3, a contradiction. The last inequality holds at a=26 by 4·8192·27>5·55³. As a increases by one, the right-hand side grows by a factor at most (57/55)³<√2, less than the increase of the left.
* At N=2·3^a all labels are coprime to 6, so D≤log_5 N<1/2+3a/4. Choose z=m or −m such that z≡1 modulo 3. Since z^e≡±1 modulo 3^a, reduction modulo 3 forces the plus sign. Now v₃(z^e−1)=v₃(z−1)+v₃(e): raising a number congruent to 1 modulo 3 to a power prime to 3 preserves v₃(z−1) by the geometric sum, and each cubing raises it by exactly one by binomial expansion. Hence e≥3^a/(m+1)>3^a/(√(2·3^a)+1)≥3^{a/2}/2 for a≥2. For a≥16 this exceeds (3(a+2)/4)^3, itself an upper bound for e. At a=16, 3^8/2>(27/2)^3; the upper bound subsequently grows by a factor at most (19/18)^3<√3 at each step.

The remaining ranges N=2^a, 2≤a≤25, and N=2·3^a, 1≤a≤15, all lie below 10^8. The six EACs in [Bożek's published finite census, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.09900) have none of size three. The conclusion does not need that full census: the companion program **independently exhausts only these 39 centers** by integer trial division as follows. The degenerate N=2 is outside the graph domain.

For a putative triple let r be its largest label. It cannot occur in both of the other rows, since their distinct positive input labels differ by less than r. It must occur in one by strong connectivity, and r does not divide N−r. Call the row not containing it p. That row can only be q^d with q the third label and d≥2. Enumerate all odd primes q≤√N and all d≥2 with q^d<N, and set p=N−q^d. Retain legitimate distinct prime labels coprime to N; in particular each label ≤(N−3)/3 as a factor of some odd composite row. Factor N−q: precisely one distinct prime other than p must occur, namely r>p,q. Then test factor closure, compositeness and strong connectivity literally on the triple. The enumeration returns no realization. Run `python3 -O check_eac_three_two_three.py`; no external data, tables or primality oracle. Both cutoffs are convenient rather than optimal.

In comparison, the [6 November v1 work on exceptional sets among polynomial values](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09386v1) counts absent additive representations on polynomial sequences, whereas this statement excludes specified factor-closed triples on two geometric sequences. These are distinct results. See also the related sources linked in [the general counting theorem](EACNonsingularSquareRoot.md).
