# The programmed four-tag box cannot be paid by a uniform reverse rank-seven count

Written research note, 2026-11-24. Use the true fixed-target CQ \(D_0\) score of ordinary mass \(m_0=969075913/7200000000\), the original reflected rows and normalization \(M_N=|J|N\mathfrak S(N)\), and the [programmed four-tag count](CQFourTagProgrammedLower.md). This result concerns the *available direct linear-sieve broad lower*, not the true value of the broad population. It uses true dyadic averaging across even targets to rule out obtaining an adequate uniform reverse upper at the currently constructed box, however that reverse upper were proved. It does not rule out a no-pair-conditioned estimate or a rank-sensitive lower.

## Genuine mean, not a synthetic sign model

For any fixed four-tag angular receiver box and fixed quotient cutoff, let \(R_7\) be its true squarefree rank-seven portion with count score \(L=\log N\). The smallest genuine prime has a fixed positive polynomial floor, and all widths are fixed bounded angular cutoffs. The same [genuine compact dyadic product averaging](CQCompleteNegativeReceiver.md), applied to the two original fixed compact angular weights \(D_0,R_7\), gives
\[
 \frac{\sum_{X_0\le N\le2X_0,\ 2\mid N} M_N\,B_N(D_0,R_7)}
      {\sum_{X_0\le N\le2X_0,\ 2\mid N} M_N}
 \ \longrightarrow\ m_0m(R_7).
\]
Here \(B_N\) is the mean of the two true orientations divided by \(M_N\); this is an actual sum over genuine reflected factor-product rows, not fixed-target independence. The inputs are compact of fixed positive floor, with sorted angular densities and harmless target-vs-own-log collars, exactly as in that averaging argument. In particular the rank-seven count score is a compact actual angular weight even though its CQ score is identically zero. If \(m(R_7)>0\), a bound \(B_N(D_0,R_7)\le C\,m(R_7)+o(1)\) uniformly over all even \(N\) needs \(C\ge m_0\). More generally a uniform proposed reverse upper must be at least this averaged population, whether quoted as a multiple of \(m(R_7)\) or not.

## At least .508 of normalized quotient level is needed

Fix true named widths of sum \(S\), suppress their common positive harmonic differential, and let \(X=1-S\), \(a=E/X\), \(t=z/X\). A rank-at-most-seven four-tag rough count needs \(t\ge1/4\), and a positive linear lower needs \(a>2t\). If \(a\le A=.508\), then \(t<A/2\) and \(2<a/t\le4A=2.032\). The broad lower coefficient and exact ordinary rank-seven density satisfy
\[
 L_{\rm broad}= \frac{2m_0}{Xa}\log(a/t-1),
 \qquad m_7\ge \frac{9}{4X}(1-3t)^2 .
\]
The latter follows from the genuine sorted simplex volume \((X-3z)^2/12\) and the lower bound \(27/X^3\) for the reciprocal density of the three remaining widths; it is not a fixed-target lower. For fixed \(t\), the broad coefficient increases with \(a\) in this range: setting \(s=a/t\), the derivative of \(\log(s-1)/s\) has sign \(s/(s-1)-\log(s-1)>0\). After inserting \(A\), replace \(t\) by \(1/4\) for the numerator upper and by \(A/2\) for the denominator lower. This yields the uniform ratio
\[
 \frac{L_{\rm broad}}{m_0m_7}
 \le\frac{8}{9A}\frac{\log(4A-1)}{(1-\tfrac32 A)^2}
 \le \frac{8}{9A}\frac{4A-2}{(1-\tfrac32 A)^2}<1 .
\]
The last strict inequality is rational: \(A=127/250\), \(4A-2=4/125\), and \(1-\frac32A=119/500\); the displayed comparison can be checked by integer cross multiplication. Since the comparison is pointwise in named widths and their measure is positive, summing any fixed box with these normalized levels preserves the strict gap. A uniform reverse upper good enough to subtract all \(R_7\) from this broad lower would therefore contradict the genuine dyadic product mean. The same holds for a union of fixed boxes uniformly below that depth. No actual negative count is claimed; a stronger broad lower or a rank-sensitive correction could evade this.

For the actual programmed box \(S=.100\pm.000004\), \(E=.4506\), \(z=.2251\), the necessary condition \(a>.508\) fails by a large margin: \(a<.501\). One may use a direct local bound \(L_{\rm broad}/m_7<.007\) in all of its box, obtained from \(2\log(E/z-1)/E\le 2(E/z-2)/E\), the displayed ordinary mass lower, and the stated rational endpoints for \(S\); whereas any uniform reverse coefficient must be at least \(m_0>.1345\). Thus the previous \(1.672\) budget is not simply an overly crude bound to optimize on this particular box.

## Scope of the presently uniform programming shortcut

The earlier all-support packing uses the sufficient condition that the largest quotient-sieve exponent can fit directly into the middle slot whenever in the critical high range. The upper rail for this shortcut is \(z\le (2-\theta-2B)/3\), \(\theta=7/32\), \(B\ge S+E\). Combine it with \(z\ge X/4\). Necessarily
\[
 a\le .515625-.109375\,\frac{S}{1-S}.
\]
At \(S=.10\) this is below .503473, too low even for the necessary dyadic threshold. The shortcut would need \(S<.06518\) to clear .508 (solve the linear fractional inequality), and crossing that threshold still does not supply a usable fixed-target reverse upper. Allowing larger sieve primes outside the middle slot using a genuinely different choice of support factors is not excluded.

The direct Buchstab idea of naming the least quotient factor of a bad rank-seven row is mathematically different, but at the actual box that fifth exponent is at least .2251 and the remaining ordinary level is at most \(E-.2251=.2255\). A literal standard upper at the true lower cutoff would thus have ratio at most \(1.00178\), nowhere near a sharp asymptotic rank-three upper; a sharp genuine switched pair estimate would be new input. This small-pass result identifies why routine sharpening cannot close the programmed box. No stronger rank-sensitive switched estimate is claimed.
