# Even represented centers can trap every maximal dyadic-order continuation

Fix \(p=3\) and **any fixed prime** \(q>3\) with \(q\equiv5\bmod8\). In particular \(q\) may be prescribed beyond any fixed taboo before sending the center to infinity. For every sufficiently large \(X\), there are \(X^{1-o(1)}\) distinct even centers \(N\le X\), with \(N\asymp_{p,q}X\) and
\[
v_2(N)=a=\lfloor\sqrt{\log X}\rfloor ,
\]
which **are sums of two primes**, but for which \(p\to q\to p\) is a closed two-cycle in the following restricted system: follow only edges whose actual prime-power contributions have maximal order \(2^{a-2}\) in \((\mathbb Z/2^a\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\). Neither \(p\) nor \(q\) is a prime endpoint; starting from \(p\) these two edges are the only choices in this restricted system. No assertion of closure in the full graph, maximality in \((\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\), positive relative density, or inaccessibility of a genuine endpoint by lower-order edges.

## 1. Two norm cofactors

Write \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), \(R=\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\), \(\bar{\ }\) for conjugation and \(n\) for the field norm. We construct many centers with
\[
N=p+q\,n(A)=q+p\,n(C),\qquad A,C\in R,\quad n(A),n(C)>1.
\]
This automatically gives \(N\bmod p=q\bmod p,\ N\bmod q=p\bmod q\). If an odd prime \(s\equiv3,5\bmod8\) divides a nonzero norm \(u^2-2v^2\), then \(s\mid u,v\) since 2 is a nonsquare mod \(s\); iteration gives even valuation. Hence in \(N-p\) the mate \(q\) occurs to an odd power while every other prime congruent to \(3,5\bmod8\) occurs to an even power. Interchange \(p,q\) for the other row. For \(a\ge3\), the signed dyadic group is cyclic of order \(2^{a-2}\), generated by 5, and its generators are exactly the classes of odds congruent to \(3,5\bmod8\). Equivalently, up to sign every odd is \(1\bmod4\), and it is a generator precisely if it is 5 mod 8 in that orientation (by the elementary 2-adic valuation of successive squares). Thus in each row only the mate’s prime power has maximal dyadic signed order. Because each norm exceeds 1, neither row is itself a prime pair.

## 2. The integral quadric and local class

Use the quaternion algebra \(B=F+Fj,\ j^2=pq,\ jz=\bar z j\). Its reduced norm on \(U+Vj\) is \(n(U)-pq\,n(V)\). The order \(\mathcal O=R+Rj\) has rank four. The algebra is a division algebra over \(\mathbb Q\): a rational nonzero field norm has even \(p\)-valuation since \(p=3\) is inert in \(F\). Thus \(n(U)=pq\,n(V)\) has no nonzero rational-quaternion solution. It splits over the reals, explicitly by
\[
U+Vj\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}U^+&pq V^+\\ V^-&U^-\end{pmatrix},
\]
where the superscripts are real embeddings. Put \(G=\mathrm{SL}_1(B)\), hence \(G(\mathbb R)=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\), and \(\Gamma=\mathcal O^1\). This is a cocompact arithmetic lattice; see [Voight, Prop. 38.1.3 and §38.1.5](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4_38). For example the left-regular representation on the integral basis of \(\mathcal O\) realizes \(\Gamma=G(\mathbb Z)\) and its usual principal congruence subgroup as \(\Gamma(D)=\{\gamma:\gamma\equiv1\bmod D\mathcal O\}\), because the integral lattice contains 1.

Set \(b_0=q+j\), of reduced norm \(q(q-p)\ne0\). For any \(a\ge3\) choose odd \(s,t\in\mathbb Z_2\) with
\[
s^2=(2^a-p)/q,\qquad t^2=(2^a-q)/p .
\]
Both right sides are 1 mod 8. The element
\[
g_2=(qs+tj)b_0^{-1}
=\frac{qs-pt+(t-s)j}{q-p}
\]
is in \((\mathcal O\otimes\mathbb Z_2)^1\): \(v_2(q-p)=1\), both numerators are even, and equality of reduced norms follows from \(q s^2-p t^2=q-p\). Its reduced-norm-one inverse is integral by quaternion conjugation. At \(q\) require the group element to be the identity mod \(q\), and elsewhere require integrality. [Strong approximation, Voight, Thm. 28.5.3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4_28) applies to the norm-one group split at the real place. The indicated product of nonempty local opens (at 2 take the ball about \(g_2\) mod \(2^{a+1}\)) meets \(G(\mathbb Q)\). Integral membership everywhere puts this rational point \(\gamma_a\) in \(\Gamma\); no assertion about its height is needed.

Let \(D=q2^{a+1}\). For \(\gamma\in\gamma_a\Gamma(D)\), write \(\gamma b_0=U+Vj\). At \(q\), \(U\equiv q=0\bmod qR\), so \(A=U/q\) is in \(R\) (its two coefficients are divisible by \(q\)); put \(C=V\). At 2, \(A\equiv s,\ C\equiv t\bmod 2^{a+1}(R\otimes\mathbb Z_2)\) since \(q\) is odd, so \(p+q n(A)\equiv2^a\bmod 2^{a+1}\), giving exactly the depth claimed. Globally \(\mathrm{nrd}(\gamma b_0)=q(q-p)\) gives the two norm identities in §1.

## 3. Counting in a *fixed* positive real sector, uniformly in its arithmetic coset

Here only the finite congruence level/coset moves; the real sector, group, and lattice do not. In the real matrix model write \(\gamma=k_1\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})k_2\) with rotations \(k_i\) and \(u\ge1\). Choose small fixed angular intervals about \(k_1=\mathrm{rot}(\pi/4)\) and \(k_2=I\) (viewed in the angular quotients by the finite centralizer). The first column of \(k_1\) and the first row of \(k_2 b_0\), the latter initially \((q,pq)\), then have both entries bounded away from zero with the same sign within each vector. Thus
\[
\gamma b_0=u(k_1 e_1)(e_1^\top k_2 b_0)+O_{p,q}(u^{-1}).
\]
On this entire sector both diagonal and off-diagonal products are positive and \(\asymp u^2\) for large \(u\); a central sign does not change either product. Thus \(n(A),n(C)\asymp_{p,q}u^2\), the four real embedding values of \(A,C\) are \(O_{p,q}(u)\), and \(N\asymp_{p,q}u^2\). Use the shell \(T/2\le u\le T\) with \(T=c_{p,q}\sqrt X\) small enough that \(N\le X\). Its Haar volume is \(\asymp T^2\) by rank-one Cartan integration: the radial density is proportional to \(\sinh(2\log u)\,du/u\).

For precision concerning the changing level, [Gorodnik–Nevo, *Counting lattice points*, Cor. 1.13, §5 and Thm. 7.1 with its proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.1515) give on this shell
\[
\#\{\gamma\in\gamma_a\Gamma(D):\gamma \text{ in shell and angular intervals}\}
=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{shell})}{[\Gamma:\Gamma(D)]}
+O_{p,q}(T^{2(1-\eta)})
\]
for some fixed \(\eta>0\), Haar normalized by \(\operatorname{vol}(G(\mathbb R)/\Gamma)=1\). Details of the specialization: the two regular angular intervals are Lipschitz well-rounded in \(K/M\), \(M\backslash K\). Theorem 7.1 and its proof apply the power-rate mean ergodic theorem and wavefront bounds for the corresponding two-sided sectors to the basic counting theorem. Corollary 1.13 gives the same absolute error after translation to lattice cosets, uniformly over the family: take \(x=\gamma_a\in\Gamma,\ y=1\), in \(\Omega=\Gamma\), a set compact modulo the fixed base lattice; the common injectivity radius is bounded below there. Equivalently \(\gamma_a\) normalizes the principal congruence subgroup, so the relevant orbit-point injectivity radius equals that at 1. The common finite \(L^{r+}\) exponent for the zero-mean representations over all congruence levels is exactly the property-\(\tau\) input in §5 for this simply connected \(\mathbb Q\)-simple group, isotropic at the real place. The rate and angular regularity do not depend on \(a\); subtract the two radius-ball estimates for the shell. This bounds the **absolute** error by a fixed power saving, not just a level-dependent relative asymptotic.

Since \(\Gamma/\Gamma(D)\) embeds in the finite units of \(\mathcal O/D\mathcal O\), its cardinality is at most \(D^4\). With \(a=\lfloor\sqrt{\log X}\rfloor\), \(D^4=X^{o(1)}\); the main term dominates the error and gives \(\gg_{p,q} X/D^4=X^{1-o(1)}\) tuples. Right multiplication by the invertible \(b_0\) does not identify distinct \(\gamma\)'s.

## 4. Multiplicity and actual prime representations

For each counted \(N\), both positive norm values of \(A,C\) are fixed and \(O(X)\). In the quadratic ring of integers \(R\), the number of integral ideals of any specified norm \(m\) is \(\le\tau(m)^2=m^{o(1)}\) in the usual uniform sense for \(m\le X\) (factor \(m\) locally; at most two primes over each rational prime). Any two generators of a principal ideal differ by a unit. Both embedding magnitudes of a generator we count are \(O(\sqrt X)\) and \(\gg X^{-1/2}\) since their product has absolute value at least 1. The units are \(\{\pm(1+\sqrt2)^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}\), so only \(O(\log X)\) among them can give generators in this box for a given ideal; use the ratio of the allowed upper and lower bound at the first embedding. This proves multiplicity \(X^{o(1)}\) for the pair \(A,C\), uniformly and independently of the dyadic level. Hence the same count \(X^{1-o(1)}\) holds for **distinct** centers.

[Montgomery–Vaughan, *The exceptional set in Goldbach's problem*, Theorem 1](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa27/aa27126.pdf) bounds the total number of even \(N\le X\) not representable as two primes by \(O(X^{1-\theta})\) for an absolute \(\theta>0\), unconditionally. Subtracting this from the preceding count leaves \(X^{1-o(1)}\) represented examples. This does **not** assert which prime pair represents any particular counted center.

**Why the restriction matters.** Here \(a/\log\log N\to\infty\), so an outward near-maximal dyadic-order theorem at a large prime-power divisor genuinely applies on these same centers. From either fixed seed \(p,q\) there must be exterior edges of near-maximal order beyond any fixed cutoff; but none of those exterior edges is of **exactly** maximal dyadic order. Thus the rule “always keep maximal dyadic order” can remain permanently trapped even though a genuine pair exists and large outward edges are forced. This does not show that permitting a loss of one bit is sufficient or necessary on all centers, or rule out a successful different strategy. The only excluded prime-endpoint route is in this specified restricted channel.
