# Strict one-sided response to genuine bounded-rank prime-factor profiles

Throughout $P$ is a large prescribed even number, $I=[P/3,2P/3]$, $L=\log P$, $X_P=|I|\mathfrak S(P)$, $\Pi(n)=L1_{n\ {\rm prime}}$, and $\langle\, ,\,\rangle$ denotes reflection $x+y=P$ on central target units. Here is an upper bound for the **actual** reflected prime, with no averaging in $P$.

Fix $\kappa>0$, $k\ge2$. Let $C(x)$ be $L$ times a bounded nonnegative Riemann-integrable function of the ordered normalized prime logarithms of the genuine squarefree product $x=p_1\cdots p_k$, $p_i>P^\kappa$, zero elsewhere. Integrability here is on the limiting angular simplex $\sum_i t_i=1$, with the ordinary Jordan convention for sets: equivalently we allow continuous weights and finite box/profile inequalities with boundary of zero simplex measure and their finite step approximations. When an inequality changes only because $\log x$ differs from $L$ by $O(1)$, its boundary must be null unless that change is explicitly retained. Write $m_C=|I|^{-1}\sum_{x\in I,(x,P)=1}C(x)$. Then
$$
 \limsup_{P\to\infty}\big(\langle C,\Pi\rangle/X_P-(200/53)m_C\big)\le0 .    \tag{1}
$$
The same statement holds with a bounded continuous macroscopic weight in $x/P$. The constant does not grow with the fixed rank; neither a power-saving error uniform in growing rank nor asymptotic independence is claimed.

For the actual native quadratic score $J=J_2$, put $J_H(y)=J(y)1_{P^-(y)>P^{2\theta}}$. For these same sources
$$
 \langle C,J_H\rangle/X_P=\langle C,\Pi\rangle/X_P-4m_C+o_*(1),
 \qquad \limsup_{\theta\downarrow0}\limsup_{P\to\infty}
 [\langle C,J_H\rangle/X_P+(12/53)m_C]\le0.                               \tag{2}
$$
Thus the **whole high side** of the actual signed remainder is strictly negative against every positive-mass nonnegative bounded-rank macroscopic profile; testing a negative-score source gives a strictly positive contribution. The high cutoff has not been imposed on a modeled prime. The order is fixed source and $\kappa$, then fixed $\theta$ and $P\to\infty$, then $\theta\downarrow0$. These one-sided statements do not settle the sign against a general signed source or prove binary Goldbach.

## 1. A genuine factored upper majorant for (1)

Here we use [Lichtman, §§3,5,6](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08522), especially Propositions 3.2, 5.2 and 6.1. The prime theorem for *triply well-factorable sequences* alone does not apply to upper Rosser weights. The construction below instead supplies three independent factors of the required lengths **for each contributing source scale**. Write $x_0=P/3$, $x\in[x_0,2x_0]$; powers of $x_0$ or $P$ at strictly separated bounds are interchangeable. Put $D=x_0^{.53}$, $z=x_0^{.18}$. Two further constants $0<\xi\ll b\ll1$ are chosen sufficiently small **after** the polynomial source ranges; they can be made arbitrarily small in that order ($b$ first, then $\xi$).

Sieve $y=P-x$ only at $p\nmid P$. On $p\le x_0^\xi$ take a dimension-one nonnegative upper divisor sum of level $x_0^b$; on $x_0^\xi<p\le z$ take the upper **classical Rosser** divisor sum of level $D$. Their product is nonnegative and majorizes the true prime indicator. In the second sum the ordered primes $l_1>\cdots>l_r$ of each squarefree label must pass $l_1\cdots l_{h-1}l_h^3<D$ for every odd $h\le r$. For exact coefficient independence, round each log exponent **up** to a fixed mesh of size $\Delta$ in the last tests. This is still a pointwise upper: expand the full inclusion–exclusion tree with decreasing labels, cutting at the first rejected odd prefix. The discarded descendants sum there to $-1$ times the prefix divisibility indicator times a nonnegative avoidance indicator for all smaller primes. Treat an equal rounded bin in the order of the underlying distinct primes. No subsequent decision depends on their internal order. The number of prime slots in an accepted label is bounded in terms of $\xi$ alone.

In the independent local model the large-bank weights approach the ordinary Rosser weights as $\Delta\downarrow0$ *after* $P\to\infty$ at fixed $\xi$. Indeed any disagreement lies on a finite union of strips of width $O_\xi(\Delta)$ about the odd-prefix hyperplanes. By Mertens the absolute harmonic sum on these strips tends to zero with $\Delta$ (all exponents are $\ge\xi$). Omitting the prime factors of $P$ or of one source integer can only decrease the absolute bounds. The large-bank Euler product is bounded below by an $\xi$-dependent positive constant. This harmless approximation is charged **in the principal only**; on the physical progression sums the rounded coefficients are used exactly.

For one accepted ordered exponent-bin vector group its prime factors as follows. Use the packing of Lichtman Proposition 6.1 with notional level $\vartheta=.531$, spectral exponent $\vartheta_K=7/32$ and $|a|=P\le x_0^{1+\epsilon}$ (the slack $\epsilon>0$ may be arbitrarily small and fixed). The rounded support implies ordinary Rosser support and hence well-factorable *size* support $l_1\cdots l_{h-1}l_h^2\le D$ for every $h$ (for an even $h$ use the preceding odd cubic condition). All $t_i\le .18$. At $t_1\ge .064$ the three functions in (6.2) are each strictly above $.531$; for the first use $(1+.064)/2=.532$, for the other two use $t_1\le.18$, $\alpha\le1.001$. If $t_1<.064$ and $r\ge3$, $t_1<\mu$ and both the last and first branches needed in (6.4) are within its stated scope: the interior minimum in $w(t_1,t_2,t_3)$ of (6.9) is greater than $.57$ (use $t_2,t_3\le.064$); hence the threshold $(1+t_1)/2\le.532$ is satisfied and $w>.57$. If $r<3$ in this small-$t_1$ case, the entire enlarged modulus is $\ll x_0^{.128+b}$ and ordinary convolution BV suffices. An empty label causes no issue. Thus no prefix length is skipped.

Specifically write $N$ for the selected short source length of §2 with $x_0^{2\delta}\le N\le x_0^{1/3+\delta/4}$, and $A=N x_0^{-\delta}$ for a sufficiently small fixed $\delta<10^{-5}$ (it may depend on the source). Proposition 6.1 applies since $D\le x_0^{.531-2\delta}$ and $A\in[x_0^\delta,x_0^{1/3-\delta/2}]$. It splits the large-bank labels into three groups of sizes $a',b',c'$, with $a'\le A$ and the three inequalities (6.3). For a bin vector choose one feasible representative to make the assignment, then keep these index groups throughout the vector. Moving to its upper box edges costs $x_0^{O_\xi(\Delta)}$; take $\Delta$ small enough compared with the slack. Merge the independent small-bank label into the third group. Its size $x_0^b$ costs at most $x_0^{(2+2\vartheta_K)b}$ in the three product inequalities. More explicitly changing $A$ to $N$ costs $x_0^{2\delta}$ in the first, $x_0^\delta$ in the second, $x_0^{(1+2\vartheta_K)\delta}$ in the third; (6.3) had respective margins $3\delta,3\delta,2\delta$. Keeping $b\ll\delta$ leaves fixed slack for all dyad endpoints and the small parameter in (5.2). The dependence on the group size in the third expression is monotone after combining its powers (in particular the middle power is $5-2\vartheta_K>0$). Thus the three dyadic groups meet **all four actual conditions** of Lichtman Proposition 5.2, including $Q_1<N/x_0^{\epsilon'}$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon'>0$ and the actual factor $|P|^{\vartheta_K}$.

The grouped coefficients are honest convolutions. Slots in distinct exponent bins are independent; within a repeated bin sum over all permutations divided by the factorial of its multiplicity, excluding actual repetitions. One may drop this last exclusion at power cost: each repeated prime $\ell>x_0^\xi$ then gives a square dividing the enlarged modulus, and $\sum_{q\le2x_0^{.53+b},\ p^2\mid q\ {\rm for\ some}\ p>x_0^\xi}1/q\ll x_0^{-\xi}\log x_0$. Crude integer progression counts $O(P/q+1)$, with fixed divisor powers $P^{o(1)}$, apply to both actual and model sums. Under the small-bank merge its label is uniquely recovered from the small-prime part as the two banks are disjoint. For a fixed bin vector all grouped weights are bounded in terms of the number of slots by unique factorization (even before normalization by multiplicity factorials); divide by these constants to invoke the 1-bounded proposition. Impose $(q,P)=1$ on each factor independently. There are only finitely many bin vectors at fixed $\xi,\Delta$, and polynomially many in $\log P$ dyadic length boxes. No assertion of well-factorability for the entire coefficient or for every length split is required.

## 2. The true source and the uniform progression hypotheses

Use $t_i=\log p_i/\log x$ on the exact angular simplex. For a continuous upper envelope replace these by $\log p_i/L$, passing first to a continuous extension on a neighborhood of the simplex and taking an arbitrarily small positive majorization. Every bounded nonnegative Jordan-integrable profile on the compact cutoff simplex has upper majorants given by finite nonnegative sums of products of smooth functions in the log coordinates with arbitrarily small excess **ordinary** angular integral (cover the boundary set by boxes, use upper step sums and then smooth upper steps). In this argument coordinates can first be symmetrized, without retaining any sharp ordering of prime slots. The ordinary integral of a symmetric profile is over the full simplex with measure $dt_1\cdots dt_{k-1}/(k!\prod_i t_i)$, by iterated PNT or Mertens on the first $k-1$ slots followed by PNT on the remaining polynomial-length prime. One may equally integrate over the ordered simplex without $1/k!$. A central macroscopic weight uses positive upper step intervals in $x/P$. We prove the bound for the majorant, apply its upper inequality on the **physical positive** prime count, then remove its excess by the ordinary PNT integral. The progression estimate is not asserted for arbitrarily discontinuous signed tests.

For one smooth separated profile use $\Lambda(p_i)/\log p_i$ on the polynomial support. Replacing prime indicators by these full von Mangoldt weights costs $O(P^{1-c_\kappa+o(1)})$ both after sum over all physical sieve labels (at worst a fixed divisor power of $P-x$) and in the principal (reciprocal total polylogarithmic). Indeed the reciprocal sum of all proper prime powers $\ge P^{\kappa/2}$, up to $P$, is $O(P^{-\kappa/4+o(1)})$ by summing integer powers on dyads, and the remaining slots have at most $O(P/n)$ choices with divisor multiplicity $P^{o(1)}$. Repeated primes and factors of $P$ in the source cost a power by the same argument with square or target-prime divisibility. Thus the genuine unit restriction can be removed before the analytic decomposition and restored when reassembling. Insert $P^-(n_i)\ge z_0=x_0^{1/(\log\log x_0)^3}$ in each original prime slot before the identity expansion; it is exact on every true prime under consideration.

Expand **each** of the $k$ von Mangoldt slots by the order-three Heath–Brown identity [Lichtman, Lemma 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08522#S3). Each of its at most six resulting variables inherits the roughness $z_0$; the Möbius variables are $\le 2x_0^{1/3}$ and the other slots have ordinary or logarithmic coefficients. The smooth log-weight, including each $1/\log n_i$, is a smooth compact function in $\log n_i/L$ times a power of $L$. Fourier inversion separates it over its factors by Mellin twists. Truncating each frequency at a sufficiently large log power costs arbitrarily many saved logs, by rapid decay and fixed divisor moments for the sums against $\tau(P-x)^{O(1)}$ or the reciprocal model. Dyadic partitioning costs further fixed powers of $L$. All such costs are taken before choosing the final log saving. Choose $\delta$ with $24k\delta<\kappa/4$.

If one of the resulting lengths belongs to $[x_0^{2\delta},x_0^{1/3+\delta/4}]$, take it for $N$ in §1. The remaining slots are one divisor-bounded coefficient at length $M\asymp x_0/N$ (split the bounded range of each product into dyads). The selected short slot is one of the same rough ordinary, logarithmic or Möbius weights used in Lichtman's proof of Theorem 1.7. These satisfy his (2.2), including auxiliary unit masks. For clarity, for a nonprincipal character of log-power conductor, the rough Möbius Dirichlet convolution is $\mu*1_{\{z_0\text{-smooth}\}}$; unit masks apply separately. The reciprocal smooth-factor tail beyond $N^{1/2}$ saves arbitrary logs by Rankin with exponent $1/\log z_0$ (Euler product $\ll(\log x_0)^{O(1)}$ versus $\exp(-c_\delta(\log\log x_0)^3)$). Use classical Siegel–Walfisz for the remaining long Möbius factor. The auxiliary unit-deleted version follows by convolution with the indicator of integers supported on primes dividing the auxiliary mask $r$: its logarithmically short Euler norm is $\ll\tau(r)^{O(1)}$, and its long tail by Rankin with a fixed small exponent is $\ll N^{-c}\tau(r)^{O(1)}$. Ordinary rough slots use the fundamental lemma on subpower primes (or the absolute version of this reciprocal smooth-factor argument for $1*(\mu\,1_{\{z_0\text{-smooth}\}})$). For smooth twists or one log use prefix partial summation; its log-power variation is harmless. As in the proof of Proposition 3.1, truncate large values of the long coefficient at $L^B$, then divide out the log powers: a discarded product has a corresponding large divisor majorant for $x$, so the total loss after all actual labels is $\ll_A P/L^A$ by higher divisor moments and Cauchy with $\tau(P-x)^{O(1)}$. The principal obeys the same estimate by the polylogarithmic harmonic sum of labels. No arbitrary Siegel–Walfisz assertion is needed about the long coefficient. The central interval $nm\in I$ is handled by Lichtman's interval-removal Lemma 4.2 as in (5.19) and then Proposition 5.2; its sub-root case is ordinary convolution BV, as in the same proof. This proves the requisite centered estimate for the weights of §1 in this branch.

If no such slot exists, each original prime slot on the nonzero support must have at least one variable $>x_0^{1/3+\delta/4}$, since the product of six variables below $2x_0^{2\delta}$ cannot reach $P^{\kappa/2}$. This observation can be made *before* Fourier separation: skip dyadic tuples on which the original weight vanishes identically. No Möbius slot can be this large, and there are at most two such variables in the whole product. Thus $k=2$, with two large free divisor slots, all remaining factors of product $M\ll x_0^{24\delta+o(1)}$. Apply Lichtman's Proposition 3.2 with its absolute modulus sum and **uniform** residue, since $x_0^{.53+b}\ll(x_0/M)^{2/3-3\epsilon'}$. Multiplying both free slots by logs and Mellin phases of polylogarithmic frequency causes only log losses: [Maynard II, §7, Lemma 7.1 and the proof of Proposition 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.07088#S7) explicitly permit two independent smooth functions with polylogarithmic bounds on derivatives after dyadic rescaling, and account for the $z_0$ restrictions and the central product interval. This also explains why no small fixed residue or two arbitrary large-variable coefficients were smuggled into this branch.

## 3. The principal at level .53 and the native signed comparison

In the centered model for a **fixed genuine original source product** $x$, the local density is $g_p=1/(p-1)$ for $p\nmid Px$ and $0$ otherwise. It comes from $1_{(x,q)=1}/\varphi(q)$, not from independence of the actual progression errors. The two banks factor in this principal. The classical Rosser upper linear sieve for this density bounds the unrounded large-bank model by
$$
 \prod_{x_0^\xi<p\le z}(1-g_p)\,[F(.53/.18)+o(1)],\qquad
 F(s)=2e^\gamma/s\quad(1\le s\le3).
$$
Here one applies the ordinary dimension-one upper bound with $g_p$ zero below the large bank. Its required upper sieve-dimension inequality is uniform, since omissions only decrease the inverse Euler product (and at 2 the density vanishes). This is the **model** bound for the specific odd-prefix upper weights; see [Kumchev–Tolev, §4.2, (4.15)–(4.19)](https://prime.sdu.edu.cn/__local/3/07/0F/32C1A34A2B081008AF70E53CBBB_B422122B_9C4B8.pdf) and Friedlander–Iwaniec, *Opera de cribro*, Ch. 12. The rounded-model error at fixed $\xi$ tends to zero with $\Delta$ by §1, relative to the large-bank product. The small upper bank has model ratio $1+O((b/\xi)^{-b/(2\xi)})$ for $b/\xi$ sufficiently large. Finally
$$
 \prod_{p\le z}(1-g_p)
  =(1+o_{P;\kappa}(1))\,\frac{e^{-\gamma}\mathfrak S(P)}{.18L}
$$
uniformly for these genuine $x$, since its primes are $\ge P^{\kappa/2}$; large target factors and omitted tails in the convergent twin product cost $1+o(1)$. Take first $P\to\infty$, then $\Delta\downarrow0$, then $\xi\downarrow0$ with $b$ fixed sufficiently small for §1. Multiplying by the actual reflected prime normalization $L$ proves (1).

For (2) use the same physical weights as the [native quadratic score](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/74d3b0e858f37c871ca0ba6d7b732d0bebaf06b9). Write $F_s=L1_{P^->P^s}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\phi(\log d/L)$, where $\phi(u)=1-u$ for $u\le\theta$, $(1-2u)^2$ for $\theta<u\le .5-\theta$, and zero thereafter. The low band $W=P^{\theta^2}<P^-\le P^{2\theta}$ has score $J_L=F_{2\theta}-F_{\theta^2}+S_L$, while $J_H=\Pi-F_{2\theta}+S_H$; the two correction terms restore genuine semiprimes, with negligible square/target nonunits and log normalization at fixed $\theta$. See §§1–4 of the companion *A signed Dickman rectangle law for the two genuine least factors at a fixed Goldbach target*. We give details of its source extension. For smooth profile $C$ in place of the prime, the finite-rank two-slot convolution BV of [the bounded-rank source transfer](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/bf13a6e1d1a9b35bdea274481e7a9c4e2d26a1b8) applies to the centered progression $(x,dj)=1$ with $x\equiv P\bmod dj$. One genuine polynomial-length prime is the Siegel–Walfisz slot, and the remaining slot products are divisor-bounded; the angular restrictions may first be smoothly separated or handled by fixed-rank prime boxes for the piecewise polynomial scores below. With $s=\theta^2$ take $d\le P^{.5-\theta}$, sieve level $P^{\theta/2}$; with $s=2\theta$, take $H=\log(1/\theta)$, $d\le P^{.5-H\theta}$ and level $P^{H\theta/2}$. The total level always has a fixed square-root power gap. The small sieve is upper/lower sandwiched according to the real signed Möbius coefficient. The normalized model multiplier is
$$
 C_s=(e^{-\gamma}/s)\int_{[0,\infty)}\phi(su)\,d\rho(u),
$$
including the atom at 0 of the extended cumulative Dickman function $\rho$ ($0$ on the negative line). Excluded source primes or target-unit omissions cost $o_{P;\kappa,\theta}(1)$; the absolute rough harmonic sum is $O(1/s)$, so the sieve's error divided by $X_P$ is $O(m_C\,s^{-2}u^{-u/2})+o_{P;\theta,\kappa}(1)$, with $u=1/(2\theta)$ or $H/4$. The last expression goes to zero with $\theta$. Integration by parts gives $C_{\theta^2}\to1$ and $C_{2\theta}\to4$: at $s=\theta^2$ the non-linear patch starts at $u=1/\theta$, while for $s=2\theta$ the signed measure has no density on $(0,1)$ so one uses the quadratic throughout its nonzero continuous support. Use $\int_0^\infty\rho=e^\gamma$; the truncated signed upper endpoint costs $O(s^{-1}\rho((.5-H\theta)/s))$.

To restore that endpoint physically put $P^{.5-H\theta}<d\le P^{.5-\theta}$, so $|\phi|\ll H^2\theta^2$. For each rough target-unit $d$, the count of $b$ with $P^-(b)>P^s$ and $P-db$ in the source is bounded above by its roughness at $\kappa'=\min(.01,\kappa/2)$. Sieve both forms up to $P^s$ and then just the second up to $P^{\kappa'}$ (for small $\theta$), using independent nonnegative upper sums of level $P^{.08}$ for each disjoint bank. CRT floors cost $P^{.16+o(1)}$ per $d$. At $p\mid d$ the possible Euler correction is bounded by $1+O(1/p)$ and $d$ is $P^s$-rough; at target primes the two roots coalesce, producing $\mathfrak S(P)$ as in the companion. The bound is $\ll \mathfrak S(P)P/(d\,s\kappa' L^2)+O(P^{.16+o(1)})$. The rough harmonic of $d$ on this exponent band is $O(H)$ in limsup, by the limiting Buchstab harmonic measure with density $\omega\le1$ in $\log d/(sL)$. This costs $O_\kappa(H^3\theta)X_P$. For $\langle C,S_H\rangle$ the corrected semiprime $h p$ has $h$ in the square-root endpoint band and normalized correction $\ll\theta^2$; the genuine terminal prime and the opposite $P^{\kappa'}$-rough form give the same elementary two-form upper for fixed $h$, costing $O_\kappa(\theta^3)X_P$ by the harmonic sum over these $h$. On $S_L$ the small tag $P^{\theta^2}<h\le P^\theta$ has relative weight $\ll \log h/L$, costing $O_\kappa(\theta)X_P$. This proves (2) and also
$$
 \langle C,J_L\rangle/X_P=3m_C+o_*(1).                                  \tag{3}
$$
For Jordan weights where no arbitrary-log estimate is claimed, bracket by smooth upper and lower approximants. The positive upper-minus-lower source controls the physical prime by (1), the signed sieve interior by the separate signed computation for smooth majorants **only for smooth inputs**. An arbitrary nonsmooth interior discrepancy cannot be bracketed against a signed multiplier; instead use fixed-$\theta$ approximation with accuracy chosen after $\theta$, as detailed next.

At fixed $\theta$, on rough support $|F_s|/L\ll_\theta1$ by the divisor-count bound. For a positive smooth upper envelope $E$ of the source-approximation error, the true joint mass against $L1_{P^->P^s}$ is $\ll_\theta m_E X_P+o_{P;\theta,E}(X_P)$: use ordinary centered convolution BV of the smooth $E$ and an upper sieve of small fixed polynomial level, with parameter $>2$. The same holds for the other correction scores which are bounded on that support, and the prime term obeys (1). Choose the approximation so $m_E$ tends to zero first with $\theta$ held fixed; the explicit sieve/shoulder bounds above persist with original sup norm and $m_C$. Thus (2), (3) do not use signed monotonicity.

## 4. Complete rank two and rank three, including their true small-factor ends

Let $J_{H,k}=J_H1_{\Omega=k}$ on the actual squarefree support. Set
$$ I_2=2,\qquad I_3=4\log2-\pi^2/6. $$
For $k=2,3$ the **full ranks**, not only a chosen angular box, satisfy
$$
 \langle J_{H,k},J_L\rangle/X_P=-3I_k+o_*(1),\qquad
 \liminf_{\theta\downarrow0}\liminf_{P\to\infty}\langle J_{H,k},J_H\rangle/X_P
 \ \ge\ (12/53)I_k.                                                       \tag{4}
$$
In particular the two ranks supply $>0.70X_P$ in one ordered high–high orientation for small enough $\theta$ and then large enough $P$. In the high-by-low channel, subtracting them from the total $-9X_P+o_*(X_P)$ gives a **positive** mass $(12\log2-\pi^2/2-3)X_P+o_*(X_P)$ on the residual high-side ranks $\ge4$. These results do not bound the weighted prime deficit for other signed configurations.

Here are details of the endpoints in (4). On any fixed compact angular piece, bounded away from the coordinate faces, use $-L$ times the ideal alternating coefficient $A_k(t)=\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{|S|}(1-2\sum_{i\in S}t_i)_+^2$. The physical high $J_{H,k}$ differs by $\ll_k\theta^2 L+O_{k}(1)$ there since only the $D$ cutoff could discard nonzero terms, which have value $O(\theta^2)$; the rank-two genuine correction restores the ideal response. The absolute means of the signed difference are $O_\kappa(\theta^2)$. To test these signed differences, handle their positive and negative parts: use (1) for the prime; the interior signed-sieve model/error and absolute shoulder of §3 apply also to these fixed-$\theta$ piecewise continuous bounded-rank sources, with the latter proportional to their sup norm. Thus it is enough on these compact pieces to use the actual nonnegative source $L A_k$ below with (2), (3). No pointwise inequality between two signed scores is used.

To remove the angular cutoff take $\kappa\in(0,.005)$ arbitrarily small but fixed next to the outer $\theta$ limit. On $2\theta<t=\log P^-(x)/L<\kappa$, the real normalized score has magnitude $O_k(t)$: pair all subsets across the smallest prime, use Lipschitz continuity of $g$ and a total endpoint jump $O_k(\theta^2)$, with the fixed-cutoff log normalization absorbed as $P\to\infty$. There are no small-patch divisors in this high source. The absolute ordinary mean on these ends is $O_k(\int_0^\kappa (1+|\log t|)^{k-2}dt)+o_{P;\theta}(1)$, by the prime simplex or by opening the first $k-1$ primes (the largest has exponent $\ge1/k+o(1)$). The same mass controls the actual prime by (1) applied with cutoff $2\theta$ fixed first. Against each $F_s$ the signed interior for either sign-part gives $C_s$ times its ordinary mean, up to the errors of §3 **times that absolute mean**. One can first use smooth upper/lower approximants at fixed $\theta$ as in the last paragraph of §3. The source supremum is bounded independently of $\theta$.

For the actual $s=2\theta$ shoulder we do not apply a bound with an uncontrolled $\kappa'=2\theta$. Instead split $t$ into geometric intervals $T<t\le2T$, $T\ge 2\theta$ (including the last partial bin). Open the actual least prime $h$ of the source, bounding its score by $O_k(LT)$. With $h\mid x,d\mid P-x$, $x/h$ is $P^T$-rough and $(P-x)/d$ is $P^s$-rough. Only $(h,d)=1$ and target units can contribute. The free CRT variable has length $\asymp P/(hd)$; each of its two affine forms has one excluded residue away from $hdP$, with possible root collision only at a target prime. Apply the two independent upper banks as in §3, first both forms up to the lower cutoff, then the remaining form to the higher. Their combined floor cost $P^{.16+o(1)}$ for each $h,d$ sums to a power saving since $d\le\sqrt P,\ h\le P^\kappa$. Their main is $\ll \mathfrak S(P)P/(hdTsL^2)$, uniformly $2\theta\le T\le\kappa$, since the local changes for $h,d$ cost $1+o_{P;\theta}(1)$. Now $\sum_{h\in{\rm bin}}1/h\ll1$, the $T$ in the score cancels the $1/T$, and there are $O(1+|\log\theta|)$ bins. With the rough harmonic bound $O(H)$ for $d$, the normalized shoulder is $O_k(H^4\theta)=o(1)$.

The $S_L,S_H$ corrections tested on these small ends likewise cost $o_*(X_P)$. Open $h$ as above and the actual semiprime tag $p$ on the opposite side; its remaining cofactor is prime, hence rough to $P^{.01}$. The same upper banks on the quotient forms now give $\ll \mathfrak S(P)P/(hpT L^2)+O(P^{.16+o(1)})$; this holds also for balanced $p\le\sqrt P$, since $h\le P^\kappa$. The source weight $T$ cancels the $1/T$; the $p$-weights have harmonic sum $O(\theta)$ (the two sizes in §3), hence total $O_k(H\theta)$. This leaves only the ordinary tail $O_k(\kappa(1+|\log\kappa|)^{k-2})$ in the ordered bound. Send $\kappa\downarrow0$ after $\theta\downarrow0$.

Finally $A_2=4t(1-t)$ at the smaller prime; against $dt/[t(1-t)]$ this has mass $2$. For rank three if one coordinate exceeds $1/2$ and the others are $u,v$, $A_3=8uv$. When all three are $\le1/2$, $A_3=2-4\sum t_i^2\ge0$ (the maximum of the sum of squares on this truncated simplex is $1/2$). The one-large part of the angular mean is $4\int_{u+v<1/2}du\,dv/(1-u-v)=4\log2-2$. On the remaining triangle, $A_3/(tuv)=-2/(tuv)+8(1/t+1/u+1/v)$, and $1/(tuv)=1/(tu)+1/(uv)+1/(tv)$. Each integral of $1/t$ on this domain is $1/2$, whereas that of $1/(tu)$ is $\int_0^{1/2}\log((1/2)/(1/2-t))dt/t=\pi^2/6$. Including $1/6$ for the symmetric ordering gives $2-\pi^2/6$. This proves the asserted masses and (4).

**Scope and comparisons.** The result is not a new upper bound on the number of true Goldbach pairs; Lichtman proves stronger direct bounds for prime sources. The uniform assertion here is for localized genuine product-of-primes sources of every fixed rank and acts on a different, signed composite score. Slava Chan's November 2026 [explicit Goldbach upper sieve](https://aixiv.science/pdf/aixiv.261111.000001v1.0.pdf) Theorem 2.1, (12)–(14), claims a simultaneous positive majorant for any fixed finite collection of smooth bounded-rank labelled products of genuine primes in fixed positive exponent intervals with smooth geometry. Its (5) uses $K=6.7617368$ in $C_P$ normalization, corresponding to $K/2=3.3808684$ here after the unit factors. This is stronger than $200/53$ on the overlapping smooth class if correct. We did not independently audit or import Chan's theorem or its numerical sieves. No priority is claimed for (1); the point here is the transparent classical-input derivation and its use for the true signed upper/lower least-factor channels including their physical face tails. No shifted-prime factorization asymptotic at a fixed target is used. The only nontrivial over-root estimate above is the established small-group factor packing and dispersion of Lichtman and the two-large-free-variable estimate of Maynard. The sign in (2) is one-sided, and no lower bound is obtained for the total signed Goldbach remainder.
