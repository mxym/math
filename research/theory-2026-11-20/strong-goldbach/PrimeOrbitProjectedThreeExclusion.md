# An unlimited seed power cannot support a six-prime orbit whose moving-factor counts are all at most three

Fix an odd prime p and a finite prime set F. On any sequence of even N tending to infinity, coprime to p, with log(N/N_F)=o(log N), consider six distinct odd prime units x_0=p,x_1,…,x_5 in one strongly connected, factor-closed, all-composite component. Suppose the five nonseeds are at least N^δ for some fixed δ>0. Then eventually at least one complement N−x_i has **at least four nonseed prime factors, counted with multiplicity**. Arbitrary powers of p are permitted in every complement. Consequently the least nonseed must be strictly smaller than N^(1/4) for all sufficiently large such centers. The strict conclusion also holds without initially assuming a lower bound N^δ: otherwise take the countersequence with every nonseed at least N^(1/4). The nonterminal alternative in [the six-label fork](PrimeOrbitSixLabelFork.md) already has subpower nonseeds, so the latter conclusion applies as well to a forward orbit of size exactly six from p whose complements are all composite. There is no bound on the size of arbitrary closures or a Goldbach consequence without the orbit hypotheses.

## Necessary tests independent of powers of the seed

Suppose otherwise on an infinite countersequence. Use [the subpower seed-mass theorem](PrimeOrbitSixSubpowerSeedMass.md), [the zero-algebra theorem](PrimeOrbitSlowCurveZeroAlgebra.md), [the residue sign theorem](PrimeOrbitSlowCurveEtaleFiber.md), [the seed-prime collision test](PrimeOrbitRamifiedSeedList.md), and the [half-above-root theorem](PrimeOrbitHalfAboveRoot.md). Write A′_ij=v_{x_j}(N−x_i) for six inputs 0,…,5, five columns 1,…,5; write E for the five nonseed columns of I_6 and B′=E−A′. The finite reduced charts must satisfy:
* A′ has rank 5 and A′w=1 with rational 0<w_i<1. Its rows have nonnegative entries and sums 2 or 3: the upper bound is exactly the branch hypothesis, and fewer than two cannot have weight one. No loop is allowed since gcd(x_i,N)=1. At least three nonseed weights are ≤1/2: the two-retained corank bound and half-above-root exclusion forbid three or more labels strictly exceeding sqrt N on this sequence. Counting limits ≤1/2 only is necessary, not asserted sufficient.
* B′ has rank 4; its kernel is generated by a primitive integer v with all 5 entries nonzero and distinct (put v_0=0). Both signs appear at least twice: extend v by the zero seed to a right eigenvector of the full nonnegative irreducible factor matrix, with eigenvalue 1 below its Perron value ≥2. Both signs appear, and every sign support contains a directed cycle, because at any coordinate of that sign the row sum needs at least one child of the same sign. There are no loops. The largest physical prime has a unique parent: every smaller parent is the same positive residue N mod that prime. Thus some nonseed column of A′ has just one nonzero entry.
* All short monomials u in nonnegative integer vectors with u·w<1 have distinct indices u·v. Write C for a rational row basis of ker(B′ᵀ), interpreted as two row vectors, d for the common reduced denominator of w and l for a primitive left-null vector of A′. Every nonzero c in row span C has index span at least d on its support, and each sign support size at least two (the same cycle argument for incoming edges in a left eigenvector). The zero-fiber sign equation is B′s=1 over F₂, for five unknowns.
* Ch=C diag(v_0,…,v_5) h=0 defines a projective line of nonzero-coordinate solutions when applicable (if any coordinate is identically zero the chart is impossible). The seed test requires on this line some projective h with no zero coordinate satisfying h^l=1 and
      (product over j>0 of h_j^(A′_0j))/h_0^k=−p^(k−1),     k=sum_j A′_0j.
  The first equation is homogeneous since sum l=0. These are necessary by the repeated-prime and rational lifting arguments in the cited seed-prime theorem. Neither equation contains the multiplicities of p.

## Two complete independent generators

Run, from a directory with the two sources, with Node and Python+SymPy:

    node projected3-charts.js 3 v | python3 -O projected3-exact.py
    node projected3-charts.js 3 w | python3 -O projected3-exact.py

The **v-first generator** enumerates the seed row and any three other independent rows of B′ via the first three nonseed labels: the seed row is nonzero and extends to a four-row basis, and before fixing any label order those chosen labels can be permuted into positions 1–3 along with their columns. Exterior minors determine the primitive null vector. Normalize by sign and permutation, then rebuild all six rows independently from every respective loopless composition of size two or three satisfying A′_i v=v_i. This normalization concerns only discovery of the vector; rebuilding imposes no artificial basis assumption in the sorted labeling. The output has 5,824 candidate normalized vectors, 353 with possibilities for all six rows, 3,329 raw charts, and 2,366 with some unique-parent column.

The **w-first generator** instead enumerates all unordered subsets of five physically distinct independent rows from the 50 unrestricted compositions of size two or three in five entries. These must include one basis of any rank-five A′. The orthogonal complement of the five augmented rows (r,−1) gives (w,1). Positive reduced weight multisets with at least three entries ≤1/2 give 260 possibilities, of which 259 allow all six rows. Independently enumerate assignments of any loopless weight-one row to each input. Follow the rank of B′ using the wedge product of the current basis; upon reaching rank four, compute its primitive kernel and require it annihilate every remaining row. Paths never reaching rank four cannot survive. This method has no v-first assumption on any prescribed basis rows. Vector sorting and orientation are now used only on the complete chart, permuting input and output labels together. After unique-parent and signed-kernel checks the second generator outputs 224 canonical charts (a few have rank-deficient A′ even though some other basis originally supplied that weight). The wedge calculations in both algorithms use exact integers: every partial row entry has absolute value ≤3 and the largest evaluated minors have order six, bounded, with their partial sums, by 6!·3^6, far below 2^53. No numerical rank tolerance is used.

The independent starting lists agree exactly after the weight test, and produce the same SHA-256 of the sorted 41 terminal chart strings including the final newline:
    
    d993ab8731b6025633e32895a9207a86c740ef0cf88dea94490a1c0778523a41

Counts after weight, short-injectivity, collision-line and tested index spans: 214,43,43,41 respectively. In the index-span step only vectors cancelling each chosen coordinate are inspected; all are necessary, even without claiming that checking them suffices. For each remaining chart, construct line coordinates H_i=u_i x+t_i. Strip all roots of its first binary product equation at which any H_i is zero. At each rational irreducible factor g of the resulting affine equation, invert H_0^k modulo g and keep the value of the second ratio only if its remainder is a constant. A value equal to a rational integer at any conjugate is constant throughout Q[x]/g. Check the point at infinity separately using the six leading coefficients. Keep only negative odd-prime (k−1)st powers. This is exact over Q, without algebraic approximations.

There are two special cases, given as A′ rows in input order:
1. The first product is identically one for 01110,02010,10100,01001,00300,00210, with v=(−5,−4,1,3,5). This case is **not** rejected on the assumption a constant function has no root: c=(−1,1,1,0,0,0) is an actual left-null vector of B′, with negative support a singleton. It fails the mandatory sign-support criterion; the binary sign equations also fail.
2. The only rational value passing the odd-prime test occurs for p=3, 00110,02000,10001,01010,00101,00020, v=(−4,−2,−1,1,2). The rows 0,4,5 of B′ add to zero over F₂, whereas their three right-hand sides add to one. Thus this apparent prime candidate is impossible, independent of whether physical primes could realize it.

There are no surviving charts. The projective-line collision result used here precedes the pencil classification: the stronger geometrical pencil theory is not needed to exclude this entire small **projected** arity class. A chart with four or more moving factors in a row remains outside this particular certificate.
