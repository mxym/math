# Goldbach written research, November 24

These results concern scoped pieces of the binary Goldbach problem. They neither prove nor refute the conjecture.

## Where to start

- **Proven in a square-root-scale prime window:** [P4 with squarefree complement](PrimeOppositeSquareRootP4.md), also with [exact prescribed small factors](PrimeOppositeSquareRootPlantedP4.md) in the quotient. The separate [sub-square-root and short-window theorem](PrimeOppositeSubSquareRootShortP4.md) gives squarefree P4 at prime scale $N^{.49}$ and in every width-$N^{.495}$ window near $\sqrt N$, also with an [exact planted profile in those two windows](PrimeOppositePlantedSubSquareRootShortP4.md). The [full asymmetric sieve kernel](PrimeOppositeExactAsymmetricP4Kernel.md) improves those two unplanted exponents to $.4832$ and $.4916$ with a slightly weaker least-factor floor, also [with exact prescribed small factors in the new windows](PrimeOppositeExactKernelPlantedP4.md). A [single middle-moment estimate](PrimeOppositeSquareRootP3Target.md) would yield P3 and remains open here.
- **Genuine square-root-window geometry:** the [three-factor angular separation](PrimeOppositeThreeFactorAngularSeparation.md) separates adjacent factor scales on a surviving rough family; [the two-level dyadic construction](DyadicTwoLevelPlantedDictionary.md) gives a further conditional branch in the actual factor graph. The [largest-label supply](PrimeOppositePlantedLargestLabelSupply.md) forces polynomially many actual first-step labels across these roots, [also in both newer windows](PrimeOppositeExactKernelLargestLabels.md).
- **Fixed targets and the signed prime score:** [a separately certified positive bilateral block](CQCompactBilateralFavorable.md) and [all true remaining negative partners](CQCompleteNegativeReceiver.md) can be booked without overlap; the latter replaces the earlier rank-five sub-block. A [disjoint reflected increment](CQReflectedBothYYIncrement.md) raises the both-negative contribution to $.06152M$ without double counting. A [pointwise obstruction](CQPointwiseLowerPriceBarrier.md) rules out paying the remaining $Y$ debit using only the certified lower prices; it does not rule out extra compensation in the true moments. The signed exterior still prevents a whole-target lower bound.
- **Rough mixed parity:** [the reduced bilateral low-rank score](SmallRadicalG23CoreBilateral.md) leaves a [dense, genuinely coprime dispersion estimate](SmallRadicalWeightedCoprimeDispersion.md). The [weighted dispersion is small for almost all ordinary targets](G23AverageWeightedDispersion.md), but the estimate does not reach fixed targets or the thin small-radical family. Major arcs and some named minor arcs are controlled below. A [nonmultiplicative sign model](SmallRadicalCoprimeCliqueObstruction.md) shows why one-coordinate controls do not settle the actual two-slope problem.

## Factor-closed components

- [All six-label components](EACSixFullStrictPowerSaving.md): at most $X^{3/4-\epsilon+o(1)}$ component-bearing centers up to $X$ with [the explicit choice](EACSixRationalSaving.md) $\epsilon=1/401$. It strengthens the [earlier three-quarter count](EACSixThreeQuarter.md), with a [paper proof of the lone-cube step](EACSixLoneCubePaper.md) and a separate chart replay.
- [Both exponent presentations singular](EACSixTwoThirdsProduct.md): the two least labels obey $pq\le N^{2/3+o(1)}$. This yields the $X^{2/3+o(1)}$ count in corank one, not in corank two. It supersedes the [seven-tenths intermediate estimate](EACSixSevenTenthsProduct.md).
- [Corank two with a pure fourth-power row](EACSixCorankTwoPureFourthSaving.md): a strict but not explicit power saving applies in this subclass.
- [All rows semiprime](EACSixSemiprimeSquareRoot.md): at most $X^{1/2+o(1)}$ centers in this narrower class.

- [Seven labels and semiprime rows](EACSevenSemiprimeQuarter.md): exactly one bipartite cofactor component, giving a quarter-power least-label bound; arbitrary seven-label arity has the weaker cube-root restriction.
- [Eight-label semiprime quarter-power minimum](EACEightSemiprimeQuarter.md): true product gaps and an equal-factor-sum reflection extend the pointwise minimum/maximum restrictions to eight labels and give exponent-matrix nullity at most two; no claim on the size of a terminal component.

- [Growing seed closures](SmallRadicalGrowingSeedRank.md), with [Kummer sharpening](SmallRadicalGrowingSeedKummer.md): at a small-radical target, bounded simultaneous all-composite orbits must contain many distinct singular cyclic components. A [feedback-depth variant](SmallRadicalFeedbackDepthRank.md) replaces the total size bound by a retained-size times acyclic-depth budget, sharpened further using [actual branching](SmallRadicalEffectiveBranching.md). No assertion is made that those components are terminal or that targets without a pair exist.

- [One-side-prime cycles](SmallRadicalOneSidePrimeCycle.md): genuine cycles using a single extra prime for all cofactors are excluded at small radical. A [high-chain scale theorem](HighFactorChainScaleCap.md) bounds narrow or small-cofactor chains, with an exact cap above $N/7$ when $7\nmid N$.

- [Prime-opposite entries](PrimeOppositeHighEntryDichotomy.md): a true prime sieve near the square root supplies many genuine entry rows, but long high-factor paths require many labels with no low prime parent.
- [Planted largest-label supply](PrimeOppositePlantedLargestLabelSupply.md): genuine square-root P4 rows with prescribed profiles force many distinct largest rough factors above $N^{.2502}$; a low-band half supplies positive reciprocal mass. The [smaller-window transfers](PrimeOppositeExactKernelLargestLabels.md) preserve this with their own exponents and count margins.
- [Vertex-disjoint genuine depth-two paths](PrimeFactorDisjointDepthTwoPaths.md): a divisor identity makes non-Goldbach mutual parent edges negligible at a quarter-plus child exponent, and a uniform prime-dictionary Rankin bound makes greedy disjoint paths possible. A nontrivial admissible profile exists for every large even target, giving $\gg L^{1/(1-.2502)}/\log L$ pairwise vertex-disjoint actual factor paths and many second-label primes, not a terminal-component bound. In the smaller $N^{.4832}$ prime window, selecting one of the top two genuine factors in the low-largest branch removes the previous caveat and gives unconditional disjoint depth-two paths there too, without requiring that every first edge be largest.
- [Polynomial matching in the genuine low-largest branch](PrimeFactorLargestFirstLowHarmonicPaths.md): five actual CRT factors in a Brun–Titchmarsh moment show the positive reciprocal cloud cannot be supported by smooth second complements; derive $\gg N^{.05}$ disjoint paths preserving the true largest first factor. The high-label alternative is not covered.
- [Arbitrary fixed depth in decreasing scales](PrimeFactorMultiscaleDisjointPaths.md): Bombieri–Vinogradov and Hall give $(1+o(1))N^{a_h}/(a_h\log N)$ disjoint proper-divisor paths when $a_0=1/2$ and $a_i<a_{i-1}/2$. This permits any divisor, so it is a separate unconditional graph fact, not a stronger largest-factor theorem.
- [Almost-all induced paths in near-equal scales](PrimeFactorAlmostAllInducedPaths.md): direct additive large-sieve means let all (h+1) layers retain the same polynomial exponent for almost all targets. The induced-path deletion and exact child valuation one also apply to the existing fixed-target path theorem in its smaller-scale ranges.
- [Directed planted paths to 3](DyadicPlantedDirectedBasinThree.md): the actual two-level high branch supplies many distinct labels that reach vertex 3. A different mod-3 profile gives the all-odd-exponent result and the genuine even-exponent rank-or-directed-label dichotomy. No terminal-component size is claimed.

- [Small low-factor dictionary](LowDictionaryHighExcursionDichotomy.md): under the stated local parameters, if the dictionary does not expand, direct telescoping forces a very long high excursion from each seed individually.

- [Single actual prime entrance](RealSinglePrimeSeedAnchor.md): a prime of fixed polylogarithmic size can reach any chosen small seed set on its very first reflected factor row.
