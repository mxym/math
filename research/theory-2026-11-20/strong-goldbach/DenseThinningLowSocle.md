# Low-weight socles survive density-one genuine-prime thinning at infinitely many cutoffs

This is a negative control, not an assertion about positive depth or Goldbach for the unthinned primes. There is ONE fixed subset A of the genuine odd primes, of relative natural density one, containing 3, such that for infinitely many even multiples of six N, A has no pair of sum N and its binary-move ring restricted to A∩[1,s] has a nonzero cubic annihilated by its whole homogeneous maximal ideal, of weight **exactly the cutoff** s=N+3. The field is arbitrary.

Take N_j=6·2^(2^j) for j≥2. For every genuine prime pair a+b=N_j, remove its larger endpoint, counting a=b once. All deletions at stage j are in [N_j/2,N_j), never 3. The classical two-linear-form upper-bound sieve bounds their number by O(S(N_j)N_j/log²N_j), with S the usual Goldbach singular factor (here uniformly bounded since the prime divisors of N_j are just 2,3). Thus their log mass is O(N_j/log N_j). The intervals are disjoint and the total of all earlier stages is negligible relative to the next stage (N_(j+1)=N_j²/6). For X between N_j/2 and N_(j+1)/2 only stages ≤j contribute and their total log mass is o(N_j)=o(X), uniformly there. Hence E=P_odd∖A has global log mass o(X) and, by dyadic summation, relative prime density zero. In particular at EVERY chosen s_j the deleted central log mass is o(s_j), not just along some favorable subsequence. The actual deletion prevents all A-pairs of each sum N_j forever. No assertion that there was a genuine-prime pair to begin with is needed.

Take any such s and the two central sign sectors modulo 3 in I=[s/8,5s/8]. The omitted o(s) log mass does not destroy the one-giant-per-sign conclusion in the qualitative form needed here. In the cut argument [CubicGiant.md](CubicGiant.md) §§2–4, let C,D partition the RETAINED prime labels in one sector into unions of components, each macroscopically sized. Their mixed true count with retained third weight vanishes. Restoring deleted third primes from E∩I contributes o(s²): on the cyclic group, Hölder with three exponents 3 and Green–Tao's ℓ³ restriction bound give O(s² sqrt(α_C α_D α_E))=o(s²), where α_K=|K| log s/s. For fixed small-prime modulus the sum of endpoint limit measures is still the full sector PNT measure because missing log mass is o(s). The unchanged spectral-gap model lower bound then contradicts this small mixed count after TT U² replacement. If the largest component missed a fixed fraction, accumulating components would produce such a partition. Each retained-prime giant therefore misses only o(s) log mass in its sector. No logarithmic-rate stability is asserted.

Use both bands and the fixed-3 chart of [CubicPrefixSocle.md](CubicPrefixSocle.md), for each RETAINED t≤s. For each of the distinct moving forms L (all eight or nine of them, not just the endpoint labels), disallow every deleted prime as its value. Also disallow non-giant central labels at each of the six endpoint forms. The total forbidden logarithmic mass at each form is o(s). Here the simple O(s³ log^9 s) slice bound would not suffice. Instead use [NineProgressionSocle.md](NineProgressionSocle.md)'s joined two-copy argument: keep one L coordinate, give each copy three otherwise independent coordinates. Every other form is nonconstant in its copy-specific block, so the joined list is pairwise nonproportional; the uniformly bounded seven-variable TT upper bound controls the sum of squared conditional slice weights by O(s⁷) with logarithmic measure on the common prime. Cauchy–Schwarz loses at most sqrt(o(s)·O(s⁷))=o(s⁴) weighted tuples. This applies to **all** moving intermediate prime labels as well as the endpoints. Constants do not depend on the retained t or band: all offsets are O(s) and the finite set of linear parts is fixed. The fixed pivot 3 is retained. Every surviving t thus kills the same difference f of the two retained giant cubics in this complete prefix of A. Since s−3=N has no A-pair, no triple of total s contains 3 and the two pure sectors do not meet. Therefore f≠0, its annihilator is exactly the homogeneous maximal ideal, and f²=0.

This disproves a proposed eventual prohibition of *low-weight* socle cubics based just on density-one genuine-prime support, its prime-pattern estimates stable under o(1) relative deletions, or its robust Freiman rigidity. It does not disprove that prohibition for the actual unthinned primes. The earlier [cutoff 317 example](PrimePrefix317.md) uses the actual unthinned primes but its cubic weight exceeds its cutoff. Neither example supplies a counterexample to binary Goldbach.

The underlying density obstacle for binary sumsets is related to [Alsetri–Shao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18576); the point of the present construction is the simultaneous cubic socle exactly at its cutoff, not priority for constructing relatively dense subsets of primes with omitted sums.
