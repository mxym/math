# A mutual factor pair with a semiprime upper row forces an isolated prime

Use the prime-factor graph of an even center N: p points to the prime factors of N−p. Work inside a nontrivial exceptional autonomous component (EAC) as in [Bożek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900); the odd prime labels are coprime to N, all complements are composite and factor entirely over the component. The *reciprocal graph* retains only pairs of distinct labels joined in both directions, as simple undirected edges.

**Local theorem, all other row multiplicities unrestricted.** For every reciprocal edge p<q such that N−q is semiprime, the factor t=(N−q)/p is a prime label with t>√N, isolated in the reciprocal graph. Different such edges give different t.

Indeed, since p | N−q and q | N−p, we have N=p+q+hpq for an integer h. Every label is at most N/3, because it occurs in an odd composite complement. Thus N−p−q>0 and h is positive. The parity of N makes h even. Thus t=1+hq>q. As p<q<t and N=q+pt, we have N<t² since t(t−p)>q. Any smaller mutual neighbor of t must be congruent to N modulo t and hence must equal q, the least nonnegative residue. But N−t≡p−1 (mod q) is not divisible by q. Nor can t have a larger mutual neighbor u: the same formula would give N=t+u+h′tu>t². This proves isolation. From t the original q=N mod t and then p=(N−q)/t are uniquely determined.

**Application when every row is semiprime.** If k is the number of labels and R the number of reciprocal undirected edges, then 5R≤2k. In detail, each vertex has reciprocal degree ≤2. At a vertex z of degree 2, its two distinct mutual neighbors v,w account for its entire row N−z=vw. Since v≡w≡N (mod z), we get N≡N² (mod z) and hence N≡1 (mod z). Its neighbors v,w are therefore both larger than z. No two degree-2 vertices can be adjacent, since then each would be smaller than the other. Every nontrivial reciprocal component is thus one edge or a two-edge star. Each uses at least 3/2 as many vertices as edges. The local theorem now supplies another R distinct vertices, all isolated, so k≥(3/2)R+R as claimed. Neither the existence of any all-semiprime EAC nor its exclusion follows. No Goldbach exception is asserted.

This works without the [full reciprocity hypothesis](EACReciprocalFive.md) and is different from the [semiprime cofactor-matrix singularity test](EACSemiprimeStar.md). The [2026 terminal-island record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460) considers a related graph under global counterexample hypotheses; its latest full text could not be checked. The bound here is only an incidence restriction for the stated genuine component.
