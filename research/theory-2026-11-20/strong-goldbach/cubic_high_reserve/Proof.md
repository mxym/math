# A positive marked subregion of the cubic high–high Goldbach remainder

Both legs here range over **all** eligible ranks. An actual prime progression estimate is necessary to get the sign even though the signed pair weight itself vanishes at a prime. On the underlying true-prime test there is a negative first-order response; after subtracting the actual rough divisor source this becomes positive in the high–high composite region. We count a symmetric union of physical events without double-counting and resolve its singleton and bulk. The rest of the region is not bounded from below.

Write $P$ for a prescribed large even integer, $L=\log P$, $I=[P/3,2P/3]$, $X=|I|\mathfrak S(P)$, with $\mathfrak S(P)=2\prod_{p>2}(1-1/(p-1)^2)\prod_{2<p\mid P}(p-1)/(p-2)$. All pair sums are ordered on $x+y=P$, $x\in I$, $(x,P)=1$. Let $J=J_3$ be the [original cubic score](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/74d3b0e858f37c871ca0ba6d7b732d0bebaf06b9): it vanishes on primes, nonsquarefree inputs and integers not rough to $P^{\theta^2}$. On squarefree rough composites of rank $\le3$ it is the ideal response
$$ -(\log n)\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)(1-2\log d/\log n)_+^3; $$
on higher ranks it uses $1-\log d/\log n$ for $d\le P^\theta$, the cubic for $P^\theta<d\le P^{1/2-\theta}$, and $0$ after that. Its calibration is $G(P)=X+\langle J,J\rangle+o_\theta(X)$ in ordered limits, where $G$ counts true central prime pairs with their logarithms and $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ denotes reflection.

Fix $2<a<b$, then choose a sufficiently small $\delta>0$. Write $s=2\delta$, $\lambda=(b-a)\delta$, $H_s(n)=1_{P^-(n)>P^s}$ and $N(n)=\#\{r\mid n:r\ {\rm prime},\ P^{a\delta}<r\le P^{b\delta}\}$. Put $K=N(x)+N(y)$. On the actual high–high sector ($H_s(x)H_s(y)=1$), with the **same cubic score** on both sides,
$$
\begin{aligned}
 X^{-1}\!\!\sum_{\substack{\rm high\!-\!high\\ N(y)\ge1}}\!J(x)J(y)
 &=120\lambda+o(\delta),\\
 X^{-1}\!\!\sum_{\substack{\rm high\!-\!high\\ K\ge1}}\!J(x)J(y)
 &=240\lambda+o(\delta),\\
 X^{-1}\!\!\sum_{\substack{\rm high\!-\!high\\ K=1}}\!J(x)J(y)
 &=240\lambda+o(\delta),\qquad
 X^{-1}\!\!\sum_{\substack{\rm high\!-\!high\\ K\ge2}}\!J(x)J(y)=o(\delta).
\end{aligned}                                                             \tag{1}
$$
Here $o(\delta)$ means that dividing by $\delta$, the iterated absolute limsup as $P\to\infty$, then $\theta\downarrow0$, then $\delta\downarrow0$, is zero. In choices: fix $\delta$ first, take $\theta$ small enough for that $\delta$, then all sufficiently large even $P$. In particular one can keep a fixed positive bank whose mass is larger than the independently chosen error of the cubic Goldbach calibration. This is not a proof that the full remainder is greater than $-X$. Coefficients in the two orientations do not represent two separate unordered populations.

## 1. Fix the physical bank before tuning the score

At fixed $\delta$ define
$$ q(t)=(1-2t)_+^3,\qquad F(n)=L H_s(n)\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)q(\log d/L),\qquad
 \Pi(n)=L1_{n\ {\rm prime}}.$$
In every pair considered here $JH_s$ can be replaced by $\Pi-F$ with error tending to zero in $X$ units in the first two limits. Indeed for $\theta<s$ only $d=1$ lies in the linear patch. Any term lost at the physical upper endpoint has weight $O((\theta+L^{-1})^3)$, and every rough squarefree input has at most $2^{1/s}$ divisors. The own-log changes cost $O_\delta(1)$ in the unnormalized score. These statements hold both for the ideal ranks and the genuinely truncated higher ranks; no generic $O(\theta)$ rank-correction bound is used. The standard upper sieve on $x(P-x)$ counts $O_\delta(X/L^2)$ doubly rough pairs; for squarefree labels the integer remainder is bounded by the number of roots and at target primes the two roots coincide. Rough squareful inputs cost $P^{1-s+o(1)}+P^{1/2+o(1)}$ by summing $P/p^2+1$; physical nonunits likewise cost $P^{1-s+o(1)}$ by marking a prime divisor of $P$. Proper powers and changes of prime logarithms are covered. In particular the same replacements are valid on every subevent in (1). $\Pi$ vanishes on all marked events since the bank is strictly below the central prime scale.

Use names $*$, $0$, $1$, $+$ for respectively unrestricted bank occupancy, exactly zero, exactly one, and positive occupancy. $F_B$ includes the indicated physical event ($F_* = F$); write $F_+=F-F_0$. Tests with $F_0$ sieve out the **entire** bank, not a finite Bonferroni approximation to the number of marks.

## 2. The model multipliers

Write $\mathcal B=[a\delta,b\delta]$, $h=\log(b/a)$ and $d\ell(t)=dt/t$ on $\mathcal B$. Put $T_s(f)=e^{-\gamma}s^{-1}\int f(sv)d\nu(v)$ with $d\nu=\delta_0+\rho'(v)dv$, $\rho$ Dickman's function extended by zero to the negative axis. Let $W$ have compound-Poisson shift law $e^{-h}\sum_{j\ge0}\ell^{*j}/j!$, with the zero-fold convolution the unit atom. These are deterministic model integrals; in particular $EW=\lambda$, $EW^2=\lambda^2+\int_{\mathcal B}t^2d\ell(t)=O_{a,b}(\delta^2)$. Define
$$
 M_* =T_s(q),\quad M_0=E T_s(\tau_W q),\quad
 M_1=\int_{\mathcal B} E T_s(\tau_W(1-\tau_t)q)\,d\ell(t),\quad
 M_+=M_*-M_0,                                                        \tag{2}
$$
where $(\tau_u f)(v)=f(v+u)$. The model has
$$
 M_*=6+O(\delta),\qquad M_+=24\lambda+O(\delta^2),\qquad
 M_1=24\lambda+O(\delta^2).                                          \tag{3}
$$
Indeed for the untruncated polynomial $\widetilde q(v)=(1-2v)^3$,
$$ T_s(\tau_u\widetilde q)=6(1-2u)^2-24s(1-2u)+36s^2.$$
This follows by parts: $\int\rho=e^\gamma$ and the first two moments of $e^{-\gamma}\rho(v)dv$ are $1,3/2$, from $v\rho(v)=\int_{v-1}^v\rho(z)dz$. Replacing $q$ by $\widetilde q$ in (2) costs $O_A(\delta^A)$ for all $A$: truncate $j$ at $\delta^{-1/2}$ using the factorial tail even with $s^{-1}(1+j^3)$, and the remaining total shift is $O(\sqrt\delta)$. Beyond $v\gg1/s$ use factorial decay of $\rho,|\rho'|$. The quadratic formula and the two elementary shift moments prove (3).

For clarity about the arithmetic interpretation of (2), for $B=1$ extract the one actual prime $r$ of the bank, put $R=r$, and open the original Möbius sum first over $1,r$. The remaining true divisor $e$ is $P^s$-rough with no bank primes, weighted by $(1-\tau_t)q(\log e/L)$. Sieve $n/(Re)$ at all primes up to $P^s$ and also the bank except for $r$; the possible $r^2$ costs a power. For $B=0$ use $R=1$ and the same bank avoidance; for $B=*$ use $R=1$ and no bank exclusions at all. In all cases $e\le P^{1/2}$. The harmonic signed measure over all such rough divisors without bank avoidance has cumulative $\rho$ in units of $sL$, by expanding squarefree products and prime Mertens: the exponential of the negative prime harmonic convolution on $[1,\infty)$ satisfies the Dickman delay equation. Deleting the bank from the divisor variable multiplies by its inverse Euler factor, giving the positive convolution exponential in $W$, whereas the **sieve on the physical quotient** contributes $e^{-h}$. Coincidences of two harmonic marks have negligible inverse-square sum at fixed $\delta$. This proves exactly the deterministic model calculation; proofs that the true arithmetic samples admit it follow next. See also the [exclusive occupancy derivation, §§2–3](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/0ff85574).

## 3. The real prime term: ordinary BV with an affordable cubic shoulder

For $B=*,0,1,+$, we claim, with $T=\log(1/\delta)$,
$$ \langle\Pi,F_B\rangle/X=M_B+O_{a,b}(T^4\delta^2)+o_{P;\delta}(1)       \tag{4}$$
for all sufficiently small $\delta$; here and below $o_{P;\delta}(1)$ is uniform in the prescribed even target at fixed $\delta$. It suffices to handle the first three tests. First restrict $e\le P^{.5-T\delta}$. The modulus is $Rej$, where $j\le P^{T\delta/4}$ is a real squarefree upper/lower dimension-one sieve label for the prescribed avoidance set. Totals obey $Rej\le P^{.5-(3T/4-b)\delta}$, below the ordinary Bombieri–Vinogradov rail by a fixed power at fixed sufficiently small $\delta$. One can use maximal BV for the central intervals (or difference at the two endpoints). The coefficient at any modulus is bounded by a fixed divisor power times logarithmic weights. To pass from usual BV to divisor-weighted absolute discrepancy, Cauchy with its arbitrary logarithmic saving and the elementary bound $O(P/q+1+P/\varphi(q))$ for the discrepancy, together with divisor moments, suffices. Source and physical powers removed as in §1. The residue is the actual $P$, and $(Rej,P)=1$. All selected divisors are coprime to $j$, by construction.

The reduced-class density on the sieve labels is $1/(p-1)$ at available primes $p\nmid P$, zero at omitted primes. Select upper or lower sieve according to the actual sign of $\mu(e)q$ or $\mu(e)(1-\tau_t)q$. The relative fundamental-lemma error for the unsigned principal is $O(u^{-u/2})$ with $u=T/(4b)$, by the [dimension-one fundamental lemma](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/superscars-for-arithmetic-point-scatters-ii/3CCB969E058792C1803466AE0EF29136); deleting primes only improves its uniform inverse-product dimension bound. Thus the normalized cost is $O_{a,b}(s^{-2}u^{-u/2})=O_A(\delta^A)$ for every fixed $A$. Here $\sum_{e\le P^{.5},\,P^-(e)>P^s}1/e\ll1/s$, and $\sum_r1/r=O_{a,b}(1)$. The omitted target primes, and changing $\varphi(Re)$ to $Re$, cost $o_{P;\delta}(1)$ since these labels are polynomially rough. The sifted product is $(1+o_{P;\delta}(1))e^{-\gamma}\mathfrak S(P)/(sL)$ times $e^{-h}$ if the bank is sieved, by prime Mertens. The choice $(r^2\nmid y)$ can be restored physically at power cost.

For the true omitted shoulder $P^{.5-T\delta}<e\le P^{.5}$ the absolute coefficient (including its possible positive shift) is $O((T\delta)^3)$. On opening $y=Re n$, the actual prime $P-Re n$ and the $P^s$-rough $n$ are simultaneously sifted. For each target-unit $Re\le P^{.5+b\delta}$ this gives
$$
 \ll \mathfrak S(P)P/(Re\,sL^2)+O(P^{.16+o(1)})
$$
with an absolute implied constant for small $s$: sieve both forms up to $P^s$ by a nonnegative upper beta sum of level $P^{.08}$ and the prime form alone on $P^s<p\le P^{.01}$ by a second such sum of level $P^{.08}$. Their product majors the actual indicators and the two banks are disjoint. CRT floors are at most $2^{\omega(j)}$ per joint label. The common small primes have two forbidden roots except for coincident roots at $p\mid P$ or a missing prime-form root at $p\mid Re$; this yields the displayed singular correction, and any additional factor $\prod_{p\mid Re}(1+O(1/p))=1+o_{P;\delta}(1)$ by roughness. In the second bank there is just one linear-form root unless $p\mid Re$. For the harmonic mass of $e$ in the shoulder,
$$ \limsup_P\sum_{\substack{P^{.5-T\delta}<e\le P^{.5}\\P^-(e)>P^s}}1/e\ll T.$$
Indeed at fixed $\delta$ the limiting positive rough harmonic measure in $\log e/(sL)>1$ has Buchstab density $\omega\le1$, by finite prime-factor expansion (reciprocal repeats negligible) and the Buchstab recursion. Its interval length is $T\delta/s$. Summing over $e,R$ costs $O_{a,b}(T^4\delta^2)X$ including the two $L$ weights. The floors total $O(P^{.66+b\delta+o(1)})$. The corresponding truncated **model** may be restored with the same absolute estimate: unsigned $e$ mass $O(T)$, factor $1/s$, and cubic $O((T\delta)^3)$. In particular the cutoff restoration makes no assumption of over-root BV for the true prime. This proves (4) also for the entire positive-occupancy event by subtraction at zero.


## 4. The rough divisor source: actual paired CRT with both labels

For $B,D\in\{*,0,1,+\}$,
$$ \langle F_B,F_D\rangle/X=M_B M_D+O_{A,a,b}(\delta^A)+o_{P;\delta}(1)       \tag{5}$$
for every fixed $A$ and sufficiently small $\delta$. We spell out the level and geometry. By bilinearity reduce to the first three choices on each leg. Each physical modulus before sieving is $R_i e_i\le P^{.5+b\delta}$; a selected mark and its signed divisor weight are grouped together within the same leg. Discard common primes between these two moduli: on physical unit pairs they are impossible; in the later harmonic product a common prime $>P^s$ costs $o_{P;\delta}(1)$ by the reciprocal-square bound. Each target-unit omission from the outer rough labels is equally negligible there. No artificial restriction is placed on the small-prime sieve at a target divisor.

On the initial progression sieve the union of the two leg-specific quotient divisibilities at the prescribed small and bank primes, with one dimension-two upper/lower combinatorial sieve of level $E=P^{.0002}$. Each side excludes its own selected mark from that sieve; $e_i$ cannot share a sieve prime with its own side. A root at a prime of the opposite modulus, if incompatible, simply does not occur. At a prime $p\mid P$ two available roots coincide, since the corresponding original moduli are target units; assign the coincident event just once. At $p\nmid P$ the two available roots are disjoint. For a fixed squarefree sieve label and choice of legs for its prime factors, the new moduli on the two sides are $R_i e_i j_i$, where $j_1j_2\le E$. Compatibility on the CRT pair is exactly coprimality; incompatible allocations contribute zero. Same-leg weights are independent of the other side for each such allocation; excluding $p\mid P$ in the initial labels and assigning a possibly coincident event at $p\mid P$ uses only fixed information about $P$ and the two sieve alphabets (any selected prime is already target-unit). Choosing sieve endpoints for the signed product does not require factoring its sign across legs: use upper and lower bounds on $\sum c\,1_{\rm sifted}$ by the average of the enclosures times $c$ and their gap times $|c|/2$. Both $c$ and $|c|$ factor. Multiple decompositions into selected mark and divisor only cost a fixed divisor power in the gathered coefficients. Removing $r_i^2$ on the marked side again costs a power physically.

The required **signed integer** progression discrepancy is the one in [the polynomial CRT sieve proof](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/PanCriticalCompositePolynomialSieve.md), with [Bettin–Chandee, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00769), not an asserted beyond-root distribution of primes. Here each side modulus is $\le P^{.5+b\delta+.0002}<P^{.501}$ for all sufficiently small fixed $\delta$. To recall the error: smooth the central interval with log-power transitions and separate dyadic weights. For side lengths $B',D'$ the zero term is the CRT integral divided by the modulus product. The nonzero phase is $e(\pm Ph\overline{b'}/d')$ and the relevant frequency length is $B'D'/P$ times a log power. Retaining the large fixed numerator in the factor $(1+P|h|/(B'D'))^{1/2}$, the two relative BCE exponents at $B'D'\ge P$ are at most $(33\cdot.501-17)/20$ and $(15\cdot.501-8)/8$, both $<-.022$. For product $\le P^{.99}$ use elementary integer CRT with at most one endpoint error per pair; the intermediate range is the $h=1$ lower-endpoint case with rapid Fourier decay for $|h|\gg B'D'/P$, as in the cited proof and [shoulder estimate](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/PanSupercriticalShoulder.md). These give $O(P^{1-.007})$ per fixed allocation, allowing the stated divisor powers as $P^{o(1)}$. The number of allocations is $\ll E\log E$ by $\sum_{j\le E}2^{\omega(j)}$. Endpoint restoration costs arbitrary log saving by Cauchy–Schwarz with fixed divisor moments and taking the log-transition sufficiently narrow. Thus all CRT errors total $o_{P;\delta}(X)$, after restoring the $L$ weights. In particular one has not multiplied two near-root moduli and assumed an unpriced unit floor error.

The conditional principal is the product of the initial reciprocal moduli times the small-prime Euler densities. For $p\le P^s$ the generic local factor is $1-2/p$; if $p\mid P$ it is $1-1/p$ instead, including $p=2$. Relative to two copies of the one-form Mertens product this gives $(1+o_{P;\delta}(1))\mathfrak S(P)$. Above $P^s$, the model bank factors give $e^{-h}$ independently for each avoiding leg since overlapping bank roots differ from the product model by $O(1/p^2)$ at ordinary primes, and the large target prime reciprocals total $o(1)$. The initial moduli and their selected primes can alter the bank densities only by $1+O(\sum_{p\mid P R_1e_1 R_2e_2,\ p>P^s}1/p)=1+o_{P;\delta}(1)$. Both rough factors supply $e^{-\gamma}/(sL)$. The dimension-two sieve has normalized unsigned error $O_{a,b}(s^{-4}v^{-v/2})$ with $v=.0002/(b\delta)$, which is $O_A(\delta^A)$: the two outer rough harmonic sums contribute $O(1/s)$ each, the selected banks $O_{a,b}(1)$, and the fundamental lemma applies uniformly after the corrected local roots. This proves (5), including both complete endpoints and all no-mark events.

## 5. Extracting the true signed union

On the high sector let $K_s=\Pi-F$. On a marked leg $K_s=-F$. Thus the oriented mass with $N(y)\ge1$ is exactly
$$ -\langle\Pi,F_+\rangle+\langle F_*,F_+\rangle $$
up to the physical replacement in §1. By (3)–(5) this is
$(M_*-1)M_+X+O_{a,b}(T^4\delta^2)X+o_{P;\delta}(X)$ before the $\theta$ error, with leading coefficient $5\cdot24=120$. The true prime test has not been silently replaced by the rough source: it contributes the subtracted $24\lambda X+o(\delta X)$ here, independently proved by (4). Replacing $+$ by $1$ gives the same leading coefficient.

Where both legs have positive occupancy the two prime terms vanish, so the genuine intersection is $\langle F_+,F_+\rangle=M_+^2X+O_A(\delta^A)X+o_{P;\delta}(X)=O_{a,b}(\delta^2)X$ after the ordered limits. The intersection with exactly one mark on one specified side and positive occupancy on the other likewise has signed mass $M_1M_+X+O_A(\delta^A)X+o_{P;\delta}(X)$. These are estimates of the **signed** intersection, not a claim it has few actual integer pairs. By symmetric reflection and inclusion–exclusion the genuine physical union has twice the oriented main to first order. Subtracting the two intersections with one specified mark gives the $K=1$ statement, and subtraction from the directly computed union gives the whole $K\ge2$ estimate without summing fixed-count errors. This proves (1).

The sign obtained is internal to the high–high cell, whereas roughness-only splitting left that cell dependent on $G(P)$. It does not price the no-mark high–high complement: on that complement a genuine prime contributes to the expansion of $K_s$ and the unknown prime-pair term persists. The first-order signal must not be added to a complete-cell formula as if it were a second reserve.
