# Four shifted-prime factors for Elliott's multiplicative Goldbach problem, conditionally

**Theorem.** Suppose some fixed $\Theta<1$ bounds the real parts of all nontrivial zeros of primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet $L$-functions. Fix $C>0$. For every sufficiently large **prescribed integer** $P$, odd or even, and all $1\le r\le s\le(\log P)^C$ with $(rs,P)=1$, there are four genuine primes $P/3<p_i<P$ such that
$$
 \frac rs=\frac{(P-p_1)(P-p_3)}{(P-p_2)(P-p_4)}.                       \tag{1}
$$
Primes may repeat. In particular $k=4$ suffices in [P. D. T. A. Elliott, *Products of shifted primes: multiplicative analogues of Goldbach's problem*, Acta Arith. 88 (1999), pp. 31–50, Conjecture II](https://bibliotekanauki.pl/articles/1390552.pdf) under the stated hypothesis and analytic inputs below. Conjecture III does not bound $k$. Elliott's Theorem 2 puts a fixed **power** of a polylogarithmic integer in the group generated by the shifted primes; here the exponent of $r/s$ is one. This does **not** settle his Conjecture I (one ratio of two shifted primes) or the binary Goldbach conjecture.

**Dependencies.** We use [Jiang–Lau–Lü, §§5–6, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2#S5) via the [projected full-target comparison](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/f22749a7/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/target_slope_switch/Proof.md) and its [polylogarithmic-label version, §§1–3](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/1b426a35/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/polylog_prime_multiplier/Proof.md). The new coefficient uniformity is proved below; JLL's original theorem itself is not a theorem with growing shift $P$. For short divisors we use [the squared near-linear row comparison, §§1–2](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/129aed68/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/large_residual_rows/Proof.md) with the modifications specified below. [OpenAI, September 30, Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf) states a fixed zero-free half-plane of the required kind for the Dirichlet family. We do not independently verify its proof and present the result conditionally.

## 1. Uniform row comparison for arbitrary deletions of whole labels

Write $L=\log P$. Fix a desired log saving $E$ and a width-loss allowance $C>0$. Choose the fixed internal precision in terms of $E,C$, then $K$, then the fixed exponents $b_1\ll\cdots\ll b_K$ of the polylogarithmic-label proof, increasing them successively as necessary. Write $w=L^j$, $j>E+20$ (increase $j$ for internal precision if needed), $z=\exp(L^{.24})$. From each interval $[L^{b_i},L^{2b_i}]$ choose one prime $\ell_i\equiv1\bmod4$, $\ell_i\nmid P$. Write $\mathcal A$ for their products. All label primes exceed $w$; all products are less than $z$. For a fixed smooth $0\le\eta\le1$ supported on $[1,1.1]$, positive on some open subinterval, set
$$
 Y=L^{3b_K/2+2\sum_{i<K}b_i},\quad H=P/Y,\qquad
 M_0=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\eta(a/Y)\asymp_{\eta,\mathbf b}Y/\log Y .
$$
For the last assertion, fix the earlier primes and apply the ordinary prime number theorem in the class $1\bmod4$ to the last prime in a fixed-relative-width interval inside its bank. The earlier reciprocal sums are bounded above and below, and target-prime omissions cost $O(L)$ per last-prime interval.

For any real coefficients $0\le c_a\le1$, not necessarily factorizable in the prime labels, put
$$
 X_c(v)=\sum_a\eta(a/Y)c_a\Lambda_1(P-av),\qquad
 U_c(v)=\sum_a\eta(a/Y)c_a R_w(P-av)/V(w),
$$
with $\Lambda_1(n)={\bf1}_{n\ {\rm prime}}\log n$, $R_w(n)={\bf1}_{P^-(n)>w}$ and $V(w)=\prod_{\ell\le w}(1-1/\ell)$ over primes. The lemma is
$$
 \sum_{\substack{H/(2L^C)\le v\le .54H\\ (v,P)=1}}|X_c(v)-U_c(v)|^2
       \ll_{E,C,\Theta} H Y^2 L^{-E}.                                 \tag{2}
$$
The same bound holds for every fixed choice of coefficients. This is not a supremum over coefficients *inside* the sum. A later choice depending on an already selected row is permitted.

### The projected major with an arbitrary tag subset

Use the [full-target rational expansion and Fourier separation, §1](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b262263b/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/near_full_cofactor/Proof.md): bilinear sizes $M,N\ge P^{.19}$, $MN\asymp P$, rational denominator $d\le L^{A_0}$, character group modulo $Pd$. The physical projector $P\mid cmn-ars$ and its rational phase expand on the reduction kernel to modulo $P$ with coefficients $\le1/\phi(P)$, including $(P,d)>1$ (here $a,c$ denote two tag labels); the individual small twists and separated real phase cost fixed log powers. Inserting bounded tag coefficients only decreases the absolute truncation bound before expansion. For either separated bilinear endpoint retain
$$
 \phi(P)^{-1}\sum_{\chi\bmod Pd}|B_\chi|^2\ll d P L^{O(1)}
$$
by orthogonality and the divisor second moment.

For a fixed required tag saving $D$, take the vectors $(\chi(a)/\sqrt Y)_a$ on the complete $\mathcal A_Y=\mathcal A\cap[Y,1.1Y]$. Their squared norms are $\le2$. If the primitive conductor of $\chi\overline{\chi'}$ does not divide $\mathrm{lcm}(4,d)$ then their inner product is $O(L^{-2D-5})$. For this estimate fix all but the last prime and sum the **unmasked** quotient character on its last-prime interval in class $1\bmod4$. Both primitive twists are nonprincipal. The explicit formula under the zero hypothesis gives $O(x^{\Theta'}\log^{O(1)}(2Pd\,x))$ in log weights for each primitive twist, for any fixed $\max(\Theta,1/2)<\Theta'<1$, here $x\asymp Y/\prod_{i<K}\ell_i\ge L^{b_1}/2$ on a nonempty interval. Subtract prefixes, remove the log by partial summation, and sum reciprocal products of the fixed primes, $O_K(1)$. Removing imprimitive and target primes costs $O(L)$ per last-prime interval and proper powers cost $O(\sqrt x\log^2 x)$. Choose $b_1$ after the precision. This is the explicit-formula estimate used in §3 of the polylogarithmic-label proof, applied only to the underlying full bank.

For every $|u_a|\le1$, at most $O(dL^{2D})$ characters modulo $Pd$ have $|\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_Y}u_a\chi(a)|>YL^{-D}$. Indeed choose one passing representative in each coset of the subgroup with conductor dividing $\mathrm{lcm}(4,d)$, of size $\le4d$. For $n$ such representatives the Gram-matrix Bessel bound gives $nL^{-2D}\le2(2+O(nL^{-2D-5}))$, since $\sum|u_a|^2/Y\le2$. Apply this separately at each Fourier parameter and small twist on each of the two tag endpoints. The exceptional sets can change with these parameters. It is **not** asserted that a chopped tag bank satisfies a prime number theorem.

The long rough Möbius factor saves arbitrary log powers for **every** exceptional character, not only those of small conductor. Here are details of this use of the fixed strip. For primitive nonprincipal $\xi$ of conductor $f\le4Pd$ and fixed $\max(\Theta,1/2)<\sigma_0<1$, one has $|1/L(s,\xi)|\le(3f(3+|\Im s|))^{o(1)}$ for $\Re s\ge\sigma_0$. Center discs at $2+it$. Fix a branch of $\log L$ there from its series. The usual polynomial growth bound for $L$ on a fixed disc inside the zero-free half-plane and Borel–Carathéodory on a smaller disc give $|\log L|\ll\log(3f(3+|t|))$. Use an outer radius strictly between $2-\sigma_0$ and $2-\Theta$. The inner disc of radius $1/2$ has $|\log L|=O(1)$; Hadamard three-circles gives $|\log L|\ll(\log(3f(3+|t|)))^{1-\delta_0}$ on the intermediate radius $2-\sigma_0$, for some $\delta_0>0$. Imprimitive reciprocal Euler factors on this strip cost at most $\exp(O(\sum_{\ell\mid Pd}\ell^{-\sigma_0}))=P^{o(1)}$ (split at $\log(Pd)$). Truncated Perron at height $P^3$ for the **ordinary** Möbius sum, at half-integer endpoints, shifted to $\sigma_0$, therefore gives $\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)\chi(n)\ll P^{o(1)}x^{\sigma_0}$ for $P^{.08}\le x\le 2P$ when the inducing character is nonprincipal. The horizontal and truncation errors are smaller. Now $\mu R_z=\mu*{\bf1}_{P^+\le z}$. For smooth divisor $j'\le\sqrt M$ the remaining endpoint is polynomially long, and for larger $j'$ their harmonic mass is $\ll(\log z)^{O(1)}\exp(-\log M/(2\log z))$ by Rankin. If the inducing twist is principal use the classical arbitrary-log Möbius bound, deleting the extra target or modulus primes above $z$ on the rough sum at cost $O(L(M/z+1))$; no fixed zero-free strip for zeta is needed. These arguments allow interval prefixes at comparable endpoints, and partial summation absorbs all the fixed-polylogarithmic archimedean twists. Hence $B_\chi\ll_F PL^{-F}$ for every fixed $F$ on either endpoint (the other coefficient is bounded by a fixed log power).

Outside the two exceptional tag lists, use the tag saving with the mean-square bound for $B_\chi$ and Cauchy. On their union use the uniform Möbius estimate, trivial tag bounds $O(Y)$, the $1/\phi(P)$ coefficient and the polylogarithmic count. Choose $D$ larger than all the predetermined separation, rational denominator and desired-log costs, then $F$ large enough to absorb the exceptional-list loss as well. The arc measure is $O(L^{3A_0}/Y)$; the projected major is $O(PY L^{-R})$ at the desired fixed internal precision $R$. These log budgets are chosen before the bank exponents.

### The minor and squared short terms

In the expanded bilinear square insert $c_a c_{a'}$ on each entering/leaving whole-label pair. In the projected version of JLL (5.7)–(5.14) modify the **edge kernel**, not the constant-on-fibres test vector. The mask depends on the entering and leaving labelled tuple products, not their internal order or any real frame. It is bounded by one and symmetric under edge reversal. The ordered-list identity, self-adjointness and reach bound remain valid. In (5.19)–(5.25) a path coefficient gains just the product of these edge factors, with no effect on its absolute bound, its required line classes, or the independent-frame comparison. In Prop. 6.6 the same edge factor multiplies both sides of each individual path identity; the independent Fejér variables in the proof of Prop. 5.7 still detect only first-entry collisions, not any factorization of our mask.

For clarity, on an all-fresh transition after fixing and ordering the shared labels as in Lemma 6.9, the two free products are now functions of the individual endpoints. The tuple-order average is unchanged (the free product is the product of the labels *not* in the shared list). In [JLL (6.10)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2#S6.SS4) the new source and target mask factors are therefore two diagonal contractions: the mask may depend jointly on **all** free labels at each end. They do not affect Parseval (6.11) or the bound for adding coincident entering/leaving labels. All transitions other than this fresh block are bounded via absolute row and weighted column sums in Lemmas 6.10–6.11. Their coefficient for each individual transition gets a factor $c_a c_{a'}\le1$ before summation, including for choices taken from the remembered set; the reversed transition gets the same factor. These absolute estimates persist also after the Fejér multipliers (modulus at most one upon the original normalization). The bad-edge estimates Prop. 6.15 already bound individual absolute kernels or count repeated pairs. Thus the identical projected minor bound with the polylogarithmic label-size checks of the cited proof holds, without treating a correlated mask as a product over banks.

To finish (2), in $n=P-av$ the whole requested range has $.406P\le n\le P(1-1/(2L^C))$. Apply Vaughan at $P^{1/5}$ as in the squared row proof. The rough/unit restrictions on the two long factors separate; the Möbius factor is full on its own interval, the other bounded by $L$. Use complete multiplicative factor boxes of ratio $1+\kappa L^{-C}$ with small fixed $\kappa$ (cut also at the separate Vaughan endpoints). All boxes meeting the physical range remain strictly below $P$ throughout. There are $O(L^{2C+2})$ such pairs, so squared-norm triangle losses are a predetermined log power and can be paid at the internal precision, **before** fixing the bank. Restrict the squared output of each box to the indicated $v$. Proper powers in the source have squared cost $P^{1/2+o(1)}$ by the number per label, Cauchy and $Y=L^{O(1)}$.

In the squared two-short comparison of §§1–2 of the row proof, multiply each pair $(a,a')$ by the fixed factor $c_a c_{a'}$. All diagonal and floor errors were summed absolutely; the factorized rough-divisor mean $O_F(L^{-F})$ and the common small-root cancellation in the descent from $R_z/V(z)$ to $R_w/V(w)$ hold **per pair** before these errors are summed. More explicitly, two short divisors $\le P^{.4}$ have prime factors $>z>1.1Y$, so a common rough prime for distinct labels would divide $P(a-a')$ and cannot occur on target units. The two small components retain their compatible joint CRT density including primes dividing $a-a'$. For $w<\ell\le z$ the ratio to the independent normalizers differs from one by $O(\ell^{-2})$ off $Paa'(a-a')$ and $O(\ell^{-1})$ on its prime divisors; the total is $O(L/w)$. The common lower-cut density cancels, also with the whole-label factors. Unit $v$ is imposed exactly by its divisor sum over $P$. The same Bonferroni depth $\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor$ has superlogarithmically small factorial tails; the total floors are $P^{.8+o(1)}$ including all label pairs, small moduli and divisors of $P$. These short arguments apply on the displayed single $v$-interval: its length is comparable to $H$, the functions $\log(P-av)$ have bounded total variation there, and only $n\asymp P$, never $n\le .50P$ specifically, was needed. If $P$ is odd no change is made to the joint small-prime field; prime 2 then sieves the actual parities of the forms instead of being a target-prime exclusion. This proves (2). In particular it uses no prime progression theorem for an arbitrarily selected set of labels.

## 2. A physical two-row intersection for all dense label subsets

For $1\le t_1\le t_2\le L^C$, $(t_1t_2,P)=1$, let
$$J_{t_2}=[.50H/t_2,.54H/t_2].$$
Let $c,e$ be two coefficient choices as above with masses $M_c=\sum_a\eta(a/Y)c_a$, $M_e=\sum_b\eta(b/Y)e_b$, both $\ge Y/L^4$. With constants uniform in the choices,
$$
 \sum_{\substack{h\in J_{t_2}\\(h,P)=1}}X_c(t_1h)X_e(t_2h)
 \ \gg\ |J_{t_2}|\,\frac{\phi(P)}P\, M_c M_e .                          \tag{3}
$$
Here interval length in a lower bound is its real length. For odd $P$ we may additionally require even $h$; for even $P$ all unit $h$ are already odd. We keep this parity restriction in the proof.

First prove (3) with $U$ in place of $X$. For fixed $(a,b)$ the two forms in the **same** variable $h$ are $P-t_1ah$ and $P-t_2bh$. At $\ell\mid P$, all coefficients are units, so unit $h$ makes both nonzero. At every odd $\ell\nmid P$ there are at most two forbidden residues (one or zero if slopes coincide or vanish). Thus relative to $(1-1/\ell)^2$ the local factor is at least $1-1/(\ell-1)^2$. At 2 our prescribed parity protects both forms: for odd $P$ choose even $h$ irrespective of the parity of the slopes; for even $P$ both slopes are odd. Including the unit density at all primes dividing $P$ gives a joint density $\gg(\phi(P)/P)V(w)^2$ with an absolute constant, even if the two slopes coincide.

This is an actual CRT lower bound on any such interval, uniformly in both labels and slopes. Use the first $\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor$ and adjacent lower/upper Bonferroni truncations for the union of forbidden classes at odd $\ell\le w$ not dividing $P$, together with the exact unit expansion $\sum_{d\mid(h,P)}\mu(d)$ and the stated parity. The density terms factor by CRT; the absolute model remainder is at most $(2\sum_{\ell\le w}1/\ell)^k/k!$ for $k=\lfloor L^{.1}\rfloor+O(1)$, smaller than every fixed inverse log power. The total absolute floors per label pair are at most $\tau(P)(2w)^{k+O(1)}=P^{o(1)}$, whereas $|J_{t_2}|=P^{1-o(1)}$. If $P$ is even, impose the odd condition once via the unit expansion (or remove 2 from that expansion and impose oddness separately). All errors are negligible relative to the stated density. Sum with the nonnegative label weights and divide by $V(w)^2$.

Next the difference of the two product sums is $O(HY^2 L^{1-E/2})$: use Cauchy, (2) for $v=t_i h$ (an injective subcollection of its unit rows), and the trivial sup bounds $|X|,|U|\ll Y L$. The model lower is $\gg H Y^2 L^{-C-8}\phi(P)/P$, and $P/\phi(P)\ll\log L$. Preselect $E>2C+30$. This makes the error negligible simultaneously for every permitted choice. No density or independence conjecture for two chosen prime progressions has been invoked.

## 3. Four genuine primes

Apply (3) first with $t_1=r$, $t_2=s$, $c=e=1$. For some unit $h_0\in J_s$ the product on the left is $\gg(\phi(P)/P)M_0^2$. Put
$$
 \mathcal B_r=\{a\in\mathcal A_Y:P-r a h_0\ {\rm prime}\},\qquad
 \mathcal B_s=\{b\in\mathcal A_Y:P-s b h_0\ {\rm prime}\}.
$$
Both have $\eta$-mass $\gg M_0\phi(P)/(P L^2)\ge Y/L^4$ for sufficiently large $P$: divide each prime log by its upper bound $L$, and bound the other unlogged row by $M_0$. This fixes **actual** subsets, which need not be products of prime banks.

Apply (3) anew, with these two subset indicators, $t_1=t_2=1$, and common $h_1\in J_1$. Positive product supplies $a\in\mathcal B_r,\ b\in\mathcal B_s$ with $P-a h_1$ and $P-b h_1$ both prime. The four primes
$$p_1=P-r a h_0,\quad p_2=P-s b h_0,\quad p_3=P-b h_1,\quad p_4=P-a h_1$$
all exceed $.406P>P/3$. Substitution gives (1) with cancellation of **both** labels and both residual variables. There is no limiting argument in the group, no variable annihilator threshold, no appeal to the intersection of two separately large populations, and no growth of $k$. The same constants work for every allowed rational at each sufficiently large $P$.

### Scope

The original primary statement asks for $1\le r\le s\le\log P$, $(rs,P)=1$, integral exponents $+1,-1$, genuine primes strictly below $P$ with repetitions permitted. The result above gives exactly four factors of that kind for the full range, and hence the (unbounded-length) subgroup consequence as well. Each of the four particular complements $P-p_i$ is composite, so none of the four constructed primes can itself be an additive Goldbach summand of $P$: the construction joins two diluted prime rows at a common residual and cancels the multipliers **multiplicatively**. It does not remove a possible additive Goldbach exception, or imply any sign in the untagged reflected covariance.

Related work on the group generated by [ratios of two linear functions at consecutive integer arguments](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579317000304) and [sum-product patterns in the fixed shifted primes, October 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03417) concerns different generating sets or a fixed shift rather than these four factors at every moving target. We make no priority claim.
