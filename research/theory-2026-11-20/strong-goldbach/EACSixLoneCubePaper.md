# Paper replacement for the lone-cube six-label chart enumeration

Supplement to [the three-quarter count](EACSixThreeQuarter.md). This proof entirely replaces that note's new 70→2 finite chart reduction; the saved chart replay is an independent cross-check only. The previously published-in-repo arity≤3 finite rank obstruction used in the **different, corank-two** counting branch is not being replaced here.

**Proposition.** For all sufficiently large even centers \(N\), a genuine six-label EAC with least labels \(p<q\), \(pq>N^{3/4}\), one row \(N-r=p^3\) and all other rows semiprime has invertible \(A\). In fact if none of the semiprime products contains \(p\), it cannot occur without any rank assumption. This has no consequence about the absence of a prime-sum representation at \(N\).

Let \(M\) be the greatest label. All six are distinct odd primes, occur as factors by strong connectivity, and \(M<N/3\). The hypothesis \(q<\sqrt N\) is automatic since the composite complement of \(p\) uses other factors. Consequently \(p>N^{1/4}\). Also \(p<N^{1/3}\) since \(N=p^3+r\), so \(q>N^{5/12}\). Every complementary product lies in \((2N/3,N)\).

## 1. At least one semiprime uses \(p\)

Define a giant as a label \(g\) paired with \(p\) in a semiprime row. Since \(p^2<2N/3\), \(g\ne p\). Each giant \(g>(2/3)N/p>(2/3)N^{2/3}\) and \(gq>N\) for large \(N\); hence it occurs only paired with \(p\). Every other label except \(p\) occurs in some pair without \(p\), so it is below \(N/q<N^{7/12}\) and is \(o(g)\) uniformly for any giant. Let \(m\ge1\) count giants, and \(n=5-m\) count the remaining labels. The unique cube contributes row \(3e_p\); each distinct giant contributes exactly one row \(e_p+e_g\). The remaining \(n\) distinct semiprime rows are precisely the signless edge-incidence matrix of a cofactor graph on \(n\) vertices, without isolates, with \(n\) edges; square factors are loops with incidence 2. In the kernel of \(A\), the cube fixes the \(p\) coordinate to zero and the giant rows fix all giant coordinates to zero. Thus singularity of \(A\) requires a bipartite component of this residual graph (a signless-incidence kernel is constant with opposite signs on its two shores, and is zero in a nonbipartite component).

If \(n\le3\), such a component is impossible: a bipartite connected graph on two or three vertices has at most one or two edges respectively, and any remaining singleton can give at most one loop, so there cannot be \(n\) edges in all. It follows that \(n=4\), \(m=1\), with sole giant \(g\). A connected bipartite component on all four vertices can have four edges only as a 4-cycle. Otherwise the bipartite component must be a single isolated edge and the other two vertices must supply their edge and both loops; a three-vertex bipartite component and a singleton can supply only three edges.

In the 4-cycle, the product of the two opposite semiprime rows equals the product of the other opposite rows. In the edge-plus-two-loops component the product of the two square rows equals the square of the mixed row. In either identity the giant cannot be an input label of any row in that identity. Indeed expand the product identity into \(N\) times a signed sum of row input labels equal to a signed difference of two products of input labels. If \(g\) were one input, its coefficient in the signed sum is nonzero (\(\pm1\), or \(-2\) for the squared row), so the sum has absolute size \(\gg g\) because every other label is \(o(g)\). The absolute size of the product difference divided by \(N\) is \(O(g^2/N)=o(g)\), contradiction; \(g/N\to0\) holds as \(pg<N\) and \(p\to\infty\).

In the 4-cycle, the largest of the four nongiant cofactor labels \(d\) appears in two edges. With \(g\) excluded as input in all four rows, their inputs are at most \(d\), contradicting divisibility of their nonzero difference, strictly less than \(d\), by \(d\). In the edge-plus-loops case write the loop vertices \(a<b\), and the disjoint-edge vertices \(u,v\). The three linked products \(a^2,ab,b^2\) differ pairwise by at least \(2a\), and \(a>\sqrt{2N/3}\) from the range of the square row. Neither \(a\) nor \(b\) exceeds \(\sqrt N\); at least one of \(u,v\) does not exceed \(\sqrt N\); and \(p<\sqrt N\). Thus there is at most one label above \(\sqrt N\) once \(g\) is removed. Among the three distinct input labels of the linked identity at least two are at most \(\sqrt N\). Their positive difference is less than \(\sqrt N\), contradicting that same product difference of at least \(2a>\sqrt N\). This proves that singular \(A\) is impossible in the giant lane.

## 2. No semiprime uses \(p\)

The five semiprime products give five distinct edges on the five labels other than \(p\), without isolated vertices. Every such factor is \(<N/q<N^{7/12}\), so \(M<N^{7/12}\). The cofactor component containing \(M\) is a star \(S\): \(M\) occurs only in one row (otherwise the even difference of its two input labels would be at least \(2M\)), and not as \(M^2\) (every other semiprime product is \(\le M(M-2)\), too far). Let \(s\) be its mate. Any other product through \(s\) has another factor \(>2M/3\), by comparison with \(sM\) in the interval \((2N/3,N)\). That other factor occurs only once by the same even-difference argument. This also excludes a loop at \(s\). We have \(s<M\), \(sM<N\), so \(s<\sqrt N\); also \(s>2N/(3M)\). The star has 2 or 3 vertices: if it had at least 4, its deficit of one edge cannot be made up by the at most one outside vertex. Let \(U\) denote the labels outside the star within the five-element cofactor graph.

If \(|U|=2\), there must be the three outside products \(u^2,uv,v^2\), with \(u<v\). Each pair differs by at least \(2u>\sqrt N\). Among the candidate inputs of these products, \(p,s,u,v\) are all at most \(\sqrt N\), so at most one can come from that group. The two star leaves are separated by at most \(M/s\le 3M^2/(2N)=O(N^{1/6})=o(\sqrt N)\), by subtracting the rows whose products are \(s\) times those leaves. At most one leaf can be an input. This permits at most two outside products instead of three.

If \(|U|=3\), there are four outside products and all components on \(U\) are nonbipartite. A connected bipartite graph on three vertices has at most two edges, and a disconnected graph on three vertices with four edges must consist of the fully looped two-vertex edge and a loop on the singleton. In a nonbipartite cofactor component, write \(w_v=\log(v/\sqrt N)\) and use \(w_v+w_{v'}=\log((N-x)/N)=O(M/N)\) along its edges, including loops. Alternating the equations around an odd closed walk of bounded length starting at each vertex shows \(w_v=O(M/N)\). Thus every \(u\in U\) is \(\sqrt N+O(M/\sqrt N)\), and, writing \(T=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\), every outside product is \((T+i)(T+j)\) with \(i,j=O(1+M/\sqrt N)\).

At most one \(U\) label can be input of an outside product: if two \(U\) inputs give such products, the input difference is \(O(M/\sqrt N)=o(\sqrt N)\), and the difference of the two products of offsets \(ij\) is \(O(1+M^2/N)=o(\sqrt N)\). It follows that their factor sums coincide exactly. Among pairs drawn from three distinct labels, the only two different pairs with equal sum are the square of the middle label and the product of the extremes of a three-term arithmetic progression. Writing \(d\) for its common difference, the product difference is \(d^2\). Two inputs in the same three-element set differ by at most \(2d\), so \(d\le2\). The labels are odd, hence \(d=2\), impossible for three sufficiently large primes spaced by 2 (one is divisible by 3).

The sole cube row and the sole star edge are the only two non-outside rows. The three distinct \(U\) inputs must therefore occupy exactly these two rows and one outside row. In particular both \(p\) and \(s\) are inputs of outside products. Their difference is below \(\sqrt N\), since \(p,s<\sqrt N\). Subtract the two outside product expansions: the main term is \(T\) times the difference of their two factor sums, which is an **even integer** because factors are odd. The remaining offset-product term is \(O(1+M^2/N)=o(\sqrt N)\). A nonzero factor-sum difference would therefore force product difference at least \(2T-o(\sqrt N)>\sqrt N\), impossible. The sums are equal, so \(s-p=O(1+M^2/N)=O(N^{1/6})\). But \(s>2N/(3M)>(2/3)N^{5/12}\) and \(p<N^{1/3}\), contradiction.

This rules out the no-giant lane entirely and proves the required lone-cube obstruction. The earlier two surviving formal doubly singular charts both fall into the two-outside-label star case. The proof is structural and does not need a search over any graph, center, or label values.
