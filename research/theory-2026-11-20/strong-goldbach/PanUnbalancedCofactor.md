# A macro-shift unbalanced range in Pan's fixed-target remainder

**Statement.** Write $P=N$ for the even target, $L=\log P$, and $Q_0=P^{1/2}L^{-20}$. Use the *literal* signed $R_3(P)$ from [Huang–Li (6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03811v2#S1): $b\mid n$, $d\mid P-n$, $b,d>Q_0$, $(d,P)=1$, with coefficient $\mu(b)\log b\,\mu(d)\log d$ and $1\le n<P$. For a dyadic sector let one of $b,d$ have scale $P^{1-\kappa}$ and the other $P^v$. If for a fixed $\epsilon>0$
\[
 \kappa\ge\epsilon,\quad 36\kappa+33v\le17-\epsilon,
 \qquad 15(\kappa+v)\le 8-\epsilon, \tag{1}
\]
then this entire sector contributes $O_{A,\epsilon}(P L^{-A})$, for every $A>0$, pointwise for sufficiently large $P$. One may sum all such boxes (dyadic intervals may have truncated ends). For example $\kappa=0.005$, $v=0.505$ is allowed, with a long divisor of scale $P^{0.995}$: this is disjoint from the [two-near-square-root sector](PanSupercriticalShoulder.md). The result concerns **neither** all large divisors **nor** bounded cofactors.

The spectral input is the dispersion proof of [Fouvry and Radziwiłł, *Level of distribution of unbalanced convolutions*, Thm 1.1, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.08672) (FR). Their **stated** theorem assumes $1\le|a|\le MC/3$ for factors of lengths $M,C$. This does *not* cover the Goldbach residue $a=P$ when $mc<P$. We give the required proof modification below, retaining fixed positive power margins. No appeal is made to an unproved extension of their corollary for prime variables.

## 1. Macroscopic residue dispersion

Let $m\asymp M$, $c\asymp C$, $q\asymp T$, $X=MC$, all dyads of form $(U,2U]$. Suppose $|a|\ge1$, $|a|/X\ll(\log X)^K$, $T>C$, $M>T X^{\eta_0}$, $C>X^{\eta_0}$; assume that for some fixed $\eta_0>0$
\[
 M^{3/20}C^{59/20}T^{33/20}+C^{23/8}T^{15/8}\ll MC^2 X^{-\eta_0}. \tag{2}
\]
For arbitrary $|\alpha_m|\le1$ and $\beta_c=1_{c\in J}$ on any interval inside the $C$-dyad,
\[
 \sum_{q\asymp T,\,(q,a)=1}\left|\sum_{mc\equiv a(q)}\alpha_m\beta_c-\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{(mc,q)=1}\alpha_m\beta_c\right|\ll X^{1-\eta_1}\tag{3}
\]
for some $\eta_1>0$ depending on the fixed margins (and $K$), uniformly over the intervals. The starting conditions also imply $M>C$.

**Delete the potentially singular short cofactors.** Replace $\beta$ by $1_{c\in J,\ c\nmid a}$. At most $\tau(|a|)=X^{o(1)}$ indices are deleted. On the progression side for each such $c$, $(q,a)=1$ implies either no solution or a unique $m$ class mod $q$. Over $q\asymp T$ this costs $O((M+T)X^{o(1)})$. The mean side costs $\ll MX^{o(1)}$. These are power-saving in $X$. Now $mc-a\ne0$ whenever $\beta_c\ne0$, even in FR's enlarged cutoff $M/2<m<5M/2$. This point matters **after** the Cauchy–Schwarz step.

**Adaptation of the dispersion proof.** In FR notation replace their $(M,N,Q,D)$ by $(M,C,T,Z)$, where $Z=X^\lambda$, $0<10\lambda<\eta_0$. Their equations (17)–(24) use only $(a,q)=1$ and a smooth majorant. The target-size uses in the rest are (25)/Lemma 6.1, the partial summation immediately after (47), and the simplification after their trilinear estimate (before (49)). We spell out the losses:

* In Lemma 6.1 use the notation $s=(c_1,c_2)$, $s_1\mid s^\infty$ the $s$-part of $c_1/s$, $r=(q_1,q_2)$, and $r_i\mid r^\infty$ the $r$-part of $q_i/r$. All divisor counts of $|mc_i-a|$ for occurring $c_i$ cost $X^{o(1)}$ since these integers are nonzero and $O(X\log^K X)$. Here are explicit counts of the discarded configurations, normalized by $MC^2$; all $X^{o(1)}$ factors below are uniform. If $s>Z$, count $(c_1,c_2)$ with $s\mid c_1,c_2$ and then count $m,q_1,q_2$ using both divisor bounds: this costs $X^{o(1)}/Z$. If $s_1>Z$, the same bound with $c_1$ a multiple of $s s_1$ gives at most $X^{o(1)}\sum_s s^{-2}\sum_{s_1\mid s^\infty,\ s_1>Z}s_1^{-1}\ll X^{o(1)}Z^{-1/2}$, since $\prod_{p\mid s}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}=X^{o(1)}$ uniformly for $s\le2C$. Counts of multiples in a dyad are $\ll C/k$ whenever nonempty. If $r>Z$, fixing $q_2$ and $c_1\equiv c_2\bmod r$, count $m$ in one class mod $q_2$ at $O(M/T)$ and then $q_1\mid(mc_1-a)$. There are $O(T/r)$ possible $q_2$ divisible by $r$ and $O(C(C/r+1))$ short pairs. Summation gives $X^{o(1)}(Z^{-1}+C^{-1})$. Finally if $r\le Z$, $r_i>Z$, fix $q_i$ divisible by $rr_i$ and count the other modulus as a divisor. The normalized cost is $X^{o(1)}\sum_{r\le Z}r^{-2}\sum_{r_i\mid r^\infty,r_i>Z}r_i^{-1}\ll X^{o(1)}Z^{-1/2}$, using $r\le Z<C$. This proves FR (30) at cost $X^{o(1)}$ in place of log powers. In returning to the full *main* term in FR §6.4, the same bounds hold directly with the residue density $O(r/(q_1q_2))$ instead of counting $m$; explicitly the $s,s_1$ cases have an additional coprime-modulus harmonic factor $\ll L^{O(1)}$ after summing $r/(q_1q_2)$ (uniformly for a fixed pair of short indices). For the $r,r_i$ cases, sum $O((T/r)^2)$ modulus pairs with density $r/T^2$ and $O(C(C/r+1))$ cofactor pairs; an additional $r_i$ divisibility saves $1/r_i$. Thus the same truncation error suffices.
* After (47) the nonmodular phase is $ah/(\gamma s s_1\nu'_1 k'_1 k'_2)$ in their variables. On good blocks $\nu'_1\asymp C/(s s_1)$, $k'_i\asymp T/(r r_i)$, $\gamma=r r_1r_2$, so its magnitude is $\ll |a|\,|h|r/(CT^2)$. In (37), $|h|\ll T^2 M^{-1}\log^{O(1)}X$ (take $1\le|h|$, otherwise there is no off-diagonal). Since $r\le Z$ and $|a|/X\ll L^K$, each of the finitely many normalized derivatives costs at most $Z^{O(1)}L^{O_K(1)}$. This is precisely the allowable kind of loss in (48). The jump to (49) uses $(1+|a|/(MC))^{1/2}$ and now costs at most $L^{O_K(1)}$. The Kloosterman-fraction theorem invoked there is uniform in its nonzero integer multiplier; the possible zero case $s_1\nu_1'=\nu_2$ would imply $c_1=c_2=s$, impossible for $s\le Z<C$. All intervening CRT identities and the §6.6 main-term variance comparison are unchanged.

Consequently FR (Theorem 1.1 with the altered error just shown) bounds the square of the left of (3), for the modified short coefficient, by a constant times
\[
 M\{(M/T)\mathcal E^*+ L^{O(1)} C^2T+X^{o(1)}Z^{-1/2}MC^2+
 Z^{C_0} X^\xi [M^{3/20}C^{59/20}T^{33/20}+C^{23/8}T^{15/8}]\}. \tag{4}
\]
Here $\xi>0$ is arbitrarily small and fixed, and $C_0$ is finite after fixing $\xi$. Choose $\xi<\eta_0/4$, then $\lambda$ sufficiently small relative to $\eta_0,C_0$. The independent $X^{o(1)}$ in the gcd tails costs at most $Z^{1/4}$ for large $X$.

**Elementary power bound for the remaining variance.** In FR (11),(13), for each $r$ and $v\asymp T/r$, the counts on reduced residue classes $t\bmod r$ use the additional condition $(c,v)=1$. For $r\le2C$ each count differs from its mean over reduced classes by $O(\tau(v)+\tau(|a|))=X^{o(1)}$: inclusion–exclusion in $v$ on the interval $J$ gives a common main term for every unit class, and deletion of $c\mid a$ costs at most $\tau(|a|)$; averaging these counts gives the displayed mean. Hence these $r$ contribute $O(T C X^{o(1)})$ in all. For $r>2C$ at most one $c$ falls in each class, so the sum of squared discrepancies over classes is at most the sum of the squared raw counts, at most $2C$. Summing over $r,v$ again costs at most $T C X^{o(1)}$. This proves $\mathcal E^*\ll TC X^{o(1)}$. Insert in (4), use $M/T,C\ge X^{\eta_0}$ and (2), and restore the deleted indices. This proves (3).

## 2. Application in the two orientations

Drop physical endpoints $\min(n,P-n)<P/L^K$ at cost $O(P L^{5-K/2})$ by the symmetric divisor second-moment argument in the [shoulder proof](PanSupercriticalShoulder.md). One may treat the remaining hyperbolic product cuts using an independent multiplicative mesh of relative width $L^{-K'}$ in the long factor and its cofactor. Boxes intersecting a physical endpoint can be discarded inside slightly enlarged endpoint intervals of total length $O(P/L^K)$ (take $K'>K+2$): choose instead to keep products on $[P/L^K,P(1-L^{-K})]$ using boxes fully contained there. All lost terms satisfy $\min(n,P-n)\ll P/L^K$. The grid uses only $L^{O_{K'}(1)}$ boxes and gives independent interval cutoffs. The discarded endpoint estimate is for the union, not per box.

If $b$ is long, take $m=b$, $c=n/b$, $q=d$, $a=P$. On the retained physical mesh $X=MC$ lies between constant multiples of $P/L^K$ and $P$, and $M=P^{1-\kappa+o(1)}, C=P^{\kappa+o(1)},T=P^{v+o(1)}$. Dividing the spectral terms in (2) by $MC^2$ gives powers $(36\kappa+33v-17)/20$ and $(15\kappa+15v-8)/8$, respectively. Thus (3) applies with fixed margins. Normalize the logarithmic part of the long coefficient by $O(L)$; the weight on $q$ costs a further $O(L)$ outside its absolute discrepancy. The uniform mean for each $q$ contains $\sum_{m\in I,(m,q)=1}\mu(m)\log m\ll_J ML^{-J}$, by the elementary coprime Mertens argument (3) of the shoulder proof and partial summation. The other factor is at most $2C$, and $\sum_{q\asymp T}1/\phi(q)\ll L^{O(1)}$. This proves the conclusion.

If $d$ is long, write $P-n=dk$ and take $g=(b,P)$. Because $\mu(b)\ne0$ only for squarefree $b$, put $b=gq$ with $(q,P)=1$; necessarily $g\mid k$ since $(d,P)=1$. For $g>P^\rho$, the union of contributions is $\ll P^{1-\rho+o(1)}$ by summing over $g\mid P$, $g\mid n$ and bounding the divisor products by $P^{o(1)}$. Choose a fixed $0<\rho$ much smaller than all exponent margins. For $g\le P^\rho$ put $c=k/g$, $a=P/g$; the congruence is exactly $dc\equiv a\bmod q$ with $(q,a)=1$. Original conditions on $q$ (including any stronger unit or squarefree masks) are included in the modulus coefficient. Original $(d,P)=1$ is a single-variable multiplier on the long coefficient. The scale $X=MC\asymp (P/g)L^{O(K)}$ has $|a|/X\ll L^K$. Changing lengths from $C,T$ to $C/g,T/g$ and changing ambient scale by $1/g$ perturbs (1) by $O(\rho)$ at most, so fixed power margins remain, and $C/g$ is still polynomial. Apply (3). The mean Mertens bound now excludes primes dividing $qP$, still uniformly (the convolution argument allows any excluded integer $\le P^{O(1)}$). Summing mean bounds costs $\sum_{g\mid P}P/g\ll P\log\log(3P)$; summing power-saving discrepancies costs at most $P^{o(1)}$. This proves the mirror orientation without silently imposing $(b,P)=1$.

**Scope.** This uses FR's trilinear dispersion and its exponents, not a stronger general spectral inequality. See also [Wright, partially fixed moduli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25177) and [subdyadic intervals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27732); the physical residue here is macroscopic, the deleted divisor-of-target exception is explicit, and no conjectural strengthening is assumed. This does not determine the sign of the remaining $R_3(P)$; in particular both divisors can be near $P$, even with both cofactors bounded. No two-prime existence assertion follows.
