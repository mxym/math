# A projected minor-arc comparison for a reflected bilinear mean square

This transfers the **minor comparison**, not the major estimate, in §§5–6 of [Jiang, Lau and Lü, arXiv:2611.12467v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12467v2) (JLL). Their theorem treats shift 1. The present assertion uses their real labelled-operator estimates as inputs. The new comparison retains a congruence modulo the **actual full target** in the major term. Neither its size nor binary Goldbach follows from this result.

## Statement

Let the target be a positive integer $P$, $L=\log P$, $M,N\ge P^\delta$, $X=MN\asymp P$. Put $z=\exp(L^{.24})$. Let $\alpha_m,\beta_n$ be bounded in absolute value by $L^C$ and supported on target units with least prime factor $>z$, in $[M,1.1M]$ and $[N,1.1N]$. Require all retained products to be strictly below $P$ (for example choose the two boxes inside the central region). Let $\mathcal P_1,\dots,\mathcal P_K$ satisfy JLL (4.1): disjoint sets of target-unit primes in $[\exp(L^{\vartheta_i}),\exp(2L^{\vartheta_i})]$, $.1<\vartheta_1<\cdots<\vartheta_K<.2$, gaps at least $1/(20K)$, reciprocal masses $1/4\le V_i\le1/2$. Set $\mathcal A=\{p_1\cdots p_K:p_i\in\mathcal P_i\}$ and $\mathcal V=\prod_i V_i$. Use a fixed smooth real weight $\eta$ supported in $[1,1.1]$, bounded by one. Define an actual reflected mean square
$$
 Q=\sum_{h>0}\left|\frac1{\mathcal V}\sum_{P-mn=ah,\ a\in\mathcal A}
 \eta(a/Y)\alpha_m\beta_n\right|^2.
$$
Let $\psi$ be even, one on $[-4,4]$ and zero outside $[-5,5]$, as in JLL. Define $\mathfrak M=\{\theta:\|\theta-b/q\|\le 2L^{A_0}/Y,\ q\le L^{A_0},\ (b,q)=1\}$. In a sum over $a,c\in\mathcal A$ and physical $m,n,r,s$, put
$$
 B=\mathcal V^{-2}\eta(a/Y)\eta(c/Y)\alpha_m\beta_n\overline{\alpha_r\beta_s},\qquad k=cmn-ars .
$$
The projected major term is
$$
 \mathcal M_P=\sum_{P\mid k}B\,\psi(k/(PY))
       \int_{\mathfrak M} e\big(\theta[k/P-c+a]\big)\,d\theta .
$$

**Comparison.** For every $R>0$ one can use fixed $A_0,K_0$ depending on $\delta,C,R,\eta$ such that, for each fixed $K\ge K_0$ and all admissible data, $Q-\mathcal M_P=O(XY L^{-R})$. A dyadic bilinear sum against an arbitrary residual $|b(h)|\le1$ has absolute value at most $O((X/Y)^{1/2})Q^{1/2}$. For general central ranges split into narrow packets and apply the latter inequality and the triangle inequality; no unproved off-diagonal comparison between separate shells is needed. The original theorem is for a packet with both $a,c\in[Y,1.1Y]$.

## 1. Physical endpoints and the determinant of the target

After exchanging the bilinear variables if needed, $M\le N$. Insert JLL's identity (5.5) with $J_0=\lfloor L^{.01}\rfloor$ shared labels per set. Write $D$ for their product and split $D$ into bins $d_0\le D<1.1d_0$ (there are $O_K(L)$). Set $H=d_0Y$, $U=HM$, $V=N$. We have $U\le VP^{o(1)}$, $UV=HX$, $\log H=O_K(L^{.21})$. The physical endpoints are
$$x=(Dcm,s),\qquad x'=(Dar,n),\qquad \det(x,x')=Dk.$$
All coordinates are units modulo $P$, including all of $D$. In both the equality and the comparison kernel the two endpoints have the same unit slope $\gamma=x_1/x_2\pmod P$. Work in the orthogonal direct sum over these $\gamma$ of copies of $\mathcal L_\gamma=\{(u,v):u\equiv\gamma v\pmod P\}$, index $P$. For squarefree $Dac$ we have $(a,c)=1$, and $k=P(c-a)$ is equivalent to the pair of equations with equal positive $h$ from the expanded $Q$. Also $k=0$ is impossible since $a\mid cmn$ would give $a\mid mn$. For the major kernel $0<|k/P|\le5Y<z$. A common divisor of the two coordinates of a single endpoint is coprime to $P,D$ (the second coordinate is $z$-rough), and divides $k/P$. Consequently each physical endpoint is a primitive integral point. It is also primitive in its slope lattice.

For a physical primitive unit endpoint $(u,v)$ there is a **unique slope** and an oriented basis of its slope lattice
$$
 g_P=\begin{pmatrix}u&w\\v&(P+vw)/u\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 w=[-P\bar v]_u\in[0,u).
$$
Apply $J:(x_1,x_2)\mapsto (x_1,x_1+x_2/P)$. Then $J\mathcal L_\gamma$ is a unimodular real lattice, $Jg_P\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$, $\det(Jx,Jx')/D=k/P$, and the first row, all first-coordinate label conditions and their ordering are unchanged. The two narrow physical endpoints have $x_1\in[U,1.331U]$, $0<x_2/P\le1.1N/P\ll1/M=O(P^{-\delta})$, hence are in the standard real boxes $[U,16U]\times[U,2U]$ after $J$. In particular the enlarged positions may have physical $-15PU\le x_2\le PU$ (from $x_2=P[(Jx)_2-(Jx)_1]$); neither a lower positivity bound on that coordinate nor physical target-unit restrictions are used on the intermediate positions. In each lattice allow **all lattice-primitive positions** in these boxes, including intermediate states not corresponding to physical unit supports. Put $F$ equal to zero off the actual endpoints. Thus neither physical nonunits in several lattices nor the large number of fictitious positions creates a multiplicity in $\|F\|^2$, which is $O(UV L^{4C})$ by divisibility and the normalized label weights as in (5.14)–(5.16). The first coordinate remains an integer on the transformed lattice, so the arithmetic label definition is exact even for the enlarged state space.

## 2. Global starting-frame average (not an estimate in each slope)

For a squarefree $S=P^{o(1)}$ coprime to $P$, give one first-row kernel line modulo each $p\mid S$. For arbitrary intervals $I_u\subset[U,16U], I_v\subset[V,2V], I\subset[0,1]$, the number of physical $u,v$ in those intervals, with $(u,v)=1,(uv,P)=1,w/u\in I$ and prescribed lines, is
$$
 |I_u||I_v||I|\,\frac1{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid P}\frac{p-1}{p+1}
       \prod_{p\mid S}\frac1{p+1}
 +O\big(UV P^{-\delta/2+o(1)}\big).
$$
All lengths in this formula are ordinary Lebesgue lengths; the error is absolute, uniform even on empty or very short component intervals. In particular the local factor at each target prime is relative to ordinary primitive points, not relative to all points with density one.

Indeed write $S=S_0 S_1$, where precisely the $S_0$ primes have the line $\ker(0,1)$. Impose $S_0\mid u$, $(u,S_1P)=1$. The remaining line conditions read $w\equiv h u\pmod{S_1}$ by CRT. Remove $(v,P)=1$ by $d\mid P$ and put $v=dv'$. Then $v'w\equiv-P/d\pmod u$ with the right side a unit. For fixed $u,d$, full periods in $v'$ contribute their exact totient count with error $O((1+V/(du))\tau(u))$ from counting units in the interval/progression for $w$. For the one residual interval complete the two interval Fourier sums modulo $u$; the Weil bound for the complete Kloosterman sum gives $O(\sqrt u\,u^{o(1)})$. More explicitly on the nonzero frequencies divisible by $g\mid u$ each normalized Fourier absolute sum is $O(\log(2u)/g)$, also for the progression since its step $S_1$ is a unit. Summing $(h_1,h_2,u)^{1/2}$ by divisors, including the axes separately, gives this error. The zero-frequency main after summing $d$ is $(\phi(P)/P)|I_v||I|\phi(u)/(S_1u)$. For the average over $u$, open $\phi(u)/u=\sum_{j\mid u}\mu(j)/j$ and remove $(u,S_1P)=1$ by inclusion-exclusion. Relative to $1/\zeta(2)$ the local factor for $p\mid P$ in this average is $p/(p+1)$, for $p\mid S_0$ it is $1/(p+1)$, and for $p\mid S_1$ it is $p/(p+1)$ before dividing by $S_1$. The interval count error is $O(\tau(S_1P)\log(2U)+P^{o(1)})$: the tail of the $j$-main uses $[j,S_0]\ge j$. Together with $\tau(P)=P^{o(1)}$ the total error is $P^{o(1)}(U^{3/2}+V+U)$, as claimed. The $S$-uniform bound allows summation over any selection of the $\prod_{p\mid S}(p+1)=P^{o(1)}$ line tuples.

## 3. Transfer of the centered operator

Use the kernel, ordered tuple symmetrization, separation cut and fibre indicators of JLL (5.10)–(5.15) **in each transformed lattice**, with determinant taken in the transformed real coordinates. In lattice basis coordinates an allowed shared edge has $D\mid\det(z,z')$. Its $t=\det(z,z')/D$ is now $k/P$ on physical endpoints, so this is exactly the displayed full-projector comparison, not the old major integral. The start separation parameter is $w/u$; more generally $\alpha_g(z)=(gw_z)_1/(gz)_1\bmod1$ is independent of the choice of determinant-one complement in the lattice, as in (5.17). A lattice-primitive vector need not be primitive in the original physical plane. It **is** primitive in its integral basis coordinates, as needed for the projective class and label transitions.

Apply JLL Lemma 5.4 to the direct sum, choosing as its index family only the physical primitive **unit** starting fibres (add zero-coefficient fibres of this type to take the full average). The powers of the enlarged operator still run over all its states; the trace on the right is only the sum of these starting diagonal entries. Within each lattice distinct primitive slopes are spaced by at least $(16U)^{-2}$ and each edge changes slope by $O(H/U^2)$; no walk can leave its lattice. Thus the reachable-start bound is $O(1+TH)$, not $\phi(P)TH$. Here $T\asymp L^{1/2}$ is even. Each physical starting endpoint occurs once in the entire sum.

For clarity the full comparison of the trace now has three components.

* In coordinates of $g=Jg_P$, each path has precisely the integral coordinate and label restrictions of JLL (5.20), with $|z_{j,2}|\ll TH$ by telescoping determinant-one slopes, and $|z_{j,1}|\le16+|z_{j,2}|$. The path coefficients and their number satisfy JLL (5.22); making a shared-product bin narrower only removes paths. For primitive $z$, the divisibility of the first coordinate of $gz$ by a label prime iff $[z]_p$ is the first-row kernel line is valid because $p\nmid P$. Only the first row of $g$ is used for these lines. For the *real* axis windows the corresponding continuous frame at physical coordinates $u,v,r$ has columns $(u,u+v/P)$ and $(ur,(u+v/P)r+1/u)$. In the normalized variables $u/U,v/V,r$, each box boundary still has bounded degree after clearing $u$. The separation tests still use only the first row. Thus the same cube boundary count as JLL Lemma 6.2 holds, independently of the thin aspect of the physical starting band: the count for bounded-degree polynomial zero sets does not depend on the size of their coefficients. Identically constant tests can be discarded. Using §2 in each cube now proves JLL's arithmetic-to-independent-lines comparison (Prop. 6.5) with the physical integral $du\,dv\,dr/(UV)$ and the extra density $\prod_{p\mid P}(p-1)/(p+1)\le1$. In fact the set of primes on a path has product $\exp(O_K(L^{.70}))$, the sum of absolute path coefficients costs $\exp(O_K(L^{.73}))$, and a mesh of inverse polynomial size sufficiently small in terms of $\delta$ still leaves a power saving. This handles all slopes together; no individual-slope equidistribution has been used.
* For **every** allowed continuous starting frame, $u$ and $u+v/P$ lie in the ordinary polynomial-sized model boxes for the actual first-coordinate range. One can restrict starts to $u\le1.34U$, since all $F$-supports are there; then $u+v/P\in[U,2U]$. Its standard frame parameter is $r$. The independent-line model transition identities and norms are therefore exactly JLL Prop. 6.6, Lemmas 6.7–6.11 and the concluding flat-path extraction. They are uniform in the real frame, so no Lebesgue equidistribution over the entire second *sheared* axis is required. A restriction $d_0\le D<1.1d_0$ rather than $2d_0$ is allowed in these proofs: in the cancellation block of Lemma 6.9 the ordered shared list, hence $D$, is fixed in an orthogonal block before taking any norm; in the other patterns the estimates are by absolute row/column bounds. The restriction is symmetric under edge reversal and the tuple symmetrization. All bounds on $H/D$ stay within the original constants. The finitely many subdivisions per dyadic size are treated separately, not assumed to cancel across bins.
* The resulting sum of starting diagonals is $O_E(UV L^{-ET})$ for suitably large fixed $K$, with the negligible power-saving averaging error as in JLL §6. Combine this with the preceding Reach bound and $\|F\|^2\ll UV L^{4C}$ exactly as in Prop. 5.5. Since $(1+TH)^{1/T}=O(1)$ and $UV/d_0=XY$, one obtains arbitrary fixed logarithmic saving after the $O_K(L)$ bins. For the non-separated endpoint error a one-edge trace calculation suffices: JLL Lemmas 6.12–6.13 use only the same global frame/line average, $r$ uniform, and the first-coordinate box yielding $O(1)$ choices at fixed edge determinant. The new density is at most one. Reverse the edge for a failure at the other endpoint.

Finally common labels or repeated shared labels cost a negligible amount, including **before** any primitive-state expansion. For fixed $a,c\asymp Y$, Cauchy on exact common $h$ costs $O((X/Y)L^{O_C(1)})$: uniformly $\sum_{h:\,0<P-ah\ll X}\tau(P-ah)^2\ll (X/a)L^3$. To see this, use $\tau^2\le\tau_4$; fix the three factors other than a largest one (product $b\ll X^{3/4}$), then the remaining factor lies in a single class mod $a$ when $(b,a)=1$. If $(b,a)>1$ it cannot produce the target-unit residue. Summing $O(X/(ab)+1)$ proves the bound since $a=P^{o(1)}$. For the projected major term, given either $u'=mn$ or $v'=rs$, both $O(P)$, the congruence $cu'\equiv av'\pmod P$ allows only $O(1)$ choices for the other because $a,c$ are target units. The divisor-square moment and $\mathrm{meas}(\mathfrak M)\ll L^{3A_0}/Y$ give the same bound up to a log power. The number of bad $(a,c)$ and the reciprocal weight of bad shared choices have the savings $O_K(J_0^2/\min\bigcup\mathcal P_i)$ from JLL Prop. 6.15 (ordinary integer counting, still valid for each retained narrow bin or on summing all bins). No unit restriction is discarded to bound the number of admissible $v'$ per $u'$. This proves the comparison.

## Interpretation and the open term

For a multiplicative indicator $b$ unchanged on removing the labels (e.g. the two-squares indicator on split tags) the exact reflected equation gives $b(P-mn)=b(h)$. The same Cauchy comparison holds if multiplication by a tag only multiplies the source by a known unit scalar, as for Liouville on a fixed number of distinct prime labels. The new major term has the projector
$$
 1_{P\mid cmn-ars}=\frac1{\phi(P)}\sum_{\chi\bmod P}
   \chi(cmn)\overline{\chi(ars)}
$$
on these unit supports. It cannot be replaced by the principal term, even at the zero arc. JLL §7 is a proof for its *original small-shift majors*, not for this projected sum. Also the auxiliary factor weight vanishes identically on a complement having no prime factor at the label scales, including a prime complement. This result says nothing by itself about their number or the sign of the binary minor arcs for the von Mangoldt convolution.
