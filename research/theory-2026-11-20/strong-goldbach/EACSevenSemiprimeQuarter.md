# The quarter-power minimum for seven-label semiprime components

Let N be an even center with an actual seven-label factor-closed strongly connected component of distinct odd primes coprime to N. In the semiprime case, make the undirected cofactor graph on the seven primes by giving each factor product N−x=uv one edge uv, with a loop when u=v. Strong connectivity makes every prime occur as a factor. Every label is <N/3 because it occurs with another odd prime factor ≥3. Consequently every product is in (2N/3,N). There are seven distinct edges and no isolates. The known largest-label star theorem supplies one bipartite component. The following argument excludes any second bipartite component by the following exact argument. [Bożek's original framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900) [Existing largest-label star](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/research/goldbach-paper-2026-11-24/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/EACSemiprimeStar.md)

1. If the largest-label star has s vertices and another bipartite component has b, they contribute at most s−1 and floor(b²/4) edges. Any remaining component on n vertices contributes at most n(n+1)/2, allowing loops. The only size partitions of seven whose capacities sum to at least seven are (s,b; rest)=(2,5; none) and (2,2; 3). This follows by checking s,b≥2 with s+b≤7, or by bounding all remaining vertices by a single complete looped component. The first shape is a single edge and K₂,₃. The second shape has two single edges and five of the six possible products on the remaining three labels.
2. In K₂,₃ let H be the largest prime in that component. Every member r of its shore is >2H/3, by comparing r and H through any common factor on the other shore and using the product interval. Two distinct products through r differ by at least 2r>H. Thus at most one product through r can be assigned an input label from within K₂,₃, because differences of those five input labels are <H. There are at most three such products across the whole shore, yet the six K₂,₃ products must use at least four of their own input labels since only two labels lie outside. This is impossible.
3. In the other shape let u<v<w denote the three labels supporting five products. Then u>(2/3)√N and w<√(3N/2). For the lower bound, if u² occurs use u²>2N/3. If it is the missing product, uw and w² occur, so w<√N and u>(2/3)√N. For the upper bound, if w² occurs use it. If it is the missing product, u² and uw occur, giving u>√(2N/3) and w<N/u<√(3N/2). Each isolated edge also has one smaller endpoint below √N. Thus five distinct input labels are <√(3N/2), and at least three of the five products supported on u,v,w must use such bounded inputs. No two of these three products share a factor because if they shared z, their difference would be ≥2z>(4/3)√N>√(3N/2). Three nonempty disjoint factor supports on three labels force all three products to be squares. Any two squares of different labels have gap ≥4u>√(3N/2), a contradiction.

It follows that the cofactor graph has exactly one bipartite component for every seven-label all-semiprime component. In particular the unsigned incidence exponent matrix has rational nullity one. The existing fixed-size theorem for a unique bipartite component now gives p≪N^(1/4) and M≫N^(3/4), where p and M are the least and greatest labels. For a seven-label component of any arity, p≤N^(1/3) once N is sufficiently large. Otherwise three factors would exceed N, every row would be semiprime, and the quarter-power bound would contradict p>N^(1/3). No inference is made about the size of a terminal component at a hypothetical Goldbach exception.
