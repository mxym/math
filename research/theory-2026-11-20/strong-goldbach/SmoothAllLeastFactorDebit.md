# An all-least-factor bound on the actual triprime quotient in a calibrated smooth ledger

The sharp coefficient of [DeeperRoughTriprimeDebit](DeeperRoughTriprimeDebit.md) left all sufficiently small reflected least factors unbounded. Here an exact new smooth replacement preserves the **whole original no-\(F_1\) ledger** and its prime atom. A recalculated negative mean pays the entire added debit on the specified genuine broad triprime quotient, with **no reflected least-factor restriction**, and the entire corrected positive contribution on the original narrow packet. Its analytic input for the formerly unbounded tiny factors is Fourier cancellation of the actual Möbius divisor sum, with the exact local coprimality mask kept before taking a nonnegative quadratic upper. This does not estimate other quotient shapes, remove the requirement of exactly one reflected \(E\)-mark, or prove Goldbach.

## 1. Same original row, with a different exact smooth coefficient

Keep \(N=2x\), \(L=\log x\), \(M_N=\mathfrak S(N)x/(2L)\), \(n=sq\in(x/2,x]\), \(m=N-n\), \((n,N)=1\), and the original \(W_s(q)\), \(C(m)\) and \(B'(s,q)=D_B(q)/\log q+2F_2(s,q)/3\) of [WholeQuotientTriprimeRemoval](WholeQuotientTriprimeRemoval.md). This section uses the entire original \(s\in(x^{.10},x^{.14})\) band in the ledger.

Choose a fixed kernel \(f\) as in §3, supported on \(t\le\beta=.310\) for \(t\ge0\), and exactly \(f(t)=1-t\) on a fixed neighborhood of \(0\). Define
\[
 D_f(m)=\log m\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)f(\log d/\log m),
 \qquad C_f(m)=\Lambda(m)-D_f(m).
\]
It has \(C_f(P)=0\) at every true prime \(P\) in the central interval. The exact scope of the smooth change is
\[
 \sum_{s,q} W_s(q) B'(s,q)\,[C_f(N-sq)-C(N-sq)]
     = O_A(xL^{-A})                                                     \tag{1}
\]
for every fixed \(A>0\), uniformly in each actual even target. This is a whole-row identity, not an assertion on restricted quotient profiles.

To prove it, write \(C=\Lambda-D_{\rm sharp}\) in the old exact short reassembly, with \(D_{\rm sharp}=T_1-T_2+T_3\) and every divisor argument at most \(Q_0=\sqrt{x}/H^2\). Thus the difference \(\Delta=C_f-C=D_{\rm sharp}-D_f\) has **no** \(\Lambda(m)\) term; its short divisor arguments are all \(\le Q_0\). If \(F\ll x^{O(1)}\) is a coprimality mask and \(y\asymp x\), the coprime \(\mu/d\) harmonic moment gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\ge1\\(d,F)=1}}\frac{\mu(d)}d\,\log y\, f(\log d/\log y)
   = F/\phi(F)+O_A(L^{-A}).                                             \tag{2}
\]
Use the exact affine equality through a sufficiently small fixed power \(d\le x^\epsilon\), and use finite coprime Mertens and partial summation for the rest, all endpoints at least \(x^\epsilon\). This also gives the analogous \(\mu/\phi(d)\) harmonic main \(\mathfrak S(F)\) for even \(F\), by the finite coprime moments of [Huang–Li, Lemma 1](https://huixil.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/on-a-variant-of-the-elliott-halberstam-conjecture-and-the-goldbach-conjecture.pdf). It is important that this is an **arbitrary-log-reserve** equality, not just a Taylor approximation with a fixed \(1/L\) error.

On the true \(q\)-prime row the ordinary full-scale product BV theorem, applied to \(s\times q\), opens both short scores up to \(Q_0\) with arbitrary log reserve. The common model for each is \(\mathfrak S(Nn)\): for the old \(D_{\rm sharp}\) this is the exact old short calculation, and for \(D_f\) it is (2) with the \(\mu/\phi\) version. This proves \(\sum_{s,q\ {\rm prime}}W_s\Delta=O_A(xL^{-A})\). Proper prime powers of \(q\) are negligible after replacing its prime indicator by \(\Lambda(q)/\log q\) (sum \(O(\sqrt{x/s}L)\) per \(s\), then use divisor bounds for the weights).

Now use the reverse Vaughan identity literally on \(\Lambda(q)\). Its short terms have divisor argument \(e\le U_qV_q=x^{.080}\). On opening \(e\mid q\), the modulus in \(m\) is \(v=se\le x^{.220}\). The high term is the bare \(\mu(r)\Lambda(k)\) sum. Its two long-free subtractions transform, as in [SwitchedLongFreeReverseColumns](SwitchedLongFreeReverseColumns.md), into actual \(m\equiv N\bmod v\) with \(v<x^{.499}\), harmonic divisor-bounded outer weights and smooth one-variable tests. This uses the original free factors, not an additional primality mask. The \(F_2\) term also only uses such \(v<x^{.499}\). On each of these short-reverse, switched, or \(F_2\) columns open the short divisor of \(\Delta\). With \(m=d h\) and \((m,N)=1\), the density is \(\phi(N)/(Nvd)\) if \((d,vN)=1\), zero otherwise, by CRT and the expansion of \((h,N)=1\). By (2) and the old analogous \(D_{\rm sharp}\) calculation, the full principal of \(\Delta\) is zero in every such column. The sum of the floors is \(O(x^{.999+o(1)})\), from divisor argument \(d\le Q_0\), changed or original modulus \(\le x^{.499}\) and divisor-power multiplicities. The log and physical \(W_s\) tests have log-bounded variation, so the usual partial summation, or fine relative boxes, applies to their uniformly vanishing principal. This proves (1) with the literal same \(B'\). In particular it does not presume a reverse Möbius BV estimate at the new shorter reflected cutoff.

## 2. The new exact centered mean

Use the actual high quotient chart \(v=saQ\) with true primes and exponent bands
\[
 (.138,.139),\quad (.068,.069),\quad (.297,.310),
\]
so its only change from the high chart of [HigherQuotientReflectedSourceSwitch](HigherQuotientReflectedSourceSwitch.md) is the wider \(Q\) interval. In particular \(v>x^{.503}\), \(b=(N-m)/v<B=x^{.501}<ab\) on the central range (the last inequality uses \(sQ<x^{.449}\)). Its independent \(Q\) label is summed as such; outside the specified triprime packet there could be several eligible labels at a physical \(q\). Let \(E=(x^{.311},x^{.321})\); retain the old \(b\)-Selberg square \({\cal R}(b)\) of divisor cutoff \(x^{.025}\). Define
\[
 h_E=\log(.321/.311),\quad h_Q^*=\log(.310/.297),\quad
 h_a=\log(.069/.068),\quad
 J_s=\int_{.138}^{.139}(1-t)(1-2t)^2\,dt/t.
\]
On this chart the exact whole mean of adding and subtracting the same physical term is
\[
 \frac13\sum_{s,a,Q;\,v\mid N-m}
       W_s((N-m)/s){\cal R}(b)\,1_{\omega_E(m)=1}C_f(m)
   =-(\rho_f+o(1))M_N,\qquad
 \rho_f=\frac{40}{3}J_s h_a h_Q^* (h_E-h_E^2+h_E^3/2)>0 .                  \tag{3}
\]
This is a different mean from the sharp \(\rho\); none of the earlier sharp charges is used with (3).

For each \(j=1,2,3\) mark \(j\) distinct true \(E\)-primes. Source \(m=p u\) with first actual mark \(p\in E\) gives the independent SW factor, while every other mark is kept in the coefficient of \(u\). The expansion of \({\cal R}(b)\) gives independent modulus slots \(q_1=Q,\ q_2=s d,\ q_3=a\), \(d<x^{.05}\); \(s\) is uniquely recovered in \(q_2\), and \(q_1\le x^{.310}<x^{.311}\). The upper limiting exponents of [Lichtman Proposition 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08522#S5) are
\[
 -.001,\qquad .969,\qquad 1.93275,\qquad 1.82425 .
\]
Here the first source exponent is in \([.311,.321]\), \(q_2\) in \([.138,.189]\) and \(q_3\) at most .069; the residue is the actual \(N=2x\). The signed weights have only divisor/log powers, so the clipping and independent fine boxes of the original higher-chart proof apply. No simultaneous factor dependence between \(Q\) and the source mark is introduced. In \(D_f(m)\) no marked \(E\)-prime can divide an active divisor argument, since \(m^\beta<x^{.311}\). If desired remove repeats of any marked prime at power-small cost before the transfer. Moreover \(\Lambda(m)=0\) whenever an \(E\)-prime divides \(m\) here: its third power is \(<x\) and its fourth power \(>1.5x\), for all large \(x\). Thus the true marked test is \(-D_f(m)\).

For the unprogressioned principal let \(F_0\) be the product of the fixed \(j\) marks, \(m=F_0 u_0\) and \(X_0=x/F_0\). Then \(X_0\ge x^{.037}\). All the marked bits are outside the \(f\) support, so apply the divisor sum only to \(u_0\) with the full polynomial coprimality mask \(D=N F_0 v d\). Uniformly for central prefixes, the \(D_f(F_0u_0)\) mean on \((u_0,D)=1\) is exactly \(1\) in units of the *unweighted interval length*, with arbitrary log reserve. To see this even if the \(f\)-divisor exceeds \(X_0^{1/2}\), split at that level. For short divisors, ordinary counting of the unprogressioned multiples gives \(\phi(D)/(D d_0)\) and \(O(\tau(D))\) per divisor, and (2) applies on this polynomial cutoff. For long ones write \(u_0=d_0 h\) so \(h\ll X_0^{1/2}\); the \(\mu(d_0)\) sums with \((d_0,D)=1\) have arbitrary log reserve on their polynomial lengths by the usual uniform coprime Möbius PNT. This follows by convolving \(\mu\) with \(1_{r\mid D^\infty}\), splitting at the square root, and Rankin bounding the remaining \(r\)-mass with \(\prod_{\ell\mid D}(1-\ell^{-3/4})^{-1}=x^{o(1)}\). The test \(\log(F_0 d_0h)f(\log d_0/\log(F_0d_0h))\) and central \(W_s\) have log-bounded variation on each fixed-\(h\) interval, and summing the \(X_0/h\) errors costs only one harmonic log. This compares the actual prefix, not an infinite divisor integral with an omitted tail. The coprime harmonic density cancels exactly in the mean \(1\). After dividing by \(\phi(vd)\), the marked-\(j\) model for \(C_f\) is therefore \(-1\) times the product harmonic mark sum and the same Selberg quadratic \(1/G_R\sim40\mathfrak S(N)/L\). The three contributions in \(1_{\omega_E=1}=\binom{\omega_E}1-2\binom{\omega_E}2+3\binom{\omega_E}3\) now give \(-h_E+h_E^2-h_E^3/2\), proving (3). All principal errors keep log reserve through the harmonic outer sums.

## 3. Uniform absolute principal for the *smooth actual Möbius* score

Let \(\Pi(u)=\prod_{\ell\mid u,\ \ell>2}(1-1/(\ell-1))^{-1}\). For each actual prime \(p\in E\), uniformly in that band, the kernel below satisfies
\[
 \sum_{x/p\le u<3x/(2p)} \Pi(u)\,|D_f(pu)|
                \le (286+o(1))\frac{x}{2p}.                             \tag{4}
\]
There is no roughness or squarefreeness restriction on \(u\). The factor \(\Pi\) is used later for the *actual* local coprimality mask; it is not an assumed independence of small factors.

### The Fourier representation and one uniform limiting argument

Take \(\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R} f(t)e^{-i\xi t}dt\). For \(m=pu\) with \(p\in E\) the \(p\)-divisors are outside support, even if \(p\mid u\). Hence, by Fourier inversion and the exact squarefree-divisor factorization,
\[
 |D_f(pu)|
 \le \frac{\log(pu)}{2\pi}
     \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat f(\xi)|
       \prod_{\ell\mid u}|1-e^{i\xi\log\ell/\log(pu)}|\,d\xi .             \tag{5}
\]
The product counts each prime once, also when \(u\) is not squarefree. All upper integrands are nonnegative, so summing over the actual finite interval before integrating is legitimate. The kernel below has \(\int(1+\xi^2)|\widehat f(\xi)|\,d\xi<\infty\).

Put \(X=x/p\) and \(g_\xi(\ell^j)=\min(2,|\xi|\log\ell/L)\Pi(\ell)\) for \(j\ge1\), multiplicatively extended. It majorizes the product with \(\Pi\) in (5), since \(\log(pu)\ge L\). Its prime-power values are bounded by 4 uniformly in every parameter, and \(g_\xi(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^\varepsilon\) follows from the standard divisor bound (use \(g_\xi\le4^{\omega(n)}\le\tau_4(n)\)). Thus [Shiu's theorem for nonnegative multiplicative functions](https://doi.org/10.1515/CRLL.1980.313.161), with modulus one on a fixed-relative interval, gives
\[
 \sum_{Y\le v\le 2Y} g_\xi(v)
     \ll \frac{Y}{L}(1+|\xi|)^2,\qquad Y\ge X^{1/2},\ Y\ll X.
\]
Indeed \(\Pi(\ell)=1+O(1/\ell)\) for odd primes, and splitting the prime sum for \(\min(2,|\xi|\log\ell/L)\) at \(\exp(L/\max(2,|\xi|))\), using the two elementary prime harmonic estimates, gives
\(\sum_{\ell\le2Y}g_\xi(\ell)/\ell\le2\log(1+|\xi|)+O(1)\). If the split point is below two, the bound follows directly from \(2\sum_{\ell\le2Y}1/\ell\). This supplies the same uniform constant for all \(\xi\), including the infinite-frequency tail of (5). In particular the contribution of \(|\xi|>T\) to the left side of (4), divided by \(X\), tends to zero uniformly as \(T\to\infty\).

For \(|\xi|\le T\) and \(P^-(u)\le x^\kappa\), isolate one small prime \(\ell\) and its exact exponent \(j\), \(u=\ell^j v\), \((v,\ell)=1\). This upper may count more than one small prime. For \(\ell^j\le X^{1/2}\), the same Shiu bound at scale \(Y=X/\ell^j\) gives total
\[
 \ll_T \frac XL\sum_{\ell\le x^\kappa}\frac{\log\ell}{\ell L}
 \ll_T \kappa X/L+O_T(X/L^2).
\]
Here the \(\ell\)-factor is \(O_T(\log\ell/L)\) and the geometric powers cost at most a factor 2. For \(\ell^j>X^{1/2}\) use \(g_\xi\le\tau_4\) and the geometric power tail \(O(X^{-1/2})\) per \(\ell\), getting \(O(X^{1/2}x^\kappa L^3)\) before logarithmic weights, power-small for fixed sufficiently small \(\kappa\). For the remaining \(x^\kappa\)-rough \(u\), squareful ones have power-small total (all squared primes are polynomial), and \(\Pi(u)=1+o_\kappa(1)\) uniformly for the actual bounded number of factors. This is one two-stage limit: retain finite \(T\) and \(\kappa>0\) while taking \(x\) large, let \(\kappa\downarrow0\) for the fixed low-frequency comparison, then \(T\to\infty\). It removes **all** arbitrary small factors at the indicated mean scale. No unquantified Shiu constant enters the final 286.

### Finite-factor densities and the exact envelope

For fixed \(T,\kappa\), use ordinary PNT on the resulting finite number of true prime-product profiles. Fix all but one prime and sum the last by PNT on its polynomial interval; the reciprocal harmonic sums of the other labels give the usual simplex density on \(\sum_i t_i=v\) with \(v=\log X/L=1-\log_x p\). Including \(\log(pu)\) cancels its single logarithmic density denominator. Uniformity in the actual \(p\)-band and \(|\xi|\le T\) follows by finite enclosing boxes, or uniform continuity of these bounded-profile weights on the compact polynomial ranges. Thus an upper envelope for the normalized principal at frequency \(\xi\), after extending the positive finite profile integrals to all ranks and dropping their lower cutoffs, is
\[
 H_\xi(v)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k!}
       \int_{\substack{t_i>0\\ \sum t_i=v}}
          \prod_{i=1}^k \frac{|1-e^{i\xi t_i}|}{t_i}
                \,dt_1\cdots dt_{k-1},
       \qquad .679\le v\le .689 .
\]
The convention for rank one is its single value \(|1-e^{i\xi v}|/v\); the \(k!\) is for the unordered distinct factors. On each simplex \(1/\prod t_i=(1/v)\sum_i1/\prod_{j\ne i}t_j\). Use symmetry, bound the free dependent factor by 2 and enlarge the \(k-1\) independent variables to the full cube. This gives
\[
 H_\xi(v)\le \frac2v\,\exp I(|\xi|v),\qquad
 I(y)=\int_0^y \frac{2|\sin(t/2)|}{t}\,dt.                               \tag{6}
\]
There is no singularity at zero. The companion rational checker verifies
\(\exp I(y)\le 4+1.42 y^2\) for \(y\ge0\). For \(0\le y\le5\) use alternating sine Taylor through its even sixth term after integration, exponential upper Taylor with geometric tail, and 500 monotone rational interval checks. For \(y\ge5\), \(e^{I(y)}/y^2\) is nonincreasing because \(yI'(y)\le2\), and its upper at 5 is already \(<1.42\). Consequently \(H_\xi(v)<12+2\xi^2\). We also use the **subquadratic** bound \(H_\xi(v)\ll (1+|\xi|)^{4/\pi}\): the periodic function \(2|\sin(t/2)|\) has average \(4/\pi\), and its mean-zero part has bounded primitive, giving \(I(y)\le(4/\pi)\log(1+y)+O(1)\) by partial summation. Combining the preceding limiting argument with (6), the desired uniform limsup in (4) is at most
\[
 \sup_{.679\le v\le.689}\frac1{2\pi}
       \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat f(\xi)| H_\xi(v)\,d\xi .
\]

### Choice of the kernel and its strict Fourier bound

Let \(\psi(t)=\cos(\pi t/\beta)\) on \(|t|\le\beta/2\), zero otherwise, \(\beta=.310\), and let \(g=(\psi*\widetilde\psi)/\|\psi\|_2^2\), \(\widetilde\psi(t)=\psi(-t)\). Initially \(f_0(t)=(1-t)g(t)\). Then \(g(0)=1\), \(\widehat g=|\widehat\psi|^2/\|\psi\|_2^2\ge0\), and
\[
 A_\psi=\|\psi'\|_2^2/\|\psi\|_2^2=(\pi/\beta)^2<103 .
\]
All derivatives here may be weak Sobolev derivatives. By Plancherel,
\((2\pi)^{-1}\int\widehat g=1\) and \((2\pi)^{-1}\int\xi^2\widehat g=A_\psi\). Further,
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int|\widehat g'|\le\beta,\qquad
 \frac1{2\pi}\int\xi^2|\widehat g'|\le\beta A_\psi.
\]
The first is Cauchy with \(\|t\psi\|_2\le(\beta/2)\|\psi\|_2\). The second uses Cauchy with \(\|\psi'\|_2\|(t\psi)'\|_2\) and the identity \(\|(t\psi)'\|_2^2=\int t^2|\psi'|^2\), by integration by parts and the zero boundary values of \(\psi\). Since \(|\widehat f_0|\le\widehat g+|\widehat g'|\), the last displayed profile integral for \(f_0\) is at most \((1+\beta)(12+2A_\psi)<285.59\).

To obtain the exact affine neighborhood required by (1), set \(f(t)=(1-t)[g(t)+(1-g(t))\chi(t/\epsilon)]\), where \(\chi\) is a fixed even smooth cutoff, 1 on \([-1,1]\), zero outside \([-2,2]\), and \(\epsilon>0\) is fixed sufficiently small. It is supported on \([-\beta,\beta]\) and equals \(1-t\) near zero. The modification \(r_\epsilon=f-f_0\) has
\(\int(1+|\xi|)^{4/\pi}|\widehat r_\epsilon(\xi)|\,d\xi\ll\epsilon^{2-4/\pi}\to0\). Indeed \(r_\epsilon(\epsilon t)/\epsilon^2\) has support \([-2,2]\) and uniformly bounded \(H^3\) norm: near zero \(g\) is \(1+O(t^2)\), with \(g'\) vanishing at zero and the remaining first three weak derivatives uniformly bounded there. These facts follow directly from the autocorrelation formula
\(g(t)=(1-|t|/\beta)\cos(\pi |t|/\beta)+\sin(\pi |t|/\beta)/\pi\) on its support. Rescale the Fourier transform and use Cauchy with the \(H^3\) norm. For fixed \(\epsilon\) the same argument also gives \(\int(1+\xi^2)|\widehat r_\epsilon|<\infty\), consistent with the Shiu tail above. Thus by the subquadratic bound in (6) the perturbation contributes arbitrarily little, even though a **quadratically** weighted shrinking-neighborhood argument alone would not justify a small error. Choose one fixed \(\epsilon\) sufficiently small that the full profile bound is strictly below 286. This proves (4).

## 4. Real upper with local small factors and the single new budget

Let \(\mathcal D_*\) consist of all actual squarefree triprime \(b\) in the **widened** true high chart from §2 with at least one actual prime in each exponent band
\[
 B_2=(.164,.16524),\qquad B_3=(.16525,.167).
\]
This retains the previous broad bands, not merely the narrower test profile. The unmarked third factor \(r=b/(b_2b_3)\) is at a polynomial scale between \(x^{.149+o(1)}\) and \(x^{.168+o(1)}\), so all three factors exceed the old \(x^{.025}\) divisor cutoff, and \({\cal R}(b)=1\). There is one \(Q\) label and one \(a\) label on each of these actual rows, because their exponents are disjoint from all the \(b\)-factors. Let \(\mathcal B\subset\mathcal D_*\) be the unchanged six-prime narrow source of [CoupledQRShortPart](CoupledQRShortPart.md).

The positive added weight \(\frac13 W_s(-C_f)_+\) on \(\mathcal D_*\setminus\mathcal B\) with exactly one \(E\)-mark is charged by a nonnegative upper that sums all allowed choices of true \(b_2,b_3\). Put \(F=(N-m)/(sa b_2b_3)\). Retain \(s,a,b_2,b_3\) as actual prime labels; drop the two true prime requirements \(F=Qr\) **only** by positive inclusion. The new optimal Selberg square \({\cal R}'(F)\) of divisor cutoff \(R'=x^\delta\), \(\delta=.017\), equals one on the entire true packet. The actual reflected source for the upper is \(m=p u\) with a true prime \(p\in E\), arbitrary \(u\), and nonnegative weight \(|D_f(m)|\); summing all \(p\) is an upper for the exact-one test. The absolute value is placed on the actual divisor statistic after its internal Möbius cancellation, not term-by-term on its divisors.

After expansion of the new square, the modulus slots are \(q_1=s b_2,\ q_2=b_3d,\ q_3=a\), \(d<x^{.034}\), exactly as in [RoughTriprimeDebitBudget](RoughTriprimeDebitBudget.md), with respective strict rail bounds \(-.00676,.981,1.98699,1.866568125\). These slots now bound the full reflected score. The first source is still the genuine prime \(p\), not a synthetic SW hypothesis for \(|D_f|\). The latter is in the long coefficient: freeze the actual \(p\) and \(pu\) on fine relative log meshes for the smooth test \(D_f\) and for the physical endpoints, not for congruence arithmetic. Since \(f\) is Lipschitz and the score is divisor-bounded, the total approximation error is \(O_A(xL^{-A})\) by physical/principal divisor moments; \(|\cdot|\) is Lipschitz as well. Coprime masks on \(N\) are independent on the source factors, and the coefficient clipping of the previously proved transfer gives arbitrary log reserve with the same rails. Thus the progression average is replaced by the reduced principal.

Here small \(u\)-primes **can** divide the sieve modulus. Do not discard their principal unit mask termwise under the signed Selberg coefficients. Write \({\cal R}'(F)=\sum_{d\mid F}\xi_d\), let \(G_{R'}\) be its optimal dimension-one normalizer, and for the finite active prime set \(\ell<R'\), \(\ell\nmid N\), take independent Bernoulli variables of probabilities \(g(\ell)=1/(\ell-1)\). The Selberg quadratic \(\mathbb E(\sum_{d\subseteq X}\lambda_d)^2=1/G_{R'}\). Since \(p>R'\), the exact part of the principal depending on the variable sieve modulus at fixed \(u\) is
\[
 Q_u=\sum_d\frac{\xi_d}{\phi(d)}1_{(u,d)=1}
     =\mathbb E\left[(\sum_{d\subseteq X}\lambda_d)^2\ \middle|\
                    X_\ell=0\text{ for all active }\ell\mid u\right].
\]
Conditioning sets the stated independent labels to zero, hence the equality. By nonnegativity,
\[
             0\le Q_u\le G_{R'}^{-1}
                    \prod_{\substack{\ell\mid u\\ \ell<R',\,\ell\nmid N}}
                     (1-g(\ell))^{-1}\le G_{R'}^{-1}\Pi(u).              \tag{7}
\]
Because \(d\) is coprime by size to the four fixed true prime labels \(s,a,b_2,b_3\), their unit mask factors separately. It can now be discarded, along with \((m,N)=1\), **after** the nonnegative (7), not inside the signed sum. The two possibly omitted actual factors \(Q,r\) were omitted by upper inclusion on the physical square, before any residue estimate. There is therefore no pointwise model assumption on their joint arithmetic and no inferred lower occupancy.

Apply (4), then sum over the genuine \(p\) with \(\sum_{p\in E}1/p=h_E+o(1)\). To keep the normalization completely explicit one may use \(W_s\le W(.138)+o(1)\) in this positive upper; \(J_s\ge W(.139)h_s\), \(h_s=\log(.139/.138)\). The physical length is \(x/2\); the quadratic is \(1/G_{R'}\sim\mathfrak S(N)/(\delta L)\), uniformly in \(N\) by the Euler product proof of the old note. The auxiliary true label sums yield \(h_s h_a h_{B_2}h_{B_3}\). Thus the ratio of the broad upper, **including its actual factor \(1/3\)**, to the single new \(\rho_f\) is at most
\[
 R_*=
 \frac{W(.138)}{W(.139)}
 \frac{286h_E h_{B_2}h_{B_3}}
 {40\delta h_Q^*(h_E-h_E^2+h_E^3/2)}
 <.810 .                                                                \tag{8}
\]
For the narrower actual \(\mathcal B\), the exact \(B'<0\) and \(|B'|<2.1\), by the unmodified divisor calculation in the earlier note. Thus its **entire** corrected positive weight is at most \((2.1+1/3)W_s(-C_f)_+\); use \(7.3\) times the narrow version of (8), with actual narrower \(s,a,b_2,b_3\) bands and \(W(.1384)\) on top. Its exact ratio is \(<.003\). The companion checker certifies both estimates with positive rational logarithm enclosures. The total is less than \(.813\) of the one mean \(\rho_f\). The two literal charges are disjoint by taking the broad added debit on \(\mathcal D_*\setminus\mathcal B\); the upper may overcount \(\mathcal B\) in that broad calculation, which only weakens the bound. At least \(.187\) of the *new* mean remains before other debits. No part of any older sharp mean is used.

This controls every reflected least-factor size on the two stated genuine packets with exactly one \(E\)-mark. It does not price the original \(B'\) positive weight outside \(\mathcal B\), any other \(b\)-shape, or contributions outside that reflected mark condition. All large-\(N\) assertions are asymptotic; no finite verification or Goldbach resolution follows.
