# Almost primary centers: a nullity obstruction and a uniform gap in prime-label sizes

Let \(P=\ell^a\mid N,\ \ell\in\{2,3\}\), and \(R=N/P\); \(N\) is even. An all-composite terminal has \(k\) distinct odd unit prime labels \(x_i\), a loopless irreducible exponent matrix \(A\), and \(N-x_i=\prod x_j^{A_{ij}}\), all rows composite. Write \(B=I-A\), \(t=\max_i\sum_j A_{ij}\), \(H=(1+t)^{k-1}\), and \(L=\log N\).

**Fixed-signature exclusion.** On an unbounded sequence with \(R\) bounded, no fixed terminal signature of rational nullity \(\operatorname{nullity}B\le1\) can recur. More generally, for any fixed signature of nullity one on a sequence with \(R=N^{o(1)}\), every sufficiently large realization has \(\min_i x_i\le 2RH\). This is a spectral restriction on a genuine component, not the assertion that a near-primary center is Goldbach or has no terminals.

**Growing-signature theorem.** Assume only \(\operatorname{nullity}_{\mathbb Q}B=1\) and \(P/H\ge8\). Then either
\[
 \min_i x_i\le2 R H,
\]
or, for every vertex,
\[
                 \log(P/H)\le H\log(kH)+2H\log x_i.                 \tag{1}
\]
Nothing in (1) uses uniformity in Siegel’s theorem or in a fixed-signature algebraic curve. In particular, if \(k\) is bounded and both \(R,t=L^{o(1)}\), all vertex sizes have the following two-scale alternative: either every \(\log x_i=L^{1-o(1)}\), or there is one prime \(x_i=L^{o(1)}\) while all other \(\log x_j=L^{1-o(1)}\). The alternative can differ at different centers; in precise uniform sequence language, call a prime small when it is \(\le2RH\). There is at most one small prime eventually, and along any subsequence every nonsmall label has logarithm \(L^{1-o(1)}\). Thus intermediate scales \(\exp(L^\beta)\) for fixed \(0<\beta<1\) are excluded at nullity one in this regime. For bounded cofactor the ultra-small alternative is excluded in §4; this is not an exclusion of every growing signature.

**Bounded cofactor, arbitrary arity.** Fix \(k\ge2,\ R\le R_0\), and assume nullity one; put \(p=\min_i x_i\). At all sufficiently large realizing centers, even with unbounded arity,
\[
  t(\log p)^{k-1}(1+\log\log p)^{2k-1}\gg_{k,R_0} L.            \tag{A}
\]
If \(t\le L^{1/(k-1)-\epsilon}\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\), one additionally has \(\log x_i\gg_{k,R_0,\epsilon} L/(1+t)^{k-1}\) for every label. Consequently \(t=L^{o(1)}\) with bounded \(R,k\) forces *every* \(\log x_i=L^{1-o(1)}\); e.g. a least label bounded by a fixed power of \(L\) instead requires \(t\gg_{k,R_0,B} L/[(\log L)^{k-1}(1+\log\log L)^{2k-1}]\) when \(p\le L^B\). These all-arity conclusions use only the finite fixed-signature/seed exclusions plus explicit uniform local bounds, not a uniform Siegel theorem.

## 1. Signed cyclic parametrization, allowing a growing Smith loss

The signed local unit group \((\mathbb Z/P)^\times/\{\pm1\}\) is cyclic: for \(\ell=2,\ a\ge3\) it is generated by 5 and has order \(2^{a-2}\); for \(\ell=3\) its order is \(3^{a-1}\), generated for example by the class of 4. These hold compatibly on reduction to smaller powers. Logarithms in this finite cyclic group convert the actual signed equations into \(B b=0\bmod |G|\). Let \(d\) be the last nonzero Smith invariant of \(B\), and \(v,c\) primitive integral right and left nullvectors. We have \(d,\max|v_i|,\max|c_i|\le H\): the product of the nonzero Smith invariants is the gcd of the nonzero maximal minors, and a nonzero cofactor vector made from independent rows or columns is a multiple of the corresponding primitive vector. Bound every such minor by row Hadamard using the Euclidean row norms of \(B\), each \(\le1+t\).

For clarity write \(|G|=\ell^n\), \(j=v_\ell(d)\). Our cutoff ensures \(a-j\ge3\) for \(\ell=2\), \(a-j\ge2\) for \(\ell=3\). Thus the compatible generator stays a generator after reduction, and the signed group modulo \(P'\) has order exactly \(\ell^{n-j}\). In integral Smith coordinates each nonzero diagonal \(d_h\mid d\) requires \(d_h z_h=0\bmod\ell^n\): the prime-to-\(\ell\) part is invertible, so \(z_h\equiv0\bmod\ell^{n-j}\). This proves that modulo \(|G|/\ell^{v_\ell(d)}\), \(b\) is a scalar multiple of the primitive free column \(v\): each torsion coordinate in the Smith basis is killed modulo this quotient, and the last column of the right basis generates \(\ker_{\mathbb Z} B\). Accordingly for \(P'=P/\ell^{v_\ell(d)}\ge P/H\) there are a unit \(u\) and signs \(\sigma_i=\pm1\) such that
\[
                          x_i\equiv \sigma_i u^{v_i}\pmod {P'} .      \tag{2}
\]
The cutoff \(P/H\ge8\) keeps the reduced cyclic group in the stated range. The reduction assertion is about 2 and 3; primes admitting additional local roots of unity cannot be substituted here.

If \(\min x_i>2RH\), every vertex is \(<P'/2\): it occurs in a composite product with another prime factor at least \(\min x_i\). Then (2) forces all \(v_i\) nonzero and pairwise distinct. A zero would give a label \(\pm1\) modulo \(P'\); equal exponents would give two distinct positive primes equal up to sign modulo \(P'\), impossible in this range.

For a fixed signature the near-primary condition \(R=N^{o(1)}\) gives \(N_{\{\ell\}}=N^{1-o(1)}\). If \(\min x_i>2RH\) were to recur, the distinct \(v_i\) give a unique exponent on the support of \(c\), so the [finite-torus theorem](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md) excludes that sequence. If \(R\) is bounded instead, the remaining inequality \(\min x_i\le2RH\) selects a fixed prime on a subsequence. The [fixed-seed independence theorem](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) would give nullity at least two. Nullity zero is independently excluded by the bounded-order path lemma in the finite-torus proof, or by applying the adjugate and the cyclotomic approximation at a smallest label \(\le\sqrt N\). This proves the fixed-signature claims.

## 2. A sparse resultant whose coefficients may grow

Keep \(\min x_i>2RH\) and allow \(A\) to change. The exact left-null identity
\[
 \prod_i (1-N/x_i)^{c_i}=(-1)^{\sum c_i}
\]
first gives an even sum by reduction modulo \(N>2\), then, by expansion modulo \(N^2\) in the rationals with denominators coprime to \(N\), gives \(\sum c_i/x_i\equiv0\bmod N\). This allows negative powers. From (2), \(\sum c_i\sigma_i u^{-v_i}\equiv0\bmod P'\).

For any prescribed \(j\) replace the common parameter by its inverse and every \(v_i\) by its negative if needed so \(v_j=m>0\). Multiply the last Laurent polynomial by a power of its variable to give an ordinary \(G(U)\in\mathbb Z[U]\) with nonzero constant term, at most \(k\) terms of magnitude \(\le H\), and degree \(\le 2H\). Because the \(v_i\) are distinct, the constant term is exactly one nonzero signed \(c_i\), without cancellation. One has \(G(u)=0\bmod P'\), \(u^m\equiv \sigma_j x_j\bmod P'\). Consider the integer polynomial
\[
 F_j(X)=\operatorname{Res}_U(U^m-\sigma_j X,G(U)).
\]
The resultant is an integral polynomial combination of its two input polynomials, so \(P'\mid F_j(x_j)\), also when \(P'\) is composite. Its constant coefficient is \(G(0)^m\ne0\). If its value at the prime \(x_j\) were zero as an integer, \(x_j\) would divide \(G(0)\), impossible since \(x_j>H\). On the other hand its complex product formula over the \(m\) roots, each of modulus \(x_j^{1/m}\), gives
\[
 0<|F_j(x_j)|\le (kH)^m x_j^{\deg G}.
\]
Using \(1\le m\le H\) now proves (1).

## 3. At most one ultra-small prime

Suppose \(k\) is bounded, \(R,t=L^{o(1)}\). Then \(H=L^{o(1)}\). If no small label occurs, (1) gives \(\log x_i\ge L^{1-o(1)}\), as required. Otherwise write \(p=\min x_i\le2RH=L^{o(1)}\), so \(\log p=o(\log L)\). For each other label \(q\) there is an actual nonzero integral relation supported on \(p,q\) with exponents \(\le H^2\) in magnitude. Indeed the rational row space is \(v^\perp\); if \((v_p,v_q)\ne(0,0)\), multiply \(v_q e_p-v_p e_q\) by \(d\) to enter the integral row lattice. If both vanish, use \(d e_p\). The latter multiplier works because \(d\) annihilates the quotient of the saturated row lattice by the row lattice. Squaring gives \(p^b q^e\equiv1\bmod P\) with \(\max(|b|,|e|)\le2H^2\), nonzero as an exponent pair.

If only the \(p\) exponent occurs, ordinary LTE gives \(\log P\ll_\ell \log p+\log(2H)\), impossible; if only the \(q\) exponent occurs, the same bound gives \(\log q\gg L\). For example, for an odd local prime take an appropriate power dividing \(\ell-1\), and for 2 use \(r^2-1\) at the odd unit \(r\). With both exponents nonzero, the two distinct primes are multiplicatively independent rational \(\ell\)-units. The [Bugeaud–Laurent two-logarithm bound, §2, Théorème 3/Corollaire 1](https://irma.math.unistra.fr/~bugeaud/travaux/logpadicdef.pdf), applied to powers of \(p^{\pm1},q^{\pm1}\), gives
\[
 \log P\ll_\ell (\log p)(\log q)\,(1+\log(2H))^2 .
\]
The height choices are \(\max(\log p,\log\ell)\), \(\max(\log q,\log\ell)\); the normalized coefficient parameter is at most \(O_\ell(H^2)\). These comparisons are uniform for \(\ell=2,3\) and \(p,q\ge3\). Since \(\log P=L-o(L)\), and the factors other than \(\log q\) on the right are \(L^{o(1)}\), we get \(\log q=L^{1-o(1)}\). This applies to every other label and proves the claimed scale gap. The finite-torus theorem is used only in the fixed-signature part; the growing-signature conclusion uses the explicit Smith loss, resultant and two-log bound.

## 4. The projected-minor bootstrap at bounded cofactor

Keep \(R\le R_0,\ k\) fixed, write \(q\) for the second least label and \(T=L/\log q>1\). All inequalities below are uniform in the two primes \(\ell=2,3\). Each row has \(t\le L/\log p\); more precisely, the \(p\)-column entries of \(B\) have magnitude \(\le1+t\) and all *other* entries magnitude \(\le 1+T\), by the actual integer row products.

On any unbounded sequence of nullity-one realizations, \(p\to\infty\): otherwise the bounded label is a fixed seed on a subsequence, contradicting the [bounded-orbit fixed-seed theorem](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) since \(N_{\{\ell\}}\ge N/R_0\). Likewise \(\log p=o(L)\): if \(p\ge N^\delta\) on a subsequence for a fixed \(\delta>0\), \(t\le1/\delta\) and only finitely many matrices occur, contrary to §1. For large \(N\), \(v_p\ne0\). Indeed if \(v_p=0\) the integral row lattice contains \(d e_p\), so \(p^{2d}\equiv1\bmod P\). LTE gives \(\log P\le O_\ell(\log p+\log(2d))=o(L)\) since \(d\le H=O_k(L^{k-1})\), a contradiction. These compactness arguments give uniform eventuality over all graphs with fixed \(k,R_0\).

The \(k-1\) columns other than \(p\) are linearly independent (a nontrivial relation between them would give a right kernel vector with zero \(p\)-coordinate). Set \(U=1+T\), and take \(C_k\ge 2 k^{k/2}\). The previous cofactor estimates applied columnwise now give
\[
 d,\ |v_p|,\ \max_i|c_i|\le C_k U^{k-1}=:J,\qquad
 \max_{j\ne p}|v_j|\le C_k(1+t)U^{k-2}.                          \tag{3}
\]
For \(d\), bound one nonzero minor omitting \(p\); for \(c\), use that full column set; for \(v\), take independent rows and omit the prescribed column. Each column norm in such a minor costs at most \(\sqrt k\) times its largest entry.

The primitive row-space relation from §3, multiplied by \(2d\), has actual congruence \(p^b q^e\equiv1\bmod P\), with \(e\ne0\) and
\[
 |b|\ll_k(1+t)U^{2k-3},\qquad |e|\ll_k U^{2k-2}.
\]
If \(b=0\), LTE yields \(L\ll_{k,R_0}\log q+\log U\); since \(U\le 1+L/\log3\), this implies \(T=O_{k,R_0}(1)\) for large \(N\). Otherwise use Bugeaud–Laurent as in §3: take bases \(p^{\operatorname{sgn}b},q^{-\operatorname{sgn}e}\) and positive powers \(|b|,|e|\). Their power difference has the same valuation as \(p^b q^e-1\), and is nonzero by distinct primes. Both fields are rational (so the paper's \(D=f=1\)); take \(\log A_1=\max(\log p,\log\ell),\ \log A_2=\max(\log q,\log\ell)\). The normalized parameter \(b'=|b|/\log A_2+|e|/\log A_1\ll_k U^{2k-2}\), even if \(t\) is large: \(t/\log q\le T/\log p\). Theorem 3 or Corollary 1 gives
\[
 T\ll_{k,R_0}(\log p)(1+\log U)^2,\qquad
 U\ll_{k,R_0}(\log p)(1+\log\log p)^2.                            \tag{4}
\]
For the inversion, \(s/(1+\log s)^2\) is eventually increasing and its inverse grows \(O(y(1+\log y)^2)\), by direct substitution. The estimate holds also when \(b=0\).

We claim \(p>2R J\) eventually. If not, (3) implies \(\log p\ll_{k,R_0}1+\log U\), so (4) implies \(T\ll_{k,R_0}(1+\log U)^3\). Thus \(T\), then \(p\), is bounded, contradicting \(p\to\infty\). This small determinant loss is decisive even if \(H\) itself is large. Now \(P'\ge P/J\) and every label is \(<N/p<P'/2\), so (2) guarantees pairwise distinct nonzero right-kernel exponents and \(p>J\) exceeds every extreme coefficient of the Laurent polynomial. For large \(N\), \(P/J\ge8\) since \(J=O_k(L^{k-1})\). Consequently §2 at the least vertex is valid with the sharper data (3): \(m=|v_p|\le J\), every coefficient of \(G\) has magnitude \(\le J\), and \(\deg G\ll_k(1+t)U^{k-2}+U^{k-1}\). The latter is in fact \(\ll_k(1+t)U^{k-2}\): the row giving the occurrence of some non-\(p\) label does not alone force \(T\le t\), but if \(t<T\) use the original bound \(|v_p|\le (1+t)^{k-1}\le (1+t)U^{k-2}\); if \(t\ge T\) use \(J\). Thus
\[
 L\ll_{k,R_0} U^{k-1}(1+\log U)+(1+t)U^{k-2}\log p.              \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(\log P'\ge L-O_{k,R_0}(\log L)\). Substitute (4), using \(1+\log U\ll_{k,R_0}1+\log\log p\) and \(t\ge2\), to get (A).

For the further power-range conclusion, if \(p\le 2RH\) along a sequence then \(\log p=O_{k,R_0}(\log L)\) since \(H=O_k(L^{k-1})\). Estimate (A) would force \(t\ge L^{1-o(1)}\), incompatible with \(t\le L^{1/(k-1)-\epsilon}\), including when \(k=2\). Thus \(p>2RH\) eventually, and (1) gives the conclusion since \(H\log(kH)=o(L)\) and \(\log(P/H)=(1-o(1))L\). If \(t=L^{o(1)}\), the same applies. No statement bounds the number of prime factors in an arbitrary graph globally, nor rules out every nullity-one growing matrix.
