# Unbounded seed-exponent columns reduce to finitely many one-parameter families

Fix the seed p and projected chart of the [ramified seed theorem](PrimeOrbitRamifiedSeedList.md). Any putative unbounded near-smooth sequence with five polynomial-sized moving labels can be restricted, after effectively enumerable reductions depending only on that chart and p, to one of finitely many one-dimensional signed p-power families. More precisely, apart from finitely many exceptional physical centers, the translate coordinates λ_j and the nonseed-input exponents a_i of p lie on finitely many affine integer lines:

    λ_j=σ_j p^(b_j⁰+n_j m),       a_i=a_i⁰+s_i m,       m∈Z.

Orient an unbounded line so m→+∞ on a subsequence. Then every s_i≥0 and at least two are positive. The origin offsets do not assert that every integer on the line actually works. This restriction does not bound m or settle the residual one-parameter families. In particular the one-variable residual cover can have other ramifications over the omitted parameters 0 and infinity; the finite interior collision list below does not count these two boundary fibers.

We give both the geometric obstruction and the finite reduction, keeping factorization over the base function field rather than assuming any putative residue root lies in a fixed number field.

## The parameter torus and its fixed collision points

Use the notation of [the étale zero algebra](PrimeOrbitSlowCurveEtaleFiber.md) and the seed theorem. For the fixed signed primitive v on the five moving columns choose a fixed integral ω with ω·v=1. Work in coefficient torus (G_m)^5 over Q with coordinates Y_j representing the **signed** λ_j; append Y₀=p, v₀=0. The gauge is Y^ω=1, because Z=X^ω and X_j/Y_j=Z^(v_j) in a nonzero algebra. At every actual point the two further toric identities are

    ∏_{j>0} Y_j^(A′_0j)=−p,      ∏_{i=0}^5 Y_i^(l_i)=1,

where l annihilates A′ and l·1=0. They follow by the zero-fiber row equations together with A′_0v=0 and ∑l_i v_i=0. In particular these hold identically on the Zariski closure of *signed* coefficient tuples. We may work there or inside this explicit closed toric locus. All starting parameter points have Y_j∈{±p^m:m∈Z}.

For the two fixed integral left eigenvectors c use

    Q_c(Y,Z)=Σ_i c_i Y_i^(−1) Z^(−v_i).

For each row c, clear the lowest power of Z and divide by its leading coefficient. All v_i are distinct, so that coefficient and the constant coefficient are nonzero Laurent monomials (times fixed constants), invertible on the torus. We have monic ordinary Z-polynomials with unit constant, with no loss of roots in the torus. Their common zero fixes a vector h_i=Y_i^−1 Z^−v_i, with h₀=1/p. Any nonzero *common double root* satisfies C h=C diag(v)h=0. By the full linear/divisor argument in the seed theorem, among projective solutions [h] of these equations with no zero coordinates the condition ∏h_i^(l_i)=1 selects a finite explicit list; the product has total degree zero and is nonconstant on that fixed line. All the actual toric constraints hold over the coefficient closure, so each double root gives one of these finitely many tuples after normalization h₀=1/p. Finally

    Z=∏_{j>0}h_j^(−ω_j),       Y_j=h_j^−1 Z^(−v_j)

by the gauge. Thus the **entire coefficient vector** at a common double root lies in a *fixed finite geometric set*. This assertion is in characteristic zero, not an assertion that the same number field contains all actual residue roots.

## Why a higher-dimensional torus stratum cannot carry an unbounded open sequence

Let W be any connected rational subtorus translate inside the explicit toric locus, containing a signed rational p-power point as base point. Its coordinate ring A is a Laurent polynomial ring over Q after translation and a basis of the saturated character lattice; in particular it is integrally closed. Let D(Z) be the monic gcd of the two normalized Q_c over **Q(W)**, allowing D=1. Its distinct monic irreducible factors G over Q(W) lie in A[Z]: roots of the monic Q_c are integral over A, so the coefficients of a monic factor are integral and in the fraction field. Their constants are Laurent units, since they divide a product with unit constant. Monic division gives the factor identities over A. Each G is separable over Q(W), hence has nonzero discriminant in A.

If dim W≥2, every such discriminant is a Laurent **unit**. A geometric zero y would produce a repeated nonzero Z-root of G(y,Z); since G divides both Q_c over A, this is a common double root of the Q_c at y. The preceding section confines such y to a finite set. A nonconstant nonunit Laurent polynomial on a split torus of dimension ≥2 has a positive-dimensional geometric zero set (by the principal ideal theorem, or by specialization in the other variables). It follows the discriminant is a unit, regardless of whether G would split over Qbar(W). The unit is a fixed nonzero rational scalar times a Laurent character of W.

The generic gcd remains the whole gcd on an explicit nonempty open subset U of W: divide each normalized Q_c by D in A[Z], then invert their nonzero resultant (or use their Bézout certificate over Q(W)); constants cause no difficulty. For y∈U the actual monic irreducible g_y of degree d≥2 must divide one of the G(y,Z), since it divides their generic gcd D(y,Z) and g_y is irreducible over Q. A generic repeated factor is harmless: work with the distinct G when bounding their splitting fields. If D=1, no g_y can occur on U.

At rational signed p-power y∈W all Laurent characters of W, translated by the chosen base point, are signed p-powers. Indeed every character lifts to an integral Laurent monomial in the ambient coordinates modulo their character relations. The finitely many G(y,Z) are therefore monic with coefficients integral outside a fixed set S of rational primes, and their discriminants are units outside S, uniformly over y∈U. Include p and fixed rational denominators/numerator primes of the discriminant scalars in S. Each splitting field is unramified outside S and has degree bounded by the degree factorial of the fixed polynomial. Their collective compositum is contained in a fixed number field by Hermite–Minkowski. Since g_y divides one of these polynomials, its own splitting field is contained there. On an infinite sequence this contradicts the established transitivity over every fixed field together with d≥2. Thus at most finitely many centers of the specified asymptotic sequence can occur with coefficients on this open part of W. If dim W=0 the same conclusion holds by fixed coefficients (after that substitution, take the gcd of the two nonzero polynomials; the original distinct v ensure neither is identically zero).

In dimension one, a generic factor giving genuinely moving residue fields must similarly have nonunit discriminant. Hence it has at least one interior double-root specialization on G_m and all of those specializations are on the explicit collision list. This concerns the polynomial discriminant. An interior double specialized root may reflect a non-normal presentation and is not by itself a proof of geometric ramification. However if the normalization of *every geometric generic factor* of G over Qbar(t) were unramified throughout G_m, then all its specializations on the actual signed p-power points would split over one fixed field. Indeed on each geometrically connected degree-b cover the only possible ramification is then over 0 and infinity. Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2 genus−2=−n₀−n∞, where n₀,n∞ are the numbers of points above those two values. Hence genus is zero, n₀=n∞=1, and in a coordinate s the map is t=κs^b. Z is a unit outside those two points (the polynomial is monic with unit constant), so Z=γs^j. These fixed algebraic constants and each fixed b-th root of any signed p-power all lie in one fixed field after adjoining the relevant roots of p and unity. Here t is a primitive character parameter after translating W by a signed p-power base point, hence is itself a signed p-power at physical points. This would contradict the moving splitting fields. Thus some generic factor genuinely ramifies in the interior on any surviving open pencil (after discarding nongeneric specializations). That interior branch lies on the finite collision list; this does not assert an inertia index greater than two. For instance a single simple branch would require 2 genus−2+n₀+n∞=1, which admits genus zero with n₀+n∞=3, so Riemann–Hurwitz alone does not rule it out.

## Effective finite union of parameter lines, not merely a choice of subsequence

The finite reductions above can be applied recursively without an ineffective enumeration of all subtorus translates. For clarity, here is the elementary rank-one-base version of the ordinary torus intersection theorem. On fixing the finitely many sign choices, substitute Y_j=σ_j p^(b_j) into any chosen Laurent polynomial over Q; its zero condition is a finite sum ∑ c_i p^(μ_i·b)=0 with fixed nonzero integral c_i. Partition the indices into minimal zero subsums. Inside one block, order the integer exponents and let C=∑|c_i|. Every gap between consecutive exponents is at most log_p C: the subtotal through a smaller exponent is nonzero by minimality, is divisible by the next power of p after clearing a common p-power, and has absolute value ≤C times the largest p-power used so far. Therefore all exponent differences in that block are bounded by (#terms)log_p C. Enumerate the finitely many partitions and the bounded integer differences, retain only exact zero sums, and solve the resulting linear equations μ·b−μ′·b=k over Z by Smith normal form. Intersect finite unions for several polynomials. Thus the signed p-power points in every such closed locus are exactly covered by finitely many computable affine lattices. The Zariski closure of each nonempty affine integer lattice is a rational translate of a connected split subtorus: use an actual signed p-power point and a Z-basis of the integer directions, then substitute monomials; the independent powers p^m in separate variables are Zariski dense in their torus. A lower-dimensional direction lattice in the integer parametrization cannot have a full-dimensional dense image when the parametrization directions have full column rank.

Start with the explicit toric locus and the sign/gauge constraints, use this procedure and work on each resulting W. On a stratum of dimension ≥2 the complement of U above is a proper closed locus, defined by fixed rational Laurent polynomials on W; these may be lifted to ambient characters/rational coordinates after translation. Its signed p-power points yield finitely many strata of strictly lower dimension by the same procedure. Iterate, descending in dimension. On each open part removed there is, as shown, no unbounded physical subsequence. There are finitely many parts. The only possible unbounded accumulation therefore lies on the computed one-dimensional affine integer lattices; zero-dimensional strata cannot carry an unbounded sequence. This proves the finite-union assertion. The result is effective as a covering of **potential parameter lines**, not an effective bound on the exceptional centers in the discarded strata.

Finally the exact relation a=e₀+B′b, with components indexed by all six inputs, makes the actual seed exponents affine integer functions of the same m. A line direction n with B′n=0 must be a multiple of v; the gauge ω·n=0 and ω·v=1 force n=0. On an unbounded feasible ray orient its nonzero slope s=B′n so s≥0. Also s belongs to col A′ (since a lies there) and to col B′. The no-single-hot spectral argument at the end of [the subpower seed-mass proof](PrimeOrbitSixSubpowerSeedMass.md) rules out support size one for any nonzero vector in that intersection supported at one index: it would place that coordinate vector in both fixed column spaces, and deletion leaves a forbidden five-by-five spectrum. Thus at least two s_i are positive.
