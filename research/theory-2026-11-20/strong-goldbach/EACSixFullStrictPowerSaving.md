# A strict power saving below three quarters in the full six-label center count

Let an EAC be an actual factor-closed strongly connected component of distinct odd primes \(x_i\), coprime to an even \(N\), such that each \(N-x_i\) is composite, factors entirely over the six labels, and the arcs point from the input label to those factors. **Theorem.** for some absolute, not explicitly effective \(\epsilon>0\), the number of even \(N\le X\) carrying such a six-label EAC is \(X^{3/4-\epsilon+o(1)}\). The same count then applies to EACs of at most six labels using the earlier square-root result for at most five. This counts actual component-bearing centers, not the exceptional set of binary Goldbach; no bounded component size is assumed at a hypothetical Goldbach failure.

Let \(p<q\) denote the least two labels, \(M=\max x_i<N/p\), \(A_{ij}=v_{x_j}(N-x_i)\), \(B=I-A\), \(\nu=\dim\ker B\). All factors are odd and each label occurs as output by strong connectivity; every row complement lies in \((2N/3,N)\). Also \(q<\sqrt N\) since the \(p\)-row does not use \(p\).

## 1. Counting inputs and precise remaining compactness problem

Use the existing \(X^{1/2+o(1)}\) counts for invertible \(A\) or \(B\), the general supported-minor lemma, and the upward-forest bound \(\nu\le2\). Corank one with \(\det A=0\) has count \(X^{7/10+o(1)}\) by [the seven-tenths least-product proof](EACSixSevenTenthsProduct.md) (the sharper [two-thirds bound](EACSixTwoThirdsProduct.md) is not needed here). Thus only \(\nu=2,\det A=0\) matters. The existing unconditional six-vertex arity≤3 obstruction forces an actual row of arity at least four, hence \(p<N^{1/4}\). Rank \(A=5\): if two zero eigenvalues coexist with two unit eigenvalues and the Perron root \(\rho\), trace zero forces the missing eigenvalue \(-2-\rho\), impossible.

Fix \(\eta=1/16\). When \(q\le N^\eta\), the least three labels are a supported-minor set of product at most \(N^{1/2+2\eta}\), since corank two forces at least three labels \(\le\sqrt N\) by the retained-set rank lemma. This yields \(X^{5/8+o(1)}\). Otherwise the [previous tangent lemma, §2](EACSixAllCenters.md) applies upon freezing the least label \(p\): dimension at most one for this system, and if one, some additional raw coordinate \(\le\sqrt N\) moves. As usual fix a moving additional coordinate and select five independent equations (Bézout, polylogarithmically many charts and bounded polylog degrees). So for \(p\le N^{1/4-\delta}\), this costs \(X^{3/4-\delta+o(1)}\) for any fixed \(\delta>0\). It remains to get a strict saving in a uniform neighborhood of \(p\) of weight \(1/4\).

For charts there, if any complementary row is a pure fourth power \(s^4\), of **any** label \(s\), freeze \(s\) and no other raw input initially. Otherwise freeze \(p\). With \(p>N^{1/4-\delta_0}\) for fixed \(\delta_0<1/100\), all rows have arity at most four eventually, and \(M/N\to0\). The choice of pure seed can be made deterministic in each chart. If the frozen seed is a nonminimal \(s\), no use is made of the old frozen-least-\(p\) lemma: put \(A'\) equal to \(A\) without the \(s\)-column. Its rank is at least four since \(A\) has rank five. At the pure row \(A'\) vanishes, so \(\mathbf1\) is independent of its columns. Hence \([A'\mid-\mathbf1]\) has rank at least five. The exact logarithmically scaled differential matrix is a perturbation by \(O(M/N)=o(1)\) of this fixed-chart matrix; thus the frozen-\(s\) tangent dimension is at most one as well. This applies uniformly across the finitely many arity≤4 charts. **No cost for also freezing \(p\)** is incurred in this pure-seed branch.

In a pure-fourth chart distinguish the exact divisor lane
\[
 N-r=s^4,\qquad N-u=ra,\qquad u^2\ \text{and at least one further factor divide }N-a
\]
where “further” counts multiplicities. Here \(s\le N^{1/4}\) and \(u^2p<N\), so \(u\le N^{3/8+\delta_0/2}\). The identity \(r(a-1)=s^4-u>0\) shows that, given \(s,u\), each divisor \(r\) fixes \(N=s^4+r\). It costs at most \(X^{5/8+\delta_0/2+o(1)}\), not requiring \(p\) or the second usual tangent coordinate. We will show that outside this exact lane either the frozen tangent vanishes or it has a moving label \(\le N^{1/2-\delta}\) for a uniform \(\delta>0\), for all sufficiently large \(N\) with \(p>N^{1/4-\delta}\). When a moving label exists, after freezing it, five variables remain including \(N\); a \(5\times5\) full-rank subsystem and Bézout apply. If the tangent already vanishes, freezing the single chosen seed is enough, using the six-equation Jacobian. Thus the total cost there is \(X^{3/4-\delta+o(1)}\).

Suppose no such uniform \(\delta\) exists. Take a sequence \(N\to\infty\), \(\delta_j\downarrow0\), with \(p>N^{1/4-\delta_j}\), outside the divisor lane, and with a nonzero frozen tangent but **no moving raw coordinate of value \(\le N^{1/2-\delta_j}\)**. Fix a chart and convergent weights \(\omega_i=\lim\log x_i/\log N\); \(\omega_p=1/4\). All labels of limiting weight \(<1/2\) have **exactly zero derivative eventually**, not just zero limiting derivative: they lie below that explicit cutoff at all sufficiently large terms of the sequence, by the strict weight separation. In particular there are at least two such labels, \(p,q\), because \(pq\le N^{7/10+o(1)}\). If no pure fourth power occurs, any arity-four row must use at least two factor labels of weight \(1/4\), so in that case \(\omega_q=1/4\).

Use \(h_i=\delta x_i/x_i,\ n=\delta N/N\), and normalized nonzero vectors in the one-dimensional tangent. At any actual arity-four row all four factors have weight \(1/4\) (sum of weights \(1\), each at least \(1/4\)); those inputs to the product have exact zero differentials by the preceding paragraph. Thus \(\delta x_i=\delta N\) at that row and \(n=(x_i/N)h_i\to0\). The tangent equations \(Ah-n\mathbf1=C(n\mathbf1-h)\), with \(C_{ii}=x_i/(N-x_i)=o(1)\), now give a nonzero normalized limit along the unique kernel direction \(k\) of \(A\). Pass to real \(k\) if needed (the complex limit is a nonzero scalar multiple of one). Its support \(S\) consists of labels which **actually move** along the sequence for sufficiently large \(N\), and has weights at least \(1/2\).

Every row containing an \(S\) factor must contain a factor of opposite nonzero \(k\)-sign by \(Ak=0\). Since weights in \(S\) are at least \(1/2\) and every other weight is positive, such a row has exactly two factors, both in \(S\), no repetitions. Every \(S\) label occurs, so all its weights are exactly \(1/2\). The cofactor graph on \(S\) made by these pairs is connected bipartite (distinct connected components would give additional independent kernel vectors) and has no isolated vertices. Put \(t=|S|\), \(Z=S^c\). Then \(2\le t\le4\) since \(p,q\notin S\). The \(S\)-pair rows have rank \(t-1\); the disjoint-column \(Z\)-only rows have rank \(6-t\) by rank \(A=5\). Therefore the number of \(S\)-pairs is \(t-1\) or \(t\); the only nontree option is \(C_4\) at \(t=4\).

A \(C_4\) on \(S\) is always impossible in this limit: if its two shores have ordered factor values \(a<a'\), \(b<b'\), the input for its minimum product \(ab\) exceeds both \(2a'\) and \(2b'\). For instance start with the positive input of \(a'b'\) and compare successively to \(a'b\) and \(ab'\); each factor change has even gap at least 2. This larger input cannot be in \(S\), and the only two other labels, \(p,q\), have weights \(<1/2\). The remaining analysis is of trees.

## 2. Any pure-fourth seed: extend the pure-least argument without extra parameters

Let \(N-r=s^4\) be the pure row, so \(\omega_s=1/4\), \(s\notin S\), \(s\sim N^{1/4}\). The previously isolated pure-least argument holds with \(s\) in place of its distinguished frozen label. The following details justify this even when \(s\ne p\).

* \(t=4\): \(Z=\{p,q\}\) now includes \(s\). If the other label has weight \(\beta\in(1/4,1/2)\), the only possible nonpure-\(s^4\) exponent choices satisfying \(i/4+j\beta=1\), \(i+j\le4\), are \(s u^2\) at \(\beta=3/8\) or \(u^3\) at \(\beta=1/3\), never both. So at most two \(Z\)-products but three are required. If both have weight \(1/4\), their distinct quartic monomials differ by at least \(2\min(s,u)^3=N^{3/4+o(1)}\) (monotone as the exponent of the greater factor increases), while \(M=N^{1/2+o(1)}\).
* For \(t=2,3\) choose a nonzero eigenvalue-1 vector with coordinate \(s=0\). Then \(r=0\) in this vector. Both signs must have a directed cycle, so the remaining four coordinates form two nonzero mutual sign pairs. A nonzero \(S\) label needs its distinct sign mate as input of an \(S\)-pair row, so \(r\in S\); otherwise \(t\) such rows are needed and a tree has \(t-1\). Also some eigenvalue-1 vector has nonzero \(s\); if all had \(s=r=0\), dimension two would give a nonzero vector with a third zero, impossible by the sign-cycle support rule.
* \(t=3\): \(S=\{r,a,b\}\), \(Z=\{s,u,v\}\), \(u<v\). At least one of \(u,v\) has weight \(<1/2\) because \(p,q\) are small and \(s\) is only one label, so \(\omega_u<1/2\). If \(a,b\) are mutual they supply both \(S\)-pair rows: the cofactor star center must be \(r\) to avoid self-factors; the products \(br,ar\) give \(b-a=r(b-a)\), impossible. Otherwise \(a,b\) have mates \(u,v\), so inputs \(u,v\) supply the two \(S\)-pair products, and two \(Z\)-only rows at \(a,b\) contain \(u,v\) respectively. The other \(Z\)-only inputs are \(s,r\). If \(\omega_v<1/2\), the \(u,v\) input difference is too small for a product difference on the \(S\)-star, at least twice its weight-\(1/2\) center. If \(\omega_v>1/2\), \(v=M\) occurs as a factor at most once, so the \(s\)-row is \(u^j\) with \(j=3,4\). The fourth-power option is impossible: then \(s,u\sim N^{1/4}\), their absolute fourth-power gap is at least \(2\min(s,u)^3\), and \(M<N/p\sim N^{3/4}\) since here \(p=\min(s,u)\). If \(j=3\), the mate row containing \(u\) must be a monomial on \(s,u\); \(i/4+j'/3=1\) with \(j'>0\) forces identical \(u^3\), impossible. Thus \(\omega_v=1/2\). If \(\omega_u>1/4\), the four products must be \(s^4,v^2,s^2v\) and the unique possible \(u\) monomial (same weight enumeration as in §2, with \(i/4+j\omega_u=1/2\) yielding no \(j>0\) term when multiplied by \(v\)). Thus \(v\sim\sqrt N\). The \(S\)-star center \(c\le(v-u)/2\le(1/2+o(1))\sqrt N\), and its leaves differ by \((v-u)/c=N^{o(1)}\). The three products \(s^4,s^2v,v^2\) have gaps \(\sim\!2\sqrt N\) or larger; at most one of their inputs can come from \(\{s,u,v,c\}\) below \((1+o(1))\sqrt N\), and at most one from the two close leaves. Finally if \(\omega_u=1/4\), quartics on \(s,u\) have gaps \(\ge2\min(s,u)^3\gg M\), so only the existing \(s^4\). Products \(v\) times distinct quadratics on \(s,u\) also have gaps \(\ge2v\min(s,u)\gg M\), allowing at most one. Along with \(v^2\) these give at most three, not four. Thus \(t=3\) is impossible.
* \(t=2\): \(S=\{r,a\}\), the mate \(u\) of \(a\) inputs the sole pair \(ra\), and \(u\) occurs as factor in \(a\)'s row. In our eigenvector with \(s=r=0\), the \(u\) and \(a\) coordinates are equal and the two remaining nonzero coordinates have their common opposite sign. If \(u\) occurs only once in the \(a\)-row, those opposite sign factors cannot occur, so the \(a\)-row is \(s^\ell u\). But then every eigenvalue-1 vector satisfies the linear identities \(r=4s,\ u=r+a,\ a=u+\ell s\), forcing \(s=0\) for all such vectors, contradiction. Hence that row contains \(u\) twice or more and at least one opposite-sign factor. This is exactly the divisor lane, contradicted by its exclusion from the sequence.

Thus no countersequence survives with any pure fourth-power row, regardless of whether the frozen label is minimal.

## 3. No pure fourth: common product observations

Now \(\omega_p=\omega_q=1/4\), \(p,q\) have exact zero differentials, and there is at least one quartic row. Any quartic monomial on just \(p,q\) is \(p^{4-j}q^j\). At most one such row can occur: two distinct exponent choices having ratio tending to 1 imply \(q/p\to1\) and \(p^4\sim N\); but even the minimal difference of two distinct allowed monomials is at least \(2p^3\), exceeding \(M<N/p\sim p^3\). If all labels have weights \(\le1/2\), the same gap argument gives this directly without using ratios. With all labels at most weight \(1/2\) and \(z\) of weight \(1/2\), at most one product of \(z\) times a quadratic in \(p,q\) is possible because such product gaps are \(\ge2pz=N^{3/4+o(1)}\gg M\). Even if a single higher label of weight \(3/4\) is present and is a \(p\)- or \(q\)-companion, at most one such scaled product occurs, provided some quartic on \(p,q\) is used: two scaled products would force \(q/p\to1\), hence \(p^4\sim N,\ z\sim\sqrt N\), and \(2pz\sim2p^3>M\).

If \(t=4\), all three \(Z\)-only rows use only \(p,q\), so all quartic, and the preceding at-most-one bound rules out the tree.

If \(t=3\), \(Z=\{p,q,z\}\), and there are four \(Z\)-only rows. If \(\omega_z<1/2\), all three \(Z\) labels are exactly stationary. Thus every such row has \(\delta x_i=\delta N\). At least one has input in \(Z\) (more than three rows), giving \(\delta N=0\); at least one has input in \(S\) (also only three labels in \(Z\)), contradicting actual movement there. If \(\omega_z>1/2\), it is the unique maximum and as factor occurs at most once, leaving at least three quartics on \(p,q\), impossible. If \(\omega_z=1/2\), the only types are one \(p,q\)-quartic, \(z^2\), and at most one scaled \(z\) times a quadratic on \(p,q\). Three cannot supply four.

## 4. No pure fourth: remaining two-support tree

Here \(S=\{a,b\}\), the sole \(S\)-pair product \(ab\) has input \(u\in Z\) (it cannot equal a factor). \(Z\) consists of \(p,q\) and two other labels, and there are five \(Z\)-only products. The two inputs \(a,b\) are necessarily \(Z\)-only rows.

### 4a. At most one \(Z\) label of weight \(\ge1/2\)

If there is none, all \(Z\) factors have zero differential. Among the five \(Z\)-only rows there is a \(Z\) input, forcing \(\delta N=0\), and there must also be an \(S\) input, contradicting movement.

Otherwise call the sole high-weight \(Z\) label \(H\); the third low label besides \(p,q\) is \(z\) with \(1/4\le\omega_z<1/2\). If \(\omega_H>1/2\), it is the global maximum and occurs as factor in exactly one row. The remaining four \(Z\)-only products use just the three low labels, whose differentials vanish. Of their four input labels at least one is low (there are only three other labels), so \(\delta N=0\). Then their four input labels cannot be \(a,b\), both moving; hence they are all of \(Z\). In particular all three low inputs read only these three low factors, violating strong connectivity.

Suppose \(\omega_H=1/2\). If \(\omega_z>1/4\), products with \(H\) are \(H^2\) or \(H\) times a quadratic in \(p,q\), with at most one scaled one by the gap bound. Low-only products are either a \(p,q\)-quartic (at most one), or a \(z\)-monomial from \(\omega_z=3/8\) (then \(z^2p,z^2q\), at most one by gap \(\ge2z^2\gg M\)), or from \(\omega_z=1/3\) (then \(z^3\) alone). These are the weight solutions in \((1/4,1/2)\) and never two different \(z\)-types simultaneously. At most four total \(Z\)-only products, not five.

If \(\omega_z=1/4\), use the exact tangent. At least two of the three \(Z\)-input rows other than \(u\) are low inputs, with zero derivative. Their products can depend differentially only on \(H\), so
\[
 \delta N=e_i(N-x_i)h_H \quad (e_i\text{ the multiplicity of }H).
\]
If \(h_H=0\), then \(\delta N=0\) and consequently also \(\delta a=\delta b=0\) from their \(Z\)-only rows, contradiction. Hence \(h_H\ne0\). For two distinct low inputs the coefficients \(e_i(N-x_i)\) can agree only if both exponents are zero: distinct bounded integers give different leading coefficients of \(N\) since \(x_i=o(N)\); the same nonzero exponent cannot agree for distinct inputs. Thus \(\delta N=0\). If \(u=H\), every low input is \(Z\)-only and its equation now forces \(e_i=0\), so the three low labels form a closed factor set, impossible. If \(u\) is low, the row at \(H\) is \(Z\)-only and its differential equation is \([H+e_H(N-H)]h_H=0\), impossible since the real coefficient is positive.

### 4b. Both remaining \(Z\) labels have weight \(\ge1/2\)

Write them \(v,w\). If both weights are \(>1/2\), each must occur as factor only paired with a single \(p\) or \(q\) (weights force both of them to have weight \(3/4\)). Neither can occur twice: that would require its two possible rows \(pv,qv\) (or \(pw,qw\)), with ratio \(q/p\to1\); then \(v,w\sim N/p\), and a gap \(\ge2v\) or \(2w\) exceeds \(M\). These two factors can thus account for at most two rows, leaving at least three \(p,q\)-quartics, impossible. If just one weight exceeds \(1/2\), it is \(M\), of weight \(3/4\), occurring in only one row and paired with \(p\) or \(q\). The other label \(z\), of weight \(1/2\), has then to supply four remaining \(Z\)-products together with \(p,q\). At most one \(p,q\)-quartic (and one must occur), one unscaled \(z^2\), and one scaled \(z\) times a quadratic are possible by the strengthened gap comparison at the start of §3. Again too few rows.

Finally both weights are \(1/2\). The only multiplicity pairs \((A_{iv},A_{iw})\) for \(Z\)-only products are the six integer points
\[
 (0,0),\ (1,0),\ (0,1),\ (2,0),\ (1,1),\ (0,2).
\]
All five rows have distinct pairs because there is at most one \(p,q\)-quartic and at most one scaled product for each \(v,w\) by the gap bound. At the three input indices \(Z\setminus\{u\}\), all rows are \(Z\)-only. Freeze \(p,q\) **only as a valid restriction of the alleged nonzero tangent** (both already have exact zero differentials in the countersequence; they are not both charged as counting parameters). These three rows give a homogeneous system in \(h_v,h_w,n\). Its matrix tends to the corresponding three rows \((A_{iv},A_{iw},-1)\), because \(C_{ii}=o(1)\) and \(h_i\) is one of \(0,h_v,h_w\) on these indices. If the points are not collinear, the actual matrix is eventually invertible, killing \(h_v,h_w,n\) and hence also \(h_a,h_b\) from their \(Z\)-only rows. So the three points are collinear. The only collinear three-point subsets of this triangular grid are its three sides (horizontal, vertical, diagonal).

The diagonal would give \(v^2,vw,w^2\) at three inputs in \(Z\). The two squares force \(v,w<\sqrt N\), so all those inputs are at most \(\sqrt N\), but product gaps are \(\ge2\min(v,w)>\sqrt N\). A horizontal or vertical edge gives, after swapping \(v,w\) if needed, \(D,mv,v^2\), where \(D\) is a quartic in \(p,q\) and \(m\) a quadratic in \(p,q\). Since \(mv,v^2\in[N-M,N]\), \(m^2=(mv)^2/v^2=N+O(M)\), so \(|D-m^2|=O(M)=N^{1/2+o(1)}\). Distinct quartic monomials differ by \(\ge2p^3=N^{3/4+o(1)}\); therefore \(D=m^2\) exactly. The distinct odd factors \(m\) (composite) and \(v\) (prime) are asymptotic to \(\sqrt N\), giving gaps \(\ge2\min(m,v)>\sqrt N\) between \(m^2,mv,v^2\). But the three row inputs in \(Z\) include at least two of \(p,q,v<\sqrt N\), again impossible.

## 5. Conclusion and exact dependency scope

All limiting stationary countersequences have been removed or put in the exact divisor lane by §2. The compactness statement in §1 therefore supplies some uniform positive \(\delta\) (take it below \(1/100\)); otherwise the displayed sequence with \(p>N^{1/4-\delta_j}\) and no moving label \(\le N^{1/2-\delta_j}\) outside the divisor lane exists and is impossible. Combining the old count for low \(p\), bounded-rank nonsingular and corank-one cases, the new moving-coordinate count for high \(p\), and the divisor count, gives \(X^{3/4-\epsilon+o(1)}\) for some absolute \(\epsilon>0\).

The general input lemmas are those of [the earlier three-quarter note](EACSixThreeQuarter.md) (nonsingular counts, supported-minor lemma, corank-two retention/tangent, and the unconditional arity≤3 finite rank obstruction), plus the [seven-tenths product bound](EACSixSevenTenthsProduct.md) to separate \(q\) from weight \(1/2\). No new finite chart search or assumption that Goldbach must have a small terminal is used. The auxiliary one- and two-variable freezes in the nonpure proof are rank checks on one already-existing tangent; none adds a counted third parameter. The only nonminimal seed counted is a fourth root \(s\) with \(s<X^{1/4}\), replacing rather than supplementing \(p\).
