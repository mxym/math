# A polynomially rough survivor in the supercritical columns of Pan's remainder

Let $P$ be a sufficiently large even integer, $L=\log P$, $L_2=\log L$, $Q_0=P^{1/2}L^{-20}$, and define
\[
 a(n)=\sum_{b\mid n,\,b>Q_0}\mu(b)\log b,\qquad E_d(f)=\sum_{1\le n<P,\ n\equiv P\bmod d} f(n)-\frac1{\phi(d)}\sum_{1\le n<P,\,(n,d)=1}f(n),\quad (d,P)=1.
\]
Here $a$ is the **first** divisor factor of [Huang–Li (6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.03811v2#S1); no $(b,P)=1$ is imposed. Put $P^-(1)=\infty$. Fix $v\ge1/2$, $\kappa>0$ with
\[
 36\kappa+33v<17,\qquad 15(\kappa+v)<8.\tag{1}
\]
Let $D=P^v$ (constant factors or truncated dyads do not matter), and let $\mathcal D$ consist of $D<d\le2D$ coprime to $P$. The following assertions hold for each fixed $A>0$, with implied constants depending also on the fixed exponent margins.

1. **Remove every input with an actual prime factor up to $P^\kappa$:**
\[
 \sum_{d\in\mathcal D}|E_d(a(n)1_{P^-(n)\le P^\kappa})|\ll_A PL^{-A}.\tag{2}
\]
2. **Remove all inputs, including primes, for sufficiently smooth squarefree moduli:** more generally for *any* fixed $v<21/40$, there exists $\sigma>0$ such that
\[
 \sum_{d\in\mathcal D,\,d\mid\prod_{p<P^\sigma}p}|E_d(a)|\ll_A PL^{-A}.\tag{3}
\]
3. Thus the **actual signed column** of Pan's fixed-target remainder, under (1), is
\[
 \sum_{d\in\mathcal D}\mu(d)\log d\sum_{n<P,\ n\equiv P(d)}a(n)
 =\sum_{\substack{d\in\mathcal D\,\mathrm{squarefree}\\ P^+(d)\ge P^\sigma}}
 \mu(d)\log d\ E_d(a(n)1_{P^-(n)>P^\kappa})+O_A(PL^{-A}).\tag{4}
\]
Only the simultaneous intersection remains: nonsmooth squarefree $d$, and all prime factors of $n$ larger than a *fixed power* of $P$. Such $n>1$ have at most $\lfloor1/\kappa\rfloor$ prime factors with multiplicity. For example $v=0.505,\ \kappa=0.008$ meet (1). The RHS is a centered residue discrepancy, **not** an uncentered subcount. Its sign is unknown; this does not prove a positive Goldbach lower bound. Statement (4) orients the coprimality constraint as printed in Pan's formula; it does not simply interchange the two coordinates.

## A. Centering short-divisor sums at the true residue

For $f(n)=\sum_{m\mid n,\ m\le P^u}c_m$, with $|c_m|\le L^{O(1)}\tau(m)^{O(1)}$, the [Bettin–Chandee Poisson proof](PanSupercriticalShoulder.md) gives
\[
 \sum_{d\in\mathcal D}|E_d(f)|\ll_A PL^{-A} \tag{5}
\]
whenever the pair $(u,v)$ satisfies both strict inequalities in that proof: $14(u+v)+5\max(u,v)<17$ and $8\max(u,v)+7\min(u,v)<8$. To see the change to $\ell^1$ in $d$, insert an arbitrary modulus coefficient of modulus at most one. In (2) of the linked proof, the zero harmonic for $(m,d)=1$ is $P\widehat W(0)/(md)$ and for $(m,d)>1$ both physical sums vanish. The centered term for $(m,d)=1$ has the same zero harmonic, by inclusion–exclusion on multiples of $m$ coprime to $d$ with an error $O(\tau(d)(1+\|W'\|_1)/\phi(d))$ per $m,d$. This error sums to $P^{u+o(1)}$; here and below we use (5) only with $u<1$. Norms of $c_m$ in any dyad are $\ll \sqrt M L^{O(1)}$ by the divisor moment bound. The nonzero dyads obey (5) in the linked proof even at $H=MD/P<1$: the factor $(1+F/H)^{1/2-j}$ for $F\asymp |h|\ge1$ gives $\sum_F F^b(1+F/H)^{1/2-j}\ll H^{j-1/2}\le H^b$ if $j>b+1$. The exponents are increasing in each length. The physical endpoint error here is $O_A(PL^{-A})$ on choosing a sufficiently high power for the smoothing width: sum in $d$ using $\tau(P-n)$ on the progression side and $\sum_d1/\phi(d)\ll L^{O(1)}$ on the mean side, then use Hölder and $\sum_{n\le P}\tau(n)^k\ll_k P L^{O_k(1)}$. This also justifies all subsequent discards of separable physical endpoint strips. The endpoint $n=P$ is always excluded.

At $u=1/2$, (5) applies up to $v<10/19$ for $v\ge1/2$; with a fixed gap it also holds for $u=1/2+\rho$ for some fixed $\rho>0$. Since $a(n)=-\Lambda(n)-\sum_{b\mid n,\,b\le Q_0}\mu(b)\log b$, this already gives the absolute transfer $\sum_d|E_d(a)+E_d(\Lambda)|\ll_A PL^{-A}$ for all $v<10/19$ in this range. For smaller $v$ the same monotonicity applies. [Stadlmann, *Adv. Math.* 2025, Thm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.00425v3#S1) is an arbitrary-common-residue estimate for the **primes**, on squarefree smooth moduli with exponent below $21/40$, not just a prime minorant. Apply with residue $P$. For conversion to $\Lambda$, partial summation for $x\in[P/L^{K},P]$ uses a slightly smaller smoothness threshold $P^\sigma$ and fixed margin in the modulus exponent, so these moduli lie in that theorem's range at every $x$. Terms below $P/L^K$ in the progression sum cost $O_A(PL^{-A})$ by the same weighted endpoint/Hölder bound; prime powers of exponent at least two cost $P^{1/2+o(1)}$ by counting $d\mid P-p^j$ for $p^j<P$. Including or excluding $p=P$ in a reference prime sum affects at most $O(P^{v}L)$ (and $P$ is even). This proves (3).

## B. From small primes to an almost-polynomial split point

Choose $\rho>0$ fixed so (5) applies at $u=1/2+\rho$. Choose a large *fixed* constant $K$, specified in C, and set $Z=\exp(KL/L_2)$. For each $n$, let $r_Z(n)=\prod_{p\mid n,\ p\le Z}p$. Except on a set of $O_H(P L^{-H})$ integers for **every** fixed $H$, $r_Z(n)\le P^\rho$. Indeed $t=\rho L/\log Z=\rho L_2/K$, $s=\log(t)/\log Z$; Rankin bounds the exceptional count by
\[
 P\sum_{e>P^\rho,\ e\mid\prod_{p\le Z}p}\frac1e
 \le P\exp(-t\log t)\prod_{p\le Z}(1+p^{-1+s})
 \le P\exp[-t\log t+O(\log\log Z+t)].
\]
For the last inequality use Mertens' prime harmonic bound and $\sum_{p\le Z}(p^s-1)/p\le s\sum_{p\le Z}(\log p)p^{s-1}\ll Z^s=t$ by Chebyshev and partial summation (eventually $0<s<1/2$). Since $t$ is a fixed positive multiple of $L_2$, the negative exponent dominates every fixed multiple of $L_2$.

Off that exceptional set, $1_{P^-(n)>Z}=\sum_{e\mid n,\,e\mid\prod_{p\le Z}p,\ e\le P^\rho}\mu(e)$. The error on the exceptional set is $\ll\tau(n)$. On $P^-(n)\le Z$, the $\Lambda(n)$ part of $a(n)$ can be discarded: its only supports are primes $\le Z$ and proper prime powers, together $P^{1/2+o(1)}$ after summing over $d$ as in A. Multiply the complementary truncated inclusion–exclusion by $\sum_{b\mid n,b\le Q_0}\mu(b)\log b$. Its errors before the modulus sum are $\ll L\tau(n)^2$ on the exceptional set; Hölder with the global divisor moments for $n$ and $P-n$ gives arbitrary log savings on both sides of the centering. The remaining expression is a short-divisor sum at $m=[b,e]\le P^{1/2+\rho}$ with coefficient $\ll L\tau(m)^2$, plus the $e=1$ term as appropriate. Equation (5) now proves (2) for $P^-(n)\le Z$. No signed estimate on an unknown set of primes has been used.

## C. Tag primes between the split and the fixed power

We record explicitly the extension needed in the [macroscopic Fouvry–Radziwiłł dispersion proof](PanUnbalancedCofactor.md), using [their §§3–6 and Lemma 8.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.08672). Let $X=MC\asymp P L^{O(1)}$ with $|a_0|/X\ll L^{O(1)}$, $|a_0|\le P$, $T\asymp P^v$, and $Z/2\le C\ll P^\kappa$. In our application $a_0=P$. Take any $\alpha_m$ bounded by a fixed divisor power times $L^{O(1)}$, and a short $\beta_c$ equal to the indicator of primes in an interval of the $C$-dyad, optionally weighted by $\log c/L$. Then the $\ell^1$ progression discrepancy (3) of the linked proof is $O_A(PL^{-A})$ for arbitrary fixed $A$. This is a *log-saving*, not a uniform fixed power-saving at these cofactor lengths.

Here are the changes relative to its interval-cofactor power version. Delete the primes $c\mid a_0$ at cost $\ll L(M+T)\max|\alpha_m| L^{O(1)}$. For all $m\ll P L^{O(1)}$, $\tau(m)^{O(1)}\le\exp(C_1 L/L_2)$ with an absolute finite $C_1$ for each fixed divisor power. Thus the deletion saves arbitrary log powers as soon as $K$ is large enough. The truncated-gcd tail in FR Lemma 6.1 as proved in the linked note is $\ll MC^2\exp(C_2 L/L_2)(Z_0^{-1/2}+C^{-1})$. Indeed its only subpower factors were bounded powers of divisor functions of positive integers $\ll P^{O(1)}$, logarithms, and products $\prod_{p\mid s}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}\le\exp(O(\sqrt L))$ (split at $p=L$). The short sequence is bounded by 1; the general long coefficient only enters its initial $\ell^2$ norm, $\ll M^{1/2}L^{O(1)}$. Set $Z_0=\exp(K_0 L/L_2)$ with fixed $K_0>4(C_2+1)$ and then take $K>20(K_0+C_1+1)$. This ensures $C>Z_0^{10}$ for large $P$ and an arbitrary-log saving in the gcd tails. These constants do not depend on $A$. The spectral factor $Z_0^{C_0}=P^{o(1)}$ is absorbed by the fixed positive margins in (1): the two FR terms divided by $MC^2$ have exponents at most $(36\kappa+33v-17)/20+o(1)$ and $(15\kappa+15v-8)/8+o(1)$. Also $M/T$ is a fixed positive power. The reflected-residue phase has exactly the same polylogarithmic and $Z_0^{O(1)}$ costs as in the linked macro-shift proof.

For the prime short coefficients, use FR Lemma 8.1 **instead** of the interval variance estimate. The Siegel–Walfisz hypothesis with an extra unit restriction $(c,r)=1$ holds uniformly: for primes in any subinterval of the $C$-dyad use the usual prime number theorem in progressions to moduli $(\log C)^{O(1)}$ and partial summation; larger moduli use the trivial $C/q+1$ bound (and $C/\phi(q)$ for the mean). At most $\omega(r)$ prime indices divide $r$, and only $O(L)$ divide $a_0$, harmless relative to every $C/(\log C)^J$ with the allowed divisor factor in $r$. Hence their (13) variance is $\ll_J C^2T(\log C)^{-J}(\log T)^{O(1)}+C^{7/4}T$. Because $\log C\gg L/L_2$, this saves arbitrary powers of $L$. These observations verify all terms in the altered FR (4), with the claimed conclusion. In particular the tagged estimate does not presume that FR's printed restriction $|a_0|\le MC/3$ holds.

Now keep the mask $r_Z(n)=1$ and put $I=(Z,P^\kappa]$. We may discard all $n$ divisible by $\ell^2$ for a prime $\ell\in I$: their count is $\ll P/Z$, and Hölder with the same divisor moments gives arbitrary log savings, even under a further fixed divisor-power weight. On the remaining $n$ with $\omega_I(n)>0$, tag one prime by $1=\sum_{\ell\in I,\ell\mid n}1/\omega_I(n)$, where $\omega_I$ counts distinct primes in $I$. For each divisor term write $n=bu$, $b>Q_0$, and distinguish $\ell\mid b$ and $\ell\mid u$. In the former put $b=\ell b'$: $\mu(b)=-\mu(b')$ on the nonsquare support, split $\log b=\log\ell+\log b'$, and take $m=b'u$. In the latter put $u=\ell u'$, $m=bu'$. In both cases $r_Z(n)=r_Z(m)$, $\omega_I(n)=1+\omega_I(m)$, and $n=m\ell$. Thus the long coefficient is a sum over factorizations of $m$ of size $\ll L\tau(m)$, times $1_{r_Z(m)=1}/(1+\omega_I(m))$, independent of the tagged prime. When extending the separated sums back over $\ell\mid m$ or other squared window primes, the sums are still bounded by fixed divisor powers on the already-discarded squareful set. The normalized short prime weight is precisely one of those in the preceding paragraph.

To enforce the cutoffs literally, discard $n<P/L^J$ and the top strip $P(1-L^{-J})<n<P$ as in A; this ensures $MC\gg P/L^J$. Use separate multiplicative meshes of ratio $1+L^{-J'}$ with $J'\gg J$ in $m,\ell$ for the product cutoff and in $b',\ell$ for the moving cutoff $b'\ell>Q_0$ (also for any optional dyadic ends on $b$). Product boundary cells fall in the preceding endpoint strips. A moving $b$-edge at $B\ge Q_0$ affects only $O(P L^{-J'}+P/B)$ integers $n$ by summing $P/b$ over $b$ within relative distance $O(L^{-J'})$ of $B$. The absolute weights including number of tags and decompositions are bounded by $L^{O(1)}\tau(n)^{O(1)}$ (and $\tau(P-n)$ for progression rows). Thus Hölder makes the total lost mass negligible; fixed divisor-moment constants are independent of $J,J'$. Only $(\log P)^{O_{J'}(1)}$ boxes occur; arbitrary log savings in the dispersion estimate pay for their count. This proves (2) on the remaining mask $r_Z(n)=1,\ \omega_I(n)>0$, hence proves (2) as stated.

## D. Recover the literal signed column, not a projected model

For any integer $s\le P^{O(1)}$ uniformly in $D\asymp P^v$ one has $\sum_{d\asymp D,(d,s)=1}\mu(d)\log d/\phi(d)\ll_A L^{-A}$. Here the function $h(d)=\mu(d)d/\phi(d)\,1_{(d,s)=1}$ is $\mu*k$: at $p\nmid s$, $k(p^j)=-1/(p-1)$ for every $j\ge1$; at $p\mid s$, $k(p^j)=1$. For $\eta=1/L_2$ the absolute series $\sum_m|k(m)|/m^{1-\eta}$ is $\ll (\log L)^{O(1)}$: primes outside $s$ give an absolutely bounded product and the other product is the Rankin estimate from the [shoulder proof](PanSupercriticalShoulder.md). Splitting $m\le\sqrt D$ in the convolution uses arbitrary-log Mertens on $d/m$; the remaining tail costs $D^{-\eta/2}$ times that series. Partial summation handles the logarithm and sharp dyadic endpoints. Thus the sum of the $d$-means of the *full* signed column is negligible by summing in $d$ first with $s=Pn$ and using $\sum_{n<P}|a(n)|\ll P L^{O(1)}$. Subtract the complete smooth-moduli error using (3); subtract the error of the small-prime-input mask from the remaining moduli using the **absolute** estimate (2). This proves (4). No Mertens assertion for a nonsmooth-only or otherwise arbitrarily masked set of moduli is made.

The analytical ingredients are the published inverse-fraction estimates, the dispersion argument with its shifted-diagonal repair, and smooth-modulus prime distribution. None supplies the missing sign in (4), which still includes genuine prime inputs, for example. Recent reflected-prime Möbius averages such as the [Aster follow-up](https://github.com/aster2024/openai-math-followups) concern a different single-prime statistic; [Lü–Wang–Wu v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20242v2#S1) states its theorem for the fixed positive shift $p+2$. No result about all columns or absolute priority is inferred.
