# New reflected Type I on an actual adverse rank-five quotient family

Retain the fixed even target \(N=2x\), \(n=sq\in(x/2,x]\),
\(m=N-n\in[x,3x/2)\), \(L=\log x\), with true prime \(s\),
\(\alpha=\log_xs\), \(c=(1-\alpha)/2\), and the true outer prime \(p\)
with
\[
                 |\log_xp-(c-\eta)|<w=\eta/1000,\qquad 0<\eta<.002.
\]
Now the target is primality of \(h=(N-n)/p\) on the **actual**
adverse incoming five-prime quotients \(q\). In the \(h\) opening
\(h\) becomes an integer; \(p\) and each of the six \(n\)-prime
labels are still genuine primes. Take \((n,N)=1\).
Use the exact \(W_s(q)\) of
[BalancedSemiprimeEvenRankNeck.md](BalancedSemiprimeEvenRankNeck.md),
or the smooth separated weights specified below.

There are two supply families:
* **Nearly equal:** the five q prime exponents are all within
  \(10^{-5}\) of \(r=(1-\alpha)/5\), on \(.10<\alpha<.14\).
* **Broad fixed shape:** in order \(s,r_1,\ldots,r_5\), the six
  true prime exponents are within \(10^{-7}\) of
  \[
             .12000,\ .09637,\ .17294,\ .18971,\ .19414,\ .22684.
  \tag{1}
  \]
  The summed exponents equal 1. All five q factors are above
  \(V=x^\eta\) of the cited \(\mu\)-only center; its maximal pair
  exponent is \(.42098+O(10^{-7})\) and minimal triple exponent
  \(.45902+O(10^{-7})\). Thus that center's **actual pointwise
  coefficient is \(-6\)**, with fixed reserve relative to its
  \(U=x^{c-4\eta}\), \(Z=q/x^{c+5\eta}\). The same sign holds
  on the nearly-equal family. Neither family is a smooth-number
  substitute: the input consists exactly of products of the listed
  distinct primes. Both have positive unconditioned reference mass
  on the central interval by ordinary prime PNT at fixed widths.

**Theorem.** For each of these families the true \(n\)-product input
has the reduced progression mean in the actual class
\[
                         n\equiv N\pmod{p e}
\tag{2}
\]
with arbitrary log saving on summing these moduli, **with arbitrary
unstructured \(e\)** in the following ranges:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{nearly equal:}& e\le D_*(s)=x^{(1-\alpha)/10+\eta-.002},\\
 \text{shape (1):}& e\le D_*=x^{.1532+\eta}.
 \end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
For shape (1) the proven input distribution is contiguous through
level \(x^{.5934}\), not a single modulus notch. This yields both
full short terms, and the early free-product part of the high
term, of a literal \(h\)-Vaughan expansion through (3). It does
**not** yield a signed upper or lower bound for the entire
\(h\)-prime population on either \(q\) family.

To be precise about the intended application, let \(G_{s,p}(n)\) be any
fixed finite sum of independently separated label weights and smooth
central product tests with bounded log-power variation in the
physical central variable. This includes \(W_s(q)(\log p/\log h)\),
where \(q=n/s\), \(h=(N-n)/p\) in the real positive interval,
by simple separation or the fine boxes below. The sharp central
test is allowed. Define
\[
 U_h(s)=D_*(s)x^{-.0005},\qquad V_h=x^{.0003}
\]
(with constant \(D_*\) for (1)). Let \(T_1\) be the actual sum of
\(G_{s,p}(n)\sum_{d\mid h,d\le U_h(s)}\mu(d)\log(h/d)\) over
the supply and \(p\mid N-n\); let \(T_2\) replace the inner sum
by \(\sum_{d k\mid h,d\le U_h(s),k\le V_h}\mu(d)\Lambda(k)\).
Then for every \(A>0\), uniformly in fixed \(N\),
\[
\begin{split}
 T_1 &=
   \sum_{n\ {\rm in\ supply}}\ 
   \sum_{\substack{p\ {\rm in\ the\ stated\ band}\\p\ {\rm prime},\ (p,N)=1}}
            \frac{\mathfrak S(Npn)}{p-1}\,G_{s,p}(n)
               + O_{A,\eta}(xL^{-A}),\\
 T_2 &=O_{A,\eta}(xL^{-A}).
\end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
The \(n,p\) sum in (4) is the reduced mean and does **not** require
\(p\mid N-n\). Any factor \(1/\log h\) already in \(G_{s,p}\)
retains the actual expression \(1/\log((N-n)/p)\) there.
The part of \((\mu_{>U_h}*\Lambda_{>V_h}*1)(h)\) with
\(e=d k\le D_*(s)\) is also \(O_{A,\eta}(xL^{-A})\).
For a nonnegative \(G_{s,p}(n)\) bounded above and below on these
central boxes, the reference in (4) has positive order \(wM_N\),
with a constant depending on the fixed shape widths. Indeed the
supply count is \(\asymp x/L\) up to these fixed widths by prime
PNT and prime harmonic sums; the p-band contributes
\(\asymp w\) and the finitely many extra macro primes in \(E\)
change \(\mathfrak S(N)\) by \(1+O(x^{-c_0})\) for some \(c_0>0\).
The proper normalization therefore does not multiply the main by
an extra \(1/\log h\) unless that was present in \(G\).

## 1. Triple source distribution at the actual residue

Use [Jain, arXiv:2611.21136v1, Proposition 3 and (3.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21136v1#S3)
in its literal arbitrary independent **triple convolution** form,
not the smooth-number theorem. Set its residue \(a_1=N=2x,\ a_2=1\),
with source scale \(X'=MNL\asymp x\) on every genuine dyadic
source box. Write \(u,\ell,b\) for the exponents of its \(N,L,M\)
slots, and \(d\) for that of its modulus scale \(R\), so
\(u+\ell+b=1+o(1)\); these letters here are source lengths,
not the integer divisors in (4).
With \(\theta=7/32\), its (3.1) rails require strict reserves in
\[
\begin{gathered}
 u>0,\quad u+\ell<2/3,\quad \ell<b,\quad
     b<d<u+\ell,\quad 2u+3\ell<1+d,\\
 (7-4\theta)u+(4-\theta)\ell<2-2\theta+(2+\theta)d,\qquad
       7u+4\ell+\theta<2+2d .
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
Keeping \(N=2x\) in the last rail is essential.

For the nearly-equal family use the exact grouping \(N=r_1\),
\(L=r_2r_3\), \(M=s r_4r_5\). The nominal
\((u,\ell,b)=(r,2r,\alpha+2r)\) has ample slack for
\(.499\le d\le 3r-.001\).
Indeed \(b\le.484\), the diagonal lower level \(2u+3\ell-1=8r-1
\le.44\), the left side of the first spectral rail is
\(13.6875r\le2.46375\), and the other is
\(15r+\theta\le2.91875\).
Widths \(10^{-5}\) and constant dyadic scale factors perturb
the exponents by less than the fixed slack.
The range below \(x^{.4995}\) is ordinary full-scale convolution
Bombieri–Vinogradov, by grouping into two lengths with one genuine
SW prime and the other product polynomially long, e.g. [Maynard I,
Lemma 8.9](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A39a52f47-3770-4ddd-b7a2-b2a4f7300931/files/srj4305733).
Thus the two ranges join at the actual \(n\)-scale, without
changing residue or making any assertion about primality of \(h\).

For (1) use these five allocations of **the same six true labels**.
The entries in the last column are log ranges for the whole modulus
\(p e\), not separate exponents of \(e\).
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 N\text{ group}&L\text{ group}&M\text{ group}&(u,\ell,b)&d\text{ band}\\ \hline
 r_2&r_3r_4&s r_1 r_5&(.17294,.38385,.44321)&[.499,.5555]\\
 r_3&s r_1 r_2&r_4r_5&(.18971,.38931,.42098)&[.553,.5783]\\
 r_2&s r_1 r_4&r_3r_5&(.17294,.41051,.41655)&[.5779,.5829]\\
 r_3&r_2r_5&s r_1 r_4&(.18971,.39978,.41051)&[.580,.5889]\\
 r_4&r_2r_5&s r_1 r_3&(.19414,.39978,.40608)&[.5888,.5934]

\tag{6}
\]
The exact rational verification in
[check_true_rank_five_h_typei.py](check_true_rank_five_h_typei.py)
finds minimum strict slack .00049 over (5) at the relevant
endpoints, and checks all overlaps. The $10^{-7}$ boxes in (1)
fit these margins, with a fixed sufficiently small theorem reserve
(such as \(10^{-6}\) before the negligible \(O(1/L)\) scale
adjustments). In each band the allocation is decided *before*
applying the theorem; it does not depend on hidden factorization
of \(p e\). Ordinary full-scale convolution BV joins below
\(x^{.4995}\). Hence this is the entire progression range up to
\(x^{.5934}\), with no holes between the five displayed bands.

In either family products on fixed independent dyadic boxes have
bounded multiplicities by prime factorization. In (1) there are
disjoint label exponents, giving uniqueness. In the nearly equal
family one can use all orders of the five q labels, dividing by
\(5!\) on distinct products; grouping with coincident intervals
has bounded multiplicity per grouped integer. Delete repeats
at a power-small cost: their affected n are
\(O(x\sum_{r_i\gg x^{.17}}r_i^{-2})=O(x^{1-.17+o(1)})\)
with divisor-moment losses in actual sums and only log-power
harmonic losses in the principal. Prime factors of n dividing N
may be excluded factor by factor. All product weights on these
independent source boxes are \(O(1)\) before the optional
separated log normalizations, so Proposition 3 applies.

The displayed Jain result subtracts a low-conductor character
kernel. Choose \(D=(\log x)^B\) with \(B\) as large as needed;
its high-conductor estimate then has arbitrary log reserve.
For each low nonprincipal character modulo a candidate \(r=pe\),
fix the other actual prime labels and use ordinary
Siegel–Walfisz on one remaining true prime in its independent
interval of polynomial length-scale \(\ge x^{.09}\).
Restriction to \((n,r)=1\) removes at most \(O(1)\) primes
at this scale; the analogous exclusions of \(N\)-factors
are harmless. An imprimitive character is precisely the
primitive one with this unit mask. The number of characters
with conductor \(\le D\) is \(O(D^2)\) uniformly for each r,
and \(\sum_{r\sim R}1/\phi(r)\ll1\). After summing the other
independent labels, all low nonprincipal contributions have
arbitrary log reserve also. The true principal kernel is
therefore \(\#\{n:(n,pe)=1\}/\phi(pe)\), not a hypothetical
prime density for h.

## 2. Literal h switches and both signed harmonic means

In (3), \(e<p\); moreover \(p e\) has at most one factor
in the true \(p\) band, since \(p_1p_2\) would be far larger
than \(x^{.5934}\) (or than the covered nearly-equal level).
Thus the sum of the absolute discrepancy over \(p,e\) costs
no hidden multiplicity. We discard \(p\mid N\) and \((e,N)>1\)
on the true unit support, as they cannot contribute there.
In the main keep the exact \((n,pe)=1\) initially.
Since all actual n prime factors are much smaller than p,
\((n,p)=1\) is automatic. Also \(\phi(pe)=(p-1)\phi(e)\).

For moving profiles, freeze \(s,p\) by disjoint fine logarithmic boxes
before grouping the input. The moving \(p\)-band is the test
\(p s^{1/2}\in(x^{1/2-\eta-w},x^{1/2-\eta+w})\).
At fixed \(s\) a possibly changed \(p\) lies in a relative
\(O(L^{-K_0})\) interval at its edge, with \(O(p L^{-K_0}+1)\)
candidates. At each such \(s,p\) the exact class
\(sq\equiv N\bmod p\) gives \(O(x/(s p)+1)\) possible
physical q before any other restrictions; \(s<p\) and
\(s,p\) are distinct units on the support. Sum the two
estimates, then use global divisor moments in \(n,N-n\)
to pay the number of h-divisor labels and logs. This
gives arbitrary log precision; the floor term \(sp\ll
x^{.60}\) stays power-small. In the main, the boundary costs
its true \(p\)-harmonic width. The moving upper \(e\)
endpoints in the nearly-equal family similarly cost at most
the physical divisor-edge count at scale \(x/(sp e)\) per
fixed \(s,p,e\), summed over the thin \(e\) interval and
over outer labels. Concretely each such \(e\mid h\) and
\(s\mid n\) imply \((s,e)=1\) physically, so the q count
is \(O(x/(s p e)+1)\). There are \(O(E L^{-K_0}+1)\)
changed e at \(E=D_*(s)\) or \(U_h(s)\) (for the simple
product threshold \(U_hV_h\) likewise), giving
\(O(xL^{1-K_0}+x^{.75})\) after summing all \(s,p\).
For the second signed convolution its short factor \(d\)
can be at the moving \(U_h\) edge; first count that \(d\)
as a divisor of h exactly the same way and pay its
log-power divisor multiplicity. Principal errors are the
same thin \(1/\phi(e)\) harmonics, before a fixed log factor.
The central boundary can be handled by a fine n-grid:
at modulus \(p e\) its transition count is
\(O(xL^{-K_0}/(pe)+1)\) on the bounded product sequence
and principal transition costs the corresponding reduced mean;
all moduli are below .6. Such separation makes every
input grouping independent. Exact product tests and
\(\log(h/d)=\log(N-n)-\log p-\log d\) are handled by
bounded variation or the same independent boxes.

In particular the short coefficient on \(e=d\) or \(e=d k\)
is log-bounded, using the exact divisor sum
\(\sum_{k\mid e}\Lambda(k)=\log e\). The true application of (2)
gives the reduced mean uniformly for all needed signed sums.

At fixed genuine \(n,p\) put \(E=Npn\) and \(H_0=(N-n)/p\).
The uniform finite \(\mu/\phi\) moments used in
[UnmarkedSemiprimeWholeTypeI.md](UnmarkedSemiprimeWholeTypeI.md),
§3, yield
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\le U_h(s)\\(d,E)=1}}
   \frac{\mu(d)\log(H_0/d)}{\phi(d)}
            =\mathfrak S(E)+O_A(L^{-A}).
\]
For \(T_2\) fix the short prime power \(k=\ell_0^j\le V_h\).
Use the exact factor
\(\phi(d)\phi(k)/\phi(dk)=1-\ell_0^{-1}1_{\ell_0\mid d}\)
and the constant finite \(\mu/\phi\) moment. In the overlap
write \(d=\ell_0 d'\), \((d',\ell_0)=1\).
Both endpoints \(U_h\) and \(U_h/\ell_0\) are
polynomially long; summing the short \(k\) harmonic costs
only a log power. For the early high-term branch \(d k\le D_*\),
\(d>U_h,k>V_h\), one also has \(k\le D_*/U_h=x^{.0005}\).
Each nonempty \(d\) interval and its \(\ell_0\)-overlap
has polynomial endpoints and its constant moment is the
difference of two log-small prefixes. Thus the mean is zero
to arbitrary log reserve for both of these signed convolutions.
This proves (4) and the early high-term assertion with
the physical \(H_0\). No uncontrolled signed piece was
assumed to have a favorable sign.

## 3. Remaining h branch and source scope

The actual adverse **prime-h** count comes from the full
literal Vaughan identity for \(\Lambda(h)\) followed by
division by \(\log h\); genuine proper powers \(h\) have
power-small total here, at most \(O(x^{.73+o(1)})\)
physical \((p,h)\) by their usual square-root count and
the \(p\)-band. Inserting (4) only leaves the actual
high product \(d>U_h,k>V_h,d k>D_*\), with sign
\(\mu(d)\Lambda(k)\) and its original free quotient.
That signed high product is not estimated here. In particular
the endpoint \(\ge x^{(1+\alpha)/4+o(1)}\) of a balanced \(h\)
divisor is still far beyond the new Type I level.

This differs from the exact slot obstruction in
[BalancedSemiprimeRankFiveCore.md](BalancedSemiprimeRankFiveCore.md):
the new input is Jain's **triple source** and absolute sum over
all moduli in (2); a convenient factor or any factorability
of e is unnecessary. The result supplies new pieces of the
genuine adverse rank-five problem, not a majorant or minorant
for all its h primes, and not the signed Goldbach budget
against the original semiprime reference.
