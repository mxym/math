# Power-accurate prime-modulus delta aliases using Dirichlet GRH

This refines the [complete alias tranches](DirectGoldbachGRHCompleteAliasTranches.md) for prime moduli, using Dirichlet GRH as verified within the [recorded Dirichlet scope](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/). It does not bound the unknown Goldbach discrepancy itself or the general composite sector.

Put \(X=N\), \(Q=X^{1-\delta}\) for fixed \(0<\delta<1/7\), \(K=X/Q\). Use genuine log-prime polynomials at \(X,X,Q\), with fixed smooth nonnegative weights \(w,g,u\) compactly supported inside \((0,1)\). Write \(R(M)\) for the \(w,g\) two-prime convolution, \(J(t)=\int w(x)g(1+t-x)\,dx\), and \(B_{v,q}\) for the actual prime-minus-own-unit-lattice-center of the existing note. Under Dirichlet GRH, for some \(c=c(\delta)>0\),
\[
 \frac1Q\sum_{q\ {\rm prime}}u(q/Q)\sum_{k\ne0}(B_{w,q}*B_{g,q})(N+kq)
 =-\frac{XJ(0)}Q(\mathfrak S(N)-1)\sum_{q\ {\rm prime}}u(q/Q)+O(X^{1-c}).
\]
The supports restrict actual offsets to \(|k|\le C K\) with \(C\) fixed. Adding the literal \(k=0\) term leaves the discrepancy \(R(N)-XJ(0)\mathfrak S(N)\), times \(\sum_q u(q/Q)/Q\), up to a power error. This concerns the genuine prime-modulus tranche before any further product-conductor filter. It isolates the actual target atom. [Existing small and small-radical polynomial alias formulas](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/research/goldbach-paper-2026-11-24/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/DirectGoldbachPolynomialDyadicAliasReserve.md)

**1. Sum the ternary errors over every offset.** Let \(D=X^\eta\), \(2\delta<\eta<(1-3\delta)/2\), and \(H=X^\zeta\) with \(\zeta>0\) sufficiently small. Use short major arcs at \(r\le D\) with half-width \(D/(rQ)\). These arcs are disjoint as \(D^2=o(Q)\). Vaughan's bound outside them is \(Q L^{O(1)}(D^{-1/2}+Q^{-1/5})\), from Dirichlet approximation at level \(Q/D\), where \(L=\log X\). The product of the long polynomials has \(L^1\) norm \(O(XL)\), so the total minor error over \(O(K)\) offsets is \(O(XQX^{-c})\).

For the map \(\alpha\mapsto-k\alpha\), a preimage center has denominator \(d=rs\) with \(s=(d,|k|)\mid |k|\), and width \(b_d=D/(|k|rQ)\). The preimage arcs are disjoint at each \(k\). In each long input, the GRH short-frequency estimate and Gauss average over numerators give centered squared norm
\[
 E_k\ll L^{O(1)}\left(X\sum_d d b_d+\sqrt X\right)
 \ll L^{O(1)}\left(XD^2/Q+\sqrt X\right).
\]
Here \(\sum_{r\le D}\sum_{s\mid k}s/|k|\ll D\log\log X\). The primitive-character lemma applies when \(d\le1/(d b_d)\le X\), since \(d^2 b_d\le |k|D^2/Q=o(1)\) and \(1/(d b_d)\le |k|Q/D\ll X/D\). Apply partial summation for the fixed smooth weights. The \(\sqrt X L^{O(1)}\) term deletes proper prime powers by single-circle Parseval on the disjoint preimage. The long principal squared norm is \(O(XL)\). Multiplying the mixed and quadratic errors by \(O(Q)\) and summing over \(k\) saves a power since \(KD/\sqrt Q\) and \(K X^{-1/4}\) do. This gives the displayed range \(\delta<1/7\). For this input use the **exponentially smoothed** GRH estimate of [Languasco–Perelli, equation (25)](https://www.research.unipd.it/retrieve/e14fb267-6015-3de1-e053-1705fe0ac030/%5B3%5D.pdf), rather than the earlier hard-cutoff form they correct. For a character modulo \(d\) this gives squared error \(O(X b L^2)\) on radius \(b\) when \(d\le 1/(db)\le X\), with exponential weight \(e^{-n/X}\) and the corresponding continuous pole. To pass to a smooth compactly supported \(v\), write \(v(t)=e^{-t}a(t)\) with \(a\) compactly supported smooth. Fourier inversion in \(a(n/X)\) expresses the desired error as the weighted convolution of exponentially smoothed errors shifted by \(z/X\). For \(|z|\le X^\xi\) with fixed small \(\xi>0\), all enlarged radii \(b_d+|z|/X\) obey the same rails (since \(2\delta+2\eta<1\) and \(d\le |k|D\)); \(b_d\gg1/X\). Minkowski costs at most \(\int|\widehat a(z)|\sqrt{1+|z|}\,dz\) in the square-root norm, which is finite. The other shifts are negligible by arbitrary-order decay of \(\widehat a\) and a trivial bound for the exponentially weighted sum and pole. The shifted pole convolution is exactly the continuous model \(V_v\). This justifies the compact smooth input without relying on the earlier hard-cutoff bound.

**2. Complete the principal calculation with a power error.** Cut the long model at \(|\beta|\le H/X\) by smooth decay. GRH and partial summation give the short principal approximation there with error \(O(\sqrt{rQ}\,H L^{O(1)})\) at each numerator, by square-root character remainders and Gauss Cauchy, since \(|k|Q|\beta|\ll H\). This sums with power slack since \(KH\sqrt{D/Q}\) saves a power for small \(\zeta\). After the full numerator sum the coefficient is \(\mu(d)^2\mu(r)c_d(N)/(\varphi(d)^2\varphi(r))\), \(r=d/(d,k)\). Its absolute sum over \(r>D/(C'H)\) is \(O(L^{O(1)}(D/H)^{-1/2})\) by Rankin in \(r\). Primes outside \(k\) cost \(O(p^{-2})\) before weighting, even if \(p\mid N\). Primes dividing \(k\) give at most a log factor. Thus this tail saves a power over \(O(K)\) offsets since \(\eta-\zeta>2\delta\). Take \(C'\) large so the remaining arcs contain the entire collar and extend the integral to the line by smooth decay. Fourier inversion gives \(XQ\mathfrak S_k(N)\int u(t)J(kQt/X)\,dt\). Remove the short log using \(u(t)\log Q/\log(Qt)\). Reverse from the main integral to the actual prime \(q\)-sum by RH smooth PNT. Derivatives stay bounded because \(kQ/X=O(1)\), and the square-root short PNT error sums with power slack.

**3. Keep the centered signs and evaluate the omitted model diagonal.** At literal \(N+kq\), the mixed and lattice terms together give \(-XJ(kq/X)+O(\sqrt X L^{O(1)}+(X/q+1)L^{O(1)})\). Use RH PNT on the smooth genuine prime marginal, with multiplier \(\rho_v(q)=1+O(X^{-1/2}L^{O(1)}+1/q)\), and the elementary bound for deleting the nonunit lattice legs. These errors sum with power slack. It remains to sum \((\mathfrak S_k(N)-1)J(kq/X)\) over \(k\ne0\).

Use the existing odd squarefree divisor expansion \(\mathfrak S_k=2P\,1_{2\mid k}\sum_{d\mid k}j(d)\), with \(\mathfrak S_0=\mathfrak S(N)\) and \(P\sum_d j(d)/d=1\). This is valid also at \(k=0\) by absolute convergence. The all-\(k\) signed sum is
\[
 2P\sum_d j(d)\left(\sum_{2d\mid k}J(kq/X)-\frac1{2d}\sum_k J(kq/X)\right).
\]
For \(d\le(X/q)^{1/2}\), Poisson gives an arbitrarily strong power error after the common integral cancels. For greater \(d\) the bracket is \(O_J(1)\) by the total variation Riemann bound, even when the step exceeds the support width. Also \(\sum_d|j(d)|d^{1/2}\ll_\epsilon X^\epsilon\) by the Euler product: \(j(p)\) is \(O(p^{-2})\) off \(N\) and \(O(p^{-1})\) on \(N\). The all-\(k\) result thus saves a power uniformly for \(q\asymp Q\). Subtract the \(k=0\) model value to obtain the displayed theorem.
