# A transverse residue test in arbitrary rational corank

Fix a strongly connected loopless nonnegative integral exponent matrix \(A\) of size \(k\), with every row sum at least two. A genuine all-composite terminal realization has \(k\) distinct odd prime units \(x_i\le N/3\) and \(N-x_i=\prod_jx_j^{A_{ij}}\). Put \(B=I-A,\ r=\operatorname{nullity}_{\mathbb Q}B\), and take column bases \(C,V\) of the saturated integral left and right kernels of \(B\). No restriction is imposed on \(r\). This note concerns a fixed signature and fixed finite base-prime alphabet, not matrices growing with the center.

**Finite residue test.** For a prime \(\ell\), enumerate the tuples \(\bar X\in(\mathbb F_\ell^\times)^k\) satisfying
\[
                 \bar X^{B_i}=-1\quad(1\le i\le k),\qquad
                      C^\top(\bar X_i^{-1})_i=0.
\]
Call \(\ell\) *transverse for the signature* if every such tuple has
\[
                         \det(C^\top\operatorname{diag}(\bar X_i^{-1})V)
                              \ne0\pmod\ell.                           \tag{1}
\]
The empty-mask case is allowed and is an immediate obstruction whenever \(\ell\mid N\) in an actual integer realization: the exact left-null identities first give even coordinate sums and \(\sum_i c_i/x_i=0\bmod N\). At corank zero use the empty determinant 1. No full-rank preservation assumption on \(B\bmod\ell\) is needed for the **sufficiency** of (1): the actual characteristic-zero torus tangent has basis \(V\), and (1) implies its *characteristic-zero* differential is invertible at every unit accumulation. If using arbitrary bases computed only over the finite field, one must verify the rational rank does not drop there or use the reductions of actual saturated integral bases instead.

**Theorem.** Fix finite \(F\), and let \(G\subseteq F\) consist of the transverse primes or any certified subset. For every \(\epsilon>0\), the fixed signature occurs only finitely often at centers satisfying
\[
                             N_G\,N_F\ge N^{1+\epsilon}.              \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(N_G\ge N^{1/2+\epsilon}\) suffices. At \(\ell=2\) there is just the all-ones tuple; an odd determinant \(C^\top V\) certifies transversality, in any corank. A separate, all-center [equitable-partition obstruction](EACEquitableParity.md) applies under its additional hypotheses. At \(r=0\) this theorem needs only \(N_F\ge N^{1/2+\epsilon}\). For a near-smooth sequence \(N_F=N^{1-o(1)}\) there is a useful additional statement without a positive-power threshold: \(\prod_{\ell\in G}N_{\{\ell\}}\) must stay bounded. A fixed transverse prime with unbounded valuation already gives the simple local branch described below.

**Proof.** In an alleged unbounded sequence select convergent logarithmic weights for all \(F\)-parts. Put \(\gamma=\lim\log N_G/\log N,\ \rho=\lim\log N_F/\log N\). Under (2), \(\gamma>0\) and \(\gamma+\rho>1\). Pass to simultaneous \(\ell\)-adic unit accumulation for the finitely many places considered, restricting to a fixed geometric irreducible physical component containing a dense subsequence. Its rational points make it rationally defined.

For each integral left-null \(c\), the actual identity \(\prod_i(1-N/x_i)^{c_i}=(-1)^{\sum c_i}=1\) follows as in the [finite-torus proof](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md), including the even coordinate sum by reduction modulo the full \(N>2\). Its regular divided equation \(H_c=[\prod_i(1-N/X_i)^{c_i}-1]/N=0\) restricts at \(N=0\) on unit coordinates to \(-\sum_i c_i/X_i\). At a growing transverse place the local limit has unit coordinates, so its reduction satisfies the finite test. In characteristic zero choose \(k-r\) independent deformed rows. Their logarithmic Jacobian at \(N=0\) in the torus variables has the same rank as these rows of \(B\); they give a smooth local space of dimension \(r+1\), with smooth \(r\)-dimensional torus slice \(N=0\) whose log tangent space has basis \(V\). The derivative of the \(r\) divided equations on that slice is, up to harmless row/column basis choices and overall sign, precisely \(C^\top\operatorname{diag}(1/X_i)V\). Its determinant is nonzero by (1) at the reduction. Consequently the full local equations selected (independent rows and divided equations) cut a smooth curve **with \(N\) itself a local parameter**: their total Jacobian in the \(k\) torus variables is invertible. The physical component, which has nonconstant \(N\), is one-dimensional and its approached normalized branch has local vanishing order \(e_\ell=1\). For \(r=0\) the same conclusion follows directly from the nonsingular characteristic-zero matrix \(B\).

By [Siegel's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2097) the infinite integral physical subsequence on the curve gives a rational curve with one or two geometric punctures. The two-puncture exclusion in [the finite-torus analysis, section 2](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md) works for every fixed \(\rho>0\), independently of the corank. With one rational puncture use its primitive rational-line parameter \(t\), rescaled to be integral at the physical points. Then \(N=Q(t)\) and all labels are rational polynomials \(P_i(t)\), \(D=\deg Q\ge1\). At every positive-mass \(\ell\in F\), pass to a fixed root \(\alpha_\ell\in\mathbb Z_\ell\) approached by \(t\), of multiplicity \(e_\ell\le D\). The weighted [Subspace Theorem calculation](EACCyclotomicPowerFraction.md) on \((t,1)\) gives \(\sum_{\ell\in F}\lambda_\ell/e_\ell\le1/D\), where \(\lambda_\ell=\lim\log N_{\{\ell\}}/\log N\) and mass-zero terms may be omitted. Indeed \(|t-\alpha_\ell|_\ell\ll N_{\{\ell\}}^{-1/e_\ell}\), and \(\log |t|\sim(\log N)/D\). All positive-mass places in \(G\) have \(e_\ell=1\); thus \(\gamma+(\rho-\gamma)/D\le1/D\), so \((D-1)\gamma\le1-\rho<\gamma\), giving \(D=1\).

For the extra near-smooth assertion even if \(\gamma=0\), an unbounded transverse place still yields the physical curve. Because \(\rho=1\) the same weighted inequality forces \(Q\) to have a single distinct root: otherwise \(D\ge2\) and \(1/(D-1)\le1/D\), impossible. At the growing transverse place the physical limit is a root (also when that place has mass zero), with \(e_\ell=1\); since all roots coincide its multiplicity is \(D\), again giving \(D=1\).

Finally \(D=1\) cannot occur, **even if several labels are constant primes**. At least one label polynomial is nonconstant; all have degree \(\le1\) by the physical bounds. Every \(Q-P_i\) has degree 1, since \(N-x_i\ge2N/3\) on the sequence. Hence each row has exactly one nonconstant child with multiplicity one, every other factor being a fixed positive prime. On the nonconstant vertices, choosing this child gives a directed cycle. Every row of the cycle has \(N-x_i=u_i x_{\rm next}\) with a fixed integer \(u_i\ge3\), as its complement is composite. Multiplying around the cycle modulo \(N\) gives \(N\mid\prod u_i-(-1)^{|\rm cycle|}\), a fixed nonzero integer. This contradicts unbounded \(N\).

**Exact controls.** Run `python3 -O check_eac_transverse_kernel.py`. The [standalone checker](check_eac_transverse_kernel.py) reconstructs two actual historical matrices by factoring their centers' rows, verifies strong connectivity, computes rational and finite-field ranks, and enumerates the boundary tuples using discrete exponent coordinates. For the 21-label matrix at 1862 (corank 2), the test **at base 11** has 200 monomial tuples, 3 compatible ones and no nontransverse tuple. For the 10-label matrix at 6142 (corank 3), the test **at base 3** has 16 monomial tuples, 2 compatible ones and none nontransverse. In both tests the mod-base rank equals the rational rank, so the finite-field kernel bases are legitimate. These are nonempty genuine-signature fixtures, not assertions that the historical centers themselves have a dominant 11- or 3-part, nor a new historical-frequency claim. The stronger historical exact occurrence result is [separate](EACHistoricalSignatureRigidity.md).
