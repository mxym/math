# The slow-height factor curve has a single geometric pole over infinity

Work along a putative unbounded sequence in the [five-macroscopic subpower-seed theorem](PrimeOrbitSixSubpowerSeedMass.md): a fixed odd seed p in a strongly connected six-label all-composite factor diagram, at even centers N with outside-F cofactor of log size o(L), L=log N, with the five other primes ≥N^δ. No power-class hypothesis on the cofactor and no bounded-arity hypothesis. Fix on a subsequence the nonseed exponent columns A′, an invertible five-row minor U on rows I, and put G=U^−1, w=G1, d=den(w). The earlier theorem gives all entries of the seed column a_i=o(L), A′w=1, all w_j in (0,1), and log x_j=w_jL+o(L). Constants below can depend on the fixed chart. The assertions are eventually true on each such subsequence, by applying the argument to any countersequence.

**Necessary structure.** For the individual specialized coefficient tuple p^(a_i) at each sufficiently large physical point, the factor equations admit a geometrically irreducible curve C over Q containing that point. On its smooth completion the center function T has degree exactly d. There is one *geometric* point over infinity, rational over Q with ramification d. It is the only pole of any of the five moving functions X_j, with pole order d w_j there. The X_j have pairwise disjoint zero divisors, all disjoint from the zero divisor of T. In particular there is an integer k for this coefficient tuple such that

    Ga_I ≡ k w    (mod Z⁵),    or equivalently    a ≡ k·1 (mod A′Z⁵).

These assertions apply to the curve carrying a hypothetical point, which can vary with N. They do not prove that curve has genus zero, that T is a monomial, or that all possible diagrams have been eliminated.

## 1. The physical component and the slow-height transfer

Use projective logarithmic coefficient heights. The six polynomials T−X_i−p^(a_i)∏_{j>0} X_j^(A_ij) (X₀=p) have uniformly bounded degree and heights o(L). At the physical point the partial Jacobian of the I-equations in X₁,…,X₅, after nonzero row/column scaling and an overall sign, is U plus entries 1_{i=j} x_j/(N−x_i). The perturbation is o(1), so the point belongs to a single smooth one-dimensional local branch of the selected five equations, and T is a local parameter. Its geometric irreducible component C is fixed by Galois, since all conjugates pass through this smooth rational point. Its degree is bounded and Chow height o(L). In this fixed-dimension/degree setting we use the arithmetic Bézout inequality: intersect successively retaining the components on which an equation vanishes identically and applying Bézout on the others. Nonnegative canonical heights (or ordinary Chow heights up to bounded degree-dependent additive errors) bound each isolated component's degree and height by O(1+sum of the input heights). See [D’Andrea–Krick–Sombra, arithmetical Bézout, Theorem 2.58](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_2013_4_46_4_549_0/). Projectivizing and discarding components at infinity is permissible. Partial intersection with another bounded degree and height-o(L) hypersurface gives the important transfer: if it contains the physical point, it contains C. Otherwise the point, of height ≥L via T=N, has height o(L) as an isolated intersection, a contradiction. This works over a fixed number field or over bounded-degree fields with normalized absolute heights. In particular the omitted factor row also holds on C.

Projection to any fixed pair of coordinates or rational functions of bounded degree and height o(L) gives a plane curve of bounded degree and coefficient height o(L), provided the image is one-dimensional. One can use affine graphs of the rational functions with equations for inverted denominators, intersect and then project by arithmetic elimination (same reference, linear projection/implicitization), or specialize the Chow form at the linear coordinate hyperplanes for an ordinary coordinate projection. Constant coordinate images have height o(L) by the same estimates, impossible for X_j with physical height ≥δL or for T. All component/image assertions concern the closure of the actual graph, not extraneous factors supported at a denominator.

## 2. The positive branch at infinity is on C

For notation below replace N by a variable T when solving the selected equations, holding p^(a_i) fixed. Put z=T^(−1/d) and n_j=d(1−w_j)>0. For Y_j=X_j/T and Y₀=pz^d the equations on the positive branch are

    Y_j=p^(−(Ga_I)_j) z^(n_j) ∏_{i∈I}(1−Y_i)^(G_ji),   j=1,…,5.

Treat the outer labels p^(−(Ga_I)_j) z^(n_j) and pz^d as independent small variables. The fixed implicit function theorem gives a convergent germ near zero with rational Taylor coefficients, using binomial branches taking value one at zero. It parametrizes actual solutions of the original I-equations: multiply the rational exponents by the integer matrix U, or first check on positive real z and then use analytic continuation. Since the coefficient factors have height o(L), every positive real 0<z≤N^(−1/d) is in this germ for all sufficiently large points. At the physical endpoint the prime ratios tend to zero and thus equal this solution by local uniqueness. Its continuous analytic branch lies on the very C from §1: its germ through that smooth point does, and its defining polynomials then vanish identically along the parametrization. The expansion for each X_j begins with p^(−(Ga_I)_j) z^(−d w_j). The value group over C at this branch contains the orders d and all d w_j, whose gcd is one by the definition of d. In the Laurent series field C((z)) the integer-valued order on the restricted function field is therefore primitive, and −ord T=d. Thus this gives a place of ramification exactly d. Its complete series coefficients through any fixed order are in the *fixed* Galois field K=Q(p^(1/h),μ_h), h=|det U|, and have heights o(L): each coefficient is a fixed polynomial in the five outer p-powers and p with rational coefficients. This assertion uses no root of the moving outside cofactor in K.

For a fixed power cutoff R, truncations at the physical z have the expected error O(exp(o(L))z^R), also for bounded monomials in X_j and T after their finite leading pole is pulled out. For example take a convergence circle |z|=exp(−o(L)) of radius (C(1+p+max_j |p^(−(Ga_I)_j)|))^−1 with fixed sufficiently large C; then the argument of the fixed analytic germ stays in a compact smaller polydisk (n_j≥1). Cauchy bounds give the stated remainder for fixed orders. Only finitely many coefficients are used after fixing R.

## 3. Degree equals the single-place ramification: moving linear forms

Write b=[Qbar(C):Qbar(T)]. This is bounded by the degree of the component. Pass to a fixed b. Suppose b>d. Choose b monomials ψ_i in the X_j that form a basis over Qbar(T), including 1. Such a basis exists among total degrees ≤b−1, uniformly: the spans of monomials of degrees ≤k contain 1, and if any two successive spans agree before filling the field then their common span is an algebra stable under all X_j, which already generate this finite extension. Fix those monomials on a subsequence. Their physical evaluations are positive integers, with archimedean orders O(L), and at the selected infinity branch their pole orders are ≤dC₀ for a fixed positive integer C₀.

For a positive integer J to be chosen large and then held fixed, take the m=b(J+1) rational projective coordinates h_{ij}=T^j ψ_i (0≤j≤J) evaluated at the point. They include 1 and N^J; their projective height lies between JL and (J+C₁)L for a fixed C₁. In Laurent expansions in z their lowest order is ≥−d(J+C₀). Gaussian elimination on the first m−1 consecutive series coefficient rows produces a basis of m independent linear forms over K such that, after ordering the resulting forms from 0 to m−1, the form in position r has zero coefficients at the first r orders (equivalently use a basis adapted to the nested kernels; each new coefficient lowers dimension by at most one). Each may be chosen with a coefficient 1 and all coefficient heights o(L), by minors of these fixed-size matrices; including zero-rank/gap cases needs no nonsingularity assumption. At the real physical point the corresponding bounds from §2 are

    log |L_r(h)| ≤ (J+C₀−r/d)L+o(L),    0≤r<m.

An exact zero on infinitely many points would already contradict the independent-function transfer below. At each archimedean place of the fixed Galois K use the forms pulled back by the corresponding embedding, so that the chosen numerical cancellations occur at *all* archimedean embeddings of the rational coordinates. There are only finitely many conjugates; the normalized total arch logarithm is ≤[m(J+C₀)−m(m−1)/(2d)]L+o(L). At all places above F use the coordinate forms: the ψ_i are integral, and the normalized sum of the logs is ≤−bJ(J+1)log N_F/2. Thus the product over these finite and infinite places has log at most

    [ (b/2)(1−b/d) J² + O(J+1) ]L + o(L).

All implied coefficients in O(J+1) are fixed independently of J. In the o(L) term J is fixed. For b>d choose J large enough to make the bracket strictly negative.

Apply the moving-target Schmidt Subspace Theorem in the independent-system formulation [Vojta, *Nevanlinna theory and Diophantine approximation*, Theorem 7.7](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book37/files/vojta.pdf), from Ru–Vojta. Take the collection of forms to consist of all the above bases with their finitely many arch conjugates; the admissible subsets are the individual bases. Each such basis is independent. Projective heights of the forms are o(L), and their displayed normalizations give total log norms o(L). On every infinite coherent subset the usual field generated by the coefficient ratios consists of elements of height o(L) in K (each field expression is fixed, with nonzero denominators off a finite set). A nontrivial linear relation of our point coordinates over that field would, after specialization on a subsequence where some coefficient is nonzero, give a bounded-degree polynomial in T,X with coefficients of height o(L) vanishing at the point. Section 1 forces it to vanish on C, contradicting the Qbar(T)-independence of the monomials ψ_i and the independence of the powers of T in each coefficient polynomial. This checks linear nondegeneracy on all such subsets. Finally the point vector consists of integers and includes 1, so its outside-S maximum norms are exactly 1. The local Weil-function formulation of Theorem 7.7 therefore gives log of the indicated product ≥−ε h(h)−o(L) for any fixed ε>0, contradicting the strict negative multiple of L for small enough ε. Thus b≤d. Section 2 gives b≥d, proving equality, total ramification and uniqueness of the geometric point above infinity; uniqueness makes that point rational over Q.

## 4. Finite-value poles and coprimality on the curve

For use here, [Bartolomé, *The Skolem–Abouzaïd theorem in the singular case*, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.3233) implies the following consequence. For plane absolutely irreducible equations P(U,V)=0 of bounded bidegree and height o(L), through the origin and nonconstant in each coordinate, a physical solution cannot have h(U)≫L and lgcd(U,V)=o(L). Indeed its vanishing order r at zero is ≥1 and deg_V P is uniformly bounded; choose a fixed sufficiently small ε in the theorem. The normalized local logarithmic gcd sums min(log⁺|1/U|,log⁺|1/V|) over all places.

Suppose X_i has a pole at a point with finite T=t₀. The projection to (T,X_i) has bounded-degree, height-o(L) defining polynomial P. A finite pole forces t₀ to be a root of the leading coefficient in X_i, by dividing the equation by the highest power of X_i and evaluating. In particular t₀ has bounded degree and height o(L) (elementary Mahler measure or the root-height bound; t₀=0 is harmless). Project instead to (1/X_i,T−t₀); the closure passes through (0,0) and the transformed equation has the same bounded degree and height o(L). At the physical solution U=1/x_i has h(U)≥δL and no small value at any nonarchimedean place, since x_i is an ordinary integer. At every archimedean embedding |N−t₀|>1 eventually, since the algebraic degree of t₀ is bounded and all its conjugates have absolute value exp(o(L)) or less. Thus lgcd(U,V)=0, contradicting the consequence above. There are no such poles. Applying the same consequence to the coordinate pairs (X_i,X_j) and (T,X_i) excludes any shared zeros: the physical integer pairs are coprime and have logarithmic gcd zero. Nonconstant functions on the projective curve give nonconstant projection coordinates in each case. This proves the stated pole and zero-divisor assertions.

Finally, for every integer row u with u·w∈Z, the function T^(−u·w)∏X_i^(u_i) has valuation zero at the rational infinity point, and its nonzero rational residue is p^(−u·Ga_I) by §2. A rational power of a prime is rational only for an integer exponent. Hence the character u↦u·Ga_I mod Z on Z⁵ annihilates the kernel of u↦u·w mod Z. The latter has cyclic image of exact order d; every homomorphism from this cyclic group to Q/Z is an integer multiple of its inclusion. Thus Ga_I−kw is integral for some k, and multiplying by A′ gives the other congruence since a∈col A′ and A′w=1. Nothing here requires a coefficient discriminant or a moving Kummer root to lie in a fixed field.

## Related work

This is a restriction in the [Goldbach factorization graph framework of Bożek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900). [Kelanemer’s August preprint, revised September 6](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460) reports a related exponent-matrix framework and claims a full Goldbach proof in its abstract. The full-text delivery remained inaccessible at the November 21 check, so this note neither relies on that claim nor asserts comprehensive nonoverlap or priority. Compare the [fixed Kummer-class result](PrimeOrbitSixKummerCofactors.md), which does exclude a restricted family; this single-pole theorem does not exclude all of its broader class.
