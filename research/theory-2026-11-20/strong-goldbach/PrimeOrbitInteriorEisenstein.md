# An analytic torus obstruction for interior prime factors at smooth centers

Fix a finite set F of primes, an odd prime p, an integer k≥2 and δ>0. Let N be an even F-smooth integer coprime to p. Suppose C is a set of k distinct primes, with p∈C, all coprime to N and less than N−1, every N−x for x∈C composite and factored entirely in C. Write A for the k-by-k exponent matrix A_xy=v_y(N−x).

**Interior theorem.** For all sufficiently large such N, if every x∈C other than p lies between N^δ and N^(1−δ), then the k−1 nonseed columns of A are linearly dependent over the rationals. There is no connectivity or eigenvalue hypothesis in this theorem. Its asymptotic threshold is not claimed effective.

**Six-label corollary.** For fixed F,p,δ and all sufficiently large even F-smooth N prime to p, there is no strongly connected six-label C as above in which all five nonseed primes are ≥N^δ. The upper interior cutoff is *not* assumed in this corollary. By the [six-label fork](PrimeOrbitSixLabelFork.md) and the [smooth 2+4 exclusion](PrimeOrbitSixSmoothTerminal.md), the same exclusion holds for the full all-composite factor orbit generated by p if it has size six (without assuming strong connectivity). This is a restriction on possible factorizations, not an assertion of binary Goldbach at any unbounded family of even integers.

## 1. A rational S-unit chart with an analytic inverse

Suppose contrariwise that N grows without bound and the k−1 nonseed columns are independent. Order the primes x_0=p,x_1,...,x_t with t=k−1, for instance in increasing order after p. Their columns have entries at most 1/δ, so pass to a subsequence fixing these columns and the t rows I on which their t-by-t matrix U is invertible. Let G=U^(-1), D a common positive denominator of its entries, and a=(v_p(N−x_i)) indexed by i∈I. Only this seed-exponent vector can vary; it is O_p(log N) coordinatewise. In the seed row the seed exponent is zero since (p,N)=1.

Write T_j=x_j/N for all j including T_0=p/N. Taking positive real rational powers of the positive row equations gives exactly, for j=1,...,t,

    T_j=b_j ∏_{i∈I}(1−T_i)^(G_ji),       b_j=N^((G·1)_j−1) p^(−(Ga)_j).       (1)

Each b_j^D is a positive rational S-unit for S consisting of the places above F∪{p} and infinity in Q. All T_i are O(N^(−δ')) for δ'=min(δ,1/2); (1) therefore gives b_j=O(N^(−δ')). All rational prime exponents of b_j^D are O(log N). Fix their residues modulo D on a subsequence (finite choices for all coordinates simultaneously) and write b_j=α_j X_j, with α_j a fixed positive real algebraic number and X_j a positive *rational* S-unit: for a local exponent Dq+r use the factor l^(r/D) in α_j and l^q in X_j. Append the coordinates X_0=1/N and X_*=1/N². For X=(X_0,X_*,X_1,...,X_t), the maximum coordinate at infinity is O(N^(−δ')), each absolute logarithmic height is O(log N), and the *projective* height h(X) is ≥h(X_0/X_*)=log N. This last assertion follows directly from the product formula in the definition of projective height.

Define analytic functions Φ_j of these t+2 variables at the origin by

    Φ_j=α_j X_j ∏_{i∈I}(1−H_i)^(G_ji),     H_0=pX_0,   H_i=Φ_i for i>0.       (2)

Choose powers by the analytic branch equal to one at zero. The derivative with respect to the unknown vector of the system “Φ minus the right side” at zero is the identity. The analytic implicit function theorem gives a unique convergent germ with Φ(0)=0. Its coefficients are algebraic: recursion by homogeneous degree uses the factor X_j on the right and the algebraic binomial coefficients/α_j. Equations (1), positivity of the chosen branch and T→0 give Φ_j(X)=x_j/N for every sufficiently large physical point.

## 2. A common irreducible coset (without assuming the original points dense)

Use [Corvaja–Zannier, *S-unit points on analytic hypersurfaces*, Ann. Sci. ÉNS 38 (2005), pp. 76–92, Theorems 1 and 3, pp. 78–80](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.ansens.2004.09.003.pdf). Precisely, Theorem 1, for a convergent power series f at zero with algebraic coefficients, a number field K, a place ν, and X∈(K*)^n tending to zero at ν with f(X)∈K, requires:

* for each coordinate the sum of its and its inverse's outside-S logarithmic denominator heights is o(h(X_i));
* the sum of all coordinate heights is O(−log ||X||_ν);
* the outside-S denominator height of f(X) is o(h(X));
* h(f(X))=O(h(X)), with h(X) the projective height of the input vector.

Its conclusion is containment of the sequence in finitely many translates of connected subtori, on each of which f coincides near the origin with a polynomial **over K**. Algebraic coefficients of the original series need not belong to K. In this application K=Q, ν=∞. For each Φ_j the first and third outside-S quantities vanish exactly (N is F-smooth), the second follows from the bounds for X, and the last follows from h(x_j/N)≤log N≤h(X). Each coordinate tends to zero, and thus has height tending to infinity. We may therefore replace Φ_j by a rational polynomial on each of finitely many cosets containing the physical points.

One must also extend the *factor identity*, not just the individual coordinate evaluations. The fixed seed-row multiplicities are c_j=A_0j, with d=Σ_j c_j≥2. Apply Theorem 3 of the same paper to the analytic function

    f_0(X)=X_0^(d−1)(1−pX_0)−∏_{j=1}^t Φ_j(X)^c_j.

Its hypotheses are precisely algebraic coefficients, local convergence, S-unit inputs tending to zero, sum of their heights O(−log ||X||_∞), and zero evaluations. The last equality follows by dividing N−p=∏x_j^c_j by N^d. It gives a finite covering by cosets on which f_0 is identically zero near the origin (if the germ is already zero, use the whole torus).

Intersect one covering for each Φ_j and one for f_0. There are finitely many intersections; each nonempty intersection is a translate of an algebraic subgroup of a torus, a finite union of translates of its connected component. One such connected coset V contains an infinite subsequence. It has a positive rational S-unit base point u from that subsequence. Each polynomial/analytic coincidence restricts to V near zero, and V contains physical points arbitrarily close to zero. Hence the polynomial identity obtained by replacing each Φ_j with its rational polynomial is true on a nonempty relative complex-analytic open subset of the irreducible V, so it holds identically there. Equivalently one may use the formal restriction in the cited theorem. This step makes no Zariski density assumption about the starting sequence.

## 3. Laurent irreducibility at the seed

Every connected subtorus of a split torus here has character lattice a torsion-free quotient of the ambient integer character lattice; see §2, p. 80 in the cited paper. Translating V by its rational point u thus identifies Q[V] with a Laurent polynomial ring over Q. Under this identification, X_0^(-1)=λ z_1^e with λ=u_0^(-1)=N(u) and e≥1 in a suitable integer basis for the character lattice: the exponent vector is nonzero since the N's grow on V, and one completes its primitive part to a basis. Here N(u) is a positive integer coprime to p. Thus X_0^(-1)−p is *irreducible* in Q[V]. Indeed λ z_1^e−p is Eisenstein at p in Q[z_1], stays prime on adjoining independent variables, and remains prime and a nonunit on inverting all the variables (its ideal meets none of the Laurent monomials).

The rational Laurent functions Q_j=Φ_j/X_0, using their polynomial representatives on V, take the values x_j on the physical subsequence. Each is nonzero and is a nonunit in Q[V]. Otherwise it would be a nonzero rational constant times a character in the translated coordinates, and each character is the restriction of an integer Laurent monomial in the ambient coordinates after translating by u. The physical coordinates and u's coordinates are rational S-units; hence the prime factors of such unit values would all belong to a fixed finite set, contrary to x_j ≥ N^δ tending to infinity. The identity in §2 now reads in Q[V]

    X_0^(-1)−p = ∏ Q_j^c_j.

It contradicts irreducibility since all occurring factors are nonunits and Σ c_j≥2. This proves the interior theorem.

## 4. The six-label boundary case and the corollary

Assume an unbounded six-label countersequence. Pass to limiting weights w_i=lim log x_i/log N, fixing its bounded nonseed columns and the arc choices below. The [seed-column theorem and its at-most-one-high lemma](PrimeOrbitSixTerminalFiveMacroscopic.md) imply that A's seed column is in the span of its five other columns, and at most one label has limiting weight one. The [one-seed theorem](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) gives nullity(I−A)=2 by the feedback upper bound. The irreducible nonnegative A has zero diagonal, each row sum at least two, and Perron eigenvalue ρ≥2. Its rank must be five: the column-span result shows ≤5; if rank ≤4 then zero has algebraic multiplicity at least two, 1 has multiplicity at least two, and the list contains 0,0,1,1,ρ. The sixth eigenvalue from trace zero would be −ρ−2, contradicting the spectral radius. The five nonseed columns are consequently independent.

If there is a high label y, by the linked lemma its unique parent x is nonhigh, nonseed and different from y; the y column has exactly the entry 1 in row x. The fixed five nonseed columns have a fixed nonzero integral left-null vector d that annihilates all columns of A by the span result. Thus ∏(N−x_i)^d_i=1. All labels are ≤(N−3)/3 since they occur as factors of composite neighboring complements. Taking logs gives Σd_i=0 for all sufficiently large N and then Σd_i log(1−x_i/N)=0. The y column gives d_x=0, and d_y=0 since |log(1−y/N)|≥y/N=N^(−o(1)), whereas the other terms are O(N^(−ε)) for some ε>0 after fixing the limiting weights. Dimensionality gives a nonzero real w with w^T A=w^T and w_x=0, hence w_y=0 by the y column. It has mixed signs by pairing with a positive right Perron vector. Removing x,y therefore leaves a 4-by-4 principal nonnegative zero-diagonal matrix E with a nontrivial left zero-eigenvector and a mixed-sign left eigenvector at 1, by restriction of d,w. This is impossible: its spectral radius r is ≥1. If r>1, the eigenvalues would be 0,1,r,−r−1 by the trace. If r=1, Perron–Frobenius gives a nonnegative left eigenvector at 1 independent of the mixed one, and the list would include 0,1,1,−2. Both contradict the spectral radius. This rules out the high case, not just the nonsingular branch.

All five nonseed weights are now in [δ,1), so on this subsequence they lie in the interior interval [N^ε,N^(1−ε)] for a fixed ε>0 and all sufficiently large N. Their columns are independent, contradicting the interior theorem. Every hypothetical unbounded countersequence admitted the indicated subsequences. Thus the corollary is uniform for fixed F,p,δ, though no effective bound is claimed.

The case of four or fewer nonseed labels above a fixed polynomial threshold in a six-label orbit, and unrestricted larger orbits, are not eliminated here.
