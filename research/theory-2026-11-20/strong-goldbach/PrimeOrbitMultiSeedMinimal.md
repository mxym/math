# A joint orbit of s prescribed primes cannot have the extremal 2s+2 labels

Fix s≥1 distinct odd primes P={p₁,...,p_s}, a finite set F of rational primes and ε>0. For all sufficiently large even N coprime to every seed, with N_F=∏_{l∈F}l^{v_l(N)} ≥ N^(1/2+ε), any **joint forward orbit** from P in which all prime complements N−x are composite has at least 2s+3 labels. This also applies to any strongly connected, factor-closed all-composite set containing P. No strong connectivity is presumed in the joint-orbit statement. For one seed the existing at-least-six statement is stronger; for two or more seeds this excludes the formerly possible size 2s+2. In particular a genuinely six-label terminal on an unbounded smooth sequence with a prescribed seed p can contain no *second fixed prime*. Combined with the [opposite size extremes](PrimeOrbitSixTerminalExtremeLabels.md), its smallest nonseed q must tend to infinity while satisfying log q=o(log N).

The conclusion applies at a hypothetical Goldbach failure since every complement reached from a unit seed is again odd composite with odd prime factors coprime to N. It proves no Goldbach representation and does not say the seeds share a terminal component.

## 1. Rank and the next-smallest label

Write C for the joint orbit, k=|C|, A for the prime-exponent matrix, B=I−A and ν for its rational nullity. A bounded-size closed orbit containing any fixed finite list of unit seeds has their coordinate classes independent in Q^C/row_Q(B) at large centers as above. Indeed a dependence would give a nonzero integral row combination supported on the fixed seeds with coefficients (log N)^{O_k(1)} by signed minors. Squaring gives ∏p_i^{2u_i}≡1 mod N, unequal to 1 over Q by distinct primality. At a fixed l∈F with v_l(N)≫_{F,ε}log N on a subsequence, the standard fixed-base p-adic logarithm bound gives valuation O(log log N), contradiction. This is the same argument as the [dyadic seed test](PrimeOrbitDyadicRank.md), with the ordinary general-prime bound from [Bugeaud, Theorem 1.3, first inequality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.00275). When just one exponent is nonzero this follows from LTE. Finite choices of the prime l cause no loss.

The [feedback upper bound](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) gives ν≤floor(k/2)−1. Hence k≥2s+2 at large centers, and in a putative equality sequence ν=s. By the [macroscopic rank theorem](PrimeOrbitMacroscopicRank.md), at least s+1 labels exceed N^η for some fixed η>0. Pick r=min(C∖P). It satisfies r≤√N: otherwise the seeds alone are a retained set, meaning each row has at most one outside factor counted with multiplicity. For a retained set of size s from which every vertex is reachable, ν≤s−1. To see this without assuming irreducibility, on outside rows the eigenvector recurrence Aw=w expresses w_i as a retained linear form or w_j plus a nonzero nonnegative retained linear form. In the latter case the nonzero term follows from compositeness. If the outside subgraph is acyclic, substitution gives a first-return s-by-s nonnegative matrix with each row sum at least two, so its fixed space has dimension ≤s−1. Otherwise summing along an outside cycle gives a nonzero linear relation on retained coordinates. Those coordinates determine every outside cycle's offset: take a path from the retained set, starting at its last retained vertex before entering the cycle, and use that retained row's equation (there is exactly one outside successor) and the successive outside recurrences. The remaining outside vertices run into these cycles or a terminating row. Hence again ν≤s−1. This extends the [retained-set proof](EACTwoRetained.md) to the joint-reachability hypothesis used here.

Every label outside P∪{r} is ≥N^η, as there are exactly s+1 such labels, the seeds are fixed, and r is the least nonseed. If r remained bounded on a subsequence, apply fixed-prime independence to P∪{r} (fix its prime value on a further subsequence), contradicting ν=s. We may assume r→∞.

## 2. Shape of the supported congruence

Put L=log N and T=L/log r≥2. The s+1 bounded-height exponent columns of B outside P∪{r} have entries O_{η}(1). Independence of the seed classes and ν=s give a nonzero rational row-space relation supported on P∪{r}, and every such nonzero relation has nonzero r coordinate. Indeed s+1 coordinate classes in an s-dimensional quotient are dependent and there is no dependence among the seeds. Take t independent columns spanning the columns outside P∪{r}, and a marked column increasing their rank. On t+1 rows witnessing the increased rank, the signed maximal minors of the t columns form a bounded integral row multiplier. Its row combination is nonzero, supported on the marked coordinates, and has a nonzero r-coordinate. Squaring the corresponding residue congruence(s) and changing sign if necessary therefore yields integers with

    r^h ∏_{i=1}^s p_i^{u_i} ≡ 1 mod N,          1≤h≤C T,        |u_i|≤C L.        (2)

The bound on h is O(T), **not** O(1) unless r is macroscopic. Negative exponents are interpreted in the unit group at N.

We record how we use a fixed integer K dividing h. Write −u_i=K d_i+j_i with 0≤j_i<K, fix the j_i on a subsequence, put X=r^(h/K), Y=∏p_i^{d_i}, and α=∏p_i^{j_i}. Then (X/Y)^K≡α mod N. At every l∈F discard seeds in F (they do not divide N). Take a nearest algebraic l-adic root α_l of Z^K−α to X/Y. Separability and the fixed finite root separations show |X−α_lY|_l≪|N|_l when l|N: in the product of distances the other root distances are bounded below by a positive constant. When l∤N the corresponding bound with |N|_l=1 is automatic. All bases are local units for l|N; on restricting F to its actual divisors of N on a subsequence, this also removes unused primes. Fix root choices on a further subsequence.

At infinity and at the places p_i use the two coordinate linear forms, and at the remaining F-places use X−α_lY and Y. On (X,Y) the product of all these absolute values is at most C' X/N_F: the real and seed-place contributions for Y cancel, and X is a positive integer prime power coprime to P. Clear the denominator of Y, supported on P, to obtain primitive integral coordinates without changing this product (total scaling product is one over those places). Their height is at most N^{C''} by (2), and tends to infinity since r does. If X/N_F≤N^(−γ) with γ>0 fixed, the [p-adic Subspace Theorem, Evertse's notes, Theorem 1.1 with r=n=2](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf) places the pairs on finitely many rational lines. A rational projective ratio has the moving prime r as its sole prime factor outside the fixed set P (with positive valuation), so cannot stay constant along r→∞. Thus this saving is impossible.

If T stays bounded, h is bounded. Fix h, take K=h, so X=r≤√N and the saving follows from the N_F threshold. It remains to treat T→∞ after passing to a subsequence.

## 3. A valuation dichotomy when the moving exponent may grow

Select a fixed l∈F with v_l(N)≫L on a subsequence. If v_l(h)→∞ on a further subsequence, choose a *fixed* power K=l^j>4C, with C the uniform constant in h≤CT in (2). Eventually K|h. This time X=r^(h/K)≤exp(CL/K)<N^(1/4); apply the preceding all-F-place Subspace Theorem. Neither K nor the binomial target varies in this application.

Otherwise take a subsequence on which v_l(h)=a is fixed. In the logarithmic form replace the last base r by r^(l^a) and its exponent by h'=h/l^a. The latter is prime to l, hence its local valuation is at most those of all fixed-seed exponents, exactly the normalization hypothesis needed below. Write Ξ=r^h∏p_i^{u_i}−1; it is nonzero by unique factorization and (2) yields v_l(Ξ)≥v_l(N)≫L.

Use [Bugeaud, *B′*, Theorem 1.3, raw inequality (1.7), pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.00275), a version of [Yu, *p-adic logarithmic forms and group varieties III*](https://doi.org/10.1515/FORUM.2007.009). The number of bases, degree 1, l and all bases except the last are fixed; the marked last height is O_a(log r). With B=max(3,|u_i|,|h'|), choose B_n=max(1,|h'|)≤B and Δ=1/(2T²), allowed as 0<Δ≤1/2. The raw inequality, up to constants fixed in this subsequence, then has upper bound

    C₁ max{ (log r) log(C₂ B_n/Δ),   Δ B/B_n }.

The first term is O(log r·log(2T)) since B_n≤CT. The second is O(L/T²)=O(log r/T) since B=O(L), B_n≥1. Thus L≪log r·log(2T), contradicting T→∞. We used (1.7) with its tunable positive Δ, not either alternative (1.8)/(1.9) in isolation. If no fixed-seed coefficient occurs, one may instead apply direct LTE at l to r^h−1; it gives O_l(log r+log(2h)), also o(L). This exhausts all subsequences and proves the claim.
