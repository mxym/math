# Power saving for the exceptional prime variables in the binary Goldbach ring

This note records an unconditional O_epsilon(X^(7/12+epsilon)) bound for the odd prime anchors that fail to generate all prime variables after inverting their single variable. The proof combines [the distinct one-variable localization theorem](GenericOneVariableLocalization.md), a prime-doubling specialization of Pintz's explicit formula, Maynard–Pandey–Radziwiłł's prime exponential sum, and Green–Tao restriction. With the common Dirichlet zero bound Theta<11/12 the same argument gives O_Theta(sqrt(X) log^100 X). This is a structural statement in the full ring, with regularity of even a single generic variable still open.

## The diagonal exceptional set

For every fixed positive even m, there is c>0 such that
    sum_{a+b=mt; a,b odd prime} log(a) log(b) >= c mt
for all but O_{m,epsilon}(X^(7/12+epsilon)) prime t<=X. It suffices to work dyadically on target n=mt in (Y/4,Y/2]. This dyadic band is inside the restriction in Part I for cutting the zero sums to fixed height; `Y` is the auxiliary prime/arc scale, not the upper endpoint of the target band. Choose a small fixed delta<1/72 and P0=Y^(4/9-delta). [Pintz Part I, Theorem 1 (2.10)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05561) explicitly chooses ONE cutoff for all target integers; its compact summary in Part II, Theorem A (2.7)–(2.14), supplies a common cutoff P in [Y^(4/9-2delta),Y^(4/9-delta)], with real major coefficient given by the principal term S(n)n, a bounded number of secondary terms, and O(Y exp(-cH)+Y/sqrt(T)+Y^(1-kappa)) on this family. Any singular-series factor in the error is bounded uniformly for fixed m, since the only new prime factor of n is t. Its weight counts genuine prime summands a,b>Y^(1-epsilon0). First choose H and T so these first two error terms are less than a small fixed fraction of the main lower bound. The zero count K is bounded independently of Y after fixing the parameters. Choose eta small enough relative to K^2. Pintz's (2.12)–(2.13) show that any secondary coefficient larger than eta in absolute value requires each participating primitive conductor r to divide C(eta)n. Because t>P and t is prime, this requires r|C(eta)m. For that finite set of conductors, compactness up to fixed height T and nonvanishing on Re s=1 imply that no zeros meet Re s>=1-H/log Y once Y is large. The pole is permitted and furnishes the main term. Every remaining term has coefficient at most eta and its beta factor is bounded by the beta function of the real parts, at most 2 for large Y. Consequently the major coefficient on ALL these targets is at least c_0Y without a common zero-free half-plane. No assumption about the existence of a real exceptional zero is needed in this use of Theorem A.

The unconditional bound in Maynard–Pandey–Radziwiłł Theorem 1.1, with the lower prime cutoff treated exactly as in Pintz–Althöfer §2.2, gives sup on minor arcs O_eta(Y^eta(Y/sqrt(P)+Y^(19/24))). Bessel and Parseval bound the number of coefficients of size at least c_0Y/2 by O_eta(Y^(2eta) log Y (Y/P+Y^(7/12))). Since 5/9+2delta<7/12, this is O_epsilon(Y^(7/12+epsilon)). An n in the chosen progression family with weighted count below c_0Y/2 must have a minor coefficient at least that large in modulus. Passing to the unrestricted genuine-prime count can only increase it. The count of t inherits the same exponent. With the common zero bound, Li–Liu v1 Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 1 provide the corresponding half-main-term exceptional count O_Theta(sqrt(Y)log^100Y), using their nonnegative bounded smooth weight. This follows by the same argument as §15, using |m_+(n)|>=c_wY/2 whenever the total coefficient is below half the major lower bound. Their unconditioned statements are used only where explicitly given.

The same unconditional major-arc argument applies to even targets n whose part supported on prime factors at most P divides a fixed integer m. This can include two prime factors both greater than P. The minor-arc exceptional count is still absolute and gives no pointwise positivity.

## Closure and bootstrap

Let C(t) be the smallest subset of odd primes containing t and closed under replacing both summands a,b already in the subset by any genuine odd-prime pair c,d with a+b=c+d. A branch CLOSED under replacement means all its prime labels, not a branch closed under just diagonal replacements. A label p belongs to C(t) if and only if some multiset of k copies of t can be changed by actual quadratic moves to a k-multiset containing p. For the forward direction, concatenate the two move paths from copies of t before firing a pair replacement. There is no need to cancel any variable. Equivalently x_p is a unit in the localization at x_t. For the converse, clear denominators in the equation for an inverse to obtain x_p x_t^j g=x_t^k in the original quotient. In the component basis of the homogeneous binomial quotient, at least one monomial of x_p x_t^j g lies in the class of x_t^k.

Set B={t:C(t) is proper}. The independent premise [GenericOneVariableLocalization.md](GenericOneVariableLocalization.md) (not merely the different fixed-binomial statement [OnePrimeCatalyst.md](OnePrimeCatalyst.md)) implies sum_{t<=x,t in B}log t=o(x). If t is in B, C(t) is contained in B because a prime in C(t) whose own closure is all primes would give C(t) all primes as well. In particular each genuine pair a+b=2t lies entirely in B if t is in B. The union of all proper supports closed under pair replacements is exactly B. This union retains closure under diagonal replacements 2t=a+b, which is all the analysis uses.

Let W(x)=sum_{p<=x,p in B}log p. If X/2<t<=X and t in B, its doubled target has weighted representation count at least cX outside an exceptional set of size E(X). For the good t all representations use two labels in B. Work on a prime cyclic group M between 8X and 16X with normalized Fourier coefficients and f(n)=log(n) 1_{n in B, n<=2X}. The total log-weighted number of triples a+b=2t is bounded above by
    M^2 sum_k |fhat(k)|^2 |fhat(-2k)| <= M^2 ||fhat||_3^3
    <= C X^2 (W(2X)/X)^(3/2).
The frequency doubling is a permutation, there is no wrap in the equation, and the second inequality is Green–Tao Proposition 4.2 with a_n=f(n)/beta_R(n), since beta_R dominates log R on primes above R, with R a small fixed power of M. For exact application, discard low t (none for large X) and pairs with a or b<=R. There are O(R) such pairs for any fixed t and their weighted contribution is O(R log^2 X)=o(X), so the same lower bound holds with the truncated f, for which sum |f|^2/beta_R is O(W(2X)). This also avoids every small-prime issue in the restriction inequality.

Thus
    (W(X)-W(X/2))/X <= C(W(2X)/X)^(3/2) + O(E(X)log X/X).
For x_k=2^k set b_k=W(x_k)/x_k and U_k=sup_{j>=k}b_j. Since b_j tends to zero, U_k does too. For dyadic E(X)=O(X^alpha log^L X), choose k large so that C' sqrt(U_k)<=delta' with 0<delta'<1-2^(-alpha). Taking the supremum gives (1-delta')U_k <= (1/2)U_{k-1}+O(2^(-(1-alpha)k)k^(L+1)). The ratio 1/(2(1-delta')) is strictly smaller than 2^(-(1-alpha)). Iteration gives W(X)=O(X^alpha log^(L+1) X). Partial summation, or counting in dyadic blocks with log t comparable to log X, gives # {t<=X:t in B}=O(X^alpha log^L X). For the unconditional epsilon version take a smaller epsilon at input. This proof keeps the same square root exponent and log^100 factor for the conditional variant.

In particular if x_t fails to make every prime variable a unit, the actual ordered representation count of 2t is O_epsilon(t^(7/12+epsilon)) and its weighted version satisfies the same exponent after shrinking epsilon. A pointwise lower bound exceeding this threshold at a specified prime double would prove that particular variable localizes the full ring to the torus. Even complete localization at t leaves injectivity at t open.

The algebraic consequence applies over any field and in the infinite ring. Let I be the physical quadratic ideal, J the toric ideal, and L the ideal generated by all x_t with t outside B. The full Laurent localization is toric by the established rank-two torsion-free relation group (Nelson §6). Since a good variable alone inverts all other variables, every prime ideal containing I either contains J or contains L, by localizing at a single x_t absent from that prime. Therefore sqrt(I)=J intersect sqrt(I+L). All prime-ideal components away from the toric variety are supported on one common power-sparse set of prime variables. This controls possible support outside the torus closure and leaves open nilpotent and zero-divisor phenomena.
