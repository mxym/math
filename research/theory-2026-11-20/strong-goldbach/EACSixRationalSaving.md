# Direct explicit six-label saving: epsilon = 1/1025

Supplement to [the full six-label strict saving](EACSixFullStrictPowerSaving.md). This improves the [earlier degree-bounded CAD estimate](EACSixExplicitSaving.md). The number of actual six-label EAC-bearing centers up to \(X\) is at most
\[
 X^{\,3/4-1/1025+o(1)}.
\]
This gives an explicit exponent, not a practical cutoff, not a count of realized six-label components, and not a count of ordinary Goldbach exceptions.

Set \(\delta=1/1025\). Use all prior branches of the strict-saving proof unchanged (including the already audited \(7/10+o(1)\) two-label product bound for doubly singular corank one). For corank two with singular \(A\), if \(p\le N^{1/4-\delta}\) the old frozen-\(p\) tangent count gives exponent \(3/4-\delta\), or the small-\(q\) branch gives \(5/8\). If \(p>N^{1/4-\delta}\), all rows at large \(N\) have arity at most 4 since \(p^5>N\), and at least one row has arity 4 by the known arity≤3 rank obstruction. Freeze a pure-fourth base \(s\) when that chart has one, and otherwise the minimum \(p\), as in the strict-saving proof. The old tangent lemma applies for frozen \(p\), and the direct rank-\([A'\mid\mathbf1]\) argument applies for frozen \(s\). A zero tangent or a moving label \(\le N^{1/2-\delta}\) gives the claimed counting cost (one seed, or seed plus that moving label). The excluded exact divisor lane costs \(X^{5/8+\delta/2+o(1)}\), still smaller. We show there are no infinitely many remaining physical components by using fixed thresholds, not a sequence of thresholds converging to zero.

## 1. The denominator gap pins the minimum at exactly 1/4

Suppose an unbounded countersequence, with \(p>N^{1/4-\delta}\), no moving coordinate \(\le N^{1/2-\delta}\) in a nonzero frozen tangent, and outside the divisor lane. Fix the chart, seed choice, and convergent logarithmic weights \(\omega_i=\lim \log x_i/\log N\). The \(\nu=2,\det A=0\) branch has \(p<N^{1/4}\), so
\[
 1/4-\delta\le\omega_p\le1/4.
\]
We may use \(p q\le N^{5/7}\) for sufficiently large \(N\) from the \(7/10+o(1)\) result. Thus \(\omega_q\le5/7-1/4+\delta<1/2-\delta\), and \(p,q\) are actually stationary along the sequence.

All factors in any arity-four row have limiting weight at most \(1-3(1/4-\delta)=1/4+3\delta<1/2-\delta\). Their *exact* differentials are therefore zero eventually by the assumed fixed threshold; this implies that row's input satisfies \(n=(x_i/N)h_i\to0\) in normalized tangent variables. As before a normalized limit lies in the one-dimensional \(\ker A\), supported on some \(S\). A coordinate nonzero in that limit is actually moving for large terms, hence \(\omega_i\ge1/2-\delta\) for \(i\in S\). Each product involving any \(S\) factor needs an opposite-sign \(S\) factor. A further factor would give weight at least \(2(1/2-\delta)+(1/4-\delta)>1\). Thus \(S\) again has its single connected bipartite pair graph, disjoint from all \(Z\)-only factor rows where \(Z=S^c\). Each factor in \(S\) has weight in the whole interval
\[
 [1/2-\delta,\ 1/2+\delta],
\]
since it has an opposite-shore neighbor and their sizes have total weight 1. As in the original proof \(2\le|S|\le4\) and the \(Z\)-block has full column rank \(|Z|\).

Take an invertible \(|Z|\times|Z|\) submatrix \(L\) of the \(Z\)-block, using fixed \(Z\)-only rows. Taking logarithms of the complementary products gives \(L\omega_Z=\mathbf1\), since \(M/N\to0\). Each integer row of \(L\) has sum at most four and hence Euclidean norm at most four. Thus the denominator of each rational \(\omega\) on \(Z\) divides \(\det L\), and \(|\det L|\le4^{|Z|}\le256\). There are two useful rational gaps:
\[
 |\omega_i-1/4|\ge1/(4\cdot256)=1/1024\quad\text{if }\omega_i\ne1/4;
 \qquad
 |\omega_i-1/2|\ge1/(2\cdot256)=1/512\quad\text{if }\omega_i\ne1/2.
\]
Since \(p\in Z\) and \(\delta<1/1024\), this pins \(\omega_p=1/4\) exactly. Also every \(Z\) label of weight strictly below \(1/2\) has weight strictly below \(1/2-\delta\), so its derivative is exactly zero eventually. Every \(Z\) label strictly above \(1/2\) has weight strictly above \(1/2+\delta\), hence dominates all \(S\) labels. These are the two facts needed in the prior stationary proof; exact half-weight of the \(S\) labels was stronger than necessary.

If there is no pure-fourth row, the existence of a quartic row still forces \(\omega_q=1/4\) (at least two distinct factors of that weight). If there is a pure-fourth row its chosen base \(s\) has weight exactly \(1/4\) and \(s\sim N^{1/4}\), whether minimal or not. All selected freezing arguments and row types from the original strict-saving proof remain valid.

## 2. Check every affected step of that proof in the widened support range

The cofactor graph on \(S\) is still a tree or \(C_4\), because the rank and edge count are unchanged. A \(C_4\) on all four support vertices still cannot occur: the input of its smallest product exceeds twice the maximum factor, while the two labels outside \(S\) are \(p,q\), both of weight \(<1/2-\delta\), and hence below the support. The graph argument uses actual gaps of at least two, not an exact limiting half-weight. The full-rank \(Z\) block and same-sign pairing for a pure row are also unchanged.

**Pure-fourth charts.** In the four-vertex tree, only the two \(Z\) labels enter the residual monomials, with one weight \(1/4\) and the other in \([1/4,1/2)\). The same exact possibilities \(s^4\) plus at most \(s q^2\) at other weight \(3/8\) or \(q^3\) at \(1/3\) hold. If both weights are \(1/4\), the quartic gaps have exponent \(3/4\), while \(M\le N^{1/2+\delta+o(1)}\), still impossible. For the three-vertex tree, retain the mutual-sign pairing \(s=r=0\); it forces \(r\in S\) exactly as before. The direct \(a,b\)-mutual case still gives \(b-a=r(b-a)\), independent of weights. In the cross-mate case \(Z=\{s,u,v\}\), \(u<v\). One of \(u,v\) has weight \(<1/2-\delta\), because the actual \(p,q\) both lie below that threshold and \(s\) is only one label. If \(\omega_v<1/2\) it is \(\le1/2-1/512\), and cannot produce the star-pair product gap of size at least \(2N^{1/2-\delta+o(1)}\). If \(\omega_v>1/2\) it is \(\ge1/2+1/512\), hence the true largest label; it has only one occurrence, and the same argument with \(u^3/u^4\) and the pure \(s^4\) row excludes it. This argument uses \(p=\min(s,u)\), still true because \(u\) is below the support. Thus \(\omega_v=1/2\). If \(\omega_u>1/4\), the four residual monomials still force \(v\sim\sqrt N\) from \(v^2\). The \(S\)-star center \(c\le(v-u)/2\) still satisfies \(c\le(1/2+o(1))\sqrt N\). The distance of its leaves is now at most \(N^{\delta+o(1)}\), because \(c\ge N^{1/2-\delta+o(1)}\); this is still \(o(\sqrt N)\), so the three monomial gap contradiction works unchanged. If \(\omega_u=1/4\), the same arity types have gaps of exponent \(3/4\), above \(M\le N^{1/2+\delta+o(1)}\). The two-vertex tree argument is exact linear algebra on \(\ker B\) and exact integer divisors \(r(a-1)=s^4-u\); no step uses the precise \(S\) weights. This lane is already excluded in the countersequence.

**No pure fourth, trees with \(t=3,4\).** Now \(p,q\) both have weight \(1/4\). With \(t=4\) at most one quartic on \(p,q\) can occur as before (two distinct near-equal products force \(q/p\to1\), \(p^4\sim N\), gaps \(\ge2p^3>M\)). With \(t=3\), third \(Z\) label \(z\): if \(\omega_z<1/2\) it is \(<1/2-\delta\), so all \(Z\) labels are exactly stationary, forcing \(\delta N=0\) and killing a moved \(S\) coordinate from the four \(Z\)-only rows. If \(\omega_z>1/2\) it is \(>1/2+\delta\), the true maximum, appearing only once; the remaining ≥3 \(p,q\)-quartics are impossible. If \(\omega_z=1/2\), then \(M\le N^{1/2+\delta+o(1)}\) and scaled \(z\)-products involving different quadratics in \(p,q\) have gap \(\ge2pz=N^{3/4+o(1)}\), still too large. Together with the single quartic and the square this gives too few rows.

**No pure fourth, \(t=2\) tree.** The two extra \(Z\) weights below \(1/2\) are exactly stationary, and any above \(1/2\) dominate every \(S\) label by the denominator gap. Thus every “unique global maximum” argument of the earlier proof persists verbatim. If one label of weight \(1/2\) is present and the other below \(1/2\), the weight enumeration with that lower label (either \(1/4\) or one of the strict interior possibilities \(1/3,3/8\) or no allowed monomial) uses only \(Z\)-weights. The scaled product gaps of exponent \(3/4\) still dominate \(M\) when none is higher; the exact one-parameter derivative and strong-connectivity closure arguments are unchanged. If one or both \(Z\) highs are strictly above \(1/2\), all are of weight \(3/4\) whenever both extra weights are \(\ge1/2\), by the same row weights. The uniqueness and ratio comparisons with \(M<N/p\) remain valid. If both extra \(Z\) labels have weight exactly \(1/2\), the same triangular grid of exponent pairs occurs. The scaled product gaps \(N^{3/4+o(1)}\) still beat \(M\le N^{1/2+\delta+o(1)}\). The three actual frozen-row coefficients still converge to exactly the same collinearity matrix, since every \(C_{ii}=o(1)\). The diagonal side of the grid yields impossible product gaps \(>\sqrt N\) between inputs in \(Z\) that are \(\le\sqrt N\). An axis side again forces \(D=m^2\), because the error \(O(M)=O(N^{1/2+\delta+o(1)})\) is below the quartic spacing \(\ge2p^3=N^{3/4+o(1)}\), and then its three products contradict the same input gaps. Here the genuinely square factors still give concrete \(\le\sqrt N\) bounds on \(v,w\), regardless of the support weights.

That exhausts the changes: the only estimates using a support point of exact weight \(1/2\) in the former proof were ordering comparisons with \(Z\) points, upper bounds on \(M\) when all \(Z\) are at most that weight, and the width of a pair of star leaves. Respectively the rational gap, the \(\delta\)-widened upper bound, and \(N^{\delta+o(1)}=o(\sqrt N)\) replace them. Exact derivative-zero uses are all now justified by the *fixed* cutoff and denominator gap.

Thus the conjectured unbounded countersequence is impossible for the fixed \(\delta=1/1025\). Using the same isolated-root or divisor counts as in the full strict-saving proof, with no third counting parameter, yields the displayed explicit bound. No CAD or new exhaustive chart search is needed. The denominator bound \(4^{|Z|}\) was deliberately crude and possibly improvable, but \(\epsilon>0\) here is fully numerical.

## Appendix: sharpened denominator constant \(\epsilon=1/401\)

This refines the determinant bounds in the preceding proof. It does not change that proof's case split, fixed-seed choice, or counting rules.

In the limiting stationary argument, the \(Z\)-only block has full column rank \(d=|Z|\le4\), and its rows have total degree at most four.

* If the chart has no pure fourth-power row, every row's Euclidean norm is at most \(\sqrt{10}\) (the worst pattern is \(3+1\); lower arities and other quartic patterns have no bigger squared norm). For an invertible \(d\times d\) minor \(L\), Hadamard gives \(|\det L|\le(\sqrt{10})^4=100\). Since \(L\omega_Z=\mathbf1\), every coordinate has denominator dividing \(\det L\). Nonzero gaps from \(1/4\) and \(1/2\) are therefore \(\ge1/400\) and \(\ge1/200\).
* If the chart has a pure fourth-power row at base \(s\), its row \(4e_s\) is a nonzero \(Z\)-row. Choose a full-row minor including it and delete its row and its column, leaving an invertible \((d-1)\)-square integer matrix \(L'\). We know \(\omega_s=1/4\); substitution in the other rows gives right-hand sides in \(\frac14\mathbb Z\). Since the other row norms are at most four, \(|\det L'|\le4^3=64\). The remaining weights can all be written as integers divided by \(4\det L'\); thus a nonzero gap from either \(1/4\) or \(1/2\) is at least \(1/(4|\det L'|)\ge1/256\), **not** an extra factor four, because both target rationals already have denominator dividing that common denominator. The coordinate \(s\) itself has the asserted gap properties trivially.

So \(\delta=1/401\) is strictly smaller than the minimum required quarter gap \(1/400\), and strictly smaller than all half-weight gaps (at least \(1/256\) uniformly). All other comparisons used in the explicit proof are unchanged: \(5/7-1/4+\delta<1/2-\delta\), \(1/4+3\delta<1/2-\delta\), \(2(1/2-\delta)+(1/4-\delta)>1\), and \(1/2+\delta<3/4\); the divisor-lane exponent \(5/8+\delta/2\) is lower than \(3/4-\delta\). In the proof's widened support audits replace the half-gap constant \(1/512\) by \(1/256\), and the quarter gap \(1/1024\) by \(1/400\). Thus the same strict-cutoff proof gives \(X^{3/4-1/401+o(1)}\) actual EAC centers. There is no additional chart computation or change in the Goldbach scope.
