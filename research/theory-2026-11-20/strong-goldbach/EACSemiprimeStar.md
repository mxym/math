# A semiprime-only component always has a singular factorization matrix

Suppose every complementary factorization N−p in a nontrivial [Bożek exceptional autonomous component](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900) consists of exactly two prime factors, **counted with multiplicity**. Let A list their exponents, rows indexed by p and columns by component labels. Then A is singular over ℚ, at every height and with no bound on the component size. This necessary condition does not exclude all semiprime-only EACs, nor does Chen's theorem on one prime-plus-semiprime representation ensure any factor-closed component made of them.

Form the undirected cofactor graph H on the k component labels. A row N−p=uv gives an edge uv, with a loop if u=v. The k edges are distinct because the k input primes are distinct, and all k labels occur by strong connectivity. The signless edge-incidence matrix, with a 2 at a loop, agrees with A with exactly that indexing. Let M be the largest label. Every label is odd and ≤N/3. If y>M/2 occurred in two distinct edges, it would divide the nonzero even difference between their odd row labels; that difference would be at least 2y>M, impossible. Thus M has exactly one incident edge.

This edge is not a loop: if M² is a complement, any other complement is at most M(M−2), whose gap from M² exceeds every possible row difference. Let v≠M be its mate. For any edge incident to v write its other endpoint z (allowing z=v for a putative loop). Then z/M is the ratio of two complements in [2N/3,N], hence z≥(2/3)M>M/2. Every such other endpoint consequently has exactly one incident edge. There cannot be a loop at v: it would give v>M/2, and then v could not simultaneously meet M. The entire H component containing M is therefore genuinely a bipartite star, including the possibility of a single edge. The vector equal to +1 on its center, −1 on its leaves, and zero elsewhere is nonzero and annihilated by A.

The star has one fewer edge than vertices. Since H has exactly k of each, some different connected component H′ has more edges than vertices. Its edge rows are linearly dependent over ℚ; clearing denominators yields a nonzero integer vector c of row coefficients supported there with ∑_i c_i A_ij=0 for every output label j. Every row has total 2, so ∑c_i=0; in particular both coefficient signs occur. At an actual component this gives the exact nontrivial relation ∏_{c_i>0}(N−x_i)^{c_i}=∏_{c_i<0}(N−x_i)^{−c_i}. It does not make the two multisets of shifted labels identical, and does not by itself produce a contradiction.

This loss of rank concerns the actual complement-factor incidence matrix, not a matrix of possible Goldbach offsets. For example the related [AMRAL Goldbach series](https://amral.evemisslab.com/en/goldbach/srbrd/) describes semiprime cells in a different offset support geometry. No exclusion of all two-factor components is inferred from rank alone.
