# Cyclotomic obstructions to corank one on fixed-prime-power centers

Consider a fixed all-composite terminal signature: \(A\) is an irreducible nonnegative integer \(k\times k\) matrix with zero diagonal and row sums at least 2, and its actual labels are distinct odd prime units \(x_i\) of the even integer \(N\), satisfying \(N-x_i=\prod_jx_j^{A_{ij}}\). Let \(B=I-A\) have rational nullity one, with primitive integral right/left nullvectors \(v,c\) and last nonzero Smith invariant \(d\). The signature and all auxiliary base primes in this note are fixed.

1. If \(N=R\,2^a3^b\), \(R\) bounded, no such fixed signature can recur at arbitrarily large \(N\), with no condition on the relative sizes of the two powers. More generally, for \(R=N/N_{\{2,3\}}=N^{o(1)}\) a realizing sequence must satisfy \(\min x_i=O_A(R)\). The same assertions hold for any fixed set of at most two primes whose allowable local torsion, namely the rational local roots of unity of order dividing \(2d\), consists only of signs. Nullity zero is already excluded in these bounded-size fixed-signature settings by the bounded-order result cited below.
2. Let \(N=R\ell^a\) with \(R\) bounded and \(\ell\) a fixed prime. For odd \(\ell\), let \(P<Q\) be the two least **distinct odd primes** dividing \(\gcd(d,\ell-1)\). An unbounded realizing sequence requires both \(P,Q\) to exist and
\[
                          k\ \ge\ 2(P+Q)-2\ \ge\ 14.                  \tag{1}
\]
For \(\ell=2\) there is no unbounded sequence at all by part 1. In particular every fixed nullity-one signature of size at most 13 is excluded at bounded cofactor of *any* prescribed prime power. Even in unbounded size (fixed signature first), all single bases with at most one odd prime dividing \(\gcd(d,\ell-1)\) are excluded. For any fixed \(k\), a positive-density class of prime bases is excluded regardless of \(d\): take primes \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod\prod_{3\le r\le k,\ r\ {\rm prime}}r\), using the prime number theorem for a fixed arithmetic progression. If the two odd prime divisors required by a circuit both exceed \(k\), (1) cannot hold either.

These conclusions rule out recurrence of specified terminal shapes, not every terminal at a given center, nor growing matrices of unbounded row multiplicity. An all-composite terminal can be present even at a Goldbach-represented center.

## Local curve or identically vanishing phase relations

Write \(F\) for a fixed finite set with \(N_F=N^{1-o(1)}\), where \(N_F\) is the full part supported on \(F\), and pass to an unbounded subsequence on which all logarithmic weights \(w_i=\lim \log x_i/\log N\) exist. At each base \(\ell\) whose exponent in \(N\) tends to infinity, take simultaneous local unit accumulation; bounded prime-power factors may be absorbed in the cofactor. Fix an irreducible component of the Zariski closure of the physical sequence, taking a dense subsequence there. Subsequences can be refined to fix finitely many phase choices. In the bounded-cofactor case \(\min x_i\to\infty\), since otherwise a fixed prime seed reaches this bounded terminal and [fixed-seed independence](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) gives nullity at least two.

Here are the local details needed from the [finite-torus](EACFiniteTorusNearSmooth.md) and [physical-curve](EACReciprocalNearSmooth.md) theorems. At \(N=0\) the equations on unit coordinates are \(X^{B_i}=-1\). On a torus translate through the actual \(\ell\)-adic accumulation point write \(X_i=\xi_{i\ell} U^{v_i}\). One can choose every \(\xi_{i\ell}\in\mathbb Q_\ell\) to be a root of unity of order dividing \(2d\): in integral Smith coordinates the first \(k-1\) coordinates solve \(Y_h^{d_h}=\pm1,\ d_h\mid d\), and the last is free. Inverting the unimodular variable change gives these phases and the column \(v\), consistently oriented at all places. At the physical points the same parametrization is valid modulo \(\ell^{a_\ell-O_A(1)}\), \(a_\ell=v_\ell(N)\), with a common unit \(u_\ell\) possibly depending on the point. Indeed \(Y_h^{d_h}=\pm1\bmod\ell^{a_\ell}\); near its fixed limiting root \(y_h\) the valuation of \(Y_h^{d_h}-(y_h)^{d_h}\) equals \(v_\ell(Y_h-y_h)+v_\ell(d_h)\) for sufficiently large \(a_\ell\), by the simple-root Taylor expansion. No prime-to-\(\ell\) torsion is discarded.

At any such place the Laurent polynomial
\[
               g_\ell(U)=\sum_i c_i \xi_{i\ell}^{-1} U^{-v_i}
\]
must vanish *identically on this actual translate*. To recall why, the \(k-1\) independent physical row equations alone cut out a smooth local surface at that point, since their logarithmic Jacobian at \(N=0\) is of rank \(k-1\). The remaining exact compatibility \(\prod(1-N/X_i)^{c_i}=1\) holds on the dense nonzero-\(N\) sequence (its sign is \(+1\) by reduction modulo \(N>2\)). Subtract 1 and divide by \(N\); the resulting regular expression on unit coordinates restricts to \(-g_\ell\) at \(N=0\). If \(g_\ell\) is nonzero on the actual one-dimensional special-fiber branch, it cuts the surface locally to dimension at most one. This argument only uses the translate through the actual accumulation point: a different translate with identically zero polynomial is irrelevant. The physical component would be a curve, contradicting the already proved [fixed corank-one physical-curve exclusion](EACReciprocalNearSmooth.md) on near-smooth centers. The same argument can be made after choosing an irreducible component over the algebraic closure containing infinitely many physical points; the rank computation and local dimension are unchanged under extension. Since the dimension obstruction uses even one unbounded place, identity is necessary at all of them.

For a value \(b\) of \(v_i\) let \(S_b=\{i:v_i=b,\ c_i\ne0\}\) and call it active if nonempty. Each active class has simultaneously
\[
          \sum_{i\in S_b} c_i\xi_{i\ell}^{-1}=0,\qquad
          \sum_{i\in S_b} c_i\xi_{i\ell}=0                              \tag{2}
\]
at each unbounded place. The second identity uses the cyclotomic automorphism of complex conjugation after identifying the algebraic roots of unity.

Suppose now \(\min_i x_i/(N/N_F)\to\infty\), as automatically holds for bounded cofactor. Every fixed integer relation \(\sum_{i\in S_b} a_i \xi_{i\ell}=0\) holding at *all* unbounded \(\ell\in F\) lifts to
\[
                            \sum_{i\in S_b}a_i x_i=0.                  \tag{3}
\]
In fact its left side is divisible locally by \(\ell^{a_\ell-O_A(1)}\) after multiplying by the common \(u_\ell^b\). The product of these coprime moduli is at least \(N_F/C\) for a fixed constant on this subsequence; bounded places cost only a fixed factor. Every actual vertex occurs in a composite row, so \(x_i<N/\min x_j\); the absolute value of the sum is \(o(N_F)\), proving (3). In particular the tuples of local phases for two distinct vertices of one \(S_b\) cannot coincide up to a single common sign. Moreover any primitive integer phase relation in such a subset must have even coefficient sum by reducing (3) modulo 2. It is *not* permissible to use different rational relations at different places in (3).

For \(F=\{2,3\}\), the only rational local roots of unity are signs in each field. If both places grow, the columns \((\xi_{i2},\xi_{i3})\) have just two types up to global sign, represented by the independent vectors \((1,1),(1,-1)\). If only one place grows, there is just one type. With duplicate types forbidden, (2) contradicts independence. This also proves the general at-most-two signed bases assertion. To obtain the claimed \(O_A(R)\) in part 1, if no such eventual bound held select a subsequence with \(\min x_i/R\to\infty\) and apply the argument just given. A fixed prime-power factor bounded on this subsequence need not be universally bounded over every conceivable subsequence.

## Single-place circuit obstruction

At a single odd \(\ell\) the phase tuple is just a root of unity of order dividing \(m=\gcd(2d,\ell-1)\). Within any active class there is by (2) a minimally dependent subset of distinct roots no two of which are equal up to sign. Choose a rational relation on it with all coefficients nonzero. Its reduced exponent \(h\) (the lcm of all ratio orders, equivalently the order after normalizing one phase to 1) divides \(m\). The [Conway–Jones theorem, Theorem 5](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-30-3-229-240), together with the squarefree reduced-exponent property for a minimal relation, gives
\[
                  h\ \text{squarefree},\qquad
                  |\mathrm{circuit}|\ge 2+\sum_{r\mid h,\ r\ {\rm prime}} (r-2).
\]
Here length counts terms with nonzero rational coefficients, not the sum of their absolute values. One elementary proof of squarefreeness: if \(r^2\mid h\), split by the exponent modulo \(r\) over \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{h/r})\); the degree-\(r\) basis forces each slice separately zero, against minimality of the support and reduced exponent.

If \(h\) has no odd divisor, normalization and non-antipodality allow no dependence. If \(h\) has only one odd prime divisor \(r\), each normalized phase is a signed \(r\)-th root of unity, no two having the same unsigned \(r\)-th root. Since \(\Phi_r=1+T+\cdots+T^{r-1}\), any dependence is the complete regular \(r\)-gon with coefficients \(\pm1\) after dividing a common rational factor. By (3) this would give a signed sum of \(r\) odd primes equal to zero, impossible. Consequently every active class has size at least \(P+Q-2\), and if these two odd divisors do not exist there cannot be any active class.

## Why at least two active classes and four classes in all

These graph facts hold on any fixed-signature near-smooth sequence of rational nullity one and do not require a single dominant prime. At most one \(w_i\) is zero: for any two coordinates there is a nonzero rational row-space vector of \(B\) supported on them, since the rational row space is \(v^\perp\); multiplying by a fixed integer puts it in the actual row lattice. The resulting congruence \(x_i^\alpha x_j^\beta\equiv\pm1\bmod N\) is at the *full* center. If both labels are subpower, clearing denominators gives the difference or sum of two coprime positive integers of size \(N^{o(1)}\), nonzero by their distinct prime supports and the nonzero exponent vector. This cannot be divisible by \(N\).

If \(v_i=v_j\), any edge \(i\to j\) must target \(w_j=0\). Indeed \(d(e_i-e_j)\) lies in the row lattice, so for the true positive integral cofactor \(z=(N-x_i)/x_j\ge3\) one has \(z^{2d}\equiv1\bmod N\). Such an integer \(z\) cannot have \(z\le N^{1-\delta}\) infinitely often for fixed \(\delta>0\) on a near-smooth sequence. For clarity, a bounded subsequence contradicts divisibility directly. On an unbounded subsequence, at each growing place of \(F\), pass to a fixed local root of unity \(\zeta_\ell\) with \(z\equiv\zeta_\ell\bmod\ell^{v_\ell(N)-O_d(1)}\) by the same unit accumulation and Taylor argument. Apply the [two-dimensional \(p\)-adic Subspace Theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2268#page=5) to the primitive vector \((z,1)\) with coordinate forms at infinity and \((X-\zeta_\ell Y,Y)\) at these places. The product of form values is \(O(z/N_F)\), a negative power of its height, so finitely many rational lines would contain an unbounded family of \((z,1)\), impossible. For \(w_j>0\) the cofactor has exactly the forbidden upper bound.

Because \(A\) is irreducible and its row sums are at least 2, its eigenvector \(v\) of eigenvalue 1 has both signs (multiply by a strictly positive left Perron vector). On its positive set, every row has a positive outgoing target by \(Av=v\), hence this set contains a directed cycle; likewise for the negative set. A directed cycle cannot lie wholly in one \(v\)-value since every one of its targets would have zero log weight; each cycle has length at least two by absence of loops. Thus there are at least two distinct positive values and two distinct negative values, for at least four nonempty \(v\)-classes altogether.

At least two classes are active: the same Perron argument gives both signs for \(c\); each positive coordinate of \(c\) requires some positive **incoming** parent by \(c^TA=c^T\). Following positive parents yields a directed cycle in \(\operatorname{supp}c\), which as just proved uses at least two \(v\)-values. All these classes are active. If \(n=P+Q-2\), two active classes use at least \(2n\) vertices, and with four classes in all the minimum is \(2n+2\) (a third or fourth active class also costs at least one). This proves (1).

**Limit of the circuit test.** For example if \(15\mid d\) and \(\ell=31\), the six phases \(\zeta_3,\zeta_3^2,\zeta_5,\ldots,\zeta_5^4\) support the single primitive relation with coefficients \((1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1)\), whose sum is even. There is only one relation since \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)=\mathbb Q\) and each set of all nontrivial prime-order powers is a basis of its cyclotomic field. This is not a prime component or an example of a recurring signature; it demonstrates why parity and the phase lower bound alone do not eliminate every single-base case.
