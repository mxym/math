# Integral binary prime rigidity tolerates positive-density deletion

**Claim.** There exists an absolute δ>0 with the following property. Let P be the positive odd primes and let A⊂P have upper relative deletion density limsup_{x→∞} |(P∖A)∩[1,x]|/|P∩[1,x]| < δ. For S⊂P let U(S) be the abelian group with generators [s], s∈S, subject to [a]+[b]=[c]+[d] whenever a+b=c+d in S. The inclusion induces isomorphisms

    U(A∖{3}) → U(P∖{3}) ≅ Z³,
    U(A) → U(P) ≅ Z²                if 3∈A.

In each case the canonical map from the variable prefix U(S∩[1,M]) to U(S) is also an isomorphism for every sufficiently large M (the threshold can depend on S). In particular the same integral affine interpolation holds for every map from A containing 3 to ANY abelian group preserving equal prime-pair sums. No assumption about the first primes 5,7,11 surviving is needed. No assertion of connectivity of unstabilized monomial fibers is made.

This is a robustness consequence of Michael Nelson, [Additive Relations and Betti Growth in Goldbach Rings, arXiv:2611.14580v2, Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14580v2#S6.SS1), combined with a bounded-arity extension argument. Nelson computes the groups for the full prime supports; his explicit cyclic moves use a common auxiliary monomial, which cancels only in the group. We require only his cyclic system at arities 3 and 6, not arbitrarily large arity.

### 1. Dense deletion and the bounded systems

Write λ(n)=(log n)1_{n prime}, λ_D=λ1_D, D=P∖A. For a finite system of integral affine forms ψ₁,…,ψ_t in m variables having fixed pairwise nonproportional nonzero linear parts, consider a convex box or polytope of diameter O(X) on which all values are positive and O(X). The intercepts may be O(X). In the applications below each ψ_j has a coefficient ±1. The loss from prescribing ψ_j in D is O(√δ X^m) for all sufficiently large X, with a constant depending only on the finitely many coefficient systems, t and the scale bounds. To see this use y=ψ_j and complete to integral coordinates (y,u), u∈Z^{m−1}, by replacing a variable with coefficient ±1. Let C_j(y) sum the other t−1 λ factors over its u-section. By Cauchy–Schwarz,

    Σ_y λ_D(y)C_j(y) ≤ (Σ_{y≪X} λ_D(y))^(1/2) (Σ_y λ(y)C_j(y)^2)^(1/2).

The first sum is O(δX) by the density hypothesis and PNT. The second expands into 2t−1 forms in coordinates (y,u,u′) on the convex fiber-square of the original polytope. Within either copy the nonproportionality is unchanged; each non-shared form depends nontrivially on the copy-specific u or u′, so the cross-copy forms are not proportional to one another or to y. The prime linear-forms theorem, applied to this fixed doubled system (λ≤Λ), gives O(X^(2m−1)) uniformly in the intercepts. Indeed its local singular factors are uniformly bounded: for all primes outside a fixed finite set, pairwise independence mod the prime gives β_ℓ=1+O(ℓ^(−2)); the finitely many remaining factors are bounded above trivially. This proves the loss assertion after a union bound over j. The version of Tao–Teräväinen, [Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02158), accommodates the variable intercepts. Any proper prime powers in a lower estimate may be removed at cost O(X^(m−1/2) log^(t+1)X) by fixing the relevant form's value; in mod-6 subboxes the same slicing bound holds.

For these finitely many specific systems the unrestricted lower mass is uniformly ≥c₀ X^m for some c₀>0. For a fixed coefficient matrix, pairwise independence modulo every sufficiently large prime gives β_ℓ=1+O(ℓ^(−2)) uniformly in the shifts. Admissibility at each remaining prime gives a positive lower bound depending only on its size and m. The boxes below all have positive normalized volume. Take δ sufficiently small, using the minimum lower and maximum loss constants over the finite list of systems. Thus a tuple entirely in A exists in every application below. This is an existence theorem for an absolute δ; no numerical threshold is asserted.

**Three-term lift.** For each prescribed p>3 there exist b,c,a∈A∖{3} with p+a=b+c. Take b,c∈[X,2X], X≫p and a=b+c−p. The three linear parts are pairwise nonproportional; admissibility at 2 uses p odd, at 3 one can set b=c≡p (p>3), and at larger primes choose b,c≠0 avoiding b+c≡p. The same construction for p=3 gives both prescribed types b≡c≡5 mod 6 and b≡c≡1 mod 6, a=b+c−3. The unrestricted lower bound in each mod-6 box follows upon substitution b,c=6u+b₀,6v+c₀: at 2 and 3 every form is nonzero by its chosen residue, and away from 6 the substitution is invertible. For the upper deletion loss discard the mod-6 restriction and use the original three-form system.

**Cyclic move.** For two fixed lists p_i,q_i of retained primes >3 with equal length d∈{3,6}, equal sums and equal mod-3 counts, reorder to match residues and use the forms a_i,b_i,r_i from Nelson, Lemma 6.1 (all values in [X,7X] for X large). His explicit residue choices give local admissibility uniformly for every allowable list, and the nonzero 0/1 linear parts are pairwise distinct and nonproportional. The preceding loss estimate supplies a successful tuple in A. Nelson's two sequences of binary moves now take place in A, so Σ[p_i]=Σ[q_i] in U(A∖{3}) after cancellation of the common auxiliary classes.

### 2. Lifting deleted primes into the retained group

For any p>3, use a three-term lift and define g(p)=[b]+[c]−[a] in U(A∖{3}). Any two choices agree: b+c+a′=b′+c′+a is a three-against-three relation with the same residue counts modulo 3. Indeed in any equal-pair-sum identity among primes >3 those counts agree: a pair can contain 0,1 or 2 entries of residue 1 mod 3, and its residue distinguishes the three possibilities. Thus use the retained d=3 cyclic move. For p∈A the original pair relation gives g(p)=[p].

For any p₁+p₂=p₃+p₄ among the full primes >3, expand their four lifts and clear the negative entries. This gives six retained terms on each side, with equal sums and equal mod-3 counts (by the previous observation in the original relation and each lift). The d=6 move shows g(p₁)+g(p₂)=g(p₃)+g(p₄). Therefore g extends to a group homomorphism U(P∖{3})→U(A∖{3}). It is canonically inverse to inclusion: one composite fixes each retained generator, and the other fixes each full generator by its lifting identity in the full group. This proves the first isomorphism.

If 3 is retained, identify the rank-three group via Nelson's full-prime formula: write A₅=[5], B₇=[7], D₆=[11]−[5] as abstract elements of it, and [5+6j]=A₅+jD₆, [7+6j]=B₇+jD₆ when the argument is prime. The notation is legitimate even if these small primes were erased. Adjoin e=[3]. The two retained p=3 lift types give e=2A₅−B₇ and e=2B₇−A₅−D₆, by comparing residue classes and total weights. Hence D₆=3(B₇−A₅). The group is generated by A₅ and B₇−A₅. Their (degree,weight) pairs (1,5),(0,2) are independent, so no further relations exist. Inclusion takes those two to the identical basis of the full odd-prime group.

### 3. Stabilization on every sufficiently large variable prefix

For each sufficiently large odd prime p∈A take b,c∈[2p/3,3p/4], a=b+c−p∈[p/3,p/2]. The same three-form lower bound and dense-deletion estimate (now X=p, coefficient matrix fixed, intercept −p) furnish a triple in A entirely smaller than p; for the >3 support these values also exceed 3. Thus every sufficiently late individual prime extension of the prefix induces a surjection of groups. Starting with a sufficiently large fixed prefix gives a sequence of surjections from a single finitely generated abelian group. Their kernels form an ascending chain, so stabilize; their union is the kernel to the direct limit U(S). Hence all late maps to U(S) are isomorphisms. This concerns variable-prefix relations (both pairs supported in the prefix), not the stricter bound on the weight of the relation.

### 4. Why this still cannot give binary positivity

For every ε>0, take a large fixed modulus W=2∏_{3≤ℓ≤w, ℓ prime}ℓ. Among its reduced classes R, remove one member of every orbit of r↔2−r in R∩(2−R), including fixed points. Remove also the class −1, and remove the finitely many primes dividing W except 3; retain 3. Outside the primes dividing W take all ordinary primes in the remaining classes. Then no even n≡2 mod W with n>6 is a sum of two retained primes: a large-large pair would lie in the removed matching, and 3 paired with another prime would require residue −1. The relative deletion density is at most

    |R∩(2−R)|/φ(W)+1/φ(W)
       = ∏_{3≤ℓ≤w, ℓ prime}(1−1/(ℓ−1)) + 1/φ(W) → 0.

So choose w large enough for deletion below min(ε,δ). This one subset of true primes has an entire progression of binary holes, the full integral rank-two group at all late prefixes, and, if desired after further shrinking δ by an absolute amount, represents every sufficiently large odd number as a sum of three primes. The last assertion follows by the same lower-mass and deletion argument for b,c,n−b−c in boxes b,c∈[n/4,n/3] with odd n (local choices at 2,3 and every larger prime exist; at 3 if n≡0, take all summands 1). This is not a counterexample to Goldbach for the unthinned primes. High-density true-prime subsets omitting a positive proportion of binary sums are already known from Alsetri–Shao, [Density versions of the binary Goldbach problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18576); the use here is to compare this phenomenon to **exact integral stabilization**, not to claim the underlying deletion obstruction is new.

**Conventions and source check, 21 November 2026.** If 2 is also retained, it contributes a separate direct summand Z[2] to every relevant group, finite or infinite. A sum 2+p with odd prime p has the unique unordered prime pair of that odd weight, and 2+2 has no other representation, so no nontrivial relation involves 2. If 2 is deleted, no such summand is present. Thus the two exceptional labels 2 and 3 have different roles. All inverse homomorphisms and all kernel stabilizations above are over Z, not just over Q; in particular there is no residual finite-index or torsion ambiguity. Nelson's [arXiv submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14580) lists Michael Nelson, v1 November 10 and current v2 November 16, as a preprint (no journal-reference field). The [v1 §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14580v1#S6.SS1) already contains the full-support cyclic lemma and the Z³/Z² theorem; v2 expands the admissibility/counting proof but does not claim the dense-deletion consequence. The aiXiv entry is an index of the same work. Tao–Teräväinen is a published input (JEMS, [doi:10.4171/JEMS/1404](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/1404)); [their Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.02158v4#S1.SS1) explicitly permits coefficient norms bounded by fixed L and affine constants bounded by L times the common scale, with error uniform in the constants. In the doubled system the unimodular chart and all new linear coefficients depend only on the finite matrices; intercepts stay O(X). Finite-center and varying-prefix uses meet the same bound, by taking X larger than every offset in the former case and X=p in the latter. Neither an explicit stabilization threshold nor a theorem on the complete binary support is asserted.
