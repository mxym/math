# Six fixed-seed forward labels must be terminal at large F-rich centers; near-smooth orbits need at least seven

Fix an odd prime p, finite set F and ε>0. At each sufficiently large even N coprime to p with N_F≥N^(1/2+ε), start at p and add all prime factors of every reached complement N−x. Suppose all the complements are composite and there are exactly six distinct reached labels. **Then all six lie in one strongly connected component.** In particular an unbounded sequence with log(N/N_F)=o(log N) cannot have such six-label orbits, by [the near-smooth six-terminal exclusion](PrimeOrbitSixNearSmoothTerminalExclusion.md). Together with [the at-least-six bound](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md), all-composite forward orbits of any fixed odd seed eventually contain **at least seven** labels along every such near-smooth sequence. This is necessary at a hypothetical Goldbach failure with p∤N; it supplies neither an upper bound on orbit size nor a Goldbach representation.

The first theorem uses only the stated F-rich threshold, not near-smoothness. Under the [six-label fork](PrimeOrbitSixLabelFork.md), a counterexample sequence not already terminal has a source unit-multiplicity two-cycle p,Q feeding a four-label terminal component with ordered labels r<s<R<J. The small labels are p,r,s with r,s→∞ and log r,log s=o(log N); the macros are Q,R,J with all three weights positive along a convergent subsequence. All six inputs are o(N), the full nullity of I−A is two and the terminal block's nullity is one. The fixed seed p is not in the terminal component. This structure is the only arithmetic use of the F-rich assumption here.

Use the stochastic row matrices P,W of [the three-macro no-high argument](PrimeOrbitThreeMacroNoHighExclusion.md): W is the diagonal matrix of weights and P_ij is the subsequential limit of A_ij log x_j/log N. All complementary factors within one fixed macro column have bounded integer exponents, fixed on the subsequence; hence an arc to a macro carries positive mass whenever it is present. All stochastic small rows have disjoint supports by the subpower-difference gcd bound, and rank(P−W)≤4. In this *nonterminal* situation the small rows have zero p coordinate exactly: the p row is loopless and no terminal row can use p. Thus the input in that no-high proof for which near-smoothness was needed there is automatic here. Also [the four-terminal double-high obstruction](PrimeOrbitSixSmoothTerminal.md), §1, rules out simultaneous weight one at R,J under these fork hypotheses: that section uses mutual-pair arithmetic, disjoint supports and Bożek's two-label theorem, not a fixed factorization of N. We do not invoke the F-smooth assumption in the later sections of that paper.

## Without a small-direct singleton macro

Here is why the reducibility of A does not obstruct the no-singleton argument. In the at-most-one-dimensional quotient of row(P−W) by the three small row blocks, any unused macro coordinate gives a nonzero functional since its own row is strictly negative there. Two unused macros give opposite functionals, forcing every outside macro row to vanish on both since it has nonnegative entries there. No small row reaches them either, and a zero entry of P at a macro is absence of that actual arc. This contradicts reachability from p: the unused pair consists of macros and does not contain the small seed p. This replaces strong connectivity, not the assertion that every strongly connected component is reached from every vertex. As before, two macros in one block give opposite quotient orientations on their own rows by taking the functional comparing the two coordinates divided by their block masses. Three in one block are impossible; if a macro is unused no pair can occupy one block. With no singleton macro the two remaining covered macros would then consume the two available small columns in distinct blocks, leaving the third block empty. Thus all macros are covered, and the only pattern is {t}, {s',A'}, {B,C}, where s',t are the two small non-p labels. In particular all three macro weights are strictly below one because each occurs with positive mass alongside another entry of its small block.

The last two sections of the [no-high proof](PrimeOrbitThreeMacroNoHighExclusion.md) now apply with no irreducibility hypothesis: F_3 has rank three by the one- and two-macro rows, the macro-free pure row fixes the full left 1-eigenplane via the bounded multipliers λ_h, and the first-order congruence forces N−K=v B C at the two-macro small input K. The only sign premise needed for that argument is that each nonzero left 1-eigenrow h has mixed signs: this holds even for the reducible full matrix, since for a nonzero nonnegative h, hA1≥2h1>h1 would contradict hA=h. Each sign has a same-sign predecessor and hence a loopless cycle, so support size is at least four as required. All other hypotheses in that congruence contradiction hold: the two non-p smalls diverge, q-type labels are units of N, and the three macro weights are below one. Concretely it fixes a row killing A', replaces 1/B and 1/C modulo N using vBC=N−K, deduces a power-close rational B/C, excludes a fixed K and ultimately contradicts the growing K-adic valuation of v^(h_B+h_C)=p^(2h_p) K^(2h_K) (−h_B/h_C)^(h_B−h_C). Thus the no-singleton pattern cannot occur.

## With a small-direct singleton

A small singleton P_t=e_X gives N−t=a X^d with a subpower and d=1/w_X fixed. Since N−X divides aN^d−N+t, its gcd with any small complement N−u divides the positive subpower integer a u^d−u+t. Consequently P_X is disjoint from the small rows and together with them forms a four-block basis for row(P−W). Every macro must have a singleton block in this basis, by the unique negative coordinate of its own adjusted row. This argument involves neither full irreducibility nor a strict upper weight bound. There are two or three direct singleton macro parents among the small rows.

If exactly two macros X,Y have direct small parents s_X,s_Y, the fourth basis row is e_H for the third macro. Both P_X and P_Y equal e_H since each is disjoint from every small row and in the four-row span. There is one small-only input t distinct from s_X,s_Y. We have

    N−s_X=a_X X^{d_X},     N−s_Y=a_Y Y^{d_Y},
    N−X=u H^f,             N−Y=v H^f,

with all coefficients subpower, and the common f exactly 1/w_H since each of the latter two stochastic rows is the singleton e_H.

* If X,Y both have weight below one, their complements share H^f of weight one but their gcd divides |X−Y| of weight below one, impossible.
* If just X of these two direct macros has weight one, d_X=1 and a_X u H^f=(a_X−1)N+s_X. Substituting v H^f=N−Y gives

      a_X u Y+v s_X=[a_X u−v(a_X−1)]N.

  Its positive left side is o(N), impossible for an integer multiple of N. This is valid for every f, including when H is intermediate. No transfer of a strong-connectivity-only resultant theorem is required.
* If X,Y both have weight one and H is intermediate, neither can divide N−H. For example N−H=z X with z subpower would give a_X H−z s_X=(a_X−z)N, whose left side is positive (H has fixed positive weight) and o(N). Thus P_H is supported only on smalls and belongs to the basis span because e_H does. It is forced to equal the only small-only block P_t. But N−H divides F(N) with F(T)=a_X u T^f−(a_X−1)T−s_X, so gcd(N−H,N−t) divides F(t), nonzero since F(t)≡−s_X mod t and t≠s_X, and of subpower size. This contradicts P_H=P_t.
* The only case left would have all three macros high, contrary to the terminal double-high obstruction for R,J.

Finally suppose all three direct singleton parents occur. The source p must directly parent the source macro Q: its actual arc of multiplicity one has positive mass, while neither terminal small can use Q. Therefore r,s directly parent R,J in some order, with N−s_i=v_i X_i^{d_i}, v_i subpower and d_i=1/w_{X_i}. The polynomial argument above makes P_R,P_J disjoint from all three macro singleton blocks. Hence the terminal macro complements actually use no macros (a positive-weight target cannot occur with zero mass) and factor only over r,s by closedness. Each v_i likewise uses only terminal smalls.

We now take first returns on r,s *inside that four-vertex terminal component*, not in the reducible full matrix. The resulting 2×2 nonnegative integral matrix K is irreducible, has row sums at least two (replacing each occurrence of a macro by its entire complementary monomial preserves or increases arity), and nullity(I−K)=1 by eliminating the two macro coordinates in the terminal fixed-vector equations. Both off-diagonal entries are positive. Thus K−I has rank one and its two diagonal entries have the same nonzero sign. They cannot both be negative, since each would equal −1, forcing the product of the off-diagonal entries to be one and K to be a permutation with row sums one. Hence K−I=a b^T with a,b positive integers and gcd(a_1,a_2)=1 by taking the primitive direction of the columns. Put Z=r^{b_1}s^{b_2}>1.

As in the terminal proof, actual first-return monomials give s_i Z^{a_i}=v_i(N−X_i)^{d_i}. Modulo N this implies Z^{a_i}≡(−1)^(d_i+1), so Z²≡1 mod N and Z≥sqrt(N+1). Real sizes imply a_i log Z=d_i log N+o(log N); fix the bounded positive a_i, then a_i=d_i/gcd(d_1,d_2). Taking the s_i valuation in the identity gives a_i|1 because a_i|d_i and s_i∤v_i. Hence a_1=a_2=1 and d_1=d_2=d. If d=1 both R,J have weight one, already impossible. If d≥2, the rationals v_i/s_i=Z/(N−X_i)^d have subpower height and agree to relative O(N^(−η)) for some η>0 since X_i have weight 1/d. Clearing subpower numerators and denominators gives exact equality, impossible because their reduced denominators are the distinct growing primes r,s. This exhausts all cases and proves the assertions.

The terminal double-high input used only for this known 2+4 fork does not imply the absence of all four-label terminal components in arbitrary settings. The conclusion for full forward-orbit size seven is near-smooth only: at general F-rich centers a genuinely six-label strongly connected orbit has not been ruled out by this transfer.
