# Square-root sparsity of all five-label exceptional autonomous components

**Theorem.** At most X^{1/2+o(1)} even numbers N≤X possess a five-label [Bożek exceptional autonomous component](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900), without restrictions on the labels, factor multiplicities or ranks. The new case has both A and B=I−A singular, where A_{ij}=v_{p_j}(N−p_i). No five-label signature enumeration or asymptotic size ordering beyond the least two labels is required. This counts centers supporting an EAC, not the Goldbach exceptional set. It does not prove that five-label EACs are impossible.

The labels are distinct odd primes coprime to N. The component is strongly connected in the orientation from each label to the factors of its composite complement, with every such factor in the component. Thus A is irreducible, nonnegative, loopless, with each row sum at least two. Each label is below N/3 since it divides an odd composite row less than N. The two smallest labels m₁<m₂ both lie below √N: the m₁ row is composite and does not use m₁.

### Reduction to fixed corank-one signatures

The cases of invertible A and invertible B are covered by the [Jacobian](EACJacobianSquareRoot.md) and [order-divisor](EACNonsingularSquareRoot.md) estimates. If both are singular, B has corank one. Indeed, were 1 a repeated eigenvalue of A, its five eigenvalues would be 0,1,1,ρ,−ρ−2 by trace zero, impossible since the Perron spectral radius ρ is at least two.

Fix any constant η>0. Cases with m₁≤N^η have m₁m₂≤X^{1/2+η}, so the [supported-minor lemma](EACSupportedMinorFive.md) bounds their centers by X^{1/2+η+o(1)}. Otherwise all row sums are bounded by 1/η. There are now finitely many matrices A, depending only on η. For each fixed one we will bound its centers by X^{1/2+o(1)}. All integer coefficient bounds and polynomial degrees below may depend on that fixed signature. This separation is essential: no uniform elimination bound in unbounded signatures is needed.

Let v span ker B and c span ker Bᵀ, primitive integral vectors. Both have at least two positive and at least two negative entries. For example Av=v: a one-signed nonzero eigenvector for an irreducible nonnegative matrix would have the Perron eigenvalue, not 1. At each positive entry some positive entry contributes, so its positive support contains a directed cycle, of length at least two since the diagonal is zero. Likewise for the negative support. Apply the same argument to Aᵀ, which is irreducible with the same spectral radius (no assumption on its row sums is needed).

### An augmented torus criterion, valid for any fixed size at corank one

Write x^u=∏x_j^{u_j}. The physical tuple of units modulo N satisfies p^{B_i}=−1 for each row B_i. In fact as a rational number p^{B_i}=p_i/(N−p_i). The identity cᵀB=0 yields ∏((N−p_i)/p_i)^{c_i}=1. Reduction modulo N gives (−1)^{Σc_i}=1 (N>2), hence

    ∏(1−N/p_i)^{c_i}=1,             Σ_i c_i/p_i ≡ 0  (mod N).

The second identity follows by expanding the first modulo N² in the ring of fractions with denominators coprime to N. Negative integer powers cause no issue for these units.

Suppose some value v_i occurs **exactly once on the support of c**. In the characteristic-zero Laurent torus consider the finitely presented rational algebra defined by x^{B_i}=−1 together with g=Σ c_i/x_i=0. It is finite-dimensional. Indeed a Smith normal form of the integer exponent matrix (row operations on monomial equations and invertible monomial changes of variables) transforms the first equations into powers of k−1 coordinates equal to signs and a remaining consistency sign. If inconsistent the algebra is zero. Otherwise over the algebraic closure these powers split simply, giving a finite disjoint union of copies of the one-dimensional torus. In the original coordinates each is x_j=ζ_j t^{v_j}, possibly after t↦1/t, for nonzero constants ζ_j and a primitive parametrization; this follows because the last monomial direction is a primitive integer generator of ker B. On each copy the unique exponent in g contributes one nonzero term that cannot cancel. Quotienting the Laurent polynomial algebra in t by a nonzero Laurent polynomial makes it finite-dimensional (possibly zero). Therefore the original rational algebra was finite-dimensional as well.

For each coordinate j there is thus an actual ideal identity eliminating that coordinate: a nonzero F_j(T) in Z[T] and constant C_j≠0 in Z have C_j F_j(x_j) in the Z-Laurent ideal of the displayed equations. To see this, apply Cayley–Hamilton to multiplication by x_j in the finite rational quotient, or use polynomial 1 in the zero quotient, and clear the finitely many rational denominators in an ideal expression. Evaluation at the physical tuple modulo composite N is valid for all Laurent monomials, so N divides C_j F_j(p_j), not just a statement about prime divisors of N.

Apply this to a least label (a finite choice of index) p_j=m₁≤√X. If F_j(m₁)≠0, there are X^{o(1)} divisors of |C_jF_j(m₁)|≤(2+X)^{O_A(1)} per m₁, by the elementary divisor bound. If m₁ is one of the finitely many integer roots, then m₁>N^η bounds N in this part of the argument. Alternatively fixing such m₁ gives the same square-root count by the supported-minor lemma applied to m₁,m₂. This concludes the unique-value case.

### Two arithmetic counting devices for mutual pairs

At fixed corank-one B, for any two chosen labels x,y the rational row space of B contains a nonzero vector supported on them, since it is v-perpendicular and of codimension one. Clearing denominators puts some such vector (a,b) in the integral row lattice. Hence x^a y^b≡±1 modulo N with (a,b)≠(0,0) fixed by the signature and indices. For xy≤√N this already gives the square-root count: there are O(√X log(2X)) ordered pairs by product, then X^{o(1)} possible divisors of the nonzero cleared binomial from x^{2a}y^{2b}≡1. Nonvanishing follows from unique factorization of the distinct primes.

If x<y form a mutual pair then N=x+y+hxy for a positive even integer h (reciprocity, primality and compositeness). Two further cases each give a square-root count:

1. If y≥√N, then xh<N/y≤√N. Also y≡−x/(1+hx) modulo N, with 1+hx invertible since N=x+y(1+hx). The fixed row-space relation gives x^{2(a+b)}(1+hx)^{−2b}≡1. Clearing positive and negative exponents yields a nonzero integer divisible by N, of size at most (2+X)^{O_A(1)}. Indeed x and 1+hx are coprime and both exceed one, and a+b,b do not both vanish. Enumerate x,h by their product and then divisors.
2. Suppose the pair is **mutually squared**, meaning y² divides N−x and x² divides N−y. For xy≤√N the preceding two-label count applies. If xy>√N, then h<√N. Now y divides 1+hx and x divides 1+hy. Consequently xy divides h(x+y)+1. The positive integer d=(h(x+y)+1)/(xy) satisfies xd=h(1+x/y)+1/y≤2h+1. Also x+y divides dx²+1, by reducing dxy=h(x+y)+1 modulo x+y. Enumerate x,d with product ≤2√X+1, then the divisors x+y of dx²+1. These choices recover y, then h and N uniquely. This last large-product count requires no signature bound at all.

### Complete sign-support exhaustion at five labels

We may now assume that no nonzero c-coordinate has a unique v-value on its support, and that the two arithmetic counting devices do not apply. If v has no zero, its sign supports have sizes two and three. In the size-two group both entries of c must be nonzero: c has at most one zero, and otherwise the remaining entry is the only one of its sign of v on the support. The two entries of v on that group must be equal. Negate v if needed so their common value is positive. In the row of either of these two vertices the only possible positive output is the other vertex (no loop), so its coefficient is at least one. If it were exactly one, no negative output would be possible in Av=v, giving row sum one since there are no zero v-entries. Thus the two labels form a mutually squared pair. This case is covered above.

Otherwise v has exactly one zero, corresponding to a label z; its other signs split into two pairs. If c_z≠0, the value zero is unique on its support and the augmented torus criterion applies. Else c is nonzero on all four other labels, and the two entries of v must be equal on each sign pair, to avoid a unique value. Each pair is therefore mutual, by its two eigenvector equations. If z≤√N, the rational row space v-perpendicular contains the vector e_z, so a nonzero integer multiple lies in the integral row lattice. Thus N divides z^{2e}−1 for a fixed positive e, giving the square-root count. Assume z>√N. If any of the remaining four labels is at least √N, its mutual pair is covered by device 1. Otherwise all four are below z. Unless one pair is mutually squared, each pair has a row with mate coefficient exactly one. Comparing its eigenvector equation, the mate already contributes the entire input value, so the row contains no opposite-sign factor. With no loop and row sum at least two it must contain z. The two rows chosen from the distinct pairs thus have different input labels u,w, both below z, while z divides both N−u and N−w. This is impossible. Every case has been covered.

Summing over the finitely many fixed signatures for each η, and combining with the X^{1/2+η+o(1)} small-minimum branch, yields X^{1/2+o(1)} by taking arbitrarily small fixed η. In conjunction with the earlier bounds for sizes at most four this also bounds all centers admitting an EAC with at most five labels. The specialized [two-thirds product gate](EACFiveTropical.md), about the sizes m₁m₂ themselves, is a separate statement and is not a prerequisite.

## Comparison and scope

[Eisenbud–Sturmfels](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9401001) give the general Laurent-binomial geometry underlying the integer Smith parametrization. [Kelanemer's August 2026 record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460) discusses related spectral and exponent-matrix restrictions under its proposed counterexample argument; the full paper was unavailable for audit. The bound here is about actual components at all even centers, not the number of missing additive representations, and does not assert completeness of any prior Goldbach argument.
