# A signed Dickman rectangle law for the two genuine least factors at a fixed Goldbach target

Let $P$ run over even integers, $I=[P/3,2P/3]$, $L=\log P$, $X_P=|I|\mathfrak S(P)$ with the usual binary Goldbach singular series, and $G(P)$ the central true prime-pair count weighted by the two logarithms. Put $\langle B,C\rangle=\sum_{x\in I,(x,P)=1}B(x)C(P-x)$. Use **the same physical score $J=J_2$** as in the [common-sign construction](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/74d3b0e858f37c871ca0ba6d7b732d0bebaf06b9), not a new cutoff slope; $G=X_P+\langle J,J\rangle+o_*(X_P)$ and $J$ vanishes on true primes. Here $E=o_*(1)$ means $\lim_{\theta\downarrow0}\limsup_{P\to\infty,\ P\ {\rm even}}|E|=0$. First fix the parameter $\theta$ of the score; the large-target threshold may depend on it.

Put $W=P^{\theta^2}$ and $v(n)=\theta L/\log P^-(n)$ on the support of $J$. Extend Dickman's $\rho$ by zero on negative inputs. If $A=[a,b)$ and $B=[c,d)$ are fixed compactly inside $(1,\infty)$, then
$$
 \frac{\langle J1_{v\in A},J1_{v\in B}\rangle}{X_P}
 =9e^{-2\gamma}\int_a^b\rho(u-1)\,du\int_c^d\rho(u-1)\,du+o_*(1).       \tag{1}
$$
Every such nondegenerate rectangle has strictly positive actual signed mass for sufficiently small fixed $\theta$ and all sufficiently large prescribed targets. Endpoint conventions do not affect the limit. On the full two-way partition into $\mathcal L=\{W<P^-\le P^{2\theta}\}$ and $\mathcal H=\{P^->P^{2\theta}\}$, the ordered correlation matrix divided by $X_P$ is
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}9&-9\\-9&G(P)/X_P+8\end{pmatrix}+o_*(1)                  \tag{2}
$$
entrywise. These are signed sums, not probabilities; we do not claim weak convergence against arbitrary bounded tests. The positive reserve and its two negative companions cancel in the total. In particular (2) leaves the true Goldbach atom unknown.

## 1. Independent physical roughness cuts

Set $g(t)=(1-2t)_+^2$, $D=P^{1/2-\theta}$, and $\phi(t)=1-t$ for $0\le t\le\theta$, $g(t)$ for $\theta<t\le1/2-\theta$, and zero afterwards. Define
$$
 F_s(n)=L1_{P^-(n)>P^s}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\phi(\log d/L),\quad
 C_s={e^{-\gamma}\over s}\int_{[0,\infty)}\phi(su)\,d\nu(u),\quad
 d\nu=\delta_0+\rho'(u)\,du .                                             \tag{3}
$$
Take $s=w=\theta^2$ or $s=c_0\theta$ for any finite set of fixed $c_0>0$. For small $\theta$, $w\le s$. Write $\Pi(n)=L1_{n\ {\rm prime}}$. The normalized patched score, before rank correction, at roughness $P^s$ is $K_s=\Pi-F_s$. This changes $\log n$ to $L$ and the argument of $g$ from $\log d/\log n$ to $\log d/L$ but keeps both physical divisor cuts $P^\theta,D$ exactly. On rough inputs the change is $O_\theta(1)$, by Lipschitz continuity inside each fixed piece and bounded divisor count at fixed $\theta$; the dimension-two upper sieve counts both rough inputs by $O_\theta(X_P/L^2)$. Squareful rough inputs (including proper powers) or target nonunits on the rough physical support cost $P^{1-\delta_\theta+o(1)}$, marking a prime square $>W^2$ or a prime divisor of $P$ above $W$. These replacements cost $o_{P;\theta}(X_P)$ even with a prime on the other side.

**Joint divisor evaluation.** For all allowable $i,j$,
$$ \langle F_i,F_j\rangle/X_P=C_i C_j+o_*(1).                            \tag{4} $$
Here are the beyond-root arithmetic details. Smooth $I$ with monotone end transitions of width $P/L^K$; restoration once on the actual sum uses Cauchy–Schwarz and the fourth divisor moment, giving $O_A(P/L^A)$ on choosing $K$. Open $d,e\le D$ respectively $P^i$- and $P^j$-rough with $(de,P)=1$ (other physical nonunits negligible as above); $(d,e)=1$ is necessary. On their progression sieve the union $p\mid x,\ p\le P^i$ or $p\mid P-x,\ p\le P^j$, using dimension-two combinatorial upper/lower beta weights independent of the local densities at level $E=P^{.001}$. Their Euler-model error relative to the sifted product is $O(u^{-u/2})$, $u=.001/\max(i,j)$ sufficiently large, by the [fundamental lemma, Theorem 2.2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/superscars-for-arithmetic-point-scatters-ii/3CCB969E058792C1803466AE0EF29136). On the progression the two excluded residues are distinct unless $p\mid P$; then they coincide. If $p\mid de$ a test on the opposite side is impossible, and such a prime exceeds $P^{\min(i,j)}$. The dimension-two Euler bound is uniform; at 2 the target is even.

For the true signed $c_{d,e}=\mu(d)\phi(\log d/L)\mu(e)\phi(\log e/L)$ compare the signed upper estimate to the true total using the **unsigned** upper-minus-lower sandwich. Both $c_{d,e}$ and $|c_{d,e}|$ factor, not their individual discrepancies. For each squarefree beta label $m\le E$ assign its primes to eligible sides; if $p\mid P$ assign left only since the residues coincide (also when just the right condition is tested). Extended moduli are $b=d m_1,c=e m_2$ with $(c,P)=1$, $m_1m_2=m$. Assigned primes do not divide the original divisor on their side by roughness or the target-unit mask. Cross collisions give incompatible pairs; $(b,c)=1$ is precisely compatibility. Coefficients in $b,c$ are independent for each fixed allocation.

Apply the [signed CRT estimate for polynomial sieve allocations](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/PanCriticalCompositePolynomialSieve.md), specifically its discrepancy argument, not its final Pan coefficient formula. This applies to arbitrary independent bounded sequences in $b,c\le 2P^{.501}$ with the stated coprimality. The exact zero mode is $P\widehat V(0)/(bc)$ for the bump $V(x/P)$. The phase is $e(\pm Ph\bar b/c)$; [Bettin–Chandee, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00769) retains $(1+PF/(BC))^{1/2}$ for frequency length $F$ and modulus lengths $B,C$. Mellin separation of $\widehat V(\pm Ph/(bc))$ absorbs large relative frequencies as in [equations (4)–(5) here](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/theory-2026-11-20/strong-goldbach/PanSupercriticalShoulder.md). On $BC\ge P$ the two relative exponents are at most $(33\cdot.501-17)/20$ and $(15\cdot.501-8)/8$, both $<-.022$. For $BC<P^{.99}$ the $O(1)$ discrepancy per pair costs $O(P^{.99})$; the intervening range uses the cited $F=1$ endpoint plus Fourier decay for $BC/P<1$. Per-allocation error $O(P^{1-.007})$ absorbs polylogs; all allocations cost $\sum_{m\le E}2^{\omega(m)}\ll E\log P$.

Reassembling zero modes gives unsieved length $|I|/(de)$ and the local conditional densities above. Their Euler product is uniformly $(1+o_{P;\theta}(1))e^{-2\gamma}\mathfrak S(P)/(ijL^2)$: primes up to the smaller cutoff give the usual joint correction to the two Mertens products; above it only one side is tested. Omitted singular factors and $d,e$ corrections cost $1+o_{P;\theta}(1)$ by reciprocal squares and $\sum_{p\mid Pde,\ p>P^{\min(i,j)}}1/p=o_{P;\theta}(1)$. The two signed harmonic sums separate: imposing coprimality and target units costs $o_{P;\theta}(1)$ by marking a common prime (reciprocal square) or a large prime of $P$. The absolute rough harmonic sums are $O(1/i),O(1/j)$ by the Euler upper bound. The signed sums are $\int\phi(iu)d\nu+o_{P;\theta}(1)$ and its $j$ version by finite squarefree prime-simplex expansion and Mertens; the cumulative at $u$ is $\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k/k!\int_{t_h\ge1,\ \sum t_h\le u}\prod dt_h/t_h=\rho(u)$ by the delay equation, including the $d=1$ atom. Thus the sieve cost relative to $X_P$ is $O((ij)^{-2}u^{-u/2})=o_{\theta\downarrow0}(1)$, proving (4).

## 2. The actual reflected prime and the critical quadratic shoulder

We claim
$$ \langle\Pi,F_s\rangle/X_P=C_s+o_*(1).                                  \tag{5} $$
For $s=w$ use the physical $D$ and sieve level $P^{\theta/2}$; for $s=c_0\theta$ put $H=\log(1/\theta)$, shorten $d\le D'=P^{.5-H\theta}$ and use level $P^{H\theta/2}$. Centered maximal Bombieri–Vinogradov for the actual prime applies since the product of the levels is below $P^{.5}$ by a fixed power for each $\theta$. Weighting by fixed divisor powers costs only logs via Cauchy–Schwarz with elementary progression bounds, paid by arbitrary log saving in the power gap. See [the detailed source transfer, §§1,3](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/bf13a6e1d1a9b35bdea274481e7a9c4e2d26a1b8). More explicitly, $d$ rough is coprime to the sieve label, target primes are omitted from reduced-class prime progressions, and at $p\nmid P$ one residue out of $p-1$ reduced residues is forbidden. With Mertens, $L V_P(P^s)/\mathfrak S(P)=(1+o(1))e^{-\gamma}/s$, where $V_P=\prod_{p\le P^s,p\nmid P}(1-1/(p-1))$. The $d$ sum has denominator $\varphi(d)$, interchangeable with $d$ since $d$ is $P^s$-rough. Choose the sieve sign separately for each sign of the coefficient: its absolute normalized full-budget error is $O(s^{-2}u^{-u/2})$, $u=1/(2\theta)$ or $H/(2c_0)$ respectively. Both costs tend to zero. The model $D'$ truncation changes $C_s$ by $O(s^{-1}\rho((.5-H\theta)/s))=o(1)$.

The actual omitted shoulder is not bounded by that signed model tail. There $|\phi|\ll H^2\theta^2$ and
$$ \limsup_{P\to\infty}\sum_{D'<d\le D,\ P^-(d)>P^s}1/d\ll_{c_0} H .      \tag{6} $$
Indeed the rough harmonic measure on a fixed log-scale interval $u=\log d/(sL)>1$ tends to $\omega(u)\,du$, $\omega\le1$, by the prime-factor expansion (or Buchstab); the interval length is $\le H/c_0$. For each target-unit such $d$, count $b$ with $P^-(b)>P^s$ and $P-db$ prime. On length $\asymp P/d$ upper-sieve both forms through $P^s$ at level $P^{.08}$ and, independently, the prime form alone on $P^s<p\le P^{.02}$ at level $P^{.08}$ (take $\theta$ small). The product of these disjoint-bank upper sums is nonnegative pointwise. CRT floors cost $P^{.16+o(1)}$ per $d$: at most $2^{\omega(m)}$ residues per squarefree label. In the Euler product, primes of $d$ have factors $1+O(1/p)$ relative to the generic upper; the product is $1+o_{P;\theta}(1)$ by $d$-roughness. Target primes give the usual singular upper factor. Thus the count is $\ll\mathfrak S(P)P/(d\,s L^2)+O(P^{.16+o(1)})$, with an absolute constant for small $s$ (the second sieve has fixed parameter $4$; a standard upper linear sieve suffices). On summing (6) the total normalized shoulder is $O_{c_0}(H^3\theta)$, proving (5).

## 3. Transfer to the published, rank-corrected score on each band

Up to the negligible normalizations of §1, $J=K_w+S$, where $S$ is supported on genuine squarefree semiprimes $x=h p$, $W<h<p$ primes. On the lower-$h$ patch $h\le P^\theta$ we have $|S|\ll L\log h/L$; if $D<h\le\sqrt{2P/3}$ then $|S|\ll L\theta^2$. Otherwise there is no correction. Indeed among proper nontrivial divisors only $h<\sqrt{x}$ can have nonzero ideal $g$; the ideal and patched coefficients agree away from those two intervals. These comparisons use the own logarithm and fixed physical cuts; replacing it by $L$ in the smooth factors costs only the error already paid. Let $a_h=\log h/L$ or $\theta^2$ on these two respective bands and zero elsewhere. Then $\limsup_P\sum_{h\ {\rm prime}}a_h/h\ll\theta$ by Mertens, and $|I|^{-1}\sum_{x\in I}|S(x)|=O(\theta)+o_{P;\theta}(1)$ by counting the terminal prime. Multiplying $S$ by $1_{P^->P^t}$ is just an independent $h$ cut. Denote any such restricted source by $S_t$.

In (5) with either sign-part of $S_t$ as source **instead of** $\Pi$, the interior BV step gives the same signed short multiplier times the ordinary source mean, with error $O(s^{-2}u^{-u/2})$ times its absolute mean, plus $o_{P;\theta}(1)$. This is the genuine finite-rank source transfer of the linked §§1,3, not BV for arbitrary constrained coefficients. Both actual prime slots $h,p$ have fixed polynomial lower length; on independent fine boxes the ordering, central interval, patch thresholds and extra $h$ cuts are finite product/ratio boundaries, and each sign-part of the log/polynomial score has bounded piecewise variation. Centered convolution BV in any fixed sub-root range follows from the two-slot large sieve and small-conductor Siegel–Walfisz for primes, with boundary boxes handled by the positive source as in that proof. The principal has the original reduced-class exclusion $(x,d m)=1$ (sieve label $m$); all primes of $x$ exceed $W$, and deleting those primes in either sieve or harmonic sums changes them by $o_{P;\theta}(1)$ uniformly. The truncated signed multiplier is bounded as $\theta\downarrow0$, by §4. Likewise $\langle\Pi,|S|\rangle/X_P=o_*(1)$ by that source distribution followed by a fixed-small-power upper sieve for the reflected prime. The absolute $\langle|S|,|S|\rangle/X_P=o_*(1)$ follows already from the [bounded-rank replacement proof, final paragraph](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/74d3b0e858f37c871ca0ba6d7b732d0bebaf06b9); hence it holds after arbitrary further restrictions.

For $s=c_0\theta$ it remains to pay the actual $D'<d\le D$ shoulder against $|S_t|$, including $h$ near $\sqrt P$ where elementary floors for individual $h,d$ would not suffice. Upper bound by opening $h,d$ with positive weights $a_h1_{h\,{\rm prime}}$, $1_{D'<d\le D,P^-(d)>P^s}$, times $O(L^2H^2\theta^2)$. On $h\mid x,d\mid y=P-x$ require only that $x/h$ be $P^\kappa$-rough and $y/d$ be $P^s$-rough, with **fixed** $\kappa=10^{-6}$; the genuine terminal prime $x/h>\sqrt{x}$ qualifies. Apply §1's joint upper sieve/CRT method now to these **quotients**. If $m_1,m_2$ are the prime allocations then the divisibility moduli are exactly $h m_1,d m_2$ including a possible prime square on the same side. The coefficients still separate for every fixed allocation, $h,d$ target units, and incompatible cross collisions are omitted. At a prime of $P$ the two quotient-divisibilities coincide since $h,d$ are units. Otherwise the two forbidden events are disjoint since simultaneous divisibility implies $p\mid P$. If $p\mid h$ or $p\mid d$, a possible local change from the generic sieve factor is at most $1+O(1/p)$; the same-side conditional quotient divisibility (when tested) still has density $1/p$, as the square in the exact extended modulus also shows. All such primes exceed $W$. The two moduli remain $\le2P^{.501}$, so the same signed CRT control applies. The upper factor is $\ll\mathfrak S(P)/(\kappa s L^2)$ with uniform constant for $\theta$ sufficiently small since $.001/\max(\kappa,s)$ is large; only an upper bound is needed. Thus (6) bounds this normalized shoulder by $O_{\kappa,c_0}(H^3\theta^2)$, using $\sum_h a_h/h\ll\theta$ in limsup. This argument includes the scarce balanced semiprimes without a spurious per-$(h,d)$ floor error. It follows that all needed $\langle S_t,F_s\rangle/X_P=o_*(1)$; the use of a signed divisor principal in the interior was essential. Consequently in any of our rough-cut correlations we can replace the corrected score by $K_s$ without affecting the ordered limit.

## 4. Exact limiting response and rectangles

For $s=w$, (3) sees only the linear patch until $u=1/\theta$. Replacing the whole profile by $(1-wu)_+$ changes the multiplier by $O(w^{-1}\rho(1/\theta))$. Partial integration now gives $C_w\to1$ since $\int_0^\infty\rho=e^\gamma$. For fixed $v>0$ set $s=\theta/v$. At the patch $u=v$, $\phi(su)$ jumps from $1-\theta$ to $(1-2\theta)^2$, a drop $3\theta-4\theta^2$; the far truncation jump has Dickman-negligible cost. Integrating by parts on both intervals and using dominated convergence in the second integral yields
$$
 C_{\theta/v}\longrightarrow C(v)=e^{-\gamma}\left\{\int_0^v\rho(t)\,dt+
           4\int_v^\infty\rho(t)\,dt+3v\rho(v)\right\}
 =1+3e^{-\gamma}\left\{v\rho(v)+\int_v^\infty\rho(t)\,dt\right\}.           \tag{7}
$$
Indeed the derivative below the patch is $-s$, above it $-4s(1-2su)$, with the original atom in (3) included in Stieltjes integration by parts. Thus $C(v)=4$ for $0<v\le1$, $C(\infty)=1$, and $C'(v)=-3e^{-\gamma}\rho(v-1)$ a.e. for $v>0$.

On $v(n)\in[a,b)$ the true pre-correction score is, apart from endpoint convention, $F_{\theta/a}-F_{\theta/b}$: the interval contains composites only. A prime exactly equal to any prescribed power threshold costs $O_\theta(P^{1-\delta_\theta}L^2)$ over central multiples. Apply (4) and §3 to both finite differences to get (1). With $z=2\theta$, the low score before correction is $F_z-F_w$, the high score $\Pi-F_z$. Equations (4),(5),(7) and $\langle\Pi,\Pi\rangle=G(P)+o_{P;\theta}(X_P)$ now give respectively $(4-1)^2$, $(4-1)(1-4)$ and $G(P)/X_P-2(4)+4^2$, proving (2). The last replacement of the prime logs uses the usual two-form upper sieve, not an assumed estimate for the number of prime pairs. No signed high–high estimate independent of $G$ follows.
