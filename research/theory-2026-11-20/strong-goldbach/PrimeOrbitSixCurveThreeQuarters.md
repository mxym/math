# Six-label prime-factor orbits of bounded arity cannot have an F-part above the three-quarters threshold

Fix an odd prime p, a finite set F of primes, an integer D≥2 and ε>0. Put N_F=∏_{ℓ∈F}ℓ^{v_ℓ(N)}. At all sufficiently large even N coprime to p such that

    N_F ≥ N^(3/4+ε),

the all-composite forward prime-factor orbit from p, if it exists, has at least seven labels **or** contains a row with more than D factors counted with multiplicity. Here one adjoins all prime factors of every reached N−x. In particular, on six-label all-composite orbits in this range the maximum row multiplicity tends to infinity; no particular growth rate is claimed. At a hypothetical Goldbach failure the all-composite premise holds, but this theorem neither proves Goldbach nor bounds the size of its prime-factor orbits. Unlike the [prior fixed-smooth theorem](PrimeOrbitSixSmoothExclusion.md), the factor outside F is allowed to be polynomial in N; unlike that theorem we need a fixed arity cap to exclude six.

The proof more generally restricts six-label orbits of bounded arity on any unbounded sequence with N_F≥N^(1/2+ε): after taking subsequences, the nonseed primes are nonconstant polynomials in one common rational parameter of bounded denominator. If d is the degree of the center polynomial, m its greatest root multiplicity, and n_i the degrees of the nonseed polynomials, then
     
    limsup log(N_F)/log N ≤ m/d < 1,       n_i+n_j ≥ m  (i≠j).
     
In taking the subsequence one may first fix the limit of log(N_F)/log N. In particular there is precisely one root of multiplicity m>d/2, which is rational. These are necessary properties on an infinite sequence, not a finite classification of polynomial models.

## 1. The residual bounded-arity case has five independent interior columns

We may argue on a contrary sequence N→∞ and fix all finite choices by subsequences. The [moving-seed bound](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md) rules out fewer than six labels. For six, [the bounded-arity rank theorem, analytic part](EACSixThreeFactorRank.md), using only N_F≥N^(1/2+ε), shows the entire orbit is strongly connected, A=(v_{x_j}(N−x_i)) is singular, and dim ker_Q(I−A)=2. Each label divides an internal odd composite, so x_i≤(N−3)/3. The exponent chart A is now fixed. Every nonseed prime tends to infinity: a bounded second label would give a subsequence with two fixed primes in this same six-terminal, contrary to the [two-seed theorem](PrimeOrbitMultiSeedMinimal.md). Take limits w_i of log x_i/log N and limits of x_i/N. The real logarithmic equations give Aw=1, w_p=0, so 1 is in the span of the five nonseed columns A′. Also rank A=5: if rank were at most four, eigenvalues would include 0,0,1,1 and the Perron root ρ≥2; the zero trace would force the last eigenvalue to be −2−ρ.

First, at most one label has positive limiting ratio x_i/N. Adapt the [geometric sign argument](PrimeOrbitSixTerminalFiveMacroscopic.md) to **positive ratios**, not just weight one. For such a label y any parent has N−x=p^a y, a≥1 fixed: its cofactor is bounded, while all nonseeds diverge. The parent is a nonseed and unique, since the ratio of two possible powers of p lies in [2/3,3/2]. An input has at most one such child. In a nonzero real vector Af=f with f_p=0, both signs occur by Perron, and each sign induces a directed cycle, of length at least two. Parent and y have the same nonzero value (two zero nonseeds would leave only three entries for both cycles). Their sign group cannot have size two: then x and y form a mutual pair, which gives xy<N since N=x+y+hxy with a positive integer h. Indeed N≡x+y mod xy, x+y<N from the label bound, and x,y are distinct units. This contradicts y≫N and x→∞. Thus each such edge lies in the only possible three-member sign group. A cycle entirely of positive-ratio labels is impossible: composing N−x=p^a y around it yields N dividing the nonzero bounded integer ∏p^a−(−1)^length. Two positive-ratio labels must therefore form a chain x→y→z together with a non-positive-ratio parent x, since the group has size three, each child has a unique parent, and each parent at most one child of positive ratio. To have a same-sign successor, z must point to x. The two chain equations now give x|p^{a+b}−p^a+1 (substitute into x|N−z and use (x,N)=1). This is a fixed positive integer while x→∞.

If the p-column increased rank, there would be a fixed integral c annihilating A′ with U=cᵀA_p≠0. Since 1∈col A′, Σc_i=0, and the exact equations give U log p=Σ c_i log(1−x_i/N). If all ratios vanish this is impossible. A unique nonzero ratio, at y, is p^(−a) by its row from a parent of ratio zero. Passing to the limit gives p^U=(1−p^(−a))^{c_y}, impossible by a prime divisor of p^a−1>1. Consequently rank A′=rank A=5. Invert five independent rows of A′ in the log equations, keeping the fixed p-column contribution. We obtain log x_i=w_i log N+O(1) for every nonseed; all their weights are positive by divergence. A weight-one label now has positive ratio. If it exists, its parent row x gives the only nonzero entry (one) of the y-column. A nonzero fixed left-null vector c of A satisfies c_x=0; summing the log equations gives Σc_i=0 and Σc_i log(1−x_i/N)=0, hence c_y=0 as well. Take a nonzero left eigenvector g of A at 1 with g_x=0; then g_y=0 by the y-column. Its signs are mixed by the positive right Perron vector. Removing x,y leaves a four-by-four nonnegative zero-diagonal matrix with a left kernel vector and a mixed-sign eigenvector at 1. If its spectral radius r>1, the eigenvalues include 0,1,r forcing −1−r by the trace; if r=1 a nonnegative Perron vector is independent of the mixed-sign left vector and the eigenvalues include 0,1,1,−2. Both are impossible (r≥1). This is the trace step of [the interior theorem, §4](PrimeOrbitInteriorEisenstein.md), independent of its later smoothness input. It follows that all five weights lie strictly between 0 and 1. Also at least two *moving* primes have weights ≤1/2: otherwise {p} and at most one other label would be a retained set for all sufficiently large terms, as every other label would exceed √N. The [retained-set bound](EACTwoRetained.md) would give nullity ≤1. This uses no strict inequality on a weight equal to 1/2.

## 2. A genuine curve, with its integral points

Fix p in the polynomial equations x_i+∏_j x_j^{A_ij}=N. In the five moving coordinates, choose five rows giving an invertible minor of A′. At a physical point, multiplying each Jacobian column by x_j and dividing each row by N−x_i changes this minor into that of A′ plus the corresponding entries δ_ij x_i/(N−x_i). The perturbation tends to zero by the strictly interior weights. Thus this selected partial Jacobian of the five equations in the five moving coordinates is nonsingular there, leaving N as local parameter for the six variables. Their common zero set has local complex dimension one and is smooth there. It has finitely many irreducible components; one curve contains infinitely many such physical points. All remaining factor equations hold identically on it. One may take it geometrically irreducible defined over Q: an irreducible geometric curve with infinitely many rational points must equal every Galois conjugate, as distinct curves have finite intersection. N is nonconstant. The generic projection degree is ≤D^5 (at fixed generic N the chosen equations have degree ≤D and their Jacobian stays invertible generically on this curve, giving isolated fiber points to which affine Bézout applies), though this coarse bound is not needed here.

On the smooth projective normalization, poles at infinity mean the union of the poles of all the affine coordinates. The integral points under discussion have **all** these affine coordinates integral. [Siegel's theorem on integral points, as precisely recalled by Alvanos–Bilu–Poulakis](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2097) implies genus zero and at most two geometric points at infinity. To pass from the affine curve to its normalization one may discard the finitely many singular points; integral elements of its affine coordinate ring take values with denominators bounded outside a fixed finite set after clearing fixed coefficients in their monic equations. Thus the lifted rational points are S-integral for a fixed S, which suffices here. A nonsingular rational point makes this genus-zero projective curve P¹ over Q. Its missing set is nonempty by nonconstancy.

## 3. A center with positive F-part exponent selects one pole

If the two missing points are both rational, choose a rational coordinate t with zero/pole at that pair. Both t and 1/t are in the integral closure of the affine coordinate ring over Q. Their monic equations evaluated at the physical integer tuples give **uniformly bounded rational denominators**, not merely arbitrary S-denominators: their coefficients are rational polynomials in the integer coordinates with uniformly bounded denominators. At each prime the monic equation therefore bounds the denominator of its rational root, and elsewhere it is integral. Both rational t and its inverse having bounded denominators leaves only finitely many values. This excludes the split case.

In the two conjugate-point case nonconstant N has poles at both, of equal order h. In primitive projective parameters (u,v), H=max(|u|,|v|), write N=P(u,v)/(c Q(u,v)^h) with c a fixed nonzero integer, P an integral degree-2h binary form, Q an integral irreducible binary quadratic for the two poles, coprime over Q. A fixed coefficient scale is harmless. Along integral N, |Q(u,v)| is bounded: common divisors of values of two coprime homogeneous binary forms at primitive arguments are bounded in terms of the forms alone, by the resultant identities (the ideal contains a fixed nonzero integer times powers of each variable). Since Q^h divides P at these tuples this applies. If Q has no real zero, H is bounded. Otherwise, on an unbounded subsequence, u/v tends to a fixed real irrational root θ of Q and |v| is comparable to H. The real product |u−θv|·|v| is bounded by factoring the quadratic; |P(u,v)| is comparable to H^(2h) because P(θ,1)≠0. Consequently N is comparable to H^(2h) in absolute size.

Here and below use the [p-adic Subspace Theorem for primitive integer vectors (Evertse's Theorem 1.1, with the dimension-two use also on page 3)](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/dio2011-padicsubspace.pdf): a product of two independent local forms at infinity and each of finitely many finite places bounded by H^(−δ) puts (u,v) in finitely many rational lines. At ℓ∈F select a unit coordinate of the primitive pair and dehomogenize by it, using that coordinate as one linear form. At a nearest root β of the dehomogenized P use the other form u−βv or v−βu. With the unit coordinate unchanged these are independent. For |P(u,v)|_ℓ small the product at ℓ is ≪|P(u,v)|_ℓ^{1/(2h)}: factor the polynomial over the algebraic closure, and distances to every distinct root other than a nearest one are bounded from below by fixed root separation. If |P|_ℓ is bounded below the inequality costs only a constant (choose independent coordinate forms if the dehomogenization is constant). All choices are finite and may be fixed on a subsequence. Thus the total product using the real forms just given is ≪N_F^{−1/(2h)}, since P=cNQ^h. This is ≤H^(−δ) for some δ>0 under any fixed positive lower exponent for N_F. The Subspace Theorem contradicts infinitely many primitive projective parameters on distinct lines. This excludes two poles altogether on the sequence at issue.

The sole point at infinity is now rational. Choose a rational parameter t with this point as its only pole. The coordinates N(t),x_i(t) are rational polynomials, all nonseed ones nonconstant. The rational t-values have bounded denominators by the same monic-integrality argument (or directly by evaluating the center polynomial and reducing its numerator modulo the denominator of t). Pass to fixed denominator and rescale t to an integer parameter. Let d=deg N(t), m the largest complex root multiplicity of N(t). The root-separation argument above at ℓ∈F, now with (t,1), gives two independent forms of product ≪|N(t)|_ℓ^{1/m}. At infinity use the coordinate forms, with product |t|. Since |N(t)| is comparable to |t|^d, an infinite subsequence with N_F≥N^(m/d+δ) for any fixed δ>0 contradicts the same Subspace Theorem. This proves the limsup bound. In particular m>d/2 under the stated F-rich premise, and its unique root of this multiplicity is rational by Galois invariance. Denote it by a.

If m=d then N(t)=C(t−a)^d. At a physical t₀, put z=(t−a)/(t₀−a). Then N(t)−p=N(t₀) z^d−p is irreducible over Q by Eisenstein at the seed p, since N(t₀) is an integer prime to p. But the seed row writes this as a product of at least two nonconstant polynomials with multiplicity: p itself cannot divide its complement and all moving labels diverge. Contradiction. Hence m<d.

## 4. Pairwise degree bounds at the multiple root

Every x_i(a) is nonzero: if a factor x_j(a)=0 then any input row using j has complementary product zero and hence its input also zero at N(a)=0. Reverse paths in the strongly connected graph eventually reach the nonzero fixed seed. Consequently near a all coordinate log derivatives are regular. Write g_i=x_i′/x_i (so g_p=0). From x_i/∏x_j^{A_ij}=−(1−N/x_i)^{-1} and the order-m root obtain
     
    (I−A)g = O((t−a)^(m−1))
     
coordinatewise as local rational functions. The row-space quotient by I−A has dimension two and e_p has nonzero class by [moving-seed independence](PrimeOrbitMovingSeed.md). Thus for every pair of distinct nonseed indices i,j some rational row combination is supported on p,i,j with coefficients u,v at i,j not both zero. Clear denominators. The resulting u g_i+v g_j vanishes to order ≥m−1. It is not the zero rational function: otherwise x_i^u x_j^v is constant in Q(t), contrary to evaluating along the infinite physical sequence with distinct unbounded prime values. This also treats one zero coefficient. Its numerator over x_i x_j has degree at most n_i+n_j−1, where n_i=deg x_i. Both denominator values at a are nonzero. We obtain n_i+n_j≥m, as asserted; also w_i=n_i/d at infinity.

## 5. Rows with exactly two moving factors

First dispose of the case in which every row has exactly two moving factors with multiplicity (possibly times p to an exponent ≤D). This case is already impossible under N_F≥N^(1/2+ε). The constant vector of halves solves A′w=1, and uniqueness from rank five shows all five moving weights equal 1/2. In the exact independent-row logarithmic solution the seed power contributes a fixed constant, while log(1−x_i/N)=O(N^(−1/2)), since x_i=O(√N). Consequently for fixed positive constants C_i on this curve sequence we have

    x_i=C_i√N+O(1).

Choose any moving j. The polynomials x_i(t) all have the same degree, and C_i/C_j is rational by their rational leading coefficient ratio. The polynomial x_i−(C_i/C_j)x_j is bounded on unbounded t, hence is constant. All moving labels are affine in x_j. The seed equation expresses N as a quadratic in x_j since its two factors are moving. Thus the full function field Q(t)=Q(N,x_i) is Q(x_j). A nonconstant polynomial x_j(t) generating Q(t) has degree one; hence d=2. But d/2<m<d admits no integer m at d=2. This contradiction rules out the all-moving-pairs case. In particular, §4 already proves the exponent 5/6+ε: that threshold forces the two least nonseed weights to sum to >5/6 and the second is ≤1/2, hence every moving weight is >1/3 and every row must have just two moving factors.

## 6. The actual degree of the polynomial center

Let H be the least common multiple of the denominators of the five positive rational w_i in lowest terms. Since dw_i are polynomial degrees, H divides d. In fact **d=H**. To see the reverse divisibility, let G be the inverse of a five-row, five-column submatrix of A′, with those rows indexed by I and α_j the positive constants from inverting their fixed seed-power terms. Set u=N^(−1/H)>0, Y_p=p u^H, Y_i=x_i/N. The exact logarithmic equations, exponentiated using the positive branch, become

    Y_j=α_j u^{H(1−w_j)} ∏_{i∈I}(1−Y_i)^{G_ji}.

All five u exponents are positive integers. With powers defined holomorphically near Y=0, the derivative of the left sides minus right sides in the five Y's at the origin is the identity. The analytic implicit function theorem gives a unique holomorphic five-tuple Y(u) with Y(0)=0, equal to the physical solutions at the sequence of positive u sufficiently near zero because their Y→0. Each x_i(u)=u^(−H)Y_i(u) is meromorphic at zero.

The rational parameter t is a rational function of N and the x_i since they generate the curve's function field. Its denominator on the curve does not vanish identically and so is nonzero at all but finitely many of the physical points. Substitution therefore produces a meromorphic germ t(u) (the substituted denominator cannot vanish identically). The identity N(t(u))=u^(−H) holds at infinitely many u→0, and thus as an identity of meromorphic germs after clearing powers of u. Consequently t(u) has a pole of positive integer order, and d times that order is H. This proves the equality.

## 7. Above three quarters only a single pure cubic can break the pair pattern

Assume N_F≥N^(3/4+ε). By §4, **distinct** moving labels have weight sum >3/4. At least two moving weights are ≤1/2. Hence the least moving weight is >1/4 and every row has either two or three moving factors, since its total moving weight is one and every single weight is <1. A triple of moving factors containing two distinct labels is impossible: their weights already sum to >3/4 and the remaining factor has weight >1/4. A pure triple has weight 1/3 on its repeated label; there can be only one such label q since two distinct labels of weight 1/3 are prohibited. It is the least moving label: any smaller one would give a still smaller prohibited distinct-pair sum. Without a triple the preceding §5 applies.

Distinct rows cannot have the same multiset of moving factors: their complementary products would have ratio an integral power of p. Distinct inputs have distinct complements, and all complement ratios lie in [2/3,3/2], excluding every nontrivial power and equality. Therefore the projected q³ row is unique and every other projected row is a distinct pair.

View the pairs as edges on the five moving labels, allowing a loop for a repeated prime. In the component of q, its only possible mates have weight 2/3; each such mate, in turn, can only meet q, the unique label of weight 1/3. Conversely any vertex of weight 2/3 must occur in such a pair. This component is a star, possibly the isolated q. On components without q, the pair weight sums are one along every edge, so a nonbipartite component has all weights 1/2. A bipartite component disjoint from q would furnish a nonzero right null vector of A′ by putting +1 and −1 on its two sides and zero elsewhere (no row q³ meets it). Full column rank excludes this. Isolated unused labels are impossible by strong connectivity. Thus if h labels have weight 2/3 there are n=4−h labels of weight 1/2, called the middle labels. They must support n+1 distinct loop-allowed pairs: subtract the one cubic row and h star edges from six. This is impossible at n=0 or 1, so there are at least two middle labels, even without an additional large/small counting theorem.

The denominators occurring therefore have lcm H=6, even if the star is trivial. By §6 d=6. The root multiplicity satisfies 3d/4<m<d, hence m=5. The polynomial q(t) has degree 2, and every middle polynomial has degree 3.

## 8. Two cubic middle labels cannot coexist at that root

By the moving-seed theorem the classes e_p,e_q are independent in the two-dimensional row-space quotient of I−A (q is the least nonseed eventually because of its strictly smaller weight). For each middle label y there are fixed rational s,b with e_y−s e_q−b e_p in the row space. Section 4 therefore gives g_y−s g_q=O((t−a)^4). All label values at a are nonzero. Integrating the logarithmic derivatives as convergent power series, or formally with constant term fixed, yields
     
    y(t)/y(a) = (q(t)/q(a))^s  mod (t−a)^5.
     
The left side has degree three. If the quadratic q(t)/q(a) has zero linear coefficient at a, the right side has only even powers through degree four, impossible for a cubic with nonzero degree-three term. Rescale the local rational coordinate to X so that q(t)/q(a)=Q(X)=1+X+τ X² with τ rational and nonzero. Write c_j(s)=[X^j]Q(X)^s. Then s≠0,1 and
     
    c_2(s)=s(τ+(s−1)/2),
    c_3(s)=s(s−1)(τ+(s−2)/6) ≠0,
    24 c_4(s)=s(s−1)[(s−2)(s−3)+12τ(s−2)+12τ²]=0.
     
Two different middle labels cannot have the same s, since their normalized cubic polynomials would agree identically, whereas their ratio takes values at distinct growing primes. Taking any two, their parameters s,t′ are thus the two distinct roots of the indicated monic quadratic. In particular their sum is 5−12τ and product 6−24τ+12τ².

All label corrections in the exact inverted-row logarithms are O(N^(−1/3)) since the greatest weight is at most 2/3 and the preliminary fixed-chart estimates are log x_i=w_i log N+O(1). Each middle value is therefore C_y√N+O(N^(1/6)) with C_y>0. The ratio of the leading coefficients of two middle polynomials is their rational limiting ratio C_y/C_z. After subtracting this multiple, the polynomial difference is O(N^(1/6))=O(|X|) at infinity; the cubic **and quadratic** coefficients of their polynomial vectors must therefore be proportional. In normalized form c_2(s)c_3(t′)−c_2(t′)c_3(s)=0. Direct expansion gives
     
    [c_2(s)c_3(t′)−c_2(t′)c_3(s)]/[s t′ (s−t′)]
       = −[12τ²+2τ(s+t′)−6τ+s t′−s−t′+1]/12
       = (4τ−1)/6.
     
Hence τ=1/4 and Q(X)=(1+X/2)². The two indicated quadratic roots are now 1/2 and 3/2. For the middle with s=1/2, the power-series truncation is just 1+X/2, contradicting degree three. This proves the three-quarters theorem. The [exact coefficient controls](check_prime_orbit_curve_quarters.py) reproduce these star counts, binomial coefficients, and symmetric/Vieta identities without floating point.

The integral-point and approximation inputs were used only for their stated curve- and line-finiteness conclusions. No conjecture on simultaneous prime values of polynomials is needed; at or below the three-quarters threshold no general six-orbit exclusion is asserted.

## Comparison (through November 21, 2026)

[Bożek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09900) introduced the underlying
factorization graphs. The repository's [fully smooth six-label result](PrimeOrbitSixSmoothExclusion.md)
does not cap arity but requires the full center to be smooth; the
[three-factor rank theorem](EACSixThreeFactorRank.md) requires only an
F-part beyond one half but excludes size six only when arity is at most
three. The present result allows any fixed arity and a polynomial-size
outside factor in the stated exponent range. [Kelanemer's August–September
2026 preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7274460)
also studies terminal exponent matrices; the indexed abstract was
accessible, but the full text was not, so this check cannot establish
comprehensive nonoverlap or priority. [Nelson's November 2026 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14580v2)
uses curves in a ring of additive prime relations rather than the integral
points of the reflected factor equations. The three-quarters threshold
is not asserted to be best possible.
