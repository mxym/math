# One-kernel linear replacement over finite fields

## Theorem

Let E=F_(q^d), let V be a finite-dimensional vector space over F_q, and let K and the indexed spaces L_i have dimension d over F_q, with each L_i disjoint from K except at zero. Suppose V/K carries one common E-vector-space structure for which the image of every L_i is an E-line. There is an E-vector-space structure on V extending the given structure on the quotient and an indexed family of E-lines K,L'_i such that:

* L'_i projects onto exactly the same line as L_i;
* if an indexed subfamily of K,L_i is in direct sum over F_q, its replacement is in direct sum over E (equivalently over F_q).

Thus the replacement retains all originally independent subfamilies simultaneously, not merely those of one designated size. It may also create independences. It does not assert equivalence of the original and linear configurations.

## Proof

Identify K with E as F_q-vector spaces and take an F_q-linear splitting V=(V/K)⊕E. For each i choose a nonzero vector q_i on the projected line. There is a unique F_q-linear function f_i:E→E with L_i equal to the graph z↦(zq_i,f_i(z)). Choose any nonzero F_q-linear functional φ:E→F_q. By nondegeneracy of the field-trace pairing, for each i there is a unique t_i∈E such that

$$
 \varphi(f_i(z))=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_q}(t_i z)\qquad(z\in E).
$$

Use the E-line L'_i generated by (q_i,t_i) in the direct-sum E-vector-space structure, keeping the vertical K. Choosing another q_i scales the assigned projective column by the same E-scalar.

Take an originally independent subfamily. If K is in it, the remaining projected directions are independent: a nonzero relation would lift to a sum in K, contradicting directness. Assume henceforth K is absent. If the subfamily has m members and their projections have E-rank r, then md≤rd+d by counting F_q-dimensions. When r=m projection alone suffices. Otherwise r=m−1, and all projected relations have the form (λ_i z) for one fixed nonzero tuple (λ_i) and variable z∈E. Their original vertical sums are

$$
 h(z)=\sum_i f_i(\lambda_i z).
$$

Original directness says h:E→E is injective, hence bijective. Therefore φ∘h is not identically zero. Since

$$
 \varphi(h(z))=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_q}\left(z\sum_i \lambda_i t_i\right),
$$

the sum of the λ_i t_i is nonzero. For the replacement, the vertical sum is exactly z times that nonzero scalar. No nontrivial relation lifts to zero, as required. ∎

This proof works equally for a field extension equipped with a nondegenerate trace pairing, if all relevant spaces have finite dimensions as above. In fact over any finite-dimensional field extension E/F one may use any nonzero F-linear functional ρ on E in place of the trace: (x,y)↦ρ(xy) is nondegenerate because multiplication by any nonzero x is onto. Thus no separability of E/F is necessary. This observation needs no special finite-field structure.

## Quaternary case and application

For E=F₄ over F₂, write f_i(z)=u_i z+v_i²z² uniquely: the sixteen choices are distinct by the polynomial root bound and exhaust the sixteen binary-linear maps. Choose φ=Tr. Trace is unchanged by squaring, hence t_i=u_i+v_i.

In binary dimension 12, if every four members of a family of at least four binary 2-spaces are in direct sum, and projection from one member puts all others in one Desarguesian spread of the quotient, the family has at most 21 members. Indeed the replacement consists of points in PG(5,4), any four of which are independent; the exact maximum of such a point set is 21. Equality is attained by field reduction of either maximum quaternary configuration. This is not a bound of 21 on unrestricted binary families and says nothing about arbitrary Sidon sets.

**Context.** Related, but different, results about equivalence of additive MDS codes themselves from several linear projections appear in [Adriaensen–Ball, *On additive MDS codes with linear projections*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09767) and [Ball–Gamboa–Lavrauw, *On additive MDS codes over small fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06183). For a different question about extensions that preserve the original code, see [Alderson, *On the Maximality of Additive Codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22297). The proof above is standalone and does not claim equivalence or an extension of an original code. For the length-21 input see [the PG(5,4) proof repository at the frozen commit](../Overview.md).
