# Primary statements and comparisons

* [Penrose, *Record times for coverage thresholds and maximal spacings*, v3, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19104v3#S5) explicitly distinguishes eventual uniqueness from an all-sample-size result. Proposition 5.1 bounds the number of winners in the **interior** of the allowed center set (also for the inscribed version), for each n and k. Proposition 5.2 proves uniqueness on the small-radius event for a full target with a continuously differentiable, Lipschitz-gradient boundary. Its purpose is record counting. We remove the small-radius and target-regularity conditions for **fixed-target** coverage, not for the constrained spacings of that paper's (2.2). Eventual uniqueness and its use for record counting are prior. The supplement uses the distance-to-boundary cap explicitly and shows that a finite-size unconditional inscribed version is false. [Submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19104): August 19, 2026, latest listed v3 September 16.
* The connection between a gradient of a farthest-distance function and a unique farthest point is classical. In particular, [Panda and Kapoor, *On farthest points of sets* (1978)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-247X(78)90131-2), study the single-query-point problem via differentiability of the farthest distance. More generally, [Ekeland and Lebourg, *Generic Fréchet-differentiability and perturbed optimization problems in Banach spaces*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1976-0431253-2), illustrate this general method in optimization. We do not claim a new differentiation principle, Hilbert-space farthest-point theory, Rademacher's theorem or the coarea formula. Here the main step is recovery of the maximizing target from an active **site** block for an order statistic of all site distances, including the exact strict interior count.
* [Edelsbrunner and Nikitenko, *Poisson–Delaunay Mosaics of Order k*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-018-0049-2), study local order-k Voronoi/Delaunay geometry for Poisson points. This is not an arbitrary fixed compact target, where a maximizing target need not be an interior Voronoi vertex or lie on a smooth surface. [Penrose, *Random Euclidean coverage from within*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-022-01182-5), obtains threshold laws for coverage of regions, not the all-size uniqueness statement here.
* Searches through November 19, 2026 included the current original and separate July–November searches for arbitrary compact targets, uniqueness of largest empty balls/holes, largest order-k spacing and covering winners, genericity and radius differentiability, furthest-point selection, and higher-order Voronoi structures. No match for the stated arbitrary-target, all-n/k result was found in the publications checked. Literature indices and accessible publications do not prove priority.
