# The constrained inscribed variant below boundary reach

[Penrose v3, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19104v3#S5) distinguishes the covering threshold, whose winner maximizes a site-distance function on a fixed target, from the maximal spacing, whose entire candidate ball must be inscribed. The latter's eventual uniqueness for suitable domains is prior, not a general new all-sample-size uniqueness assertion. Here is a short deterministic differentiability proof in the positive-reach range and a finite counterexample without it.

Let $A=\overline{A^\circ}\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a compact domain with $C^{1,1}$ boundary of reach $\tau>0$; put $g(b)=\operatorname{dist}(b,\partial A)$. With $d_k(b;X)$ the $k$th distance to the sites, the maximal *radius* of an inscribed open ball with at most $k-1$ sites strictly inside is
$$ \Psi(X)=\max_{b\in A}\min(g(b),d_k(b;X)). $$
This is the radius version of Penrose's equation (2.2); the volume of the maximal spacing is $\omega_d\Psi^d$. The equivalence follows because the open ball of radius $s$ is admissible at $b$ exactly when $s\le g(b)$ and $s\le d_k(b;X)$. With finitely many sites and a domain with interior, $\Psi>0$.

For jointly absolutely continuous sites, almost surely on $R=\Psi(X)<\tau$, the maximizer is unique, and its ball contains exactly $k-1$ sites strictly inside. In particular this holds eventually for nested sites for which the inscribed radius tends to zero. We work at a differentiable configuration of the Lipschitz function $\Psi$, with gradient $G$ in the site coordinates.

If $g(b)=R<\tau$, projection to the boundary has a unique nearest point. The distance function is differentiable there, with inward unit gradient $v$, and $g(b+t v)=R+t+o(t)$ for $t\downarrow0$ (small displacements stay inside the domain). This distance derivative follows by comparing the distance to the old nearest point and to the nearest point after displacement; compactness and uniqueness force the latter to converge to the former. Thus a maximizing $b$ cannot have $d_k(b;X)>R$: a small move in this inward direction would leave that strict site slack intact while improving the cap. When $g(b)>R$, necessarily $d_k(b;X)=R$ as well.

Write $I=\{i:|x_i-b|=R\}$ and $u_i=(x_i-b)/R$ there. At any maximizing $b$, fixed-center directional testing as in the [unconstrained proof](PROOF.md) yields
$$G\in\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{e_i\otimes u_i:i\in I\}\cup E_b\bigr),\qquad
 E_b=\begin{cases}\{0\}&g(b)=R,\\ \varnothing&g(b)>R.\end{cases}$$
Indeed adding a fixed active cap adds zero to the possible directional lower bounds. To see that $G\ne0$ even with a cap, choose a maximizer $b$, let $v$ be the above inward direction if $g(b)=R$ and zero otherwise, and test configurations $x_i+t(v+u_i)$ for $i\in I$ with other sites fixed. At candidate $b+tv$, the active distances become $R+t$, the inactive distances keep their sides, and the cap is either slack or improves by $t+o(t)$. Thus $\Psi$ improves by at least $t+o(t)$. Since $G\ne0$, any nonzero block $G_i$ in its convex representation identifies *every* maximizer as $x_i-RG_i/|G_i|$. If there were fewer than $k-1$ strictly inside, a one-site displacement in either sign at fixed $b$ has a nonnegative first-order lower bound for the $k$th site distance, and also for its minimum with the fixed cap. As in the unconstrained proof this would force $G=0$.

The reach restriction cannot simply be removed for this assertion. In the plane take the stadium $A=[-3,3]\times\{0\}+\overline B(0,1)$, which has $C^{1,1}$ boundary. For iid uniform sites there is positive probability that all lie in the radius-$1/10$ disk about $(-5/2,0)$. Then every center $(x,0)$ with $0\le x\le2$ gives an empty inscribed open unit disk. No larger disk fits in the strip $|y|\le1$ containing the stadium. This applies to every finite $n$ and $1\le k\le n$, and concerns only the *constrained* notion, not uniqueness of the ordinary finite covering winner.
