# Independent internal review: inscribed spacings below reach

Reviewed INSCRIBED.md SHA-256 b18020ddcb270786efecb3e67167bc1f8b6a8a90a8b06c2bc5068014665e0c0b. This is a short alternate proof in the reach range and an explicit obstruction at finite sample size; the older eventual result is not claimed anew. Internal AI review only.

The min of the site-order radius and clearance is the admissible inscribed open radius at each center. With R positive below reach, an active clearance constraint has a unique closest boundary point and differentiable clearance in the inward direction; if the kth site distance had slack, moving inward alone would contradict maximality. At differentiability in the site configuration, active gradients therefore have the same convex lower-bound comparison as for ordinary coverage, but the constant cap can add a zero vector. If the cap is active, testing the center shift and site displacements jointly raises its clearance and every active site distance by first-order t; if slack, the center need not move. This gives a nonzero configuration gradient, so an active nonzero block recovers the same center for every maximizer. The zero-vector option cannot avoid the one-coordinate test forcing zero whole gradient if fewer than k−1 sites are strictly inside. The C^1,1 stadium example fits in a strip of width two, so no larger disk fits, while positive probability puts all sampled sites far from a continuum of inscribed unit disks. It confirms that the ordinary all-n theory cannot be directly transferred to constrained spacings. No global all-n uniqueness is asserted for inscribed balls.
