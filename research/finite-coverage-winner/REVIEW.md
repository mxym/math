# Independent internal review: uniqueness of the extremal coverage center

Reviewed PROOF.md SHA-256 16c1a35c0e31826f6635db784993a8c02301a640d84d28e66a102632716e80ee. The argument proves unrestricted finite-sample uniqueness for the fixed nonempty compact target in the stated norm class. Internal AI review, not external peer review.

The maximum of kth order statistics is Lipschitz in the site configuration, so at each differentiability point one may compare to every maximizing fixed target without differentiating that maximizing point. Active sites give the kth-minus-below order statistic of first directional terms (or a lower bound from fixed norming covectors). The global gradient therefore has pairing at least the minimum of these terms in every direction, which separates it into their finite convex hull. Site blocks are disjoint, so its summed (dual) block magnitudes are one. A single nonzero block recovers the maximizing point via Euclidean normalization; for a strictly convex norm its nonzero covector has a unique unit supported primal vector. If two or more active ranks must move inward to reduce the kth statistic, moving any one site yields a nonnegative first variation in both signs, forcing each gradient block to vanish, a contradiction. Hence the open ball contains exactly k−1 sites. Adjacent equality would make a lower-multiplicity maximizer a maximizer at the higher multiplicity with an impossible pair of open counts.

For the record probability, hitting the open ball improves the sole old maximizer; all other target values do not increase, and compactness and continuity imply a strict improvement of the global value. Otherwise the old maximizing value survives. The law of the new independent point supplies its ball mass, regardless of the old joint site density. Rademacher with conditioning on any independent compact target makes the exceptional set null. The Euclidean block lower bound on gradient length is sufficient for the coarea conclusion about arbitrary null sets of radii. The maximum-norm segment is indeed a positive-measure set of examples without uniqueness. No constrained maximal-spacing conclusion follows from the unrestricted proof alone; the ordinary single-site farthest-point precedents and Penrose's previously proved eventual uniqueness are not reasserted as priority.
