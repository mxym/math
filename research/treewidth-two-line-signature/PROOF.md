# The fixed-rank line-graph signature for every connected graph of treewidth at most two

Let G be finite, simple and connected. Put c=|E|−|V|+1, Q=D+A, M=Q−2I, sig=n₊−n₋ with zero eigenvalues ignored. We prove that if G has no K₄ minor, then 2 sig M−3c≤−1, equivalently 2s(L(G))≤c+1 by the usual incidence identity. Prior cactus constructions in Paone–Paone Theorem 5.3 attain floor((c+1)/2) for all c≥1 even within this class. No claim for graphs of arbitrary treewidth. The proof is computer-assisted: the exhaustive rational checks below are part of the proof, not a sample of graphs. It does not depend on an unverified numerical search.

### 1. Port states and uniform comparisons

In a network with boundary vertices s,t, add (xᵢ+xⱼ)² for each edge ij and subtract 2xᵢ² exactly once for each **interior** vertex. Thus port degree contributions from its own edges are included, but the intrinsic −2 at either terminal is not. If attaching to a graph along terminals, the host supplies the remaining terms. Put c(N)=e−v+1 for the connected network itself, so the objective includes −3c(N). Two connected networks in series have additive c; parallel composition adds their c and one more cycle, because two vertices rather than one were identified. Joining a pendant block to a single existing vertex adds exactly its own c.

A two-port state is (C,U,Q), with U a linear subspace of ℝ² and Q a quadratic form on U. On a real host V with form H and a boundary map T:V→ℝ², it gives the upper value 2sig((H+Q∘T)|T⁻¹(U))−C. We will dominate each network against **every** host, not only against nonsingular ones or independent boundary coordinates. An internal pivot x of nonzero coefficient d increases sig by sign d and subtracts its Schur square divided by d; thus the cost decreases by 2sign d. A zero pivot coupling as 2xλ(z) imposes λ(z)=0 on the exterior, with zero net signature from the removed kernel/hyperbolic directions. This remains correct if the pulled-back λ vanishes identically: then x is just a zero direction. If an existing boundary constraint already determines x linearly in the exterior, substitute that expression directly. These rules follow by elementary congruence, including when other constraints on the exterior persist; [the pullback details](BOUNDARY.md) treat all rank-deficient cases. They show also why an upper state can replace a subnetwork in any of the operations below: internalize remaining coordinates only *after* taking the uniform comparison.

Here is the domination certificate. For states A=(C,U,Q) and B=(D,V,R), let W=U∩V. Then A is bounded above by B on every host whenever
  C−D ≥ 2(dim U+dim V−2dim W)+4 n₊((Q−R)|W).
Indeed restrict both pulled-back forms to the common preimage of W, losing at most the sum of their codimensions in absolute signature by interlacing. These codimensions cannot exceed dim U−dim W and dim V−dim W, even when T is not surjective. On the common subspace the positive inertia of the pulled-back difference is at most n₊((Q−R)|W); each positive rank-one update raises sig by at most two and negative semidefinite updates never increase sig. Multiply by two for the objective. This is a sufficient domination test; no converse is used.

### 2. Explicit finite menu

There are four one-port comparators P_j=(j, y_j x²), with (y₀,y₁,y₂,y₃)=(0,2/3,2,4), j=0,1,2,3. All have full one-dimensional domain. Write F(C;a,b,d) for a full two-port form ax²+2bxy+dy² at cost C, and E(C;h) for the constrained state y=x with form hx² at cost C. Use all independent sign switches of x,y and the exchange x↔y on these representatives:
 
1. F(i+j−1; y_i,0,y_j), for 0≤i≤j≤3 (ten representatives).
2. F(j;1,1,1+y_j) and E(j;2+y_j), for j=0,1,3 (six).
3. F(−1;−1/2,3/2,−1/2) and F(1;3/4,1/4,3/4) (two).

These give 18 representatives or 36 distinct oriented states. They are upper comparisons; no realization of the whole menu by actual networks is necessary.

**Finite closure lemma.** Each of these operations on the oriented states has an upper comparator in the indicated menu:

- Base: a single edge gives F(0;1,1,1).
- Series: put A on (s,x) and B on (x,t), intersect their constraints, add both forms minus 2x², start cost C_A+C_B, eliminate x. One of the 36 two-port states dominates the result.
- Parallel: put A,B on (s,t), intersect their constraints, add the forms, cost C_A+C_B+3. One of the same states dominates.
- Decorate either terminal by any P_j: add y_j x² to the selected coordinate and j to the cost. One of the same states dominates.
- Forget the terminal t: subtract 2t², eliminate t, leaving s as the only port. One of the four P_j dominates.

The included [verify.py](verify.py) supplies a complete finite proof by rational arithmetic. For details equivalent to its elimination, write the univariate interior terms as dx²+2x(g s+h t) (t here a surviving exterior coordinate). If any constraint ax+bs+et=0 has a≠0, substitute x=−(bs+et)/a everywhere, removing that pivot without any cost change. Otherwise all constraints are on (s,t); if d≠0 subtract (gs+ht)²/d and subtract 2sign d from cost; if d=0 append the constraint gs+ht=0. Reduce any resulting one-dimensional domain to its basis whose first nonzero coordinate is 1, evaluating the form on that basis. In dimension two the positive index of a form difference is computed exactly from determinant and trace: one if determinant is negative; zero or two if positive according to the sign of the trace; for determinant zero, one iff trace is positive. In dimension one only the sign matters. Enumerating all ordered pairs gives 1296 series and 1296 parallel checks, the two ends and four decorations give 288 further checks, and 36 checks forget a port. For the last check a formally unused second exterior coordinate is retained to reuse the two-dimensional comparison test; the claimed one-port comparison follows in particular by setting that coordinate identically zero. Every comparison uses the displayed domination certificate on the actual emitted domains. The checker fails explicitly if any state lacks a comparator. It is independent of the exploratory symbolic search that led to the menu; it needs only standard Python.

### 3. Connected graphs, including arbitrary cut vertices

Each biconnected block of a K₄-minor-free graph is a two-terminal series-parallel network with the ends of any prescribed edge as its terminals. In particular we may prescribe one terminal r (the attachment towards the root), choosing a neighbor in that block as the other. This is the classical series-parallel characterization and terminal-selection lemma; see D. Eppstein, *Parallel recognition of series-parallel graphs*, Information and Computation 98 (1992), particularly Lemma 9 of the author version at https://ics.uci.edu/~eppstein/pubs/Epp-IC-92.pdf. A bridge is simply a one-edge block. Hence no assertion is being made that every connected graph in this class itself has a single two-terminal decomposition (a three-leaf star need not).

Root the block–cut tree at a block containing some chosen graph vertex r. Its other blocks meet their parents at unique vertices. Inductively, each rooted child block with its descendants is compared by a one-port P_j at this shared vertex. Within the current block take any TTSP decomposition as above. Distribute each child decoration at vertex v to **one edge leaf incident at v** in this decomposition (choose an arbitrary such leaf, allocate the decoration only once), where v is a terminal of that leaf. Multiple decorations at a vertex can be inserted one at a time using the decorate rule. Now run series and parallel operations up the decomposition. Identifying shared ports assembles all degree terms correctly regardless of which leaf owned a decoration; exactly one intrinsic −2 term is paid when the vertex is finally internalized. This yields a two-port comparator. Forget the nonparent terminal to obtain a P_j at the parent vertex. This proves the induction for arbitrary configurations of nested blocks and cut vertices.

At the top keep only r by the same procedure (if r is a cut vertex, all child blocks there are handled as decorations). Pay the last intrinsic −2r². Each comparison now gives the scalar upper bound 2sign(y_j−2)−j, namely −2,−3,−2,−1. The maximum is −1, proving 2sig M−3c≤−1 for the whole graph. The isolated vertex has c=0, M=[−2], and also satisfies the inequality. There is no restriction on graph order, tree attachments, subdivision lengths or cycle rank.

**Limits and prior.** The conjecture is Paone–Paone, *Line-graph signature beyond the 2-core*, Conjecture 5.4. Their path responses, matching lower constructions, roses/generalized-theta results, bridgeless residue criterion, and protected equality families are direct precedents. The earlier one-port cactus proof covers a proper subclass (its 4-state palette is used here); the simple-suppressed-kernel result is incomparable since K₄ itself has a simple branch kernel. The asserted extension is the bounded, universally dominating **two-port** algebra closed under unrestricted parallel composition, series composition, multiple decorations, and terminal forgetting. This is an inequality, not an exact inertia formula or equality classification; it does not prove the bound for unrestricted graphs. Bounded source checks are not an assertion of priority.
