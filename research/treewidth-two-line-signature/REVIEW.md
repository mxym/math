For a prescribed subspace constraint, if an equation has nonzero coefficient of the internal scalar, substitution on that subspace is a linear isomorphism onto the resulting exterior domain. If the interior scalar is unconstrained and its quadratic diagonal is nonzero, ordinary Schur congruence supplies the rank-one quadratic correction and signature charge. If its diagonal is zero and the coupling is nonzero on the exterior subspace, the interior variable and one exterior direction are a hyperbolic pair; the remaining form is the exterior restriction to the coupling's kernel. If the coupling vanishes on that exterior, this just removes a zero direction. This establishes all degeneracies in the stated emission rule. [Eppstein 1992, Lemma 9](https://ics.uci.edu/~eppstein/pubs/Epp-IC-92.pdf#page=9) explicitly allows the endpoints of any existing edge as terminals of a biconnected series–parallel block. The absence of K4 minors is the classical characterization of this class blockwise. One cannot use a single two-terminal decomposition for arbitrary connected graphs; instead each child block joins at one vertex in the block-cut tree and its offset/cost can be assigned to exactly one incident edge leaf of its parent decomposition. Every block finishes by forgetting its other port and charging that intrinsic term. At the global root the four computed scalar bounds after the last -2 are -2,-3,-2,-1; this yields the inequality. The previously available extremal cactus examples supply sharpness at each cycle rank and are not new. The finite all-rank cactus case and the earlier simple suppressed-kernel case should be credited separately; the latter is not a subclass of treewidth at most two.
