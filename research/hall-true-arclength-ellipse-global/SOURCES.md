# Formulation and bounded prior comparison

- [*Probability that Triangle on Ellipse is Acute*](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/5014676/probability-that-triangle-on-ellipse-is-acute), posted 22 December 2024, asks for the probability at given semiaxes for three i.i.d. true-perimeter samples; it does **not** formulate Hall's conjecture for this law. The January 2025 answer by Christophe Boilley already expresses the probability using an orthogonal chord and an arclength integral. That conditioning idea and the circle baseline are prior. Nor does a plotted simulation prove strict global extremality.
- [The preceding local result, #212](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/212), treats true boundary measure in every fixed ambient dimension and proves the ellipse's strictly negative quartic variation near the circle. Its independent [review](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/29ce062427f5ac3f1bdc05250233eaccef77b64b/research/hall-true-area-ellipsoid-local/REVIEW.md) already derives the single-triangle integration domain used here and the fourth coefficient by Fourier arithmetic. This manuscript reuses that integral; the global ingredient is a rational uniformly controlled truncation on the middle range, together with a simple bound in the thin range. The local coefficient is not a new result of the global manuscript.
- [#201](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/201) proves a monotone comparison for *affine pushforwards*: its sample law is uniform in the original parameter rather than in the stretched perimeter. It supplies relevant geometry but not the present global theorem. [Akopyan–Edelsbrunner–Nikitenko](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2021.1980459) also explicitly distinguish their ellipsoidal homeoid law. [Hall (1982)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3213533), [Khan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05913) and [related work on Hall's comparison](https://www.proofatlas.ai/collaboration/halls-random-triangle-conjecture/) concern volume/interior sampling or affine deformations, not the true-arclength global claim. [#179](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/179) concerns isotropic radial distributions, not arclength on stretched ellipses.
- **Current external search, 19 November 2026.** Alongside reading the original public question and answer and the above primary sources, checked published works and preprint-index results for acute/obtuse inscribed random triangles, elliptic boundary, uniform perimeter/arc length or arclength, maximizers, and the authors' names; also searched French and English variants. No general strict true-perimeter comparison for all axis ratios was identified. This negative search result is bounded, not a guarantee of priority; no claim of an exact elliptic-function closed form or monotonicity for every pair of aspect ratios.
