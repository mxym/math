# Independent review: the two-spectrum Taylor-coefficient result

**PASS.** The main proof (frozen pre-typesetting text SHA-256 `f87b54c22fa6aa7981260631aab0900f9b450bbbac85d6e3b5d96c451d46b7ea`) proves both singular partial-sum comparisons in size two, their direct-sum corollaries, and the stated signed odd-order classification under the global two-spectrum hypothesis. No general-dimension claim outside these classes or additional condition is assessed here. The public [proof](PROOF.md) was typeset from that text and checked against it; its distribution digest is in [SHA256SUMS](SHA256SUMS). This is an independent internal AI review, not external peer review.

1. **What Zhang asks.** [ArXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07286v1) distinguishes Problem 1.1 (the decreasing **signed** eigenvalues of $H^k$ compared with the **singular** values of $Q_k$) from Conjecture 5.5 (signed eigenvalues on both sides, using $R_k$, for $k\ge5$). The [final publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379526004416) independently confirms that the original comparison is now **Problem 1.2**, that Section 5 gives a two-dimensional counterexample to the signed intermediate test at order five, and that the higher-order original question is left open in general. The final version explicitly states unitarily invariant norm consequences for $k=3,4$. The quartic theorem is numbered 4.3 in v1, 4.2 in the journal preview. The low orders are not contributions of the small-block result. A separately documented [Pauli counterexample for every odd order at least five](https://www.trureturing.org/research/zhang-2026-taylor-coefficient-weak-majorization/) is also prior; this review did not recompile its Lean formalization.
2. **Classical scalar input.** In [Forrester–Thompson, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.2008), (2.5) and the paragraph after (2.6) explicitly compare each even coefficient after introducing a common scaling variable. With radii $a,b$, normalized correlation $\rho$, and $h^2=a^2+b^2+2ab\rho$, this is exactly Jensen's inequality for the moments of $X\in\{(a+b)^2,(a-b)^2\}$ with probabilities $(1\pm\rho)/2$. Multiplying both generating functions by a scalar exponential explains the signed trace defects after any scalar shift. This is previous mathematical input, not a new all-order trace estimate. The identification of the odd projection follows independently by trace cyclicity: $F'(t)=\tau(K e^{taU}e^{tbV})$, with $K=aU+bV$, so $\tau(K E_j)=F^{(j+1)}(0)$. An inequality between complete exponentials without the termwise argument would be insufficient.
3. **Centered moments, including degeneracies.** Traceless Hermitian matrices in dimension two square to a scalar matrix. Noncommutation implies $a,b>0$ and $|\rho|<1$, hence $h^2=(a-b)^2+2ab(1+\rho)>0$. The centered exponentials are sums of an identity term and a multiple of the respective symmetry. Taking their Hermitian part leaves only the identity term at each even order: $U V+V U=2\rho I$. Each odd term is traceless; by the derivative identity its projection on $J=K/h$ is $M_{j+1}/h$ at order $2j+1$. The zero atom $a=b$ is harmless, with $M_0=1$ separately. Strict Jensen at $j\ge2$ holds exactly as used, since the two distinct atoms have strictly positive masses.
4. **Both Ky Fan tests.** Write $u=\tau(R_k)$ and $v=\tau(JR_k)$. For $c\ge0$ all powers of the spectral-midpoint shift carry nonnegative binomial weights. The (even, odd) estimates lower bound $(u,v)$ by the half-sum and half-difference $(U_0,V_0)$ of $(c+h)^k,(c-h)^k$. Both bounds are nonnegative, including when $c=0$. Testing against the rank-one projection $(I+J)/2$ gives $\|R_k\|_\infty\ge u+v\ge(c+h)^k=\|H^k\|_\infty$. Next $I$ and $J$ both have operator norm one, so trace-norm duality gives $\|R_k\|_1\ge2\max(u,v)\ge2\max(U_0,V_0)=\|H^k\|_1$. This uses a second independent test when $k$ is odd and $c<h$: a trace bound alone cannot give the required trace norm. For $c<0$, reflecting both inputs multiplies each degree-$k$ matrix by $(-1)^k$; it preserves singular values but must not be used to assert a signed statement at odd $k$. Every Ky Fan norm contracts under Hermitian symmetrization by the triangle inequality, giving the second leg for all matrices.
5. **Signed classification.** At $c\ge0$ the rank-one test and the ordinary trace test give the two signed prefix sums. Reflection proves the even orders for $c<0$. For odd $k\ge5$ and $c<0$ the trace-defect binomial coefficients have negative sign at every nonzero moment defect; the fourth centered moment defect is strictly positive, appears for every such $k$, and therefore forces a strict negative full signed difference. This proves necessity and sufficiency, not strict failure of all prefixes. The signed full-sum sign alone is a consequence of the scalar prior; it is the positive prefix tests that complete the classification.
6. **Direct sums.** With at most two eigenvalues in each matrix, a nontrivial pair can be written as scalar shifts and positive multiples of Hermitian symmetries. For $W=UV$ we have $UWU=W^*$. For each nonreal conjugate eigenvalue pair, $x,Ux$ give mutually orthogonal two-dimensional reducing blocks, and both restricted symmetries have eigenvalues $+1,-1$. The $W=\pm1$ spaces commute and split into common eigenlines. This is the classical [Halmos two-projections decomposition](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1969-0251519-5). All noncommuting blocks inherit the **same** global $c$; in larger dimensions this need not equal the normalized trace $\operatorname{Tr}(H)/n$. The largest $r$ entries of a concatenation are obtained by optimal allocation of $r$ slots to blocks; this works for negative entries too, as the total is exactly $r$. Full-sum trace defects add without cancellation in the adverse case. For arbitrary common blocks of size at most two, only the singular comparison is asserted without the global midpoint assumption.
7. **Independent replay.** The standard-library script uses Gaussian-integer matrices and $Q_{j+1}=AQ_j+Q_jB$. It compares the actual largest singular values and full trace norms on both legs via exact quadratic-surd comparisons, as well as both signed prefixes. All 9,324 matrix/order cases through order 18 passed, including 1,393 predicted strict signed trace failures. This checks sample instances only; the proof is analytic.
