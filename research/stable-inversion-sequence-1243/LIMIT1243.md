# A positive partition series for the stable inversion sequence of 1243

Let $c_k=\lim_{n\to\infty}|\{\pi\in S_n:\pi\text{ avoids }1243,\ \operatorname{inv}\pi=k\}|$. Here avoidance is classical, not consecutive. We give an explicit positive multiple series determining every $c_k$, the sequence requested in Problem 8.3 of Claesson, Linusson, Úlfarsson and Verkama (arXiv:2604.01143v2). It does not assert a product of independent one-dimensional partition series or solve the separate coefficientwise 1324 monotonicity problem.

**Formula.** For $m\ge2$ form the following finite list $D_m$. Choose equally large subsets of $[m]$ for the nonrecord positions and the nonrecord values. Write their values in decreasing order in the chosen positions; place the complementary values in decreasing order in the remaining positions. Retain only choices where the nonrecord positions are exactly the entries with a smaller earlier entry, and there is at least one such position. Write $u$ for the resulting word. Define $t_i$ ($0\le i\le m$) to be the smallest value of a nonrecord among the first $i$ entries of $u$, or $\infty$ if there is none. Set $s=t_m$ and $I=\{0\le i<m:t_i<\infty\}$. Then
$$
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{k\ge0}c_k q^k =
\sum_{m\ge2}\;\sum_{u\in D_m}q^{\operatorname{inv}u}
\sum_{(a_i)\in\mathbb N_0^I}
\frac{q^{\sum_{i\in I}(m-i)a_i}}
{\prod_{j=s}^{m-1}\bigl(1-q^{m-j+\sum_{i\in I:\,j<t_i}a_i}\bigr)}.}
\tag{1}
$$
The expression involves just finite subsets, nonnegative integers and geometric series; $\operatorname{inv}u$ is the usual number of reversed pairs in the two explicitly written decreasing lists taken together. Every fixed coefficient needs only $m$ satisfying $\binom{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}2+\binom{\lceil m/2\rceil}2\le k$. The series converges absolutely for $|q|<1$. The first 18 entries (starting at zero inversions) are
$$1,2,5,10,20,37,66,114,193,317,511,810,1265,1946,2956,4437,6592,9699.$$

**Stable ambient space.** Append a fixed maximum to a finite permutation. This cannot create a 1243, whose last entry is not the maximum, and adds no inversions. Hence the injections identify the stable objects with finite-support permutations of the positive integers avoiding 1243, extended by an increasing tail of fixed points. To see that each inversion count stabilizes to a finite number it suffices to prove the finite-coefficient bounds below: the finite-n sets increase to the union, and any finite subset of the union is already present for large enough $n$.

**Boundary and filling.** Draw a point at each $(i,\pi(i))$. Call it black if it does not strictly dominate an increasing pair of earlier points (both coordinates must be strictly greater). Otherwise call it white. The black points form a downset in the southwest order; white witnesses can always be chosen black: in the finite southwest set below a white point choose a chain of maximal length; its first two elements are black (otherwise the chain could be lengthened). In particular whether a cell dominates a pair can be tested on black points alone. The black set of a finite-support permutation is finite because every sufficiently far tail point dominates two earlier tail points. In position order, its pattern $u$ cannot contain 123 since then the last point has a pair below it. It must contain 12, since there are white points with black witnesses. In any 123-avoider the nonrecords decrease: if two nonrecords increased, a smaller earlier entry before the first would extend the pair to a triple. The records also decrease, and conversely a word made of these two decreasing subsequences contains no 123. Thus $D_m$ above indexes exactly the permitted patterns without multiplicity.

Interleave the black positions with $a_i$ white positions after exactly $i$ black ones ($0\le i<m$), then an infinite white position tail. In value order do the same with $b_j$ white values after exactly $j$ black values ($0\le j<m$), followed by an infinite white value tail. This determines the black points' actual positions and values. The admissible white positions must have $i\in I$ unless they are in the final tail, where $t_m=s<\infty$. In a row following $i$ black entries white values are eligible precisely above the actual black value of rank $t_i$, so values below black rank $s$ will never be eligible: $b_j=0$ for $j<s$. **There are no further feasibility restrictions** on the finite $a_i$ and $b_j$.

Indeed, assign each white position in order the least unused white value eligible in its row. The supply is unbounded in every admissible row. Once all black positions are past, every remaining white value is eligible by the restriction $b_j=0$ for $j<s$; the rule therefore gives a bijection, eventually the identity. Each designated white point dominates a black pair. No previously inserted white point is southwest of a black point, because its earlier black witness pair would then also be southwest of that black point, contradicting 123 avoidance in $u$. Hence the black labels remain correct (a putative pair below a black point can only be black, again impossible). The greedy choice is forced in a 1243-avoider: if it skipped a smaller eligible white value destined for a later row, the witnessing black increasing pair in the current row, the current larger entry, and that later smaller value form 1243. Conversely if a 1243 occurred in the constructed permutation, its final two points are white; already at the first of those two rows its later smaller value was eligible, since the initial increasing pair below that value can be chosen black in the southwest rectangle strictly left of the current row and strictly below that later value (take the first two of a longest chain in that rectangle). This contradicts greediness. This proves the bijection in both directions.

**Mark the inversion weight.** Black/black inversions number $\operatorname{inv}u$. No white point is southwest of any black point. A white row in gap $i$ precedes $m-i$ black points, all of which it inverts. A white value in gap $j$ is smaller than $m-j$ black values, all of which occur before it. These two counts are disjoint and account for all mixed inversions, with tail gaps having zero coefficient. For a white entry in a row of threshold $t_i$, the white inversions with it as first endpoint are exactly the remaining smaller, ineligible values. Earlier thresholds were at least $t_i$, so all white values in gaps $j<t_i$ remain unused; every smaller eligible value has already been used. The number is $\sum_{j<t_i}b_j$ (zero in the final tail). Consequently the weight exponent is
$$\operatorname{inv}u+\sum_i(m-i)a_i+\sum_j(m-j)b_j+
\sum_{i,j:\,j<t_i}a_i b_j.$$
Summing independently over the $b_j$ gives (1).

**Finiteness and analytic meaning.** The two decreasing lists in $u$ contribute at least the displayed balanced sum of binomial coefficients to its inversion count. Every gap variable has positive linear cost. This proves finite coefficient extraction. For fixed $0<r<1$ ignore the nonnegative cross charge: each gap sum in either dimension is bounded by $\prod_{h\ge1}(1-r^h)^{-1}$ since the linear costs in each dimension are distinct positive integers. There are at most $4^m$ pairs of subsets to try, so the quadratic lower bound on the base energy yields absolute and locally uniform convergence on $|q|<1$. There is no enumeration of 1243-avoiders hidden in the formula.
