# Sources, overlap and scope

## Problem and closest contemporary work (including 1 July–19 November 2026)

* Borga, Féray and Winkler, [*Permutons*, arXiv:2611.12104v1 (9 November)](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1#S8.SS3). Section 8.3 Problem 6 asks for permuton LDPs for uniform members of exponential classes. Problem 8 asks, among other things, how the sampling entropy of a singular permuton relates to local counts and rate functions. The present family-specific answer is not a general LDP for all pattern-avoidance classes.
* Budzinski, Dubach, Féray, Kammoun and Maïda, [full April preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23751v1) and [AofA 2026 proceedings](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.AofA.2026.18), already give speed-$n$ large deviations for uniform permutations avoiding a fixed length-three pattern, alongside constrained Mallows results. Propositions 4–5 in the full version concern the underlying path/measure parameters; Corollary 20 explicitly contracts to permutons for 321-avoidance. Their work is an actual permuton LDP and an important methodological predecessor: related spatial parameterization, integration of local costs and contraction, but via conditioned Dyck paths rather than finite geometric-grid traces. Neither speed $n$ nor contraction for pattern classes is claimed new. Even the *possibility* that catropy and the typical shape's neighborhood exponent differ is not new: the uniform 321-avoiding permutation has an increasing permuton limit but Catalan order counts, while independent samples from the increasing permuton have entropy zero.
* Maga, [*On the Sampling Entropy of Permutons*, Random Structures & Algorithms, July 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.70086); [preprint, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18518). Gives the sampling rate for graphs of finite piecewise smoothly measure-preserving functions, including stars up to transposition. This is directly overlapping for those examples, not the counting LDP for uniform gridded classes. [Maga–Szepessy, v1, 9 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12789) instead use order patterns of successive iterates of an interval map, not independent permuton samples.
* Falgas-Ravry, Markström, Räty and Skerman, [*On first-order limiting probabilities for small permutation classes*, v1, 25 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34328): Theorems 1.2–1.4 concern logical convergence for TOTO first-order sentences. Theorem 1.3 concerns signed permutation-matrix grids. Their local logical events and asymptotic probabilities are distinct from the weak-topology neighborhood counts and rate in this manuscript.
* Borga, Das, Mukherjee and Winkler, [*Large deviation principle for random permutations*](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnad096), analyze the LDP for permutations induced by sampling a fixed base measure (and Gibbs variations); that law is not uniform counting of a geometric class. The log base just fixes the factor $\ln2$. [Mogul'skii's classical path LDP](https://doi.org/10.1137/1121035) already gives integrated local rate functions for iid walks. With no commutations, the counting profile reduces to the familiar multinomial letter-entropy case; the trace quotient is the issue in general.

## Structural and counting inputs

* Albert, Atkinson, Bouvel, Ruškuc and Vatter, [*Geometric grid classes of permutations*, Trans. AMS (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2013-05804-7), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.6319). Sections 4–7 give refinement, order-preserving word coding, and gridded trace equivalence. Theorem 8.1 gives regular encodability and rational enumeration. **Theorem 10.3 already identifies every geometrically griddable class with a finite union of grids allowing isolated dot cells**. The extension from whole-grid LDPs to subclasses here is consequently a short bounded-deletion reduction; we do not claim the decomposition. Global rational counts alone are not local large-deviation rates.
* Hàn, Kiwi and Pavez-Signé, [*Quasi-random words and limits of word sequences*, §8.1, Theorem 32](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.03664), provide the finite-alphabet measurable-word-profile limit theory and ordered word sampling. See also [Hoppen et al., *Limits of permutation sequences*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2012.09.003). The compact profile space is prior. The note makes explicit its geometric rank pushforward and proves the entropy and count formulas along its fibers.
* [Cartier and Foata's monograph](https://irma.math.unistra.fr/~foata/paper/pub11.html) provides the partially commutative monoid inversion formula, hence the reciprocal weighted clique polynomial. [Bevan, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.4246) gives geometric class growth from the matching polynomial of the double refinement. Neither the trace generating series nor the overall growth is claimed here. The weighting, type entropy duality, and dyadic concatenation keep spatial frequency profiles visible at exponential scale.
* Alshammari and Bevan, [*On the asymptotic enumeration and limit shapes of monotone grid classes of permutations*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/15958), derive growth for fixed cell proportions and deterministic limit shapes for connected **monotone** grids, including exponential concentration estimates in §3 (in particular Proposition 3.9). These conclusions overlap where monotone and geometric griddings coincide, notably forest grids. Their fixed-cell and typical-shape results should not be described as absent. They are not a weak-topology good rate on all profiles in arbitrary geometric grid classes and their hereditary subclasses.
* This repository's [segment-uniform catropy manuscript](../geometric-grid-catropy/PROOF.md) already gives the homogeneous iid trace rate, strict entropy-rate loss for a connected noncomplete alphabet, insertion/interiority, and the optimization of the **segment-uniform** model. Those trace statements are inputs here. The extension concerns *all* limit permutons of the class via heterogeneous profiles, not merely a new proof for uniformly populated segments. The comparison of local counts with iid entropy uses the resulting gap throughout the connected nonstar limit class, not just at one previously studied deterministic limit.
