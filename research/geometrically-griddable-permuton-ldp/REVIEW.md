Scope is uniform permutations in whole finite geometric grid classes and those classes' permuton limit closures; not every monotone grid, subclass, or class of exponential growth.

* Finite-alphabet measures with fixed atomless uniform time marginal are compact. The strip-cumulative map gives uniform marginal rank coordinates, with zero-width/height cases never sampled. On a shared strip, sampled ranks respect time or its reversal outside null ties. The exact finite sample pattern law from profile inputs is continuous, since only equal times can obstruct continuity, and the weak permuton topology is characterized by finite pattern densities. The word step-profile differs from its empirical permuton only on same-rank-interval sampling collisions, yielding a uniform vanishing error. Dyadic approximation and integer count rounding realize arbitrary profiles along finite class sequences, while compactness supplies all limit profiles.
* Fixed-count trace concatenation is injective even for many blocks, because same-letter chains reconstruct the occurrences allocated to each block and restriction of dependent pair orders determines each trace. The finite-alphabet number of bin types is polynomial for each fixed partition. The local upper exponent is by definition upper semicontinuous; repeated blocks give a lower bound through all sufficiently large lengths and imply concavity by concatenating approximating types. The weighted clique polynomial reciprocal counts traces with positive weights by Cartier–Foata; its radius equals the smallest positive root. Maximizing exponent over polynomially many types and using compactness gives the pressure equality. Closed concave duality applies at boundary types as well.
* For step profile sampling, conditional bin counts give independent IID subwords in the bins, and their trace concatenation loses only the entropy of per-bin counts, hence o(n) for fixed bins. In a coupled letter edit the old/new edited letters plus every letter's count in each intervening unchanged piece recover the old restriction in each piece using same-letter chains; there is no reliance on choosing the true old representative from all trace linear extensions. In reverse the encoding works symmetrically. Counting weak compositions gives the claimed log-cardinality bound and Jensen gives a modulus tending to zero with integrated TV. Dyadic approximation, continuity of h, and boundedness give the integral catropy formula for every profile; it is invariant across fibers because the ordinary ungridded sample law is.
* For counting, the dyadic Jensen upper approximations decrease to the integral of e using bounded reverse Fatou and e upper semicontinuous. Hence the fiber maximum exists and is upper semicontinuous after the continuous compact contraction. In the upper bound the sets counted are all traces having some suitable representative; any represented empirical permutation in a closed set must eventually have such a near-fiber representative (indeed all its representatives do), by compactness and uniform approximation. The finite cover and bin type overhead are subexponential in n. In the lower bound the fixed selected counts in each bin permit distinct products to be recovered by same-letter chains, so no exponential loss before forgetting the rank gridding; that last loss is polynomial. Bin cumulative tolerances give weak control sufficient to fit inside the fixed open target. Thus both bounds have rate log g−S, with no illicit interchange of the n limit with growing bins. Normalization is base two, and a constant maximizer of e realizes rate zero.
* For connected nonstar original grids, the refined graph either stays connected noncomplete or splits into two isomorphic noncomplete components; unrestricted profiles may use unequal halves, but the alphabet-only strict gap from #219 applies to each component independently. Thus max h is strictly below log g while max S is log g. This is a genuine mismatch on rate-zero limit shapes, not an assertion that catropy always computes local enumeration or that all rate-zero shapes have nonvanishing limiting probabilities.
