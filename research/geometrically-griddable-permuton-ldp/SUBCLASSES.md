# Corollary: all infinite geometrically griddable permutation classes

Let $\mathcal D$ be an infinite hereditary permutation class contained in a finite geometric grid class. This is the established meaning of *geometrically griddable*; it need not be the whole containing grid. By [Albert–Atkinson–Bouvel–Ruškuc–Vatter, Theorem 10.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.6319), $\mathcal D$ is a finite union of geometric grid classes of $\bullet$-isolated matrices with entries $0,\bullet,\pm1$. Each dot is a single optional point in its cell, so deleting at most the number of dots leaves a pattern drawn on the other segments. This decomposition is entirely prior. Let $M_i$ be their undotted cores (replace all dots by zeros); ignore all-zero cores, whose dotted classes have bounded size. At least one nonempty core remains. For each such $M_i$ let $S_i$ be the unnormalized local exponent from [the whole-grid theorem](PROOF.md) (equal to $-\infty$ outside its limit class), and let $g_i=\mathrm{gr}(\mathrm{Geom}(M_i))$.

**Corollary.** For a uniform length-$n$ member of $\mathcal D$, the empirical permuton satisfies a speed-$n$ LDP in the weak topology with good base-two rate
\[
 I_{\mathcal D}(\mu)=\log_2(\max_i g_i)-\max_i S_i(\mu),
\]
infinite when all $S_i(\mu)=-\infty$. The limit permutons of $\mathcal D$ are exactly the union of the limit permutons of its cores. Their finite sampling catropies are given by the profile integral of any representing core. The corollary does not assert a finite strict gap for every subclass, nor a result outside the geometrically griddable family.

**Proof.** Every core class is contained in its dotted class and hence in $\mathcal D$; we may elect not to use a dot. Thus for every open set of permutons the lower unnormalized log-count bound at the identical order $n$ is at least the maximum of the finitely many whole-grid lower bounds. There is no divisibility or padding requirement; each nonempty core has members of every positive order.

Conversely every sufficiently long member $\sigma$ of $\mathcal D$ becomes a member $\tau$ of its associated core after discarding $j\le B$ entries, where $B$ is the largest number of dots in any of the finitely many components. A fixed order-$(n-j)$ pattern has at most $\binom nj^2 j!$ such one-step extensions to order $n$: specify the added positions, their values, and the bijection between these sets. In particular the total multiplicity over $j\le B$ is polynomial. Uniformly in these pairs, the distance between $\mu_\sigma$ and $\mu_\tau$ tends to zero in a compatible weak metric. Indeed a uniformly chosen fixed $k$-subset in $\sigma$ hits one of the deleted entries with probability at most $kB/n$, and conditional on avoiding them its relative pattern is that from a uniform $k$-subset of $\tau$. Passing from subset to permuton sample costs only the standard interval-collision $O_k(1/(n-B))$. A summably weighted pattern metric proves the assertion simultaneously for all pairs; compactness then gives the limit-set statement.

For closed $F$ and fixed $\epsilon>0$, all contributing cores eventually lie in its closed $\epsilon$-neighborhood $F^{\epsilon}$. Up to the polynomial factor just bounded, the upper count is bounded by the sum of the core counts in $F^{\epsilon}$ at orders $n-j$, $0\le j\le B$. Because $(n-j)/n\to1$ uniformly and there are only finitely many pairs $i,j$, the normalized log-count limsup is at most $\max_i\sup_{F^{\epsilon}}S_i$. Compactness and upper semicontinuity allow $\epsilon\downarrow0$, giving $\sup_F\max_i S_i$ (with the empty set handled trivially). The open lower and closed upper bounds, applied also to the whole space, give the normalization $\lim n^{-1}\log_2|\mathcal D\cap S_n|=\log_2\max_i g_i$. Subtracting yields the LDP. The maximum of finitely many upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous, so the rate is good. The catropy statement follows from the identified union of limit classes and item 1 for each core.

This corollary uses no presumed equality between regular-language rational enumeration and a spatial large-deviation principle. The earlier classification reduces this LDP to the whole-grid counting theorem; the decomposition itself and the counting growth of individual cores are not claimed here.
