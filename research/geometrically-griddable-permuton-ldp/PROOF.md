# Local enumeration, sampling entropy and a large-deviation principle for geometric grid classes

Let $M$ be a nonempty finite signed grid matrix, $\mathcal C=\mathrm{Geom}(M)$, and $g=\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal C)$. Equip the compact space of permutons with its weak topology, equivalently convergence of all finite pattern probabilities. Write $\mu_\sigma$ for the empirical permuton of a finite permutation (uniform mass in the corresponding permutation squares), and $\mathcal P_M$ for the limits along orders tending to infinity of members of $\mathcal C$. Logs are base two. Standalone definitions of the two entropy functions and the map in the formulas follow below.

**Theorem.** Choose a consistently oriented presentation, using the standard double refinement if necessary. Let $A$ be its alphabet of nonzero cells. A profile $p$ is a measurable function from $[0,1]$ to the probability simplex on $A$, up to a.e. equality. It determines a permuton $\Phi p$ via ordered sampling; its image as $p$ varies is exactly $\mathcal P_M$. For a probability vector $u$, let $h(u)$ be the entropy rate of the trace of an iid word with letter law $u$, and $e(u)$ the fixed-composition counting entropy defined in §2. Then:

1. For **every** $\mu=\Phi p$ the sampling catropy exists, is finite, and is $C(\mu)=\int_0^1 h(p(t))\,dt$. The formula gives the same value for every representing profile.
2. Set $S(\mu)=\max_{\Phi p=\mu}\int_0^1 e(p(t))\,dt$ for $\mu\in\mathcal P_M$ and $S(\mu)=-\infty$ otherwise. For uniform $\sigma_n\in\mathcal C\cap S_n$, the empirical permutons satisfy a full speed-$n$ large-deviation principle with good rate function $I(\mu)=\log_2 g-S(\mu)$ in base two (multiply by $\ln 2$ with natural logarithms). Equivalently $S(\mu)$ gives the limiting exponential neighborhood-count rate as radii decrease, using either the inner limsup or inner liminf in $n$.
3. These entropies need not agree: if the original nonzero cells form a connected nonstar (adjacent means sharing a strip; a star lies in a single strip), then $\max_{\mu\in\mathcal P_M} C(\mu)<\log_2 g=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal P_M} S(\mu)$. Thus even at a zero of the LDP rate, $\log_2 g-C(\mu)$ can be strictly positive. This distinguishes iid sample entropy from local counting throughout this family. No formula for arbitrary small permutation classes is asserted.

## 1. Word profiles and their permutons

Use the finite oriented-grid encoding of Albert–Atkinson–Bouvel–Ruškuc–Vatter. A word $w$ of length $n$ encodes a gridded permutation as follows. The left-to-right order groups letters by column. Within each column they occur in word order or reverse word order, as prescribed by its orientation. The bottom-to-top order similarly groups by row and uses its orientation. Reading these two orders gives the ungridded permutation $\pi(w)$. Every gridded member of the oriented class is represented, and two words represent the same gridded object iff they represent the same trace: consecutive letters in distinct rows and columns may commute. Equivalently the trace is specified by the ordered subsequences on each dependent pair of letters (equal or sharing a strip), with own-letter chains ordering repeats. The number of traces over any one ungridded permutation is at most $(n+1)^d$, where $d$ is the number of rows plus columns, since it suffices to supply grid dividers in the two rank orders. Double refinement preserves the ungridded class; in this proof no constraints relating the masses of the two halves are imposed, since all finite members of the class are under consideration.

View a profile as a measure $p_a(t)\,dt$ on time times the finite discrete alphabet, with uniform time marginal. These measures form a compact metrizable set in the weak topology. One compatible metric is the maximum over letters and times of the absolute difference of their cumulative masses: all the cumulative functions are 1-Lipschitz. Discrete words have step profiles $p_w$ equal to the pure letter on each rank interval $[(i-1)/n,i/n)$. This is the finite-alphabet word-limit model; see Hàn–Kiwi–Pavez-Signé.

For the law $p$, draw $(t,a)$ with those joint masses. In its column $j$, let $F_j(t)=\int_0^t\sum_{a'\in j}p_{a'}(s)ds$ and let $w_j=F_j(1)$. Set $x$ to the total width of preceding columns plus either $F_j(t)$ or $w_j-F_j(t)$ according to the column orientation. Define $y$ in the same way from the row. The resulting pushforward $\Phi p$ has uniform marginals by the continuous distribution function transform in each positive strip; zero-mass strips cost nothing. Within a shared strip the order is a.s. exactly the time order or its reversal, since uniform strip coordinates have no ties. Consequently every $k$-sample from $\Phi p$ is obtained by drawing $k$ iid time-labelled pairs, sorting by time and applying the word encoding. The output word-pattern probabilities are continuous in $p$ for its weak topology: product measures converge weakly and the order conditions have only time ties as possible discontinuities. This proves continuity of $\Phi$.

For any length-$n$ word $w$, the empirical permuton of $\pi(w)$ and $\Phi p_w$ differ uniformly by $o(1)$ in a compatible permuton metric as $n\to\infty$: in a fixed $k$-sample both induce the same subpermutation law conditional on all $k$ rank intervals being distinct, failing with probability at most $\binom k2/n$. Use e.g. a summably weighted metric on countably many pattern probabilities to obtain the uniform statement. Every $\mu\in\mathcal P_M$ thus arises by compactness. Conversely every profile is a weak limit of finite words: first approximate on equal dyadic time blocks, then round the counts on each block with error at most a constant per label, letting the mesh decrease after the words grow. Since all code images lie in the class this proves $\Phi(\mathcal W)=\mathcal P_M$, where $\mathcal W$ is the profile space. Limits need not have uniform mass on straight segments. In fact the displayed map puts their support on a finite union of Lipschitz parametrized curves (one for each letter), so these permutons are singular.

## 2. The entropy of traces with prescribed letter frequencies

Let $T(v)$ count the traces with exact nonnegative integer count vector $v$, and $|v|=\sum v_a$. If two consecutive blocks have prescribed vectors $v,w$, concatenation of their traces is injective: among the ordered occurrences of each letter the first $v_a$ belong to the first block, and restricting dependent-pair orders recovers both traces. Thus $T(v+w)\ge T(v)T(w)$. One has $1\le T(v)\le |A|^{|v|}$.

For $u$ in the closed simplex put $F_n(u,\delta)=\max\{n^{-1}\log T(v):|v|=n,\ \|v/n-u\|_1<\delta\}$, with maximum of the empty set $-\infty$, and $e(u)=\inf_{\delta>0}\limsup_n F_n(u,\delta)$. This agrees with the corresponding definition using liminf. Indeed for any fixed $\delta,\eta>0$ pick one block type $v$ with $\|v/|v|-u\|_1<\delta/2$ and $\log T(v)/|v|>e(u)-\eta$. At all sufficiently large $n$, concatenate $\lfloor n/|v|\rfloor$ blocks of this type and a fixed word of the remaining length. The resulting type is within $\delta$ of $u$, and its rate has liminf at least $e(u)-\eta$. This proves the claim. In particular every length can be used in a sufficiently large block.

The function $e$ is bounded, nonnegative, upper semicontinuous and concave, including its boundary. For upper semicontinuity use $B(u',\delta/2)\subset B(u,\delta)$ if $u'$ is close to $u$, then let $\delta\downarrow0$. For concavity, for each large $n$ and two arbitrary target types take blocks of lengths $\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor$ and $n-\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor$ approximating the two target vectors and their entropies within arbitrary prescribed tolerances, using the liminf construction. Concatenation with these fixed counts is injective. Let the tolerances tend to zero. The cases $\lambda=0,1$ are identities.

A finite algebraic pressure specifies this ingredient without enumerating any permutations. Set $P(\theta)=\log (1/R_\theta)$, where $R_\theta$ is the smallest positive zero of the weighted clique polynomial
\[
   \sum_{Q}(-z)^{|Q|}2^{\sum_{a\in Q}\theta_a},
\]
over all subsets of pairwise commuting distinct letters including $Q=\varnothing$. Classical Cartier–Foata inversion identifies its reciprocal with $\sum_v T(v)2^{\theta\cdot v}z^{|v|}$; equivalently, inclusion–exclusion on the nonempty set of possible final letters gives the coefficient recurrence. By positivity and finite exponential bounds the radius of this series is its first positive pole (Pringsheim). Then
\[
 P(\theta)=\max_u(e(u)+\theta\cdot u),\qquad
 e(u)=\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb R^A}(P(\theta)-\theta\cdot u).
\]
For the first equality use that length-$n$ types number at most $(n+1)^{|A|}$: take a convergent subsequence of maximizing types for an upper bound, and the local lower construction for each $u$ for the reverse bound. For the second use closed concave duality on the simplex, extending $e$ by $-\infty$ outside. No differentiability or irreducibility is assumed. In particular $\max e=P(0)=\log g$ by gridding multiplicities or Bevan's formula.

## 3. Sampling: the integral of the iid trace rate

Let $L_n(u)$ be the entropy of a trace obtained by projecting a length-$n$ iid $u$-word. Subadditivity and prescribed-count recovery on blocks give $0\le L_n(u)/n-h(u)\le |A|\log(n+1)/n$ uniformly up to simplex faces: recover each of $k$ consecutive length-$n$ traces from the whole trace and their block count vectors, then let $k\to\infty$. Hence $h$ is continuous and bounded. Gridding costs only $O(\log n)$ bits for every sampled word law.

First suppose $p$ is constant on finitely many intervals, with values $u_j$ and lengths $b_j$. Given the vector $(N_j)$ of their sample counts, the sorted letters produce independent iid subwords with laws $u_j$. The vector of subtraces determines the total trace by multiplication; conversely the total trace and the per-letter block counts determine all subtraces. Entropies thus differ from $E\sum_jL_{N_j}(u_j)$ by at most $O_{A,b}(\log(n+1))$, including the information to specify $N_j$. Since $EN_j=nb_j$, the limiting rate is $\sum b_jh(u_j)$.

For general $p,q$ couple independent sample pairs using common times and maximal letter couplings at each time. Let $K$ be the number of unequal letters, $EK=n\eta$, where $\eta=\int \mathrm{TV}(p(t),q(t))dt$. The symmetric entropy difference of the two projected length-$n$ traces is at most
\[
  \log(n+1)+2n\eta\log|A|+|A|\,n\eta\log(e_0(1+1/\eta)),
\]
where $e_0$ is Euler's number and the last term is zero at $\eta=0$. To reconstruct the second trace from the first, give $K$, the old and new letters at the $K$ ordered changes, and the counts of each unchanged letter in the $K+1$ intervening blocks. For each fixed letter its total unchanged count is known from the old trace and the list of old deleted letters, so this last information takes at most $\log\binom{n+K}{K}$ bits per letter in the conditional-entropy bound. Own-letter chains, the old changed letters and the supplied counts determine the original block boundaries on every dependent-pair projection. Recover the unchanged traces and concatenate with the new letters. The same code works conversely. Use the binomial bound $(e_0(1+n/K))^K$ and concavity of $x\log(e_0(1+1/x))$; information bounds here are log-cardinalities, not the lengths of rounded binary encodings. Dyadic step averages of $p$ converge in $L^1$ and a.e.; the modulus just proved and bounded continuity of $h$ transfer the integral formula to all $p$.

## 4. Counting: the integral of the fixed-type rate

Write $E(p)=\int e(p(t))dt$. For equal dyadic partition $\mathcal D$ define $E_{\mathcal D}(p)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal D}|J|e(\bar p_J)$, where $\bar p_J$ is the average on $J$. These are upper semicontinuous in the weak topology (there are no atoms at bin boundaries). Jensen makes them decreasing under refinement and bounds each below by $E(p)$. Almost everywhere convergence of the dyadic averages to $p(t)$, upper semicontinuity of $e$ and bounded reverse Fatou then give $E=\inf_{\mathcal D}E_{\mathcal D}$. Thus $E$ is upper semicontinuous on compact $\mathcal W$, and $S$ in the theorem is upper semicontinuous with attained fiber maxima.

We give the two bounds for unnormalized counts. Fix $p$ and $\epsilon>0$. Choose a fixed dyadic partition with $E_{\mathcal D}(p)\le E(p)+\epsilon$. For each bin pick a tolerance $\delta>0$ so that all sufficiently long blocks within that type tolerance have rate at most $e(\bar p_J)+\epsilon$, using the definition with limsup. There is a weak open neighborhood $U$ of $p$ such that any $p_w\in U$ has these bin averages strictly within half the chosen tolerances. Cut words at nearest rank boundaries to the grid times, with a uniform $O(|\mathcal D|/n)$ error in the bin averages. All traces with *some* representative in $U$ then have count at most $2^{n(E(p)+2\epsilon+o(1))}$: there are only polynomially many tuples of bin types; at a fixed tuple the products of their numbers of traces give an upper bound. Multiple descriptions of the same trace do not invalidate this direction.

For a closed permuton set $F$, cover $\Phi^{-1}(F)$ by finitely many such $U$ centered there. All sufficiently large permutations of the class with empirical permuton in $F$ have every word representative in their union: otherwise a compact subsequence outside the union has image in $F$ by the uniform approximation in §1 and continuity. If the fiber is empty there are eventually none. The finite cover proves log-count limsup divided by $n$ is at most $\sup_{\mu\in F} S(\mu)+2\epsilon$. Let $\epsilon\downarrow0$.

For open $G$ fix $p$ with $\Phi p\in G$. By compactness/continuity, for some sufficiently fine *fixed* dyadic partition and sufficiently small tolerance, every profile within that tolerance of $p$ on each bin average belongs to a neighborhood of $p$ whose images remain at a fixed positive distance from the complement of $G$ (if $G$ is all permutons there is no restriction). Indeed coarse cumulative masses control the cumulative functions up to twice the bin mesh and the bin-average tolerance, by their 1-Lipschitz property. For each large $n$ cut into integer bin lengths differing from $n|J|$ by at most two. In each bin choose a type within half the tolerance whose rate is at least $e(\bar p_J)-\epsilon$, by §2. All products of any traces of these fixed types are **distinct** by count recovery; concatenate one representative per block. Their word profiles satisfy the chosen bin constraints, and the empirical permutation lies in $G$ for large $n$ by §1. At most $(n+1)^d$ traces collapse upon forgetting the gridding. The log-count liminf per letter is therefore at least $E_{\mathcal D}(p)-\epsilon\ge E(p)-\epsilon$. Take the supremum over the fiber of $G$ and let $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Together with $\lim n^{-1}\log|\mathcal C\cap S_n|=\log g$ this proves the asserted LDP. The shrinking-ball formula follows by applying the two bounds to open and closed balls and using upper semicontinuity and compactness. The maximum $\log g$ of $S$ is achieved by the image of a constant maximizer of $e$.

Finally $\max_{\mu\in\mathcal P_M} C(\mu)=\max_{u\in\Delta(A)}h(u)$: each integral is bounded by that number, and constant profiles achieve it. For a connected nonstar the refined dependence graph is connected noncomplete or a pair of isomorphic connected noncomplete copies, with the same component growth. The preceding segment-uniform note proves for each connected noncomplete dependence alphabet that any maximizing iid law is interior (neighbor insertion, its §2), and that its entropy rate is then strictly below log growth (guarded switches, §3). Distinct components' conditional rates average by their total masses. Apply these *trace-alphabet* statements to unrestricted refined probabilities; they do not require that the halves of an original cell have equal mass. This proves the strict inequality throughout the entire permuton limit closure, without identifying its maximal-canonical-entropy or typical permuton explicitly.

The results concern whole finite geometric grid classes, not all monotone grid classes, arbitrary subclasses or all exponentially growing permutation classes. An LDP with this rate is not a claim that every zero dominates on the unscaled probability level.
