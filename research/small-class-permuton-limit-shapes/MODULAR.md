# Modular extension: arbitrary regular inputs and MSO orders

Retain the two-order, one-output-point-per-input-position interpretation of SHAPES.md. Allow arbitrary regular input languages, and allow both comparisons to be MSO-definable rather than FO-definable. There is an integer $P\ge1$ such that on every class of lengths modulo $P$, either there are eventually no accepted words, or the uniform accepted word has a permuton limit in law of exactly the finite-mixture, uniform-simplex, uniform-line-segment form (3)–(4) of SHAPES.md. Periodicity cannot in general be suppressed: on a unary input language take word order for the first output order, and word order or its reversal according to the parity of the length for the second. The small-class *full*-sequence statement uses the FO argument with primitive original components, not a full-sequence claim for arbitrary MSO.

**Finite return recorder.** MSO on finite words gives a common finite recognising monoid $M$ for the two flagged tests; aperiodicity is not assumed. In every cyclic SCC of the trimmed input automaton choose $v$ and let $S$ be the monoid of products of unflagged closed walks at $v$. Choose an idempotent $e$ in the minimal ideal. Then $G=eSe$ is a group with identity $e$: the $e=xty$ sandwich and finite monoid argument in (1) of the main note show that every $t\in eSe$ is a unit. There is a nonempty walk $B$ realizing $e$. Indeed, if the only representative so far were empty then $e=1$, and $S=G$ is a finite group, so a positive power of any nonempty closed walk realizes the identity.

Build a labelled directed graph $\widehat C$ on the states $(x,t)$ reachable from $(v,e)$ by internal edges, updating $(x,t)\mapsto(y,t\eta(a))$ on edge $x\xrightarrow{a}y$. This finite graph is strongly connected. From a reached state choose any continuation back to $v$ followed by $B$; the resulting recorder value is $g=e s e\in G$, since the full underlying walk started at $v$ and returned there. There is a walk $B R B$ with product $g^{-1}$ (every element of $G$ has the form $e\eta(R)e$ for a closed $R$). Following it gives $gg^{-1}=e$ and returns to $(v,e)$. Conversely all included states are reachable by definition. Every outgoing internal edge from a lifted vertex projects bijectively onto the outgoing internal edges from its underlying vertex. Thus the original positive Perron vector pulled back is a positive eigenvector of the lift with the same eigenvalue, necessarily its spectral radius.

Use a **lifted** buffer: the edge walk $B$ beginning at $(v,e)$. It ends there too. If two disjoint occurrences of this lifted buffer enclose the lifted walk $R'$, its projection $R$ satisfies $e\eta(R)=e$, because the enclosing states coincide at $(v,e)$. Consequently $\eta(BRB)=e$. We need compare neither arbitrary underlying occurrences of $B$ nor arbitrary loops in $S$ by themselves.

**No change of sampling law.** Fix a route of the input automaton as in the main proof. For the specified entry vertex $u_i$ in each cyclic SCC choose once and for all an underlying internal path $D_i:v_i\to u_i$. Start that segment in the fixed lifted vertex $(u_i,e_i\eta(D_i))$. The original path determines a unique lifted path from that entry. Refine the route by its finitely many possible lifted exit states, independently resetting the recorder at the next component. Acyclic singleton SCCs have internal length zero and need no recorder. The resulting finite partition counts each accepted word once, and conditioned on its internal lengths and its refined route its cyclic segments are independent uniform lifted bridges. The buffer table in the main note always multiplies **original input letter products**, including across different components; the lift merely chooses erasing markers within each segment.

**Supported residues and length asymptotics.** Let $P$ be a common multiple of the periods of all cyclic lifts (take $P=1$ if there are none). Refine each route by the congruence of each cyclic internal length modulo $P$, retaining only compatible residues for its specified endpoints. In every irreducible component with period $p\mid P$, an endpoint pair has a unique allowed residue mod $p$, and on that residue has paths at all sufficiently large lengths; on a compatible finer residue $j\bmod P$, $(A^j)_{xy}/r_i^j$ tends to a strictly positive constant and is bounded for all lengths. This follows from the ordinary primitive Perron theorem on the cyclic classes of $A^p$. Incompatible lengths have no paths. For each refined route the total length residue is now fixed, crossing edges included. Routes with no cyclic component supply only bounded lengths. If a total residue has no cyclic compatible route, it is eventually empty; otherwise each compatible cyclic route supplies words at every sufficiently large length in that residue, since one can fix admissible lengths in the other components and increase a single one by multiples of $P$.

For such a route put $r$ equal to its largest component radius, $d$ to the number of its radius-$r$ components. Along its total residue the asymptotic path count is a positive constant times $n^{d-1}r^n$. Indeed the same proof as §1 applies, with critical lengths $j_i=\ell_i+P h_i$ and with the continuous-simplex composition count changed asymptotically by the constant $P^{-(d-1)}$. The finite offsets disappear after division by $n$; noncritical particular paths have summable majorant $r^{-b}$. Among the finitely many compatible routes at this residue only the lexicographically largest pair $(r,d)$ survives. Importantly the active value of $r$ need not be the maximal radius on **other** residues. Conditional on a surviving refined route the critical fractions and subcritical paths have precisely the independence and Dirichlet law of §1.

**Periodic bridges.** The fixed-endpoint conditioning probability $(P_C^j)_{xy}$ of a compatible large length tends to $p\pi(y)>0$ for the lifted Perron chain with transition matrix $P_C$. For finite initial/terminal windows the admissible middle transition also tends to $p$ times the corresponding stationary mass: its endpoint classes agree automatically after deducting the window lengths. The common $p$ cancels, giving the independent forward and stationary-reversed boundary windows in §1. The shift of the two-sided stationary irreducible finite Markov chain is ergodic even if the chain is periodic (one can also apply the ergodic theorem successively to the primitive $p$-step cyclic classes and average over one period). Each lifted buffer occurs within a bounded window with uniformly positive probability from every lifted vertex by irreducibility. Consequently the truncation/bridge argument, deterministic prefix count limits (2), marked tables, and (3)–(4) apply without further changes. This yields on each supported residue a genuine full-sequence-in-that-residue distribution, not only a subsequential compactness assertion. No external sampling of the recorder or stationarity assumption on crossing states is involved.
