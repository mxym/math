# What was already available and what this note proves

**The input coding and its hypotheses are prior.** [Vatter, *Small permutation classes*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/pdr017) establishes the structural small-growth threshold; associated rational language structure was developed by Albert, Ruškuc and Vatter. Markström and Räty, [arXiv:2610.22218v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22218v2), are the direct input: Theorem 36 transfers free point variables and preserves first-order formulas, Theorem 37 gives unique representatives, Theorem 62 proves aperiodicity of each dominant strongly connected component of the canonical ungridded language, and §6 combines the restrictions for the given hereditary class. Theorem 1(3–5) already gives count asymptotics and logical convergence (modular for MSO). We use only the fully proved forward coding, not their sketched inverse or alternative encoding. [Falgas-Ravry, Markström, Räty and Skerman, arXiv:2610.34328v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34328v1), Thms 1.2–1.4, determine the finite polynomial-growth sentence-probability spectrum and density for exponential growth. Their §§3.1–3.3 block-length description already explains simplex masses in polynomial families: this case is not presented as a novel general principle.

**Existing geometric results.** [Bassino, Bouvel, Féray, Gerin, Maazoun and Pierrot](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2022.108513), Thm 3.3, obtain deterministic four-segment X shapes in the linear finite-substitution-specification regime under their hypotheses, with different limits in the branching regime. Their earlier [substitution-closed work](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/993) includes the expected-pattern-density criterion for convergence of random permutons. [Alshammari and Bevan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00607v4), Thm 3.10, establish deterministic segment shapes for connected monotone grid classes; the subsequent discussion anticipates simplex-random weights for disconnected gridded models. [Borga, Féray and Winkler, §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1#S5.SS2) discuss these and the random rectangular shape of square permutations. None of the word-interpretation hypotheses or all-small-class scope should be read into a theorem stated for those other families. The repository’s [#228](../geometrically-griddable-permutation-limit-shapes/README.md) proves the analogous full distribution for arbitrary geometrically griddable classes using their literal signed-cell coordinates and canonical word section; it is a direct conceptual and technical antecedent, not a theorem about arbitrary two-order interpretations. The sets of hypotheses are different. In particular, knowing individual examples of deterministic X's, random rectangles, or independent uniform-simplex phase lengths is not new here.

**Probability, semigroups, and logical interpretations.** The one-dimensional interpretation of outputs by regular/MSO tests is classical: [Engelfriet–Hoogeboom](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9906007) characterize MSO word-to-word transductions by deterministic two-way transducers; [*Lexicographic Transductions of Finite Words*, §2](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.50) distinguishes interpretations of positions from more general tuples or sets and from operations using copies. These are structural transduction statements, not random-permuton laws. We use the classical Büchi–Elgot–Trakhtenbrot MSO/regular equivalence, Schützenberger–McNaughton–Papert FO/aperiodic equivalence, minimum-ideal group structure, and [Parry's Perron Markov law](https://doi.org/10.2307/1994009); the needed erasing and recorder claims are proved in the text. [Goldwurm and Lonati](https://lonati.di.unimi.it/lavori/stacs05.pdf) study pattern counts in multicomponent rational models; their framework precedes the route/simplex argument. [Nešetřil–Ossona de Mendez](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07627) formulate the general structural-limit viewpoint via free-variable sampling probabilities. Logical convergence of sentences is not itself a geometric law. Indeed a weaker existence conclusion can also be approached by counting marked words to obtain expected sampled-pattern densities and then applying the existing moment criterion. What is at issue here is the distribution of the whole limiting measure: the two-flag table determines **affine ranks**, with deterministic frequencies, finite boundary randomness and simplex weights. The MSO recorder proves the corresponding statement without assuming primitive input or aperiodic marked tests.

**Search scope.** On 19 November 2026, retrospective and July–November searches covered small, monotone and geometric classes, substitution specifications and word models of permuton limits; logical transductions of regular words, order/origin semantics, multicomponent additive statistics and structural limits. We checked the arXiv submission histories and specific statements of the direct October papers (Markström–Räty v2, revised 19 October; Falgas-Ravry et al. v1, 25 October) and November’s [permuton survey v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12104), 9 November. No matching full finite-segment distributional result for all small classes, or for the general one-point-per-position MSO setting, was identified in this bounded search. This is not a determination of priority.
