# Source and scope comparisons

Checked 19 November 2026. This is a bounded comparison of primary statements and searchable records, not a priority certificate.

* O. Arizmendi and G. Salazar, [*Large Area Convex Holes in Random Point Sets*](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1024184), SIAM J. Discrete Math. **30**(3) (2016), 1866–1875. The publisher supplies the bibliographic information and abstract; for the full statements compare the [author-hosted manuscript](https://www.ifisica.uaslp.mx/~gsalazar/RESEARCH/convex.pdf) and [arXiv:1506.04307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.04307). Their Theorem 1.1 concerns homothets of a single shape (coefficient 1); their Theorem 1.2 instead uses unrestricted convex holes, and Theorem 1.3 requires a convex polygon with sample vertices. Section 6 explicitly proposes 1 for **both** latter leading coefficients. Their §5 already localizes prospective sample vertices near the ends of a large empty strip and constructs a quadrilateral for the lower coefficient 1. They observe that order bounds extend to higher dimensions. None of those prior contributions is claimed here.
* Y. Peres and B. Yang, [*Maximal volume of convex bodies avoiding random points and lacunary dilates*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09790), v1 7 October, revised v2 13 October 2026. **Theorem 1.3 in this distinct paper already gives the exact almost-sure iid constant d for unrestricted empty convex bodies in any fixed convex ambient body.** Its [§1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09790v2#S1.SS2) explicitly discusses the disproof of Arizmendi–Salazar's unrestricted assertion, citing their Theorem 1.2, and [§3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09790v2#S3.SS2) constructs thin targets and estimates their pairwise overlap and second moments. Its iid lower bound counts targets with empty interiors, not simplices whose vertices are sample points. Its upper bound directly gives ours. We modify the lower construction by permitting occupied corners and minimizing volume over a matched corner selection; a block occupancy event covers all intermediate sample sizes, without monotonicity of the sample-vertex maximum.
* J. Balogh, H. González-Aguilar and G. Salazar, [*Large convex holes in random point sets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.0805), consider the maximum **number of vertices**, not area. I. Bárány and Z. Füredi, [*Empty simplices in Euclidean space*](https://www.renyi.hu/~barany/cikkek/BarFurEmpty.pdf), study numbers of empty simplices and volumes of a single random simplex; neither is the largest volume among sample-vertex empty simplices. A. Dumitrescu, S. Har-Peled and C. D. Tóth, [*Minimum convex partitions and maximum empty polytopes*](https://jocg.org/index.php/jocg/article/view/2927), address deterministic partitions and empty-volume approximation; arbitrary Steiner vertices are allowed in the Steiner problem. These results are not iid sample-vertex leading constants.

**Search limits.** We checked the original source by theorem, author-hosted copy, publisher metadata and the recent arXiv submission history. Targeted searches used the paired statistics, original authors, Peres–Yang and its identifier, “PolyMax”, largest-area empty triangle, and maximum-volume empty simplex with uniform/random/sample vertices. Separate searches restricted to July–November 2026 and to the period after the October revision identified the Peres–Yang result as the materially overlapping theorem. We did not find an explicit sample-vertex lower coefficient d in the checked sources. Search does not exclude unindexed, unpublished, differently phrased, or later results.
