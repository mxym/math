# Source scope and attribution

## The open question being answered

- Jiaxin Guan, Daniel Wichs and Mark Zhandry, [Multi-Instance Randomness Extraction and Security against Bounded-Storage Mass Surveillance](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/409), full version revised June 5, 2023, [public author PDF](https://ntt-research.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Multi-Instance-Randomness.pdf), [TCC 2023 proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-48621-0_4), pp. 93–122. In the full version, §1.3 (printed p. 7) explicitly asks whether every standard seeded extractor is also a multi-instance extractor. §1.2 (printed p. 6) already identifies the concurrent Dinur–Stemmer–Woodruff–Zhou universal-hashing result as multi-instance extraction in their sense. §3.1 Definition 3.1 (printed p. 15) defines statistical distance on the **complete vector of public seeds and outputs**; the replacement set is jointly distributed with the source, with a pointwise size guarantee, and the outputs at unselected coordinates are retained. Section 3.2 defines hinting and gives the ordinary strong-extraction to list-decoding implication (citing [V+12], Proposition 6.25) and separately defines hinting. The individual row cap of this note is a direct special case, not a new list-decoding bound. Lemma 3.3 and Corollary 3.4 prove the desired kind of guarantee for their Hadamard and Reed–Muller families by a different argument. The question in §1.3 does not specify numerical single-instance parameters. This note answers it quantitatively using those ordinary parameters, not by deriving the extra hinting property.
- [AICR c/0103](https://aicr.info/c/0103/) is a way to locate the above question, not a substitute for the paper. Its extractor-independent numerical wording must account for the ordinary extractor's min-entropy threshold and its own error. Sections 5–6 of this note specify sufficient bounds and give abstract parameter-limit examples. For ordinary extractors with k=O(m+log t+log(1/ε)) and sufficient single-instance accuracy, the asserted order of near-rate loss follows. No claim says every high-threshold or inaccurate extractor works at arbitrary source rate, or claims the sufficient single-instance error bound is necessary. The parameter-free near-rate and positive-fraction readings, if quantified over all source rates for every ordinary strong extractor, are false; Section 6 isolates the threshold issue with a family whose ordinary error tends to zero.

## Closest prior work and what changes

- Itai Dinur, Uri Stemmer, David P. Woodruff and Samson Zhou, [On Differential Privacy and Adaptive Data Analysis with Bounded Space](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/171), [full version](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/171.pdf), [EUROCRYPT 2023 text](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10423331). The full version §6.1 Definition 6.1 uses all-subset block density. Its §6.4 Lemma 6.4 (printed pp. 25–26; published pp. 61–63) applies independent **universal hash functions** to dense blocks, bounds their joint public-seed output by a collision argument, and does not state the arbitrary-strong-extractor case. §6.2 Lemma 6.2 and Appendix D Lemmas D.1–D.2 then combine density restoration and extraction with classical leakage. In the universal case, only identical inputs can be collision exceptions. The new argument here bounds the product collision moment using the ordinary extractor's known cap on higher-than-baseline collision neighbors of **each** input, without pairwise universality. This use is the missing hypothesis substitution. The existing collision-to-TV strategy and density decomposition are attributed to prior work. One recovers the universal-hash bound by taking each exceptional set to be just the identical input, without incurring an unrelated extractor threshold.
- Sandro Coretti, Yevgeniy Dodis, Siyao Guo and John P. Steinberger, [Random Oracles and Non-Uniformity](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/937.pdf), EUROCRYPT 2018, Claim 2 and Appendix A of the full version. This gives the equal-alphabet dense/bit-fixing decomposition with a budget proportional to deficiency plus log(1/γ), and attributes earlier variants to Göös et al. and Kothari–Meka–Raghavendra. Section 4 here re-proves this known maximal-overweight-assignment construction in weighted heterogeneous form, preserving a deterministic label of the original source. It is not the claimed new extractor mechanism.
- Mika Göös, Shachar Lovett, Raghu Meka, Thomas Watson and David Zuckerman, [Rectangles Are Nonnegative Juntas](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~diz/pubs/junta.pdf), SIAM J. Comput. 45(5):1835–1869, §2.2 Definition 11 and Lemma 13. Their blockwise two-source inner-product gadget uses two independent dense sources and controls the output marginal by parity bounds; it does not assert the operative public-seed joint law for an arbitrary ordinary strong seeded extractor. Pravesh Kothari, Raghu Meka and Prasad Raghavendra, [Approximating Rectangles by Juntas and Weakly-Exponential Lower Bounds for LP Relaxations of CSPs](https://doi.org/10.1145/3055399.3055438), STOC 2017, is a predecessor of the density-decomposition step as cited in the two above full versions.
- Chi-Jen Lu, [Encryption against Storage-Bounded Adversaries from On-Line Strong Extractors](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00145-003-0217-1), J. Cryptology 17:27–42, and later neighboring work on bounded storage or everlasting privacy, use different models. This note only proves the classical extraction statement of Definition 3.1 and its explicit heterogeneous extension. It does not assert a shared-seed statement, a pointwise-in-seed statement, a quantum side-information theorem, a concrete security judgment, or an efficient good-index selector.

The targeted duplicate check examined the full and publication records of both 2023 papers, prior density restoration, the blockwise communication-complexity line, and subsequent titles citing the multi-instance extractor paper. No primary-source statement was located proving the same arbitrary-strong-extractor implication with public independent seeds. This is a scoped check, not an exhaustive priority claim.
