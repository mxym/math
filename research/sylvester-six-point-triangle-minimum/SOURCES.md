# Sources and scope

1. J.-F. Marckert and S. Rahmani, [*Around Sylvester’s question in the plane*](https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.12110), Mathematika 67 (2021), 860–884. Both extrema for five independent uniform points are prior. The [earlier preprint by Marckert](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03658) gives the conditioned vertical-comb split and one-sided chain recursion (eqs. (16)–(17)); these are explicitly reused, not claimed here. The four-point extrema are classical results of Blaschke.
2. P. Valtr, [*The probability that n random points in a triangle are in convex position*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01271274), Combinatorica 16 (1996), 567–573. The triangle formula for every n gives 91/900 at six. Computing a value in one fixed body does not minimize over bodies. Likewise [Marckert's disk formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3512) is not an unrestricted shape optimization.
3. S. Campi, A. Colesanti and P. Gronchi, [*Shaking compact sets*](https://usiena-air.unisi.it/handle/11365/6814) (2001), for the classical operation of moving parallel slices onto a hyperplane. The present contribution is the monotonicity certificate for this particular six-point probability, not the introduction of the operation.
4. L. Morin, [regular polygon asymptotics](https://doi.org/10.1017/apr.2024.63), Adv. Appl. Probab. 57 (2025), 811–870, introduction and Appendix B. The introduction distinguishes the solved four- and five-point comparisons from the n≥6 questions; Appendix B recovers Valtr’s formula. Morin's [general convex polygon asymptotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11706), published online in May 2026, is a large-sample result for a fixed polygon, not a fixed-six extremal theorem.
5. J.-F. Marckert and L. Morin, [*The Sylvester question in R^d: convex sets with a flat floor*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08456), v2 (August 2026). A fixed floor participates in the hull event; the Q(2) comparison for that event is not the six-iid event here. The related unrestricted d+2-point problem also counts only four points in dimension two. Their [bi-pointed hull paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26330) studies a specified triangle edge and the asymptotics of hulls conditioned on their number of vertices.
6. R. Cowan and S. N. Chiu, [technical report on convex quadrilaterals](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/richardc/quadtechnical.pdf), concerns a restricted class of sampling bodies and several distinct hull statistics. I. Bárány’s [limit theorem](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1022874826) is an asymptotic one for convex position. Neither comparison gives the claimed universal finite-six triangle minimum.
7. Campi, Colesanti and Gronchi's earlier [random-polytope moment theorem](https://www.openstarts.units.it/items/ac389773-c662-480b-986a-f7e12e2a9494) is about volume moments. Maximizing expected area in the triangle is a different statistical assertion from minimizing the probability of six extreme vertices.
