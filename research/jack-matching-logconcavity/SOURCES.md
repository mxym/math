# Scope and prior comparison (2026-11-19)

## Formulations and methods

* W. Y. C. Chen, [Log-concavity and q-log-convexity conjectures on the longest increasing subsequences of permutations](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.3392), Conjecture 1.4. His matching polynomial counts matchings by **maximum cardinality of a crossing**, not by total pairs of crossing arcs. The within-polynomial log-concavity statement is distinct from Conjectures 1.5–1.6 and from his later iterated q-comparisons between different polynomials.
* M. Bóna, M.-L. Lackner and B. E. Sagan, [Longest increasing subsequences and log concavity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08653), §4, [journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00026-017-0365-x). Their Conjecture 4.2, read in the typeset PDF, concerns the involution counts (letter i) on even-column shapes by ordinary LIS; their 4.3 has permutation counts (letter ℓ). They verify the former through 2n=80, and Theorems 4.4–4.5 address restricted shape classes. The two original statements credited here are not statistically interchangeable. BLS 1.2 concerns all involutions and is not proved here.
* W. Y. C. Chen, E. Y. P. Deng, R. R. X. Du, R. P. Stanley and C. H. Yan, [Crossings and nestings of matchings and partitions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0501230), establishes maximum crossing–nesting equidistribution. This classical result, not any equality of crossing and LIS, is what converts one Jack marginal to Chen's matching statistic.
* S. Kerov, [Anisotropic Young diagrams and Jack symmetric functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9712267), and S. Matsumoto, [Jack deformations of Plancherel measures and traceless Gaussian random matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.5619), supply the classical Jack framework. Particularly relevant are Matsumoto's Lemmas 3.1–3.2, which identify the two special parameters and the LIS/LDS statistics, and Lemma 4.1 for the finite-row gamma products. These identifications are **not new** here. Here θ=1/α in Matsumoto's notation.
* Y. Li, [Log-concavity of longest increasing subsequence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36483), v1, 27 October 2026 (no newer revision displayed when checked). Theorem 1.1 proves Chen 1.1 for ordinary permutations at every fixed size. Sections 4–5 introduce the ordered coupling, stationary adjacent-slice argument and monotone flux proof which we use. The displayed transition laws and marginal theorem in that version concern ordinary Plancherel, i.e. θ=1. What must change are the weights, all-parameter inequalities, strict birth comparison, and the two matching interpretations. An earlier [paper by Xia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18235) treats ordinary permutations for sufficiently large size, not the two matching distributions.
* J. Baslingker, M. Krishnapur and M. Madiman, [Log-concavity in one-dimensional Coulomb gases and related ensembles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15116), v2, Theorem 1, proves row marginal inequalities for a **size mixture** of measures with weights (f^λ)^β. Their β-deformation is not the fixed-size Jack–Plancherel measure used here; their ordinary Poissonized result by itself does not imply fixed-size log-concavity.
* A. L. L. Gao, M. H. Y. Xie and A. L. B. Yang, [Schur positivity and log-concavity related to longest increasing subsequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06382), study restricted shapes and Schur positivity. Iterated polynomial questions are distinct too: for instance [Liu's note on two infinite q-log-convexity conjectures](https://zenodo.org/records/23448071) concerns sign changes after iteration across sizes, not the coefficient inequalities proved here.
