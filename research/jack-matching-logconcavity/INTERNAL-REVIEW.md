For fixed positive θ the padded shifted coordinates are strictly ordered with gaps of magnitude at least θ. The only vanished down/up factors are precisely blocked rows, including x_L=0 and repeated zero parts; all allowable choices are strictly positive. The residues of P± at the distinct coordinates give the indicated products. Evaluating P+ at −θ uses the exact zero caused by x_L=0 and yields the up normalization. For P− the z^-2 coefficient gives the stated down normalization after subtracting the shift sum. The gamma recurrence gives the bond ratio (1) for both signs of the row coordinate difference; a legal move keeps all actual gamma arguments positive and avoids the potentially zero shifted denominator. The bond ratios and probability sums prove normalization and detailed balance, and the claimed mass is the known Jack–Plancherel measure in the specified convention.

At a tight row all new pair differences are no smaller and remain on the same side of zero as before, including with ties among unshifted parts. The down factors are nondecreasing and the up factors nonincreasing; up in the first row is strictly lower on the right due to its prefactor and strictly positive factors. Matching the entire left removal probability at every tight row, or the entire right addition probability in the up coupling, is simultaneous because each row's designated mass lies in its counterpart. If a tight coordinate is otherwise at risk, integrality of the slack gives one box of margin. The preserved relation survives the down then up steps. For mixed censorship, the first coordinate determines the only possible escape direction (left up, or right down); restoring the original offending diagram only moves its other coordinates in the safe direction. Each censored slice is irreducible by moving a lower removable box to a fresh bottom singleton, reaching the hook, and reversibility supplies the reverse. A stationary related pair consequently has the exact conditional marginals.

In adjacent slices, right growth forces left growth and left shrinkage forces right shrinkage. If the right diagram has a removable lower box, there is positive probability of a right lower removal, then necessarily a left lower removal, so the two predecessors maintain first-row tightness. Conditional on any such coupled removal, strict up-probability comparison gives strictly additional left-only growth. If the right is already a horizontal row it cannot increase, while the left hook can. Positive neighboring flux follows from hooks. Taking stationary averages and dividing the two detailed-balance flux identities gives the strict interior inequalities at fixed n, not strictness beyond the support.

For θ=2 the doubled-row hook coordinates are precisely (x_i+1,x_i), giving H(z)=z²(z²−1) and the matching factorials. For θ=1/2 the doubled-column hook coordinates are 2x_i and H(z)=z; duplication of gamma functions leaves only a partition-size constant. In a decreasing subsequence of a fixed-point-free involution, upper-diagonal pairs precede lower-diagonal ones and both groups define a nesting when their endpoints are sorted in the appropriate order. Conversely any nesting gives both groups in one decreasing subsequence, so LDS is twice the nesting number. RSK and crossing–nesting equidistribution convert the transposed doubled-row measure to maximum crossing. Thus the original Chen crossing conjecture and BLS fixed-point-free LIS conjecture are distinct, although both follow. Regular permutations at θ=1 and the ordered slice method are due to Li; do not conflate all involutions or squared weights with the result here.
