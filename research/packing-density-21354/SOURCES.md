# Sources and scope

* **Original proposal.** M. H. Albert, M. D. Atkinson, C. C. Handley, D. A. Holton and W. Stromquist, [*On packing densities of permutations*](https://doi.org/10.37236/1622), EJC 9 (2002), R5, §4, p. 19, already propose **both** four layers for the pattern with sizes $[2,1,2]$ and the symmetric proportions defined by the cubic $40x^3-32x^2+9x-1$. [Full text](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v9i1r5/pdf/). Their Theorem 2.2 gives the finite layered-maximizer reduction going back to Stromquist; the layered polynomial approach goes back to A. Price.
* **Earlier merge method.** P. A. Hästö, [*The packing density of other layered permutations*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v9i2r1), EJC 9(2) (2002), R1, §4. Lemma 4.1 already gives valley shape and endpoint-to-neighbor inequalities. Theorem 1.3 gives a four-layer optimizer for $[k,1,k]$ when $k\ge3$; it does **not** include $k=2$. Remark 4.2 discusses the obstacle at $k=2$ and a prospective five-layer bound; the text after Proposition 4.4 expressly leaves $[2,1,2]$ unhandled. The ten-layer bound here is a convenient input to a finite certificate, not a claim that Hästö's suggested bound is false.
* **Quantitative predecessor.** J. Sliačan and W. Stromquist, [*Improving bounds on packing densities of 4-point permutations*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/4263/pdf), DMTCS 19(2), §4 Table 2 and §5, revisited the 2002 cubic construction and gave a rigorous, separately computed flag-algebra upper bound for this five-point pattern. The displayed bounds agree to the quoted decimal digits; their equality was left open. See also [Sliačan’s Open University thesis](https://oro.open.ac.uk/55735/1/thesis.pdf), which lists the corresponding certificate. The result here proves the exact matching upper bound without claiming a new lower construction.
* **Known boundedness and its limits.** A. Kabela, D. Král’, J. A. Noel and T. Pierron, [*Density maximizers of layered permutations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01723), EJC 29(3) (2022), P3.56. Theorems 4 and 5 apply to $[2,1,2]$: all optimal layered permutons have finitely many layers and the number of layers of finite optimal layered permutations is uniformly bounded. Neither gives the four masses or the equality here. Their counterexample to the unrestricted $[a,1,b]$ assertion in Albert et al., Proposition 2.8, is why we do not invoke that general assertion. For other families see [D. Warren’s thesis](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/00/98/40/00001/warren_d.pdf), especially §3.2, and [Bastos and Coregliano](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07312) on patterns with an initial block of singleton layers.
