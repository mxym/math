# Sources and scope

- [BDGIKT] Dor Bitan, Zachary DeStefano, Shafi Goldwasser, Yuval Ishai, Yael Tauman Kalai and Justin Thaler, [*Sum-check protocol for approximate computations*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2152), EUROCRYPT 2026 / IACR ePrint 2025/2152. The ePrint index records 16 March 2026 as the last revision. The author's [public PDF](https://cs.nyu.edu/~zd2131/papers/25-sumcheck-eurocrypt.pdf) and this [public copy of the extended PDF](https://opencryptology.org/2025/2152/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf) retain Remark 3. Definition 3 is in §2.4; Remark 3 is immediately after Theorem 4.8 in the extended text (p.22); their Figure 1 counts one prover-and-verifier pair per round and verifies at the last challenge, consistent with their description of v rounds. The question is the **uniform precise** upper bound p^k for kr parallel copies of an r-round proof with RBR error p. Their example applications, computational settings, and protocol-specific error analyses are not being reanalyzed here. [AICR c/0077](https://aicr.info/c/0077/) indexes the same statement. The publisher's exact pagination is not used as a proof dependency.
- Noor Athamnah, Noga Ron-Zewi and Ron D. Rothblum, [*Linear Prover IOPs in Log Star Rounds*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1269), TCC 2025 with June 19, 2026 [ECCC revision #2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/090/revision/2/download/). Appendix B, Definition B.1 (pp.59–60) and Lemma B.2 (pp.60–61) explicitly use conjunction, surviving copy counts, independent conditioned challenges, binomial stochastic domination, and a prescribed threshold profile. This is the closest method antecedent; we do not claim the count argument or threshold profiles as new. The result there yields exponential bounds for a profile satisfying specified rapid-decay conditions, not the exact extremal cutoff optimization and necessity for arbitrary labels in (1). Their transcript ends with an additional prover message and they use a final consistency clause, rather than counting it as an extra random challenge.
- Ran Canetti, Yilei Chen, Justin Holmgren, Alex Lombardi, Guy N. Rothblum and Ron D. Rothblum, [*Fiat–Shamir From Simpler Assumptions*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/1004), 2018 six-author long version ([public PDF](https://ntt-research.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Fiat-Shamir-from-simpler-assumptions.pdf)). Proposition 5.5 already labels transcripts backwards using the same form of recursion with a fixed tolerance derived from ordinary soundness, as a sufficient construction. Corollary 5.7 converts ordinary soundness of independent repetition into an RBR bound; its footnote 27 uses all-accept with perfect completeness. The corresponding seven-author STOC 2019 paper (adding Daniel Wichs) is [*Fiat–Shamir: From Practice to Theory*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313276.3316380). The numbered results here refer to the 2018 long version. BDGIKT Remark 3 cites the corollary for the case of maximal separation. We do not claim backward labeling itself as new. Section 3 uses the maximal viable set to prove **necessity** for the extremizer, thereby determining the exact envelope from the one-round RBR profile. No claim that either bound dominates for every individual protocol with an additional ordinary soundness guarantee.
- Justin Holmgren, [*On Round-by-Round Soundness and State Restoration Attacks*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1261), IACR ePrint 2019/1261, uses the same challenge-prefix notion and relates it to a different security formulation. No operational game or reformulation of that theorem is needed for (1).
- Thomas Attema, Serge Fehr and Michael Klooß, [*Fiat–Shamir Transformation of Multi-Round Interactive Proofs (Extended Version)*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00145-023-09478-y), J. Cryptology 36:36 (2023), Theorem 5, is about the Fiat–Shamir transform for restricted special-sound families, not exact RBR certificate error of arbitrary base protocols. It notes the relevance of the count of copies still unresolved. Its underlying attack games are not used here.
- Yuanju Wei, Kaixuan Wang, Binwu Xiang, Xinxuan Zhang, Yi Deng, Xudong Zhu, Hailong Wang, Li Lin and Lei Wang, [*SNARKs over Small Prime Fields without Extension Field Multiplication*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/719), Sept. 14, 2026 revision, ASIACRYPT 2026, [full text](https://opencryptology.org/2025/719.pdf), §§1 and 1.2. This already describes the rough exponent ceiling m/r for independent repetition of its structured classical sumcheck model and studies a different, coupled verifier. We do not claim the exponent in (4) is new in that model, and do not analyze its actual polynomial protocol. The contributions here are exact minimax guarantee, tightness over arbitrary labels in a standalone random example, and uniform-vs-fixed-parameter resolution.
- [*TensorSwitch: Nearly Optimal Polynomial Commitments from Tensor Codes*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2065), [Aug. 2026 text](https://opencryptology.org/2025/2065/versions/20260814T054517Z.pdf), §2.5 and Remark 5.2, notes the round-dependent loss of ordinary-to-RBR amplification for its concrete commitment opening and instead uses a distinct oversampling construction.

A bounded duplicate search on 2026-11-27 included the source title, ePrint 2025/2152, and combinations of “round-by-round soundness”, “parallel repetition”, “exact”, “binomial”, “threshold”, and “general amplification property”. We identified the above antecedents, but did not find the exact universal formula, an arbitrary-label sharpness argument for it, or a resolution of the literal uniform inequality in the primary papers examined. This does not establish priority over unpublished work.
