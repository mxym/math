# Formulation, precedents and bounded overlap checks

1. **The precise question.** L. Rademacher, [*On the monotonicity of the expected volume of a random simplex*, Mathematika 58 (2012), 77–91](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579311002063), [author preprint, §4, discussion item 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.3944). Rademacher attributes to Meckes and Reitzner the nested-body random-polytope problem, notes that a pair-dependent threshold is immediate, and explicitly asks whether the threshold can depend on \(d\) alone. His displayed hull uses indices \(0,\ldots,n\); changing from that convention to the count of draws affects only the threshold by one. M. Beermann and M. Reitzner, [*Monotonicity of functionals of random polytopes*, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.08342), pose the dimension-only alternative for the expected unnormalized \(d\)-volume with \(n\) independent uniform samples. Their §3 concerns a different monotonicity in the number of points, not the containing body.
2. **Small samples and previous methods.** Rademacher's Theorems 1–2 establish some monotonicity and counterexamples for the single-simplex case \(n=d+1\), including dimension \(d\ge4\) counterexamples for expected volume. His Lemma 12 and underlying variation formula already compare a fixed boundary point with an ordinary sample in testing inclusion monotonicity. We do not claim that comparison principle as new. S. Kunis, B. Reichenwallner and M. Reitzner, [*Monotonicity of the sample range of 3-D data: moments of volumes of random tetrahedra*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01893), complete the three-dimensional counterexample. [Related random-simplex moment work](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.07295) is not a dimension-only large-sample threshold.
3. **Other nearby results.** [Bárány, Fradelizi, Goaoc, Hubard and Rote on random polytopes and the wet part](https://doi.org/10.5802/ahl.44) study missing mass and vertices. Uniform approximation and small-cap estimates are established random-polytope tools. [Devillers, Glisse, Goaoc, Moroz and Reitzner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.7020) study the expected face counts as sample size changes. [Zaporozhets and Moseeva (2025)](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/znsl7602) compare beta parameters and radially ordered random-vertex laws, not arbitrary nested convex bodies. [Ferudun, 2026, separate computational research note](https://eulersolve.org/papers/owr-15215-006/) addresses the size of the multiplicative loss for the weak, simplex-size inequality in growing dimension, not eventual monotonicity at fixed \(d\). Inclusion at fixed equal Poisson intensity would allow a direct coupling; in the present binomial model a prescribed count is redrawn separately in each body.
4. **External search (19 November 2026).** Checked the original sources and searched exact and variant phrases, including “expected volume,” “sample range,” “under set inclusion,” “eventual monotonicity,” “nested convex bodies,” “dimension-only threshold,” “sufficiently large sample size,” and combinations with the authors' names and identifying citations. No public proof or disproof with these uniform quantifiers was found. This is a bounded literature search, not evidence of a priority date.
