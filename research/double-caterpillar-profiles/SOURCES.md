# Sources and boundaries of the result

**The question and existing exact points.** Daniel Brosch and Diane Puges, [*Getting to the root of the problem: sums of squares for limits of trees*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-025-02305-1), *Mathematical Programming* **218** (2026), 505–561, published online 16 December 2025 ([arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12838)). Section 2.3 defines the tree-profile by simultaneous limiting densities, not by the existence of finite trees whose densities match exactly. Section 5.2 defines the double-caterpillar construction; (12) and (13) already give its two coordinates. Conjecture 1 there concerns every integer $k\ge4$ and every split proportion $p$. The authors already prove the balanced double-caterpillar upper-boundary points for $k=4,5,6$ and discuss other characteristic points and numerical certificates. The contribution considered here is proving the full proposed arcs for those three orders by inequalities valid on every limiting profile, and refuting the universal-in-$p$ assertion for every higher order. It is not a description of the entire two-dimensional feasible set.

**Branch methods and unconditional extrema.** Éva Czabarka, László A. Székely and Stephan Wagner, [*Inducibility in binary trees and crossings in random tanglegrams*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.07149), *SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics* **31** (2017), 1732–1750, introduced this leaf-induced density setting. They count subsets by their allocation among root branches (see in particular Proposition 2 for the balanced quartet); they determine extremal single-pattern limits for the even trees and caterpillars. The use of branch recursions here is not claimed as new. The present comparison ties the $E_6$ density to a prescribed second density. Audace A. V. Dossou-Olory and Stephan Wagner, [*On the inducibility of small trees*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/5804), bound single-tree inducibilities, including the different five-leaf shape $A_5$ whose branches are a leaf and a balanced quartet. These are not the fixed-$C_k$ profile questions.
