# Sources and scope

- Tianren Liu, Angelos Pelecanos, Stefano Tessaro and Vinod Vaikuntanathan, [*Layout Graphs, Random Walks and the t-wise Independence of SPN Block Ciphers*](https://opencryptology.org/2024/083/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf), IACR ePrint 2024/083 / CRYPTO 2023. Their Definition 3 defines closeness to pairwise independence as total variation of the output pair for every distinct input pair, under independent uniform round keys. The introduction and Section 6 prove the censored-AES bound and conjecture that adding the removed AES mixing layers does not hurt convergence. The paper itself poses an AES-specific statement, not the universal monotonicity principle refuted here.
- [AICR c/0090](https://aicr.info/c/0090/) is an AI-written adaptation of the 192-round AES conjecture. It explicitly isolates as a clean sub-question whether inserting a single invertible linear layer can ever increase the distance, and says that an artificial counterexample would show the informal monotonicity intuition cannot be used as stated. The present example answers only that sub-question; it does not resolve the AES conjecture.
- Martin Baignères and Serge Vaudenay, [*Proving the Security of AES Substitution-Permutation Network*](https://doi.org/10.1007/11502760_5), SAC 2005, and Liu–Pelecanos–Tessaro–Vaikuntanathan above are prior sources for difference-transition/Markov analyses of key-alternating SPNs. We claim no novelty for difference tables, whitening, or total-variation invariance under bijections.

A bounded search on 2026-11-27 used the source title and the phrases “censoring mixing layers”, “mixing layer increases statistical distance”, and “pairwise independence monotonicity”. It found the source conjecture, the AICR adaptation, and generic work on censoring monotone Markov chains, but no prior explicit SPN counterexample matching (1). This is not a priority claim.

The construction is deliberately tiny and artificial. It is a finite probability calculation about keyed permutations, not cryptanalysis, a distinguisher implementation, a key-recovery method, or a statement that any real cipher is insecure.
