# The Segre subquadrangle determines the Payne flock generalized quadrangle

**Theorem (Penttila–Storme Conjecture 5.6).** Let $q=2^n$ with $n$ odd, and put

$$\mathcal O=\{(1,t,t^{1/6}):t\in\mathbb F_q\}\cup\{(0,1,0)\}.$$

If a flock generalized quadrangle of order $(q^2,q)$ contains a subquadrangle isomorphic to the Tits quadrangle $T_2(\mathcal O)$, the ambient generalized quadrangle is isomorphic to the Payne flock generalized quadrangle. The proof is **computer-assisted**: its uniform finite claim is an equality of regular languages, checked to closure, not tests on finitely many field sizes. It does not require field-degree bounds, degree bounds on the last function, an assumed automorphism group, or a monomial clan.

The [original conjecture](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1997.2855) is on p. 40. The key statement proved [in full here](PROOF.md) is stronger in a specified Segre normalization: for **every** odd $n\ge3$, the possible last functions in the coefficient triple $(x,x^3,G(x))$ are exactly $G=x^5+C^2x+Cx^3$ over $\mathbb F_q$. The associated GQ is unique up to isomorphism. This does **not** imply that every flock representing that GQ, in arbitrary coordinates/basing, is projectively equivalent. These objects are known to differ: see [S. E. Payne’s survey, entry for the Payne herd](https://cage.ugent.be/~fdc/contactforum/payne.pdf).

## From the geometric problem to the fixed equation, and back

1. First let $q\ge8$. An order-$q$ subquadrangle of a flock GQ in even characteristic that misses the base point forces the classical ambient case and is $W(q)$: [Brown–Thas, Part I (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2003.12.006); in flock rather than dual notation see [Thas–Thas (2009), Theorem 7.5 and Remark 7.6](https://doi.org/10.2140/iig.2009.10.133). In even characteristic $W(q)\cong Q(4,q)$, and $T_2(\mathcal O)\cong Q(4,q)$ iff $\mathcal O$ is a conic ([O’Keefe–Penttila (2001), Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.2000.3119)). Our oval is not a conic: on substituting $(1,x^6,x)$ into a general quadratic, the six exponents are $0,12,2,6,1,7$. Their functions on the whole field are linearly independent, including at $q=8$ where $x^{12}=x^5$. Thus the subquadrangle contains the base point.
2. [O’Keefe–Penttila (2001), Theorem 8, p. 226](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.2000.3119) sends an order-$q$ subquadrangle through that point by an ambient automorphism to a standard herd subquadrangle; Corollary 12(i), p. 228, identifies the defining nonconic ovals of isomorphic nonclassical Tits GQs up to projective semilinear equivalence. The herd thus contains the prescribed oval up to that equivalence. [Penttila–Storme (1998), Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2, pp. 28–29](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1997.2855), or [O’Keefe–Penttila (2000), Lemma 10, pp. 182–184](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1005153406421), allows recoordinatizing the herd so the indicated monomial representative is the first coefficient, while preserving the **isomorphism class of the GQ**. The axis/order of the Segre power is relevant: we use $t^{1/6}$, not $t^6$ as a replacement for that coefficient.
3. The normalized coefficient triple is now $(t^{1/6},t^{1/2},a(t))$, $a(0)=0$, including its possible scalar multiplier in $a$. Substitute $t=x^6$ and $G(x)=a(x^6)$; this reparametrizes all $q$ items because $\gcd(6,q-1)=1$. For every two distinct parameters, their difference quadratic $(x+y)v^2+(x^3+y^3)vw+(G(x)+G(y))w^2$ must be anisotropic. Since $x+y$ and $x^3+y^3$ are nonzero, the standard characteristic-two quadratic criterion says exactly $T((x+y)(G(x)+G(y))/(x^6+y^6))=1$. Indeed a root would solve $z^2+z=(x+y)(G(x)+G(y))/(x^6+y^6)$, whose solvability is trace zero. This is the normalized clan criterion of [Penttila–Storme, equation (2)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1997.2855).
4. The [proof](PROOF.md) gives $a(t)=t^{5/6}+C^2t^{1/6}+Ct^{1/2}$. In the cone parametrization $(X_1,X_2,X_3)=(v^2,vw,w^2)$ of $X_1X_3=X_2^2$, the canonical Payne plane form uses $xv^2+x^3vw+x^5w^2$. Substituting $(v,w)\mapsto(v+Cw,w)$ changes just the last coefficient, to $x^5+C^2x+Cx^3$. This is induced by the invertible cone-preserving linear map $(X_1,X_2,X_3)\mapsto (X_1+C^2X_3,X_2+CX_3,X_3)$, leaving $X_0$ fixed. The normalized triples therefore give the standard Payne $P_1$ flock up to a projectivity. Reversing step 2 yields precisely the asserted GQ isomorphism.
5. **Small-order convention.** If $n=1$ is included, over $\mathbb F_2$ the sole normalized difference matrix has middle entry 1, and its two diagonal entries must have product of trace 1, so both equal 1. The only normalized clan is the Payne formula at this order, equivalently (1) at $(0,1)$ forces $G(1)=1$. The usual Payne geometry is classical at $q=2$ and agrees with the FTWKB geometry at $q=8$ ([Payne survey](https://cage.ugent.be/~fdc/contactforum/payne.pdf)). The nonconic and automaton arguments were used only for $n\ge3$.

## Reproducing the computer-assisted lemma

Use unoptimized Python 3.10+ with only the standard library, from this directory. All three programs reject `python -O` because assertions are part of the checks. Download the individual source files, not just this README:

```sh
python3 verify_language.py
python3 verify_reverse.py
python3 verify_field.py
```

* [`verify_language.py`](verify_language.py) generates every reachable relation by appending each of the four letters. It checks all **25,906** reachable comparison states and finds no discrepancy for any odd eligible length. A deliberately damaged target grammar is rejected on its explicit length-five negative control.
* [`verify_reverse.py`](verify_reverse.py) generates relations by prepending the new edges, reads in the opposite orientation, defines the comparison language from local binary digits rather than the first program’s allowed-letter pairs, and minimizes both DFAs separately. It compares the actual canonically numbered transition/acceptance tables, not just state counts or a hash. They agree on all **19** minimal states. SHA-256 of the canonical JSON table is `adfe996b3d8e62368fdcb8b1e72341f73e0f9d1e5e342118d3bf62fb9c4a4c95`.
* [`verify_field.py`](verify_field.py) is an independent small-order regression, **not the all-orders proof**. At $n=3,5,7$ it builds the homogeneous equations directly in all possible function values, checks every pair, the affine particular solution and all $n$ basis gauges, and finds ranks $18,150,882$ (nullities $3,5,7$). It then compares the literal finite-field trace-expansion kernel, the cyclic-threshold kernel, and the transducer acceptance for **every** integer pair $(e,b)$ at each order (56, 992 and 16,256 masks respectively). Exactly $n$ nonzero binary masks pass in each field, in the asserted positions.

## Prior results and scope

* This is a uniqueness/converse theorem, **not** a construction of a new flock or oval. [Penttila–Storme (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1997.2855) classify the possible *monomial oval types* in Theorem 5.1 and classify *monomial flocks* in Theorem 5.2. Neither assumption identifies every possible remaining coefficient of a clan with one Segre member. They explicitly pose the arbitrary ambient GQ converse in Remark 5.5 and Conjecture 5.6. [O’Keefe–Penttila (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1005153406421) handle translation ovals, distinguishing the then-undetermined Segre case. Their [2002 joint paper on herd automorphism groups](https://doi.org/10.1006/eujc.2001.0550) requires the relevant cyclic group of order $q-1$ to extend to the herd; the occurrence of the Segre oval alone supplies no such hypothesis.
* [Monzillo–Penttila–Siciliano (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05344) complete the quadratic-cone flock classification through $q\le71$, including $q=8,32$ but not all odd extension degrees. [Bartoli–Durante–Grimaldi (2024), Main Theorem, case 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-024-01365-4) gives an important prior result for an unrestricted type of low-degree ovoid. In their equation (3), $f_1(X,Y)=Y+X^3$, $f_2(X,Y)=G(X)$ yields precisely the trace condition here. Under $q>6.31(\max(3,\deg G)+1)^{13/3}$ their theorem forces $\deg G=5$; it does not treat arbitrary degrees. The present proof has no degree restriction. Earlier internal finite-range and bounded-degree results are not needed for this proof.
* A check on the original paper’s forward citations (OpenCitations and Semantic Scholar) and targeted searches for Segre–Bartocci herds, the conjecture number, Payne flock/subquadrangles, and recent flock classifications (including a cross-check for 2025–2026 papers) found no earlier general converse in the primary sources examined. Examples checked include [Bader–Ghinelli–Penttila on monomial flocks](https://doi.org/10.1006/eujc.2001.0503), [Penttila’s survey of oval configurations](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00022-003-1700-4), and [Cherowitzo–O’Keefe–Penttila on cyclic constructions](https://doi.org/10.1515/advg.2003.002). A literature search is not a proof of priority, and repository verification is not journal peer review.
