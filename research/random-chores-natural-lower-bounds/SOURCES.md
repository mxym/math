# Sources and scope checks

* **Primary problem.** [P. Manurangsi and W. Suksompong, SIAM J. Discrete Math. (2026)](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/full/10.1137/25M180161X), Theorems 1.1–1.2, Lemma 3.1 and §5 concluding discussion; [IJCAI 2025 version](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0444.pdf) and [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20704). Their lower bound uses items selected as favorite by two or more agents, requires only non-atomicity, and holds below approximately 1.1256 with a fixed gap. Their positive result uses a probability density bounded above and away from zero, at all $m\ge2n$. The journal paper explicitly distinguishes the difficulty of strengthening the all-law negative bound versus improving the positive result.
* **Own prior; important overlap.** Repository [#120](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/120) proves that no coefficient below 2 works for *every* PDF-bounded law. For each proposed coefficient the construction gives a fixed special law and a subsequence. It already contains the deterministic ownership induction and a finite-depth Poisson argument for lists of fixed length on active rows. Neither the induction nor the basic local-tree approach is claimed anew. The current draft improves the negative bounds for *all* sizes and for every law of the indicated classes: (i) stratum counting by singleton holders and bundle composition, and (ii) the actual row-length distribution for uniform and index-one cost tails. #120 also clearly acknowledges [Gan, Suksompong and Voudouris](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00468) on conflict deletion in the surplus-house setting, a different model. [Manurangsi and Suksompong, *Closing Gaps in Asymptotic Fair Division*, §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.05563) already settle the separate multiplicative $e$ threshold for assignments in that model. Its unassigned houses must not be conflated with our full partition into variable-size bundles.
* [Dickerson–Goldman–Karp–Procaccia–Sandholm (2014)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/8884) treat *goods*. The 2026 primary source, §1.2, distinguishes the favorite-item arguments in the goods and chores models. We do not claim first-moment bounding, random-list collisions, order-statistic inversion, geometric counts or Poisson thinning as new mathematical tools. [Pyke, *Spacings*, JRSS B 27 (1965), 395–436](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1965.tb00602.x) surveys classical uniform spacing constructions; we include the elementary normalization argument.
* **Current search.** The primary source, [Suksompong's publication list](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~warut/) and targeted exact/random chore, multi-rank first-choice, nonatomic, regular-variation and follow-up constant searches retrieved no publication matching these two lower bounds. Nov. 2026 exact *divisible* chore protocols, worst-case MMS, online EF1 and ex-ante or subsidized allocations are distinct. This is not a historical priority guarantee.
