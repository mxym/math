# Primary sources and contribution boundaries

* **Original problem.** [Aldous–Janson, *The Critical Beta-splitting Random Tree II*, v3 §6.2 Open Problem 16](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.02529v3#S6.SS2) asks about sums of powers of subtree sizes and gives expected-value predictions. It does not state or conjecture this precise centered transition. [Aldous–Janson III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09655) supplies the common exchangeable fragmentation; [Aldous–Janson–Li](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-ECP645) give the limiting harmonic occupation probabilities.
* **Critical-tree CLTs already known.** [Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel (BCM), v3, Theorem 1.1(i), Proposition 1.3, Corollaries 1.4–1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24821v3#S1.SS2) establish monotonicity, a general CLT assuming polynomial error bounds for linear mean/variance and a moment bound on the toll, and CLTs for fixed clade counts and total *continuous* tree length. Their general proposition already gives the entire p<1/2, p≠0 normal conclusion **once its elementary moment hypotheses are established**. Here are details of this attribution. The nonnegative excess identity of §1 in the main note shows for any toll |r_m|≤C m^a log^B(2m), 0≤a<1, B≥0, that its expected sum equals nρ+O(n^a log^{B+1}(2n)): on m≤n take the maximum of |r_m| in the positive excess sum O(log n/n), and on m>n sum a(m)|r_m|/m directly. For p<1/2 apply this first with a=max(p,0), B=0. The one-step conditional variance g_m is then O(m^{2a} log²(2m)). The variance of the full statistic is the expectation of the additive sum of g_m by orthogonality. A second use with 2a<1 gives Var S_n(p)=nσ_p²+O(n^{2a}log³(2n)); the mean, variance, positivity and deterministic toll m^p satisfy all conditions of BCM Proposition 1.3. The note provides a direct predictable-bracket proof too. Do **not** attribute a new CLT mechanism for this subrange to the present work. BCM's proposition is not a theorem for variances of order n log³n or n^{2p}log²n. [Svihla, October 2026, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00347v1#S2.SS3), gives more occupation asymptotics and another use of BCM for a fixed finite set of stopping clade sizes.
* **External path length at p=1.** [Aldous–Pittel, Theorem 1.2](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AAP2112) gives the mean expansion and the CLT for the edge-depth of a *single random leaf*. [Kolesnik](https://doi.org/10.1214/25-ECP658) reproves that tagged CLT by contraction. [Svihla–Lladser, Theorem 2.1 (10)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-026-01655-x) already gives the asymptotic variance of the tree-wide external path length, including the same leading constant as here. It is not a distributional limit for the centered realized external path length. The companion entropy result identifies the limiting random variable and proves it is nonnormal. The existence of classical fragmentation additive martingales and differentiation in their parameter are **prior** ([Bertoin–Rouault](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024610705006423); [Bertoin](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/148)). The argument concerns which limit appears for the discrete tree, not discovery of the general martingale or of the known variance.
* **Nearby fragmentation statistics.** [Fuchs–Steel, Prop. 10 and Thm. 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09270v2) prove a fixed sampled MRCA's depth law, used for a second-moment bound in the entropy companion. [Ma, extremal separation and clade dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14325) measures separation times and counts at given times. [Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel, extremal subtrees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24973) study extremal shapes. [The full beta family via homogeneous fragmentations](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18320v1) studies tagged discrete and continuous height, separations and related potential theory. [Bertoin–Martínez, *Fragmentation energy*](https://doi.org/10.1239/aap/1118858639) concerns a continuous fragmentation energy down to a **mass threshold**, rather than the sum of costs on each visible split in an n-label discrete restriction. [Bertoin's self-similar fragmentation area](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v9/09-02.pdf) determines the continuum area law used in the established p>1 range.
* **Other tree distributions.** There is a well-established normal/nonnormal square-root toll threshold for the uniform split of Quicksort: [Hwang–Neininger](https://doi.org/10.1137/S009753970138390X); also [Fill–Kapur, multiway search trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0306050). [Fill–Janson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02715) and [Fill–Janson–Wagner](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44007-024-00087-0) treat the corresponding subtree functional in conditioned Galton–Watson trees. Their split distributions are not the logarithmically critical q_m(i) of this note; no qualitative Gaussian threshold across all random-tree models is claimed as original.
* **Prior work in this repository.** [#203](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/203) concerns a sum of subtree heights, not masses. [#205](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/205) has already proved the leading-order laws at all fixed real p and the law of large numbers in the near-one window. It implies the centered p>1 row verbatim. Its window supplies the predicted bracket at p=1/2 here, but does not give the fluctuations at p≤1. The main additional cases are the boundary p=1/2 Gaussian law at the larger normalization, the explicit compensated-power limits and nonnormality for 1/2<p<1, and the entropy limit at p=1. For example, the boundary variance admits the elementary closed form (π−4 log 2)/(12ζ(2)log²2): at p=1/2, J=(2√(xy))/(1+√x+√y), hence B_p=∫₀¹ [2/(1+√x+√(1−x))²] dx. Put √x=sin θ and s=tan(θ/2) to obtain ∫₀¹ [4/(1+s²)−4/(1+s)]ds=π−4 log 2. The digamma duplication formula (or substitution t=√x in the defining integral) gives κ(1/2)=−2 log 2.

The literature screen compared those primary statements, related fragment limits, subtree-power recurrences, tagged versus whole-tree limits, and author publication/citation results through 19 November 2026. No matching earlier centered critical-tree result across p≥1/2 and p≤1 was located. This bounded search is not a claim of global priority.
