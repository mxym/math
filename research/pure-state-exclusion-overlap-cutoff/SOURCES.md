# Sources and scope

**Source definition.** [Johnston–Russo–Sikora, Quantum 9 (2025), 1622](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-02-04-1622), [arXiv:2311.17047v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17047v2), §2.1, Theorem 5.1, Corollaries 5.3–5.4 and §6. The Frobenius bound is already sharp, and Corollary 5.4 is known to be sharp for two to four states. Their n≥5 question concerns the *maximum absolute value* of a pairwise overlap among distinct family labels, not a fixed real pairwise inner product (which would be a different specialization). The draft's number $s_n$ is the **square** of their amplitude cap. Rank and spectral calculations use unit-normalized vectors, not Hilbert–Schmidt-normalized Gram matrices.

**Direct predecessor.** The [trureturing research record](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Problems/johnston-russo-sikora-2025-antidistinguishability-tightness.html) reports a formal, quantitative solution for n≥10. Checked the actual [Lean declaration at commit 74acadd20e08](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/74acadd20e08e9582da3ab1767a129b2145a8695/D5/S3/Quantum/Measurement/AntidistinguishabilityOverlapNonTight.lean): n≥10, arbitrary finite d, all vectors of norm one, all *squared* off-diagonal overlaps at most $s_n+(n-9)/(16n(n-1)(n-3))$ imply ordinary antidistinguishability. No restriction to circulant matrices. The cited implementation proves a quantitative fourth-eigenvalue/rank-three approximation followed by the rank-nine matrix-of-squared-overlaps obstruction. The project page explicitly leaves 5 through 9 outside its proof, records a separate prose-only $T_n\to1$ result, and does not claim exhaustive literature coverage. No local Lean replay was needed or performed. We do not claim its n≥10 result, its explicit margin, or its methods as new.

**Related measurement work.** [Diebra–Llorens–Bagan–Sentís–Muñoz-Tapia, PRR 8, L012001 (2026), Eq. (7)](https://doi.org/10.1103/2k5d-bprn) give a necessary and sufficient exclusion criterion for arbitrary finite-group orbits, extending the earlier circulant criterion. Group averaging and the triangle condition in the five-state check are known ingredients. [Hillery–Andersson–Puthoor, J. Phys. A (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ae1079) develop covariant elimination for qubit sequences; the public [arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02055) was inspected. The journal's accepted-manuscript copy was not independently matched to the arXiv version. [Roy et al., arXiv:2601.14410v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14410v2) study multiple copies, nonzero effects, and special equal-real-inner-product ensembles. [Johnston–Lovitz–Russo–Sikora, arXiv:2609.17411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17411), Theorems 2–3 and §§2–3, establish sharp frame-potential and converse classification bounds, including higher-list-size and many-copy variants; optimizing a Frobenius bound is not the tightness of this maximum-individual-overlap guarantee. None of these inspected quantum papers was found to identify the 5-versus-6 cutoff for JRS Corollary 5.4.

**Geometry is prior, not a claimed line-classification discovery.** [Et-Taoui–Lehbab–Makhlouf, Advances in Geometry 24 (2024) 507–533](https://doi.org/10.1515/advgeom-2024-0025) describe four through nine equiangular lines in complex dimension three; [Lehbab’s thesis record](https://theses.fr/2023MULH6508) describes a construction scheme and continuous families. Journal abstract and university thesis record were checked; the complete text of the paywalled journal article and corresponding thesis were not obtained. No priority claim is made about the existence, novelty, parametrization, or uniqueness of a five-line configuration. Qutrit geometry by itself is not the proof that a given configuration is on the boundary of non-antidistinguishable tuples. [Balla (2025), Equiangular lines via matrix projection](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2025.110620), especially §2 on complex equiangular lines, uses the regular-simplex structure of rank-one projectors and Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal projection. We do not claim the projection technique, the absolute dimension-square bound, or tight-frame facts as new. More specifically, Balla’s arXiv v5 **Theorem 2.3 and Lemma 4.3** already give a cubic in the top Gram eigenvalue. As shown after §2 of the proof, its direct specialization at the JRS limiting boundary rules out seven through nine states and beyond. This is an inference from two ingredients, not a published assertion by Balla about quantum exclusion. Five-state sharpness and the transition at six are the focus of the quantum application, not a priority claim about line geometry. In fact, the classical degree-two projective Jacobi addition formula in [Koornwinder (1973), §3, eq. (3.1)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0125027) and [Delsarte–Goethals–Seidel (1975), Theorem 3.3](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/bounds-for-systems-of-lines-and-jacobi-polynomials/), presented with explicit projective parameters in [Cohn–Kumar–Minton (2016), §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.3188), implies nonsharpness from six on when combined with the boundary reduction. Section 2 gives this deduction explicitly. We did not find the JRS conclusion in those geometric papers. The n≥10 quantitative result remains prior. No use is made of Balla’s general numerical size Theorem 1.11.
