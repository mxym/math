# Scope and bounded source comparison

- [Madiman–Nayar–Tkocz, *Sharp moment-entropy inequalities and capacity bounds for log-concave distributions*, arXiv:1811.00345v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00345). Their introduction formulates capacity under a quadratic power budget and Corollary 5 bounds the cost of restricting to symmetric log-concave inputs. The **unnumbered final paragraph** of the discussion asks about log-concavity of a maximizing input for symmetric log-concave noise. Their separate numbered Question 1 asks which **noise** maximizes capacity when the noise variance is fixed. Our instance addresses the former question only, and its qualitative negative answer can already be inferred from general earlier results.
- [Fahs–Abou-Faycal, *On properties of the support of capacity-achieving distributions for additive noise channel models with input cost constraints*, IEEE TIT 64 (2018), 1178–1198; full preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00878), Theorems 1 and 3. Superlinear cost versus exponential noise tails already gives compact input support for the Laplace model. Moreover an application of their strip-analytic discreteness theorem already answers the **qualitative** log-concavity question for another noise. For clarity choose the symmetric strictly log-concave density $p(x)=\operatorname{sech}(x)/\pi$, real additive map $f(x)=x$, and cost $x^2$. Their lower tail envelope is $p$ itself; $-\log p=\log(\pi\cosh x)$ grows as $|x|$ and satisfies C7 (e.g. $\cosh(x+y)\le2\cosh(x)\cosh(y)$). On $|\Im z|<\pi/4$, $|\cosh(x+it)|^2=\cosh^2x-\sin^2t\ge\cosh^2x/2$, giving an exponential upper envelope and the strengthened C8 integral. The map and cost are entire, the identity inverse preserves connectedness, and the cost grows faster than the negative log tail. Hence Theorem 3 on the real line gives finite optimal support. More than one point is necessary at positive power: any equiprobable two distinct feasible input values induce distinct output translates and positive mutual information. Such a finite support is not log-concave. This is an implication of their theorem; we do not attribute this particular example or discussion of the later question to them. Their analyticity hypothesis does not hold for the Laplace density at zero, and their support theorem does not identify the optimizer, atoms, or capacity here.
- [Dytso–Bustin–Poor–Shamai, ISIT 2017, *On additive channels with generalized Gaussian noise*](https://shamai.net.technion.ac.il/files/2017/04/dbs-ISIT2017.pdf), §VI, especially Propositions 7–10. These treat general upper bounds and discuss the Laplace **first absolute moment** case separately; that constraint is not a quadratic budget. [Verdú, *Spectral efficiency in the wideband regime*](https://web.mit.edu/6.441/www/reading/verdu_wideband.pdf), Theorem 3 and the surrounding discussion, concerns low-SNR information for Laplacian noise rather than the optimizer and capacity for every power.
- [Tridenski–Somekh-Baruch, *The Shannon upper bound for the error exponent*, arXiv:2509.08425](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08425v1), Theorem 4, treats the very same **Laplace noise/second power** pair but bounds the block **error exponent**; the rate at which that upper bound reaches zero is a Shannon upper bound for capacity, not a capacity-achieving output or input.
- [Yihan Zhang, *Capacity of uniform noise channels under average input power constraints*, arXiv:2607.14352](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14352v1), characterizes a different noise law by periodization and obtains an absolutely continuous optimizer. [Stapmanns et al., arXiv:2501.16145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16145), studies *uniform* noise with an imposed peak-amplitude constraint in addition to cost. Neither is the unrestricted-amplitude Laplace problem.
