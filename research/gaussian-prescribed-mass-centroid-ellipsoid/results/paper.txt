A sharp Gaussian first-moment inequality for every prescribed mass
                             vector

                                             Yongxian Zhang

                                             8 October 2026


Abstract
We establish a sharp first-moment inequality for every measurable simplex-valued function on Gaus-
sian space with prescribed positive mean. The label metric is the negative Hessian of the Gaussian
multi-bubble perimeter profile. The equality functions are exactly the indicators of the translated
regular simplex partition with those probabilities, extended cylindrically. The result holds for every
number of labels and every ambient dimension. Its proof derives a global tangent bound for the
squared optimal Gaussian assignment value from the established multi-bubble perimeter theorem
and a radial differential inequality. An exact nonnegative integral measures its deficit. At equal
masses this recovers the sharp ordinary squared-centroid sum; at unequal masses the metric is gen-
erally anisotropic. We do not assert the false unequal-mass Euclidean simplex conjecture or the
full functional multi-bubble isoperimetric inequality. The profile Hessian identity is prior work; our
statement is a bound for all functions, not only perimeter minimizers.

6. Proof and verification dependencies
The non-elementary geometric input is [MN22, Theorem 1.1], used only with 𝑘 = 𝑛 + 1, its positive-
mass hypotheses and its interface-perimeter normalization. The established profile Hessian identity
is stated with its tangent-space convention and independently normalized in Section 2. The remain-
ing tools are Gaussian integration by parts, dominated convergence, the implicit function theorem,
matrix square roots, pseudoinverses and one-variable calculus. There is no numerical or solver
premise, no differentiation at a singular endpoint, no assumption of covariance concavity and no
rank classification.
The package includes internal model reviews, finite exact diagnostics and partial Lean proofs with
explicit scope. They do not certify Gaussian measure, the imported geometric theorem or the entire
analytic endpoint. Internal model reviews are not external human peer review. The earlier equal-
mass result is preserved as its own immutable record; this theorem is a generalization of the same
proof mechanism, not an independent counting of the equal-mass case.
