# Sources and comparison

1. Nikhil Bansal, Christoph Dürr, Nguyen Kim Thang and Óscar C. Vásquez, [*The local–global conjecture for scheduling with non-linear cost*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10951-015-0466-5), Journal of Scheduling 20 (2017), 239–254. The [authors’ full preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.6188) poses Conjecture 1 in §3. Equation (2) in §5 tests a strict interchange at every start time; Lemma 5 establishes the endpoint monotonicity. Theorem 1 (§6.1) handles a preferred job no longer than its comparator; Theorem 2 (§6.2) handles every power at least one. Theorem 3 (§6.3) covers longer preferred jobs at concave powers under a stronger weight-ratio condition. Our analytic inequality closes the remaining strip, not the separate complexity question. Use the numbered statements to fix the direction of the duration comparison. Our proof distinguishes an attained adjacent tie from strict precedence.
2. Natalia Jorquera-Bravo and Óscar C. Vásquez, [ICORES 2022 conference paper, full text](https://www.scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2022/108716/108716.pdf), and [Annals of Operations Research 338 (2024), 335–345](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-023-05801-9). The former's Theorem 1/Corollaries 1–2 concern constraints on sequences of three comparable consecutive jobs and search-space pruning. The journal abstract also highlights cases for strictly convex penalties. These do not state the arbitrary intervening-block implication for all positive concave powers. We inspected the public journal abstract, not its subscription-only full text, and the full conference version.
3. Robbert Fokkink, Thomas Lidbetter and László A. Végh, [*On Submodular Search and Machine Scheduling*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.07598), Math. Oper. Res. 44 (2019). Their broader subset/precedence model admits approximation and decomposition results, a different objective from certifying universal pairwise precedence here.
4. Óscar C. Vásquez, [*For the airplane refueling problem local precedence implies global precedence*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11590-014-0758-2); also [*A Fast Greedy Algorithm with a 0.45 Approximation Guarantee for the Airplane Refueling Problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15222) (2026). The airplane scheduling formulation maximizes weighted inverse completion time, not minimizes a positive concave power. A proof or approximation for that problem is not this result.

**Search scope, 19 November 2026.** We queried the title, DOI, authors, local/global precedence and pairwise dominance with concave and sublinear power costs. We examined citation-index leads and the primary sources above; no matching later proof was located. A search is not a guarantee of priority. Related speed-scaling reductions, approximation results and search-space pruning do not by themselves settle Conjecture 1.
