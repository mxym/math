# Sources and scope

1. Sumit Rout, Paweł Horodecki and Some Sankar Bhattacharya, *Robust
   Certification of Non-projective Qubit Measurements without Inputs*,
   [arXiv:2611.33559v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.33559v1)
   (25 November 2026, latest located version on 27 November 2026).
   Their model of independent private randomness is in Section II;
   Proposition IV.1 / Equation (27) and Appendix A.1 give the covariance
   witness, and Theorem IV.2 already proves exact thresholds for regular
   trine and tetrahedral SIC. The full two-parameter equal-trace family
   is Equation (35), with an explicit \((1/2,1/4,1/4)\) example.
   Its known bounds come from Equations (34)–(36). Their Section V
   explicitly asks for exact thresholds beyond those symmetric cases.
   The present result answers this for every unbiased extremal four-outcome
   qubit POVM and gives a precise theorem for diagonal sign correlations.
   Their robust criterion addresses whether both devices can be PS.
   For the same diagonal class our distinct one-PS criterion quantifies
   when each device can separately be certified non-PS.

2. Andreas Argyriou, Rina Foygel and Nathan Srebro, *Sparse Prediction
   with the k-Support Norm*, [arXiv:1204.5043v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1204.5043),
   Proposition 2.1 and the section preceding it. The gauge (2), the
   decomposition into at-most-two-coordinate Euclidean atoms, and the
   dual description with the largest two coordinates are the known
   2-support-norm geometry; we give direct proofs for the three-dimensional
   specialization. We claim the implementation of the atomic boundary
   and the matching two-device covariance criterion, not the norm.

3. Fionnuala Curran, *Quantum randomness beyond projective measurements*,
   [arXiv:2605.18291v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18291v1), Section 4,
   Theorem 2 and Appendix D.1.1. The disphenoid geometry of all unbiased
   extremal four-outcome qubit MICs and its covariance are already
   described there. The target in that paper is intrinsic randomness,
   not the threshold of the input-free bipartite PS correlation model
   studied here. Our four-vector orthogonality argument is included
   to identify the scope without depending on a parameter convention.

4. Michał Oszmaniec, Leonardo Guerini, Peter Wittek and Antonio Acín,
   *Simulating positive-operator-valued measures with projective
   measurements*, [arXiv:1609.06139v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06139v2),
   [Phys. Rev. Lett. 119, 190501 (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.119.190501).
   The convex description and single-measurement SDP for qubits,
   as well as the regular tetrahedral case, are prior work.
   Raphael Brinster et al., [arXiv:2511.04446v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04446v2),
   give a robustness hierarchy for single-measurement witnesses, with
   the regular qubit SIC case among their examples. These do not
   establish the exact two-qubit correlation criterion (1)–(3).

Prior-art scope checked on 27 November 2026: the RHB title/identifier,
“disphenoid” / “tetrahedral” with “projective simulation” and “visibility”,
“semisymmetric” with “projective simulability”, and the 2-support norm
literature above. No matching exact threshold or diagonal correlation
characterization was found in this bounded check. In particular,
we do not claim novelty for the covariances, geometric normal form,
regular tetrahedral threshold, or general local simulability SDP.
