# Independent audit of the macroscopic path-coefficient gap

**PASS, with a substantive prior attribution to include.** Audited the frozen proof SHA-256 `cab4ffca2cfb4a7104083a690a6b6e444cf392251bfd065fecdbbd5b523251a2`. This is internal AI-assisted review, not external peer review. Proof applies on one event of probability at least 1−exp(−cn) simultaneously at all path-supported indices above εn. No deterministic strict path improvement or fixed positive rate for k=o(n); no optimal rate claimed.

1. **Claws and residual coefficient.** The three isolated leaves with forbidden center contribute 1+3x+3x²+x³, versus 1+3x+x² for the three remaining vertices on a path. With center unrestricted the contributions are 1+4x+3x²+x³ versus 1+4x+3x². Thus a common lower bonus x³ can be taken irrespective of the parent configuration. In the product comparison the residual forest for each fixed chosen marked subset is in fact a *connected* tree of size n−4s: the root remains outside all proper claws; unselected slots are paths, even if several share a parent. Different choices of bonus slots with the same s are separate terms of an algebraic product; no surmised disjointness of counted configurations in a hand-built injection is needed. Every residual tree then has the Wingard path lower bound. Leaf exclusion leaves a tree, and inclusion followed by adding edges to connect the remaining forest cannot increase its independent-set coefficients; induction on size recovers the path recurrence if a self-contained proof is desired. Deal directly with 0,1,2 vertices.

2. **Good probability.** In the parent map each choice of center z≠1 and three children disjoint from {1,z} requires three pointers to z and all n−4 remaining pointers outside the four labels, including the pointer at z. This gives the claimed mean. The outgoing edge of 1 is absent. A moved pointer i→u changed to i→v changes the childsets of u and v only; for other centers only the status of u or v as a leaf child can change. Such a child has just one parent before and just one after. Thus six is valid even with loops. The tree event has probability n^{n−2}/n^{n−1}=1/n, exactly, so a change in the absolute exponent suffices to pass from the map tail. No conditioning assumption on unobserved pointers.

3. **Endpoint and rate.** Write t=n−k−s+1, j=k−3s. Replacing (t,j) by (t−1,j−3) gives ratio j(j−1)(j−2)/[t(t−j+1)(t−j+2)]. At odd n and k=(n+1)/2 the initial t=j, and both denominator factors remain nonzero. n−4s≥1 and the residual path coefficient is positive for all used s. With L=min(N, floor(εn/6)), j≥εn/2 and each denominator component is positive and at most n for large n. The explicit rate min(e^{−4}/12, ε/12) log(1+ε³/27) follows by the binomial theorem. No union bound over k; the same claw event suffices for all k (indeed the event is independent of ε). There is no restriction at either parity ceiling beyond the initial positive path benchmark.

4. **Question and prior.** [Heilman, published Q8.3 and Lemma 7.3](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225) refer to the normalized exponential rate relative to the mean for random labeled trees. The exact factors to use are binom(n−k+1,k) and E i_k=binom(n,k)(1−k/n)^{k−1} from Lemma A.1; they should not be replaced by the misleading finite-n identities printed in the derivation of Eq. (15). The proof's normalized *exponential rate* for α≤1/2 is correct, uniformly with O(log n/n) by a Stirling estimate with factorial arguments allowed to be zero; the exact odd endpoint does not require logs of a negative argument. The question does not explicitly fix the scaling of k. For any k=o(n), log binom(n,k)=o(n), so no fixed positive rate is possible. At 1−ρ<α≤1/2 the known (ρ+o_p(1))n largest independent set already gives a strict exponential gap, since its size is larger by Θ(n) than n−k+1. These earlier arguments do not supply the all-macroscopic simultaneous conclusion. Wingard's prior deterministic inequality can be found also in [Basit–Galvin](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~dgalvin1/pdf/journal/random_trees_J.pdf) (see the author paper link in the main manuscript for the theorem statement). [Csikvári](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.21693) gives underlying tree-shift monotonicity.

5. **Prior for exponential probability, not merely weak convergence.** [McDiarmid, *Pendant appearances and components in random graphs from structured classes*, EJC, Theorem 1.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2024.103994), [full text Theorem 1.1(a,b)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.07051): labeled trees have growth constant e, reciprocal radius 1/e; the class is closed under attaching and detaching any fixed pendant rooted tree. For the center-rooted claw, h=4 and Aut=6. The theorem thus gives both tails of the count at multiplicative distance ε from e^{-4}n/6 with failure probability e^{-Ω(n)}. The theorem states the strict increase of the exponential generating function radius for exceptional graphs; dividing their count by the Cayley class count converts this to the probability assertion. For n>8 all distinct pendant appearances of the 4-vertex claw are disjoint: small equal sides in compatible edge splits cannot contain one another strictly or cover the larger tree by their union. Only one can contain label 1, so the fixed rooted-fringe count loses at most one. Taking ε'<ε if necessary absorbs the difference. In particular the parent-map proof is an elementary self-contained *alternative*, not a new or stronger claw-count tail bound. The coefficient amplification is the claimed extra step.
