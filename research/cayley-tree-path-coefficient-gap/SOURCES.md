# Reference and search notes

* [Steven Heilman, *Independent Sets of Random Trees and Sparse Random Graphs*, JGT 109 (2025), 294–309](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225). The published version's Question 8.3 asks for an exponential improvement on the typical-tree lower bound in Lemma 7.3, which is derived from the deterministic number on the path. Our theorem is uniform over $\varepsilon n\le k\le\lceil n/2\rceil$; it does not claim a fixed positive exponential rate for sublinear $k$. The polynomial-to-exponential normalization using the classical expected coefficient is in §4 of the proof. We do not address log-concavity or adjacent-coefficient ratios here.
* G. C. Wingard, *Properties and Applications of the Fibonacci Polynomial of a Graph*, University of Mississippi dissertation, 1995, Theorem 5.1, is the source credited by Heilman. [Basit and Galvin, *On the independent set sequence of a tree*](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~dgalvin1/pdf/journal/random_trees_J.pdf) explicitly use this fixed-cardinality bound. [Péter Csikvári, *On a Poset of Trees II*](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.21693) proves coefficient comparisons under a generalized tree shift. Neither the path benchmark nor qualitative comparisons under branching should be read as new.
* [Colin McDiarmid, *Pendant appearances and components in random graphs from structured classes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2024.103994), [Theorem 1.1(a,b)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.07051), already gives exponential tails about the density $e^{-4}/6$ of the center-rooted pendant claw in uniform labeled trees. The fixed-root descendant count differs by at most one. The independent-pointer argument in §2 of the proof is a short alternative, not an improvement to the known frequency or its tail bound. [Janson's conditioned Galton–Watson fringe theorem](https://www2.math.uu.se/~svantejs/papers/sj264.pdf) also gives the limiting density in probability.
* As recalled in [Heilman's Theorem 2.10](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23225), the typical maximum independent-set fraction is $\rho$ where $\rho e^\rho=1$. Subsets of a maximum set consequently yield an exponential gain over the path already at every fixed $1-\rho<k/n\le1/2$ in the limiting fixed-density sense. This does not cover the smaller positive densities handled here. Results for the total number of independent sets (all sizes combined), maximal sets, or extrema at fixed degree sequence are not the present uniform coefficient statement.
