# Source and attribution notes

1. Mohammad Etemad, Mohammad Mahmoody, and David Evans, [Optimizing Trees for Static Searchable Encryption](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/052.pdf), IACR ePrint 2018/052. Definition 3.1 gives OR trees. Section 3.1, Algorithm 1 (printed pp. 5–6), allows arbitrary monotone formula distributions and selects a pair minimizing the union's probability. Appendix B.1, Lemmas B.2–B.3 and the "Equivalent cost function" paragraph (printed pp. 21–22), give the root-tested cost 1+2 times the sum of local costs, and discuss the optional precomputed-root convention. Definition B.2 fixes the internal sum, the unrestricted optimum, and the balanced optimum. Theorems B.1 and B.2 in Appendix B.2 are for **Algorithm 2**, against unrestricted or balanced optima respectively, not an approximation proof for best-first. Their example of an unbalanced best-first tree uses disjoint keyword sets to prove the lower gap for Algorithm 2 versus the optimum, not a lower bound on Algorithm 1 versus the optimum. Section 4.2 studies nonoptimal examples among eight real sampled files. The "Open questions" paragraph before Section 6 (printed p. 16) requests theoretical analysis for best-first and hybrid, with its informal constant-factor expectation limited to real data. We do not treat that statement as a formal worst-case conjecture. The guarantees here refer to exact Algorithm 1 comparisons; approximation errors in estimating local probabilities and the hybrid algorithm are not evaluated.

2. [AICR c/0015: A Constant-Factor Guarantee for Huffman-Style Search Tree Construction](https://aicr.info/c/0015/) is the explicit AI-written worst-case formulation over all monotone workloads and every tie-breaking, distinguished on that page from the weaker source-paper wording. Our sharp arbitrary-monotone ratio settles that formulation negatively; the single-keyword family also excludes a constant in the special case mentioned on the page. Section 3's harmonic upper bound is for best-first on disjunctions, independently of Algorithm 2's known bound. The near-logarithmic lower bound and harmonic upper bound leave a gap on that special case. Ordinary additive Huffman coding is not this overlap-sensitive objective.

3. Frank Harary and Stephen Hedetniemi, [The achromatic number of a graph](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9800%2870%2980072-2), Journal of Combinatorial Theory 8 (1970), 154–161. With one deterministic query given by a graph's edge DNF, greedy zero-weight clusters are independent sets, and at the end of the zero phase their partition is a complete coloring. The graph structure used for the deterministic lower family is standard. The two-query perturbation, exact approximation ratios, weighted threshold-frontier argument and affine-subfield coverage family are the tree-cost results here, not claims about novelty of graph coloring or finite-field incidence.

Title/citation searches and queries for Huffman-like merging with set-union costs did not find a prior exact bound in this objective among accessible sources. The closest broadly related adaptive decision-tree problem mentioned by AICR chooses tests and has a different objective; it is not this fixed-leaf OR tree. A repository search for search-tree work found no matching archived result under the search vocabulary. These are pure optimization statements within the mathematical OR-tree model, not a security assessment or a claim about a production workload.
