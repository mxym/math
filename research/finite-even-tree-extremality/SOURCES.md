# Formulation, proof ingredients and prior work

**The finite question.** Éva Czabarka, László A. Székely and Stephan Wagner, [*Inducibility in binary trees and crossings in random tanglegrams*](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1060741), *SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics* **31** (2017), 1732–1750. Their [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.07149), Conjecture 1 following Theorem 5, asks for a maximizer of the **number of copies at each given host size**. Theorem 5 already determines the asymptotic inducibility of every even tree. A maximizer of the limiting density need not maximize every finite count. Their finite bound for the balanced quartet at powers of two also precedes this work.

**A solved special case and an exchange precedent.** Tomás M. Coronado, Arnau Mir, Francesc Rosselló and Gabriel Valiente, [*A balance index for phylogenetic trees based on rooted quartets*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-019-01377-w) ([author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.01651)), already prove the finite $k=4$ case with **uniqueness** (Theorem 17 of the preprint). The rooted quartet index in binary trees is proportional to the number of balanced four-leaf subsets. Their grandbranch exchange arguments in Lemmas 15 and 16 leading to Theorem 17 are direct methodological antecedents. The present exchange tracks all occupancy patterns and uses determinants comparing *different* pattern orders. We do not claim a new quartet theorem or assert uniqueness at other orders.

**The growth bracket is a prior ingredient.** Éva Czabarka, Audace A. V. Dossou-Olory, László A. Székely and Stephan Wagner, [*Inducibility of d-ary trees*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2019.111671) ([author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.03817)), proof of Theorem 3, pp. 9–10 of the preprint, already derive **both** leaf-deletion and cherry-duplication inequalities for unrestricted maximum counts by averaging leaf participation. Equation (2) in our proof is exactly their pair of inequalities rewritten for the candidate counts at host sizes where extremality has already been established. We include its short derivation for use inside the simultaneous induction, not as a new lemma for maxima. Their convergence-rate theorem answers a different conjecture (Conjecture 2 of the CSW preprint).

**What remains outside the theorem.** Audace A. V. Dossou-Olory and Stephan Wagner, [*Further results on the inducibility of d-ary trees*](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/81/ajc_v81_p001.pdf), *Australasian Journal of Combinatorics* **81** (2021), 1–24, Conjecture 1.1, propose the exact finite-size maximizer also for higher arity; their Theorem 1.2 gives the limiting inducibility. This note establishes the binary instance, not their assertion for all $d$. Daniel Brosch and Diane Puges, [*Getting to the root of the problem: sums of squares for limits of trees*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-025-02305-1), *Mathematical Programming* **218** (2026), 505–561, restate the finite binary question in their concluding open-problems section. Their own numbered Conjecture 1, by contrast, concerns the simultaneous limiting densities of a caterpillar and $E_6$, as studied separately in [the double-caterpillar note](../double-caterpillar-profiles/README.md).
