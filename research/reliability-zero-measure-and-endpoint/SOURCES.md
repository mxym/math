# Sources and scope

## The zero measure
[Buys, *Density of reliability roots of simple graphs in the unit disk*, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07541v1#S4.SS1) asks whether the zero-counting measures of $K_n$ tend to uniform arclength on the unit circle. This is an **unnumbered sentence after Question 4.1**. Question 4.1 instead asks whether, for each $n$, every zero other than 1 has modulus strictly less than one. A weak limit constrains proportions, not every zero. Neither Question 4.1 nor Question 4.2 on closure of root sets is answered here.

The coefficient-height criterion is the deterministic part of [Hughes–Nikeghbali, Theorems 1–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0406376), including the Erdős–Turán angular estimate. The positive tree expansion is the classical activity expansion of the Tutte polynomial, here derived from Kruskal's algorithm. Theorem A is a graph application of these two facts plus the exact multiplicity of 1. It is not a new criterion for arbitrary polynomials. [Chang–Shrock](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0208538) study accumulation sets of zeros in other explicit graph families; support and multiplicity-weighted weak limits are different questions.

## The endpoint and its rate
[Omar, *Real Reliability Roots of Simple Graphs are Dense*, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03530v1#S4) asks for explicit simple graph families with real roots close to −1 and for convergence rates. Real-root density is already due independently to Omar and Buys, following [Brown–McMullin](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2026.03.037). See also [Brown–Colbourn](https://doi.org/10.1137/0405047) for real-root containment in the multigraph setting and [Brown–DeGagné](https://doi.org/10.1002/net.21938) for exclusion of −1 for simple graphs.

There is a classical dictionary with the Mallows–Riordan tree inversion enumerator $J_n$: the labelled connected-graph identity gives
$$
R_n(q)=q^{\binom n2}(q^{-1}-1)^{n-1}J_n(q^{-1}).
$$
In particular $J_n(-1)$ is the positive $(n-1)$-st Euler zigzag number (the number of alternating permutations). Thus **the endpoint sign and the resulting mod-4 existence statement already follow from a classical evaluation**. Moreover, Buys's Lemma 2.1 gives $R_n\to1$ on every fixed compact subset of the open unit disk. This already forces **any** negative real roots in $(-1,0)$ along $n\to\infty$ to tend to −1. The quantitative content of Theorem B is the moving window with common first-order constant 2, not the qualitative consequence.

For the enumerators, see [Gessel–Sagan–Yeh, *Enumeration of trees by inversions*](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190190402) and [Goodall–Merino–de Mier–Noy on the Tutte evaluation at $(1,-1)$](https://web.mat.upc.edu/marc.noy/uploads/2013/05/Tutte-1-1-2-1.pdf). [Alsmeyer–Bostan–Raschel–Simon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03016) study the same inversion polynomials in a persistence problem; Remark 2.2 discusses positivity and monotonicity on $[-1,\infty)$ and a different sign question below −1. [Sokal's slides on the deformed exponential](https://www.icms.org.uk/sites/default/files/downloads/sokal.pdf) give the connected-graph logarithm, a dominant-zero method on the convergent side $|1/q|<1$, and the earlier zero-free-disk question in the reciprocal variable. Here $1/q=-e^t\le-1$: only a finite Taylor jet is used, with uniform estimates as the parameter moves. The logarithm, pole method, and endpoint evaluation are not claimed as new.

## Boundaries of the search

We searched the primary graph-reliability and enumerative literatures by author, question, and alternate polynomial names, including the reciprocal variable, equidistribution terminology and negative endpoints. No earlier result with exactly the graph-sequence equivalence or the all-choice first-order rate was identified in this bounded search. This is not proof of priority.

Repository comparison was performed at mxym/math main 18cbd8b4469b41679cb5b231570af4270742c478: 21,990 tracked paths (including 12,604 under formalizations/ and 148 under releases/) and all 48 contemporaneous tag refs. Text layers of PDFs, supported source files and archived texts were searched. Two Lean files named Reliability concern quantum extraction; the mention of Buys concerns Shannon capacity. There was no relevant match in the inspected release inventory: 102 of 175 public release assets had hashes matching repository material, and the other 73 were digest-checked and searched. This was not an OCR pass over images, an inspection of every untagged revision, or a search of nonpublic archives. A failed anonymous refresh of the release API was not treated as evidence of absence; the live main/tag refs were separately verified.
