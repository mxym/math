# Primary literature and scope

1. [David Aldous and Svante Janson, *The Critical Beta-splitting Random Tree II*, v3, §6.2, Open Problem 16](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.02529v3#S6.SS2). The statistic counts all nonsingleton clades, including the root. Formula (76) forecasts the subcritical mean, (77) gives the critical mean, and (78) conjecturally gives the p=2 mean. The present theorem addresses fixed **real** powers in all three regimes and identifies a random limit for the supercritical sample statistic. The broader problem includes complex powers; the current fixed-real theorem neither treats these nor claims a full theory of fluctuations.
2. **Earlier tree and clock results.** [Aldous–Janson III](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09655) gives the consistent exchangeable interval/partition model (the sampled discrete genealogy here is obtained by forgetting finite-clade lifetimes). [Heng Ma, *The Height of Discrete-Time Critical Beta-Splitting Trees*, Theorem 1.1 and §1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14329v2#S1.SS2) already explicitly uses iid exponential clocks after clade-rate rescaling and expresses their sum along a *single* ancestral line as a time integral. We use that clock observation and apply it to the *whole-tree* power sum.
3. **Earlier means and concentration in the first two regimes.** [Aldous–Janson–Li, *The Harmonic Descent Chain*](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-ECP645) determines the limiting occupation probabilities; [Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel, §1.1, unnumbered assertion after (1.7), and Cor. 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24821v3#S1.SS1) imply the entire subcritical expected-value formula and give fixed-clade count variance and CLT. At p=1 the sum is the external path length: [Aldous–Pittel, Thm 1.2](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AAP2112) and [Svihla–Lladser, Thm 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-026-01655-x) are direct prior, including a sharper variance expansion for this statistic. [Iksanov–Nikitin–Yakymiv](https://do.csc.knu.ua/iksan/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2025/10/IksNikYak131025subm.pdf) prove joint CLTs of tagged leaf edge/time heights and counts of fixed *decrements* in ancestral clade size; that is neither the random conditional average of common-ancestor depths nor the supercritical power-sum law.
4. **The integer supercritical means are already implicit in the MRCA literature.** [Michael Fuchs and Mike Steel, Theorem 2(ii), Proposition 10 and Theorem 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09270v2) show that for each fixed integer k≥2 the edge-depth of the MRCA of one uniformly chosen sample of k *distinct* leaves divided by log n converges to an exponential variable of mean 1/H_(k−1), including moment convergence. On any fixed tree, S_n(k)/n^k is the expected number of common internal clades hit by k iid uniform labels sampled with replacement. For distinct labels that number equals the MRCA edge-depth plus one. Repeated labels have probability O_k(1/n); the discrepancy from the distinct-sample average is bounded after expectation by O_k((1+E maximum height)/n). Their result thus implies the annealed integer-k mean in the present theorem, including the proposed (78). It does not determine the random limit of these *conditional* tree-wide averages; at k=2 the latter variance is 2/11, not the exponential variance 1. The k=2 average is close to the usual total cophenetic index after dividing by the pair count and accounting for the root-depth convention; see [the original cophenetic-index paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.1223) and [Bartoszek on its Yule-model limit](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08954).
5. **The continuum law is prior.** [Jean Bertoin, *The area of a self-similar fragmentation*, ALEA 9 (2012), 53–66](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v9/09-02.pdf), §3.4 p.64 explicitly identifies the homogeneous integral of the (1−α)-powers of block frequencies with the area for index α<0. Corollary 2.2 gives all integer moments for binary dislocations. Therefore J_p is precisely a known class of fragmentation-area laws; its moments, including the variance specialization in the note, are not new general continuum identities. The binary Beta example after Corollary 2.2 is illustrated for −2<β<−1, while that corollary itself applies also at our critical β=−1 dislocation measure.
6. **Model comparisons.** [Haas–Miermont–Pitman–Winkel](https://www.math.univ-paris13.fr/~haas/AOP2008365.pdf) develop continuum-tree scaling for discrete fragmentations under a positive regular-variation index, a different regime from the logarithmic critical genealogy here. [Fill–Janson and related conditioned Galton–Watson work](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44007-024-00087-0) analyze subtree power sums in another distribution. [Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel on extremal clades](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24973), [harmonic occupation expansions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00347), [homogeneous-fragmentation asymptotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18320), and [ancestral diversity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15500) cover extremal shapes, rate estimates, separation times or the number of distinct MRCAs, not the discrete-to-area convergence proved here.
7. The owner's [earlier drawn cladogram note](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/203) treats the leaf-up geometric length via an additive sum of **subtree heights**, not sums of powers of **subtree leaf counts**. Its dyadic harmonic-descent visit device is reused in the subcritical proof, not claimed as an independent innovation. Targeted search also covered clade-size powers, cophenetic and MRCA averages, area/energy of homogeneous and self-similar fragmentations, sampled genealogies and recent critical-tree papers. No exact earlier tree-wide supercritical limit was found; this does not establish world priority.
