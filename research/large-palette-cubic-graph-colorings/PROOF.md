# Large palettes in the Petersen–Heawood proper-coloring problem
Candidate research note. AI-assisted with independent internal AI review; not externally peer reviewed or proof-assistant certified. Priority is not claimed.

Let P be the Petersen graph and H the Heawood graph. Write f_G(q)=log(chi_G(q))/|V(G)| for a nonempty finite simple graph, where chi counts proper colorings with labeled colors at positive integers. Graphs need not be connected.

**Theorem (large-palette ranges).** If G is cubic and triangle-free, f_G(q) ≥ f_P(q) for every integer q≥37. If G is cubic and has no 4-cycle (NOT necessarily triangle-free), f_G(q) ≤ f_H(q) for all q≥88. In either case equality holds iff every component is a copy of the indicated extremizer. These are uniform-in-order partial ranges of Cambie–Jooken Conjecture 5.2(a),(b), not a proof or refutation for all palettes. The thresholds are not asserted best. We also give paper-only thresholds, without the eighth-order enumeration.

## 1. Local gaps

Write c_j(G) for the number of unoriented simple j-cycles, without a chosen start, divided by |V(G)|. More generally c_F is the normalized number of (not necessarily induced) edge-subgraphs isomorphic to F, ignoring isolated vertices. For now let G be **connected** and not the stated extremizer.

**Odd side.** For v in a triangle-free cubic graph, let A=N(v). For each vertex x reached in two nonbacktracking steps put m_x=|A∩N(x)|. These x are all outside A∪{v}, and sum m_x=6. For an edge xy among these second-layer vertices the parent sets are disjoint, since a common parent makes a triangle. The exact number of 5-cycles through v is therefore sum_{xy} m_x m_y. The only unordered pairs of positive multiplicities on an edge are (1,1) and (1,2). Charge 1/2 to each endpoint in the former case, and charge 1/2 to the singleton and 3/2 to the double endpoint in the latter. A singleton has ≤2 second-layer incident edges, a double has ≤1, and a triple has none. Every x pays at most m_x, strictly less for m_x>1. Consequently each v is on at most six 5-cycles. Equality forces six distinct second-layer vertices, all with their remaining two edges in that layer, saturating the whole connected component on ten vertices. In particular if |G|≠10, every v is on at most five 5-cycles. If |G|=10 but G has no 4-cycle, the six distinct second-layer vertices induce a 6-cycle: this must be a 2-regular simple graph without triangles. Siblings sharing a parent must be antipodal in that 6-cycle (distance one makes a triangle; distance two a square). This describes P uniquely. Conversely each vertex of P is on six 5-cycles. In the remaining 10-vertex case c_4≥1/10. Thus always
  6/5−c_5(G)≥0,     6/5−c_5(G)≥1/5−2 c_4(G).                 (1)

**Even side.** Fix v in a C4-free cubic graph. There are twelve nonbacktracking length-three walks from v. Let a_y count only the simple ones ending at y. Their penultimate vertices are distinct for each given y: two paths sharing their penultimate vertex give a square between their different first steps (or are identical). Hence a_y≤3. The 6-cycles through v pair two internally disjoint paths to the same y, so their count is at most sum binom(a_y,2) ≤ sum a_y ≤12. If there are twelve cycles, all twelve walks are simple (in particular, v belongs to no triangle), and exactly four endpoints each have a_y=3. The first layer A consists of three vertices; the six second vertices B are distinct (by C4-freeness) and disjoint from v,A (no triangle at v). For every one of the four endpoints y, its three distinct penultimate vertices exhaust its neighborhood in B. Such y is not v (simple path), not in A (A has a neighbor v outside B), and not in B (B has a parent in A). Thus 1+3+6+4 vertices are distinct, and all their incident edges have been exhausted. They constitute a cubic bipartite C4-free graph: sides {v}∪B and A∪{four endpoints}. Regard them as seven points and seven lines. Each pair of points is on at most one line, and 7*binom(3,2)=binom(7,2) forces exactly one. The design is the Fano plane: the three lines through a fixed point pair off the other six points; labeling pairs (a0,a1),(b0,b1),(c0,c1), each of the other four lines selects one member of each pair, with each cross-pair appearing once. After swapping c labels the triples are (a_i,b_j,c_{i xor j}). The incidence graph is H. Conversely every point (and dually line) of H lies in twelve 6-cycles (choose two other points not collinear with it: binom(6,2)−3). Thus
  c_6(H)−c_6(G)≥1/6,      c_6(H)=2.                           (2)

For disconnected G, normalized logs are vertex-weighted averages of the connected ones. It suffices to prove strict inequalities for all connected non-extremizers; do not average a fixed positive penalty over arbitrarily many extremal components.

## 2. Chromatic-log coefficients and tail

Put z=1/q and define a power series
 B_G(z)=log(z^n chi_G(1/z))−m log(1−z)
for n=|V(G)|, m=|E(G)|. The edge term m/n is always 3/2 for our comparison. Define b_F(z) by Boolean Möbius inversion on the edge subsets of F:
 b_F=sum_{A⊆E(F)} (−1)^{m(F)−|A|} B_{(V(F),A)}.
Then B_G=sum_{F⊆_E G} b_F with each edge set counted once, and corresponding differences divided by n give differences of f. Vertices without edges do not affect B. If F is a singleton edge then b_F=0. If its edges split into two nonempty subgraphs meeting in at most one vertex, B_{(V,A)} splits additively along this partition (colorings multiply, divided by q when one common vertex is identified), so the alternating sum is zero. Thus only 2-connected supports with at least three vertices survive. This general subgraph-additive principle for chromatic root moments is known; see Csikvári–Frenkel.

Whitney inclusion-exclusion gives z^n chi_F(1/z)=sum_{A⊆E(F)}(−1)^{|A|} z^{r(A)}, with graphic rank r(A)=|V(F)|−components(V(F),A). In the expansion of log(1+u) an ordered tuple of nonempty subsets A_i survives edge Möbius inversion only if their union equals E(F). Thus every term has degree Σ r(A_i)≥r(E(F))=v(F)−1. The scalar edge subtraction has no multiply-supported term. Hence coefficients through order K involve only supports with ≤K+1 vertices.

For a simple j-cycle, b_C=log[1+(−1)^j(z/(1−z))^{j−1}]. At order v(F)−1 the first coefficient on any 2-connected support F equals (−1)^{m(F)}: a tuple with repeated edges strictly overshoots rank by submodularity, and a partition of its edges into ≥2 nonempty sets has sum of ranks greater than total rank. For the latter, any nontrivial bipartition has a crossing circuit, as in a 2-connected graph any two edges lie on a common simple cycle. Spanning forests in the parts then have dependent union, giving strictness. Alternatively the low-order weights below can be computed directly by Whitney inversion, without this assertion. In particular signs must not be inferred just from cycle parity for multicyclic supports.

Bencs–Regts Table 1.1 proves every subcubic chromatic root has modulus ≤3·2.321<7. Factoring the monic chromatic polynomial yields kth coefficient −Σ roots^k/k of its normalized log. For any two subcubic graphs, the difference of normalized logs after truncation through K has tail with absolute value at most
  T_K(z)=2 (7z)^{K+1}/((K+1)(1−7z)),      0<z<1/7.            (3)
In our cubic comparisons the log-edge term cancels at every degree. This is uniform in graph order. All logs agree with real logs of positive functions at our q.

## 3. Paper-only proof of uniform large-q regimes

For the two cases use g=5, M=P, sign s=−1; or g=6, M=H, s=+1. We bound D=s(f_M−f_G) below. Up to degree g−1, only the following supports occur. On the triangle-free side: C4, C5, K_{2,3}; on the C4-free side: C3,C5,C6 and the theta with path lengths 1,2,4. To check the lists, start with a cycle, then add open ears, including single-edge chords. Extending C4 on at most five vertices without a triangle allows only a path of length two between opposite endpoints (K_{2,3}), with no further legal edge. A chord of C5 produces a triangle. On the C4-free side a triangle has only a 4-edge ear between adjacent endpoints as its first possible extension on ≤6 vertices: a 1-edge ear duplicates, a 2-edge ear creates a square and a 3-edge ear creates a square. From C5 no chord is possible since one of the resulting cycles is a square; a 2-edge ear is allowed between adjacent endpoints (theta 1,2,4), forbidden between nonadjacent ones. From C6 a chord between vertices at distance two gives that theta and one between antipodal vertices makes squares. On the resulting theta, the four vertices still of degree two consist of the middle point of the 2-path and the three internal points of the 4-path. No further new chord is possible: each nonadjacent pair of degree-two vertices has an existing 3-edge path between them, so creates a square. (Pairs already adjacent cannot be used.) Thus the list closes. A first-ear existence proof: starting with a cycle in a 2-connected F, a component outside the current 2-connected subgraph has at least two distinct attachment vertices, providing a simple outside ear; once all vertices are included, add any missing edges as chords.

C4 has series z³+3z⁴+..., C5 starts −z⁴−4z⁵, C6 starts +z⁵. C3 contributes −z²−2z³−(7/2)z⁴−6z⁵. The extra supports have leading signs + (K_{2,3} has six edges) and − (theta has seven). Their contributions to D are favorable since neither occurs in its corresponding M. Equations (1),(2) therefore give
  [D]_{≤4} ≥ z⁴/5 + c_4(z³+z⁴)          (P),
  [D]_{≤5} ≥ z⁵/6                        (H).
By (3) these inequalities remain strict for the exact D if q>7+2·7^g: in particular q≥33622 and q≥235306, respectively. This argument does not use the optional finite high-order computation.

## 4. Finite certificate strengthening to q≥37,88

Use K=8. The full finite recipe is included to specify exactly what is certified. Enumerate **all** unlabelled 2-connected subcubic graphs of order ≤9 not containing the forbidden cycle (C3 in case P, C4 in case H). Start with all cycles of lengths 3 through 9 except the forbidden one, extend by ears with distinct endpoints of degree two, new internal vertices, allow the single-edge case only for nonadjacent endpoints, and discard any new forbidden cycle. The ear argument above proves completeness and monotonicity of the forbidden test. Canonical isomorphism labeling removes repetitions. For each F in increasing edge count:
1. Compute its chromatic polynomial by Whitney's formula. Let L_k be the degree k coefficient of log(z^v chi_F(1/z)), with L_0=0. If P_j denotes the degree j coefficient in the normalized polynomial, L_k=P_k−(1/k)Σ_{0<j<k}j L_j P_{k−j}.
2. Compute w_{F,k}=[z^k]b_F=L_k+m(F)/k−Σ_{A proper subset of E(F), A 2-connected on its nonisolates} w_{A,k}. The entries may be rational. Automorphism order is a_F.
3. Let X={4} for P or X={3,5} for H. Maintain a main-row polynomial U and a row V_l for each l∈X, initialized to zero. If F=C_l, l∈X, add −s w_{F,k} to V_{l,k} (M has no such cycle). For any other F containing a cycle from X, choose the smallest such l. There are at most c_l(G)·2l·2^{v(F)−l}/a_F normalized F-copies in G: after fixing a marked oriented l-cycle of the domain, its vertex images have 2l times the unmarked host-cycle count possibilities. Add each remaining vertex adjacent to an old vertex; at least one of its parent's neighbors has already been used, so there are at most two choices. Divide the injection count by a_F. Subtract max(s w_{F,k},0) times this coefficient from V_{l,k}; this is a lower bound irrespective of sign.
4. For F=C_g the signed Taylor coefficients through order 8 are nonnegative and the leading coefficient is 1 at degree g−1. The cycle gap is nonnegative by (1),(2), so keep only its leading term. Add 1/g to U_{g−1}, and in case P subtract 2 from V_{4,4}, by (1).
5. For every remaining F, compute the exact c_F(M) by injective vertex maps, and use bound c_F(G)≤b=3·2^t/a_F. Here t is the minimum number of nonleaves in any spanning tree of F: root such a tree at one leaf, choose its image in |G| ways and its child's image in at most three ways. At any nonroot internal vertex, either one child or two ordered children have at most two choices relative to the parent edge (in the latter case at most 2! choices). Add s w_{F,k} c_F(M)−max(0,s w_{F,k}) b to U_k.
These rules bound the Taylor polynomial coefficient by coefficient: [D]_{≤8}(z)≥U(z)+Σ_l c_l(G)V_l(z) for z≥0. In step 4 the estimate uses a nonnegative aggregate cycle gap, not independent bounds for its individual graphs.

For a row R with leading degree d, set L(R,z)=R_d+Σ_{k>d}min(R_k,0) z^{k−d}. Each is nonincreasing on positive z, and R(z)≥z^d L(R,z). The positive T_8(z)/z^{g−1} is increasing there up to z=1/7. At z=1/37 (P) and 1/88 (H), exact rational comparison gives all L(V_l,z)≥0 and L(U,z)>T_8(z)/z^{g−1}. The final theorem follows for the whole ranges, not just the two endpoint integers. The cutoff calculation is deliberately conservative; unproved possibilities for smaller q are not implied by it.
