# Independent internal review: large palettes for the Petersen/Heawood coloring problem

**Disposition: PASS on the two stated ranges.** The originally reviewed manuscript, before layout and reference edits, had SHA-256 `447d8939f2199dc17adfeddfb524e8683b9d7eb9ea07045d0063da2b2858e51d`. This is an independently written, AI-assisted internal audit, not external peer review, a computer proof of the original full conjecture, or a priority assessment.

## Scope and source checks

[Cambie–Jooken, Conjecture 5.2(a),(b)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05542v2#S5) is about the geometric mean per vertex of **labeled proper vertex-colorings**. It specifies triangle-free cubic graphs and q≥4 in (a), and cubic graphs without 4-cycles in (b); (b) does not impose a triangle-free hypothesis. The candidate proves q≥37 and q≥88, respectively, not the conjectural whole ranges and not the all-target Question 5.1. The higher-palette conclusions do not contradict the authors' example at q=3 on the Petersen side. The order and number of components are not bounded.

I checked [Bencs–Regts, published Table 1.1](https://escholarship.org/content/qt91f52796/qt91f52796.pdf) against the [authors' preprint table](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04366v1#S1.T1): the recorded upper bound for **maximum** degree ≤3 is 2.321 times 3, not 2.321 and not the general factor 4.25 times 3. In particular 7 is a valid absolute root radius for all supports/hosts used here. Cluster/moment expansions and earlier extremality for independent sets and matchings are precedents for methods, not automatically the same large-palette result. My targeted search did not identify an earlier identical uniform coloring bound; I did not repeat every inventory operation in the separately documented prior-art scan, and make no priority guarantee.

## Independent proof checks

1. **Five-cycles.** In a triangle-free cubic graph, a 5-cycle through v is counted exactly once by choosing one undirected edge xy inside the second layer, then one of each endpoint's first-layer parents. Disjointness of the two parent sets rules out every multiplicity pair except (1,1) and (1,2). The specified charges sum to the correct product on each edge. Multiplicity 2 has charge at most 3/2<2; multiplicity 3 gets zero. Equality therefore forces every multiplicity to be 1 and full two-neighbor incidence within the six-vertex second layer. The component then has 10 vertices. A 10-vertex square-free candidate has six distinct second-layer vertices at every v, regardless of whether equality was assumed, by the absence of squares and saturation of the order; they make a single 6-cycle and siblings are opposite. This determines Petersen. Otherwise c4≥1/10 in the exceptional order, and the inequality 6/5−c5≥1/5−2c4 follows from nonnegativity. Off this order every v on at most five pentagons gives c5≤1. Counts here are unoriented, with per-vertex cycle-incidence sums divided by cycle length and by graph order.

2. **Six-cycles, without silently excluding triangles.** Among the 12 nonbacktracking 3-walks from v, discard nonsimple walks. Two different simple paths to a given y cannot share their penultimate vertex: the two different first vertices would create a 4-cycle with v and the penultimate vertex. Hence a_y≤3 and each 6-cycle through v determines one unordered disjoint pair counted by the upper bound Σ binom(a_y,2)≤Σ a_y≤12. Equality forces no walks discarded (so no triangle through v) and all nonempty endpoint counts equal to 3. The six penultimate/second-layer vertices are distinct and disjoint from v and the first layer. Each of the four endpoints has all three neighbors in that second layer; v, first-layer and second-layer vertices themselves cannot be such endpoints (v is excluded by simplicity, and the other two classes have a neighbor outside the second layer). This saturates all degrees of a 14-vertex bipartite component. Its 7 lines use all 21 point pairs, precisely once. The remaining four triples off one point form a two-by-two Latin square, forcing the Fano design. Every point and line has 12 hexagons through it, yielding c6(H)=2; otherwise all vertices have ≤11, hence gap ≥1/6. Globally positive strictness is proved componentwise before averaging; there is no unwarranted order-independent gap for mixed graphs with a vanishing fraction of nonextremal vertices.

3. **Support and normalization.** The normalized polynomial is the edge-subset sum of signed graphic-rank monomials. Single-vertex-sum factorization makes the logarithm additive and kills all multiblock supports under edge-set Möbius inversion; the edge subtraction kills single edges, and isolates contribute no factor. Any log tuple covering the support has total rank at least v−1. For equality, a repeated edge gives rank loss via a nonempty intersection, whereas a nontrivial disjoint edge partition in a biconnected support has strict rank loss by a crossing circuit. Thus the first possible degree is v−1 with coefficient (−1)^m. The truncation through degree 8 needs all supports of order ≤9, not only those having at most eight edges. Density is injections/(host vertex count · support automorphisms), including noninduced occurrences. The universal log(q) and (3/2)log(1−1/q) cancel, since all comparison hosts are cubic. For z=1/q, the root-moment difference at degree k is bounded in absolute value by 2·7^k/k after division by each graph's own order, with no dependence on either order or on the short-cycle counts. For K=8 summing gives 2(7z)^9/[9(1−7z)]. On real q>7 the normalized polynomial has no zero, is positive by continuity from sufficiently large q, and the expansion takes the ordinary real logarithm.

4. **Lower-bound rows.** A marked directed cycle in a fixed support has at most (2l) times the host's undirected l-cycle count possible images; each newly exposed vertex has a previously used neighbor of its parent, hence at most two options. The images need not be induced. Rooting a spanning tree at a leaf gives 3 first choices; at each internal vertex, one child gives ≤2 and two ordered children give ≤2·1 options. Thus 3·2^t before dividing by its automorphism count is valid. In signed D=s(f_M−f_G), a coefficient w of an unrestricted support is bounded below by s w c_F(M)−max(0,s w)cap. For a support containing a short forbidden-in-M cycle c_F(M)=0, not necessarily c_F(G)=0. The row for C3 in the Heawood calculation is essential. The signed g-cycle coefficients at all retained degrees are nonnegative, allowing non-leading terms to be dropped because the *true* cycle gap is nonnegative before invoking the weaker signed lower bound at the leading degree. The paper-only smaller-order support lists close under the stated allowed ears; the resulting normalized tail inequality q>7+2·7^g and integer cutoffs are correct.

5. **Endpoint to interval.** For q≥q0 the nonpositive corrections in each normalized lower row shrink in magnitude. The worst normalized tail is at q0, since the explicit function is 2·7^9/[9(q−7)q^(9−g)] for g=5,6. Exact lower-row slack after subtracting the tail is 85823/708432858 at 37, and 2393573/993586176 at 88. Auxiliary lower rows are positive: 617588249/624095613 (square); 5572844738365/5572849041408 and 359794249/359817216 (triangle, pentagon). This yields strictness for connected nonextremizers and the stated componentwise equality criterion. It is not a uniform bound obtained by testing finitely many host orders.

## Software checks

Both supplied generators were run unoptimized so their assertions remained enabled. I compared their output to the frozen logs, and ran the stand-alone endpoint checker. In the initially reviewed version, its raw `assert` was disabled under `python -O`. The version in this directory uses an explicit exception on failure, including when run with `-O`. Use unoptimized Python for the two generators. The two generators are complete for stylized but different reasons: any 2-connected subcubic graph arises from a cycle by open ears, each with distinct current degree-2 ends; alternatively every connected graph can be grown one vertex at a time through connected induced subgraphs (order by a rooted spanning tree). The latter generator's ban tests on attachment vertices, adjacency for triangle and a shared neighbor for square, detect exactly forbidden new cycles. Isomorphism minimization preserves all types in each algorithm.

The separate, standard-library script `independent.py` imports **no** candidate code. It first makes all subcubic trees by leaf addition, then all simple connected subcubic graphs by unrestricted edge addition to a spanning tree of the final order; exact bijective adjacency-preserving maps decide isomorphism within degree-invariant buckets. This produced 1, 2, 6, 10, 29, 64, 194, 531 types of orders 2 to 9 and 200 biconnected types overall. Only afterward does it exclude supports with actual embeddings of C3 or C4, independently obtaining 87 and 51. For every retained support it evaluates the **direct signed sum of chromatic logarithms for all edge subsets**, rather than recursively subtracting the weights of smaller supports. It builds all subset polynomials by a Boolean zeta transform, with reversible signed base-2^26 coefficient packing (each coefficient ≤2^13 in absolute value); coefficients of logs use exact integers / degree. A minimal-nonleaf spanning tree is found by embedding already generated abstract trees into the support. This third enumeration and exact arithmetic agree on all five printed rows and both endpoint checks. Four supplemental exact-integer comparisons of raw chromatic counts use independent-set partitions, including K3,3 as a square-containing candidate and truncated K4 (four triangles but no 4-cycle) against Heawood.

Reproduction: `python -B -O independent.py`. Its invariant/row/endpoint tests use explicit exceptions, not assertions; see [CHECKS.txt](CHECKS.txt) for reproduced output.
