# Prior work and scope (18 November 2026, UTC)

1. S. Cambie, J. Jooken, [original Conjecture 5.2 in arXiv v2 §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05542v2), [Math. Comp. 95 (2026), 2091–2102, electronic 2025](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/4121). The question is about proper **vertex** q-colorings, normalized by the vertex count using the geometric mean. Part (a) asks for Petersen minimization in triangle-free cubic graphs for all q≥4. Part (b) asks for Heawood maximization in C4-free cubic graphs; forbidding a 4-cycle does not forbid triangles. They already record failure of the (a) extension to q=3. Their disproof of five other occupancy/independence conjectures does not answer this coloring question. Neither does the 2026 disproof of Jaeger's Petersen **edge**-coloring conjecture. Our q ranges, while independent of graph order, do not establish full Conjecture 5.2. The varying-target homomorphism question (the authors' Question 5.1) also is not settled here.
2. G. Perarnau and W. Perkins, [Counting independent sets in cubic graphs of given girth](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2018.04.009): analogous Petersen/Heawood results for the hard-core occupancy fraction/independence polynomial. That partition function is not the q-coloring one.
3. E. Davies, M. Jenssen, and W. Perkins, [A proof of the upper matching conjecture for large graphs](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10330735), Def. 5, Thms. 7,16,17, §6. Their strict (d,g)-optimality comparison uses short cycle counts, including Moore graphs; perturbative extremality for matchings and independent sets, including a coefficientwise Heawood theorem. Their approach is a direct conceptual precursor. We do not claim the idea of exploiting a Moore graph's per-vertex cycle gap or of comparing early connected terms to a uniform tail. Here we specialize to the chromatic polynomial, with triangle-allowed C4-free case, and derive finite palette certificates.
4. M. Abért and T. Hubai, [Benjamini–Schramm convergence and the distribution of chromatic roots](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3861). P. Csikvári and P. E. Frenkel, [Benjamini–Schramm continuity of root moments of graph polynomials](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2015.07.009). Their moment expansions in small connected and 2-connected subgraph counts precede the present elementary finite coefficient implementation. Whitney's chromatic inclusion–exclusion and block factorization are classical. Weakening those known facts is not a novelty claim.
5. F. Bencs and G. Regts, [Improved bounds on the zeros of the chromatic polynomial of graphs and claw-free graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04366), [published version](https://escholarship.org/content/qt91f52796/qt91f52796.pdf), specifically Table 1.1's K_3≤2.321, not just the headline bound 4.25Δ. The argument only uses the corollary that subcubic roots have modulus below 7. The uniform absolute chromatic-root-bound method goes back to A. Sokal. This proof does not give a new bound on roots.
6. D. Galvin's counting question and the [reverse Sidorenko theorem of Sah, Sawhney, Stoner, Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09462) concern extremizers including complete bipartite graphs without the same short-cycle restrictions. Related large-q dense-graph maximization (Loh–Pikhurko–Sudakov, Ma–Naves) is not a bounded-degree result with these restrictions.
