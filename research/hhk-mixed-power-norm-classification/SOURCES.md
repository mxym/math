# Sources, prior results and scope

1. [M. Hayajneh, S. Hayajneh & F. Kittaneh, *On some classical trace inequalities and a new Hilbert–Schmidt norm inequality*, Math. Inequal. Appl. 21 (2018), 1175–1183](https://files.ele-math.com/articles/mia-21-81.pdf). Conjecture 1 (p. 1177) asks for every unitarily invariant norm, with $A\ge0$ and $B$ Hermitian, not necessarily positive. Theorem 1 in §2 proves the Hilbert–Schmidt inequality throughout $1/2\le s\le t\le1$. The calculation on p. 1179 proves the special pair $(1/2,1)$ for **every** unitarily invariant norm. The present work does not challenge either result. Its fixed-pair classification excludes $t=1$.
2. [trureturing, 2018 mixed-power monotonicity dossier and Lean result](https://www.trureturing.org/research/hayajneh-2018-unitarily-invariant-norm/). A $2\times2$ positive definite trace-norm example at $(s,t)=(1/2,3/4)$ with squared-norm gap $89/729$ already refutes the original universal conjecture. The dossier explicitly distinguishes the operator norm and the universal high Schatten-exponent restrictions, which that example does not settle. The Lean statement was not recompiled for this note.
3. **Related norm rigidity and corner deletion are classical.** [Bhatia, Choi & Davis (1989), *Comparing a matrix to its off-diagonal part*, Proposition 3.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-0348-9144-8_4), as described by [Bhatia, *Pinching, Trimming, Truncating, and Averaging of Matrices*, introduction](https://indico.ictp.it/event/a08167/material/0/0.pdf), characterizes the Frobenius norm up to scaling by monotonicity under entrywise absolute-value reduction. [Banks & Harcharras (2004), *On the norm of an idempotent Schur multiplier on the Schatten class*, Lemma 1](https://mospace.umsystem.edu/bitstreams/3fdcf73f-5337-4e4c-8fbe-6c9dff0e5545/download) study the two-dimensional mask deleting a single corner. They prove noncontractivity away from $p=2$ (using duality for conjugate exponents), and already use a one-variable corner completion of a matrix of ones in their proof. After a column permutation it is the same type of symmetric corner model. These results precede the 2018 mixed-power problem. The contribution claimed here is the implication from comparison of each fixed mixed-power pair to the restricted real symmetric corner tests, the resulting classification, and the explicit failures for the mixed-power expression, not a new general characterization of contractive Schur multipliers. The pairwise circle argument proves the needed restricted-test rigidity without treating a projection hypothesis for all unrestricted matrix entries as already established.
4. [Liu, Wang & Sun (2017)](https://files.ele-math.com/articles/jmi-11-77.pdf) settle a different conjecture involving sums of products of commuting pairs, and [Bottazzi, Elencwajg, Larotonda & Varela](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7472) treat expressions involving powers of two positive operators. These are not the 2018 fixed-pair classification here.
