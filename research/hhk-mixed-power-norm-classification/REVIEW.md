# Independent review: fixed-interior mixed-power norm comparison

**PASS on the stated theorem.** This is an independent internal AI check, not external peer review or a priority determination. Scope: any fixed dimension d≥2 and any single pair 1/2≤s<t<1; A is positive semidefinite, B Hermitian with no sign restriction. The checked private proof had SHA-256 `3c987e3db5b6a4f2102b68796dff5eda65df582aa67cb6c1790c14fcc87d803b`.

1. **Original question and positive cases.** The [2018 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.7153/mia-2018-21-81), Conjecture 1, §2 Theorem 1 and the calculation on p. 1179, distinguish the known Hilbert–Schmidt result on the entire interval from the endpoint 1/2,1 valid for every unitarily invariant norm. Its Conjecture 2, involving logarithms and a separate spectral restriction, is not the question here. In the eigenbasis of A the entry at (i,j) is (aᵢᵘ+aⱼ¹⁻ᵘ)Bᵢⱼ. Pairing (i,j),(j,i) under the squared Frobenius sum yields coshes of real numbers with magnitude increasing in u≥1/2. The passage to singular A for the interior pair uses only positive powers. [trureturing](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Problems/hayajneh-2018-unitarily-invariant-norm.html) already supplies a dimension-two, positive-definite A and B trace-norm reversal at 1/2,3/4 with a squared gap 89/729. Therefore the bare refutation and positivity of an earlier trace-norm example are prior.

2. **Two-coordinate test.** Direct multiplication for A=diag(1,δ) verifies that the active off-diagonals tend to √(xy) for both exponents. The lower right is h times [δ^(u+t−1)+δ^(t−u)]; at u=s both exponents are strictly positive, and at u=t they are 2t−1>0 and 0. The other block contributes exactly diag(z). Each B for positive δ is finite; the quantifiers allow the sequence to be unbounded. Alternatively, homogeneity lets one separately divide both sides of the comparison by a common positive norm of B for every fixed δ. No upper bound on the condition number of A is imposed. The nonsymmetric intermediate matrices present no difficulty to continuity of the norm.

3. **Tangent, gauge and folding.** The characteristic polynomial of the limiting block H(h) is λ²−(x−y+h)λ+(x−y)h−xy. Its two simple roots at zero are x,−y, with derivatives y/(x+y), x/(x+y). The active singular-value derivatives are therefore y/(x+y),−x/(x+y), even if x=y: differentiating *unsigned sorted* singular values at equal x,y would not be legitimate, but the proof instead follows the opposite-sign eigenvalue branches and then uses symmetry of the gauge. Coincidences with spectator singular values likewise cause no problem. The gauge is Euclidean Lipschitz by the norm triangle inequality and finite-dimensional norm equivalence; consequently its composition with any fixed circular arc is Lipschitz, hence absolutely continuous and differentiable almost everywhere *on that arc*. Taking a small step of either sign gives the claimed one-sided quadratic-error bound at each interior point, with no uniform-in-θ remainder needed. At differentiability points it forces zero derivative and hence constancy of the entire arc, endpoints included. Folding successively with unchanged spectators yields proportionality to the Euclidean norm on the entire nonnegative orthant. This is fixed-dimension and requires no cross-dimension normalization of matrix norms.

4. **Every indicated Schatten exponent and the signs.** With r=δ^(1−t), each deviation at u=s is o(r) because s+t−1>0 and t−s>0. At u=t the first perturbation is E=[[0,2],[0,c]]; terms δᵗ/r vanish. An independent way to find the singular-value derivatives is to use M(h)=H+hE. Its squared singular values equal 16,1 at zero; their sum has derivative 8 and their product has derivative −8(3c−4). Solving these two linear equations gives singular-value derivatives (4+c)/5 and (4−4c)/5. On substituting T=4^(p−1) and c=−(4T+9)/(T−4) the Schatten-power first derivative equals −p for every finite p≥1 other than 2. For p<2, c>8/3 so B is strictly positive; for p>2 it is indefinite. The operator norm corresponds to the simple largest singular value; c=−8 gives −4/5. A strict negative leading coefficient suffices for every sufficiently small δ at fixed s,t,p. It is not a uniform δ threshold as the parameters vary. The rational standalone op-norm example passes exact 2×2 Sylvester tests.

5. **Scope and checks.** The result proved here neither includes 1/2<s<t=1 nor asserts analogous high-p failures for B≥0 or A≥I. The 1/2,1 endpoint and equal-parameter pairs do not contradict the classification. The script `independent.py` does not call the author's checker or use floating point; it verifies the rational bracket, independently derives the perturbation responses via trace and determinant, checks nine finite examples at p=1,3,4, and verifies 512 signed-eigenvalue derivative identities. It was executed under `python -B -O`, and the logical proof, not its sample checks, covers all parameters and all unitarily invariant norms.
