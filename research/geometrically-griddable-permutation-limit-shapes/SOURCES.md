# Prior work, scope, and reproducibility

## Which part is new in this note?

* Albert, Atkinson, Bouvel, Ruškuc and Vatter, [*Geometric grid classes of permutations*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2013-05804-7), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.6319v2): Propositions 4.2 and 7.1 supply the partial-multiplication refinement and trace encoding. The marked, hereditary witness language and the canonical ungridded section are **their proof of Theorem 8.1**, not a new regular encoding. Theorem 10.3 describes subclasses as finite unions of generalized grid classes with isolated point cells; this is a structural description, not an assertion about uniformly weighted ungridded samples or their random limits. Here the pointed prefix/suffix witness test, together with the lexicographic trace scanner, yields primitive cyclic components for the unambiguous presentation.
* Bevan's [thesis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.06688), Chapter 6 (Propositions 6.1 and 6.3), treats the shape of connected **monotone** grid classes. Chapter 7, Theorem 7.2, and [Bevan's geometric-grid paper](https://doi.org/10.37236/4834) give the **exponential growth rate** of full geometric grid classes. The two models need not agree. No novelty is claimed for these results.
* Alshammari and Bevan, [*On the asymptotic enumeration and limit shapes of monotone grid classes of permutations*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00607v4), Theorem 3.10, give deterministic shapes for connected monotone grid classes. This already applies to the full geometric classes when the connected row-column graph is a tree. Immediately after their theorem they also discuss, without proof, simplex-random masses for several dominant components in the disconnected monotone setting. This is a direct precedent for the random-phase description. Their paragraph considers the gridded model; applying its component weights verbatim to an ungridded permutation can change the law, as below. Theorem 3.10 itself concerns connected matrices and is unaffected by the example.
* A simple scalar random-phase law for polynomial-growth classes also follows from the explicit greedy block-length decomposition in Falgas-Ravry, Markström, Skerman and Räty, [arXiv:2610.34328v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34328v1), §§3.1–3.3: the dominant families are counted by weak compositions with bounded decorations. Their stated Theorems 1.2–1.4 concern logical limiting probabilities, including a zero–one result for geometric classes of signed permutation matrices. Markström and Räty, [arXiv:2610.22218v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22218v2), Theorem 1, prove a logical convergence law **and** the exponential-times-polynomial count asymptotic for all small classes (upper growth below their threshold). Thus the count assertion in this manuscript is not new on that overlap. Braunfeld's [decidability result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04201) gives effective regular/enumerative structure, not the permutation scaling law.
* Goldwurm and Lonati, [*Pattern occurrences in multicomponent models*](https://lonati.di.unimi.it/lavori/stacs05.pdf), analyze symbol-count limits in rational stochastic models with chains of components. Goldwurm, Macci, Vignati and Villa, [arXiv:2602.02391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02391), treat multivariate symbol statistics in primitive rational models. Perron bridge laws, summable subdominant contributions and uniform limits for weak compositions are standard word-side tools. The proof uses them on the particular canonical geometric encoding, before the permuton pushforward.
* The [November 2026 permuton survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12104v1), §5.2, distinguishes geometric from monotone classes, describes the connected monotone shape work and cites the geometric-grid structural paper. The repository's [geometric-grid catropy note](../geometric-grid-catropy/README.md) concerns independent segment sampling. Its [geometric-grid large-deviation note](../geometrically-griddable-permuton-ldp/README.md) concerns exponential probabilities for arbitrary subclasses, not the distribution on the zero-rate set. Its [oscillating-class counterexamples](../permutation-class-limit-law-counterexamples/README.md) concern different larger classes. None should be counted a second time here.

## One example of why the sampling convention matters

Take the diagonal three-cell matrix with signs $(+,+,-)$ in direct-sum order. A gridded permutation is a weak composition $(i,j,k)$ of $n$. If $k\ge2$, the corresponding ungridded permutation depends only on $s=i+j$: it is the increasing permutation on the smallest $s$ values followed by the decreasing permutation of the largest $n-s$. Different $s\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}$ give different permutations; $k=0$ and $k=1$ give just the identity. Thus under ungridded uniform sampling the initial increasing mass converges to a uniform $U$ on $[0,1]$. Under gridded sampling the number of triples with $i+j=s$ is $s+1$, and the mass converges instead to a $\mathrm{Beta}(2,1)$ variable. The terminal cases have vanishing probability in both models. The limiting permuton has an increasing diagonal piece of mass $U$ followed in direct sum by a decreasing piece of mass $1-U$; the breakpoint is identifiable in the plot. Hence the two laws differ even *after* forgetting the gridding. This polynomial example illustrates de-duplication, not the new range of the theorem.
