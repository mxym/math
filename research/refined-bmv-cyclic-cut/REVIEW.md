**Finding.** No mathematical defect found in either the full positive-exponent cyclic-cut equivalence (including the unboundedness at fixed real dimension 3) or the normalized integer-average threshold. The zero-exponent reduction, open-square qualifier, and explicit connection to the old positive theorem are essential, not optional niceties.

1. Independently deriving the cut: each occurrence has either spectral state H or L. X in state L costs its exponent; Y in state L costs its exponent; each adjacent HH costs 1/2. Set I=the high X vertices, J=the low Y vertices. The total cost is exactly d, including the two copies of the same pair for a word with one run of each color. Summing constants at the network endpoints checks both flow conditions and the min-cut orientation. Selecting both major eigenvectors can cost one half on an edge without a negative coefficient; that point is what the old zero-overlap test could miss.
2. The proposed geometric vectors have positive length-one normalization, orthonormality on each side, and cross Gram matrix [[sqrt(t),sqrt((1-t)/2)],[sqrt((1-t)/2),(1-sqrt(t))/2]]. Every cyclic projection product is a product of *real nonnegative* overlaps, so there are no phase/cancellation objections. For t below 1/4 every non-HH overlap is at least 1/4. The clustered denominator is t+2t(1−t)/2+t²(1−sqrt(t))²/4, hence at most 3t. One summand gives the t^(d−1) lower bound. The singular rank-two matrices have three distinct eigenvalues (one is zero); regularization is at a fixed positive t, after the target ratio is chosen, and produces real positive definite witnesses of the same size.
3. For feasible margins the two powers at a run can be placed on its incoming/outgoing boundaries because they are powers of one matrix; concatenating then cyclically choosing a start produces the claimed balanced factors. Factors with zero edge weight are identities, including on kernels by the prescribed power-zero convention. For 0<s≤1/2 ALT really has exponent 1/(2s)≥1; raising the Schatten norm to power 1/s inserts precisely this exponent on the positive squared modulus. Schatten Hölder uses reciprocals s whose sum is 1. Alternatively insert zero powers to rewrite Y^sX^s as consecutive run pairs (0,s),(s,0); all eligible prefixes then alternate differences in [0,1/2] with the signs required after left and right steps. Thus [AHO Theorem 2.1 and (1.8)–(1.9)](https://files.ele-math.com/articles/mia-03-32.pdf#page=3) apply after a trace rotation. This proves the full valid side already implicit in AHO, not the purported originality of that side. Their stronger *singular-value* inequality applies to the appropriately rotated product, not automatically to an arbitrarily chosen original start of the word.
4. With k=2 the cycle is K2,2, so only c=1 singleton/complement-singleton cuts can be obstructive. Their least capacity is 1/2+min(a,1−a)+min(b,1−b); equality counts as valid. If any of the four exponents vanishes, the original cyclic product reduces to Tr(XY), and this can happen outside the diamond on the square edges. A statement omitting the edges/open-square qualification would be false. AHO Cor.2.2(i) already gives the positive diamond; their Cor.4.3/4.4 and Thm4.5 on restricted spectra do not prevent the present witness. [Carlen–Lieb, last paragraph following Example 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.06136#page=12) genuinely ask if the upper-right-quadrant 3/2 condition is sharp.
5. In the integer question one must average *all linear words* with equal binomial weight, not inequivalent cyclic classes. Reversal pairs ensure reality in general. For cuts with c=0, unless trivial, two distinct neighbors at each end force at least two left small runs and two right small runs when k≥2; for c=1 both required sums are nonempty. When k=1 the parallel edges cannot give c=1. This establishes the hit side including 3,6 and 4,4. For the failing side n,m≥3; the six-run word contains exactly n As and m Bs even when n or m is 3. Two secondary modes on the A-side and two on the B-side give the numerator contribution t^(2n+2m) times (1/sqrt(2))^4(1/2)^2=1/16. Its linear-word weight 1/binom(n+m,n) is legitimate because every other trace in this real example is nonnegative. Denominator t^(nm)+t^(2nm)/4. Thus the reciprocal threshold, unbounded fixed dimension-three statement, and least failures at total 9 all follow. No lower refined BMV assertion follows.
