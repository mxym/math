# Source and duplicate check (18 November 2026)

This is an author-side search, not independent peer review or a guarantee of priority. The precise conclusions and the single-word/average distinction are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

* [Ando, Hiai, Okubo (2000), Eqs. (1.6), (1.8)–(1.9), Theorem 2.1, Corollaries 2.2, 2.4 and §4](https://files.ele-math.com/articles/mia-03-32.pdf): the general multiletter run pattern and the sufficient partial-sum conditions are theirs; their log-majorization even implies a stronger inequality where applicable. After inserting zero powers between balanced pairs and a cyclic rotation, their positive theorem yields our entire sufficient direction. Their diamond is exactly the upper-valid *interior range* in the four-factor case; they also proved every (4,4) word and the 2×2 positive results. We give a combinatorial characterization and necessity with a real size-three witness, not an independent discovery of their positive diamond or their proof strategy. Corollary 4.3 gives the positive result when each matrix has at most two distinct eigenvalues; Corollary 4.4 covers every 2×2 pair. The singular counterexample here has three distinct eigenvalues (0,t,1), not two.
* [Plevnik (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13226-016-0180-9) gave an explicit failure of the real-part form. [Carlen–Lieb (2022), §4, Example 4.2 and the subsequent sharpness question](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.06136) gave a different example at s=t=.79; the original source asks whether the s+t=3/2 threshold is sharp. The upper-right result of the present real exponent criterion is precisely a sharpness answer (with degeneracies at s=1 or t=1 excluded). Their special matrix class and Golden–Thompson monotonicity are not being challenged. [Hayajneh–Hayajneh–Kittaneh (2017), Cor. 2.6](https://www.theta.ro/jot/archive/2017-077-001/2017-077-001-005.pdf) proves a sufficient diamond for the real parts of *complex* exponents, not a full classification outside it. [S. Hayajneh (2024)](https://www.pmf.ni.ac.rs/filomat-content/2024/38-19/38-19-20-22715.pdf) treats two identical copies of a Hermitian factor and redistribution of powers of the other factor. We make no complex-exponent or new two-identical-factor claim.
* [Drury (2009), Theorems 2.1, 3.1](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/ELA/ela-articles/articles/vol18_pp13-20.pdf) classifies *ordered, noncyclic* product operator norms relative to the operator norm of AB. Partial-sum conditions, low-dimensional constructions and a divergence mechanism are related, not new as general methods. The comparison here concerns the trace of a cyclic word against Tr(XY), which is not invariant under the same operations as an operator norm. No theorem of Drury is contradicted; he does not assert this trace criterion. [Alaifari–Cheng–Pierce–Steinerberger](https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/14831) treat real symmetric 2×2 operator-norm rearrangement. [Bhatia (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4863899) gives a related Hilbert–Schmidt-norm implication in a partial exponent range.
* [OQP 40, original statement](https://oqp.iqoqi.oeaw.ac.at/refinement-of-the-bessis-moussa-villani-conjecture): two inequalities around the **binomially normalized** word trace average. [Cha–Lee, arXiv:2603.19927v5, §§II–V, Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19927v5) already found real size-three failure, unbounded ratios for every n,m≥5, the nonnegative spectral-projection expansion, and the paired six-run bridge. In particular their Proposition 3 is exactly the zero-crossing neighborhood-cost minimization for their fixed projection graph; this cut-like formula is prior work. In the full cyclic criterion the missing overlap is instead of order sqrt(t), so a crossing costs 1/2 and the necessity test also detects one-crossing cuts. Our negative integer construction uses their projections and bridge with two different small spectral weights; the same extension is elementary. Their fixed equally decaying weights give order 4 versus min(n,m); the differing weights give order 2(n+m) versus nm. We do not claim the first counterexample to the general upper. The new exponent threshold has first failures at length nine (4,5) and (5,4), not the previously established (5,5).
* [Dinh (2026), §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17782v1) analyzed the spectral bridge, proved the original clustered upper for m=2 and supplied a pinched lower bound. [Dinh–Le–Vuong (Oct. 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31851) prove the **corrected pinching** conjecture for all orders; that reference distribution is not the clustered trace. [Hägele (Oct. 2026), Theorems 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00129) treats the other, trace-exponential **lower** inequality, including min(n,m)≤2 in every dimension, arbitrary integer multiplicities n,m for d≤2, and n+m≤7 in d≤3. The present result neither proves nor disproves the general lower bound; original BMV positivity was already proved by Stahl.

## Exact checks (author-side, not independent review)

Run `python3 verify.py` (standard library). A cyclic one-variable interval solver and a brute-cut enumerator agreed for all 1,004 nonconstant binary words of total length ≤9. The universal integer-pair threshold matched every one of the 16,356 labeled words through total length 13 (78 positive ordered exponent pairs); the single-word criterion was also checked against 3,025 rational four-factor cases. Direct exact matrix multiplication of the binomially normalized word sum on rational strictly positive definite 3×3 matrices gave 45 < ratio < 46 at (4,5) and 521 < ratio < 522 at (3,7). The (4/5,4/5) four-factor check on rational PSD matrices gave 100 < ratio < 101. These bounded enumerations are not the proof of universal validity or unboundedness.
