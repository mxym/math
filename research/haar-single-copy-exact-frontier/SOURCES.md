# Primary sources, scope, and duplication check

* Amit Behera, Zvika Brakerski, Or Sattath, and Omri Shmueli, [Pseudorandomness with Proof of Destruction and Applications](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/543), TCC 2023, pp. 125–154, [proceedings chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-48624-1_5). [AICR c/0104](https://aicr.info/c/0104/) records the one-Haar-state-versus-many negligible-distance question as its §1.3, p. 10, and the weaker one-sided domination as Lemma 2 and the discussion around p. 18. The ePrint metadata records a 20 September 2023 revision; the current PDF endpoint was not accessible in the source check. We attribute those exact later-version locators to the catalogue rather than claiming we checked the revision bytes. The earlier accessible [17 April 2023 preprint](https://opencryptology.org/2023/543/versions/20230417T185733Z.pdf) has different open-problem text and pagination. The question is only the independent classical-output comparison stated in §1 of the note; the optimization allows even more classical adaptation. No cryptographic application of the source is claimed here.
* Sitan Chen, Jordan Cotler, Hsin-Yuan Huang, and Jerry Li, [Exponential separations between learning with and without quantum memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.05881v2), FOCS 2021, §5.2, Theorem 5.11, Lemma 5.12, Eq. (120) and Theorem 5.13. They give the same two averaged ensembles in the no-quantum-memory adaptive model; the path likelihood lower bound already yields an O(t^2/d) bound on total variation, which in particular implies the negligible-distance comparison. They also use repeated same-basis measurements with O(sqrt(d)) samples to achieve constant distinguishing probability. Neither the Haar Gram permanent likelihood, the scaling, nor the use of a fixed basis is new here. The distinct-record factor in our exact TV, best over all adaptive trees in the stated range, is the additional claim.
* Jacob Beckey, Luke Coffman, Ariel Shlosberg, Louis Schatzki, and Felix Leditzky, [Product testing with single-copy measurements](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07820v2), §3.1, Lemma 3.1, Proposition 3.2 and Eq. (3.6). They explicitly identify the Gram permanent and cite its Hadamard-type lower bound; they characterize its equality by orthogonality and give the same O(t^2/d) bound in the adaptive model. Their Open Question 1 concerns purity testing against arbitrary states separated from the pure set and further locality restrictions, not the exact finite binary Haar-mixture experiment here.
* Oren Akresh and Jacob Beckey, [Tight Bounds for Purity and Product Testing from Partial Transposition](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27217v1), Theorem 1 and Eq. (5): for the same two averaged states, their larger PPT-BOTH class satisfies the finite bound t(t-1)/(2d), which is sharp in scaling, not the equality of (1). Srinivasan Arunachalam and Louis Schatzki, [Optimal Stabilizer Testing and Learning with Limited Quantum Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02444), Appendix B, Theorem B.3, treats positive coherent-memory budgets. Sitan Chen, Brice Huang, Jerry Li, and Allen Liu, [Tight Bounds for Quantum State Certification with Incoherent Measurements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07155), establishes copy complexity for the different, composite mixedness/state-certification hypotheses; this note does not give general testing guarantees.
* V. S. Shchesnovich, [Explicit form of the Bayesian posterior estimate of a quantum state under the uninformative prior](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.5158), Section 3, Eqs. (7), (9)–(12): pure Haar prior and arbitrary rank-one POVMs, evidence and posterior mean evaluated with Gram permanents and their minors. Naqueeb Ahmad Warsi, Ayanava Dasgupta, and Masahito Hayashi, [Exact Posterior Prediction from Product Haar Measurements and a Randomized-Mesh Maximum-Likelihood Bridge](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00117v1), §§III-C–F: the same permanent/minor structure for posterior prediction from the fixed product Haar POVM, with decision-rule optimality for quantum relative-entropy risk. Our operator B in (5) is the corresponding unnormalized one-particle marginal up to the stated convention/scale; the CCR derivation is only a proof of the needed identity. These results do not optimize the TV of the two competing record laws over which one-copy POVM to choose at each history.

A bounded literature search through 28 November 2026 used the source title/identifier and exact/adaptive/finite-sample/Gram-permanent/purity-testing queries and reviewed the cited related primary papers. We found the earlier negligible-distance proof, joint-copy/PPT bounds and exact prediction identities above, not the finite optimum (1) or the all-dimension three-step optimum (2) for classical feed-forward. The latter claims are limited to the stated probabilistic experiment and do not assert absolute priority. The nearby repository note [Quantum-channel all-copy contraction](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/ee8ecd27c770ce64fb31f84f4b0f694a23c65d81/research/quantum-channel-all-copy-contraction) instead bounds subleading spectral radii of independent products of same-sampled channels; it does not address these transcript laws.
