# Sources, model correspondence, and overlap

**Direct prior in the unified repository.** [Vanishing distance of fixed-index split quasi-abelian codes](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/b9ca8e3573888ffc25cae820bf0545f6efcb1dda/research/split-quasi-abelian-distance-collapse/PROOF.md) (commit b9ca8e3573888ffc25cae820bf0545f6efcb1dda, PROOF blob 51cc786a0d584239d59ff6d9cbcd037d7a15d5f3), §§2–5, already gives the split Fourier constituent/common-line/cube/product-support argument, deterministic O(1/m) in the binary group, and the split systematic quotient lift. It refutes c/0105 for all q>2. The present supplement adds bounded-exponent torsion cosets and trace descent for nonsplit character fields in Theorem 1, and the independent partial-spread probability bound in Theorem 2. The q=2 nonsplit family is an additional counterexample, not a second claim of first solving c/0105.

1. Maxime Bombar, Geoffroy Couteau, Alain Couvreur, Clément Ducros, [Correlated Pseudorandomness from the Hardness of Quasi-Abelian Decoding, ePrint 2023/845](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/845), [full PDF](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/845.pdf). Section 2.2 p.9 gives the informal extension to arbitrary abelian groups. Section 4.5 pp.19–20, Remark 18 and the paragraph after Theorem 20 specify constant k and ell, growing |G|, and that their setting enforces systematic form; the paragraph heuristically expects linear minimum distance. There is no numbered GV conjecture with a formal probability threshold in that paper. [AICR c/0105](https://aicr.info/c/0105/) spells out the plain GV/high-probability assertion for an independent uniform systematic generator; (5) disproves even a positive constant relative distance uniformly over growing abelian groups. The algebra quotient map itself also appears in [BCCD] §6.1; here its only use is to lift codewords and compare coefficient-Hamming distances. No syndrome assumption or cryptographic conclusion is drawn.
2. Zhe Li, Hongqing Liu, Chaoping Xing, Yizhou Yao, Chen Yuan, [More Efficient SNARKs via Quasi-Abelian Codes, ePrint 2026/939](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/939), [mirrored full PDF](https://opencryptology.org/2026/939.pdf). Section 1.1 p.5 directly asks whether QA code is asymptotically good for fixed index and field size after Theorem 1.1 and Table 1, in the discussion of G=(Z/2)^m. Definition 3.11 pp.11–12 gives the rank-one group-ring row ensemble, and Section 4.2 pp.21–22, item 2, Algorithm 3, and Theorem 4.4, use a systematic first block fixed to 1 with other blocks uniform. The archived split result (also recovered in Theorem 1) applies to both ensembles whenever their rates stay positive; their specified systematic rate is exactly 1/c. Their Theorem 1.1 concerns concrete large fields, with an additive log_p |G|/c gap, and is not contradicted. For fixed p, the archived result answers asymptotic goodness only for their stated group family; the different target of a tight probability curve is not resolved.
3. Yun Fan and Liren Lin, [Thresholds of Random Quasi-Abelian Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5377), Theorem 2.1, and [Asymptotic Properties of Quasi-Group Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00958), §5; the threshold quoted as Theorem 20 of [BCCD] holds for fixed G and growing index. Fan–Lin 2022 Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.5 do supply a fixed-index two-block result on special growing group-order sequences, requiring log_q |G|/μ_q(|G|)→0, not on the bounded-exponent sequence here. Philippe Gaborit and Gilles Zémor, [Asymptotic improvement of the Gilbert–Varshamov bound for linear codes](https://eudml.org/doc/10250), Annales de l'Institut Fourier 58 (2008), §3, use structured double-circulant families with special growing cyclic parameters, not arbitrary cyclic sequences. Binary group-algebra results of [Bazzi and Mitter](https://mitter.lids.mit.edu/publications/108_some_randomized_IEEE.pdf), IEEE TIT 52 (2006), Theorem 2.1, instead require growing minimal nontrivial constituent dimension in their goodness regime; that hypothesis fails for the bounded-exponent case here.
4. Somphong Jitman and San Ling, [Quasi-abelian codes](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-013-9878-4), Designs, Codes and Cryptography 74 (2015), §3, is a direct antecedent for group-algebra Fourier constituents and the elementary-group cyclotomic classes. Martino Borello, Cem Güneri, Elif Saçıkara, Patrick Solé, [The Concatenated Structure of Quasi-Abelian Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01246), §3 and Theorems 4.1–4.2, obtain distance lower bounds and asymptotically good families by fixing the underlying group and allowing the index to grow in their explicit proof. Neither conflicts with a fixed-index upper bound for bounded exponent. The transform, CRT decomposition, trace, and cube density inequality are standard ingredients, not claimed as discoveries.
5. László Babai, Amir Shpilka, Daniel Štefankovič, [Locally testable cyclic codes](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/b-shp-ste.pdf), Theorems 1.9–1.10, discuss Berman's cyclic prime-factor restrictions but do not address the elementary abelian fixed-index family. [Abelian Codes in Principal Ideal Group Algebras](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2012.2236383) and the [modular-code distance-obstruction package](https://www.evidencepress.org/releases/modular-code-obstructions/) concern nonsemisimple repeated-root or characteristic-dividing-group order obstructions; the cited obstruction results do not address the coprime Fourier constituent regime. The 2026 WAIFI chapter [The Non-asymptotic Landscape of Abelian Codes](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-27574-5_5) has related title and abstract for codes that are ideals; its full text was not accessible during the overlap check, so no absolute priority is claimed for the index-one special case.

6. Li et al., [ePrint 2026/196](https://opencryptology.org/2026/196.pdf), Theorem 1.2 and §4, give older concrete lower-distance bounds for the odd-field elementary 2-group. The same authors' [ePrint 2026/1940, November archived PDF](https://opencryptology.org/2026/1940/versions/20261112T174349Z.pdf), §6.2 Theorem 6.6, restate and apply the 2026/939 concrete bound for split extension-field examples; the fixed-field growing-group conclusion remains distinct. Related scheme-specific publications, including ePrint 2025/892, are outside our model; no cryptanalytic procedure is studied here.

A scoped overlap search (2026-11-27 UTC) used the named original and follow-up papers, citations in both directions, and searches combining quasi-abelian, fixed index, elementary abelian, bounded exponent, Fourier support, affine cubes, and asymptotic badness. The unified branch duplicate for the split-elementary core is explicitly credited above. We did not find the nonsplit/bounded-exponent extension or the independent-band tail (5) in the papers checked. This is not a priority claim; the scope is the coefficient-Hamming theorem only.
