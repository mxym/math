For fixed mean r, the logit density has both the correct Jacobian and center tilt. Conditional on the scaled absolute logit V, the logistic sign probability is independent of a and strictly decreasing for r>1/2. Taking the ratio of the marginal cosh powers cancels the common numerator; its log derivative is strictly positive at every v>0 for the larger a. Covariance of an increasing likelihood ratio with the decreasing sign probability gives strict decline of F_a(1/2). Reflect for r<1/2. At the mean (assume r<1/2), the two branches of D share a level and have root displacements L,R. The derivative of D(r+bl)−D(r−l), after positive factors, is (b²−1)rq−(q−r)(b²+b)l. When b=1 it is negative, giving R>L. For b>1 it begins positive and then changes sign at most once; at the strictly positive zero of the difference the derivative is strictly negative. The derivative in b is positive, so R/L rises along the roots. The Jacobian for each branch exactly cancels x(1−x), giving branch densities proportional to e^(−az)/L or /R. Higher a downweights large z; the strictly increasing left branch probability then has strictly smaller expectation. The density-ratio sign pattern proves the known unique crossing, now strictly located between the two landmarks. At r=1/2, symmetry gives the crossing, not equality of cdfs away from it. Hornik (Entropy 2024 Thm1) varies one shape, not the radial shape scale; Portnoy–Torrado–Veerman already give existence and uniqueness.

For the floored-binomial extension in Fokkink–Meester–Pelekis (ALEA 2023 end of §7), differentiation gives the beta representation with the correct two shapes k and (b−1)k+e. The ratio to the k=1 density is strictly increasing before 1/b, so the ratio of the cdfs is also increasing there. The e=0 endpoint follows from the radial theorem. Adding the final Bernoulli contributes u times the previous binomial mass at k−1, equal at u=1/b to u times the new binomial mass at k. The latter is a maximum point mass, nonincreasing after convolution with another independent block. This supplies the classical reciprocal endpoint, which Chaundy–Bullard already prove. In the non-reciprocal case the ceiling is no larger than the hypothesis b0, the floor at k is at most bk−1, and the floor at 1 is b−1. These handle all parameter endpoints including t=1 and p=0. The 2022 right-skew theorem supplies a legitimate alternative at the e=0 integer endpoints: chosen nonunique mode k is allowed and the extra block has integer mean one, with left-loadedness from Lemma 2.1. No new unrelated discrete endpoint theorem or general unequal-stakes result is claimed.

For Fokkink–Papavassiliou–Pelekis (PMS 2022, Definition 1.2, Conj.4.1), the binomial ratio for reflected masses follows by multiplying the two adjacent ratios. The difference of numerator and denominator factors with the asserted linear dependence on j(j+1) and begins strictly positive when u=n−m>m. Thus the ratios first rise and then fall starting at one. Extending the shorter side by zeros gives an initial nonnegative and terminal negative pattern for d_j. The tails A_i then begin nonincreasing until they are nonpositive, and all later are nonpositive. The finite full sum of A_i is exactly the first-moment difference E(m−X)=0; its last term is strictly negative, so its first term is strictly positive. Every prefix is nonnegative, and restriction to the defined indices gives both L1 and L2. At n=2m symmetry gives equality. The reflected approach and first-tail case already appear in older Simmons/Feldheim work and the Poisson argument of the conjecture paper.
