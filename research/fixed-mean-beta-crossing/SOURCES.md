# Original questions, earlier methods and limits of the search

1. **Beta crossing.** S. Portnoy, N. Torrado and J. J. P. Veerman, [*The Bunching and Monotonicity Properties of Families of Probability Distributions*, arXiv:2406.02894v1 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02894v1), Theorem 4.3 and §6. The authors prove uniqueness of the interior crossing for proportional beta shapes. They attribute to Subhash Kochar the question of monotonicity at one half; this is the question they describe as conjectural. Their [earlier author manuscript](https://web.pdx.edu/~veerman/bunching.pdf) is dated November 20, 2023 and has the discussion in §5. We do not assert their general multivariate suggestion or any condition on the variation of the crossing with the mean.
2. **Historical access limitation.** Portnoy et al. identify K. Alam, *A note on the incomplete beta function*, Clemson University Technical Report 316 (1979), as earlier work on the ranking-and-selection motivation. We could not retrieve the report itself using public full-text and catalogue searches by author, title, report number, ranking/selection, or the [Clemson Archives departmental indexing](https://media.clemson.edu/library/special_collections/findingaids/manuscripts/archives/archivesindex.htm) (Mathematical Sciences, Series 147). Its exact conclusions remain unverified: we do not maintain that it fails to prove either landmark. The claim here is a self-contained proof of the question **as stated in 2024**, not an assertion of historical priority.
3. **Neighboring continuous results.** I. Arab, P. E. Oliveira and T. Wiklund, [*Convex transform order of Beta distributions with some consequences*](https://doi.org/10.1111/stan.12233), and K. Hornik, [*Two Monotonicity Results for Beta Distribution Functions*, Theorem 1 (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11593182/), give comparisons of beta probabilities at their means when one of the two shape parameters is held fixed. Movement in the two coordinate directions has opposite effects; these do not directly give monotonicity on a ray of simultaneous changes. [Finner–Roters (1997)](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/oldpdf/A7n313.pdf) treat related shape inequalities. [R. F. Lyon, *On the median of the beta distribution in relation to the median of the gamma distribution* (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39202) concerns the position/bounds of a median; that is distinct from comparing probabilities at either fixed landmark as total shape varies.
4. **Inverse-level integration is not new.** [J. M. Kane, *Monotonic approach to central limits*, Proc. AMS 129 (2001), 2127–2133](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-00-05776-2), proves the classical binomial integral-mean result by pairing inverse branches of the same kernel. In beta notation the real-parameter quantity in his proof of Theorem 2.1 is the shifted function $I_r(tr,t(1-r)+1)$, not $I_r(tr,t(1-r))$. [F. Petrov's May 2024 proof](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/471193/proof-of-the-monotonicity-of-a-regularized-incomplete-beta-function#471238) pairs the branches for the shift $+2$. Our mean calculation uses the unshifted branch displacement ratio; the one-half comparison uses a scaled absolute logit rather than this pairing.
5. **Floored binomial maximum.** R. Fokkink, L. Meester and C. Pelekis, [*Optimizing stakes in simultaneous bets*, ALEA 20 (2023), 153–165, end of §7](https://alea.impa.br/articles/v20/20-07.pdf), explicitly ask whether the first index maximizes the displayed tail on the reciprocal-separated region. This is different from their Conjecture 1.1 attributed to E. Csóka, which optimizes over all weights. The reciprocal *diagonal* is the older [Chaundy–Bullard theorem (1960)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3614890) and follows from the general binomial integral-mean theorem of [Jogdeo–Samuels (1968)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177698243). On the other discrete endpoint a direct application of the right-skew/left-loaded convolution theorem already in the next source works as well (the mode chosen there need not be the mean or unique). Neither endpoint comparison in isolation is claimed as a separate new theorem.
6. **Left-loadedness.** R. Fokkink, S. Papavassiliou and C. Pelekis, [*On the monotonicity of tail probabilities*, PMS 42 (2022), 133–141](https://www.math.uni.wroc.pl/~pms/files_doi/g00050/g00050.pdf), Definitions 1.1–1.2, Theorem 1.1, Lemmas 2.1 and 3.6–3.7, and Conjecture 4.1. They already give both a centered-tail identity and a related reflected-mass argument for the Poisson law; they report binomial (L2) for means 1 and 2 and (L1) for $4\le m\le n/3$. The conjecture says at least one of the two conditions holds for $n\ge2m$. The [companion](LEFT-LOADED.md) derives both, without a noninteger-mean or negative-binomial extension.
7. **Historical credit for the discrete calculation.** The first tail imbalance is T. C. Simmons’ 1894–95 theorem. E. Feldheim, [*Nouvelle démonstration et généralisation d’un théorème du calcul des probabilités dû à Simmons* (1941), §2](https://www.numdam.org/item/JMPA_1941_9_20__1_0.pdf), already compares the two reflected binomial masses across their crossing and uses the full centered first moment. [A. Rényi, *Probability Theory* (1970), Ch. III, §18, Ex. 35, p. 167](https://real-eod.mtak.hu/12669/), explicitly gives the same sign formula for consecutive reflected-mass ratios, with threshold $j(j+1)=np(1-p)$, and credits Feldheim. Rényi's [1957 note](https://acta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/13819/1/math_018_fasc_001_002_021-022.pdf) also cites Feldheim’s 1938 and 1939 accounts. See also [Perrin–Redside (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spl.2006.09.006). The companion applies this classical calculation to the 2022 definition by summing the signed mass differences; the original crossing, centered identity and factorization are **not** new contributions.
8. **Search scope.** Public searches through November 19, 2026 covered the three original works, title/author and citation searches, proportional beta shapes, fixed-mean crossings, incomplete beta at one half and at its mean, comparing two gamma variables and scaled F degrees of freedom, the two different discrete conjectures, and Simmons/Feldheim. No complete historical citation census was available. In particular a nonretrieved source is not evidence of absence.
