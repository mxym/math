Sonnleitner arXiv:2508.16442v1 §1 immediately before Remark 2 explicitly asks for higher-dimensional analogues of the two earlier polygon factor laws. This proof concerns fixed full-dimensional polytopes (including nonsimple) under uniform volume or surface sampling. It is not the adjacent question of nonuniform densities on smooth bodies.

The tangent cone is pointed so a positive linear form bounds all radii below; the nearest Poisson norm is strictly positive and finite, giving D>0. At every extreme ray, arbitrarily distant points with distance tending to zero exist a.s., via disjoint small ambient balls in volume or relative facet balls in surface measure. This simultaneously rules out zero or negative dot product for a supporting nearest-point normal against any extreme ray (the assertion holds uniformly for any such vector once the sequence is fixed). It puts the actual support direction strictly in the dual interior, with uniform positive dot product against all cone directions. The bounded cap is locally finite, so the contact set is finite and nonempty with a positive scalar gap on other points. In combinations approaching the projection, the summed gaps tend to zero; coercivity then makes the weighted norms outside the contacts vanish too. Hence the projection is genuinely a finite convex combination and the indicated finite ball determines it even after adding arbitrary points of the cone outside. This excludes hidden nonlocal effects in obtuse cones.

Local translated balls have exactly the measure λ_v/n under the two iid sampling models and all lie in disjoint vertex neighborhoods for fixed R. Their joint multinomial occupancies converge in total variation to independent Poisson counts, and conditional local positions already agree. Coupling these configurations identically on success and using the determining test at all finitely many vertices yields TV convergence for the actual full finite distance vector. Convexity of distance gives the exact maximum over vertices, and local mass bounds furnish a uniform exponential-of-power tail sufficient for all positive moments. The strict small and large probability events for the limiting distances prove nondegeneracy.

Metric projection lies in the relative interior over a minimal affinely independent contact subset of size ≤d. For any admissible tuple, the supporting hyperplane has positive offset and hence zero intensity measure even on facets through the origin. Poisson Mecke therefore excludes extra contacts almost surely and counts each nearest projection exactly once after dividing by j!. Emptiness of the open cap is precisely the projection criterion with probability one. The cap measure scales like D_x^s; simultaneous tuple scaling shows each positive distance has intensity-zero level set. This establishes formula (1), continuity and finite positive moments without assuming all contact facets contain exactly d vertices.
