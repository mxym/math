# No finite parallel gadget attains the exponential growth rate for minimal vertex cuts

Research note. The value of the Erdős–Nešetřil growth constant is not determined here. The assertion concerns arbitrary finite *parallel two-terminal templates*, not all structured or recursive constructions. All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. A global minimal cut is an inclusion-minimal S for which the remaining graph is disconnected (has ≥2 components). A minimal s,t separator is inclusion-minimal for separating the specified vertices, which are not in S. Call such a separator **clean** if the remaining graph has exactly two components. This distinction is important.

## A finite characterization of the global exponential constant

Let c(N) be the largest number of global minimal cuts of a graph on N vertices; h(p) the largest number of clean s,t separators over connected two-terminal graphs with p internal vertices. For p≥1, h(p)≥1. Parallel composition identifies sources and sinks only. Combining a clean separator from each cell gives a clean separator of the union: every remaining vertex is attached to its own terminal, the terminals are disconnected, and restoring any cut vertex reconnects them. Thus h(p+q)≥h(p)h(q). By Fekete, β=lim h(p)^(1/p)=sup h(p)^(1/p) exists. In a connected graph a clean minimal s,t separator is a global minimal cut: each cut vertex has neighbors in both shores, so restoring any nonempty subset reconnects the graph. Hence c(p+2)≥h(p). A p=3 path gives β≥3^(1/3).

Conversely, in a global minimal cut S, every remaining component has a neighbor of **each** vertex of S: restoring one vertex must reconnect the entire graph. Consequently N(C)=S for every component C. A previously disconnected graph has just the minimal cut ∅ in this convention and cannot affect the exponential rate. For connected G of order N:
* Cuts with precisely two components are counted by at most N² h(N−2), choosing one representative terminal in each.
* For cuts with precisely three components choose (a,b,c), one point in each. In G+bc the same cut is a clean a,b separator: the two components containing b,c merge; restoring any cut vertex still joins both shores. For a fixed ordered triple the modified graph is fixed and all such cuts are distinct, giving ≤N³h(N−2).
* For this step we use the fixed-root, fixed-boundary counting lemma of Fomin–Villanger (Lemma 3.1); we include the encoding for completeness. With at least four components let C be a smallest component and a a representative vertex. Put i=|C|, j=|S|. Then 4i+j≤N. For a fixed vertex a and a fixed ordering of the vertices, an exploration of C starting from a and querying an as yet unclassified neighbor of the explored set encodes C by i−1 “in” and j “out” symbols. Always choose the least available neighbor. By connectivity all of C and N(C) are queried, and only those; stopping is reconstructible. Thus, for fixed sizes i,j and a, there are at most binom(i+j−1,i−1) possibilities, and C determines S. Equivalently charge four units per “in” and one per “out”. The number of all strings of cost ≤N is O(τ^N), where τ>1 satisfies τ⁴=τ³+1: the numbers at exact cost obey F(k)=F(k−1)+F(k−4), with F(0)=1 and negative indices zero. Summing a geometric series controls all costs. Thus all these cuts together number O(N τ^N). Here τ<7/5<3^(1/3), since (7/5)^4−(7/5)^3−1>0 and (7/5)^3<3.

It follows that lim c(N)^(1/N)=β. This proof does **not** assume that a minimal s,t separator must be globally minimal. It does not assert an identification of the two separator counts graph by graph. The existence of the global limit was previously established by Bradač; see [Sources](SOURCES.md).

## A template amplifier

A cell with no clean separator has rate zero, already beaten by a path. Fix a connected base graph with terminals s,t and p≥1 internal vertices and g≥1 clean separators. Then s,t are nonadjacent. Suppose first that the graph minus s is connected. For k≥2 let r=floor(k/2), let M=binom(k,r), and introduce new s' plus k vertices u_i each adjacent to s'. Use a single terminal t' in common for all M cells, one per r-subset E⊂[k]. Each cell uses p private vertices with the same induced graph as the original interior and the same edges to t'. Replace each original s-to-x edge in that cell by all edges u_i x for i∈E. There are pM+k internal vertices total. There are at least
             g^M + M g^(M−1)                            (1)
clean s',t' separators. Indeed:
* With no gate removed, select any clean separator in every cell. The left shore of a cell is anchored via at least one live gate whenever it contains interior vertices; all gates are adjacent to s'. The right shore is attached to t'. Upon identifying s' and all gates as one contracting shore the cell behavior is that of its original graph. In particular no bypass via another cell can connect the two shores, and restoring any local separator vertex reconnects both.
* Choose one E₀, delete exactly its r gates, leave its cell entirely intact, and pick a clean separator in every other cell. Since all gate neighborhoods are distinct and equally sized, each other cell still has at least one live gate. The inactive cell with s deleted is connected to t'. Thus there are still exactly two components. Every removed gate of E₀, when restored, gives a path from s' to t' via the intact cell, using any original s–t path; witnesses for other cut vertices are in their own cell. Distinct gate subsets and local choices give distinct cuts. This argument accounts for undirected walks across cells: any switch between cells occurs either at a gate on the s' shore or at t' itself.

For a general base graph, prune all components of G−s not containing t, retaining s. They have no edge to the retained graph except through s. They cannot contain a member of any minimal s,t separator, and removing them preserves each clean separator and its minimality. The reduced graph G' is connected and G'−s is connected. Its internal order p'≤p; a nonadjacent s,t path remains so p'≥1. Its clean count g'≥g. It therefore suffices to improve the reduced base rate ρ=(g')^(1/p')≥g^(1/p). Clean separators form an antichain of subsets of the p' internal vertices (in particular not all 2^p' subsets), so ρ<2. Choose k with M>g' ρ^k; this is always possible as M≥2^k/(k+1). Then (1) is greater than (g')^M ρ^k=ρ^(p'M+k). The new rooted graph has a strictly better normalized finite rate. This includes the case g'=1.

Consequences: for every p≥1, h(p)^(1/p)<β. For each p some integer l satisfies h(lp)>h(p)^l, by taking p parallel copies of an improved order-q graph and putting l=q. The maximum clean rate of any fixed finite dictionary of connected rooted gadgets with positive internal order is therefore strictly less than β. The same amplification, without claims about extra components or global minimality, works for the number g_root(p) of all minimal s,t separators and establishes nonattainment for its own Fekete supremum. This last assertion does not require identifying g_root's limit with β.

## What unrestricted global cuts of a parallel sum count

Let G have p≥1 internal vertices and two specified terminals s,t. In r≥2 copies in parallel of a connected G having an s–t path, every global minimal cut whose surviving terminals lie in different components is precisely a union of r clean s,t separators. An additional component disjoint from both roots would lie inside one cell, and every removed vertex would have to be adjacent to it; the whole cut would be confined to that one cell, leaving an intact path in a different cell. In the absence of an extra component, every surviving internal vertex connects to one root inside its own cell (the first root hit by a path is reached locally), and restoring any cut vertex gives a local s–t route: its same-shore and opposite-shore neighbors are local or terminal. Conversely clean choices always compose.

For any other global minimal cut, either S⊆{s,t}, or it is confined to a single cell together with at most both terminals. If there is a component disjoint from surviving roots, every removed vertex has to touch it. If at least one terminal survives and the surviving terminals are together, disconnection forces such a component. If neither survives, all components are disjoint from roots. Including the root-only cuts, the number is at most 4+r·2^(p+2), hence O_G(r). Consequently the global count is h(G;s,t)^r+O_G(r), with h(G;s,t) the individual clean count. The bound covers adjacent terminals via h=0. For a parallel sum of r cells chosen from any fixed finite menu, the same proof yields a product of the individual h values plus O_menu(r). As total order tends to infinity this gives rate at most max(1,max_G h(G;s,t)^(1/p_G)), strictly less than β if p_G≥1 throughout. In particular **no fixed connected two-terminal gadget, or fixed finite menu, attains the global extremal exponential rate by parallel replication**. The result does not exclude templates of increasing order, infinite repertoires, or general nonparallel assemblies.

The gate bank is an adaptation of Gaspers–Mackenzie's published middle-layer construction, not a claim to have invented the basic gate idea. Its role here is to substitute arbitrary cells and strictly improve each finite rate. The conclusion is structural, not a competitive numerical estimate of β. The bound below two and existence of the limit were already known.
