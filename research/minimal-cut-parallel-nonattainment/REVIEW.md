## Mathematical checks

**Global vs rooted.** A global inclusion-minimal cut $S$ has at least two surviving components and every $v\in S$ sees *each* component: on restoring $v$ alone, the whole graph must reconnect. Conversely, if every deleted vertex sees every surviving component, restoring any nonempty subset connects the graph, not merely the two designated terminals. For a rooted minimal separator, the corresponding necessary and sufficient condition only requires contact with the two *terminal* components. A rooted separator leaving exactly two components is therefore global. The unrestricted rooted notion does not imply global minimality: take two length-two paths $s-a-t$ and $s-b-t$, plus a leaf at $a$. Here $\{a,b\}$ is rooted-minimal but $\{a\}$ is already globally disconnecting. The complete graph is allowed to have no cut by the specified convention; the already-disconnected graph has only the empty minimal cut. These exceptions have no exponential effect.

**Characterization of the exponent.** The clean counts are positive at all positive internal orders (use a path) and bounded by $2^p$. Root identification supermultiplies them because there are exactly two connected shores after choosing one clean separator in each cell; each deleted vertex has a local witness in each shore. Thus the Fekete limit and supremum agree. Each two-component global cut is clean for the endpoints chosen in its different components. If there are three components, add the edge between two representatives from the two components not containing the first representative. No graph outside this fixed ordered triple enters that count; the same separator now has exactly two shores with all deleted vertices touching both. There are only polynomially many choices of representatives. With at least four components, choose deterministically one smallest component $C$ and a root $a$ in it. Its boundary is exactly $S$. The least not-yet-classified vertex adjacent to the current connected set is queried at each step, so all and only $C\setminus\{a\}$ and $S$ are queried. The known graph, root and answer tape decode the component and boundary with no oracle. The fixed-parameter bound $\binom{|C|-1+|S|}{|C|-1}$ is **exactly the prior Fomin–Villanger Lemma 3.1**, not just a related estimate. With $i=|C|$, $j=|S|$, the four-component condition gives $4(i-1)+j\le N-4$. The number of binary decision tapes of this budget is at most a constant times $\tau^N$, $\tau^4=\tau^3+1$, since exact weight counts satisfy the two-term recurrence and geometric summation suffices. Multiplying by the $N$ choices of root covers all larger-component cuts. At $7/5$ the separating polynomial is positive and $(7/5)^3<3$, so $\tau<7/5<3^{1/3}\le\beta$. The resulting polynomial times $h(N-2)$ plus the exponentially subdominant error yields the global limsup at most $\beta$; the reverse liminf follows from $h(N-2)\le c(N)$. This avoids an unsupported identification of arbitrary rooted separators with global cuts on an individual graph.

**Pruning and the gate construction.** A component of $G-s$ not containing $t$ is attached to the rest only via $s$. No simple $s$–$t$ path visits it, and it stays on the $s$ shore of every clean cut. Removing all such components preserves each previously clean separator and its local restoration witnesses. For positive clean count the terminals are nonadjacent; connectedness guarantees that at least one internal vertex remains. In the reduced cell, deleting $s$ leaves all interior vertices joined to $t$. The case with all gates live follows by assigning each surviving private vertex to its old terminal shore; the left-side terminal is replaced by a nonempty set of gates joined through the common source. No right-side private vertex was adjacent to the old source, and no interface to other cells exists except through a gate or the common sink. If exactly the gates $E_0$ are deleted, that chosen cell has no live gate and is connected to the sink in its entirety. Every other equally sized but distinct gate set has a nonempty difference from $E_0$; hence these cells have the same valid shore labeling as in the first case. Restoring a deleted gate restores an $s'$–$t'$ path in the intact cell, and restoring an internal cut vertex uses its own active cell. All cuts are distinguishable by their gate masks and restrictions to the disjoint interiors. This verifies $g^M+Mg^{M-1}$ with $pM+k$ private vertices. The finite antichain bound gives $g^{1/p}<2$ even at $p=1$ (it cannot contain both the empty and the full set). Therefore the central binomial coefficient eventually exceeds $g(g^{1/p})^k$. This is enough for strict gain, including $g=1$. Taking $p$ copies of a strictly better graph of order $q$ yields the claimed strict inequality at order $pq$. The same witness argument applies to arbitrary rooted-minimal separators without claiming that their additional components belong to a shore or that their rate equals the global one.

**Arbitrary parallel menus and endpoint-containing cuts.** If the two undeleted roots lie in distinct components, each cell must be cut. A third component would live wholly inside a single private cell, and every deleted vertex must touch it, forcing the entire cut into that cell. This contradicts the surviving root path in any other cell. Thus the cut is clean. Each vertex on a terminal shore reaches its root inside its cell up to the first root encounter. A deleted internal vertex has a neighbor in each shore and these are local vertices or roots. It follows that all cell restrictions are clean, giving exactly the product of the cell clean counts. In every other cut, including those containing one or both roots, some component has no surviving root (unless a cut consists only of roots, which can already be counted separately). Touching that component forces every cut vertex into its one cell or into the two roots. Consequently the exceptional count is at most $4+\sum_i 2^{p_i+2}$, hence linear for a fixed menu, uniformly over orders and repetitions. A cell with adjacent terminals has zero clean choices and simply kills the product. If $R=\max(1,\max_i h_i^{1/p_i})$, the product term is at most $R^{\sum_i p_i}$, the polynomial term has rate at most one, and $R<\beta$ by strict improvement and finiteness. Connectedness of each cell and the requirement $p_i\ge1$ have been kept throughout.

## Sources and claim boundary

- [Erdős (1988), p. 81](https://webmail.math-inst.hu/~p_erdos/1988-27.pdf) asked about inclusion-minimal disconnecting vertex sets; he recorded Seymour's disjoint length-four paths. [Bradač (JGT 2025), Proposition 2 and display (1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02974) gives the earlier fixed-terminal, parallel-gluing and rate argument. It states a comparison of extremal functions, not that every rooted separator is globally minimal on the same graph. The new argument uses the clean counts and does not require a global/unclean-rooted comparison for a single graph. The existence of the limit and the bound below two are *not* new results of this note.
- [Fomin & Villanger (2012), Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 4.1](https://fedorvf.github.io/articles/2012/2012n.pdf) already give the fixed-root connected-set count with exact boundary and use the smaller full component to count minimal separators. In this note the four-shore budget and the three-shore augmentation apply that old lemma specifically to **global** cuts. The [later Gaspers–Mackenzie branching proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.01203) is a second relevant context.
- [Gaspers–Mackenzie, published conference version](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-53174-7_9) and [JGT article](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.22179) give the prior explicit lower-bound family. The printed conference abstract says $1.4457$, not the $1.4521$ in the older arXiv v2 construction. [Gaspers's own Dagstuhl report, §4.4](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/04dagstuhl-reports/volume08/issue10/18421/DagRep.8.10.63/DagRep.8.10.63.pdf) also identifies $1.4457$. The [2018 Golovach seminar account](https://www.lirmm.fr/~goncalves/sgt2018/uploads/Enum-15-06.pdf) explicitly exhibits the corrected architecture: nine gates at each side, 126 equal-size gate neighborhoods, and parallel terminal identifications. The key added step here is to substitute *arbitrary* cells, use growing gate count, require clean separations and prove strict gain for every fixed template. I accessed the final primary abstracts and the explicit public lecture account, not the paywalled full JGT text; no theorem number for new generality is inferred from that lecture. No new decimal record or solution of the exact-rate problem is asserted.
- I found no prior explicit theorem of strict nonattainment for all such finite menus in searches for bounded templates, repeated rooted cells, minimal cut/separator growth, the source papers and related work. Search results do not establish priority. The available repository inventory through #100 included text, tagged blobs, formalization and release payloads. I checked the live increment through #103 (`dbd59e86427ab13aa1a0bc9688835fb9fe5ba7a4`): all new files belong to the shifted simplex energy, Taylor-coefficient, and single-deletion symmetry projects.

## Independent finite checks

The accompanying [independent program](independent.py) enumerates **all proper subsets for gate-cut validation** and finds global minimal cuts using inclusion against previously found qualifying subsets, without assuming connectivity of arbitrary supersets. It validates 27,474 connected labeled graphs of orders 3–6, 4,384 three-shore transformations, and 221 four-or-more-shore encodings including decoding and the actual budget. Among 1,764 ordered pairs of small (possibly distinct, possibly adjacent-endpoint) templates, all root-separating global cuts equaled the predicted product and all 3,754 exceptional cuts, including 3,082 containing terminals, localized as required. A further 326 claimed gate witnesses across small configurations passed direct tests; the normalized strict gain at $p=3,k=8$ was checked by exact integers. These tests are independent checks, not a substitute for the proof.
