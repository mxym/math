# Source map and scope

1. Jarosław Błasiok, Paul Lou, Alon Rosen and Madhu Sudan, [Expanders Meet Reed–Muller: Easy Instances of Noisy k-XOR, arXiv:2604.04188v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04188v2), April 11, 2026; IACR ePrint 2026/664. [The source's Conjecture 16 and footnote 6 of Theorem 27](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04188v2) specify the power-of-deficit weak random erasure statement and define recovery as uniquely determining the codeword. The stronger polynomial additive gap is Conjecture 14 and is not addressed here. The theorem in this note concerns only erasure recovery for binary linear codes.
2. Ehud Friedgut and Gil Kalai, [Every monotone graph property has a sharp threshold](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-96-03732-X), Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 124 (1996), 2993–3002. Theorem 3.1 provides the \(1/[p\ln(1/p)]\) lower bound on maximum pivotal influence, and the following Theorem 3.2 specializes to symmetric monotone events. Theorem 3.4 supplies the max-to-total influence inequality used in the alternate gadget proof. The small-bias threshold estimate is a known theorem, not a new inequality here.
3. Shrinivas Kudekar, Santhosh Kumar, Marco Mondelli, Henry D. Pfister, Eren Şaşoğlu and Rüdiger L. Urbanke, [Reed–Muller Codes Achieve Capacity on Erasure Channels](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04689); see also the [STOC 2016 version](https://pfister.ee.duke.edu/papers/stoc16-paper.pdf) on the EXIT area property, stabilizer transitivity, and bit-to-block bounds. The independent early approaches are [Kumar–Pfister, arXiv:1505.05123](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05123) and [Kudekar–Mondelli–Şaşoğlu–Urbanke, arXiv:1505.05831](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05831). These works provide the classical area / sharp-threshold method and capacity results at interior limiting rates. We give an endpoint-uniform application, anchoring the small-bias EXIT transition by the actual area deficit and integrating over a fixed ratio of bias logarithms. We do not claim a new EXIT identity, threshold theorem, symmetry principle, or an interior-rate capacity result.
4. Emmanuel Abbe, Amir Shpilka and Avi Wigderson, [Reed–Muller codes for random erasures and errors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4590), and S. Bhandari, P. Harsha, R. Saptharishi and S. Srinivasan, [Vanishing Spaces of Random Sets and Applications to Reed–Muller Codes](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2022.31), CCC 2022, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, give strong results for high-rate regimes, based on different techniques. These specialized results do not by themselves give the uniform parameter statement of Conjecture 16. The present theorem has far weaker erasure rates than capacity in regimes where capacity is quantitatively known, and does not claim superiority there.

We found the ingredients and more precise known results in other parameter regimes, but did not find this combined endpoint-uniform conclusion identified as resolving Conjecture 16 in the checked sources. No absolute priority claim is made.
