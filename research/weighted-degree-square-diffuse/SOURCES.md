# Scope, prior results and comparison

- **Original question.** [Braverman, Efremenko, Kol, Saxena and Zhang, ITCS 2025, §1.3, Conjecture 5, p. 22:7](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22), also [ECCC TR22-179, revision 1, Conjecture 1.5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/revision/1/download/). The edge weights sum to one in the source, and its desired cut surplus is one eighth of the sum of squared weighted degrees; our normalization makes that P/2. The authors give the equally weighted odd cliques as tight. The asymptotic order-sharp leading coefficient is not a new sharp example. The result proved here is the finite **weaker** lower bound P/[2(1+2√P)] for the normalized surplus, hence the full conjectured leading coefficient for general P→0. Neither the exact positive-P inequality nor an optimal order of the error is proved. BEKSZ's separately numbered ITCS Conjecture 3/ECCC 1.3 concerns cut imbalance and its feedback-channel interpretation; this note gives no new channel threshold.

- **Order-based guarantees and nearly uniform degree profiles.** [Gutin–Yeo, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 37 (2023), Prop. 2.3, Cor. 2.4, Thm. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.05536) include matching, order and depth-first-search tree bounds, and credit the order estimate to Haglin–Venkatesan. [Gutin–Nielsen–Yeo–Zhou, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.1(i)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05827v1) give the bound C/W≥(1+1/n)/2, with sharper parity in Lemma 2.1 and a simultaneous constraint on within-side weight in Theorem 2.1. In particular if nP→1 the desired first-order surplus is already known. For exactly regular weighted degrees P=1/n, it is known with no error term. Their Prop. 2.1 (a weighted star on a clique) also shows why an additive constant times the largest weighted degree is unavailable in general; we do not claim discovery of nonuniform examples. For equal-weight simple graphs the usual Edwards–Erdős bound, applied componentwise, gives the exact degree-square inequality as noted in [repository #132](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/132).

- **Maximum support degree.** [Gerke–Gutin–Yeo–Zhou, Theorem 1.1 / Corollary 1.2, arXiv:2611.22760v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22760v1) obtain a weighted *bisection* of relative weight (at least) (1+1/k)/2 for maximum ordinary degree at most odd k, and (1+1/(k+1))/2 for even k. This subsumes the corresponding ordinary-degree cut guarantee, and can exceed our bound. It is not a general estimate with P in place of 1/k. As a comparison of the displayed expressions: start with r disjoint unit-edge stars with r² leaves each, complete the entire support with positive weights of total at most one. Here W∼r³, P∼1/(4r), n∼r³ and ordinary maximum degree n−1. The maximum weight of a matching is at most r+1; any DFS tree of this complete support is a path of weight at most 2r+1. Thus the cited order, maximum ordinary degree, matching, and DFS-tree expressions can simultaneously have surplus o(WP); no claim is made about the best cut of this example, which is nearly bipartite by weight. [MaxCut in graphs with sparse neighborhoods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09309) studies unweighted size and neighborhood restrictions; it does not impose the arbitrary weights considered here.

- **Method comparisons.** Degree-product centering of adjacency is classical: [Newman, Finding community structure in networks using the eigenvectors of matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0605087) uses a degree-product null matrix for modularity and spectral methods. At λ=1 the centered quadratic form f(ε)−m² is exactly of this type, up to normalization. Our use of it is a different comparison: a *pointwise* Max-Cut lower constraint on the energy of a centered and rescaled cube point, multiplied back by its squared radius **before averaging over** independent initial signs. The independent-coordinate rounding fact is simply the multilinear extension of a quadratic form with zero diagonal. [Goemans–Williamson](https://math.mit.edu/~goemans/PAPERS/maxcut-jacm.pdf) give a multiplicative SDP approximation by hyperplane rounding; it does not by itself assert a P-dependent surplus above one half. [Guruswami et al., Finding almost-perfect graph bisections](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~venkatg/pubs/papers/MaxBisectionICS.pdf) use a balance constraint to approximate promised near-perfect bisections, not a uniform extremal bound from weighted degrees. Neither centering, rounding a fractional cut, random-sign energies nor their elementary moment identities is claimed as an invention.

- **External overlap search.** Looked under the BEKSZ paper, authors and both relevant conjecture numbers; maximum weighted cut surplus vs weighted degree sequence, degree squares and collision probability; unbiased/biased random cuts, degree-weighted centering and rescaling, center-and-clip, correcting by independent flips of the degree-heavier side, modularity/degree-product subtraction, quadratic-form cube rounding, bisection/judicious partition with weighted degrees and random hyperplanes. We did not find an earlier result stating this finite formula or its unrestricted diffuse-profile asymptotic. This limited search is not a priority guarantee; equivalent inequalities might have been expressed as matrix estimates or in different terminology.
