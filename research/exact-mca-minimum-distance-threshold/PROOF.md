# Exact mutual correlated agreement at the minimum-distance threshold

## 1. Model and statement

Let F be a finite field, S a nonempty subset of F, Sigma a nonzero finite-dimensional F-vector space, and C an F-linear subspace of Sigma^n. For a nonzero C let Delta be its minimum nonzero Hamming weight; one may assign Delta = infinity to the zero code for upper bounds. Fix a generator degree d >= 1 and put h = d+2. For words u_0,...,u_d define u(X) = sum_j u_j X^j. At integer radius r a seed x in S is exceptional if there is a set T of at least n-r coordinates such that u(x)|_T is in C|_T but u_j|_T is not in C|_T for at least one j. Here C|_T is the punctured code, not the shortened code. The MCA error is the maximum exceptional count over all tuples divided by |S|. This is Manzur's Definition 3.4 and BCGM's Definition 3.14. A real radius gamma has integer radius floor(n gamma).

**Theorem A.** Suppose 1 <= r < n. Write L = d(r+1).
* If Delta > hr, the exceptional count is at most min(|S|,L).
* If Delta = hr, it is at most min(|S|,max(L,floor(n/r))).
* More generally, if 1 <= j = hr-Delta+1 <= r, it is at most min(|S|,max(L,A(n,r,j))), where A(n,r,j) is the largest size of a family of r-subsets of [n] with pairwise intersections of size less than j. In particular A(n,r,1)=floor(n/r) and A(n,r,j) <= floor(binomial(n,j)/binomial(r,j)).
* Whenever Delta > r+1, some tuple gives at least min(|S|,L) exceptional seeds. Thus the strict-branch result is exact for each fixed code; so is the packing bound whenever its packing term is no larger than L. At Delta = hr the stated upper bound is the exact worst case over scalar linear codes of that distance for every F,S,n with n >= hr, and hence also over codes with any fixed nonzero F-vector alphabet.

**Theorem B.** Suppose n >= hr, F has q elements, r divides q-1, and q-1 >= n. There is an ordinary (unweighted) scalar Reed–Solomon code of length n and distance hr whose full-field MCA numerator at radius r is exactly min(q,max(d(r+1),floor(n/r))). One explicit tuple has exactly floor(n/r) exceptional seeds, with precise identities and distinct r-coordinate error supports. This tuple is separate from the one realizing the root-profile lower bound in Theorem A.

## 2. Unique errors and interpolation

The unique-decoder/full-agreement reduction and root-profile counting are standard ingredients (Manzur, Lemmas 4.2 and 4.5). The distinction used here is that an overlap among fixed anchors bounds the support of every later interpolation relation at once.

Whenever Delta>2r, each word u(x) that is within distance r of C has a unique such codeword c_x, by the triangle inequality. Let e_x = u(x)-c_x, and let E_x be its support. A set T of size at least n-r witnessing mixture membership in the punctured code must be disjoint from E_x: any witnessing codeword must be c_x. Conversely every such T disjoint from E_x witnesses mixture membership. In particular x is exceptional exactly when at least one u_j cannot be made zero outside E_x by subtracting a codeword. Necessity: a codeword agreeing with u_j outside E_x also agrees on every admissible T. Sufficiency: take T = [n] minus E_x itself. This reduction does not require every arbitrary padded erasure to remain an exceptional witness.

For each exceptional x choose any r-set P_x containing E_x. In all hypotheses used for the upper bounds, Delta>2r: in the packing case Delta=hr-j+1 >= (h-1)r+1 >= 2r+1. Suppose a set of d+1 distinct exceptional anchors A has a union of padded supports of size strictly less than Delta-r. Interpolate their errors by a unique polynomial e(X) of degree at most d with coefficients in Sigma^n. For any other exceptional x, the residual e_x-e(x) belongs to C: the scalar interpolation coefficients are the Lagrange coefficients for evaluation at x, so applying those coefficients to u(a) for a in A reconstructs u(x) exactly, and the residual is a linear combination of codewords c_x and c_a. Equivalently this is the weighted d+2-seed error relation, with coefficient 1 at x and minus the d+1 Lagrange weights at the anchors, all nonzero. The residual is supported on P_x together with the padded anchor union, of size less than Delta. It is therefore zero. The same argument works whenever every such d+2 union has size below Delta, even without a separate small-anchor-union description.

If the number of exceptional seeds is at most d+1, it is already at most L, since r>=1 and 2d>=d+1. Otherwise:
* If Delta>hr, any set of d+1 anchors works; even after adding x all h supports have union at most hr.
* If 1<=j<=r and some pair P_a,P_b overlap in at least j coordinates, take d+1 anchors containing a,b. The union of these anchors and any extra x has size at most hr-j=Delta-1. Thus all the exceptional errors follow the single polynomial e(X).
* If no pair overlaps in at least j coordinates, the padded sets are distinct because j<=r. They form the stated packing, and each j-subset is used at most once. When j=1 they are pairwise disjoint. In the endpoint non-profile branch the unpadded supports also each have size exactly r: if one had size at most r-1, include its seed among the d+1 anchors and use the actual supports in the union bound. This would give a single polynomial. When there are at most d+1 seeds we do not assert this dichotomy.

It remains to bound the number of exceptional errors when they follow a polynomial e(X)=sum_j e_j X^j. At each anchor u(a)-e(a) is in C. Interpolation of these d+1 words shows u_j-e_j in C for every j. Let W be the union of the supports of e_j; every coordinate polynomial on W is a nonzero Sigma-valued polynomial of degree at most d. Such a coordinate has at most d roots: project by an F-linear functional with nonzero value on one nonzero coefficient and use the scalar root bound. If |W|>=r+1, take any r+1 coordinates in W. Each exception has at least one zero among those positions because its error has weight at most r. The union of their root sets has size at most d(r+1). If |W|<=r and no coordinate in W vanishes, then E_x=W and all e_j are supported on E_x, so the seed is not exceptional by the reduction above. Thus at most d|W|<=dr of these seeds can be exceptional. This proves the upper bounds, with no restriction on d relative to |F|; the small-seed case bypasses interpolation.

The upper bounds also apply to an MDS generator G:S -> F^ell of full dimension ell>=2 on taking d=ell-1. Indeed any ell distinct generator rows form a basis. Replace polynomial interpolation by the linear span through these anchor values with coefficient vectors uniquely determined by that invertible row matrix. The relation expressing G(x) in its basis is exactly the same vector relation on the combined words. At a coordinate whose discrepancy coefficients are not all zero, at most ell-1 generator rows annihilate it (project by a scalar functional if needed), since any ell rows are independent. The identical support/packing and root-count arguments follow. For r=0, ell exceptional seeds would force every coefficient in the code by the same row inversion, giving at most ell-1. We claim sharpness for the powers generator, not for every MDS generator.

## 3. Sharpness for arbitrary fields and restricted seed sets

### 3.1 One shared error profile

The degree-one tangential construction is classical (BCIKS Remark 1.1, Diamond–Posen Remark 2, and Chojecki Proposition 4.2). The following form takes the inclusive threshold, degree d, and arbitrary S together. Whenever Delta>r+1, choose r+1 distinct coordinates, a nonzero symbol in Sigma, and m=min(|S|,d(r+1)) distinct seed labels in S. Partition the labels among the coordinates, with at most d labels in each part. On that coordinate, let u(X) be the chosen symbol times the monic product of X minus its labels; the empty product is 1. Use zero on every other coordinate. At a designated seed exactly its own coordinate polynomial vanishes among these r+1 coordinates; the combined word has weight r. Use the complement of this support for T. The coordinate that has just vanished has a nonzero coefficient in some u_j. If such u_j agreed with a codeword on T, that codeword would be supported among the r+1 chosen coordinates and nonzero at the vanished position, contrary to Delta>r+1. The seed is therefore exceptional, including when |S| is too small to fill all root parts.

For r=0, the only witness is T=[n]. Projecting u(X) into Sigma^n/C shows at most min(|S|,d) bad seeds. If C is proper, multiply a fixed word outside C by a nonzero scalar polynomial with exactly min(|S|,d) seed roots to attain it. If C is the entire ambient space no seed is exceptional. For d=0 the mixture is its sole coefficient and there are no exceptional seeds at any radius.

### 3.2 Repeated coordinates: the other endpoint branch for all F,S

Let n>=hr and b=min(|S|,floor(n/r)). If b<=L the preceding profile already attains the capped endpoint bound on any distance-hr code, for example a one-dimensional code supported on hr coordinates. Suppose b>L. In particular b>=d+2=h: for r>=1, L>=2d, and 2d+1>=d+2 for d>=1. Pick b distinct a_i in S. Let H0 be the (h-1)-by-b matrix with column (1,a_i,...,a_i^d) at coordinate i. Every h-1 columns are independent by Vandermonde, so ker H0 has dimension b-h+1 and distance h. Since H0 is surjective, choose u'_j with H0 u'_j the jth standard coordinate vector, indexed j=0,...,d. Then H0 sum_j x^j u'_j=(1,x,...,x^d). This syndrome lies in the span of one H0 column if and only if x equals its label a_i (its first coordinate is 1, and its second is x). It is never zero. At x=a_i the error e_i, the i-th unit word, corrects it. At least one coefficient syndrome is not in that column span: for example H0 u'_1 has first coordinate zero and is itself nonzero.

Repeat each coordinate r times in both the code and the words, and append n-br always-zero coordinates. The lift is injective and multiplies Hamming distances by exactly r. It therefore gives a length-n code of distance hr which has no radius-r close mixture outside the b designated labels. At each a_i, the complement of its repeated block is a valid witness. If a lifted coefficient agreed there, the original coefficient would be correctable by erasing coordinate i, contrary to the preceding syndrome statement. This gives exactly b bad seeds. The scalar construction embeds in any larger vector alphabet along a nonzero scalar direction. Together with the profile this proves the claimed universal optimum at every field size, including when r is a multiple of its characteristic. It does not claim every code at the endpoint attains the packing branch.

## 4. Ordinary Reed–Solomon endpoint family

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem B, put b=floor(n/r), and choose an evaluation domain D in F* of size n consisting of b entire fibers of a -> a^r and, if n-br>0, a proper subset of one further fiber. The power map has fibers of size r over its nonzero image because F* is cyclic and r divides q-1; the size condition ensures enough fibers. Let k=n-hr+1 >= 1 and C=RS_F(D,k), the values of polynomials of degree less than k.

For a in D set lambda_a = 1/product_{b' in D, b' != a}(a-b'), and let H have columns lambda_a(1,a,...,a^{hr-2}) indexed by a in D. The leading coefficient identity in Lagrange interpolation says sum_a lambda_a f(a)=0 whenever deg f <= n-2. Applying this to a monomial times each degree-less-than-k evaluation shows C subset ker H. The Vandermonde rank of H is hr-1, so dimensions give equality. Every set of hr-1 columns is independent and any hr columns are dependent, so the minimum distance is exactly hr.

For j=0,...,d choose u_j with H u_j the standard basis vector at moment row r-1+jr. Thus the syndrome moments M_l(x) vanish except M_{r-1+jr}(x)=x^j for 0<=j<=d (the constant at j=0 is 1 also for x=0). For a complete fiber a^r=x define a word e_x supported on it with entry a^{1-r}/(r lambda_a); division by r is legal as the characteristic does not divide q-1. Its moment l equals (1/r) sum_{a^r=x} a^{l+1-r}. Summation over the r-th roots of unity makes it zero unless r divides l+1-r; in that case it is x^{(l+1-r)/r}. Among rows 0,...,hr-2, the nonzero-pattern indices are exactly r-1+jr for j=0,...,d. Therefore H e_x=H u(x), and all the errors have weight exactly r.

For the complement of that fiber to witness exceptionality, at least one coefficient syndrome must not lie in the span of its r columns. Project those r columns to their first r-1 rows: the restricted matrix has rank r-1 (zero for r=1), while the r full columns have rank r. Thus the intersection of their span with the zero-first-r-1-rows subspace has dimension one. All d+1 independent coefficient syndromes lie in that zero-first-rows subspace, and d+1>=2. Hence not all are in the fiber span. Every complete fiber label is exceptional.

For the converse suppose H u(x)=H e with wt(e)<=r. Write B for the support of e and a in B for its evaluation point, absorbing the nonzero lambda_a into the weights. If |B|<r, the first r-1 moments, all zero, include enough independent Vandermonde rows to force all weights zero, contradicting M_{r-1}=1. This argument includes |B|=0. Thus |B|=r. Write P(X)=product_{a in B}(X-a)=X^r+sum_{j=0}^{r-1} p_j X^j. For i=0,...,r-1,
  
    M_{i+r} + sum_{j=0}^{r-1} p_j M_{i+j} = 0,

by summing a^i P(a) times its weight. The first 2r moments are available since hr-2>=2r-1. They are zero except M_{r-1}=1 and M_{2r-1}=x. Hence the equations for i=0,...,r-2 give p_{r-1-i}=0, and the last gives p_0=-x. This proves that P(X)=X^r-x, including r=1 where only the last equation is needed. Since B consists of distinct nonzero points, x is nonzero and all r roots must occur in D. It follows that the b chosen complete fibers give exactly the full set of close seeds, and all of them are exceptional. The tuple and the root-profile tuple of Section 3.1 give the two terms in the exact capped optimum for this one code. (For S not equal to F the constructed tuple still has precisely the complete fiber labels in S; the stated code's exact optimum is only claimed for full-field seeds.)

## 5. Implication for the Johnson buffer

Take Delta=hr and gamma=r/n, with h=d+2>=3. Then gamma<(Delta/n)/2; moreover (1-gamma)^h>1-h gamma for 0<gamma<=1/h, so gamma<J_h(Delta/n)=1-(1-Delta/n)^{1/h}. Thus the endpoint lies inside the hypotheses of Manzur's Theorem 7.1. In the full-field construction with n a multiple of r the constructed numerator is exactly n/r. This disproves a uniform replacement of the upper bound by O(d max(1,n gamma)/|S|) throughout both strict radius constraints: already fixed positive d,r and n tending to infinity have numerator n/r versus the proposed O(dr). For r=1 the ordinary RS construction works over every field of size q>n; for general r one can use any field satisfying the stated divisibility, including arbitrarily large prime fields p=1 mod r by Dirichlet's theorem. No algorithmic prime finding is needed.

For clarity, the relevant gap dependence also has the correct order. If n>=2hr, put y=r/n, so hy<=1/2. The function f(y)=1-(1-hy)^{1/h} satisfies f(0)=0, f'(0)=1 and f''(t)=(h-1)(1-h t)^{-(2h-1)/h}. On [0,y], its second derivative lies between h-1 and 4(h-1). Hence

    (h-1)y^2/2 <= J_h(hy)-y <= 2(h-1)y^2,
    1/(4y) <= d y/(J_h(hy)-y) <= 2/y.

The lower bound is an order statement on this family, not a claim about the exact asymptotic constant or the best gap function for other distances. In particular, when n>2(h-1)r^2 the family in this range is strictly within the one-coordinate gap band n(J_h-gamma)<1. When Delta/n<=1/2 the generalized radius is also smaller than delta/2: J_h(delta)<=J_3(delta)<delta/2, since (1-delta/2)^3<1-delta for 0<delta<=1/2.

## 6. Two finite-sample source corrections

### 6.1 Arnon–Boneh–Fenzi survey: the radius inequality

In the July 6, 2026 revision of Arnon–Boneh–Fenzi, Theorem 4.8 prints delta <= delta_min(C)/3. Its Definitions 4.1 and 4.3 count agreement inclusively. Take C={(a,a,a):a in F_5} and u(x)=(0,x,2x+1). At x=0,2,4, respectively, the first two, the first and third, and the last two coordinates agree; these are the only close seeds at radius 1/3. The slope word (0,1,2) does not agree with any constant on any pair of coordinates, so all three seeds are exceptional. The two coefficient words have no common two-coordinate agreement set, so this is also a CA example. The claimed upper bound at this inclusive endpoint is 2/5, whereas the actual maximum MCA error here is 3/5 (Theorem A). This correction is to the survey statement; the Roth–Zémor theorem as presented in Diamond–Posen Theorem 1 is strict in 3r<Delta. An appropriate integer-radius replacement for the survey bound is 3 floor(n delta)<Delta (in particular delta<delta_min/3). The general inclusive numerator bound in Theorem A is sharp as a worst case for every parameter set; it does not assert pointwise equality of CA and MCA for an individual coefficient tuple.

### 6.2 Bordage–Chiesa–Guan–Manzur: the numerator and the radius inequality

In CCC 2026 Theorem 9 (and in Theorem 3 of the May 19, 2026 full version), the small-radius bound is printed as dr/|S| at an integer grid radius r/n <= delta_C/(d+2). The full version's Theorem 6.1 and Lemma 6.2 already require a strict radius for their first branch, but still print d max(r,1)/|S| for this specialization. Both use inclusive witnesses (full version Definition 3.14). The [FlashProve source comparison](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/9e2bdd8ff5bf9c62f18263d7dac189b0400b260c/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md#L235) already noted that the printed max(e,1) differs from e+1 in the established line bound. Merely making the radius strict therefore is not a complete repair of that bound; the result here supplies the all-degree corrected bound and the distinct exact equality regime, not priority for the line observation.

For example take the length-four repetition code over F_5 and u(x)=(x-1,x-2,0,0); d=1, r=1 and Delta=4>3r. Precisely x=1,2 are close, at each with weight one relative to the zero codeword. On the three-coordinate complement of the error both coefficient words are nonconstant, since the extra agreement coordinate has both a nonzero constant coefficient and slope 1, while the last two coordinates have both coefficients zero. Thus there are two exceptional seeds, not one. In the printed Lemma 6.2 proof the anchor interpolation is valid under the strict radius, but its concluding claim about the common agreement size need not hold: in this example the common size is two, not greater than three, although the unique error profile exists. The root count of Section 2 yields the corrected sharp d(r+1) numerator for the powers generator. The same upper correction with d=ell-1 applies to the general full-dimension MDS-generator branch as shown above. The disjoint branch at equality is a separate obstruction: it can be unboundedly larger than d(r+1), so replacing r by r+1 alone does not repair an inclusive radius claim. No other branch of the source theorem is assessed here.

The length-three survey repetition example is a small illustration of the failure of Diamond–Posen Conjecture 1 as originally worded; Diamond–Gruen already disproved it by a different explicit Reed–Solomon example. Nothing here proves the amended Diamond–Gruen Conjecture 4.3 at all unique-decoding radii, or addresses Manzur's distinct §1.4 open questions.
