# Primary sources and exact scope

1. Sarah Bordage, Alessandro Chiesa, Ziyi Guan, and Ignacio Manzur, [All Polynomial Generators Preserve Distance with Mutual Correlated Agreement, CCC 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.24), Definition 1, Theorem 9 and §2.3.1; [ePrint 2025/2051, May 19, 2026 revision, archived September 15](https://opencryptology.org/2025/2051/versions/20260915T125246Z.pdf), Definition 3.14 (p. 18), Theorem 3 (§2.3, pp. 10–11), Lemmas 5.2–5.3 and Remark 5.4 (§5), Theorem 6.1 and Lemma 6.2 (§6.1, pp. 29–30), and Definition 8.1 (§8, p. 37). The CCC theorem and full-version overview print the small-radius expression d*n*gamma/|S| at an inclusive outer radius. The detailed MDS-generator theorem/lemma and Definition 8.1 require a strict outer radius and print d*max(n*gamma,1)/|S| when specialized to powers. Both use an inclusive agreement witness. The F5 length-four example contradicts the displayed strict-branch numerator already at gamma=1/4. The support interpolation itself is standard here, in particular Lemma 5.2. Their Appendix B (p. 47) separately discusses proximity with strict inner comparison 'distance < e'; translating that comparison into an inclusive radius r=e-1 is not the same as the statement of Definition 3.14 and Lemma 6.2. Appendix B's Lemma B.1 already uses a degree-d polynomial construction in the maximum-distance setting with a divisibility condition and different (strict) endpoint. See PROOF §6.2; these observations do not concern MCA existence or assess the other quantitative branches.

2. Ignacio Manzur, [Mutual Correlated Agreement and List-Decoding: Improvements and Separations, ePrint 2026/2617](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2617) ([Nov. 3 revision text](https://eprint.iacr.asia/2026/2617.pdf)), §§3.1–3.2, Definition 3.4 (p. 25), Lemmas 4.2 and 4.5 (pp. 27–28), Theorem 7.1 and Corollary 7.2 (pp. 40–43). The theorem's assumptions after setting gamma=floor(n*gamma)/n are 2*n*gamma<Delta and gamma<J_{d+2}(Delta/n). The bound is

       15d/|S| * max(1,n*gamma,gamma/(J_{d+2}(Delta/n)-gamma)).

   The first part of Corollary 7.2 drops the third term when n*(J-gamma)>=1, not everywhere below J. Manzur already supplies the maximal-agreement reduction and the d(r+1) count on a single codeword curve; the present advance is the global interpolation/packing dichotomy and the exact second-family endpoint attainment. This construction proves the gap term cannot uniformly be omitted, and matches its order in the specified regime. It does not address the square-root/list-decoding separations, positive converse question, or Reed–Solomon equivalence question in §1.4.

3. Gal Arnon, Dan Boneh, and Giacomo Fenzi, [Open Problems in List Decoding and Correlated Agreement, ePrint 2026/680](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/680) ([July 6 revision text](https://eprint.iacr.asia/2026/680.pdf)), Definitions 4.1 and 4.3 (p. 17), Lemma 4.6 and Theorem 4.8 (§4.2.1, p. 18). Theorem 4.8 prints the upper bound (n*delta+1)/|F| at delta <= delta_min(C)/3, including equality. At the grid boundary this is contradicted by the length-three F5 example in PROOF §6.1. The strict range is correctly stated in the Roth–Zémor result in updated Ligero, as explicitly reproduced and credited by Diamond–Posen below. The endpoint correction concerns the survey's printed inequality, not the underlying strict theorem. The two definitions have different quantifiers for fixed tuples; our correction uses a tuple violating both claimed numerator bounds.

4. Scott Ames, Carmit Hazay, Yuval Ishai, and Muthuramakrishnan Venkitasubramaniam, [Ligero, CCS 2017](https://acmccs.github.io/papers/p2087-amesA.pdf), §4.1.1 Lemma 4.3 has a strict e<d_min/3 hypothesis for the stated row-span result. The later [Designs, Codes and Cryptography version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-023-01222-8), Appendix A, is the version identified for the Roth–Zémor statement by Benjamin Diamond and Jim Posen, [Proximity Testing with Logarithmic Randomness, IACR CiC 1(1), 2024](https://doi.org/10.62056/aksdkp10), Theorem 1 (p. 9). The latter gives e<=floor((d_min-1)/3), with the (e+1)/q ceiling for close points of an affine line when its two generating words do not have common proximity e. See also their Remark 2 (p. 10) for the sharp one-coordinate-root example, credited to Eli Ben-Sasson, Dan Carmon, Yuval Ishai, Swastik Kopparty, and Shubhangi Saraf, [Proximity Gaps for Reed–Solomon Codes](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10467091), Remark 1.1 (JACM 2023; ePrint 2020/654). Benjamin Diamond and Angus Gruen already disproved Diamond–Posen Conjecture 1 in [Proximity Gaps in Interleaved Codes, ePrint 2024/1351](https://opencryptology.org/2024/1351/versions/20260428T172000Z.pdf), §1.3 and Example 4.1. The tiny length-three example is an alternative illustration, not a new resolution. The amended Conjecture 4.3 concerning numerator n throughout the unique-decoding interval is outside our scope.

5. Przemek Chojecki, [Shortening Bounds for Reed–Solomon MCA, ePrint 2026/1463, November 13 archived revision](https://opencryptology.org/2026/1463/versions/20261113T112414Z.pdf), Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.3, Proposition 4.2 and Corollary 4.3, Theorem 4.6, Proposition 4.10 (p. 19, OP5–6), and §9.1 (p. 30, DR1–2). Proposition 4.10 already gives the degree-one MDS packing bound in exactly our parameters: their j=3r-(n-k)=3r-Delta+1; at j=1 their packing term is floor(n/r). Their strict deep range also has the exact line value, and their overlapping supports/locator methods are close antecedents. The present distinction is the degree-d arbitrary linear-code upper bound (including non-MDS and vector-symbol codes), exact endpoint attainability over every field/restricted seed set, and the separate ordinary Reed–Solomon realization for complete r-th-power fibers. The use of a locator recurrence is standard, not itself a novelty claim. This note does not improve their post-Johnson Reed–Solomon machinery.

6. Alessandro Chiesa, Guy Weissenberg, Eylon Yogev, and Kai Zhe Zheng, [Affine Combiners for Reed–Solomon Proximity Gaps, arXiv:2611.15128v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15128v2), §6 Propositions 6.1–6.3. Proposition 6.1 with zero agreement loss is the scalar-line coordinate-root lower mechanism; positive agreement loss there uses larger accidental-agreement blocks on a single fixed codeword. It is not the many-disjoint-decoding-support case below, or an all-degree exact endpoint upper bound. Their affine (coordinate-dependent) combiner has different seed/degree/loss definitions. No claim here about that combiner.

7. Yiwen Gao, Hong Yang, Yang Xu, and Haibin Kan, [List-Decoding Counterexamples Yield Lower Bounds on Mutual Correlated Agreement Error, arXiv:2607.10572v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10572v1), Theorems 1–2 and Corollary 2. They pass from a list-decoding counterexample to an MCA example by changing at most one evaluation coordinate in the structure-preserving case. At our radius the list size is one because 2r<Delta, so their hypothesis with L>=1 cannot hold at that same radius and for that starting code. They do not provide the exact power-generator endpoint packing or the root-fiber tuple here.

8. Rohan Goyal, Venkatesan Guruswami, Yihang Sun, and Mary Wootters, [Locality of Curve-Decoding and Improved Proximity Gaps, APPROX/RANDOM 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2026.61), Definition 49, Corollary 51, Theorem 52. This is a general curve-decoding/row-span formulation of the interpolation geometry; Manzur Remark 4.8 already explains the connection. Its exact locality theorem is not the numerical minimum-distance endpoint theorem here.

9. [FlashProve, PUBLISHED-WORKS.md](https://github.com/anthropics/FlashProve/blob/9e2bdd8ff5bf9c62f18263d7dac189b0400b260c/PUBLISHED-WORKS.md#L235), pinned commit 9e2bdd8ff5bf9c62f18263d7dac189b0400b260c, lines 122 and 235, explicitly notes the discrepancy between the February 6 BCGM Theorem 6.1 numerator max(e,1) and the e+1 numerator for the established strict line result; it also notes Corollary 8.6's multiple of the printed expression. The May 19 full-version formula retains that discrepancy. We credit this public observation and do not claim its priority; it is a line-fold source comparison, not the all-degree strict/endpoint/packing theorem or sharpness characterization proved here.

Public searches and the unified research directory were checked for the combination of degree-d packing, the exact all-fields endpoint, and the stated ordinary Reed–Solomon family. The line-specialized and single-curve antecedents above are explicitly credited. The check did not find a prior statement of the full combination and is not a claim of priority for its individual standard ingredients.
