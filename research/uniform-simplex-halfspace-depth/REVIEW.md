# Independent internal review: reconstructing the uniform simplex from its depth

Primary formulation verified in Nagy–Schütt–Werner, Data depth and floating body, Problem 12, p.43 (the last subquestion, uniform simplices in every dimension), and Nagy's October 30, 2025 updated GeMS list, Problem 1 (uniform triangle). Not the general finite-first-moment case.

Support identification works before any compactness assumption on the competitor: the closed convex hull of support contains the interior of the simplex by strict separation. At each interior hull point the positive open halfspace masses are lower semicontinuous on the sphere, hence have a positive minimum, even for an unbounded measure. Since the hull is closed full-dimensional and its interior cannot contain an exterior zero-depth point, it is contained in the simplex. For the now compact support, inclusive tails are left-continuous and their attained directional quantiles are Lipschitz in direction via a uniform displacement bound. At a smooth patch point the continuous slack forces an active normal, and the one-sided varying contour parameters recover the actual inclusive tail, including for atomic competitors.

The homothetic cap arguments check: intercepts (d+1)b_j<1 define the subset with centroid x, while intercepts d b_j define exactly its Grünbaum minimal-volume cap extending to the same cut of the outer simplex. The local formula is proved on an open neighborhood rather than globally assumed. With r>1/(d+1), the supplied bound on the slope radius makes all b_j positive and less than 1/(d+1) for all r<t<1. The nonzero gradient of log-depth is a negative multiple of the tilted affine functional's gradient, so the proper direction is the outward upper tail. At t=1 tail masses vanish by continuity from below in the threshold. Differentiate any smooth primitive that vanishes below r, using compactness to dominate each derivative; this tests arbitrary products of a cutoff of the base projection with transverse polynomials. Polynomial density and exhaustion then give zero signed mass on each of the d+1 caps. Since only the centroid remains and total mass agrees, this proves the whole-law result without a density hypothesis. No external peer review or global priority is claimed.
