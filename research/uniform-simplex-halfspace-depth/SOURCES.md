# Primary sources and limits of the comparison

- [Nagy–Schütt–Werner, *Data depth and floating body*, arXiv:1809.10925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.10925#page=43), Problem 12: the last question asks whether the uniform law **on the entire simplex** in dimension \(d\) (not on its vertices) is determined by depth. The [journal version](https://doi.org/10.1214/19-SS123) is titled *Halfspace depth and floating body*. The preceding subquestion on finite expectation is distinct. The later [GeMS problem list, updated 30 October 2025](https://gems.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/Depth_problems_update.pdf), Problem 1, specifically asks about a triangle.
- [Rousseeuw–Ruts, *The depth function of a population distribution* (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00020903), §5.3, already computes the triangle depth. Nagy–Schütt–Werner, Example 1, also points to Schütt's 1991 Lemma 1.3 and its proof for higher-dimensional forward calculations. We claim no priority for a forward depth formula. Our needed patch also follows quickly from [Grünbaum (1960)](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257).
- [Kong–Mizera, *Quantile tomography*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0056) relates depth regions to directional quantile envelopes. [Kong–Zuo (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmva.2010.06.007), Theorems 3.1–3.2, give tangent-mass retrieval (with a hyperplane-mass term for general measures) and global smooth-contour identification. Global smoothness is not available for the triangle. Nagy–Schütt–Werner, Example 5 and §8, distinguish convex from Dupin floating bodies: the latter need not exist for a triangle. Here an open patch of smooth normals and variation of the offset along each fixed direction suffice, also for competitors initially allowed to charge hyperplanes.
- See [Nagy (2020), *The halfspace depth characterization problem*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-57306-5_34) and [the author's detailed assessment of previous claims](https://chres.is.cuni.cz/media/documents/2023/01/09/Nagy_ThesisShort.pdf), especially the summary of paper (B). In particular, earlier statements sometimes quoted as yielding identifiability from bounded support or integrability alone do not supply a verified simplex theorem. [Laketa–Nagy, *Partial reconstruction of measures from halfspace depth*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03959) recovers information on mass and support but does not give this diffuse simplex identification. [Nagy's distinct general counterexamples](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09207) do not disprove identifiability for a specified reference law.
- [Boman–Lindskog, *Support theorems for the Radon transform and Cramér–Wold theorems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.4373) is background for extracting measures from halfspace/projection data. The elementary local mixed-moment argument is supplied here; no new general tomography theorem is claimed. [Molchanov–Nagy (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26108) concerns a different depth defined using translates of convex test bodies.
