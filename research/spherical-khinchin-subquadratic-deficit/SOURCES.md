# Sources, overlap, and scope (checked 19 November 2026 UTC)

**Exact question.** J. Jakimiuk, C. Tang and T. Tkocz, [*Khinchin inequalities for uniforms on spheres with a deficit*, Math. Z. 313, article 11 (published 7 May 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-026-04016-5); [arXiv:2506.22040v4, 4 March 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22040v4). Their Theorem 1 obtains the sphere-to-Gaussian deficit for $p\ge2$, and Theorem 2 compares to the flat list of the same length. Their §3 conjectures a quartic deficit for the **reversed** Gaussian comparison (3) when $d\ge3$, $0<p<2$. That is the present scope; the separate real-sign and fixed-length questions in the same section are not settled here.

**Method and earlier sharp bounds.** JTT Lemma 3 and its application (8) already differentiate the spherical mean twice, use Gaussian-radius replacement, and exploit the radial bi-Laplacian. The proof here reuses that mechanism with the reversed sign, distributional smoothing and a curvature estimate averaged over the remaining summands. [Baernstein–Culverhouse, *Majorization of sequences, sharp vector Khinchin inequalities, and bisubharmonic functions* (2002), Theorems 1–2](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/90752) had already developed the underlying bisubharmonic majorization method. The sharp qualitative Gaussian comparison is prior; see also [Chasapis–Singh–Tkocz, *Haagerup’s phase transition at polydisc slicing*, Table 1](https://msp.org/apde/2024/17-7/apde-v17-n7-p07-p.pdf).

When $d=3$, one scalar coordinate of a sphere point is uniform on $[-1,1]$. G. Chasapis, K. Gurushankar and T. Tkocz, [J. Anal. Math. 149 (2023), 529–553](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11854-022-0256-x), [author arXiv:2105.14079v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.14079v3), Theorem 1, already identify the sharp comparison for scalar uniforms at $0<p<1$. Their Lemmas 17–18 with Claims A/B give the essential strict Fourier integral input, and **their Theorem 21** supplies the stronger estimate for a dominant coefficient; the latter is not a consequence of just invoking the sharp constant. The new quantitative step is to bound the normalized excess uniformly by the fourth coefficient mass; see the large-$s$ estimate and the overlap in THREE.md. The $p=1$ seam is treated separately rather than by substituting the endpoint into CGT.

**Nearby 2026 works and versions.** Searches explicitly combined Khinchin/Khintchine, lower/subquadratic/positive moments, uniform sphere or interval, fourth/fourth-order mass and Gaussian deficit/stability, also using the authors' names. The July–19 November 2026 window was examined separately from the historical search. The named primary results below were compared by distribution, exponent and extremizer:

- [Chávez–Sheng, arXiv:2606.19313v2, Thms 1.2 and 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19313v2) derive stability from moment-sequence log-monotonicity for **even integer** exponents. These do not supply a positive subquadratic exponent.
- [Gao–Qian, arXiv:2608.17802v1, Thms 1.1–1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17802v1) study fourth-mass stability for **Rademacher signs**, including the Gaussian comparison for $p\ge4$ and a third-moment statement. These are not uniforms on higher-dimensional spheres.
- [Fang–Wang, arXiv:2608.27908v2, revised 1 November](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27908v2) and [Xie, arXiv:2610.07526v1, Thms 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07526v1) concern the scalar Rademacher absolute first moment and sharp/robust comparisons with the two-coordinate extremizer. They do not give the comparison with a Gaussian for $d\ge3$.
- [Jakimiuk's scalar antecedent, arXiv:2503.07001v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07001v2), concerns $p\ge3$. The [Warsaw seminar description](https://www.mimuw.edu.pl/en/seminars/talk/stability-of-khintchine-type-inequalities-revisited/) refers to the earlier two papers, not to an announced subquadratic spherical theorem. [Tkocz's publications listing](https://www.math.cmu.edu/users/ttkocz/mathematics.php) was also checked. No matching later primary theorem was located. An unindexed, inaccessible or unpublished independent result is not excluded.
