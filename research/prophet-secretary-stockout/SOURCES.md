# Scope, antecedents and duplicate check

**Direct questions.** [Nick Arnosti and Will Ma, *Tight Guarantees for Static Threshold Policies in the Prophet Secretary Problem*, Operations Research 71 (2023), 1777–1788](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2022.2419); [authors' full manuscript](https://www.columbia.edu/~wm2428/papers/prophet_secretary.pdf). Their model permits independent nonidentical nonnegative values and uses a uniform random permutation. Remark 1 specifies the independent coin for ties, Eqs. (4)–(6) define utilization/acceptance statistics, Lemma 1 and its proof decompose the welfare guarantee, and Lemma 7 computes the Poisson benchmarks. They already proved the best uniform static-threshold constant, impossibility of improving it even on iid inputs, and realized it with an expected-utilization rule; their results also cover expected demand with its stated restrictions. The **unnumbered final question of Section 5** (printed p. 24 of the full manuscript, immediately after Proposition 2, just before the references) proposes the *different* statistic $\Pr(D\ge k)$ and matching $\Pr(\mathrm{Pois}(k)\ge k)$. Their usage means $\ge k$, not $>k$. Our theorem covers both the independent product model and the narrower iid case mentioned in some listings. The $k=1$ case predates this proposal; cf. [Ehsani, Hajiaghayi, Kesselheim and Singla, SODA 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11213) and Arnosti–Ma's discussion.

[Mathieu Molina, Nicolas Gast, Patrick Loiseau and Vianney Perchet, *Prophet Inequalities: Competing with the Top $\ell$ Items is Easy*, SODA 2025](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611978322.38); [arXiv:2408.07616v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07616v2). Eq. (8) uses reward per $k$ against the prophet's top-$\ell$ average; their Theorem 4 characterizes the iid limiting static ratio. Proposition 10 proves the matching asymptotic upper even when the static price and common tie parameter are chosen for the instance. The last paragraph of Section 6 explicitly asks about nonidentical distributions under random arrivals. We supply a uniform stockout calibration in that model. The existing iid **upper bound** is all that is required from their analysis for sharpness among general static policies at large horizons. Our finite-horizon formula is for a **prescribed stockout target**, not for this stronger optimized-instance benchmark. We do not address optimal adaptive stopping, or assert that the earlier expected-demand policy works.

**Two substantial overlaps credited.**

1. [Chawla, Devanur and Lykouris, *Static pricing for multi-unit prophet inequalities*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.07990), Section 5, particularly Lemmas 5–6, optimize *utilization* (throughput) subject to *fixed nonstockout probability*, via extremalization among Bernoulli sums. Their pricing scheme and welfare benchmark concern adversarial order and balance utilization with nonstockout probability. [Ganesh, Hartline, Sinha and vonAllmen, *Fundamental Limits of Throughput and Availability*](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19292v2), introduction and Sect. 5, explicitly explain that the worst unit-demand throughput at any given availability is given in the limit by Poisson, citing the former paper, and develop bounds for demands of sizes up to one. Consequently the lower-tail/utilization half of the result here is not claimed as a new extremal principle. Neither paper is being extended here to new results for fractional demand. Our other coordinate concerns conditional *random-order* acceptance against competitor demand, not simply whether stock runs out.
2. [Nick Arnosti, *IID Prophet Inequalities*, Oct. 2, 2020](https://nickarnosti.com/blog/iidprophet/), Sec. 3. For iid inputs fixing a threshold quantile is equivalent, at fixed $n,k$, to fixing demand probability $p$ (hence stockout). This article explicitly records the fixed-quantile worst case $\mathbb E\min(k,\mathrm{Bin}(n,p))/\max(k,np)$ for the equal-capacity iid problem. Its examples are values near a constant and values with a vanishing probability of a very large reward. The limiting shape in (2) for $\ell=k$ and these witness ideas are thus anticipated in this iid expected-demand parameterization. One must not infer our *heterogeneous* finite formula from the iid curve: our competing-count term has separately matched $p_{n-1,k}(\phi)$; the finite iid acceptance term instead uses $p_{n,k}(\phi)$ even for its $n-1$ competitors. In particular no priority is claimed for an iid fixed-quantile characterization or for rare-large-value examples.

**Probability machinery.** Relative log-concavity of the Poisson-binomial law with respect to binomial and Poisson references is classical (Newton's inequalities, with a proof supplied). See [Yaming Yu, *Relative log-concavity and a pair of triangle inequalities*, §§2.1, 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.2043): §2 treats Bernoulli convolutions and §3 uses the central positive interval/one-crossing method, with explicit credit to Karlin and Studden. His stated convex ordering matches means; here matched tail masses locate the CDF comparison on each side of a fixed cut. The zero-crossing method itself is not claimed as new.
