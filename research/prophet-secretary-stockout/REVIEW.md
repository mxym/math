# Independent internal audit: stockout calibration

Reviewed frozen proof SHA-256 0a3956e9453c988eae7b8f764d5c9dce6f0db905010282bb39cfa0c2e73b61b6. PASS for the sharp fixed-calibration finite profile and the limiting optimal static guarantee in the stated independent, random-order model. No external peer review.

* Newton's inequality used on the generating polynomial follows by the stated logarithmic derivative and Cauchy–Schwarz. The ratio to the fixed-N binomial law is normalized Newton coefficients times a positive geometric sequence. The extra binomial-to-Poisson ratio is log-concave by decreasing successive terms; the product extends by zero outside contiguous support. Equal tails give equality of total mass on each side separately. A log-concave ratio has an interval as its weak upper-level set at 1. Because both sides of the split have a nonempty such portion (or exact equality), the sign pattern is nonpositive then nonnegative below k, and nonnegative then nonpositive from k onward. The lower conditional law shifts up and the upper conditional law shifts down. Therefore both the capped demand and the decreasing surplus-acceptance factor improve in the relevant direction. Finite sanity check exhaustively enumerates all Bernoulli assignments for 630 rational examples, with rigorous rational brackets for the calibration parameter; this is not a substitute for the comparison theorem.
* Taking a quantile of the kth order statistic and interpolating a common probability with independent individual tie coins is sufficient. At each buyer, conditional on eligibility and the leave-one-out number d, the independent random ordering makes its service probability exactly min(1,k/(d+1)). The positive surplus depends only on that buyer's value and the rivals' stockout count is independent. This gives the precise decomposition, including atoms. The leave-one-out tail is never larger than the full tail; when n>k, increasing the rivals' independent means up to ones can match the upper target and only lowers this service expectation. When n=k it is exactly one. The offline inequality uses the sum of only the top ell and does not assume that every large value fits the offline capacity.
* The finite sharpness examples actually calibrate a unique price: a continuous almost constant distribution for the first term, and a rare independent giant among n−1 mixture competitors for the second. The probability law changes strictly with price on each side of t=1 near that price; monotonicity then excludes another calibrated price even where there is a support gap. The limiting giant surplus dominates all bounded contributions, and the prophet necessarily includes a realized giant for any ell≥1. The n=k check has all eligible observations served and the same dominance monomial; no binomial matching on an impossible n−1≥k is invoked.
* At each fixed k,ell the n→∞ calibrated binomial parameters obey np→lambda by bracketing with fixed neighboring Poisson means. The Poisson comparison applies separately to every n and the size shift gives lambda times the service expectation equal to capped utilization. Strict increase of the latter and strict decrease of the service factor give the unique optimum lambda=ell. The optimized finite-instance uniform optimum is not asserted: the upper application uses the prior asymptotic iid counterinstances and lower applies to heterogeneity. No adaptive threshold claim follows.

The two exact open formulations are in [Arnosti–Ma, closing paragraph of §5](https://www.columbia.edu/~wm2428/papers/prophet_secretary.pdf) and [Molina–Gast–Loiseau–Perchet, §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07616v2). The original optimal static constant for equal capacities (with other calibrations), prior one-sided utilization extremality at fixed availability, and the iid models and upper limits are not new.
