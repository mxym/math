# Sources and what changes here

1. **Tom Leinster and Emily Roff, [The Maximum Entropy of a Metric Space](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haab003), Q. J. Math. 72 (2021), 1271–1309.** Theorem 7.1 and Corollary 7.3 give the simultaneous-optimization framework, including reduction to order two. Section 10, Question (2), asks for a maximizing measure on a Euclidean ball or cube. Their finite-concentric-sphere conjecture concerns **balls**, not cubes. Their interval example is the separate one-dimensional case.
2. **Shilan Salim, [The q-spread dimension and the maximum diversity of square grid metric spaces](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/29673/1/PhD_thesis.pdf), Sheffield PhD thesis (2021), Chapter 4.** Sections 4.1–4.2 study discrete square grids numerically. Theorems 4.3.3–4.3.4 and Corollary 4.3.5 give qualitative small-scale orbit and weighting results; these imply small-scale corner optimality on each fixed grid in their scope by a one-orbit variation. This is not merely a numerical prior. Grid spacing $t$ corresponds to full side $(n-1)t$. These statements do not give the two positive side thresholds for *solid higher-dimensional cubes* considered here.
3. **Adam Dajani, [Maximum Diversity and Support Transitions on Disks and Three-Dimensional Balls](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38031), preprint (2026).** Section 2, [equations (4)–(5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38031), gives Fourier positivity and the necessary/sufficient equilibrium inequality for exponential energy on a compact domain. Its ball results are precedents for support-transition tests, not theorems on cubes. The discussion on p. 28 explicitly distinguishes the Euclidean cube from the taxicab box (for the latter, the kernel factors).
4. **Emily Roff and Simon Willerton, [The microscopic weighting on a metric space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05349) (2026).** The small-scale limit of signed magnitude weightings and the associated distance energy are distinct from optimizing probability measures for an exponential kernel at a positive side length.
5. **Repository predecessor, [the square vertex phase](../euclidean-cube-diversity-vertex-phase/README.md), [PR #105](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/105), merged at [a5c9079](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/a5c9079a1b3ac3f8b770c17cf1a5d66ece3ce2b2).** That note establishes the square threshold and a bound sharp simultaneously over all $d\ge2$. The current note does not claim that result again. Its additional ingredients are the averaged face trace and a comparison of the reflected edge terms at larger scales. They yield the separate thresholds for dimensions three and four and the stated uniform consequences.

A repository search through the predecessor's parent history and releases, the delta to #105, and searches of contemporary mathematical sources did not uncover the same higher-cube thresholds. This check cannot establish global priority. No external peer review is claimed.
