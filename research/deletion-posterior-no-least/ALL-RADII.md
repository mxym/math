# Every fixed binary deletion radius at least two eventually has no least posterior

No claim on which word maximizes Shannon entropy for three or more deletions.

Let M_d(y) be the largest number of embeddings of a binary y of length m in any host x of length m+d, and P_d(m)=min_y M_d(y). The following claims hold:

1. For each fixed d≥1, the raw peak P_d(m) is nondecreasing in m and is eventually a finite constant. An explicit, not optimized upper bound is binom(2d²+3d,d). The normalized peak is obtained by dividing by D=2^d binom(m+d,d).
2. For every fixed d≥2, for all sufficiently large m there is no least element among the posteriors under majorization. This holds without knowing a Shannon maximizer.
3. For every fixed d≥2 and every fixed real alpha>2, for all sufficiently large m the alternating word does not maximize Rényi entropy of order alpha. The [two-deletion result](BINARY.md) reaches all alpha>1. Statement 3 neither settles the order-one maximizing question nor asserts that its displayed competitor is an optimizer.

**Bounded-peak construction.** Take y to be the length-m prefix of the infinite word (0^d 1)(0^d 1)... . Its infinite period L=d+1 block has a unique 1; hence at a shift h with 1≤|h|≤d no consecutive L pairs of letters can agree. This remains true within any finite prefix.

For any host x admitting y, let E=(e_i) and F=(f_i) be the greedy leftmost and rightmost embeddings. Every embedding G satisfies e_i≤g_i≤f_i for all i by the usual forward and backward first-match inductions. Delete from the host index interval all positions unused by E or unused by F (at most 2d positions); the remainder is split into at most 2d+1 consecutive intervals. Throughout each remaining interval E and F read successive ranks of y. The rank difference h is therefore constant, with |h|≤d since every rank used at position t belongs to [t−d,t]. A nonzero-difference interval has length at most d, by the no-shift agreement property of y. Each position used by E but not by F accounts for at most one rank and there are at most d such positions. Consequently at most J=d+(2d+1)d ranks i have e_i different from f_i. All other ranks are forced in every embedding. After reserving the m−J' forced positions, where J'≤J counts the actual unforced ranks, each embedding is determined by its d omitted positions among d+J' remaining sites. Therefore M_d(y)≤binom(d+J,d)=binom(2d²+3d,d).

For any length-(m+1) word, append its last letter to a host witnessing the peak for its first m letters; using that additional site shows the peak has not decreased. Taking minima yields P_d(m+1)≥P_d(m). The displayed uniform bound and integrality give stabilization; neither the sharp plateau nor its onset is asserted in general. At d=1, alternating words yield P_1(m)=2; at d=2 the exact plateau 9 and onset 13 are given separately.

**Obstruction from singleton hosts at all radii.** Atashpendar, Beunardeau, Connolly, Géraud, Mestel, Roscoe and Ryan, [*From Clustering Supersequences to Entropy Minimizing Subsequences for Single and Double Deletions*, Eq. (17) and Theorem IV.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.00703), already give the number of hosts with exactly one embedding. In their notation the sum of run insertion slots rho_0+rho_1 is m−R+2, where R is the number of runs: each run contributes its length minus 1, with one extra for each word end (including a single run). Their Eq. (17) is therefore binom(d+m−R+1,d). For d≥1 it strictly decreases with R, so only alternating words minimize this count. Equivalently, a unique embedding must be both leftmost and rightmost. In any nonempty internal gap its skipped bits must all differ from both neighboring retained bits; thus the gap lies within a run and the skipped bit is forced. At either outer gap its bit is forced by the one neighbor. Conversely, filling these gaps with the prescribed opposite bits makes the retained mask both greedies, hence unique. Distributing d skips among m−R+2 allowed gaps gives the same formula. The support size also does not depend on y: scan each uniform binary host left to right, matching against the next needed symbol of y and, beyond its completion, against a fixed 0. The indicators of a match are independent fair bits, since each prescribed bit depends only on the earlier host bits. The host contains y precisely when at least m of these m+d trials are matches; thus the support has K=sum_{h=0}^d binom(m+d,h) hosts. (This classical support identity is also reviewed by Atashpendar et al.)

If A has a singleton hosts and B has b>a, then at descending prefix K−b the B row has raw sum D−b. The A row's b removed positive entries have sum >b, hence its prefix at K−b is strictly smaller. Therefore B cannot be least. Any least row must come from an alternating word (up to complementation). But the alternating word has peak at least m+1 for any d≥2: in the alternating host of length m+2 starting with the same bit, deleting any of the m+1 consecutive pairs yields the pattern; append d−2 extra positions and omit them in all these masks. Once m+1>binom(2d²+3d,d), the bounded-peak competitor beats it on the first prefix. This proves 2. Notice that the two tests occur at opposite ends of the Lorenz curve and no newer entropy extremizer theorem was used.

**A strict fixed-order Rényi obstruction.** Write A_m as a prefix of length t=floor(m/2) followed by its alternating suffix of length u=m−t. For the prefix fix the alternating host of length t+2 starting with the prescribed bit. For each choice of d−2 *distinct* runs of the alternating suffix, extend the chosen runs by one letter. These give binom(u,d−2) distinct suffix hosts as soon as u≥d−2: their ordered run-length lists identify the choices. Concatenation produces that many distinct length-m+d hosts, each admitting ≥t+1 embeddings via the prefix choice and at least one suffix embedding. Thus
```
sum_x K_{A_m}(x)^alpha ≥ binom(u,d−2)(t+1)^alpha
                        = Omega_{d,alpha}(m^{d−2+alpha}).
```
For the bounded-peak competitor y in part 1, K_y(x)≤C_d=binom(2d²+3d,d), whence its sum of alpha-th powers is ≤C_d^{alpha−1}D=O_{d,alpha}(m^d). For alpha>2 the former eventually exceeds the latter, and at equal D the larger alpha-moment means smaller Rényi entropy. This finishes 3.
