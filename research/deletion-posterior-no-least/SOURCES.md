# Sources, provenance, and boundaries

## Entropy questions and the exact prior obstruction

Atashpendar, Roscoe and Ryan, [*Information Leakage Due to Revealing Randomly Selected Bits*](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/21251/1/information_leakage_due_to_revealing_randomly_selected_bits_preprint.pdf), §3.6, motivate the binary alternating **Shannon** maximizer from experiments. Their study plots Shannon, collision and min-entropy separately. [Atashpendar's thesis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11663), §§3.2.2–3.2.6, explicitly defines leakage through Shannon entropy and discusses minima for the other orders. Our higher-order counterexamples do not refute the Shannon statement.

Atashpendar, Beunardeau, Connolly, Géraud, Mestel, Roscoe and Ryan, [*From Clustering Supersequences to Entropy Minimizing Subsequences for Single and Double Deletions*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.00703), Eq. (17) and Theorem IV.6, already count binary hosts admitting a **unique** embedding, and identify the alternating/constant extrema for that count. The common support size and unique-embedding count used in [the general proof](ALL-RADII.md) are prior results. The additional step is a periodic observation whose *largest* multiplicity is uniformly bounded at fixed deletion radius, contradicting the opposite necessary prefix comparison. Their work also counts host types in the two-deletion case. The [hidden-word-statistics paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.11609) examines fixed observed length as the host length tends to infinity; that limit differs from fixing the radius while the observed length grows.

Singhvi, Sabary, Bar-Lev and Yaakobi, [*Conditional Entropies of k-Deletion/Insertion Channels*, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2025.3581849), [public text](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10026v2), Lemma 2 gives the deletion posterior. Lemma 4 and the proof of Theorem 3 supply the *full host classes* needed for our two-deletion optimization, including the alternating-segment case with singleton neighbors. We specialize and optimize those prior counts; we do not claim a new two-deletion histogram theory. Theorem 5 in §V-A1 already gives the one-deletion Shannon maximizers over every alphabet, not just the binary one.

Ma and Zhang, [*Maximal input entropy for binary two-deletion and two-insertion channels*](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01064v1), Theorem 2.2 (4), characterize both alternating words as the only Shannon maximizers for **every** positive received length at deletion radius two. Their two-insertion thresholds and exceptions concern a different posterior. Their Conjecture 6.2 on Shannon entropy at radius three is not decided here. Their [accompanying research dossier, Scope assessment](https://www.trureturing.org/research/singhvi-sabary-barlev-yaakobi-2024-deletion-channel-max-input-entropy/) explicitly proposes a peak obstruction comparing the length-12 word $(0011)^3$ with alternation and labels it outside the provided proofs and numerical checks. At length 12 those peaks are 9 versus 13, whereas the actual optimum is 8. We do not claim to originate that observation. The present note gives the exact two-deletion optimization over all received words and lengths, higher-order witnesses, and the eventual obstruction for each fixed radius.

## Other comparisons

- Menon and Singh [define the fixed-pattern maximum $M_{n,p}$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07870) and study in particular patterns with at most three runs. Bruda's [Theorem 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02578v1) bounds the number of embeddings for a given pair of binary words from run parameters, with equality for two alternating words; his eventual polynomial enumeration fixes the pattern and the multiplicity. Our $P_d(m)$ additionally **minimizes over all length-$m$ patterns**, with length difference fixed.
- Bar-Lev, Gershon, Sabary and Yaakobi [study a fixed-word embedding-number decoder](https://yaakobi.net.technion.ac.il/files/2022/08/122_Dec_t_Del_ISIT21.pdf), and Sabary, Bar-Lev, Gershon, Yucovich and Yaakobi treat this decoder explicitly in [arXiv:2201.02466v2, Definition 23 and Lemma 25](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.02466v2). Their main loss is expected normalized edit distance, not the minimax posterior peak as the observation varies; [the journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-026-01873-3) is published, but the publisher's accessible text was a preview, not the complete manuscript. Our fixed-word calculation uses the actual multiplicity classes in Singhvi et al., not a longest-alternating-segment decoder rule.
- Morozov and Duman, [*Simple Finite-Length Achievability and Converse Bounds for Deletion and Insertion Channels*](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20961v4), use sums of output-wise maxima for code-size bounds, and explicitly distinguish maximizing over run extensions from a general upper bound. Our objective is a minimum over observations rather than that sum. [Fang, *Maximal Number of Subword Occurrences in a Word*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AofA.2024.3) instead minimizes over **hosts** the maximum number of occurrences of a **subword**; that reverses the optimization.
- The single-deletion result for ample alphabets is not new as a Shannon statement. The [earlier repository theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/c07483ed7729e5efaa0a97e89c08661a91c3a56e/research/deletion-posterior-majorization) proves that the constant posterior **majorizes** all others. The [local refinement proof](SPACED.md) modifies its coordinate-by-coordinate cylinder alignment; the method is not independent of that work. The prior [single-deletion incidence symmetry note](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/c07483ed7729e5efaa0a97e89c08661a91c3a56e/research/single-deletion-incidence-symmetries) concerns row/column permutations, not an entropy extremum.
