# Local collision refinement for deletion posteriors

Let q≥2, m≥1, d≥1 and n=m+d. Under uniform input and retention of a uniform m-subset of [n], p_y(x)=K_y(x)/(q^d binom(n,m)), where K_y(x) counts the retained masks yielding y. Write
E_d(y)={{i,k}:1≤i<k≤m, k−i≤d, y_i=y_k}.
If E_d(z) is contained in E_d(y), then p_z≺p_y. Under this inclusion equality as sorted vectors holds exactly if E_d(z)=E_d(y).

**Proof.** The prior [constant-output majorization note](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/c07483ed/research/deletion-posterior-majorization) aligns cylinder indicators one host coordinate at a time. We adapt the same technique to arbitrary refinement. Change the requirements of all retained masks, coordinate by coordinate, from letters prescribed by y to those prescribed by z. At a fixed host coordinate j, the feasible retained ranks i lie between max(1,j−d) and min(m,j); all differ pairwise by at most d. For those ranks the partition into z-symbol classes refines that into y-symbol classes. Fix the other host symbols arbitrarily; they restrict the accepted masks identically before and after the current step, even at intermediate stages. Let C be the number of accepted masks avoiding j and D_i the number satisfying the other sites and using j at rank i. When the letter at j varies across q symbols, the fiber entries before the change are C+sum_{i:y_i=a}D_i and afterwards C+sum_{i:z_i=b}D_i. Each new nonempty group lies in one old group. For k largest new sums, the associated old groups are at most k in number and their total is at least the chosen total. Empty groups add zeros; the common C does not affect the comparison. Both fiber totals coincide. Pooling disjoint fibers preserves this majorization: every set of k post-change coordinates selects k_j in each fiber and their total is at most the sum of the largest respective k_j before the change, hence at most the global sum of the k largest entries. Composing these comparisons gives the stated order.

If the two E sets coincide, the symbol partitions agree at each site. For each site independently, the bijection between symbols used by y and z on its feasible ranks extends to a full alphabet permutation; its coordinatewise product transports all masks, so the posterior vectors are permutations. For a strict inclusion take i<k whose old letters coincide but new letters differ, r=k−i≤d. Perform the change at site j=k first. Consider two masks sharing positions h for ranks h<i and h+r for ranks h>k. For ranks i,...,k one mask uses i,...,k and the other uses k,...,k+r, so j holds rank k or i respectively. These positions fit in [m+r] inside [n]. The only overlap of the two middle intervals is j; requiring both masks away from j imposes consistent letters. In that fiber D_i,D_k are positive, lie in one old class and in different new ones. Splitting strictly reduces the sum of squared group totals; other fibers do not increase it and neither do later changes. Consequently the sorted vectors cannot agree.

If q≥min(m,d+1) there exist words with E_d empty (cycle the first min(m,d+1) distinct symbols); the largest feasible window has size min(m,d+1), so the alphabet condition is sharp for their existence. These and only these words produce a least posterior and hence maximize every strictly Schur-concave functional, including Shannon and every finite positive-order Rényi entropy. Here 'only' refers to the words maximizing any one such functional. This does not include order infinity. It recovers the *q-ary* Shannon equality set for d=1 already given in [Singhvi et al., §V-A1, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10026v2). For d≥2 this ample-alphabet conclusion is not a binary alternating-word theorem (unless m≤2). No converse order characterization for incomparable collision sets is implied.

**Peak.** For such a spaced z and host x, at each site at most one feasible rank i has x_j=z_i. Let N_i count those sites, so sum_i N_i≤n, and each embedding makes a different ordered choice of one of N_i sites for each i. Thus K_z(x)≤product_i N_i. For nonnegative integers of sum at most n that product is bounded by b^{m−s}(b+1)^s where b=floor(n/m), s=n−mb. This follows by transferring one unit from any larger factor to a positive factor at least two smaller, and is immediate with a zero factor. Conversely every y, not only a spaced one, has a host made of consecutive blocks y_i whose lengths are b or b+1 and sum to n. Choosing a site in each block independently gives at least the displayed product in embeddings. This proves the sharp minimax peak in the ample alphabet regime, without implying that all minimax-peak words are spaced. E.g. at q=3,m=3,d=2, a direct five-site scan gives peak 4 for non-spaced 010 too.
