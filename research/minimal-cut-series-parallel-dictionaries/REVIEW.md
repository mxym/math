## Rooted states and small orders

For connected undirected cells with distinct terminals, a clean separator leaves exactly the two terminal components. In a parallel composite, root-separation is equivalent to separation in both children. A root-free extra component of a child cannot attach to its sibling. Minimality and cleanness therefore characterize the Cartesian product, even when a terminal edge makes that product empty. Cutting a series join w costs a singleton. In fact that singleton is clean exactly when each child minus w is connected. If w survives, any minimal cut for the outer terminal pair lies wholly in a separating child and is a clean cut there; conversely a clean child cut so included is still clean. This proves the stated upper bound without presuming the join singleton always clean.

For zero interior vertices connectedness forces one edge and no rooted cuts; with one internal vertex there is at most one cut; with two there are at most two by the antichain property. With η the real cube root of 3, the separate order-zero and order-one induction cases in the text are necessary: using only the exponential estimate on both children at very small orders would not prove the claimed recurrence. The boundary inequalities follow from η>7/5, η³=3 and η²>2. For p,q≥2, 2ρ^q+1≤3ρ^q≤ρ^(p+q+1). No planarity or stronger form of terminal connectivity enters.

## All globally minimal cuts

A disconnecting set S is globally inclusion-minimal precisely when each v∈S touches every remaining component: individually restoring v is necessary, and restoring any nonempty subset then connects every original component and every restored vertex. Distinguish this from rooted minimality and from minimum cardinality. For a parallel cell with both roots surviving separately, an extra root-free component in one factor cannot touch an internal vertex of the other factor. Thus the latter has no cut vertex and connects the terminals, a contradiction. Without that third component the global cut is a clean rooted separator. The capped composite does not admit this case.

Otherwise, after omitting cuts contained entirely in the two roots, take a component avoiding the surviving roots. If no roots survive all components qualify; if just one survives or both are together, disconnection ensures at least one qualifies. The component is in one child A. Every cut vertex touches it, so none is internal to B. Since S contains at least one internal vertex of A, every surviving component of the original composite has a representative in A. The cap within A cannot merge components when both surviving roots are together; adding it has no effect when at most one survives. Thus A with its cap is disconnected by S, but is connected after removing any proper subset of S: shortcut excursions through B by the cap (or erase an excursion returning to its entry root). This validates the localization and the count with four exceptional root subsets. Only an upper bound is needed; overlapping encodings do not impair it.

The uncapped series bound is the one-vertex-sum bound. In the capped series, use the alternative parallel decomposition A || C, where C consists of B with a pendant edge at the far end. Its cap is B in parallel with a two-edge path. The path has one clean and one global cut (none in its cap). Applying the parallel estimate twice yields the stated constants 5 and 9, including when an atom is a single edge. Order, simplicity after duplicate-edge collapse, and both external terminals are preserved. The proof is combinatorial and permits arbitrary nonplanar constituents.

The reduced expression tree counts positive leaves by internal order, series nodes by their distinct joins, and remaining edge leaves by charging to series parents. Parallel edge children become unary caps, whose further cap is idempotent and whose clean count is zero. Leaf states are bounded by a constant for the finite dictionary. This proves the stated polynomial times ρ^p bound for all recursive expressions, not just paths of bounded recursion depth. The comparison with the unrestricted β invokes the earlier finite-template nonattainment result, not this recurrence alone.

On any clean cut, both terminal components are touched by every cut vertex, so it is also globally minimal. A winning positive-order seed, including the path with three internal vertices, may be repeated in parallel. If the desired total internal order is rq+j, where q is the winning seed's order and 0≤j<q, add j distinct parallel paths each with one internal vertex. Each such path has clean count one. The constructed graph has exactly n=rq+j+2 vertices, with at least ρ^(rq) clean/global cuts. Thus both orderwise maxima have limit of their nth roots equal to ρ, not merely a favorable subsequence.

## Planarity and scope of credit

[Bouchitté–Mazoit–Todinca, Props. 2–3, Remark 1 and Lemmas 3–4](https://www.labri.fr/perso/mazoit/pdfs/journal_2003_Discrete_Math.pdf) give the radial Jordan cycle for a full component in a biconnected plane graph. Its two open sides contain representatives of distinct surviving components. Their Lemma 4 states the absence of cofacial chords under 3-connectivity; its proof invokes Prop. 4 only to obtain global inclusion minimality, already available here. A cofacial nonconsecutive pair produces either a two-edge chord through an unused face or a one-edge chord to a face already on the radial cycle. Either chord splits the disk on its side into two, one containing the chosen representative; that subdisk excludes the representative on the other side of the original cycle. For the two-edge chord, each original arc omits an intermediate original vertex. For the one-edge chord its face endpoint is not adjacent on the original cycle to its original endpoint, and the two distinct cycle-neighbors of the face belong to opposite arcs. Thus either resulting radial Jordan curve uses a proper subset of S. Its separator contradicts global minimality. The induced-cycle implication (one-way) follows on the simple cofacial graph with no auxiliary vertices. Do not transfer the whole triconnected equivalence, uniqueness, or any converse to biconnected graphs.

[Grigoriev et al., IPEC 2025, Prop. 9 and Cor. 11](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol358-ipec2025/html/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.21/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.21.html) explicitly treat the triconnected latching graph (the nonempty-region condition matters for triangles). [Morrison–Scott, Cor. 1.3](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/scott/Papers/maxinduced.pdf) gives O(3^(n/3)) for all induced cycles, with finite exceptions absorbed into a constant. Every cut of size at least three injects by its vertex set into those cycles, and the smaller cuts cost only a quadratic term. The vertex-sum argument, convexity bound on the sum of block exponentials, and Seymour lower family give the stated all-planar specialization. TTSP and treewidth at most two have the same rate as an implication of these prior tools; [Cosme-Llópez–Pous](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol083-mfcs2017/LIPIcs.MFCS.2017.76/LIPIcs.MFCS.2017.76.pdf) give the block/partial-2-tree characterization. No predetermined terminal choices on every block are needed.

[Bradač](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23207) attributes the length-four-path lower family to Seymour. The 2025 Gomes–Nogueira–dos Santos paper studies separator reconfiguration, not this finite-menu global growth formula. Directed edge cuts and planar maximum/minimum *cardinality* separators also differ. No prior source for the general dictionary formula was located in this review, which is not a novelty guarantee. ## Independent finite checks

The C++17 verifier uses literal proper-subset minimality and independent rooted-network representatives. It checks cut *sets*, not only the counts: the clean series and parallel identities; both cap states; and localization into a child's capped cut family. It checks all 190 root-preserving isomorphism types with two to five vertices, plus three nonplanar seeds, for 37,249 ordered pairs (1,149 involving one of the explicit extra seeds). The independent cofacial test derives faces from bipyramids by deleting any subset of ring edges, optionally inserting a vertex in a triangular face, at ring sizes 3 through 9. It verifies 5,871 cuts of size ≥3 in 3,034 explicit plane embeddings. Both original Python checkers were run with `-B -O` and reproduced their recorded output. All checks supplement the proof; they do not imply completeness above the enumerated orders.

The public presentation edits status, cross-references, definitions, and the explicit full-order padding argument. Its mathematical scope is the reviewed exponential rate.
