# Prior-work boundary for the finite-dictionary series/parallel result

- [Bradač](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23207), Fomin–Villanger, Seymour's melon (already recorded by Erdős in 1988), Gaspers–Mackenzie, and [repository #104](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/104) cover the old general rate framework, benchmark, gates and arbitrary finite **parallel** menus. These are not contributions of the extension.
- [Gomes–Nogueira–dos Santos, Some results on vertex separator reconfiguration, Observation 2, Figure 4 and Section 5](https://www.rairo-ro.org/articles/ro/pdf/2025/01/ro230078.pdf) give explicit exponential examples with two interior vertices per branch and analyze vertex-separator reconfiguration in 2-connected series-parallel graphs. Their Observation 2 proves non-tameness, not the optimal global cut base. Seymour's earlier length-four paths already yield the larger lower base 3^(1/3) inside this class. Classical system reliability's read-once series/parallel minimal-failure minterm recurrences are elementary additions and multiplications; do not claim invention of those recurrences or an unrestricted edge-cut result. The count of global vertex cuts at arbitrary terminals in arbitrary recursive cells is not just an evaluation of a terminal minterm formula.
- More importantly: counting all global minimal vertex cuts on **all planar graphs** at the 3^(n/3) scale already follows by combining earlier theorems. As a result, the bare-edge, TTSP and treewidth-at-most-two cases can already be obtained from prior machinery. We have not identified the combined planar count stated explicitly; this is not a novelty claim. Here is the full reduction, provided to distinguish that implication from the arbitrary *possibly nonplanar* finite-dictionary formula.
- [Bouchitté, Mazoit & Todinca, Chordal embeddings of planar graphs, Props. 2–3 and Lemmas 3–4](https://www.labri.fr/perso/mazoit/pdfs/journal_2003_Discrete_Math.pdf) represent minimal vertex separators in any 2-connected plane graph by simple cycles in the incidence graph of vertices and faces. They attribute the underlying representation to Eppstein. Their Lemma 4 (stated for 3-connected graphs) proves nonconsecutive separator vertices are not cofacial; here its proof requires only a globally minimal separating set. [Grigoriev–Kobayashi–Tamaki–van der Zanden, IPEC 2025, Prop.9 and Cor.11](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol358-ipec2025/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.21/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.21.pdf) explicitly state the induced-cycle characterization in the cofacial ('latching') graph for 3-connected planar graphs, with the nonempty regions condition on triangles. We use only a one-way inclusion, not a bijection in general. [Morrison–Scott, Cor.1.3](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/scott/Papers/maxinduced.pdf) bound the maximum induced-cycle count on any n-vertex graph by O(3^(n/3)), with exact expressions for sufficiently large n. They also credit Robson's earlier unpublished exponent-only bound.

## Prior-derived planar specialization

Fix an embedding of a 2-connected planar graph G and form its cofacial graph L on the same original vertices: xy is an edge exactly if some face of G is incident to both. For every global inclusion-minimal cut S with |S|≥3, choose two surviving components. Every vertex of S contacts both, and therefore S is rooted-minimal for their representatives. By BMT Prop.3/Remark 1 there is a simple cycle J of G's vertex-face radial graph, whose original vertices are exactly S, with representatives a,b on opposite sides. In particular successive original vertices on J are cofacial.

Suppose nonconsecutive original vertices x,y of J share a face f. If f is off J, the radial path x–f–y is an embedded chord of J. Its interior is entirely on one side of J. One of the two curves formed by this chord and the two J-arcs separates a,b: the original point on the chord's side lies in one of the two resulting disks and the other point is on the other side of J. The chord passes through neither a nor b and its edges meet the drawn original graph only at original vertices. Each resulting curve uses a proper subset of S (x and y are nonconsecutive in the original-vertex order), hence the separating curve provides a proper globally disconnecting subset, contradiction. If f is on J, at least one of x,y, say x, is not one of the two original cycle-neighbors of f, so the radial edge x–f itself is an embedded chord. Both resulting curves again have proper original subsets, since the two original neighbors of f on J are distinct from x and lie on opposite arcs. Their separation contradiction is the same. This is the argument of BMT Lemmas 3–4 with the relevant minimality hypothesis already given in the global-cut setting. Thus L[S] is an induced cycle, including triangles when |S|=3. We do not assert the converse for 2-connected graphs.

Consequently the number of global cuts in a b-vertex block is bounded by O(b²)+O(3^(b/3)) = O(3^(b/3)), with a universal constant after accommodating the finitely many small b. For a connected planar graph with block orders b_i≥2, c(G) ≤ number_of_blocks−1 + sum c(B_i) by the vertex-sum argument in [ALL-ORDERS.md](ALL-ORDERS.md). Put T=n−1=sum(b_i−1) and η=3^(1/3). Convexity yields η^x−1 ≤ x(η^T−1)/T, 0≤x≤T, and so sum η^(b_i)≤η[number_of_blocks+η^T−1]=O(η^n). Disconnected graphs have only the empty inclusion-minimal cut. For all sufficiently large n, put floor((n−2)/3) length-four paths and (n−2 mod 3) length-two paths in parallel to get the lower bound 3^floor((n−2)/3). This produces exactly Θ(3^(n/3)) for the planar maximum; since the lower family is TTSP, it already gives the same order and rate for TTSP and graphs of treewidth at most two. No sharpened leading coefficient or exact finite extremizer claimed.

The unrestricted finite atom menu in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) may contain arbitrary nonplanar rooted graphs, so neither the planar cofacial argument nor the maximum-induced-cycle theorem as used here gives its exact dictionary-dependent rate. Search terms covered series-parallel and bounded-treewidth vertex cut/separator counts, minimal cut-set reliability, rooted/marked series and parallel networks, cofacial/latching graphs and induced cycles, planar separator enumeration, and early/current sources. We did not find an explicit general formula for arbitrary two-terminal dictionaries; this is not assurance of originality. Smaller roots or edge-failure cuts in directed networks concern different quantities.
