# Sources, prior implications, and scope

* Ryan McCulloch, Brendan D. McKay, Alireza Salahshoori and Thomas Zaslavsky, [*The Cycle Counts of Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-026-03047-3), *Graphs and Combinatorics* 42, 53 (2026), [author version v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02260v3). Section 6(a) proposes the exact 21 exceptions in this note and reports every other count through 3000. Their Theorem 1 treats inseparable graphs; Theorem 2 permits parallel edges in its cubic hosts. Their Lemma 4 is the path increment of an added ear. Their Theorem 3 credits Hassler Whitney, [*Non-separable and planar graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1932-1501641-2), Theorem 19. The [author data page](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/graphs.html) hosts finite data for other classes. We do not use any published finite list to interpolate an occurrence or exclusion.
* Rajveersinh Pardeshi, [*Cycle Counts of k-Connected Graphs: A Sharp Minimum, a Decidability Theorem, and the 3-Connected Spectrum*](https://github.com/rajveersinh-is-dev/cycle-spectra-connectivity/blob/0ea823d5c1a0340be89ce06957d6c6c4c9a238db/paper/main.tex), public manuscript, October 2026. The counting-identity lemma gives exactly the expression for cycles through a deleted vertex as the sum of path counts between its neighbors; the decomposition itself is not new here. The manuscript also treats minimum counts, decidability, and the complete finite host-order census through order 9. The same 21 exceptions are observed among positive counts up to 50 there. Its limitations section expressly does not assert that all larger integers occur, or exclude higher-order hosts using the computation alone. The present quadratic branch polynomial and all-residue four-square coverage are an inverse realization construction, not a method of deciding one fixed count at a time.
* **The global small exclusions already follow by combining prior evidence.** A. Alahmadi, R. E. L. Aldred, R. de la Cruz, S. Ok, P. Solé and C. Thomassen, [*The minimum number of minimal codewords in an [n,k]-code and in graphic codes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2014.11.015), *Discrete Applied Mathematics* 184 (2015), 32–39, [author manuscript](https://orbit.dtu.dk/files/128144478/KAU3.postscript.pdf), proof of Theorem 7. Taking the weaker floor in the triangular term gives a lower-bound sequence with initial values 7 and 13 at orders 4 and 5, and at order p the minimum of the values at p−2 plus binom(floor(p/2)+1,2)+1, at p−1 plus 3p−14, and p²−3p+3. At orders 6–11 these weakened lower bounds are 14,20,25,31,41,47. Induction now gives a bound greater than 34 at every order at least 10. Combined with Pardeshi's order-nine census, this already rules out the proposed exceptions globally. The ear and binary cycle-space censuses here provide reproducible independent checks, not a priority claim for those exclusions.
* Related fixed-parameter extremal bounds are not prescribed-count theorems: Lane Clark and Roger C. Entringer, [*The minimum number of cycles in graphs with given cycle rank and small connectivity*](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%2003/vol-003-paper%2014.pdf); Martin Knor, [*On the number of cycles in k-connected graphs*](https://www.math.sk/knor/PREP/p011.pdf), *Acta Math. Univ. Comenianae* 63 (1994), 315–321. We use the classical four-square theorem of Lagrange without claiming it as a contribution.
