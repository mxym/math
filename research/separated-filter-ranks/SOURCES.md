# Sources and comparison

- G. Brightwell, S. Felsner and W. T. Trotter, [*Balancing pairs and the cross product conjecture*](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/97.pdf), *Order* 12 (1995), 327–349. Conjecture 3.1 is the positive adjacent-cell inequality at arbitrary indices; Theorem 3.2 proves its (1,1) case at all widths. Their proof partitions at the middle mark, sums products of rank counts over the lattice of filters of its incomparable subposet, and applies Ahlswede–Daykin. We use this same decomposition and summation, not a newly attributed proof device.
- P. C. Fishburn, *A correlational inequality for linear extensions of a poset*, *Order* 1 (1984), 127–137; R. Ahlswede and D. E. Daykin, [*An inequality for the weights of two families of sets, their unions and intersections*](https://math.bme.hu/~balint/oktatas/perkolacio/percolation_papers/ahlswede_daykin.pdf) (1978). The ordinary extension-count inequality for filters underlies the starting compulsory-anchor lemma. Comparison for **unconditioned** first-element laws under filter inclusion is a classical consequence; see BFT Theorems 3.3–3.4 and Lemma 3.5.
- S. H. Chan, I. Pak and G. Panova, [*The cross-product conjecture for width two posets*](https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/8679), *Trans. AMS* 375 (2022), Theorems 1.4 and 1.6: both positive adjacent cells and arbitrary positive rectangles are already known at width two. In particular we make no novelty claim for that subclass. Here it reappears because all elements incomparable with y lie on the other constituent chain. Those authors' q-analogue and equality theory are not obtained here. Their [*Canadian J. Math.* paper](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X24000087) 77 (2025; online 2024) gives general-width quantitative bounds for neighboring cells, plus an exact zero-condition case. Our sufficient Δ criterion is not an optimality claim for the original conjecture.
- J. T. Virtanen, [*An inclusion coupling of prefix laws and the Chan–Pak rank inequality for all finite posets*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25227v1), October 2026. Its Theorem 1.2 concerns the prefix set at consecutive ranks of a marked point in **one fixed poset**, and a scalar inequality for that point's rank counts. Its use of endpoint domination and balancing the residual masses is an important methodological precedent. Section 3 compares unconditioned first/last probabilities using Fishburn. Our conditional first-element comparison at fixed rank is obtained here by reapplying the separated-rank statement at a smaller upper rank; the nested-filter consequence compares the *full rank distributions in different induced filters*. We did not locate that different statement as a theorem in the cited preprint; this is not a claim that no alternate derivation from its techniques exists.
- For orientation, see [Chan–Pak's survey](https://ems.press/journals/emss/articles/14298658), §9.7; [their correlation theory for P-partitions](https://msp.org/pjm/2023/323-2/pjm-v323-n2-p01-p.pdf) deals with generally nonbijective order-preserving maps. [Chan–Pak, arXiv:2607.06275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06275) treats equality questions for other classical correlation inequalities. Searches of the primary literature through November 19, 2026 included the original conjecture, width three, middle incomparable sets, arbitrary rectangles, likelihood-ratio rank monotonicity, adding/deleting a minimal point, filter-rank comparison and first-element coupling. No earlier proof in the stated scope was located; searches cannot guarantee priority.

It does not give the present arbitrary-lower-rank conditional comparison on its own. [#156](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/156) concerns other auxiliary statements; no part of its counterexample is used here.
