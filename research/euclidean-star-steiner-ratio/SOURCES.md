# Prior statements, methods and search scope

- S. P. Fekete and H. Meijer, [*On Minimum Stars and Maximum Matchings*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004540010007), Discrete & Computational Geometry 23 (2000), 389–407. [Full paper, §7](https://www.ibr.cs.tu-bs.de/courses/ws2324/ag/papers/Ch8/2000-On_Minimum_Stars_and_Maximum_Matchings.pdf?theme=2019): Conjecture 2 is exactly \(\rho(S,SS)=4/3\) for three-dimensional Euclidean point sets. Conjecture 1 is the separate planar prediction \(4/\pi\). Their \(\sqrt2\) upper estimate and the uniform-circle/sphere lower bounds predate the present argument.
- A. Dumitrescu, C. D. Tóth and G. Xu, [*On stars and Steiner stars. II* (SODA 2009)](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611973068.35), [authors’ manuscript](https://adriandumitrescu.org/star2.pdf). Conjecture 2 at the end of §3 of the linked manuscript predicts \(\rho_d=c_d\) for **all** \(d\ge2\); theorem numbering can differ between versions. Their analysis gives the sphere constant as a lower bound, strict improvements on the dimension-free upper bound, and the limit \(\sqrt2\) as dimension grows. Their separate conjecture for each finite planar cardinality is not settled here. Bounding sums of distances on the sphere via uniform spherical measure is classical, as their references to Fejes Tóth, Björck, Alexander and Stolarsky emphasize.
- D. Ismailescu and J. Park, [*Improved upper bounds for the Steiner ratio*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disopt.2013.10.004), Discrete Optimization 11 (2014), 22–30, §§2–4. They refine the comparison between the nearest input center and the equal-weight mean of all stars, obtaining upper bounds 1.3546 in the plane and 1.3801 in space. Their conventions for the sphere constants in the pair-sum estimate use half the mean chord length. The decisive step here is a reciprocal-radius weighted star average and its comparison with the spherical angular law for the **same list of radii**, not the equal-radius energy bound alone.
- W. Jung, [*A New Upper Bound for the Steiner Ratio*](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/PrintPdf/11362), publicly available competition abstract. It announces a bound of \(4/3\) **in the plane**, using the nearest-center/average comparison and a quadratic optimization. We credit the announcement without claiming to have audited a full proof from the abstract. The planar bound in the appendix is smaller; it is not the conjectured optimum.
