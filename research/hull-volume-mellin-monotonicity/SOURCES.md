# Source distinctions and bounded comparison (19 November 2026)

1. **The historical question.** Mark Meckes's [2006 San Antonio problem-session statement, problem 4](https://math.gmu.edu/~vsoltan/SanAntonio_06.pdf) explicitly formulates inclusion for the **mean simplex volume**, not a theorem about all real orders. Luis Rademacher's [*On the monotonicity of the expected volume of a random simplex*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579311002063), [preprint introduction and §4 item 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.3944), attributes the wider questions about more points and higher moments to Meckes, and asks whether an eventual mean-volume threshold can be chosen independently of the particular nested bodies. Beermann–Reitzner [§2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.08342) restate the latter first-moment question; it is separate from increasing the sample size inside one fixed body.
2. **Prior finite-sample counterexamples.** Rademacher's Theorem 2 already uses shallow protrusions/truncations and proves failure of high integer moment comparison for a random simplex in every dimension at least two. Reichenwallner–Reitzner [Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.07295) give an exact integer-order cutoff of 3 for failure in the planar *simplex-size* problem, with further results in dimension 3; [Kunis–Reichenwallner–Reitzner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01893) give a rigorous mean-volume counterexample for a three-dimensional random tetrahedron. [Reichenwallner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05943) treats lower-dimensional random simplices with at most \(d+1\) points, not arbitrary full-dimensional hulls with more points. Neither the failure phenomenon in the simplex case nor the idea of a truncated tip is claimed as new. Our obstruction places the exact upper-tail dilution and moment-ratio limit at **every finite** prescribed sample size \(n\ge d+1\).
3. **Prior bounds which enter the proof.** [The owner's #209](../eventual-inclusion-hull-volume/PROOF.md) ([version archive](https://github.com/mxym/math/releases/tag/eventual-random-hull-inclusion-v1.0.0)) already proves the unrestricted fixed-dimension eventual comparison for the mean (\(p=1\)), including the cap and essential-label estimates. Here they are used as prior. Brunel [§5, Theorem 4, display (10)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.01620), also [Theorem 1 in the earlier preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2902), supplies the normalized missing-volume deviation bound, not an inclusion theorem for growing moments. Its exponent \(2/(d+1)\) is credited to the established concentration theory. See also the [journal record](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/deviation-inequalities-for-random-polytopes-in-arbitrary-convex-bo/).
4. **Different comparison principles.** [Paouris–Pivovarov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00519) treat stochastic comparisons under rearrangement, e.g. to symmetrized laws; these are not comparisons of the normalized uniform probability measures on arbitrary nested containers. [Zaporozhets–Moseeva (2025)](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/znsl7602) compare beta distributions and radially ordered laws. In the present problem, the count is fixed and the uniform sample law is separately renormalized for each body; simple inclusion coupling of a fixed-intensity Poisson process is not applicable.
5. **Search scope.** Checked the original formulations and searched for higher, inverse and logarithmic moments, Mellin transforms, stochastic ordering or domination of random hull volumes, uniform-order intervals, sample-size thresholds, nesting and dilution of near-extremal tails. No full result with the indicated growing-order and every-finite-sample quantifiers was located. Unpublished work or alternate formulations may be missed; this is not a priority determination.
