# When does enlarging a body increase the moments of its random hull volume?

For each convex body, sample \(n\) points independently from its **own** normalized uniform distribution and take the actual (unnormalized) volume \(V\) of their hull. The hull of a sample in a larger body need not stochastically dominate that in a smaller one, however large the finite sample size. Yet for sufficiently large samples it dominates for a growing interval of moment orders.

- [Fixed orders and an obstruction](PROOF.md). In each fixed dimension a sample threshold works simultaneously throughout any prescribed bounded interval of signed orders. Enlarging the body strictly increases positive raw moments and expected log volume and strictly decreases negative raw moments. For each fixed \(d\ge2\), \(n\ge d+1\), a pair of nested polytopes has strictly reversed survival probabilities throughout an interval up to their common maximum, in an exact constant ratio. Its high positive moments are reversed too. This last construction may depend on \(n\).
- [How far the interval grows](GROWING.md). Let \(\alpha=2/(d+1)\), \(d\ge2\). Uniform comparison holds for \(|p|\le B n^\alpha\) for each fixed \(B>0\), with a quantitative constant independent of \(n\). It even holds for \(|p|\le\kappa_d n^\alpha\log n\), with a weaker quantitative factor of order \(n^{-\alpha/2}\). The optimal range is not known here. In one dimension the range of uniform points in an interval just scales with its length; the growing-order statement is not asserted there.
- [Prior results and scope](SOURCES.md). The mean-volume theorem is already in [the predecessor](../eventual-inclusion-hull-volume/PROOF.md). The high-order obstruction for simplex-size samples and its truncated-tip idea go back to Rademacher, following Meckes's questions. The growing-order proof uses Brunel's published concentration bound.
