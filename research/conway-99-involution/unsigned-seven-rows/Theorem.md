# The unsigned fifteen-space and short positive-sector eigenfunctions
**Scope.** Conditional necessary properties of the one-fixed-vertex involution in a putative srg(99,14,1,2). No assumption on the signed rank modulo 7 is required. This is not an exclusion of any of the five ambient types, the whole involution, or the graph. 

## Rational and integral forms
There are seven neighbor pairs of the fixed vertex (axes) and forty-two exterior pairs, two for each two-subset of seven axes. Let Z have columns e_i±e_j, B=|Z|. Between exterior pairs write T for the unsigned number of neighbors of a member in the other pair and L for the signed number, same minus opposite representatives. The two members of an exterior pair are nonadjacent. Each has two different axis neighbors; these are exchanged with their non-neighbors under the involution, so the twins have no common axis neighbor. They must have two common neighbors, both from a unique exterior pair joined double. All other occupied entries in that row of T are ten singles, since each vertex has degree 14 and two axis neighbors.

On symmetric vectors, with one coordinate repeated on each pair, the adjacency matrix has blocks
```
   0     2·1ᵀ      0
   1       I       B
   0      Bᵀ       T .
```
The physical dot metric here is diag(1,2I_7,2I_42). Substitution in A²+A=12I+2J gives T1=12·1, BT=4J−2B, T²+T=12I+4J−BᵀB. Also BBᵀ=10I+2J. Hence T acts as 12 on 1, as −2 on Bᵀ1⊥ (i.e. images of axis vectors orthogonal to 1), and has only 3, −4 on ker B. Its diagonal is zero, giving multiplicities 1, 6, 20, 15. The [unsigned projector](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/main/research/conway-99-involution/grids) was previously used as a PSD sieve. Thus
```
       H=6I−2T−BᵀB+J=14 P
```
for the ordinary 42-coordinate orthogonal projector P onto the 15-dimensional −4 eigenspace. Its positive form, obtained by quotient by its rational radical, is isometric to 14 times the ordinary dot form on im P: send x to Px. Its Gram lattice L⁺ is the integral span of p_y with this Gram; it has a 14-tight frame of forty-two norm-five vectors, and Bp=0 (each unsigned star sums to zero).

Here ε_p denotes the Hasse invariant of a rational positive form; all statements about determinants in this paragraph are up to rational squares. The [unrestricted rational eigenspace theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/main/research/conway-99-involution/rational-eigenspaces/Theorem.md) gives the 44-dimensional ordinary physical −4 form square determinant, ε_p=−1 at 2,7 and +1 elsewhere. The preceding [signed rational-form calculation](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/main/research/conway-99-involution/seven) computes the rank-15 positive space of F=12I−4L−ZᵀZ with square determinant and ε_p=−1 only at 2,7, **before** the residue-rank case division. The form G=3I−S on the 49 *single* antisymmetric coordinates (S has diagonal block −I on axes, off-diagonal Z, and exterior block L) is rationally the orthogonal sum 4I₇⊥F/4 by the upper-left Schur complement. Thus its positive form has rank 22 and exactly the same determinant and Hasse invariants as F. Indeed G=7P⁻ for the orthogonal projector in the ordinary 49-vector metric; this is NOT the doubled physical metric. For any n-dimensional form Q and a nonzero scalar c, diagonalizing Q and multiplying each pairwise Hilbert symbol gives
```
 ε_p(cQ)=ε_p(Q) (c,c)_p^[n(n−1)/2] (c,det Q)_p^(n−1).
```
The physical antisymmetric subspace has form (2/7)G. Its determinant is still a square (rank 22), while the extra Hasse factor is (14,−1)_p: n(n−1)/2=231 is odd, (c,c)=(c,−1), and det G is square. This factor is −1 only at 2,7 (2 is the sum of two squares, and use the ordinary symbols for 7). Therefore the physical antisymmetric subspace has all Hasse invariants +1. Its orthogonal complement W⁺ in the full −4 space has square determinant and Hasse −1 only at 2,7: the Hilbert cross factor of the two determinants is trivial.

Within W⁺ the exterior-only part has form H/7: physical metric is twice ordinary on exterior coordinates and H=14P. There are seven perpendicular boundary degrees of freedom. For a mean-zero axis vector a one has the symmetric −4 vector (0,a,−Bᵀa/2), of physical norm 7·(a,a). For constant a=t·1 use (−7t/2,t·1,−t·1/8), of physical norm (21t/4)². These are perpendicular to each other (by the zero mean) and to the exterior-only vectors (by ker B). Denote the sum-zero rational subspace of ordinary Q⁷ by A₆; then I₇=A₆⊥⟨7⟩, so 7I₇≅7A₆⊥⟨1⟩. In total 7W⁺≅I₇⊥H. The determinant of W⁺ was square and scaling by 7 in dimension 22 multiplies ε by (7,−1), cancelling the two nontrivial places. Hence **H has square determinant, all Hasse invariants +1, and positive signature; its positive space is rationally isometric to I₁₅.**

The quotient defining L⁺ has free rank 15 since its integer radical is primitive. In a unimodular change of basis putting that radical last, H consists of an integral nonsingular Gram followed by a zero block. The nonzero Smith factors of H are therefore those of L⁺. Let C=im(H) in Z⁴². For v integral in its rational span, Hv=14v by the projector identity, so 14 times the saturation of C lies in C; all nonzero factors divide 14. Over F₂, B has image the 6-dimensional even-weight hyperplane E in F₂⁷, generated by e_i+e_j. Dot on E is nondegenerate (its perpendicular is the all-ones span of odd weight). The all-ones row on 42 labels is not in the row span of B: no assignment b_i has b_i+b_j=1 on every edge of a triangle. Thus x↦(Bx,Σx) surjects E⊕F₂, where the form is nondegenerate; H≡BᵀB+J has rank 7 modulo 2. Its discriminant group is elementary at 2 of dimension 8. At 7 the dimension is 15−rank_7 H, which is even, say 2m, since det L⁺ is square.

For a primary discriminant group killed by p, multiply its Q/Z-valued discriminant pairing by p and read the numerator mod p. At 2 a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form of even dimension over F₂ is metabolic. If alternating, choose a symplectic basis. Otherwise it is diagonal type I_{2a} (or split off odd lines until the remainder is symplectic); the diagonal form has the canonical all-ones characteristic vector e≠0, with e·e=0, and e⊥/⟨e⟩ is nondegenerate alternating. Lift a Lagrangian from there and include e. At 7 the local Gram is a unit part and a block 7U of size 2m, U unimodular of unit determinant d. The unit part has trivial Hasse and even-many cross powers, while ε_p(7U)=(−1/7)^m(d/7). Hence (d/7)=(−1)^m, so the residual 7-discriminant form has split type in dimension 2m (inversion of d preserves character). It too has a Lagrangian. The inverse image in (L⁺)* of the direct sum of these Lagrangians is an integral unimodular rank-15 overlattice V⁺. It need not be unique; evenness is not required (indeed it contains a norm-5 vector).

Split off every norm-one line in V⁺ by integrality and apply Conway–Sloane, *The unimodular lattices of dimension up to 23*, Theorem 1 and Table 1 ([original article](https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me102.pdf)). The complete no-unit cores through rank 15 are the empty lattice, E₈, D₁₂⁺, (E₇⊕E₇)⁺ and A₁₅⁺. See also [Chenevier's Gram lists](https://gaetan.chenevier.perso.math.cnrs.fr/unimodular_lattices/index_uni.html). Thus any chosen V⁺ is one of I₁₅, I₇⊥E₈, I₃⊥D₁₂⁺, I₁⊥(E₇⊕E₇)⁺ and A₁₅⁺. The plus superscripts denote the unimodular glue, not the unglued root lattices. No local rational isometry alone would have implied an embedding in the *standard integral* lattice.

## Every unit has seven positive and seven negative coordinates
For u in (L⁺)* of norm 1, let x_y=(u,p_y). Then Bx=0, Tx=−4x, Σx=0 and Σx²=14; all entries are integers of absolute value at most 2 by Cauchy against norm 5. Were there a +2 (negate if needed), let a,b be the number of +2,−2 entries, and let t be the common positive and negative total mass. The energy is 2t+2(a+b)=14, so t=7−a−b. The negative load available to any T-row is at most t+max_{x_j<0}|x_j|, since its occupied columns are distinct singles except one double. If b=0 this is ≤7; if b≥1 then a+b≥2 and the bound is again ≤7. This contradicts its required value −8 at a +2. Therefore **exactly seven +1, seven −1, all remaining 28 zero**. In the ambient I₁₅ each p_y has exactly five nonzero unit-coordinate entries, all ±1; in general the same support restriction applies on all split norm-one axes.

Write P,N,O for the seven positive, seven negative and 28 zero labels. Let d=B1_P=B1_N, e=T_PP1, f=T_NN1, k=(T1_P)|_O=(T1_N)|_O. Then T_NP1=f+4·1 and T_PN1=e+4·1, so s=Σe=Σf is nonnegative even and Σ_O k=56−2s. Pair T²+T=12I+4J−BᵀB against 1_P: its right side is 280−Σd² and its left side is Σe²+Σ(f+4)²+Σk²+s. Since Σd=14, this rearranges to
```
      Σ_P e²+Σ_N f²+Σ_O(k−2)²+Σ_axes(d−2)² = 28−s.
```
The first two terms sum to at least 2s and the third is at least |Σ(k−2)|=2s by integrality. Hence s≤5 and therefore **s∈{0,2,4}**. Each sign class induces at most two unsigned edges counted with their multiplicities.

## Seven orthogonal unit rows are forced, not optional
Glue only the 7-part Lagrangian, obtaining an integral L₀⊃L⁺ of determinant 2⁸. For every y∈L₀*⊂(L⁺)* the integral row x=(y,p) has Σx=0 and hence 14||y||²=Σx² is even. Thus 7||y||² is an integer. The inverse Gram of L₀ has only 2-power denominators, so ||y||² is an integer. Polarization now shows M=√2 L₀* is an even integral lattice. Its determinant is 2¹⁵/2⁸=128. The 2-primary discriminant space L₀*/L₀ is elementary of dimension 8 as before, and now **alternating** because every dual norm is integral. Any subset of mutually integrally pairing dual vectors maps into an isotropic subspace and is simultaneously contained in a unimodular overlattice of L₀ by extending that space to a Lagrangian.

We use specific rigorous *lattice* packing upper bounds, not a numerical estimate of the Hermite constant: N. Zubrilina, [Improved lattice-packing bounds in small dimensions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35830), 26 October 2026, v1, §3 and Table 1, p. 6. The exact-certified upper bounds for ordinary occupied-volume densities in ranks 15 and 14 are respectively 0.0256118267 and 0.0352392080. If M had no roots, min(M)≥4 and its radius-one balls would have density at least vol(B¹⁵)/√128. This exceeds the rank-15 ceiling, so L₀* has a norm-one vector u. The corresponding root α=√2u in M is primitive. For M₁=M∩α⊥, det M₁=det(M)·(α,α)/div(α)²≤256: extend a basis of the kernel by a vector of generating positive pairing div(α) and project to ⟨α⟩. If M₁ were rootless then its ball density would be at least vol(B¹⁴)/16, again too large. These two strict comparisons can be done over Q: use π>3.14 and √128<12 in vol(B¹⁵)/√128=2¹⁵·7!·π⁷/(15!·√128) and vol(B¹⁴)/16=π⁷/(7!·16). Thus there are two orthogonal units in L₀*, which extend to a common unimodular overlattice V. In the complete list above this leaves I₁₅, I₇⊥E₈, I₃⊥D₁₂⁺.

In the last case C=2L₀*/2V lies in V/2V because the exponent of L₀*/L₀ is two. It has dimension four since [L₀*:V]=16. Every element of C has representative squared norm 0 mod 4. Projection to the three unit coordinates has nonzero kernel, so C contains a nonzero such class in D₁₂⁺/2D₁₂⁺. We claim every nonzero norm-0 mod 4 class of D₁₂⁺ contains a norm-4 vector. Use D₁₂={z∈Z¹²:Σz even}, D₁₂⁺=D₁₂∪((1/2)¹²+D₁₂). Vectors in the second coset have odd squared norm. For an integer vector, the Hamming weight of its parity pattern is 0,4,8 or 12, and adding twice the spin vector toggles all its parities. It suffices to consider weight 0 or 4. In the first case the nonzero residue is represented by 2e_i (the half has odd sum); in the second take ±1 on the four occupied positions and zero elsewhere. Adjusting one sign changes the parity of the sum of the half-difference and so permits matching the required residue modulo 2D₁₂. This proves the claim. Half the norm-four representative is a new unit of L₀* perpendicular to the original three standard units. Their pairwise integral classes extend together to another unimodular overlattice. Since no unimodular rank-15 lattice on the complete list has four through six unit coordinates, **one can always choose V⁺=I₁₅ or I₇⊥E₈**. This is an existence-of-choice assertion, not that every initial glue necessarily has units. Every possible graph with a one-fixed-point involution must therefore support at least seven pairwise orthogonal ternary 7-plus/7-minus rows with the sparse sign-class property above. This does not alone exclude a graph.

If V⁺=I₇⊥E₈, the standard-coordinate support size of p_y is 1,3, or 5 because the E₈ factor is even, and those entries are ±1. With n_i the counts of the three sizes, tightness on I₇ gives n₁+3n₃+5n₅=98; hence n₁=14+n₅ and n₃=28−2n₅. In I₁₅ all 42 supports have size 5 and each of the 15 coordinates occurs fourteen times.
