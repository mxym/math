# Exact finite reduction

This proof excludes an involution of a putative srg(99,14,1,2), using the cited prior signed/Hamming results and the separately bundled low-rank theorem. It does not exclude the graph itself. The only central axis needed is one of EVEN noncollapsed degree. All closures are direct in each parity; no odd relabeling, maximum-degree bound, or odd-degree case is needed.

## Primary input

Use the proved [unsigned simplex theorem](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/main/research/conway-99-involution/unsigned-simplex), the [signed 7-adic invariant](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/main/research/conway-99-involution/seven), and the low-principal-rank exclusion. Write B,Z for unsigned/signed 7 by 42 endpoint incidence matrices of a doubled K7. Orient an ij twist label, i<j, from its positive i-parent; its signed column is e_i+(-1)^t e_j. T,L are the unsigned counts/signed links on distinct exterior orbit labels, so T∈{0,1,2} and L=0 for even T, L=±1 for T=1. Put H=6I−2T−BᵀB+J, F=12I−4L−ZᵀZ. H is the Gram of 42 integral signed words in 15 coordinates, each of weight 5; every ij class has six possible supports (intrinsic Hamming coset), independently checked from the [15,4,8] code. F is real PSD; over F7 its nonsingular quotient has dimension 11 or 13, of square determinant when 13. A rank 11 quotient of *either* determinant embeds orthogonally in a nonsingular 13-space of square determinant by adjoining an appropriate plane.

At the unique fixed vertex o, each transposed pair of neighbors is adjacent: otherwise their two common neighbors consist of o and another fixed vertex. Conversely a transposed pair outside the closed neighborhood is nonadjacent: its unique common neighbor if adjacent would have to be o. Every exterior vertex is uniquely determined by its nonadjacent pair of axis parents, since the other common neighbor of these parents is o. This proves the seven axes and doubled K7 labeling without an extra orbit assumption. The fact that *every* Conway involution has exactly one fixed vertex is the separate published [Makhnev–Minakova Theorem 1 (2004), p. 96](https://doi.org/10.4213/dm144). Choose central axis 0 at the fixed vertex o. Its 12 exterior labels A admit like and opposite perfect matchings P,N on their positive lifts, as explained in the low-rank prerequisite. The baseline group permutes six peripheral axes and flips either axis member. Mark M(j)=j xor 1. Then H_A=5I−M−2P−2N and F_A=11I+M−4P+4N−J; F_A has zero row sum. The 3,580 ordered chart orbits are enumerated by an exact catalogue with Burnside check. Of these 637 and 2,838 have local 7-rank 10 and 11 respectively. Define U on all seven axes by putting edge ij precisely when its two H-Gram columns on that underlying ij are not negatives. This is independent of which endpoint is central, and interchanging labels does not change it. For a fixed central star the squared length of the sum of twins j,j' is 5+5+2H_jj'=8−4[P(j)=j']−4[N(j)=j']; hence that edge is absent precisely when both matchings pair the twins. Counting nonzero twin sums therefore gives exactly the ordinary graph degree at the central axis. Since the sum of seven degrees is even, at least one degree is even. Include all possible d=0,2,4,6; no separate exclusion of low-degree graphs is presumed. The Hamming axis cosets come from edges of K8 omitting one vertex, and the induced coordinate group on K8 is A8 (exhaustively regenerable). For clarity, an orthonormal q=1 frame b₁,…,b₇ determines seven q=2 classes 1+bᵢ (1 denotes the sum of the frame, the characteristic class); any two have binary dot 1. On the 28 q=2 classes write bᵢ+bⱼ as the edge ij and 1+bᵢ as i8. Binary dot 1 is precisely intersection in one vertex. The seven classes 1+bᵢ therefore form a star for any frame (a pairwise intersecting set of at least four distinct K8 edges has a common endpoint). The 21 other classes are the edges away from that omitted endpoint, and the column classes are their complements 1+bᵢ+bⱼ. Thus no different family of 21 classes is omitted. Fixing the omitted and central vertices permits arbitrary EVEN peripheral permutations: retain **two** label parities per chart. Flips of an integral coordinate are a gauge, not changes in a signed-graph orientation; normalize each coordinate's first nonzero entry positive.

For each of 30 outer labels y not in A the full graph has two neighbors on each side of the lifted central star (the central member has two common neighbors with the nonadjacent exterior vertex y, and the exterior parents lie on two other axes). Enumerate both unordered pairs and both parents; the positive representative therefore has a mask on the 39 old vertices (o, fourteen axis members and 24 anchor members). Its mate mask is obtained by involution. All 39, 41 and 43-vertex physical capacity inequalities are of the form number of common neighbors in this induced subgraph ≤2−adjacency. Keep the possibilities allowing at least both 2+2 new lifts pairwise. A common positive/negative attachment at anchor j can occur only if P(j) and N(j) differ, by the direct common-neighbor count below. The exact unary criterion further keeps only H and F rows orthogonal to the respective nullspace with nonnegative Schur-complement squared norm. H_A is a weighted graph Laplacian on components of M,P,N, and F_A is positive semidefinite with kernel spanned by the all-one vector; on their images adding all-ones per kernel component does not change the required inverse inner products. RForm computes checked fraction-free inverses; an incompatible zero or negative pivot cannot silently pass.

## Finite complement, shared across all labels

Choose an r×r nonsingular principal pivot Q of F_A, r=10 or 11. The sum of all twelve anchor vectors is **zero in the complete signed realization**, not only in its anchor Gram: F_A has zero row sum, and since the global real F is PSD, the corresponding integral row sums of F vanish for every outside label. Hence in char 7 the last anchor vector is the negative sum of the first eleven in the nonsingular quotient. One can use the eleven first anchors, split off Q, and the remaining nonpivot anchors have residual Gram zero. For r=11 none remain. For r=10 there is one, whose residual n either vanishes (mode 0), or is nonzero isotropic (mode 1). Since arbitrary nonsingular symmetric forms over F7 are classified by dimension and determinant squareclass, the residual 13−r space is diagonal (1,d) or (1,1,d), d=1 if det Q is square, 3 otherwise. The 13-space determinant is square, so the required residual determinant character equals that of Q. Selecting a particular nonzero null n in mode 1 is permitted by Witt extension, or direct enumeration of all isometries. Mode stays fixed for every outside label.

For a 39-attachment define f_y=−4L_Ay−Z_AᵀZ_y, u_y=1−B_AᵀB_y−2T_Ay. The common residual y has norm 10−f_y[I]ᵀ Q⁻¹ f_y[I] mod 7. If r=10, its pairing with the residual nonpivot n equals the nonpivot entry of f_y minus the corresponding pivot projection. Enumerate exactly every vector with this norm and pairing; if n=0 a nonzero requested pairing fails. For the first selected outside label only, use one representative per isometry orbit fixing n, separately preserving zero and every scalar multiple of n whenever necessary. Independent controls reconstruct *all* orthogonal matrices via orthonormal bases, then verify that the actual chosen representatives under actual isometries cover every vector of every fiber. Controls cover both determinant types and both r=10 modes and compare all pair-product boolean masks directly.

A complete choice for y consists of a signed Hamming word, a 39-mask and a common mod-7 vector. The first two must agree with T_Ay derived from word dots. For a pair y,z force T_yz=(1−B_yᵀB_z−p_yᵀp_z)/2; if T=1 allow either like or opposite L, if T=0 or 2 force L=0. Require
   residual(y)·residual(z)=−4L_yz−Z_yᵀZ_z−f_y[I]ᵀQ⁻¹ f_z[I].
Test common-neighbor capacities with both complete 2-vertex lifts included. A legitimate graph passes these tests for all labels at once. The implementation branches on only eight distinct outside labels and demands every unselected label have an individual compatible extension. Returning open therefore is *not* a realization; returning closed after exhaustive search excludes the chart. An upper limit on search nodes counts as a truncation, never a closure.

## Safe refinements

When quotienting individual star-word assignments inside a chart, only even peripheral permutations preserving P and N (together with actual peripheral member switches) are used. Their GL(4,2) coordinate lift transports all 21 Hamming classes simultaneously. A partial branch is removed only if an element preserves its chosen set and gives a strictly smaller first-use sign-normalized word, comparing in the same fixed variable order. The incident chart reorientation extends to every outer edge: under peripheral permutation q and six switches h_i, ij,t maps to q(i)q(j),t xor h_i xor h_j; apply the physical involution to the image representative if h_i (for q(i)<q(j)) or t xor h_j (otherwise) equals one. This relabeling preserves all physical capacities, signed Gram and mode (dimension of residual anchor image). No odd parity transfer is needed: the two chart orientations are searched independently in every case.

The stronger search also tests real residual Cauchy for the two new signed rows. Let n_y=10−f_yᵀF_A⁺f_y≥0. Round 128F_A⁺f_y componentwise to nearest integers m_y, and round 128n_y UP to integer N_y. Each |f_z|_1≤20 (four signed incidences of size four plus four signed endpoint contributions). Therefore the exact 128-times cross gram c is within 10 of c₀=−128(4L_yz+Z_yᵀZ_z)−m_y·f_z. Only if max(0,|c₀|−10)^2>N_y N_z do we reject. All operations after fraction-free inversion are integer. Overflow: projected row norms are at most 10, so the exact scaled pivot cross product is ≤1280 in absolute value by Cauchy. Adding diagonal-link/endpoint terms and rounding error leaves magnitudes <2100 before squaring. The independent direct 128-bit rational test compares 750,000 signed pairs and checks no exact positive Cauchy-allowed pair is dropped.

**Physical coincident-anchor detail.** If the two anchors at j± share both the vertices of the P(j)=N(j) orbit, those two nonadjacent anchors already have two common neighbors. The new orbit with its representative adjacent to both would force a third, which is forbidden.

## Independent controls of the symmetry step

There are two different group quotients. The first is a finite orthogonal action on the *residual vector* of just one newly selected outside label. In ranks 11/10 the direct checker constructs every linear isometry by listing all ordered orthogonal bases with prescribed nonzero diagonal norms, not using a transitivity assumption in its implementation. The binary group orders are 16 and 12; in both ternary determinant types the order is 672. With nonzero residual anchor n, it checks its orbit consists of all 48 nonzero null vectors and explicitly restricts to the stabilizer of order 14. For all 120 nonempty norm/pairing fibers across the six metric/mode fixtures, the union of orbits of the representatives actually retained by the program is the WHOLE fiber. An isometry acts on all not-yet-selected vectors simultaneously and fixes n; it cannot alter the words, attachments or any residual Gram entry. There is no second fresh normalization at a subsequent branch.

The second group quotient is on the 12 *anchor word* assignments. A separate exhaustive DFS for #0, #1, #11 enumerates every full anchor assignment modulo coordinate sign gauge, with no chart automorphism prefix pruning. For both chart parities, the separate checker applies every preserving even permutation and peripheral switch to the retained full assignments, normalizes their actual completed words again in fixed physical-index order, and checks equality of the resulting SET with that independently enumerated raw set, not just equality of counts. Each retained representative lies in a previously unmarked orbit. Thus the large first chart with 23,040 symmetries and the two 768-symmetry charts receive a complete explicit orbit audit in addition to the general proof.
