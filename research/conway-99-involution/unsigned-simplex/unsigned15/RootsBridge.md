# A root-or-simplex dichotomy for the positive-sector lattice (research note)
Assume the hypotheses and notation of the unsigned seven-row theorem. Choose a 7-primary gluing L₀, determinant 256, and its guaranteed integral unimodular extension V of type I₁₅ or I₇⊥E₈. Let C=2L₀*/2V in V/2V. It has dimension four, all its word norms vanish modulo four, and L₀/2V=C⊥ in the mod-two bilinear form (pair with all half-words of C).  A *root of L₀* in this note means an element **in L₀ itself** of squared norm two; this is distinct from the roots of √2L₀* used in the packing argument.

**Dichotomy.** At least one of (i) L₀ contains a root and has the signed-integer-valued but **unsigned-orbit-sector** eigenfunction below, or (ii) V=I₁₅ and C is the unique binary [15,4,8] simplex code up to coordinate permutation and choice of basis. The alternatives are necessary, not constructions. If V=I₁₅, a zero generator column implies a unit in L₀; this is in fact impossible (see below). Two equal nonzero generator columns imply eᵢ±eⱼ are roots of L₀. Otherwise all fifteen columns are the distinct nonzero vectors of F₂⁴. This is exactly the simplex code. For V=I₇⊥E₈, let D be the projection of C on E₈/2E₈; it has dimension ≤4. Every nonsingular vector in the even quadratic space E₈/2E₈ (form v²/2 mod 2) is the class of a root. Indeed there are 120 nonsingular vectors in this plus-type eight-dimensional space, and the 240 roots project in pairs ± with different images apart from opposite roots: a non-opposite difference has square 2,4 or 6, whereas nonzero 2E₈ has minimum 8. The plus type can be checked in a simple-root basis directly (see roots7e8.py). If D⊥ contains a nonsingular vector, its E₈ root belongs to L₀, as claimed. Otherwise D⊥ is totally singular of dimension ≥4, which forces dim D=4 and D=D⊥ totally singular by the Witt-index-4 bound. The E₈ projection of C is now injective and every projected norm is 0 mod 4. The binary I₇-projection is then a doubly even subspace, of dimension at most 3 (ordinary dot on F₂⁷ is nonsingular). If its kernel is nonzero, use a norm-four representative of its nonzero singular E₈ word to get a dual unit orthogonal to the seven I directions. This forces a simultaneous unimodular extension I₁₅ by the seven-row theorem classification, where case (i) or (ii) follows. Alternatively, require that the original mixed ambient is needed, so its I₇-projection is injective; then the projected doubly even [7,4] code is impossible. Singular nonzero E₈ classes all have norm-four representatives: W(E₈) acts transitively on nonzero singular classes (or enumerate the 2160 norm-four vectors). Thus the mixed argument is an existence-of-choice assertion. A rootless L₀ has an I₁₅ extension with simplex code.

**Row profile.** Fix y∈L₀ of norm 2, and set x_e=(y,p_e). Since [L₀:L⁺] is odd, y=∑v_e p_e mod 2L₀ with integral v. The vector x mod 2 obeys x≡Bᵀb+s1, with b=Bv mod 2 of even weight and s=∑v mod 2, from H mod 2. Also Bx=0, Tx=−4x, ∑x=0, ∑x²=28. The doubled cut of size 2|b|(7−|b|) is divisible by 4, the complementary cut has size 2 mod 4, so reduction of ∑x² mod4 forces s=0. Since L₀ integral, the equality modulo 2L₀ gives y²≡vᵀHv mod4. Here 6vᵀv≡2s=0, vᵀTv is even, (∑v)²≡0, and ||Bv||²≡|b| mod4. Hence |b|≡2 mod4, i.e. b has weight 2 or 6. The odd support is exactly one of the 21 doubled cuts with small side 2 (20 entries), or one of the 7 star cuts with small side 1 (12 entries). A unit y∈L₀ would have exactly 14 odd entries by the seven-row theorem; neither a doubled K₇ cut nor its complement has 14 entries, ruling out units.

Cauchy gives |x_e|≤3. In the 12-odd case write a=#|2|, d=#|3|, so a+2d=4 and m=∑_{x_e<0}|x_e|=10−d. Negate x to put +3 at e if d≥1. For d=1 the T-row negatives can contribute at most m+2=11; for d=2 at most m+3=11; both contradict Tx_e=−12. In the 20-odd case a+2d=2, so a +3 entails no magnitude 2 and no other 3, with m=11. All T-neighbors of e must then be negative on the cut, since even one missing unit of negative load makes 12 impossible with a unique double. The weighted incidence into the small side is then 12, but BT=4J−2B gives only 8−2=6 at a cut column. Thus in every case there are only ±1 on the doubled cut and respectively four or two ±2 outside, all other entries zero.

**Additional constraints for the small side 1.** On its unique star A, Bx=0 gives six entries of each sign. The four heavy labels have two of each sign since ∑x=0. The induced unsigned star has T_A=P+N for two matchings. For i∈A let u_i count its same-sign T_A neighbors with multiplicity. Then the signed heavy load opposite to i minus that of its own sign is exactly 1+u_i. Each heavy label has T-load 4 into A. If E_same counts the (weighted, undirected) same-sign edges of T_A and w is the total same-sign A-to-heavy load, it follows 16−2w=12+2E_same. Each matching has an even number of same-sign edges on a 6+6 partition, whence E_same=0 or 2 and w=2−E_same. For each heavy j, the opposite-sign load among the other heavy labels less the same-sign one is 2+w_j, where w_j is its own same-sign load into A. It has only two heavy labels of the opposite sign, and at most one doubled neighbor, whence w_j≤1. These are preliminary necessary conditions, not an elimination of the row.

**Additional constraints for the small side 2.** Write C for the two labels internal to its small side, and I,E for the signed sums of all heavy entries over internal and external labels, respectively (here external means that both supporting axes lie outside the small side). Summing Bx on the small side and summing x on all labels gives ∑cut x=−2I and ∑cut x=−I−E, so I=E. If exactly one heavy label is internal, the other would have to have its sign. The T-load on the cut at an internal label is at most 4 by the small-axis sum of BT, so this cannot give the required opposite load 8. If both are internal, their signs are opposite; put t equal to their mutual T-entry. The cut load at either is 4−2t and the other heavy supplies at most 2t more, again too small. Therefore the two heavy labels u,v are both external and opposite. Each of the two small stars is 5+/5− on its ten cut labels by Bx=0. Writing r_y for y's same-sign load on the cut and a_y=∑_{c∈C}T_yc, each heavy y has cut load 8−2a_y, giving T_uv=a_y+r_y for y=u and v.
