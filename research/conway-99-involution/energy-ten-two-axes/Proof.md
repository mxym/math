# Shell obstruction in Conway's 99-graph: exactly two nonzero axes at folded energy ten

**Target.** Suppose a strongly regular (99,14,1,2) graph has an involution with one fixed vertex o. Let an integral eigenfunction f with eigenvalue −4 change sign under the involution. At folded energy E=∑fᵢ²=10, where i runs through the 49 twin pairs, can exactly two of the seven axis pairs (the neighbors of o) have nonzero entries? The theorem certified by a complete run of the accompanying checker is that **this is impossible**. It does not exclude E=10 with zero or four supported axis pairs or any graph unconditionally. It does not require the rank-13 hypothesis or the large E≤9 replay.

We use A²+A=12I+2J throughout. In N(o) the graph is a matching. Neighbor twins must be adjacent: otherwise their two common neighbors would include o, hence also a second fixed vertex. Exterior twins cannot be adjacent, since their unique common neighbor would have to be fixed. Each exterior has two neighbors of o, on distinct axes, by the SRG pair counts. For each unordered pair of axes all four oriented pairs of neighbors need one common exterior besides o, giving two exterior orbit labels, one for each relative sign. Thus there are 42 exterior labels. Pairing an exterior with both twins on another exterior orbit supplies two common neighbors to its own twins; each exterior has at most one such double partner.

A value with magnitude ≥2 needs ≥8 opposite neighbor mass by the eigen-equation. At E=10 a magnitude ≥3 gives total opposite mass ≤4; two magnitudes two give total ≤6. With a single high pair ±2 only six ordinary positive and negative vertices remain, so the high vertices must be adjacent to each other and to all six opposing ordinary vertices. An ordinary positive needs at least two ordinary negative neighbors apart from its high negative neighbor. It shares these and its high negative neighbor with the high positive, impossible. Hence f takes values 0,±1, with ten positive vertices P, ten negative twins N.

Let e be the number of edges inside P, dᵢ their degrees; a positive has 4+dᵢ negative neighbors. Every zero has h neighbors in P and h in N. Degree counting yields Σh=100−4e. Counting common neighbors of pairs in P, subtracting contributions through P and N, gives Σ binom(h,2)=30−9e−2Σᵢ binom(dᵢ,2). There are 79 zeros and binom(h,2)≥h−1, hence e≤1. Except at o, zeros occur in twin pairs of equal h; h(o)=2 by hypothesis. The coincidence sum is therefore odd, forcing e=1. Let X=P∪N and F be the four endpoints of the two internal edges. The above two identities give Σ binom(h−1,2)=4 (with h=0 contributing 1). Since o contributes zero, exactly two zero twin pairs have h=0 or 3, all other zeros h=1 or 2. If h(v)=3, at most two zero vertices have h=0. But A²1_X(v)=40−6=34. Of v's neighbors six lie in X, with ≥4 neighbors there; at least six more have ≥2 neighbors there, forcing ≥36. Contradiction. Thus exactly two zero twin pairs have h=0. Let Zⱼ={zeros with h=j}. The indicator identities are
 A1_X=2·1+2·1_X+2·1_F−2·1_Z0+2·1_Z2,
 A(1_Z2−1_Z0)=6·1_X+6·1−(3/2)A1_X−A1_F.
The latter follows by multiplying the first by A and substituting A²1_X=12·1_X−A1_X+40·1.

## An exhaustive support core, not an assumed chart

Label elements of P by 0,…,9, with edge (0,1), and label N by their twins. The cross matrix M is symmetric; its row sums are 5 at the edge endpoints and 4 elsewhere. Its two diagonal ones belong exactly to supported axes. Both endpoints cannot be axes since the two would belong to different axis pairs and hence be nonadjacent. We specify either neither (use labels 2,3) or one (use labels 0,2). No off-diagonal entry between different axes is allowed. core2label.cpp fills every upper-triangular row with its required number of ones, with only row-capacity and already excessive common-neighbor counts as cuts. With adjacency H on the 20 vertices, necessary counts are (H²+H)ᵢⱼ plus the known contribution of o (both indices axes) at most two off the diagonal. There are 2,880 labeled matrices in the first case and zero in the second. coreorbit.py generates the entire orbit of the single supplied matrix under S₂×S₂×S₆ and compares the two sets for exact equality. It checks 2,880 **different** images.

There are five unsupported axes. Choose one side of each unordered neighborhood pair on X. Its neighbors are h positives and h negatives, neither on a supported axis and never both members of a twin pair. Reversing the axis lets the positive 10-bit mask be smaller than its negative mask for nonempty choices. For exterior support label i the required total number of missing axis incidences is 2−the number of its neighbors among the two supported axes. Summing over eight exterior labels gives 10; hence Σh over the five axes is 5. Each distinct pair of unsupported axes has the known common vertex o and can have at most one more common vertex. axis2.cpp enumerates sorted distinct nonempty masks in each h-category, allows empty axes by padding, and enforces these degree and common-neighbor capacities. Repeated nonempty oriented/twin mask is impossible: even an h=1 pair shares ≥2 additional neighbors. The enumeration allows h=3 as a safe relaxation and at most two exceptional axes; the stronger h≤2 condition above is used in the subsequent exact system. All 194 resulting outputs are retained. Their axis order follows this enumeration; permuting or independently reversing unsupported axes only relabels the graph.

## First-layer equations

Index the core of 31 by o, all seven axis twin pairs, then the eight supported exterior twin pairs. Write H for its exact induced adjacency and R=12I+2J−H−H². A remaining exterior column v gives its 0/1 adjacency to these 31, its twin v′=v∘ι. It has its prescribed unused axis label, meaning one neighbor on each of two axes with a prescribed relative sign; orient the exterior so the first is positive-axis indexed. Enumerate all 2¹⁶ incidence patterns on the 16 supported exterior vertices. Require equal numbers h≤2 of positive and negative neighbors, no h=0 if both exceptional slots are already accounted for, at most one seen double orbit, and H v+v≤2. Require vvᵀ+v′v′ᵀ≤R coordinatewise. For a genuine graph there is exactly one choice per unused label and
 ∑(vvᵀ+v′v′ᵀ)=R.
In addition, summing signed columns (+ for h=2, − for h=0, zero for h=1) yields the missing part of the displayed A(1_Z2−1_Z0) identity on 31 core rows. Blockwise shifts by the minimum coefficient per label preserve the equations because exactly one style is selected per label. These equations give Qx=r with 34 one-choice groups. For any triple of distinct core vertices the number of common neighbors is ≤1; otherwise two neighbors of this triple would have three common neighbors. Subtract the existing core triple count and impose the remainder as an at-most constraint, counting both twin orientations of each style.

The 156 root exclusions require Qx=r only. Given a signed integer multiplier y, verify y·r>34 max(0,maxⱼ (yQ)ⱼ). This contradicts a nonnegative integral selection with ∑xⱼ=34. Remaining charts are checked as specified in the manifest. For a style xₜ to be impossible, it suffices that yQ+10⁶eₜ≤0 and y·r>−10⁶; this implies xₜ<1. Every style removed by pruning is checked against the **original** Q,r, not merely against an already filtered heuristic array.

## Local cuts and Boolean checks

For a selected oriented exterior style v with group g let u=2·1−v−Hv. This is the vector of still missing common-neighbor counts between this exterior vertex and each core vertex. For any actual neighbor outside the 31-core with orientation w we necessarily have w=0 on coordinates with u=0, and v·w≤1 (these two vertices are adjacent, so have only one common neighbor). Exclude the exterior's own orbit, since distinct exterior twins are nonadjacent; no loops either. For another group style j count, at each core coordinate p, how many of its two admissible orientations have wₚ=1, where admissible just means these necessary tests hold. Call that count Wⱼₚ. We have the valid implication
   xᵥ=1  ⇒  ∑ⱼ Wⱼₚ xⱼ ≥ uₚ.
It is harmless to include only some coordinates/styles. We subtract per-group minima from both sides and omit inequalities with more than the specified number of nonconstant groups. A group with surviving styles indexed by S has a predicate for any event T⊂S, meaning its selected style lies in T. It is precisely the OR of the corresponding style variables; its complement is the event S\T. Decompose a weighted group contribution into these threshold predicates. The base equalities and inequalities use the bidirectional totalizer in cnf.py. At the merge of left/right sorted threshold vectors A,B impose for each target threshold l both Aᵢ∧Bⱼ⇒Yₗ when i+j=l, and Yₗ⇒Aᵢ₊₁∨Bⱼ₊₁ when i+j=l−1; constant boundary entries mean true for threshold zero and false above capacity. The local lower bounds have residual RHS at most two; an OR suffices for one, and for two require an OR after omitting every single-weight literal, as well as the full OR for repeated-literal cases. Condition each clause on xᵥ.

For two difficult charts (31 and 98) the nine possibilities of one smallest-domain label partition all solutions. All nine alternatives are certified, including alternatives directly contradicted by a proved style exclusion. No choice among the two zero-touching orbits is used to cover these nine branches.

The independent proof checker begins with exactly the regenerated base clauses and a harmless true sentinel (the solver labels vertex variables starting at 2). A purported clause is added **only** if its negation conflicts by unit propagation against those clauses and earlier accepted lemmas. Retaining clauses that a solver deleted cannot invalidate this implication. The checked empty clause is mandatory. No solver is trusted to declare UNSAT, and no rational LP solver is used for checking the lattice/graph infeasibility conclusions.

**Additional certificate formats.** Some charts may use a pure pseudo-Boolean RUP proof or a multivalued domain split tree instead of totalizer RUP, as explicitly indicated in coverage.json. In the former, original equations are split into two inequalities: upper bounds are complemented literal-by-literal to lower bounds. Every row is Σᵢ wᵢ[ℓᵢ]≥b with positive integer weights. Under a partial assignment let slack be the sum of not-yet-false weights minus b. Slack <0 is a contradiction; any unassigned literal of weight larger than slack must be true. pbrup.cpp combines this propagation with ordinary watched learned clauses and admits only checked RUP clauses, including the final empty clause. No native PB solver lemmas or cuts are presumed. For the domain tree each label has a finite domain of candidate styles. cspinput.py groups the coefficients of every row by label and stores nested threshold masks. Thus the minimum and maximum possible contribution of a label are known exactly within its current domain. If the total minima exceed the upper bound or the maxima are below the lower bound, a contradiction is proved; a style whose individual weight already forces either event may be removed. treeverify.cpp reads the binary tree, verifies both disjoint exhaustive children at every split, and permits a leaf only if these propagation rules detect contradiction. The replay tests end of file and never accepts a search budget as an exclusion.
