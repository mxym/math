# Dichotomy at the grid-free threshold

Let $n_3$ count unordered pairs of disjoint triangles joined by two edges, $P$ unordered pairs joined by three edges (triangular prisms), and $g$ induced $3$-by-$3$ grids. For a marked triangle, the three 12-point groups at its three points are linked across each pair by a perfect matching. Thus the number of cross-group edges is 36. Summing over the 231 triangles gives $2n_3+6P=8316$, i.e. $n_3+3P=4158$.

At a vertex $o$, its 14 neighbors are seven two-point axes. For any pair of axes $a,b$, the four choices of a signed point on each determine four distinct cell points: for signed labels $u,v$ the two common neighbors are $o$ and the unique cell point labeled $(u,v)$. Call an edge of the cell a *flip* if its endpoints agree in one coordinate and differ in the other. A prism with marked root $o$ is equivalent to a flip in a rooted cell. Explicitly, the two triangles of the prism are $T=(o,a_+,a_-)$ and $T'=(b_s,x_{+,s},x_{-,s})$; the other flip direction exchanges $a,b$. The three edges between them are exactly the matching (the SRG common-neighbor bounds disallow additional edges). Hence the sum of flips over all rooted cells is $6P$.

Each rooted cell has at most four flips. Three flips form a spanning path on its four sign pairs. Its two interior vertices have used both of their inside-cell neighbors: a point in the cell can have at most two neighbors carrying either of its axes, by the common-neighbor counts with the two opposite signed parents. Any possible diagonal of the square is incident with a degree-two interior vertex. If the fourth flip is absent, the four-point cell therefore has exactly three edges, and the associated nine-point skeleton has exactly $14+3=17$ edges, contrary to the [nine-point density gap](README.md). Four flips give an induced grid on the root, the four neighbors on the axes, and the cell. Conversely each induced grid containing the root consists of two full axes through the root and their four cell points. Thus over $99\binom72=2079$ rooted cells, exactly $9g$ carry four flips, the others at most two. Consequently
$$6P\le2(2079-9g)+4(9g)=4158+18g,$$
and
$$n_3\ge2079-9g.$$
In particular, every grid-free graph would have $n_3\ge2079$; whenever $n_3<2079$ there must be a grid. By the [unconditional flag bound](../order-nine-flags/README.md), $n_3\ge1602$. A hypothetical graph attaining $n_3=1602$ must have at least 53 induced grids. The converse is not asserted. This is not an existence or nonexistence result.
