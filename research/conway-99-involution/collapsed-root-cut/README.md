# A four-edge noncollapse condition for Conway’s 99-graph involutions

**Theorem.** Suppose an srg(99,14,1,2) admits an involution. At its unique fixed point, call the seven adjacent two-point orbits the axes. For each pair of axes there are two associated outer two-point orbits. At least one axis is incident to **four or more** pairs of axes for which the associated two outer orbits are **not** joined by all four possible edges. This is a necessary condition, not an exclusion of the involution. It applies to both signed residue-rank branches.

### A complementary root Gram matrix

Use the [one-fixed-point quotient](../README.md) and its unsigned matrix T. The columns of X are the 0–1 incidence vectors of the underlying two-subsets of the axes, each appearing twice. The matrix H = 6I−2T−XᵀX+J is 14 times an orthogonal projection and H Xᵀ=0. Realize it by column vectors p_e,p′_e for each edge e of K₇. Thus each vector has squared norm 5, the full frame satisfies ∑p pᵀ=14I on its span, and ∑_{e∋v}(p_e+p′_e)=0 for every axis v.

For the paired columns on one edge, H_{e,e′}=−1−2T_{e,e′}. Therefore u_e=p_e+p′_e vanishes exactly when T_{e,e′}=2 (all four edges are present). Write C for this set of collapsed edges, and U for the noncollapsed graph on the seven axes. The star relation shows U has no degree-one vertex. Also U cannot contain a triangle all three of whose vertices have degree two: the star relation would make its three nonzero u_e alternate sign around an odd cycle.

Choose q_e=p_e=−p′_e for e in C. If two different collapsed edges meet, the pairing of the chosen columns is −2T≤0, and so is the pairing after one sign is flipped; it is therefore zero. If the edges are disjoint, both these pairings have the form 1−2T≤1, and are opposite. Being odd, they must be ±1. The collapsed columns contribute ∑2q_e q_eᵀ to the 14-tight frame. This gives a positive semidefinite Gram matrix
```
K = 7I − ((q_e,q_f))_{e,f in C}.
```
It has diagonal 2 and off-diagonal entries ±1 exactly where the edges of C are disjoint, and zero elsewhere.

In the disjointness graph on C, consider a connected component. The corresponding principal block of K is the Gram of squared-length-two vectors in its positive real span (no assertion of nonsingularity on the columns). Their integer span is discrete: it lies in the dual of the lattice generated by any linearly independent subfamily spanning that real space, because all those pairings are integral. All norm-two vectors of this integral lattice form a finite simply-laced root system: reflection in any of them subtracts an integral multiple of that root, and preserves lengths. The connected selected roots belong to one irreducible component, hence to type A, D or E by the classical simply-laced classification (see the root-lattice method in [Cameron–Goethals–Seidel–Shult](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(76)90162-9)).

* For type E the actual block has rank at most 8. Reducing its integer entries modulo 2 cannot increase rank; the reduced block is precisely the ordinary disjointness adjacency. Its binary rank is therefore at most 8.
* In types A and D each root has a two-coordinate support and nonzero entries ±1. Two selected roots with the same support are orthogonal, since equal/opposite roots are ruled out by the off-diagonal magnitudes. Their open neighborhoods coincide: any other support meets both in exactly the same number of coordinates, and different supports yield a nonzero pairing exactly when they meet once. Keep one representative per identical open neighborhood. No surviving supports coincide, so the induced graph on them is a line graph of a simple graph and contains no induced claw. Different supports with the same neighborhood do not cause a problem: the remaining graph is still the line graph of the surviving supports.

It follows that a component of binary rank greater than 8 whose identical-neighborhood quotient contains a claw is impossible. The rank here is that of the **original component**, not required to be computed after quotienting.

### Exact seven-axis check

For contradiction assume the maximum degree in U is at most three. The independent verifier enumerates every one of the 2²¹ labelled subgraphs of K₇ and admits precisely those with maximum degree at most three and without degree-one vertices. It constructs the disjointness graph on the complement edge set C, computes connected components, and performs binary elimination and a direct induced-claw check on the specified representatives. Among 50,208 admitted graphs, 50,173 have an impossible component. The other 35 are precisely the labelled copies of K₃ ⊔ K₄: in each, the three degree-two vertices form the degree-two triangle already excluded by the star relation. The transcript reports the partition by degree sequence. The computation uses neither floating-point arithmetic nor a catalogue of graph isomorphism classes.

Replay with `g++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify.cpp -o verify && ./verify`. All inputs are generated internally.

### Scope and related work

This only justifies keeping search templates with at least one axis of noncollapsed degree at least four; it does not say every axis must have this degree. No finite-prime rank hypothesis, Hamming embedding or search budget is used. Adjacent Conway work includes the orbit-search framework of [Thakkar–Severini v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11211v3), the 84-vertex residual spectrum of [Natal–Al-saadi v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16266v2), and [triangle-orbit congruences](https://www.clawxiv.org/abs/clawxiv.2610.00005). The [marked-graph theorem of Chen–Liu–Shan–Stanić](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11870v1) treats a different least-eigenvalue inequality; the present test uses an ordinary norm-two Gram whose nonzero signs may vary, then discards those signs over F₂ to obtain a necessary rank restriction.
