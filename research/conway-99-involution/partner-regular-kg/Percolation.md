# Every vertex has at least eight 2-percolating partners

Write cl(S) for the closure obtained by repeatedly adding vertices with at least two neighbors already present. In a hypothetical SRG(99,14,1,2), every vertex o has at least **eight nonneighbors** y such that cl({o,y}) is the entire graph. In particular there are at least 396 unordered generating pairs. This follows from the no-partner-regular-vertex theorem; no construction or nonexistence conclusion follows.

**Closure criterion.** A nonadjacent pair fails to percolate exactly when it lies in a 3-by-3 rook grid. This specializes the closure argument of [Ibrahim, LaFayette and McCall, *Minimum 2-percolating sets in 2-connected, diameter 2 graphs*, Australas. J. Combin. 93 (2025), 60–89, Lemma 4.9 and Theorem 4.19](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/93/ajc_v93_p060.pdf). Here is a direct verification. In the induced graph on the closure every pair retains all its common neighbors, so adjacent and nonadjacent distinct pairs have respectively one and two. Write B for its adjacency, d for its degree vector. Then

    B²+B = 2J + diag(d−2).

Commuting with B shows (d_i−d_j)(2−B_ij)=0, so all degrees equal k. With a nonedge present, the order is v=1+k²/2 by summing a row. Each local neighborhood is a matching, hence k is even. If the closure is proper then k≤12. All restricted eigenvalues solve θ²+θ−(k−2)=0. For k=6,10,12 their discriminants 17,33,41 are nonsquares, forcing equal multiplicities and trace k−k²/4 ≠ 0, impossible. At k=8 the roots are 2,−3 on dimension 32, and trace zero would give multiplicity 88/5 for 2. Degrees 0 or 2 cannot accommodate the original nonedge and its common neighbors with v=1+k²/2. Hence k=4, v=9. This graph must be the rook grid: the four neighbors of a root form two matched axes, its four remaining points take the four sign pairs, and the interior matching at each of the four root neighbors forces the square. Conversely a rook grid has its internal pairwise λ,μ saturated, and is closed, so no pair in it percolates outside.

**Counting locally.** Any grid containing o must use two entire axes at o. The four remaining points of the grid are uniquely determined as the four non-root common neighbors of cross-axis signed pairs. Thus at most one grid corresponds to each of the 21 axis pairs, and a grid contains exactly four nonneighbors of o (in its own cell). By the main theorem at least one signed-neighbor matching M_x is not the partner matching. Two distinct perfect matchings on twelve points differ on at least two edges of either one. Two missing partner edges at x therefore break the sign flips of two *different* axis-pair cells. Neither cell extends to a rook grid; each of their four exterior points consequently percolates with o. This proves the bound.

The paper gives a possible global count up to 231 grids, with equality exactly when the 2-percolation number is three. Do not interpret the local result as the first reason to rule out that equality: a positive aggregate prism defect already does so. The conclusion specific to this argument is the uniform eight-partner lower bound at **every** vertex, even if the graph lacks any symmetry.
