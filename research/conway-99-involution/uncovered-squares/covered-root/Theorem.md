# A covered-root motif inequality and exclusion of the 53-grid endpoint

A point o of a putative SRG(99,14,1,2) is **covered** if every square through o belongs to at least one triangular prism. Define H to be the nine-vertex graph consisting of three independent centers, with two private, independent common neighbors for each center pair and no other edges (the double subdivision of the three sides of a triangle, i.e. mask 0xf67e0000 in the [positive identity](../../order-nine-flags/README.md)). Write h(o) for the number of induced unlabeled copies with o as one of the three centers and d(o) for the number of nongrid four-cells among the 21 rooted at o. Then at every covered point,
 h(o) ≥ (2/3)(d(o)−14).
This uses no graph automorphism and makes no assumption that any other point is covered. The bound is trivial when d(o)≤14. The exact certificate individually verifies the weaker real LP bound (2/5)(d−14) and a strict bound high enough to obtain the displayed integer inequality.

At n3=1602 the positive identity gives total copy count H≤24: a labeled embedding has coefficient 1/104 and this motif has 48 automorphisms (permute its three centers and the two vertices in each of its three common-neighbor pairs); thus each unlabeled copy has weight 6/13 against surplus 144/13. The sum of h(o) is three times the number of copies, at most 72. The [companion switching lemma](../Switch.md) proves that if g=53 at this endpoint then all 99 roots are covered. Their d sum is 2079−9·53=1602. Hence the new bound would give sum h(o)≥(2/3)(1602−14·99)=144, a contradiction. In particular n3=1602 would require 54 grids, not 53. The original graph problem and the 54-grid branch are not settled by this argument.

## Rooted incidence, vectors and flows

Fix a covered point o. The neighbors of o form seven signed axis pairs on its seven triangles. Each exterior point belongs to the four-cell C_ab with signed parents on distinct axes a,b, one point for each of the 84 label pairs, by the two-common-neighbor condition. Points in a cell have two neighbors in the exterior carrying each of their two axes, one of each sign: for the signed parent, the triangle partner through that parent; for the opposite sign, the second common neighbor besides their own signed parent. In particular if a vertex has two internal neighbors, both must be its two standard flips, changing just one sign each. A flip at a signed point is equivalent to a prism direction for its square with o: its edge and their common signed parent form the triangle matched to the other root axis. All cells of a covered root thus have no isolated vertex on the graph of flip edges. Three flips are impossible by the [nine-point 17-edge gap](../../grid-density-gap/README.md) (the degree-two points rule out an additional diagonal), and four is exactly a grid. In a nongrid cell the flip edges are therefore a matching of two parallel flips. There is no extra diagonal: it would give a point of internal degree two without two standard flips. Orient this dirty axis pair a→b if it preserves the signed a-coordinate and reverses b.

Let S_e include o, the four parents on pair e and its four-point cell. Set g=3I−A+J/9 and v_e=−g1_(S_e)=(A−3I)1_(S_e)−1, where A is the graph adjacency. From A²=12I−A+2J one has g²=7g, g(A+4I)=0. The vector v_e vanishes on o and its neighbors by counting their contacts with S_e. At x in C_h its value is
 v_e(x)=|N(x)∩C_e|−2[h=e]−[e,h disjoint].
Its norm square is 14(4−m_e) by g²=7g and the 14+m_e edges of S_e, m_e=# internal edges of C_e. Thus clean columns are zero and dirty columns have norm square 28. Each dirty column is ternary: on its own cell it is −1; on a disjoint cell it is between −1 and 1 since an outsider can meet at most one end of each internal matching edge (their unique common neighbor is their signed parent). In a different intersecting cell it is 0 or 1: a dirty point has only one additional neighbor carrying their shared axis, and a clean point has none.

For each axis a, sum_(e incident a) v_e=0. Indeed if t_a indicates its two signed neighbors, then sum 1_(S_e)=(A+4I)(e_o+t_a), by counting. Across two different cells meeting at a, any edge must join two dirty cells having a in the same role. A dirty point in a cell with tail a has already used its same-sign neighbor internally and its remaining a-neighbor has opposite sign; a point with head a has used its opposite-sign neighbor and the remaining one has the same sign. These requirements cannot agree for opposite roles. Since
 ⟨v_h,v_e⟩=−7 sum_(x∈C_h) v_e(x)
by g²=7g, the two opposite-role groups of columns at each axis are orthogonal. Their combined sum is zero, so each group sums to zero separately. Let B be the bipartite graph of dirty arcs, an arc a→b joining the source copy of a to the target copy of b. Its vertex-wise sums of v_e vanish. No node of B has degree one; no arc is a bridge. At each exterior point x, w(x)=(v_e(x)) is an integer circulation with coordinates 0,±1 (orient all edges from source to target). On its own dirty h the coordinate is −1; other dirty edges intersecting h in the same role have nonnegative coordinates; all opposite-role intersections give zero. In a clean h all intersecting coordinates vanish. These are the only pointwise membership constraints used.

## Counting obstructions to H at the root

For distinct disjoint pairs of axes h,f there are 16 pairs v∈C_h, z∈C_f. None of their four signed parents are adjacent (the underlying axes differ). If v,z are nonadjacent, they have two further common neighbors p,q, both exterior since their parent labels are disjoint. The six private common neighbors for the center pairs among o,v,z are therefore the four parents and p,q. The two latter are nonadjacent since otherwise that adjacent pair would have common neighbors v,z. Hence the nine points induce H unless p or q touches one of the four signed parents. Such a neighbor is the unique triangle partner of v or z through the corresponding signed parent. Conversely, an induced H centered at o requires disjoint underlying axes h,f because its four parent vertices are distinct, independent vertices in N(o). There is no overcount across cells or label pairs: the other two centers determine the entire copy.

For a point p∈C_i with row w (extend it by zero on clean columns), let
 k_f(p)=2[i=f]+[i,f disjoint]+w_f.
Let j_h(p) count its signed-parent triangle partners in C_h. When i=h this equals two in a clean cell and one in a dirty cell. For i≠h, it is w_h if i,h are dirty with the same head, and zero otherwise: only the remaining same-signed link at the head gives an external parent partner. These depend solely on the cell and row of p. Writing L_hf=sum_(p∈C_h) k_f(p), define
 M_hf=L_hf+sum_p (j_h(p)k_f(p)+j_f(p)k_h(p)).
Every pair v,z not giving H is counted at least once by M_hf: adjacency contributes L; a common neighbor p that is a parent partner of v contributes in the first product, or of z in the second. This is an upper bound by simple union, so double counting marked configurations would be harmless. We have proved
 h(o)≥sum_(unordered disjoint h,f) max(0,16−M_hf).
There are 105 such axis-pair pairs. The certificate only uses this lower bound.

## Necessary row moments and exact linear certificates

In a given diagram let D be the integer fundamental-cycle matrix of B, with chord rows the identity; every row w=Dt is determined by its ternary vector t of chord values. A candidate in cell h is any t with Dt ternary satisfying the pointwise membership tests above. Let n_(h,t) count its points, so n≥0 and each cell has four. No graph is assumed to exist from such counts. Write s_h=sum n_(h,t)t. The four rows in each clean cell sum to zero, since its column v_h=0 and ⟨v_h,v_e⟩=−7 sum_(x∈C_h)v_e(x). For dirty cells, the linear relations among columns (all rows w=Dt), together with this Gram formula, give s_h=sum_j D_hj s_(chord j). This also covers a clean h by the convention D_hj=0. The chord Gram formula further gives
 sum_(h,t) n_(h,t) t_i t_j = −7(s_(chord i))_j.
Denote all these homogeneous moment coefficients by A, omitting tautological chord-cell mean equations. Denote the matrix of the linear M_hf by Q. For integer signed moment multipliers u and nonnegative weights 0≤a_hf≤s=100000, one rigorous lower numerator at every chart is
 B0=16 sum a_hf + 4 sum_h min_(t in h) [−(Aᵀu+Qᵀa)_(h,t)].
Indeed s max(0,16−M_hf)≥a_hf(16−M_hf), An=0, and each cell has mass four. The checker recomputes B0 in signed 64-bit integer arithmetic with proven room. It checks 5B0≥2s(d−14) and B0>s(ceil(2(d−14)/3)−1); the latter implies h(o)≥ceil(2(d−14)/3), since h(o) is an integer. The stored inequalities require no solver, real arithmetic, or assumption that a relaxation is feasible.

## Complete enumeration and replay

Enumerate oriented simple graphs on up to seven axes. From every representative on n−1 axes append the new axis with each of its 3^(n−1) incidence vectors (absent, forward, reverse), which reaches every isomorphism class by deleting one vertex. Deduplicate by partition refinement (split classes by their in/out neighbor counts into every old class, sorted invariantly), then minimize the adjacency key over *every* permutation respecting the resulting classes. No graph automorphism of G is assumed. There are 2,7,42,582,21480 representatives for n=2,…,6. At n=7 we need only d≥15 and may discard degree-one nodes or bridges of B. For a dirty arc a→b the row at any of its points is a circulation nonzero there and zero on arcs entering a or leaving b. Thus after deleting these forbidden arcs the edge a→b must still lie on a cycle. The enumerator's last reachability test checks connectivity between its endpoints after deleting that edge. The retained list has 9,057 representatives. Directions are not identified under reversal unless an actual permutation maps them. The verifier rejects repeats and checks that every regenerated representative has a certificate.

Run python3 verify.py with a C++17 compiler. The numerical discovery process is not part of the proof. The fundamental cycles, all ternary rows, cell means, moments, every motif-count coefficient and each minimum are reconstructed from the diagrams by the integer verifier. The per-chart data are only the sparse integer multipliers.
