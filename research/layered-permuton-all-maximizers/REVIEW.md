This addresses Problem 3 of Kabela–Král’–Noel–Pierron (2022) for pattern order at least two. It does not settle their other numbered problems, uniqueness of unspecified optimizers, or finite equality.

1. Checked the admissibility of cloning a point with opposite uniform tie keys: ranked samples yield finite permutations at all sample sizes; applying the finite maximum to their expected pattern densities gives the extremal upper bound as sizes tend to infinity. First variation with B the whole space gives h≤p, and its integral makes h=p on a single common full-measure set. The second variation coefficient and its B-then-A average give the displayed c_A+c_B≤2F_AB with the correct signs and no set disjointness condition.
2. In an original successful occurrence the two incomparable prescribed roots are in the same decreasing target layer; they have identical relation vectors to all fresh labelled points. Their replacement by two copies at either original root preserves every comparison, since every distinct pair in the same layer remains an inversion and all comparisons across layers are increasing. Thus the ordinary completion event is contained in either clone event, not just its probability bounded. On separated incomparable rectangles the rooted inequalities force equality almost everywhere. Fubini gives equality of full labelled signatures for almost every fresh tuple and almost every incomparable pair, including the failure symbol.
3. For each support inversion and compact interval inside its open span, positive-mass opposite neighborhoods force a common fixed signature across them. A point between them has an inversion to at least one end, since otherwise the two ends would be increasing via that point. Fubini controls these comparisons. The equality continues through overlapping spans by uniform first marginal; a countable subcover handles nulls. Ordinary points are in the support almost surely and miss countably many component endpoints, so pairs in distinct blocks or involving dust never invert. Uniform second marginal then identifies each component with its same-coordinate square and validates arbitrary permuton substitution there.
4. Within a positive block at an original successful occurrence, the selected consecutive subpattern is a union of whole target layers. Any simultaneous nonsingleton layer and other layer would specify two incomparable roots and an interior sample of opposite layer, forcing every point of the block to have a fixed strict comparison with that interior point by the label transfer. A uniform first coordinate on the block rules this out. Conditioning on outside and on sample counts gives the exact nonnegative expansion by internal patterns for any replacement; the all-internal coefficient is |I|^m.
5. For a genuinely mixed internal law q∈(0,1), two disjoint sampled pairs with different orientations occur with positive probability. Some triple of the four is mixed; exchangeability gives strictly fewer monotone r≥3 samples than the corresponding pair. The mixture of two pure replacements dominates every term by this bound and the original zero for nonmonotone terms. Equality from global optimality forces all positive candidate monotone coefficients of r≥3 to vanish and forces the r≤2 affine expression to be identical to p at both endpoints. A uniform internal replacement then adds at least |I|^m/m! for the (nonmonotone) target and contradicts p. Monotone targets are immediate by maximizing at one. Each remaining pure component and diagonal dust excludes 231 and 312, because the inversion edges of each of these patterns connect all three vertices. This supplies the intended layeredness and compactness consequence without a finite-to-limit inference.
