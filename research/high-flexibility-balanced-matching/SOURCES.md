# Sources, scope, and prior-work check

1. **Model and original design question.** D. Freund, S. Martin and J. K. Zhao, [*Two-Sided Flexibility in Platforms*, v3 (March 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04709v3). Their independent-edge sparse bipartite model is used here, not one of their spatial extensions. Their Theorem 1 characterizes the easier objective based on non-isolated vertices. For maximum matching, Theorems 2–3 give particular comparisons, and §8 (first future-research question) asks whether a maximizer is always one-sided or balanced. The present result is a regime statement for that question, **not** a global resolution at arbitrary rates.
2. **Exact input, existing limits and prior comparisons.** T. Ameen, F. Sentenac and S. H. Yu, [*Flexibility allocation in random bipartite matching markets: exact matching rates and dominance regimes*, v2 (May 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02295v2). Their Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, Eqs. (1)–(3), give the exact type-dependent variational formula. Their Propositions 3–4 give the limiting one-sided and balanced objectives; Appendix C proves them by compactness. Their Eq. (26) extends the optimization domain in the balanced case. These tools and the two special limits are prior. Their Theorem 4 proves eventual balanced dominance over one-sided for budgets at least 2−2e/3 and below one. Theorem 5 covers sufficiently small and sufficiently large regular rates at each fixed partial budget. The text following these theorems identifies a remaining intermediate range at smaller budgets. Their Theorem 2 (full budget) and Theorem 3 (zero regular rate, high flexible rate) are distinct parameter regimes where the one-sided vs. balanced comparison goes the other way. Neither is included here.
3. **What is added.** The scalar limiting formula is derived for every budget split, including the possible zero-unmatched plateau. For the two canonical splits, the equal-value companion of the scalar maximizer handles multiple critical points; the earlier small-rate comparison already used the same exponential tangent inequality. The separate positive-curvature argument proves exact global optimality at sufficiently large *finite* flexible rates, even when multiple allocations tie after taking that rate to infinity. It uses the full established matching formula. Limits and comparisons are for large-market maximum matching rates, not a finite-population version.
4. **Related but different settings.** [Bordenave, Lelarge and Salez, *Matchings on infinite graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0712) provide foundational local-weak and recursive methods behind the ASY matching theorem. Their formula is not an allocation-design comparison. The same ASY authors' [*A uniformity principle for spatial matching*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13426v3) compares service-range assignments among individual supply nodes in a random geometric graph. It does not allocate binary flexibility probabilities across the two sides of this independent-edge block model. The authors' [May 2026 seminar description](https://calendar.hkust.edu.hk/events/department-industrial-engineering-decision-analytics-joint-iedaisom-seminar-flexibility?organizer_landing=14424) also distinguishes the two problems.
