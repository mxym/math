**PASS for both directions**, for frozen [the characterization](PROOF.md), in its pre-formatting version, SHA-256 2cb3a37ab89c9445aac392783187dff7450e95c81f53d08aeb7d98da4e0220e4, with its referenced rectangular allocation proof (90af67fc…) and existing finite-prefix theorem of [#120](https://github.com/mxym/math/blob/45e49af8f0271d64eac7a3a7b6c4a028d34d71aa/research/random-chores-factor-two/PROOF.md). Scope: exact complete ordinary EF, one fixed atomless cost law on [0,1], independent across each agent and chore. The criterion is an iff for a **uniform-width, one-sided band of sizes below 2n**. It is not an iff for existence at any particular fixed ratio, nor a sharp ratio threshold for each law. **Quantiles and sufficiency.** Extend F by 1 above the support and take its left inverse on (0,1). Atomlessness gives continuity and F(0)=0. By the intermediate value theorem and compactness, Q(u)>0 and F(Q(u))=u; strict increase is unnecessary. A finite limsup is exactly existence of a finite D bound for **all** sufficiently small positive u, allowing D strictly above the limsup. Use the latent, measurable row variable z_i=X_i+d max Y_i rather than an unknowable cost from the losing entries of reserve columns. On the already verified, exposure-measurable event, Σz_i≤1733 and z_i≤u_n=C(log n)²/n. The uniform quantile of each assigned A or candidate B cell in its own row is ≤z_i. Hence their combined cost is ≤T_i=2Q(z_i). The original proof does not need a bound on ΣT_i: conditional safety has success probability at least Π(1−F(T_j))≥Π(1−Dz_j)≥e^{-2D·1733}, and the last inequality is valid for n large, Dz_j≤1/2. Independence across reserve columns is inherited without further conditioning on the resulting safe set. Next choose the deterministic common mark threshold 2Q(u_n), not a data-dependent threshold; its probability under F is ≤D u_n. Thus the unconditioned iid marked graph has precisely the sparseness needed and the independent rectangular Hall event is unchanged. For k≤n·min{1/48,e^{-3466D}/3462} the proof supplies the asserted exact partition, valid for every such deterministic sequence. On the failed latent-budget event no Q(z_i) need be evaluated, so even z_i>1 off event causes no defect.

**Necessity.** If the ratio is unbounded in limsup, take u_j↓0 with v_j=F(2Q(u_j)) satisfying v_j/u_j→∞. For m_j=⌊1/√(u_jv_j)⌋, which diverges since v_j≤1, the simultaneous estimates m_j u_j≤√(u_j/v_j)→0 and m_j v_j≥√(v_j/u_j)−v_j→∞ handle the floor and include v_j bounded away from zero. For one row let x=Q(u_j). A union bound excludes every observation at or below x at probability cost at most m_j u_j. The independent count in (x,2x) is binomial with expectation m_j(v_j−u_j)→∞ and variance bounded by the same mean; no independence between the count and the preceding exclusion event is necessary. Atomlessness justifies both end conventions, including x, 2x=1 or 2x>1. Thus for **each fixed** K, with probability tending to one the two smallest costs sum to more than 2x, yet the first K are all below 2x. This certifies the requisite prefix. Rows are independent and their rank labels are iid uniform permutations independent of the sorted observations. Independently thinning eligible rows by 0.9/γ_j gives exactly the row/list model in #120. This is only a proof device; any valid EF allocation forbids the derived overload for every realization of thinning.

I reread the cited obstruction instead of equating a 'cheap K-list' with nonexistence on its own. At m/n→c∈(1,2), an EF assignment requires ≥2n−m singleton bundles because all costs are positive almost surely. No more than 2(m−n) chores can be nonsingletons. Each fixed-depth double request certifies a nonsingleton in *any* EF allocation. The convergent one- and two-root local computations in #120 give the fraction 1−(1+λ)e^{-λ} for the finite K and finite depth model; those fixed choices really can exceed 2(1−1/c) since the increasing infinite-list iteration would have a finite fixed point only if y e^{-y}=0.9/c, impossible because 0.9/c>1/e. First pick finite depth, then finite K with a positive margin. No simultaneous limit of K, depth and n is needed. In particular the sequence of m_j is fixed using **only F**, independent of c. Set n_j=⌊m_j/c⌋ only after c is fixed; eventually 1<m_j/n_j<2 and its limit is c, so #120 applies. Given any proposed width δ, take c strictly between max{1,2−δ} and 2. The resulting violating sizes eventually lie inside its supposedly successful band. This proves the converse with the stated common chore-count subsequence and pointwise-in-c probability, not uniformity over all c.

**Boundary examples and prior.** Regular variation of F at zero with finite positive index s gives F(2Q(u))/u→2^s (assuming the usual positive support arbitrarily near zero), whereas a positive lower endpoint has ratio→∞ since F(2Q(u)) is bounded below by a positive constant. For e^{1−1/x} near zero, F(2x)/F(x)=e^{1/(2x)} as stated. A local two-sided linear CDF gives bound D=2b/a; the bespoke linear proof gives a better sufficient coefficient, correctly not marketed as optimum. The prior [Manurangsi–Suksompong positive theorem](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/full/10.1137/25M180161X) starts at 2n for globally PDF-bounded laws. It gives no contradiction to this *below* 2n result, nor does this dichotomy itself prove m≥2n sufficiency under arbitrary atomless laws. #120 supplies the key earlier converse engine and a fixed law depending on each targeted coefficient; [#122](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/122) gives lower bounds for all nonatomic laws and for uniform/index-one tails, not this classification.
