# Prior results and what this classification does not claim

**The allocation problem.** Manurangsi and Suksompong, [*Asymptotic Fair Division: Chores Are Easier Than Goods*, journal text](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/full/10.1137/25M180161X), [IJCAI 2025 text](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0444.pdf). Theorem 1.1 gives high-probability exact EF for m≥2n and every fixed globally PDF-bounded law, with an algorithm. Their Theorem 1.2 proves high-probability nonexistence below about 1.1256n under non-atomicity alone. The question here is different: which *fixed* laws have a positive-width interval immediately **below** 2n in which every size sequence has EF with high probability? The theorem does not characterize all feasible ratios or identify a threshold even for the uniform law. In particular, the original theorem for m≥2n must not be cited outside its hypotheses.

**Repository predecessors.** [Fixed-law subsequences and the worst-distribution coefficient](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/45e49af8f0271d64eac7a3a7b6c4a028d34d71aa/research/random-chores-factor-two), PR #120: for each c<2 a tailored but fixed PDF-bounded distribution defeats that coefficient along a subsequence (the density bounds need not be shared over c). The present necessity direction uses its deterministic ownership implication, finite Poisson-tree limit and strict-margin choice of finite K and t directly, with full credit; what changes is how a *given* F supplies the certified prefixes. [Distribution-free and natural-law lower bounds](https://github.com/mxym/math/tree/a0f06c175ded0762cfd105080d580bcb30b4f92a/research/random-chores-natural-lower-bounds), PR #122: whp nonexistence below about 1.260168934 for all nonatomic laws, and below about 1.361451486 for uniform/index-one regular variation. These are lower obstructions, not sufficient conditions in a left band at 2 and not the optimal thresholds. For PDF-bounded laws, the earlier worst-law result and the present positive result differ in the order of the quantifiers.

**Prior proof tools.** [Manurangsi–Suksompong, *When Do Envy-Free Allocations Exist?*, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1279125); [direct conference PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/4042/3920), Proposition 3.9 and Figure 1, plus Lemma 3.12. In the balanced goods assignment, the authors already prune potential two-item envy using overlapping copies of K₂,₂ and use resilient matching in a binomial threshold graph. Our overlapping-four-cycle and Hall steps adapt these ingredients, not a discovery of the forbidden-cycle argument. Expansion of unions of random permutations is classical; see, for the square model, [Bezáková et al., Lemma 20](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume21/19-580/19-580.pdf). Here the rows available to each bijection differ, and singleton rows are chosen after observing the budgets. The proof supplies the needed rectangular, colored incidence estimate rather than invoking square random regularity.

For the iid exponential assignment sum see [Linusson–Wästlund](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0303214) and [Nair–Prabhakar–Sharma](https://web.stanford.edu/~balaji/papers/05proofsof.pdf). Concentration is also known: [Wästlund, *An easy proof of the ζ(2) limit in the random assignment problem*](https://www.math.chalmers.se/~wastlund/easyzeta2.pdf). [Frieze–Sorkin, Theorem 2 and preceding discussion](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/Texfiles/atsp.pdf), study maximum selected edge costs for uniform assignments via short alternating cycles, following Karp–Steele. We give an elementary exponential-cost version and row-truncation concentration argument; these estimates are tools, not claimed new assignment results. Quantile coupling, bounded differences, random-permutation occupancy bounds and elementary regular variation are used in their standard meanings.

**Evidence boundary.** Queries on exact envy-free random chores, fixed-law thresholds below two, distributions near the origin, density/quantile doubling, singleton obstructions, robust matchings and cited-paper follow-ups located the original model and those related results, not an earlier characterization of this left-band property. Goods, free-disposal house allocation, subsidies, and MMS or EFX relaxations have different conclusions. All proofs and checks are AI-assisted; checking by another AI does not constitute external review or establish priority.
