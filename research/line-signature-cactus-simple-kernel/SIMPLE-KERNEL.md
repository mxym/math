# All simple branch kernels

Let $M=Q(G)-2I$ where $Q$ is the signless Laplacian, and let $s(L(G))$ be the signature of the line-graph adjacency. The incidence identity gives $s(L(G))=\operatorname{sig}(M)-c+1$. We use the [branch/path extremal reduction](../line-signature-cyclomatic-kernel/PROOF.md), §§1–3: removing pendant forests and rounding marked degree-two runs produces an upper comparator with the same suppressed kernel, unchanged cycle rank, arbitrary bare path residues and only optional pendant leaves at branch vertices. Odd paths contribute signed links; even paths impose signed equalities. A retained leaf increases the branch diagonal by 2 and costs one unit of signature. We prove the proposed fixed-rank upper bound for arbitrary c whenever the loop- and parallel-edge-permitting kernel obtained by suppressing degree-two vertices of the connected 2-core in fact is simple. All bridges, all positive subdivision lengths and all attached trees allowed. The original graph itself is always simple, which by itself does **not** imply simplicity of the suppressed kernel.

First suppose the kernel K is simple cubic of order b=2c−2. Mark h branch vertices where a leaf survives. After path elimination the signature of the full vertex-space matrix is sig(B|U)−h. U is cut out by the even-edge relations z_u=±z_v. Pick r linearly independent relations spanning all of them. Let X consist of the h marks and all endpoints of the r chosen relations. Then |X|≤h+2r and every even edge touches X: a relation supported on two unmarked outside-X coordinates would be a nonzero vector not in the span of the basis relations supported inside X. On outside-X vertices the form represented by B has diagonal 1; their links are odd, with entry ±1. All coordinate vectors supported outside X lie in U.

If h+r≥b/4 the dimension estimate suffices: sig(B|U)−h≤b−r−h≤3b/4. Otherwise the simple subcubic graph K-X has at least 3b/2−3h−6r edges. By Andersen's strong edge-coloring theorem (Discrete Math. 108 (1992), 231–252, DOI:10.1016/0012-365X(92)90678-9), its edges split into ten induced matchings. The two largest color classes cover k≥(3b/2−3h−6r)/5 edges. For each selected edge (i,j) of sign e, take w_ij=e_i−e e_j, an isotropic vector for B. In a single color all pairs of these vectors have zero bilinear products because that matching is induced and avoids X. Vectors from both colors are linearly independent: the two matchings together have no cycle, as a three-edge alternating path with distinct edges would make the two edges of the same color at distance two. Each component is a single edge or a two-edge path; alternatively leaf-stripping gives independence of signed edge vectors on a forest. Their span W⊂U has signature zero: in the independent edge basis its matrix has zero diagonal square blocks, hence pairs eigenvalues ± by changing the sign on one color class. Principal interlacing (in an orthonormal refinement, or maximal-positive/negative dimension directly) gives sig(B|U)−h≤b−r−k−h≤7b/10 + r/5 −2h/5≤3b/4, since r<b/4. Because c=b/2+1, the desired bound permits sigma≤3b/4+1. In fact this proof gets the displayed one-unit saving.

Now assume the kernel K is any simple graph of minimum degree at least three. Split a degree-d vertex into a trivalent tree of d−2 new vertices with d exterior half-edges (each new vertex has total degree three). Assign the d old incidences bijectively to precisely those ports, one endpoint on the former vertex tree for each old edge. Since K was simple, the resulting cubic kernel K' is simple: no old edge was a loop, at most one connected any pair of different vertex trees, and all within-tree links are simple. Replace the split links by length-four paths, thereby imposing equality of endpoint coordinates; their hyperbolic contributions have zero signature. Assign any old branch mark to exactly one of its copies. Each trivalent coordinate contributes diagonal 1, hence after enforcing those equalities their total contribution is d−2, the original diagonal, with all other old couplings and even-edge constraints unchanged. Thus c, h, and the signature are preserved exactly after this lifting (not the full positive/negative counts). b' now equals 2c−2, and the first case applies. No claims here for unavoidable loops or parallel kernel links; the terms 'signed graph' and 'suppressed kernel' refer to formal matrix reductions, not to a relaxation of the simple root graph hypothesis. The coloring theorem and path reduction are existing ingredients; this is a separate application of them.
