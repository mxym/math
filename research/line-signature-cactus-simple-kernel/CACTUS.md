# The fixed-cycle-rank line-graph signature for every cactus

A cactus is a finite connected simple graph in which each edge is on at most one simple cycle; cycles may meet at vertices, have any length, or be connected by bridges and branching trees. We show that for every cactus G,
  2 s(L(G)) ≤ c(G)+1,  c=|E|-|V|+1.
Paone–Paone, Beyond the 2-Core, Theorem 5.3, already construct attaining integer signatures floor((c+1)/2) on cactus graphs (including even c by their attached C4); thus this determines the maximum in the cactus class at every rank. It is not a proof on all graphs. Their Roses and Generalized Thetas, Prop. 10.1, gives a sufficient residue-count criterion for bridgeless cacti and Remark 10.2 explicitly discusses many-port obstacles. Their path responses and the incidence identity, not new here, are used below.

### Universal port bookkeeping

Write M=Q−2I, sig=positive minus negative index. The desired bound is 2 sig M−3c≤−1. Odd bare paths, after removing their zero-signature interior, contribute a signed link ±1 between terminals. Even bare paths instead constrain their terminal coordinates to be equal up to sign and leave zero net signature from the eliminated directions. Specifically an odd path of length 1 mod 4 has sign +, one of length 3 mod 4 sign −; lengths 0 and 2 mod 4 impose equality and opposite equality respectively. The terminal degree diagonals are retained. This follows by Schur elimination on path adjacency, including the alternating kernel of odd-order interiors; see [the earlier kernel note](../line-signature-cyclomatic-kernel/PROOF.md) and Paone–Paone, *Roses and generalized theta graphs*, §§3–4. Signed formal links or constraints can always be realized by ordinary paths with disjoint interiors and length 3,4,5 or 6.

Make cycles vertex-disjoint before rooting: for each original vertex v on cycles, give each incident cycle its own port copy v_C carrying the two incident edges of that cycle. Join each such copy to the central v by a fresh length-4 path. Bridges remain on central vertices. If v had k cycle incidences and d other incident half-edges, the new central diagonal is d+k−2; the k degree-three port copies contribute k more. The length-four paths equate all k copies to v, so the sum on this subspace is the original diagonal d+2k−2. The cycle rank and signature are both preserved. This construction keeps each copy pair of a cycle together; arbitrarily splitting a vertex would not necessarily keep the cactus property. Contracting each cycle in the expanded graph gives a tree (parallel connections or any resulting cycle would violate the cactus condition). Thus a rooted branch consists of point vertices and cycle blocks joined via single ordinary bridges. All ports on a cycle have at most one bridge each. Length-four connector interiors count as ordinary point vertices. Choose a central vertex as root. For trees already no preliminaries are necessary.

At the incoming bridge of a branch, let w be the coordinate functional at its parent (more generally any real linear functional) and H any real symmetric host form before adding this bridge. The bridge adds w² on the parent and a preloaded +1 on the root, as well as their unit coupling. We compare contributions to 2 sig minus three times **branch** cycle rank. A canonical message of budget C and shift y means the function
    F_(C,y)(H,w) = 2 sig(H+y wwᵀ)−C.
The four canonical choices are (0,0), (1,2/3), (2,2), (3,4). We prove every rooted branch has its value dominated by at least one of these functions for each host. In fact the reductions allow a fixed choice for the given branch, independently of the host. In the induction any direction of host singularity, or coincidences of terminal functionals, are permitted.

For a finite root block B with signature sigma, cycle budget and already charged child costs giving C=3c_block+sum paid costs−2 sigma, suppose the root coupling ℓ is range-compatible, that is Bx=ℓ is soluble. Then its outgoing canonical form has y=1−g, g=ℓᵀx, by ordinary range-compatible Schur congruence; g is independent of the solution. An arbitrary shift at budget C≥4 is dominated by (0,0), since changing one diagonal functional changes sig by at most two. At C=0,1,2,3 it is enough to prove y≤0,2/3,2,4 respectively (monotonicity under positive-semidefinite changes). When ℓ is not in the range, the uncoupled kernel at the root block pins the host functional w=0 with one hyperbolic pair if w≠0. The value is 2 sig(H restricted to w=0)−C, at most 2 sig(H)+2−C by interlacing. The same inequality holds for w=0. Thus any such case with C≥2 is discarded.

### A point vertex

After eliminating all child branches via their responses, put T=sum C_i. The root pivot is x=−1+sum y_i; the initial −1 is for the single incoming edge, all child degree terms already being in y_i. With canonical children 3x is an odd integer. Thus output C=T−2 sign x and g=1/x. For T=0,1,2,3 the maximal sum of y_i is respectively 0, 2/3, 2, 4. If x>0 this implies T≥2, and C=0,1 respectively give x≤1,3. If x<0, C=T+2: at C=2, T=0 and x=−1; at C=3, T=1 and x=−1/3. At positive pivots with C=2 or 3 one has g>0, sufficient. These are precisely the needed small budget bounds, with C≥0 in every case.

### Erasing decorations along a cycle, without touching its port

The parent bridge of a cycle uses a port r with no child bridge. Every other cycle vertex has at most one child decoration. A canonical decoration adds y to the diagonal and charges C in the doubled-signature objective. Unmarked cycle vertices have diagonal zero, and r has diagonal 1 (preload). We use comparisons valid against every exterior symmetric form, including the parent context. Roots never lie among the marks removed. A replacement link on the cycle preserves the one unit of cycle rank for the cycle itself. Deleted children have already been accounted for in their C, so no further cost is implicit.

A single internal marked site of weight y∈(0,2] between two terminal coordinates a,b (sign switches allowed) pivots positively, contributing 1 to sig and the exterior form −(a+b)²/y. The bare odd link −2ab dominates that form: its difference has diagonal 1/y and off-diagonal 1/y−1, hence eigenvalues 1 and 2/y−1. Thus a (2,2) mark can always be erased at its paid signature cost C/2=1. Empty messages require nothing. Realize replacement links by fresh paths if needed. All other marks have type 1 or 3.

Take two consecutive such marked sites along the cycle cut open at r. Their separating undecorated path is either a signed odd link of sign e or an even equality up to sign. Take external terminal coordinates a,b at the immediate neighbors outside the two sites and separating path; they may coincide, switched signs are harmless, and the rest of the graph is arbitrary. The outer incident edges can be treated as unit links, by realizing earlier replacements as ordinary paths first. Four-subdivision is harmless if necessary.

* If the intermediate path is even, impose equality up to sign and combine the sites. There is now one effective degree-two pivot with diagonal y_i+y_j. In the (1,1) case this is 4/3, and the single-site rule above erases it at cost 1. In the other cases the total available cost (C_i+C_j)/2 is at least 2. Reset the combined diagonal to zero at a signature loss for the comparison of at most 2 by the rank-one bound. The resulting unmarked signed path is equivalent to a bare path.
* If the intermediate path is odd, the two-site interior matrix is D=[[y_i,e],[e,y_j]], with two external unit couplings (outer signs may be switched in a,b).
  - For (1,1), D has determinant −5/9 and signature zero. On the boundary hyperplane b=e a the vector (a,b) has nonnegative inverse quadratic form: its numerator is (y_i+y_j−2)a²=−(2/3)a² and its denominator is −5/9. Hence the Schur term −(a,b)D⁻¹(a,b)ᵀ is nonpositive on that hyperplane. Restricting any ambient form to a hyperplane loses at most one unit of signature upwards (sig original ≤ sig restricted + 1). The constrained, uncharged bare even link therefore dominates after the available cost 1. When the two boundary coordinates coincide, the restriction has codimension zero or one; the statement is unchanged.
  - For (1,3) or (3,1), D is positive definite, its signature 2 exactly paid by the two budgets. A bare signed link of sign e dominates its exterior correction −D⁻¹: the difference [[0,e],[e,0]]+D⁻¹ has positive diagonal and determinant 3/(y_i y_j−1)−1≥0 since y_i y_j=8/3≤4. Hence the shift is positive semidefinite.
  - For (3,3), D is positive definite, so its exterior correction is negative semidefinite. Pay its two positive pivots, restrict to either signed equality at cost at most 1, then forget the negative correction. The total charge is 3 as available.

These comparisons replace two marks by one bare link and no marks, with any change in twice the signature bounded by their two budgets. Iterate, leaving zero marks or one odd mark besides the unchanged port.

### The finite cycle boundary states

For a bare cycle, length 1 mod 4 yields the port scalar 1+2=3, so C=1 and g=1/3. Length 3 mod 4 gives 1−2=−1 and C=5, discarded. Length 0 mod 4 imposes no constraint, with root scalar 1, C=1, g=1. Length 2 mod 4 pins the root to zero, C=3, g=0 in the compressed zero-dimensional space. In each case diagonal +1 at the port is the parent preload; other eliminated directions have signature zero.

With one mark (j,y)=(1,2/3) or (3,4), two arcs of residues a,b connect root and mark. Let k be the sum of signs of the odd arcs and record the sign s of each even equality. The residual matrix is B=[[1,k],[k,y]] restricted to the even constraints, port functional z_root. If both signs of equality occur the residual is zero dimensional (sigma,g)=(0,0). If one sign s occurs, the compression in coordinates (z,sz) has scalar d=1+y+2ks; sigma=sign d, g=1/d when d≠0. If neither equality occurs, det B=y−k²; since tr B>0 the signature is 2,0,1 according as the determinant is positive, negative or zero. At nonzero determinant g=y/(y−k²). In every case C=3+j−2 sigma. The reduced port treatment is valid even when the eliminated path block had a kernel: do the reduction with the exterior coupling present; kernel constraints depend only on the two site coordinates and the external functional then acts by the restricted root coordinate.

The 32 residue pairs and choices of j give compatible states with the following minimum g at each attained cost:
       C :    0       2       4        6
     min g:   1     3/11   −1/5      −3 .
The only incompatible states have j=3 and residues (1,1) or (3,3); these have C=4 and are discarded by the port rule. These few rational cases are also rederived by [verify_cactus.py](verify_cactus.py). In particular each output is dominated by a canonical message. This finishes the block-tree induction.

### Remove the dummy parent

Take the real parent host H=[0], w=1. Adding its endpoint of the bridge makes the actual parent diagonal 1. Pivoting this scalar subtracts the +1 preload at the root and contributes +1 to sig, so the bounded objective is exactly 2 sig M(G)+2−3c. The four possible bounds 2sig([y])−C are 0,1,0,−1; the maximum is 1. Therefore 2 sig M(G)−3c≤−1, as claimed. The proof is an inequality, not a complete inertia formula or an equality-case classification.
