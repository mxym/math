# Original question and boundaries of this note

1. Andrea Paone and Marco Paone, [*Line-Graph Signature Beyond the 2-Core*, version 1.3](https://aletheia-technologies.it/en/research/line-graph-signature-beyond-the-2-core/reader/) ([deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21706797)), Conjecture 5.4, ask about all finite simple connected root graphs. Proposition 5.2 gives the prior coarse upper bound at every rank, already sharp at ranks 0 and 1. The odd-rank cactus amplifier is from Andrea Paone's [*Unbounded Signature of Line Graphs*](https://aletheia-technologies.it/en/research/unbounded-signature-line-graphs/reader/); *Beyond the 2-Core*, Theorem 5.3, includes the even-rank neutral square extension. Together these already give all the matching cactus **lower** bounds. For rank zero the isolated vertex, for example, attains signature zero. The incidence reduction and rooted Schur bookkeeping are established ingredients.
2. Paone and Paone, [*Line-graph inertia of roses and generalized theta graphs*](https://aletheia-technologies.it/en/research/line-graph-inertia-roses-generalized-theta/reader/) ([deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21744051)), §§3–4, establish the balanced path blocks and singular endpoint constraints. Their closed inertia formulas for roses and generalized theta graphs give other all-rank classes. Proposition 10.1 treats bridgeless cacti and implies the conjecture when the counts $n_i$ of cycles of length $i\bmod4$ satisfy $n_0+n_1\le n_2+3n_3+1+2\mathbf1_{n_0>0}$. Remark 10.2 explains the limitations with several articulation vertices. We derive the inequality (not full inertia) **for every cactus**, with bridges allowed and no residue condition, using a four-state upper envelope in place of an exact transfer of the boundary response.
3. Paone and Paone, [*Response Protection for Line-Graph Equality Families*](https://aletheia-technologies.it/en/research/response-protection-line-graph-equality-families/reader/) ([deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21793638)), already propagate response inequalities through four-subdivisions and selected module attachments, and treat one- and two-edge extensions of their protected family. Neither a new lower construction nor a generalization of all their stronger response/equality claims is intended. [Francis and Uptain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22874) also obtain unbounded signatures and bridge transfer arguments. These refutations concern [Akbari et al.'s uniform bound](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2025.114953), **not** the fixed-rank inequality here. See also [Wang and Fan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1003) for bounds using residue classes of cycles in the line graph rather than the cycle rank of its root; the four-subdivision and path techniques have earlier precedents.
4. Lars Døvling Andersen, [*The strong chromatic index of a cubic graph is at most 10*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(92)90678-9), Discrete Mathematics 108 (1992), 231–252, covers every simple graph of maximum degree at most three. See also Horák, Qing and Trotter, [*Induced matchings in cubic graphs*](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/85.pdf). The edge-coloring theorem is theirs; our application is two color classes giving a zero-signature restriction after deleting selected coordinates. For reducing arbitrary pendant trees and marked degree-two runs while keeping the same branch kernel, we use this repository’s [earlier note, §§1–3](../line-signature-cyclomatic-kernel/PROOF.md) ([PR #143](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/143)).
