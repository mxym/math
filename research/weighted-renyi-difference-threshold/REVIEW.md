# Independent adversarial review: local test for the weighted Rényi difference bound

**Disposition: PASS within the stated quantifiers.** The internal review was performed on the mathematical text and accompanying exact-arithmetic script before editing for publication. The second certificate derived in this review has since also been included in the [proof](PROOF.md); the final files are identified by SHA256SUMS. This is an internal AI audit, not external peer review, a formalization, or a global priority assertion.

## Quantifiers and sources

[Han–Zhan–Zhou–Zhu, v1, Theorems 3, 4, 5, Eqs. (58), (61)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15716v1) use independent copies of one log-concave Lebesgue density, all positive weights and 1≤p≤∞ for the upper bound. They prove sharpness and classify equality at finite p≥1, with a larger class for min-entropy. They also give the exponential lower bound for the subunit *unweighted* supremum. For each fixed p<1 they already disprove the unweighted bound in all sufficiently high dimensions by a simplex; their (58) can be tested at individual finite n and p. Their (61) asks for the **unweighted** supremum in a fixed dimension, the optimizing laws, and whether equality orders form an interval adjacent to 1. The present calculation gives a strict **subrange** in (61), not a complete determination of that constant, its ordering, its interval question, or a universal converse for each fixed pair of weights. In their Remark 16 the uniform three-simplex already fails at p=1/2. The earlier [Melbourne–Tkocz paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10930) treats the unweighted higher-order bound, among other results.

The actual universal-quantifier statement is: in **each** fixed n≥2, the family of bounds over **every** positive pair and every density holds exactly for p≥1. Equivalently, for each p<1 some pair (depending on p) and some density fail. Neither the same pair for all p<1 nor every pair for every p<1 is established. At equal weights the sufficient range is p<2−2^(1/n), strict. At the boundary there is only absence of a positive right first derivative along the prescribed finite-moment paths. In particular, no established theorem for p≥1 is contradicted.

## Analytic checks

1. After setting λ+μ=1, each base difference coordinate has asymmetric Laplace density exp(−z_+/λ−z_−/μ), including the normalization factor one. Its p-power integral is 1/p, as for the input exponentials. The transformation f_t=f_0 exp(−tV)/Z_t preserves log-concavity, full-dimensional support on the orthant, and finite p-power integrals; needed positivity of the normalizers and integrals is immediate. If a finite convex V is chosen with atypical boundary values, take the canonical upper-semicontinuous density representative: boundary values do not change the laws. The actual witnesses use a smooth potential.
2. Change measure on the *baseline* joint law by g_0(Z)^(p−1)/∫g_0^p. This tilt, normalized because Z has density g_0, does not change conditional X,Y given Z. For W=V(X)+V(Y)≥0, (1−E[e^(−tW)|Z])/t≤E[W|Z]. Since R∈[0,1] and R^p≥R, the same envelope bounds (1−R^p)/t; its pointwise limit equals p E[W|Z] (finite by the marginal moment assumptions). This validates the one-sided derivative despite the negative exponent p−1 in the tilt and the unbounded V. Differentiating the log of (1) needs only these four first moments; new joint-law independence of X,Y is neither true nor assumed. The right derivative times (1−p)/p is P_p V+P_1 V−Q_X V−Q_Y V with that sign.
3. Independently integrating the tilted density directly over y at fixed x in one coordinate gives terms pA(exp(−p x)−exp(−x/μ)) on 0≤y≤λx/μ and exp(−x/μ) on the other part. Their sum is pA exp(−p x)+(1−A) exp(−x/μ)/μ for A=λ/(1−μp). Interchange λ and μ for Y. The tilt factors over coordinate pairs, giving independence across coordinates and the stated two product-of-mixture (not mixture-of-products) marginals.
4. For independent exponential scale variables the summed two probability laws have total mass 2 and coordinate integral 1+1/p. Because 1/p>1>λ,μ, the all-slowest-rate atom has **exactly** A^n+B^n. If that mass is at least 1, subtraction of one unit leaves a positive probability measure with mean vector ones. Applying Jensen for each fixed exponential vector (linear diagonal change of scales), then integrating, proves sufficiency for all right derivatives to be ≤0. This first-variation classification assumes the **four finite expectations** of subsection 1, V≥0 convex and finite on the orthant, and fixed support. It is a criterion for existence of a *positive right derivative*, not for general local or global optimality. If the corner mass is smaller, V_s=exp(sΣx) is convex and finite at every point; at each 0<s<p all four means are finite, so the asymptotic after division by (p/(p−s))^n proves positive derivative for some s<p. One first fixes s, then takes t↓0; there is no interchange of these limits. Total-variation convergence of f_t follows from dominated convergence.
5. With μ=(1−p)^3/4, λ=1−μ, B<1 and B^n≤B²≤(1−p)μ/4, whereas 1−A^n≥1−A≥(1−p)μ. Hence the dimension/lower-order universal claim holds including p arbitrarily close to 1, with possibly very small μ,t. For λ=μ the exact substitution A=B=1/(2−p) gives the stipulated strict threshold. No upper assertion for the complementary order range follows.

## Explicit planar certificate, independently derived

For n=2,p=4/7,s=9/16, let P=P_p V, U=P_1 V, Q=Q_X V=Q_Y V. Direct integration gives P=4096, U=256/49, Q=1081600/529, D=P+U−2Q=311040/25921. The original multiplicative estimate for the integrals is valid: concavity gives C^p≤1−p(1−C) and C^p≥C, the cross term is at most pt² PU, and 0≤e^(−z)−1+z≤z^(1+δ) for 0<δ≤1. With δ=1/126 one obtains K=17422205952/461041, t^δ=1/4096, and D−K/4096=55223727/19909444>0.

An alternative proof avoids a cross term. By Jensen twice, first conditionally and then under the tilted Z law,
log E_T R_t^p ≥ −pt (2Q).
By log u≤u−1 and the preceding scalar remainder,
log P_p(e^(−ptV))+p log Z_t ≤ −pt(P+U)+pt^(1+δ) C0,
where C0=P_p(V^(1+δ))+P_1(V^(1+δ))=128²+(224/97)². For the given t=2^(−1512), the gap J(t) is at least t·7802780287/731672067>0. This is a finite rational certificate, not numerical entropy quadrature. The triangle's ratio at p=4/7 is 98/55; (98/55)^7<64=4³, so this particular pair/plane/order is not explained by the triangle formula. The witness is a continuous real-valued density using exponential functions and an exact rational t, **not** a finite discrete or rational-valued density.

The author's script passed in Python -O. The separate standard-library independent.py uses the direct marginal-density integration, verifies exact finite atom/mean identities including both regimes and recomputes both signs, the alternative lower bound, and the triangle comparison. It passed normally and with python -O. Grid tests supplement the analytical proof rather than standing in for it.
