# Problem, prior results and search boundary

**Direct source.** Ryan McCulloch, Brendan D. McKay, Alireza Salahshoori and Thomas Zaslavsky, [*The Cycle Counts of Graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00373-026-03047-3), *Graphs and Combinatorics* **42**, article 53 (2026); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02260). We use §6(b,d) of the version of record. They ask whether there are any further missing cycle counts beyond those seen in their computations. Counts refer to all undirected simple cycles, not just Hamiltonian cycles, distinct cycle lengths, or cycles in a particular basis.

| Prediction | Reported observation, not used as a premise |
| --- | --- |
| §6(b), simple 2-connected cubic graphs | The printed list omits 16 while asserting that the other counts through 4000 occur. Theorem 1 excludes 16 in every inseparable graph. We do **not** use (b) as a verified complete positive list. |
| §6(d), planar subclass | Witnesses are reported through 4000 outside the (b) list and the additional (d) list (largest 131). The text of (d) omits the adjective “simple”; its explicit inclusion of the (b) exclusions and the distinct multigraph Theorem 2 specify the interpretation used here. |

The finite observations belong to those authors. We did not find an order cutoff and complete witness catalog for the simple cubic §6(b,d) observations in their data statement or [linked catalog](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/graphs.html). The data statement concerns inseparable planar graphs through order eight in another part of the paper; the order-14 examples in §4 concern Theorem 2. We do not certify either finite list here or use either to claim all unlisted counts occur. Separate checks of small counts are in progress.

**Why earlier constructions do not supply this conclusion.** Their Theorem 2 determines the cycle spectrum for *cubic multigraphs*, even planar ones. Their Lemma 7 and Theorem 8 supply a tree/outerplanar-dual construction. Parallel edges in that construction are allowed, and subdividing one does not preserve cubicity. The [repository's previous subtree note, cycle appendix](../logarithmic-subtree-counts/CYCLES.md), uses that correspondence to obtain logarithmic order for cubic outerplanar multigraphs and simple **subcubic** outerplanar graphs, not simple cubic graphs. The present construction allows a two-vertex separator but preserves both simplicity and degree exactly three. Thus it does not supply the authors' cases requiring 3-connectivity.

**Related extremal question.** Clark and Entringer, [*The minimum number of cycles in graphs with given cycle rank and small connectivity*](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%2003/vol-003-paper%2014.pdf), study the minimum at a fixed rank and connectivity. Extremal bounds by themselves do not show that every specified, sufficiently large count is attained in the simple cubic planar class.
