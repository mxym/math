# Matrix language and a known prime-power theorem

Identify a bipartite vector with its complex d by d coefficient matrix A, with vector norm the Frobenius norm $\|A\|_F$. A state with $\|A\|_F=1$ has reduced density matrices $AA^\dagger$ and $(A^\dagger A)^T$ in this convention. Bell states have A=X/√d with X unitary. A general bipartite unitary is precisely a complex-linear Hilbert–Schmidt isometry $V:M_d\to M_d$. Define the optimal *amplitude*, not its square,

$$b_d(V)=\frac1d\max_{X,Y\in U(d)}\left|\operatorname{Tr}\big(Y^\dagger V(X)\big)\right|.$$

By Cauchy–Schwarz b_d(V)≤1, with equality exactly for an exact Bell-to-Bell pair, since a global phase can be absorbed into Y. Projectively the Bell manifold is the orbit of [I] under left SU(d)-multiplication, namely PSU(d) in $\mathbb{CP}^{d^2-1}$. Every bipartite gate acts by a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism on projective space: write the gate exp(iH) and use the standard Hamiltonian projective action along exp(itH). The nonzero self-Floer cohomology of this Lagrangian implies intersection with every Hamiltonian image (the Floer chain complex for disjoint Lagrangians would be zero). Thus $b_q(V)=1$ for **every** gate in local dimension $q=p^a$ (a positive power of a prime). The nonzero self-Floer computation is due to [Iriyeh, Theorem 8, for powers of two](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0777) and [Evans–Lekili, Proposition 1.3.5(c) and Example 7.2.3](https://doi.org/10.1090/jams/909); see [Jack Smith, Theorems 4.10–11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04005) for the orbit identification and the full prime-characteristic account. Smith's vanishing result for arbitrary coefficient fields, relative spin structures and flat line bundles in non-prime-power dimensions does **not** imply displaceability, even for a Hamiltonian action, much less a projective-linear one. These are external published inputs whose Floer theory we do not re-prove.

## A finite-dimensional compression that yields uniform asymptotic UBB

Fix d≥2 and a prime power q=d+k in [d,2d); such a q exists, e.g. the next power of two. Take the *smallest* such q if best bounds are wanted; when d is already a prime power take k=0. Given V on M_d, extend it to the isometry on M_q that acts by V on the upper left d by d block and as the identity on its orthogonal complement. The prime-power result above supplies a pair of **unitary matrices** in dimension q, with their scalar phase aligned, of the form

          [ A   C ]                             [ B   C ]
     X  = [ D   E ],       and        Ṽ(X) =   [ D   E ],    B=V(A).

The identity on the complementary *matrix-entry subspace* is essential for the identical-marginal conclusion. Row and column identities of these two unitary blocks give

$$AA^\dagger=BB^\dagger=I_d-CC^\dagger,\qquad A^\dagger A=B^\dagger B=I_d-D^\dagger D. \tag{1}$$

Thus A,B are contractions, have identical left and right Gram matrices, and have at least d−k unit singular values since rank C, rank D≤k. In particular $s=\|A\|_F^2=\|B\|_F^2\ge d-k>0$. Set ψ=A/√s. The state Vψ=B/√s has *exactly* the same two reduced density matrices as ψ (not just equal spectra). Both marginals have largest eigenvalue ≤1/s≤1/(d−k). Thus each of the two states has entanglement min-entropy at least ln(d−k); the same lower bound holds for von Neumann entropy and for positive-order Rényi entropies. Consequently the largest attainable mean entanglement entropy across the input and output splittings lies between ln(d−k) and ln d, a gap at most −ln(1−k/d) nats. This guarantees high entanglement for one state in both splittings; when k>0 it does not assert maximal entanglement.

For a *perfectly* entangled input and output benchmark, each square contraction is the average of two unitaries. Indeed for SVD $A=P\,\mathrm{diag}(s_j)\,Q^\dagger$, use the two diagonal phases exp(±i arccos(s_j)) between $P$ and $Q^\dagger$. Write A=(X₊+X₋)/2 and analogously B=(Y₊+Y₋)/2. The average of the four real numbers $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}[Y_\sigma^\dagger V(X_\tau)]$ is $\operatorname{Tr}[B^\dagger V(A)]=s$. At least one has real part ≥s. We have proved, for every V,

$$1\ge b_d(V)\ge 1-\frac{q-d}{d}.\tag{2}$$

In particular actual squared output overlap with some Bell target, for an exactly Bell input, is at least (1−k/d)². Equation (1) and the rounding step give **two existential statements**: the proof does not guarantee the same input for both. An arbitrary eigenvector of V already preserves its marginals; what (1) adds is simultaneous high entanglement. For example d=6,q=7 guarantees an exactly marginal-preserving state with min-entropy ≥ln5 and a Bell input with Bell-overlap amplitude ≥5/6. This example does not settle exact UBB for arbitrary 6×6 gates.

Use [Baker–Harman–Pintz, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/83.3.532), which yields a prime in $[x-x^{0.525},x]$ for sufficiently large x. With $x=d+2d^{0.525}$ we have $x-x^{0.525}\ge d$ for all sufficiently large d, giving $k=O(d^{0.525})$. Hence uniformly over **every** gate on d×d, both the amplitude deficit in (2) and the entropy deficit for ψ from (1) are $O(d^{-19/40})$. The qualitative o(1) only needs a prime q≥d with q/d→1, as follows from the prime number theorem by fixing any relative interval length and then letting it shrink. For the entropy deficit use $-\ln(1-k/d)\le2k/d$ once $k/d\le1/2$. We need no claimed record exponent; [Runbo Li, arXiv:2308.04458v8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04458v8) announces 0.52, but the stated exponent avoids depending on that preprint. In particular, for any fixed gate on dimension d₀≥2, $b_{d_0^n}(V^{\otimes n})\to1$ even without assuming that optimal input matrices are products over the copies. A high-fidelity multi-copy witness does not imply a single-copy exact one.
