# Exact Bell-to-Bell states for controlled gates, in all dimensions

Write a controlled operation as $V=\sum_i |i\rangle\langle i|\otimes U_i$, with arbitrary Uᵢ∈U(d). It suffices to choose an orthonormal basis x₁,…,x_d for which U₁x₁,…,U_d x_d are also an orthonormal basis. Then all vectors $d^{-1/2}\sum_i z_i |i\rangle\otimes x_i$, $|z_i|=1$, are sent to Bell states. The family contains d orthogonal vectors by using d Fourier phases across i.

Here is a short proof of the orthogonalization claim. Let F=U(d)/T^d be the space of ordered orthogonal complex lines L₁,…,L_d. Form the vector bundle $E=\bigoplus_{i<j}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(L_i,L_j)$, interpreting Lᵢ as the tautological line bundle on F. A smooth section has (i,j)-component

$$s_{ij}=\left.P_{L_j}U_j^\dagger U_i\right|_{L_i}.$$

Its zeros are precisely the flags wanted. As a real vector bundle E≅TF: tangent vectors are skew-Hermitian infinitesimal changes of frame modulo diagonal infinitesimal phases. Giving the maps from Lᵢ to Lⱼ for i<j prescribes all off-diagonal blocks, because the reverse block is the negative adjoint. This construction is coordinate independent and smooth. The Euler characteristic of F is d! (for example its complex Schubert decomposition has one even-real-dimensional cell for each permutation). Consequently Poincaré–Hopf implies that there is a zero, and if all zeros are transverse their number is at least d!: each index is ±1 and their signed sum is d!, after compatible orientation. The signed count is a count of ordered **flags**, with phases already quotiented, not of orthogonal vectors or physical states; in particular it does not promise a Bell basis of d² different outputs.

The flag assertion is an immediate special case of the older universal-subspace theorem [An–Đoković, Thms. 3.4 and 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1915): take simultaneous conjugation of the tuple $(U_j^\dagger U_i)_{i<j}$ and require one (j,i) entry in its matching matrix to vanish. The complementary torus weights are one of each root xⱼ−xᵢ for i<j, giving, up to orientation, the type-A Vandermonde. [Bykov, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.1419#page=24) identifies F with the Lagrangian of orthogonal lines in $(\mathbb{CP}^{d-1})^d$. Its self-intersection number is then ±χ(F), by the usual normal/tangent bundle identification.

One can make the factor d! sharp *in the controlled family* for transverse solutions. Take Uᵢ(t)=exp(it cᵢ H) for a Hermitian H with simple spectrum and pairwise distinct real cᵢ. Divide the smooth section above by t; the constant term before division vanishes on every flag because Pⱼ|_{Lᵢ}=0. At t=0 the section extends with components i(cᵢ−cⱼ) Pⱼ H|_{Lᵢ}. Its zeros are exactly the d! ordered eigenline flags. The derivative at such a flag, represented in a moving orthonormal frame with generator K skew-Hermitian and zero diagonal, has each (j,i) block proportional to (cᵢ−cⱼ)(hⱼ−hᵢ)K_{ji} with nonzero proportionality. Thus the real derivative is invertible. Each eigenflag persists (in fact is an exact zero for all t); by the implicit function theorem it is alone in a fixed neighborhood for small |t|. Compactness and uniform convergence of the rescaled section exclude any further zeros away from these neighborhoods. Hence the lower bound is attained for all sufficiently small nonzero t. Generic independent choices of the Uᵢ give transverse intersections: the group U(d)^d acts transitively on the product of projective spaces, so the evaluation map (U,flag)↦(U₁L₁,…,U_dL_d) is a submersion; transversality to the embedded copy of F follows for almost every parameter by parametric transversality. At an intersection, the conditions defining F there have full normal rank (one complex orthogonality between each pair), giving section transversality as well. No transversality is needed for existence.
