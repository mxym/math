# Original questions, prior results and scope

1. Theodore D. Drivas and Michael Retakh, [*How doth the random triangle*](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18414v1), 2025 preprint, [author's publication listing](https://math.stonybrook.edu/~tdrivas/research.html). They study nondegenerate iid triples drawn from an **arbitrary** law, seek the extreme obtuse probabilities and treat high-dimensional bounds. Their equation (2) is the correct conditional sphere cap sum; the next exact display integrates it over the angle. Our §5 compares the relative asymptotics of that exact expression with the subsequent near-orthogonality substitution. Our theorem is sharp within the rotation-invariant class only and does not refute an unrestricted upper bound. For earlier planar extremal angle proportions (with angle versus triangle normalization distinguished), see Conway, Croft, Erdős and Guy, [*On the Distribution of Values of Angles Determined by Coplanar Points*](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-19.1.137), 1979, [authors' PDF](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1979-13.pdf). Their Theorem 2 proof already uses a separated-cluster perturbation of finite configurations.
2. Brandon M. Greenwell, [*Random Triangles on Concentric Circles*](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06591v1), August 2026. Sections 5–6 give a fixed-radii formula for the plane, the bound and the mixture interpretation. Section 9 distinguishes the routine all-dimension conditioning observation from the higher-dimensional fixed-radius formula and points toward incomplete beta functions. Our (1) is an integral expression for positive, not necessarily equal radii; the main point here is uniform optimality for large dimensions, not a new conditioning principle.
3. Vamshi Jandhyala, [*Why the sphere beats the ball*, author page](https://vamshij.com/research/why-the-sphere-beats-the-ball/), [preprint PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvvk/mathematics/main/why-the-sphere-beats-the-ball/paper/note.pdf), v2 of 10 October 2026. Theorem 1 gives an exact expression for every triple of positive radii in **three** dimensions; the minimum is attained when the two largest coincide. The paper also establishes uniqueness of a single sphere in the iid radial class in that dimension. We make no priority claim for either three-dimensional result. We do not claim that dimension four already suffices.
4. The all-dimension **equal**-radius cap integral already appeared in [joriki's 10 August 2012 public derivation](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/181091/a-question-about-a-metric-space), which also evaluates the case of three points on the 3-sphere, i.e. in ambient dimension four. Arseniy Akopyan, Herbert Edelsbrunner and Anton Nikitenko, [*The Beauty of Random Polytopes Inscribed in the 2-Sphere*, Lemma 4 and Eq. (9)](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2021.1980459), give the ambient-three sphere probability and a general spherical Blaschke–Petkantschin shape disintegration. Neither a classical cap integral nor its beta-variable rewriting is presented as the new extremal theorem.
5. Glen R. Hall, [*Acute triangles in the n-ball*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/abs/acute-triangles-in-the-nball/4EAAF3C4F8653033C210E089E11E6800), treats uniform **volume** in the ball. Gabriel Khan, [*Hall's Conjecture on Extremal Sets for Random Triangles*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05913), concerns convex sampling regions and local extremality. Bennett Eisenberg and Rosemary Sullivan, [*Random Triangles in n Dimensions*](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.1996.12004742), and Alan Edelman and Gilbert Strang, [*Random Triangle Theory with Geometry and Applications*, §3.4](https://math.mit.edu/~edelman/homepage/papers/focm.pdf), treat the Gaussian answer, not a universal sharp result over radial laws. These sampling models are not interchangeable with three prescribed sphere radii.
6. Balázs Gerencsér and Viktor Harangi, [*Acute sets of exponentially optimal size*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03411), construct $2^{d-1}+1$ points with every triangle acute. The small-neighborhood and repeated-label argument in §4 is an immediate consequence of that existing construction: it supplies the comparison with nonradial laws, not a new acute set.

No inspected source established the uniform sharp minimizer and strict deterministic equality case for all sufficiently large ambient dimensions. Index coverage is incomplete.
