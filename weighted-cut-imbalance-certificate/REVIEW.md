## Reduction at arbitrary graph order

1. Normalize total edge weight to one, except for the trivial zero graph. For a random sign at each vertex, edge ij contributes w_ij(ε_i+ε_j) to the signed degree sum and 2(w(S)−w(Sᶜ)) takes precisely the same contribution. The authors' equivalent target thus reads E|Σ d_i ε_i| ≤ (15c−6)/4. Independently, the triangle inequality applied edge by edge gives E|Σ d_i ε_i| ≤ Σ w_ij E|ε_i+ε_j|=1. This already handles c≥2/3. The uniform cut gives c≥1/2; isolating a top-degree vertex gives d≤c. With its side fixed and every other vertex on the opposite side with probability p, the expected crossing mass is pd+2p(1−p)(1−d). Each of 13 resulting inequalities is necessary. It does not matter that the polygon also contains unrealizable points.

2. For the top singleton H, its next b=k−1 vertices G, and the rest R, I avoided elimination entirely. In the order (GG, HG, RR, HR, GR), the nonnegative edge-mass vector e has (1,1,1,1,1)·e=1, (0,1,0,1,0)·e=d. The degree prefix is (2,2,0,1,1)·e. In the randomized cut used for k, the five corresponding coefficients are
   (2r(b−r)/(b(b−1)), r/b, 2p(1−p), p, (r/b)(1−p)+(1−r/b)p).
   In the sole b=1 case the GG mass vanishes; coefficient zero and both signs of its mass inequality are valid. A cut has expected weight ≤c regardless of how many edges share endpoints. The sampling of G is without replacement; the tail vertices are independent of it. Ties can be ordered arbitrarily, isolated padding is harmless and no independence of edge indicators was presumed.

3. In the five coordinates, for each chosen support I solved directly for coefficients of its actual cut rows and negative coordinate inequalities, adjoined to the total-mass and maximum-degree equality rows. Direct rational row reduction and substitution in all five coordinates showed these combine to (2,2,0,1,1), with every coefficient of an inequality nonnegative. This independently derives 2,286 bounds (including the trivial total and order-statistic bounds); no dual optimality, rank completeness, or unverified row is needed. The occasionally named LP witnesses are just elementary nonnegative linear combinations.

4. Sorting/permutation makes K(v)=E|Σv_iε_i| symmetric and it is convex, hence Schur-convex in each common padded dimension. Equivalently, maximizing the signed bounded test function as in the manuscript produces a scalar product with decreasing coefficient sequence after permuting the signs. Abel summation then uses only prefix dominance and equality of totals. For each cell, the certificate chooses the **same affine** upper bound at all its vertices for each 2≤k≤31. This is essential: a minimum of affine upper bounds need not be bounded by the interpolation of its corner values. The convex combination of the certified decreasing corner vectors is decreasing, dominates every true prefix through 31, and has total 2 already at rank 32; all true prefixes from 32 onward are ≤2. It is the **majorizer**, not the graph, that has no tail past 32. This covers any finite number of vertices and arbitrary nonnegative real weights, not merely rational input samples. Convexity of K and affinity of (15c−6)/4 give the interior inequality, including shared faces.

## Independent exhaustive replay

My checker differs in each of the four substantive routines:

- Recover every bound from the actual five physical edge masses, using Gaussian elimination, not the three reduced rows or Cramer's rule.
- Intersect every pair of boundary lines in integer homogeneous coordinates and keep the corners satisfying **all** halfspaces; retain irredundant edge halfspaces before descending. Each split subtracts two verified affine functions and imposes the two opposite non-strict signs in the two children. Bounded full-dimensional polygons are equal to the convex hull of their resulting vertices. The halves cover by logic, not just area; twice-area was also calculated through oriented endpoints of each edge, giving 130792/765765 at the root and in the sum of leaves.
- Construct the least concave majorant of each raw prefix sequence by pooling adjacent increasing slopes, rather than maximizing forward secants. Check every prefix, sign, order, total and dimension directly afterward, so no algorithmic optimality lemma is required.
- After optional second-moment bounds, group equal weights and recursively integrate the **positive part** of the signed integer sum. Conditional on partial sum v and undecided remaining absolute mass R, the total positive part over all residual choices is v times the number of choices if v≥R, and zero if v≤−R. Otherwise sum the conditional answers for the next block with exact binomial weights. Symmetry doubles the final positive-part expectation to obtain the absolute first moment. This does not use the author's meet-in-the-middle arithmetic. Microchecks also compare the routine to all 32 sign outcomes for each of 243 vectors and enumerate the actual cuts for 182 row laws, including p=0, p=1 and b=1.

Both runs independently returned 956 proper splits, 957 leaves, 3,791 corner incidences, 2,318 (corner,leaf-choice) checks by Cauchy and 1,411 by exact signed expectation. The 3,729 figure is **not** the number of geometrically distinct vertices. Closed convex polygons, not necessarily triangles, are the final cells. The coarse fixed-witness proof does not classify equality of graphs; a tight auxiliary bound does not imply that any graph realizes it.

## Sources and prior boundary

The [BEKSZ ITCS 2025 version](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol325-itcs2025/html/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22.html) gives Theorems 2 and 4 and Conjecture 3. The [full ECCC revision](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/revision/1/download/) gives Conjecture 1.3, its equivalent imbalance form (footnote 2), Theorems 1.2/1.4 and the precise ε-dependent constant-rate assertion in Theorem 9.1. It is enough to establish the graph inequality for unrestricted normalized finite edge-weight systems, not merely an eight-color specialization. Conversely the channel argument and protocol construction are **theirs**: one noiseless feedback round in the adversarial binary corruption channel has optimal **supremum** 7/23, and every 7/23−ε is attainable with constant rate depending on ε. This is not a positive-rate claim at exactly 7/23, a multi-feedback result, or a new direct efficient construction. Their separate Conjecture 1.5 on squared degrees is not proved here.

BEKSZ §11 of [rev.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/) treats all equal-weight complete graphs and unweighted graphs of ≥10⁹ edges, with pre-existing triangle and five-clique equality examples. Earlier same-repository [#110](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/110) covers equal present-edge weights at all orders; [#115](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/115) covers arbitrary weights on eight-colorable positive support. Neither is being presented as new and neither is needed in this proof. The recorded previous full integrated term scan looked at 14,852 distinct main blobs and 277 additional tag blobs across 48 tags, with parsed preprint and archive content; a previous supplementary release-asset scan examined 73 additional assets.
