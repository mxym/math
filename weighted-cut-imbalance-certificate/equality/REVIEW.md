# Independent internal review: equality in the weighted BEKSZ inequality

**PASS, finite loopless, undirected graphs with arbitrary real nonnegative edge weights.** For positive total mass, equality holds iff, after discarding zero-weight edges and isolated vertices, the graph is a triangle or a five-clique and the aggregate edge weights on that clique are all equal. The zero-total case is always an equality. No near-equality, stability, graph-limit or further channel assertion was reviewed. This is an internal AI-assisted check, not external peer review, a proof-assistant formalization, or a claim about global priority.

Frozen equality proof SHA-256 `2050bbaf00daab52f3c77ca21359ef6b73e90a9cf4c01261c97db6bfe7658b71`, first equality checker `3a8ea1fc2795c1478b0a3740427ac37d869561f89617697158616fb2d8c0771c`, original published certificate `353673afb1e06fdf1529ec8d81e518e688b4ded7d750f079f8499ccd154a0b9d`. ## 1. Exact corner-exhaustion check

For normalized total 1, 2 is the total degree and B(c)=(15c−6)/4. The cheap bound K(d)≤1 follows directly by the triangle inequality edge by edge: each edge contributes its nonnegative weight times two endpoint signs whose sum has expected absolute value 1. Thus c>2/3 is strictly excluded, not merely weakly proved. The boundary c=2/3 is included in the finite cover.

My checker `independent_equality.py` reproduces the five-coordinate dual bounds by rational row reduction, intersects all supporting lines of closed polygons in homogeneous integer coordinates, constructs a nonincreasing majorizer by pooling adjacent slopes and, when necessary, computes the absolute signed first moment by a grouped, branch-and-bound positive-part recursion. It reads only the hash-pinned JSON from #123. Every split uses the two complementary closed halfspaces, so the union does not discard a tight edge, a zero-area intersection of adjacent closed cells, or a common corner. Equality testing is keyed by the ordered pair (corner, complete tuple of all prefix choices); distinct evaluations at geometrically coincident corners are not merged accidentally.

The 956 proper splits, 957 leaves, 3,791 corner incidences and total twice-area 130792/765765 all match. There were 3,729 distinct (corner, choice) keys. All 2,318 Cauchy branches are **strict**: the squared threshold is at most 1 and the vector mass is 2, ruling out one positive coordinate. With two or more, flipping one strictly positive coordinate of the all-positive outcome strictly reduces absolute value. Thus the absolute sum is not almost surely constant; equality in Cauchy is impossible. Among the remaining 1,411 exact integrals, 1,395 are strict and 16 tight. The complete set is:

| (c,d) | positive coordinates of majorizer | number of distinct tight keys |
|---|---|---:|
| (2/3,2/3) | three copies of 2/3 | 10 |
| (3/5,2/5) | five copies of 2/5 | 5 |
| (3/5,107/260) | three copies of 107/260, two of 199/520 | 1 |

A key count is not a count of actual graphs, and the last row is not a third graph construction. This information is about the fixed certificate only. The proof does not assume the tight sets of all possible comparison vectors are exhausted.

## 2. Passing to equality in an arbitrary graph

For a real degree vector w in nonincreasing order and h=sgn(w·epsilon) (ties=0), put g_i=E[epsilon_i h]. Conditioning on all coordinates except i,j gives g_i−g_j=(1/2)E[sgn(T+w_i−w_j)−sgn(T−w_i+w_j)]≥0. Thus the chosen common h itself has the right sorted influences; no equality-case claim about all permutations of subgradients is needed.

In a closed leaf interpolate the relevant vertex comparison vectors a^z by **nonnegative** barycentric weights theta_z. Every prefix of the resulting a dominates the true degree prefix: use the one fixed affine bound of the leaf for k=2,...,31, rank-one affine maximum degree and the total 2 for k≥32. In particular, no as-yet unknown finiteness of the true positive support was assumed. The difference (a−w)·g is the sum over proper prefix gaps multiplied by g_k−g_(k+1), all nonnegative; the terminal term vanishes by equal total. Hence
w·g ≤ sum theta_z E[(a^z·epsilon)h] ≤ sum theta_z K(a^z) ≤ B(c).
The last equality of budgets uses affinity. Equality from end to end now forces every *positive* interpolation weight to belong to a tight corner **and** to give pointwise maximizing h for that corner on the entire padded sign cube. This follows because each gap and each elementary sign-outcome gap is nonnegative; every sign outcome has positive probability even after adding arbitrary finitely many inert vertices.

The three-coordinate table row has maximizer M3 (simple majority of the first three). Both five-coordinate rows have M5 because each sum of their two largest entries is <1 and the total equals 2. No corner has a signed tie. M3 and M5 disagree on (+,+,−,−,−), precluding a mix of those types. No assertion is needed about whether two corners share a leaf. In either pure case c is determined by the corner x-coordinates. Outside the s active majority coordinates each g_i vanishes by symmetry and independence; inside it equals the probability the other s−1 signs tie, namely 1/2 for s=3 or 3/8 for s=5. These are enough to recover true support: the equality w·g=B(c) forces the degree mass among the first s to be **all** of 2. Nonnegativity of the original weights is used to infer isolated vertices from zero degrees.

## 3. Recovering edges and checking sufficiency

At s=3, each of three singleton cuts is ≤2/3, and its average is 2/3. They all have that value. Solving for the three edge weights gives 1/3 each. At s=5 use all ten two-vertex sides (pad back to five if fewer are nonisolated). Each edge crosses six of them. The mean is 3/5, equal to the maximum; all ten equations must hold exactly, with no inequality slack. For fixed vertex i, summing d_i+d_j−2w_ij=3/5 over j≠i gives d_i+2=12/5. Every d_i is 2/5, then every edge weight is 1/10. This both precludes absent edges and rejects the artificial parameter d=107/260. Conversely direct evaluation gives c=2/3, K=1 or c=3/5, K=3/4, satisfying equality. Homogeneity, parallel edge aggregation and addition of isolates do not affect this conclusion.

The independent script directly inverts the edge-by-cut incidence systems at orders three and five, verifying full ranks 3 and 10 and the unique uniform normalized solution. It checks the majority and each signed influence by exhaustive summation, including two extra inert directions. As a supplementary falsification test (not proof of the universal claim), it exhausts all 59,048 nonzero labeled graphs on five vertices with pair weights in {0,1,2}. Only the 20 uniform labeled triangles and two uniform five-cliques attain equality. These finite graph counts were not used to discover or infer the all-real statement.

## 4. Sources and scope

[Braverman–Efremenko–Kol–Saxena–Zhang, ECCC revision 1, Conjecture 1.3 and §11.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/179/revision/1/download/) explicitly give equal-edge K3 and K5 as equality examples. The same is stated beside [ITCS 2025, Conjecture 3](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2025.22). Their treatment of cliques with equal present-edge weights is prior. Repository [#110](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/110) has the full equal-edge simple-graph classification already; [#115](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/115) addresses eight-colorable weighted graphs without equality necessity. [#123](https://github.com/mxym/math/pull/123) proves the full weighted inequality and explicitly excludes an equality classification from its claims. Its degree/cut reduction, all interpolation witnesses and the matching original data are prior to this note. Comparing its merged commit with [main through #124](https://github.com/mxym/math/commit/27450c79c053d28f9d62ce7009d3808de88b13f0), all thirteen changed filenames concerned random chores, not cut equality. External targeted searches for the BEKSZ title, the author names, weighted equality, K3/K5, and the one-feedback coding model did not reveal an earlier unrestricted weighted uniqueness result. This search does not guarantee priority. The proposed contribution is **necessity of these old equality examples at arbitrary weights**, not an improved noise threshold, new examples, a classification of optimal codes, or resolution of the separate squared-degree conjecture.
